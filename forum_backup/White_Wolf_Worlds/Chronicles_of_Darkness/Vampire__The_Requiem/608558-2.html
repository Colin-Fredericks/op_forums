        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
        Since it is already a trope, as I pointed before, I think it is worth it, or at least as worth as Archmastery. The perceived balance between splats also have impact on the WoD setting, it is also an important theme as you can always be sure both that your equals can achieve god-like power as that even them there are things out there that can munch you. And I'm not talking about adapt the theme of another game, but use the themes and tropes of each gameline to work the same scale, if you want to work such scale.
        Uh, why?

        There's no reason for every line to follow the same standard. They're, from the ground up, already not balanced against each other, and it makes even less sense for all of them to have a "next level" state that is roughly equal in power to all the others if their cosmologies don't lend themselves to it and work on very different paradigms and weight in respect to the World Of Darkness as a whole.

        Pretending that this is necessary to ensure the lines are 'balanced' in their setting space by being able to operate at the same scale uniformly is also a shaky notion at best, considering the rarity of these ascended states and the narrative role of the characters that occupy this level of power, which often ensures they are detached from the struggles that entangle the vast majority of the splat and don't tend to visibly operate or interfere with that layer of the story and don't usually influence it in a way that is felt by most, if they do at all; much less when dealing with the other game lines! The spheres would only cross in the advent of truly world-shaking events occurring in the scope of a common domain or large scale concept that matters to different supernatural beings, before causing more widespread conflict down the pyramid of power; assuming the matter would not be taken over, interrupted, or another manner of stopping event entirely within the range of action of these superior beings, without even affecting those that are not at the same level.

        And finally, the World Of Darkness is only as comprehensive and inclusive as you wish for it to be, so there's no need to worry about enforcing some artificial line of balance between Templates+ when their presence might not even be considered relevant at all or true depending on the chronicle. And of course that goes for the regular Templates as well.

        P.S.: I mean, I guess if that sort of major, reality defining conflict between every splat is someone's thing, sure go ahead, but it doesn't really lend itself to most of them IMHO.

                            @MCN, monteparnas:

                            Imperial Mysteries pretty much opens up with "the beings this book focuses on operate out of the scope of the other lines and make a stark difference in the rough balance of the setting, and we could avoid presenting them to maintain the integrity of crossover. But we're not, because this is what archmages are, and it would be a bit of a drag to not deliver on them as a logical followup to Mage."

                            It goes on to say it assumes there are mechanisms to allow the other games to express themselves in a world where archmasters exist, so it's not going to tone the latter down to accomodate the former. What mechanisms those are is up to the storyteller, if it's even necessary to define them at all. So, like I said, if you feel like the other splats need equals to archmages to maintain their relevance, sure, go ahead. But that doesn't mean every game lends itself to it like mage does, as a natural stretch to their premise.

                            See also my point about inclusivity in the World Of Darkness. I don't think the perception of balance is important, because not all monsters are equal, and not all monsters are the same. An elder vampire is scary. A master mage is scary. An apex werewolf is scary. But they're scary for different reasons and powerful in different ways. Vampire is strawberries, Mage is apples, and Werewolf is, uh, oranges*. And two werewolves may inspire awe for different things, and they'll be frightening to each other, to other werewolves and other supernaturals for those differing factors.

                            Whatever perception of balance is necessary for the WoD, is at the most basic level taken care of by itself, in my opinion. It's not a matter of what's a "better" choice. They can't be compared like that, because they're all distinctive (and there aren't really any gross differences of power if we assume they share the shadows according to what each of their books establishes). And at the heights of Archmages, that equillibrium is even less necessary to present as an assumption of the setting, because beings that play on that level tend to matter very little indeed to what constitutes the core experience of the games of the World Of Darkness.

                            Bottom line: I don't think balance like that is necessary for crossover in the World Of Darkness, nor do I think the books are presented around that notion. But hey, it's the WoD. You can organize its features in your sandbox as you like.

                                            I have experience with crossovers since the 90's with cWoD. That setting took too long to understand the importance of said balance. Werewolf vs Vampire was all about UNbalance. Starting werewolfs where just too more powerful than starting vampires. Elder werewolfs where respectable heroes, whyle elder vampires where god-like. A 6th generation vampire with some centuries was powerful enough to wipe the floor with a Rank 4 pack. Both 6th generation and Rank 4 where concrete possibilities for players after some game time. Baba Yaga was a vampire so powerful she veiled the entire Russia against umbral expeditions for almost 10 years, enslaved packs of Silver Fangs of all ranks, and could only be destroyed by Deus Ex Machina, and you actualy got both the opportunity to sense her presence in the world, meet her and/or witness her demise in a published adventure. And a starting Demon can simply ignore the full extent of Dominate of an Antediluvian.

                                            Those things affect the perception of balance in a variety of ways, even when they never enter play themselves. It impacts game experience. If you're into Mage, or like the notion that a mortal can somehow achieve such god-like power that no other creature lesser than "true" gods can compare, that's good, for sure. But if someone doesn't like the idea that a Mage can somehow attain such power, but others can't, this player will feel the spotlight going in the wrong direction. Even if at no moment at all this will actualy be more than a background collor.

                                            And again, its about options. With Imperial Mysteries there are the option for Archmasters to exist and play a role in my game. But I can simply rule them out of my chronicle. But I can't rule the Elder Vampires in, since there are no rules for them. That, and the fact, again, that there ARE possible tropes for the other splats going into this level of godhood.

                                            You don't see vampires achieving it in the same sense mages do? Well, to begin with, it doesn't have to be in the same sense. As the splats aren't about the same themes and tropes, but retain the perceived balance, you can expand all of them through their themes and their tropes, and achieve a perceived balance with Archmasters without being the same thing. The elder vampire IS a trope, and with longer tradition than the archmaster, mind you.

                                            Werewolfs can attain enough spiritual power to become god-like entities themselfs. Elder vampires can command the night itself, and control entire nations whyle their lesser kin struggle over a single city. Changelings can go to the kingdoms of the Fae, and return as gods of dream and madness themselves (actualy, thet had this option ALREADY on 1st ed, but as a loose-character scenario). Even Hunters can become true legends and go beyond the limits of mortals and monsters alike in their dedication (tough not yet worked in the nWoD, the special hunter that go beyond and necomes a nightmare to the monsters IS a common trope).

                                            But if you don't like them, you can allways simply rule them out. Each one just adds to the possibilities of the setting. You don't have to use them if you don't like them.
