        They are not talking about the action per se, they're talking about the cost.

        Awe's action is Instant. PenDragon is saying to think of Idol less as a separate power, and more as a new option added to Awe. It's still an instant action to activate Awe, but now you can choose to pay the higher cost to boost its power. That cost is paid as a reflexive action either immediately when you activate Awe (still an Instant action) or at any later time while Awe is running (in this case it can be done at any time, even outside your own turn since paying the Idol cost is a reflexive action).

        Awe's action is always instant. Boosting Awe with Idol is reflexive, but need not be done at the same time as you spend your instant action to activate Awe.

                            08-22-2014, 04:51 PM
                            Idol enhances Awe, therefore it cannot be used unless Awe is in effect. However, it doesn't have to be activated simultaneously with Awe. A Vampire could, if they want, activate Awe (and Instant Action) and simultaneously activate Idol (Reflexive), or activate Awe then wait for a while and turn on Idol several turns later (Before people ask: Yes, that means he can turn on Idol in the middle of someone else's action). What he can't do is go from being his average, non-majestic self, straight to being the singular locus of adoration, without first taking an action to turn on Awe.

                            Hopefully that clears things up.
