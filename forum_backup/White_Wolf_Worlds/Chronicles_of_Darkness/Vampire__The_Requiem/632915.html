    [2ED] Five Big Covenants Around the World

    08-19-2015, 09:34 PM
    I proper to run Beast official SAS from corebook and I'm doing so catching up with Requiem 2ED for the crossover puopuse, even if I am rather passerby fan of setting ( more "reading like vampire works as NPCs" than playing chronicles ).

    One thing that strike me in 2ED corebook is that Lancea et Sanctum sounds now as it is only Catholics thing - and we do not have like Protestant, Islam or Judaism version of Second Estate. For a first I could see it cannot work - LS is great Church, not working with various little congregations of Protestant faiths. But Islam and Judaism variants would not be as much different than Catholics based and I would rather suspect to have a bit different flavor LS in Middle East, but still Lancea et Sanctum at the heart - at least Islam is treating Jesus as a prophet, so I suspect vampire after him - the Longinus - would also work for Muslim vampires.

    Second is Circle of the Crone - Am I get it right they are random cults up until XIX century and then are transformed in to proper Covenant by mass disobiediance in Scotland? Something like more limited Great Refusal of Free Council in Mage? Because we have settings like New Orlean, where we have both Baron Cimitière and Nathanial Blanch factions that seems like two almost completely different miniCovenants in to themselves. How could mish-mash like that work as one Coveneat? In New Orlean voodoo vampires would be much more older than XIX pagans of "Circle". What even more disturbing is that Cruac should steam from local believes - or does it all now Irish and Scotland "blood magic", with previous pagan being sticked to mainstream Circle powers? Baron would rather do not want his and his ancestors powers to be called "lesser magic" or "not proper". How Cimitière and Blanch work inside one group - they faiths are almost uncompatible...

    To end this post, we have Invictus - that is said to evolve naturally in local setting with groups connected to power. Where 2ED Lancea and Circle sounds as too strict and force to me to work on global scale, the Invictus was left to be decentralized and local colorful as much as possible. Why is that a matter of things, I ask?

      The Lancea Sanctum is Christian, not just Catholic. If a Protestant, Eastern Orthodox, or Oriental Orthodox church was the Christian church dominating the locale then those would be who the Lancea Sanctum are associated with. Islam and Judaism are not covered with the Lancea Sanctum, although I think you could actually just use a variant form of the Lancea Sanctum where those religions are dominant. In that case, you should lose the tie to Christians when using the Lancea Sanctum. That does not seem to be the intention. I think the intention was for Lancea Sanctum to remain Christian and for a place where Islam or Judaism is more prevalent the Invictus would have more religious overtones, without Theban though.

      In New Orleans, those two mini-covenants would be factions of the Circle of the Crone, or Baron Cimitiere (IIRC wasn't a Crone) would run a mini-covenant. They would be two Circle cults in the same location that could be loosely allied, strongly allied, or enemies. The Circle isn't big on rules. The decision is based on what the Baron might use as a Houngan to work his magic and personal choice. If he uses Cruac then his group is Circle too. If he uses something else or nothing (no real magic) then he might not associate with the Circle at all. Either way, the groups would be independent.

          I don't think of the Covenants as big global organizations, or even global doctrines. But more like schools of thought that get passed around - what works tends to be common in many Domains, but each will have its own variations. And although there might be lines of loyalty from individual to individual between Domains, there's no global authority.

          I think the best analogue is really political parties. Plenty of countries have a Green party, a Conservative party, a Social Democratic party, etc. Does that mean the Green Party in Canada and the Green Party in Germany share organization, have the same policies, and are beholden to some Global Green Council? Certainly not. But they probably have a lot in common, and if you're aligned with the Greens in Germany, when you move to Canada you'll probably look first at the local Greens...though some of their priorities might seem quite strange.

                  First of, the five Covenants are just sample. They are the most widespread Covenants, but far from the only ones out there. So, if a Covenant doesn't fit in your city, just scrap it out. If you need another group with other doctrines, just implement it.

                  The Lancea et Sanctum IS Roman Catholic at the core. It was formed by early Catholics, took a mold in the Medieval Catholic Church, and tries to keep ties to the Catholic community in the cities where it is present. That said, though, it isn't the same institution and lacks the centralization, so distinct communities of the LeS can have a lot of variations. So you CAN have a city where the LeS commune with other christian churches and take local customs to their rites, but that can be neighboring heresy if taken too far. Usually non-catholic vampires do find the LeS strange. You can even do non-christian LeS, but that is positively heresy. Of course, a heresy that covers an entire city can be completely impossible to get rid of, so the LeS would likely ignore them.

                  But that said, it is simple to take the LeS as a basis to create other religious Covenants, or just let the local vampires without one. You don't need a Covenant to be religious, and local Kindred can simply have common personal interpretations over their state in the view of the mortal religions they follow. Jew and Muslim Kindred, in special, I imagine simply following their religions mostly as they did before. A Jew is, still, a Jew, and maybe a Vampire Rabbi can open the synagogue for the night to the undead community. I don't even know if mosques close at night, and at least some musallas don't, so Muslim vampires have an easy time just being Muslims after death.

                  Circle of the Crone created Cruac as a mix of traditions, so it is theoretically a corpus of rites that you can adapt to any religious magic tradition that can follow the precept of blood magic and sacrifices. Curiously, that puts some mystic Jewish traditions under Cruac. That said, it is also an alliance of pagan religions against the Lancea et Sanctum's power. Distinct communities of the Circle can have entirely distinct religions, and if some can be very open and even have various religions in one group, others can even openly fight against each other if they don't need for now to work together to defend themselves against the Lancea. Again, outside a context where the CotC and the LeS are at each one's throat, you can get rid of the CotC as a Covenant if it pleases you.

                  Invictus isn't less centralized than CotC and LeS. Those aren't centralized either, they don't operate in global scale. None of the Covenants do. So the Invictus is globally kind of a club that spreads ideas about how to achieve and maintain power, but indeed work in practice in the local scale. But, again, that's what EVERY Covenant do. Their true global power is almost non-existent. And "lesser" Covenants abound, just as powerful where they are.

                      Looking at some of the merits in the Secrets of the Covenants open development blogs opens the door to speculation about a few of the Covenants. While I think it's rather ironic that the Invictus don't have a centralized hierarchical command structure, the existence of the InBoard and other ideas are very suggestive of how extensive they are. The Sanctified having a special capacity to harbor criminal refugees too, also speaks to how widespread they are. Those two are also some of the few Covenants we know of to have a continuous existence for almost 2000 years, so it's not really surprising if they can be found in a lot of places.

                      Like the Mage Orders (if not moreso) the key is to understand that while these are organization which can be found in all four corners of the globe, they are not centralized.

                      This does not mean that they don't talk or form alliances over distance, though. In fact, the whole concept of the 'cloister' in 2e's core neatly puts the idea to rest that they don't talk to one another. Rather, maintaining a powerbase over multiple domains can be very difficult, and while the Invictus in New York and Philadelphia might talk and send people between them, that probably doesn't translate into a single chain of command in both cities.

                          The impression I get is that the Covenants more-or-less went global as a result of European imperialism. Where Christian missionaries went, so too did the Lancea et Sanctum (which, properly speaking, predates the Catholic Church and probably has members who, in life, adhered to pretty much all forms of Christianity). Where the nations of Europe imposed their own power structures on foreign lands, so too did the Invictus expand their conspiracy. Where European science and mysticism captured the imagination of local vampires, there did the Ordo Dracul find new recruits. The Circle of the Crone and the Carthian movement emerged, to a large extent, in response to the persecution of the Lancea et Sanctum and the excesses of the Invictus, and they're pretty much found in the same places that the older covenants are.

                          That said, it's also true that the Covenants aren't monolithic entities. They're more like philosophies, ways of being undead, and as a result, similar bodies have arisen from time to time all over the world, though they would certainly use different names.

                          Anyway, like the European colonial powers, the Covenants met resistance in many places where they tried to impose their own vision of the Requiem. Even in a place like Athens, close to the heartland of the European covenants, local traditions often trump more widespread groups. While the Cthonic cults of Greece have mostly thrown in with the Circle of the Crone, the Kataraménon practices a weird blend of pagan Apollonian traditions with Greek Orthodox Christianity. Due to the influence of this old and powerful faith, the Lancea et Sanctum haven't been able to make inroads in the city, despite the fact that the Greek-speaking Byzantine empire was once one of the most vibrant centers of their faith.

                          Go to some place like Beijing, and you leave the familiar covenants almost entirely behind. The mystical schools known as the Way of the Dragon and the Dragon's Path resemble the Ordo Dracul in their goals, and have been influenced by Rites of the Dragon in modern times, but their methods are steeped in Chinese philosophy. Meanwhile, temporal power is held in the city by a bureaucracy of the Damned made up of ministries that can be as fractious as the Covenants in western cities. The Lancea et Sanctum is there, as might be expected, but are of little significance.

                                              Well, this is my personal take on it.

                                              Each of the seven covenants (including VII and Brood) have a covenant "power" - Oaths and Law, holy sorcery and blood magic, Coils and Investments, and VII's blessings. These seven abilities are universal throughout the world. Learning and adapting to each set of powers brings its own restrictions and mindset along with it.

                                              Each local iteration of those mindsets form the basis of a covenant. The Lancea et Sanctum is, at heart, primarily Catholic. There are Jewish, Islamic, and Egyptian covenants that all use Theban, plus likely some Hindu as well as others, throughout the world. The non-Lance have different histories and beliefs, but they all practice Theban Sorcery (possibly by another name), develop a focus on academia as a result, and (due to vampiric nature) use said academia to revision history, and so on.

                                              The Circle of the Crone is basically vampire-neopaganism, with a heavy dose of corrupt Wicca. Voodoo do not fit into the Circle of the Crone as it is. The two have different histories, cultures, beliefs. They share a common mindset, however, and practice of blood magic.

                                              In Tibet, there's a Buddhist-themed covenant run by the Naga Lord, and learn "treasures" that work exactly like Coils, but it is not the same scientific cult that worships Dracula.

                                              The same holds true for any vampire nobles or manipulative underdog rebels. Each has different cultures and histories from the Invictus and Carthians, but spiritually they act a lot a like. Even in Tokyo, where we have three vampire covenants, they act suspiciously like a local variation of the Invictus, the Carthians, and a shinto-inspired blood witch.

                                              For the fun of it, I state that these universal principles aren't just tropes or convergent evolution, but actual forces in the vampire world.

                                                      I particularly like the idea of those seven principles being just examples or convergent evolution in most of my games, actually, but the notion of them as actual forces in the vampire world isn't bad for some specific chronicles. Of course, the convergent evolution idea is mostly a means to an end: simplify things using the same rules again and again, so you don't have to reinvent the wheel each time you do some little variation of something (like they did to exhaustion in Masquerade).

                                                      Over Muslim countries I would expect variations of Invictus and Ordo Dracul composed only of faithful, but nothing like the Lancea et Sanctum or the Circle of the Crone based on Islam proper. Of course, those can still be present as foreign religions. Or maybe scholar Covenants similar to the Ordo but working with either form of ritualistic sorcery instead of Coils.

                                                      The Voodoo Circle, as any other of the various religions born of African roots mingled with Christianity, could still be found in the Circle for political reasons. The Circle IS already an ecumenical group by definition, formed to fight against the oppression of a Christian majority, and linked by a system of blood magic created to empower them against their common enemies. I can easily see the Circle proper, or something very similar, working wherever something akin to paganism exists as a minority group. It is formed and characterized by religion, but is ultimately a political group with view on self-defense.

                                                      Curiously, that means that some places could have another kind of change: a Circle of the Crone proper, but without Crúac, because it doesn't works well with the religions involved.
