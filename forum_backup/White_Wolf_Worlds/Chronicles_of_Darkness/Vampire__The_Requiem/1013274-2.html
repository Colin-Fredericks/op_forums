    Originally posted by schizoPOP View Post
    I dislike the notions of &quot;improved masquerade is requiem&quot; or &quot;masquerade sans embarassing bits isn't masquerade.&quot; I think there's plenty to make the game fun and distinct with more grandiose stakes and oddly specific character types. You can have illusionists with strange compulsions and curse-prone assassins be staple character types without being racist (which don't really map to the broader-in-concept requiem clans and covenants.) Most the powers and mechanics are perfectly capable of having more elegant and balanced permutations without ceasing to recognizable. (Except Generation, which is unfair in various specific, narrative-vital manners).

    Any sort of change is bound to make some people upset, especially given that there's plenty of dissent about various editions and character types already. It'd still be nice for there be an edition for me for when I'm feeling a bit more &quot;Vampires who throw fireballs&quot; without having to deal with rock-paper-scissors or 4 attack rolls.

    (For the record, I prefer requiem but I don't like one game being used to shut down discussion of the other.)
    Ok, you do make some really good points there. Plus I see what you're saying in regards to Requiem's Clans and Covenants lacking an instantly recognizable archetype or concept that's pimarily universal to all members. (I.E. The abundance of options can seem like a lack of options. For instance, say Ordo Dracul and any number of images will pop up in any given players mind but say Tremere and one universal idea pops up.)

    However, I would still have to disagree with you in regard to the oddly specific character types not being racist in VtM. Or, more accurately that up until at least Revised the character types in Masquerade suffered from Clan being equated to Race rather than Culture. An yes, a lot of this has been addressing in the more recent V20 supplements (and even DA:V20, which was such a pleasant suprise) like Lore of the Clans and Becket's Jyhad Diary. However, despite all the effort that's been made to make the Clans fully fleshed out cultures with regional differences the fact that once you're embrace you'll be a part of that Clan forever will always reinforce the idea of Race over Cutlure. Even if it's just on a subconscious level. This is why stereotyping the Clans is so easy in Masquerade, and why it's so dangerous since the stereotyping will also be equated with Race over Culture.

    To bring this full circle I think this is exactly why Requiem's Clans are based upon the vampire's condition so heavily over any given culture, while the Covenants are pure culture without any association to any given Clan. That way, despite what Clan you were Embraced into you'd be free to choose whatever culture or ideology that best fit your character. Like mentioned above this is also probably one of the reasons for the Vampire-Lite misconception, since Masquerade players used to the Clan's culture wouldn't find any at all.

        I'm arguing less a case that they "aren't" racist so much as they "don't have to be" racist while still being able to retain a core identity. OWOD does repeatedly suffer from straddling the line between inherent nature and cultural imperatives.The examples I (quite deliberately) chose, however, are racist because they draw an equivalence between real world stereotypes and real world ethnicities . Designing an oppressively dominate culture for a clan isn't actually being racist in a real world sense. (Though a case could be argued that kind of thinking is at least related to racist attitudes.) The Tremere for example rarely come up when discussing this issue despite having a very specific society entwined with it.

        I also chose to describe those examples in terms that don't actually tie a specific culture. The illusion+compulsion combination can conceivable be meaningfully applied to anyone from anywhere without relying on a clan history. "Assassin" might be a bit more culturally charged term, but stealth and murder are concepts found all over the world and a that's what a particular clan's innate abilities focus on. I think you can dial back on the cultural aspect without sacrificing these flavors. I'm not saying that's what has happened, or is going to happen, but that it can happen. (And even in this case, the concepts are too narrow to fit requiem's clan paradigm.)

            Because, it's funny you mentioned the Tremere as an example because unlike the obvious racial & cultural stereotypes Clan Tremere is by far the most subtle and sinister culprit of pushing a eugenic subtext even if unintentionally. And that, of course, is because Clan Tremere represents Academia in the Masquerade setting. They're a race..erh, I mean Clan of occultists and scholars who are known for their intellect, have rigid criteria for new members, indoctrinate potential new members for years about the benefits of completing their harsh ciriculum and the temporal rewards it'll bring alongside the intellectual, enslave new members into debt to their mentors and superiors, don't share esoteric secrets outside of the raClan...the Clan unless payed heavily for them, and upon reaching mastery after decades of hardships and peer pressure they subject the next members through the same bullshit with a smug sense of entitlement and superiority...oh, and they also can do literal magic and are the only raceeaaaClanClan qualified to do literal magic. (Yes, I know. It's not a good joke, but it's supposed to illustrate how sinister the Tremere as a concept is once it interacts with the patern recognition part of your brain.)

            And yes, a looooooooot has been done to remedy this over the years. The Tremere, like all of the Clans, have evolved into an organization of Kindred who share common powers and heritage rather than racial or social stereotype. However, the nature of Clan itself hasn't changed. And that's where the Eugenic subtext comes from. Because the Tremere would work just fine as is if they were just an organization, because that's how Academia is in real life. The fact that you're made into a Tremere, and once made cannot be anything else other than a Tremere, and then have cultural norms thrust upon you irrevocably ties Clan to race. And I feel that's such a waste because so much more could be done in it's place. And luckily, I hope anyways, a good amount of people realize this.

            Because like you said, you can dial back the cultural aspects without sacrificing the Flavors the Clans bring to the table. I completely agree with you in this regards, and feel the easiest solution is to take a leaf from Requiem and have the Masquerade Clans represent a person's physical condition rather than be equated with culture or race. That way, the Cursed Assassin is what she is not because of the society she was Embraced into, but rather because her curse makes her the ideal killing machine. And where or not she chooses to use those talents or not, she'll always have that potential because of the curse.

To add something meaningful to the Vampire Lite discussion, my biggest fear is that Masquerade players who escribe to the Vampire Lite belief for Requiem mistake the Eugenist undertones of the Clans for depth and substance, and see's their absense in Requiem as a lack of depth and substance.                                         Resplendent Fire, crap you're right! I completely forgot to elaborate on the Eugenic undertone. What I meant by that is that the Clans try to parody antiquated beliefs and concepts, like eugenics, to emphasize how removed from human society most elders of the Clans have become. However the inherent mechanics of the game itself accidentaly legitimize some of those belief by reinforcing them mechanically. This creates a subtext within the gameplay itself that subtly enforces such antiquated ideologies, like Eugenics. The biggest perpetrator of this is the Clan's insistence upon cultural history and customs to dictate Embraces, harkening back to the Eugenic belief of "Good Breeding" that was so popular with high society during the hight of imperialism. The Kindred would be discouraged from "bringing undesirables into the family" on pain of disownment or even Final Death if they did ever want to Embrace someone outside of their cultural or occupational circle. Thus the Clans remained "pure" and "strong" in the eyes of the elders, despite this concept being utter shit...except when we add Clan Disciplines into the mix.

                                        With the inclusion of the Clan Disciplines the subtext begins to undermine the original subversive purpose of the Elder's antiquated beliefs. Because, in Masquerade there are inherently more powerful Disciplines than others. And all of them are unique Clan Disciplines. To be more specific, I'll just use the most objectively powerful Discipline in the game as an example: Thaumaturgy. The Discipline of Thaunaturgy is, or was thanks to some damage control by Onyx Path, Clan Tremere's unique Discipline. And Clan Tremere's Embrace requisite, their "good stock" as it were, is for the candidate to be intelligent, comfortable in hierarchical organizations, have some knowledge or understanding of the occult, and be a throughow reasearcher with some experience with competitive academic environments (A.K.A. Academia.) Ergo, those Embraced into Clan Tremere are academics. Which means mechanically speaking, if you weren't an Academic then though luck. You'll never be accepted into Clan Tremere. Which meant tough luck, you'll never learn Thaumaturgy naturally. Which means tough luck, you'll always be inferior to a Tremere mechanically (And yes, with enough Celerity nothing else really matters. But this is about subtext. And the subtext is there.)

                                        This is the heart of the Eugenic subtext. It's why Clan Tremere is often either adored or reviled as the best or worst Clan. It makes it so that if you want to play a Tremere you have to play to their idea of "good breeding" if you want to get the most out of the Clan and benefit from the Clan Discipline to it's fullest. And people who don't want to play a "ideal candidate" get the shaft because the game makes Kindred Society so brutal when it comes to those who forsake tradition. And this is supposed to parody everything wrong with the Gentry, but instead it mechanically incentivizes romanticising the Peerage instead...*slowly looks directly into the camera with cold dead eyes*...which sucks because all they really need to do to fix this is either remove the Clan's Embrace prerequisites or make Thaumaturgy no longer a unique Discipline to the Tremere.

                                        Which the latter they did, because I think they realized too that unique Clan Disciplines set a dangerous subtext in a game about showcasing the worst of human societal norms and how they over time could lead to a world of pain, misery, despair, and darkness.

                                                    Without words to express how much I liked the thoughts constructed by both Dusksage and @schizoPOP.

                                                    Specially I found it true and clarifier the part on eugenics. Or, to be maybe better put, on how the game, by its mechanics and requisites, reinforced paradigms it was meant to criticize.

                                                    A hell of a lot of players I know do this. Specially on Vampire and Werewolf. They mindlessly replicate the very concepts the game tries so hard to criticize. The problem of the game isn't the racist stereotypes existing. It is the game working in such a way that the players find it fitting to replicate what they "should" be striving to surpass.

                                                    The game says that keeping Humanity is so hard, most vampires shrug it out or stop caring. It clearly wants the players to try hard, challenges them to keep Human. But the players mostly throw Humanity away and become monsters, because the game indeed makes high Humanity hard and unrewarding. Sometimes even uncool. The pattern repeats itself endlessly, with so much details.

                                                    I remember a Werewolf game where the pack was every detail the assholes to Kinfolk, they did everything in the book. Uncaring rapist slaver monsters that sucked everything they could and just expecting that the Kinfolk were grateful for the opportunity to serve and striving to serve ever more, answering any slight with extreme violence. And they got sincerely surprised when the Kinfolk turned to their enemies. What was really sick about it is that the players went on to be "Gaia's Sacred Warriors", and never actually perceived for a minute what they were doing. They just bought on the "it must be done" justification and thought themselves as pristine selfless heroes in the context of the game. The game meant to deliver a "this is wrong, it is prejudice" message, delivered instead the prejudice itself.

                                                    Not that Masquerade is a bad game, it isn't. Nor is Apocalypse. But there are problems. And Requiem got rid of a lot of them. They aren't the same, and as much as Requiem isn't Vampire-Lite, it isn't Vampire-but-Better, either. It's just Other-Vampire. But it did corrected a lot of mistakes, or rather, avoided committing them. The saddest thing is that a lot of those problems can be solved in the Masquerade proper, but never was. I hope, though it is an admittedly weak hope, that they are addressed in the 5th edition.

                                                    Just to showcase a little the point, let me take, instead of Thaumaturgy, Presence, and instead of Tremere, Toreador. You have a clan strongly linked to the arts, and you make a prejudicial view of said clan where it is considered vain, narcissistic and self-centered. Then you give said clan a Discipline that commands emotions, mostly to make people love you. And you finish by making it work mostly by using social rolls, but turning them to eleven in the most direct "love me" way. You finish by suggesting that Toreador dismiss or actively hate members who aren't on the arts. You make every tiny detail work to reinforce the very prejudice of the "cynical and short-sighted" society.

                                                    Requiem did what? Since 1st edition Thaumaturgy was split in 3 different kinds of powers, each linked not to a family, but to a secret society that accepts believers as members. It was both made a lot less breaking and more interesting, and the game even went as far as to talk about Kindred with command of those powers who either got it by stealing or leaved the group, and it was consequential instead of "daddy made a ritual and the Antitribu go boom". Or the less showy, but ever present clan blood hunt that was almost unavoidable in Masquerade, like how the Tremere hunted every Thaumaturgy user so thoroughly together with the traitorous teachers.

                                                    Majesty on 2nd I simply fall in love. The Daeva, though with the same powers of the Brujah, was very much like the Toreador. That alone made a world of difference in 1st edition. But then I saw B&S Majesty. A discipline that made the first dot from "cool people are kings" into "anyone can be a king". That first dot alone was clever, simple, and made a world of difference both in rules and in setting. A Daeva can look every bit the Masquerade's Nosferatu, and yet command social situations with ease. The other levels built in to command emotions in a way far more complex and versatile, the Discipline was more awesome in every way and less "vain", while still being as good for a character that wanted to be every bit the old Toreador.

                                                    Finishing things, I'm sorry if some of it isn't very clear, I'm writing too much with only one good hand tonight, and something had to be left without add or correction. Yes, I write too much.

                                                        In my book, the most important thing in any RPG is the choices that player's make. Whether its choosing to kill an NPC or to buy that 4th dot of obfuscate, player agency is what defines the themes of the game. Both vampire gamelines tend to play the fascism card as part of the "standard vampire court" design. (Requiem 2e and Beckett's Jyhad Diary are bit more explicit that other systems do exist, but it's still a sort of assumed baseline for vampire politics.) Both of these games also give the player the ability to "opt-out" of the fascism by being Carthians or Anarchs. Bigoted NPCs can be shunned or thwarted.

                                                        Removing those choices (or ignoring them from outset) is where things get uncomfortable for me. When an offensive concept shows up and the PCs are just expected to accept it and move on, the implication is that the aforementioned concept is somehow unimportant or "no big deal." Requiem avoids it by having Clans be broad and subject to interpretation, and leaving just about everything else subject to player decisions.
