    Blood and Smoke Combat Question

    07-13-2015, 07:48 PM
    I tried searching around, and did not find anything referencing this, so if I missed it, please point me to where it is. In B&S, defense goes up, but attack dice do not. Its possible for a starting character to have a 13 defense (not counting merits)! Even with this being reduced with each successive attacker, each round, it would take 5, STR 5 Brawl 5 assailants to get a single attack at greater than a chance die. This just seems broken to me. Or, to a more realistic scenario, a STR 2 Brawl 2 attacker has a chance die versus a Wits/Dex 2 Athletics 2 defender. Two equally matched combatants, 10% chance to hit.

    My conclusion: I must have missed something. What am I doing wrong?

          That's a white room, one in a million character design that's so fixated on having high defense that you might as well give it to them?

          Like, you're talking someone with Wits 5 and Dexterity 5, Athletics 5 and Celerity 3.

          That person has ridiculously low attributes in all other things, and if I were being myself with my players, I would ask them how this is a real person.

          If you're entertaining this character concept, than they have invested so much into dodging everything then I have to ask you, why bother taking that away?

          Besides, it's not as if they'll be in a great position to hit anything, either.

          You could also build a Strength 5, Brawl 5 Vigor 3 character who has a dice pool of +13 to hit, but it's the same scenario. 1. That's ridiculous. 2. if they really want to do that so bad why deprive them of it?

          and to deal with both of these things, you have the fact that they reduced their other stats to nil just to get there. It's certainly not gonna be any easier to resist getting mind controlled, or shot (FYI only that +3 defense from Celerity will apply if the opponent has a gun) or any other numerous things that could happen to a character.

          So yeah, if you're not building an actual human being and instead going for a punch machine/dodge machine, this could be a problem, but it's one quickly negated by numerous other solutions.

          As for the situation where the two are evenly matched: Presumably, they'll use Willpower and get the leg up on their opponent. Maybe they'll also use an All-Out Attack. If they have a weapon, they might be able to hit you hard enough to Tilt you with even a basic attack (especially if you're a mortal) but they might also arm wrack you, forcing you to drop your weapon and disadvantage you.

          Or they miss, and their All-out Attack has sacrificed their defense, and you take the opportunity to tilt them yourself.

          Or if you'vre the attacker with Brawl, maybe you try to intitiate a Grapple so that suddenly, that Defense score is a whole lot less relevant.

          Don't forget you've probably got merits on top of all that to further tip things in one direction or another.

          AND if you're a Vampire, you could also spend a Vitae for Physical Intensity.

          So let's take this situation of the STR 2 Brawl 2 guy versus the Wits/Dex Ath 2 defender. They're both BP 1. STR 2 Brawl 2 guy spends a Willpower, makes an All-out Attack and spends a Vitae. Suddenly he's got a whopping +7 extra dice to hurt you with. Nice! If he hits you, he'll probably grapple you, and then he could spend the Willpower and Vitae again for +5 on the contested strength roll, giving him a major advantage to... I dunno, bite you with his fangs and do Lethal. Scary!

          Or if he misses, he's defenseless, and if you're also STR 2 with Brawl 2, you could then make that same move he just did, except he doesn't benefit from defense. So you've got a dicepool of +11 in that situation. Maybe you take a penalty to inflict a Tilt, so that even when his Defense is back, it's still hobbled. Congratulations, you are now advantaged.

          This completely fails to take into account additional factors like environment, allies and other things that can add up, but trust me, combat is fine in practice. It can be pretty fun once you realize there's more to it than just making regular attacks until they die. Fight smart. This ALSO doesn't take into account situations where Beaten Down and Surrender might be in effect. In the above situation, if you get off that Willpower + All-out Attack, you might score enough damage to inflict Beaten Down on someone, which might lead to them just surrendering.

                  It's working as intended. If you dump all of your character creation resources into being hard to hit, you should be very hard to hit. You're also not accounting for Vigor, Willpower, Physical Intensity, and bonus dice from Frenzy. If this Dex 5, Wits 5, Athletics 5, Celerity 3 character goes up against a Strength 5, Brawl 5, Vigor 3, BP 3 character (also possible at character creation), the attacker can frenzy, spend Willpower, and spend 2 Vitae for physical intensity and 1 for increased damage from Vigor, they'd have a total dice pool of 10 after subtracting Defense, and deal +3 damage on a hit. If they use Weaponry instead of Brawl, they'd have a very easy time getting the 5 damage required to stake the opponent. It's also worth noting that both of these characters are one-trick ponies, and this white room analysis doesn't account for a great deal of the realities of game play.

                      You've got that exactly right. WoD 2e did a great deal of beefing up of both attack and defense options, and its totally possible to build a character with colossal defense or exceptional armor. Of course, the reverse is also true and one can create a character with exceptional damage and accuracy. Or all of the above.

                      Combat in WoD 2e is not the standard exchange of dice until one person runs out of hit points. Cunning, circumstance and (let's face it) pure luck play a big part. The system doesn't shy away from letting characters be good at things. You can build a starting character who will buzzsaw through people like butter and the system will support you. And that might be a broken if WoD was a tactical combat game like Dungeons and Dragons. But it is not. There are ample ways for a character with high defense to get screwed over, mind effects, surprise attacks, superior armor, hell one lucky it with a sledge hammer would be enough to drop (or nearly drop) a mortal.

                                          Attacks are less likely to inflict damage in 2e, but more likely to inflict higher amounts of damage when they do inflict damage. The Beaten Down rule is another consideration; any amount of lethal damage is expected to put the hurt on somebody who's fighting for less than extreme odds, and nearly all weapons now do lethal damage (though, of course, not so much against Kindred).

                                          Originally posted by tunglashr View Post
                                          So two evenly matched opponents should have a 10% chance to hit each other unless they spend willpower? That is as intended?
                                          Well, if you weren't spending Willpower in combat, honestly, yeah, that part was kind of unintended. Willpower, especially in 2e, is meant to be spent liberally, recovered and spent again. There's a sidebar emphasizing that in the initial 1e Rules Update; I'm not sure if it made it into the Vampire 2e core. You spend Willpower when you need to depend on something to succeed. If a serious combat situation with some kind of 13 Defense battle ninja isn't a situation where you would strain and push yourself every turn to the greatest extent you can, well, you're either entirely too laid back for your own good, or you're probably that crazy dude who can beat a 13 Defense by 10 dice.
