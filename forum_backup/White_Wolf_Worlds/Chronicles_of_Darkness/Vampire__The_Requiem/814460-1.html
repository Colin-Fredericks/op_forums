                                    This is why binary "yes/no" social mechanics suck when aimed at PCs! It's also why the important and fun bits of modern social mechanics center around this dilemma, often breaking the binary, working with incentives, etc. to make it interesting. The CofD version of offering Beats if you give in is a good attempt at making bad choices palatable, and coming up with an alternative and taking a condition is an okay mechanic (if a bit clunky and not really exciting) that's pretty much the bare minimum for making a "you refuse, but..." option. I don't really have a problem with these parts of the social maneuvering system when it comes to PCs - like I said, it's pretty much when it comes down to the longer term Impressions and Doors part that makes it awkward and obtuse for me, to the point that I'm just not going to use it! I'll either stick to roleplaying with one-off rolls giving a metric for how strongly an argument resonates with you, and probably offer Beats to incentivize successful rolls.

                                            Shifting gears just a little with some advice. Awe used against PCs is a delicate procedure for the Storyteller. As I see it there are two approaches, heavy and light:
                                            • Heavy: This involves rolling dice and informing the player that they should be convinced, or that the vampire they are speaking to must be received positively.
                                            • Light: This approach is wholly narrative and requires the storyteller to constantly describe the NPCs actions in a positive light, lay hints little narrative hints that this NPC is cool/likable/a valuable person to know.
                                            Between the two heavy is a lot easier, but light is far more compelling. Albeit they both depend on how much metagaming your table indulges in.
