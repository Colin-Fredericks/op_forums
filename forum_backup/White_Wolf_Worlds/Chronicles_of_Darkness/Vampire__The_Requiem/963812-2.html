        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
        Mehh, I'm a bit hesitant to say vampires are part of the Lovecraft hierarchy, since they all pretty much agree that discovery of their existence would lead to their extinction. The horror of Lovecraft isn't that there are alien monsters out there, it's that there are invincible alien monsters out there. Humanity is completely helpless to defend itself against such creatures, and we should thank whatever gods exist that they deign to give us the bliss of ignorance.

        But when it comes to the Chronicles of Darkness, it seems like humanity is the one holding the cards. If they found out the supernatural existed, they'd go to war, a war that most splats seem to agree they would be victorious.


        If you want a more Lovecraft feel, I'd say that Mummies and Geists are more appropriate. Mummies because they're can't be permanently defeated and have cults of mortals dedicated to them. And Geists because they've seen what lays beyond death and decided a second chance at life was worth cohabiting a body.
        Michael already pointed out that's not quite what I said (also, thumbs up gender neutral pronoun 'they' club ). But just to add to the other points you raise, I think mummies actually fit less well for a Lovecraftian game. I actually don't know the Mummy line and maybe you could play up the arbitrary / meaninglessness nature of existence and produce a somewhat bleak and uncaring universe feel, but I think that the immortality and the fact that mummies have a purpose goes against making players feel the true horror of a lovecraftian universe. Not necessarily of course - you can make most things work. For example, you could focus on the way all of human civilizations and achievements fall with time. A kind of Ozymandias / Gaze on my works view. Again, I haven't read Mummy so maybe that actually is the focus. But for what I want - fear of the unknown, fragility of existence - immortality and the benefit of a long and mystical view on existence would probably detract for it. It kind of depends whether you're playing up the uncaringness of the universe aspect or the fear / horror aspect of Lovecraft. For me, it's the latter that is most interesting.

        I also think in a Lovecraftian universe, hammering home the Masquerade is not quite as critical. Important, don't mistake me, but the reason it's needed in a vanilla V:tR game is because there needs to be something out there that keeps vampires in check, something to alarm them and restrain them. Elders are the other popular one for this role. But if you're playing a Lovecraftian vampire game, you already have a strong contender for that slot.

        Originally posted by Dwight View Post
        Just jumping in but I find the idea of a circle worshiping a Dark Young and a group of Ordo trying to stop them from summoning more to be an awesome plot idea.
        Heh. That's a nice idea. Chess-playing elder vampires and princes with Majesty up the wazoo become cliché with time. But encountering an unhinged group of vampires devoted to worshipping some blood-dripping tree monster, now THAT is creepy.
