    Kindred Population Numbers?

    10-23-2015, 07:38 PM
    So, I was looking over setting information in the book, and kindred populations seem... utterly mind bogglingly small. like, Tokyo apparently only has about three hundred vampires... and this is noted as especially high... in an overall city area with over 45 million people. So, I guess I should ask, what human/vampire number ratio do you think is appropriate for a sustainable vampire population without blowing things wide open?

              If it matters, the first edition suggested an average ratio of 1 vampire for every 50,000 people in large cities, and 1 vampire for every 100,000 being 'not unusual,' elsewhere, with a caveat that notoriously populous cities such as New York and, perhaps, Tokyo might have even more.                               Tokyo is interesting because the author said he actually originally wrote it as 1000 kindred.

                              I tend to think that there isn't really a corelation between mortal population and kindred population. What matters is the limited social space of kindred society. A city generally only has one hierarchy (plus a few covenants ones). That means there's a limited amount of places for a vampire. Ultimately the only place that can really expand a lot is the very bottom and that creates it's own huge problems leading to a general pressure to limit the population which afterall is a key part of the Prince's role.

                              I'm not saying you have to follow that logic, but it's a reasonable possibility.

                                              It probably depends on the nature of the city, as well. Cities with a reputation for danger or with inadequate or especially corrupt police protection might be able to support more vampires, as might cities in parts of the world where things like smartphones are less common. I tend to imagine that particularly dense populations of kindred might exist in the massive slums found in cities like Mumbai and Nairobi. Such places might also have a disproportionate number of very low humanity vampires, though, due to the nightmarish conditions in which they surround themselves.

                                              It's also been suggested that certain cities seem to attract vampires for whatever reason, usually ones with a popular reputation as centers of nightlife and sinful behavior in general. New Orleans is one of these, and New York is mentioned as well, I think, but I imagine that places like Las Vegas and Los Angeles would qualify as well. Heck, a whole coterie of vampires could probably spend their entire requiem in one of Vegas' massive casino-hotels, without ever having to see the sky if they didn't want to.
