    Originally posted by Tessie View Post

    I believe that's the reason animals started social groups in the first place; cooperation to ensure a higher rate of survival.
    That's not to say we humans don't care about each other unless it's for our personal benefit. We also have a psychological need to form societies. And considering that Lancea et Sanctum and Circle of the Crone are two of the five major covenants I'd say vampires, to a certain degree, also feel the need to belong.

    Besides, solitary describes behaviour rather than intent. It's certainly not a healthy society, but it's still there and it's upheld by vampires feeling a need (regardless of why that need is) to cooperate.

    Well, using human mentality doesn't really work for vampires, and using religious groups is the worst possible example. The Lancea Sanctum isn't so much a social group as it is a religious cult. I believe both editions make it clear that these are not your modern day, play nice Christians, either. They're the medieval, put-you-on-a-breaking-wheel-if-you-don't-convert style Christians. As for the Circle of the Crone, they're scattered and disorganized unless they're faced with a significant threat that encourages them to pool their resources together. First edition even states that mass genocide against pagan faiths is what lead to the founding of the covenant to begin with, they needed something to fight back against the Lancea; but that doesn't mean they necessarily wanted it.


    The Invictus flat out reaffirms my claim as to them being a bunch of vicious sharks who will eat their wounded first chance they get. The Carthians are primarily formed out of young vampires who haven't fully realized the gravity of their situation and the Ordo are off doing whatever it is they do in the name of science.

        I kinda see vampires as akin to lions in this matter. Cats are generally solitary by nature, but lions form prides because it is a more efficient way to hunt and to breed. However, they're still not great at living in social groups. There is a lot more infighting than you see with more social animals like wolves.

        Most "solitary" animals will form social groups out of necessity when food or other necessary resources are scarce, and sometimes this behavior is successful enough that it persists over multiple generations, making more social individuals more likely to pass on their genes. This may be why lions are some of the only pack-forming cats.

                                        05-04-2017, 06:31 AM
                                        I remembered started a thread a while back where asked a question about Requiem where I forgot to specify that I wanted info on 1E. I let slip that I was wary of 2E because of the "God Machine". I said that my problem was that it effectively made the rest of the WoD a sci-fi (or whatever people wish to call it) where in every setting we now had to accept that just behind the curtain there was a bunch of nuts and bolts that were controlling everything. It was kind of like when you watch a movie with a twist you think is lame, you really enjoyed the rest of the movie,but now you have to judge the whole flick based on the twist, which ruins the whole movie. I was also looking forward to Demon: the Descent, which I hoped would effectively expand on the Inferno demons,and correct the problems with the Fallen, instead we got something which I found... really weird. It would have been fine as something of an AI splat ("Robot: The Activated"? "Android: The Programmed"?) but as demons it was just too weird, even for WoD at its most creative. In both oWoD and CoD the splats are still fundamentally the same as their mythological/folklore inspirations, vampires are still vampires, werewolves are still werewolves and so on, but the same cannot be said of demons in DTF.

                                        I've learned to separate what I like and what I don't like, and DTF and the God machine are not in my personal "canon", which has allowed me to enjoy CoD the way I used to, even 2E content. My hope is that they don't make the God Machine so omnipresent throughout the CoD that it becomes too difficult to ignore it.

                                        That said there are still reasons to play 1E: you may want to make use of a lot of the wealth of content built up throughout 1E and most of the stuff exclusive to 2E can be ignored or replaced, easier than adapting a whole bunch of 1E stuff to 2E. Basically, depending on how much content from 1E you want to make use of it might just be easier to go full 1E. Then there's also the question of source-books like Inferno, one of my personal favorites, which from what I understand is 100% incompatible with 2E rules.

                                            The good thing about the God-Machine is that it's completely optional, just like all the other gamelines. Only MtA would lose out since it's all about Mysteries and the other gamelines are good sources for them.
