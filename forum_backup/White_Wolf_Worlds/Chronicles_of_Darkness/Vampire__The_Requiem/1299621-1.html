        In my opinion, 2e is a vast improvement over 1e.

        A couple of the (in my opinion) most important improvements:

        1. The theme of 1e was that it sucks to be a vampire, and you just have to deal with it. The theme of 2e is that being a vampire is not fun, but it isn't without its perks either. You're a monster now, but you're a bad ass monster. You can actually kick some serious ass straight out of the gate, even as a fledgling turned yesterday. In 2e, you get abilities like Lashing Out with your predatory aura, buffing your physical capabilities with Physical Intensity and you're immediately an amazing blood hound with your new and improved Kindred Senses. You also downgrade all mundane damage to bashing damage now, making you a lot more durable than you were as a human, even in a knife fight. Finally, your first dot Discipline abilities are a lot stronger in 2e and they're all free to use, they don't cost you any Vitae, so you can immediately bully the humans around you with your new Discipline powers, without having to worry that you're going to grow hungry.
        So yeah, being a new vampire is still not great, but it doesn't suck outright, and you're actually a scary monster straight out of the gate, so that's a huge improvement over 1e.

        2. The Humanity system is a lot more elegant in 2e. It isn't a glorified morality system anymore, but it now actually does what it's supposed to do: tracking your humanity (I know, wild, right?). Committing mass murder is still a Breaking Point, but not because murder is bad and you should feel bad, but because murder feeds the inner Beast, makes it grow stronger, makes it crawl to the surface. Monstrous acts are Breaking Points, but circumstances that remind you of how much of a monster you really are, are Breaking Points as well in 2e. Spending a few hours in the sun (yes, that's possible in 2e if you're young and have high Humanity) is a Breaking Point. Surviving something that should have killed you if you were human, is a Breaking Point. Going without human contact for more than a week, is a Breaking Point. These are all things that remind you that you aren't human anymore, and thus they are Humanity Breaking Points.

        3. Touchstones are the best new addition in 2e. I love the whole concept of it. Touchstones are things that keeps your vampire character connected to his human side, to keep the Beast at bay. This Touchstone often comes in the form of a human character your vampire cares about. It forces players to anchor their vampire characters in human society (or suffer the consequences of not doing so), it forces the vampire characters to interact with humanity, which in turn leads to good dramatic scenes and paints a clear picture of how being a vampire sets you apart from the rest of humanity. It keeps the game grounded and it reminds the players of how monstrous their characters really are. Imagine your vampire accidentally murdered his meal last night during a hunger frenzy, and then when he visits his Touchstone, he has to listen to her crying about receiving the news that some sick psychopath assaulted and murdered her boyfriend last night. It puts things in perspective.

        4. Conditions. This has replaced the Derangements from 1e. It's much better. Instead of being (semi)permanent, they are temporary nerfs and boons, often coming in the form of a dice penalty or bonus, which affects your character in certain fields, and can be resolved in certain ways, granting your character a Beat (the new way of tracking character progression in 2e). It's more flexible, it feels more organic, it might seem a little game-y at first and perhaps it is, but it's game-y in a good way. The Condition encourages good dramatic roleplaying from the players, by giving the players penalties and buffs, and rewarding them with Beats if the player roleplays the Condition in an interesting way.
        The new vampire Disciplines are also tied into this Condition system, as others before me mentioned. Using Dominate for example, applies the Mesmerized Condition to the target, which means the target is subject to your will, and can be commanded for the duration of the scene. However, the Mesmerized Condition is resolved when the target experiences a Breaking Point, or if the target suffers damage, which means your grip over her slips. This means you have to be tactical about how you use your Dominate. Yes, Dominate is more powerful in 2e, but the target also has more ways to break free if you aren't careful. It all feels so much more organic, dynamic and interesting than 1e did without the Conditions.
