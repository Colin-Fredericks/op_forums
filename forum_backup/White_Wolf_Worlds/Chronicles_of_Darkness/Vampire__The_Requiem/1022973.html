    The Beast as a Player Character

    12-27-2016, 05:56 PM
    Running of a suggestion in the 2e corebook I'm going to be implementing that each player will be responsible for playing another PC's Beast.
    I think it sounds extremely interesting and like something that can both increase the drama as well as a method for each character to gain the spotlight - even without the ST passing the baton, so to speak.

    I'm curious as to whether or not any of you have any experience with this method, and what those experiences may be?

    What techniques did you use?
    What guidelines did you give the players beforehand?
    How did your players feel about this style of play?
    How did this addition affect the game?

    I have a bunch of questions, so it's probably best if I hold back and keep it to the questions above and whatever else you may feel is relevant to share

    I'm currently considering a couple of things, including the Beast as a narrative player devise or an approach with a seperate character sheet for the Beast (adding dots to certain abilities, attributes etc. while the Beast has control) and certain clan-specific abilities that help underline why each clan is perceived to be they way they are or be able to do some of the things they are rumored to do.
    But I'd like to hear what you guys have to say before I put too much (or too little) effort into this trail of thought

          Be careful about giving control of one player's character to another player. If your group is fine with someone else role playing their character when that person is temporarily absent it may be okay, but taking away player agency is a touchy issue between ST and player. So I expect it'd be exacerbated with players doing it.

          I got to watch a Wraith chronicle fall apart a long time ago in a similar scenario and can tell a few horror stories about what happened when players got control of other players' characters. I suppose it depends on the group but I personally would be unlikely to play in a game where other people arbitrarily get to seize control my character's actions and development without my input because the GM has a taste for out of game melodrama.

                          I've always had the Beast as a more palpable thing, but never this much. Like a dark voice inside the player can use to embellish their descriptions or I can to get across how a vampire might feel about a certain situation.

                              Originally posted by MonkeyMann View Post


                              I don't see this as "Out of game melodrama" at all. In fact I see it as a firmly in game thing. The Beast isn't something you control. And it's something that's ever present. Having another player control it during a frenzy is not really different than having the ST control it in that situation.
                              Two things about this though
                              1. It's been my experience that when a character succumbs to frenzy or the character becomes mind controlled, the ST (or other PC) still doesn't take control of the character. (I've only seen it on three specific occasions and that was at the player's express request.) That character's player retains control, it's a matter of the player keeping in mind that another sentience is the one declaring the actions in broad strokes and they are the one providing the details. If the player still acts in a manner deemed "out of character" for the circumstances, that is when the ST intervenes.
                              2. It's different because the ST is more likely to simply have the character act simply to keep the game going whereas another player is likely to use the opportunity to sabotage the temporarily controlled character out of spite for another incident, use the circumstances to directly advance their own character or agendas or do the same thing you'd typically take control from the player for doing, and that's behave in an uncharacteristic manner to avoid too much potential collateral damage to the group or individual.

                              I'm not saying don't do it, I'm advising to be careful about how it's handled. I would suggest some sort of ground rules on what sort of actions are permitted or forbidden by players who don't own the character and don't have to take responsibility for their choices.

                                  Not that you shouldn't go ahead with this idea, but it's something to keep in mind when GMing things: if you see anybody acting in a way that's just being an asshole as opposed to being in-character, don't be afraid to pull them into line very, very quickly, telling them your expectations. Your minimum expectation should be that the players should all respect one another and their 'property' enough not to ruin another player's fun. Basically, the players should take their duty as a Beast with the same seriousness and care you'd expect from a GM judging a scene.

                                          *Some* groups could do it well. It'd require a sit down talk first. Set the expectation of the player controlling the Beast making the PC's life hell as much as possible and make sure that there will be no ooc Feels.

                                          Also shut down using beast time to forward your character's goals.

                                          After the first time it happens talk to your players again about it to make sure its still okay.

                                          I would also sidebar anyone who it happens to privately after the game and I would reinforce both good beast plays *and* accepting those plays with praise.

                                              One thing i'm experiencing with in my current game, is giving each player a 'dark aspiration' that represent what each characters beast wants. I tell the players that accomplishing these aspirations will trigger an degeneration roll but earn them a beat as per the regular aspiration rule. Whether they go for it or not i leave it entirely in the players choice.
                                              What that does for me is that the beast is still there even beyond frenzy rolls. That there is always these small thoughts nagging at the back of the characters head. It makes the beast a subtle thing but still there.
