                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                        I have only seen assertions, without supporting arguments. If it removes all depth, how does it do so?
                        I have made supporting arguments. I made supporting arguments in the post you just responded to.

                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                        Vampire is already about self-deluded monsters masquerading as people. The only difference this change makes is that the monsters didn't used to be human; a fact which we are informed of, but have no way of actually verifying, because they have a human's memories, thoughts, emotions, personality, everything.
                        Vampire is about people who were once human struggling to retain their humanity. When you remove the "once human" part, it completely changes the context. Vampires are not deluded about having been human, at least in the default setting. Vampires per the OP's proposal are deluded about having been human.

                        Also, I think the "no way of actually verifying" thing is overplayed, because people who object to this idea are objecting from the position of players and storytellers, not from the position of in-game characters. If I know, as a player, that my character was never really human and is just a parasite who's stolen human form, memories, personality, then there's nothing there for me to play because it ain't Vampire. I would not play in such a game, and I would quit and leave if this were introduced as a truth of the setting after the game started. I don't want to play a Vampire game like that because there's no personal horror in such a revelation.

                        I don't agree because I haven't yet seen the logic that leads from the assumption, "the character isn't really human" and the conclusion, "therefore there is no depth to playing the character."
                        I would disagree that you haven't seen the logic. You have seen it, even in the post you responded to here. You may not agree with it, but that does not make it absent.

                        But I'll say it one more time: If the character was never human, the struggles aren't real. It's a sham, a con, at best a bait and switch. You're going to play Vampire! No, wait, you're actually going to play a game where vampires were never human, and have no true connection to humanity, making a mockery of the emphasis on humanity, degeneration, and touchstones. Your character isn't who they appear to be and never was. Your character is completely delusional about who and what they really are. It's great if you want a game where the characters are pod people, but I don't think Vampire should be that game.

                        And I mean games where you play pod people have their place, and have their own horror, hence SOMA. I mean SOMA practically tells you what's going on early on, so it's not even a surprise.

                        I think the questions as to whether we're the same people after we sleep and wake up are irrelevant. I mean I guess it's useful for someone to debate that idea but I think it's pretty much a non-starter. That comic strip with the alien going on about humans only living for 16 hours at a time just struck me as being about an arrogant alien fucking with a human.
