            OK- a couple of things. First, we are aware of how long the new VtR2 books have taken. Onyx Path works on a system of picking a developer for a game line and then letting that developer work with the writing team they prefer on each individual book. In the case of VtR2, that developer has been Rose Bailey, and because of her experience with bringing forward 2nd Editions, she has been assisting with other CofD books, including combing through the core to eliminate all the references to nWoD, as well as developing the Vtr2 books she has slated. Her Development Manager position made her part of the Onyx Path inner circle, and she was just getting the hang of that when an accident caused a debilitating shoulder injury that dropped her output to something like 10% of what she was used to accomplishing. So for this past year you can see more progress as she has been healing and getting back to her usual productivity.

            Including VtR2 in Dark Eras and its associated books was part of providing all of you with _something_ while she got back to speed. Understood that it's not a whole book, but it was what was possible.

            The thing is, Rose could not have created VtR2 without loving it, a lot, and so she has tried to keep pushing forward with the line. But even with that love for Requiem, she has identified that this current delay has been way too long, and is actively working with other folks to step in and help with the development in order to get more VtR2 books out faster in the future.

            Second, I'm really, really, glad to hear from folks that they want more of any given game line. That is just what a creator wants to hear! Please just bear in mind that your enjoyment of any given line, and the enjoyment you hear from folks in social media, does not change the various factors that go into getting projects approved and out the door. You love VtR2 and might be thrilled for a book a month, Bob, though, loves Mummy and wants all the Mummy we can make as fast as we can fire out the door. And Sally just wants Werewolf and nothing else! If those lines have healthy sales and we still have great ideas for them, we try and get them on our list to publish, but that list can't have infinite projects on it. Only the amount of projects that make sense for us in any given stretch of time.

            Which is why we have done books like Hurt Locker, or Dark Eras, or the upcoming Crossover Chronicle, so that even if we can't get out a book for a line you are into this year, we provide you with something for it that we hope will prove interesting and useful.

            Finally, there is some validity in the idea that a line or individual book that sells really well will increase the chance that we'll do more like it. So liking something, buying it, getting your friends to buy it, etc., all will be noticed when I look at sales numbers as one of the determining factors for what gets on the publishing list.

            The flip side, withholding your purchases, is more problematic. There are many reasons for lack of sales, and we have no way of knowing which of them are in play when we see a particular project lag. You are sending a message we do not have the equipment to receive and understand. For example, if Promethean 2nd has bad sales because a whole bunch of folks decide not to purchase it, even though they would like to, in order to send a message to us about publishing more VtR2 books, we are not going to understand your intent and instead will stop making Promethean 2 books because the sales indicate we will lose money trying to advance the game line.

            That being said, please don't buy Onyx Path projects unless you want to. Because you like the line, the individual book and what is in it, the art, the writers, Onyx Path itself; these are all great reasons to buy. Similarly, don't buy them if you don't want to, or our policies and practices, or our publishing methods, bother you. We aren't owed your money, and should expect that you will go elsewhere if what we do doesn't work for you.

            Thanks!

                Unpopular opinion: I prefer the long gaps between releases over the constant supplement treadmill of the old White Wolf days. The books we do get are far better quality, and there's not nearly so much z-splat bloat, which to be honest was pretty taxing to me during 1e.
                Plenty of the 1E books were essentially stuff that could be made up at the game table by a reasonably competent GM, leading to z-splat bloat "Les Canards are a Parisian bloodline who have perfected the duck bonding discipline known as Mallardous".

                Mechanically VtR2E is a very complete game, the themes are laid out clearly, there's several premade settings, alternate rules, e.t.c., so a justified supplement really has to explore some part of the world in depth in terms of lore (e.g. Secrets of the Convenets) or deal with playing, not just stats for, a character type not fully explored in the core (e.g. A Thousand Years of Night). This takes a good bit more time, but they're likely to be far better books.

                I'd love them a bit quicker of course, but CoD is a big line with many games!

                                                            I'm sure you know better but it just seems odd.
                                                            For an interesting look behind that curtain at this, Caleb Stokes detailed the process of having to decide between doing Print on Demand or an offset physical print run for his own RPG on the Game Designer's Workshop series of the Roleplaying Public Radio podcast. The cost of bulk printing hardcover, full-color books of decent length and then shipping copies of those to dozens of hobby shops is enormous, and with an industry as niche as tabletop roleplaying where those books aren't guaranteed to sell... well, there's a reason the only big names left in the industry primarily fund themselves with collectible card and deluxe board games. This industry as a whole is barely profitable at the best of times, and the choice isn't really "physical books in stores or print-on-demand," it's "print-on-demand or we go out of business."

                                                            OPP has something like four permanent employees. It just isn't feasibly for them or anyone else smaller than Wizards of the Coast to go for the old distribution model, and that's okay; things have changed in the consumer's favor with the shift to digital and PoD.
