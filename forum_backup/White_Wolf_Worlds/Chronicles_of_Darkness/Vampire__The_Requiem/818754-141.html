    1.) The cumulative penalty mentioned is per drink. Same Elder drink three Vitae three times from the same person will roll 3 dice, 2 dice and 1 die. The fourth drink would be a chance die.

    2.) The effects of Addicted (Persistent) aren't that bad, nor as persistent as you'd think. And addictions does not have to be as bad as those you've experienced. People whose lives are turned upside down because of drugs are people who had other problems (often mental health problems, or leading to mental health problems) before they started with drugs. These are not represented by the Addicted Condition. Rather, think about functional alcoholics instead. People who definitely have a problem, but a problem that's manageable for the time being, and might even stop drinking on their own without support groups and such.

                                    Actually, I was just referring to people with addictions that are still capable of managing their lives (rather than sober addicts that you're describing). Their addiction is certainly making it harder to do and it will have consequences (especially on relationships) but an alcoholic that manages to keep both their occupation (even if not as productive) and their family (though with declining relations) for several years can only be referred to as a functional addict (and are classified as such by professionals). They also have a much bigger shot at kicking the habit (or resolving the Condition in game terms) without outside intervention while they still have a stable life.
                                    The only myth about functional addicts (or rather something they tend to tell themselves) is that they can manage their addiction so it doesn't eventually become worse. Only complete sobriety can fix that, and only for as long as they keep sober.

                                    As for resolving Addicted, that should be fairly trivial (if somewhat time consuming) for player characters.
                                    High Humanity PCs have an easy time triggering Breaking Points and will sooner or later fall (or buy up their Humanity if they fell below their usual level before getting the Condition) while low Humanity PCs will encounter less Breaking Points but much worse in terms of dice pools and therefore fall easier once they have to roll. They also have a bigger incentive raising Humanity due to the possibility of draugrhood.
                                    Human PCs on the other hand gets Breaking Points from interacting with the supernatural, and in a game about supernatural horror (in this case vampires) it means they should encounter at least one Breaking Point per session on average. Some of these will be easy (meaning easier time getting an Exceptional) while others will get the full -5 penalty (and therefore easily fall unless they have monstrous Res.+Comp. and exceptionally high Integrity).
