            I really think it's just a matter of "any damage that's enough to decapitate a vampire is enough damage to kill them normally, too."

            If decapitation does 20L worth of damage, then no vampire is going to survive it. If a vampire does survive it, then it wouldn't decapitate.

            Correlation vs causation and all that. The damage kills them and decapitates. The decapitation doesn't kill them.

            Of course, Resilience could easily have a vampire survive a shotgun blast to the face that removes their head. That's what it does. Lets you survive while still showing the effects of the damage you negated.

            Of course, I'd probably also put them in Torpor for it. They lose the functionality of completely removed limbs, after all, even with Resilience.

                Decapitation is THE method for Sparkle Vamps to die, just for the record. Yeah, I saw the movies. Yes, they're horrible. The second-to-last is outright offensive. But I digress.

                People writ about vampires for a looooooong time. Some legends about blood-sucking monsters or undeads are as old as writting itself. Those legends are much more varied than current steriotip vampires. In the Middle Ages, some people tought the only way to kill a vampire would be to exumate him, stake his heart, cut-off his head and fill his mouth with blessed bread and water. Them set the entire thing on fire. Failure in any step would be a sure way for the creature to come back.

                You are forcing a rule where none is needed. No, there are NO necessary logic on that. The only rules we need are the ones in the book that make the game work. Anything else can be flavoured by the ST.

                I'm NOT saying you shouldn't declare a decapitated vampire dead. I'm saying you should be open to possibilities. A given chronicle can benefit of strangely tough vampires, able to survive almost anything. This plays well to the trope of the Undead Unnatural Monster. The thing can live without any given piece of the body. It can't even be sure of what it realy needs for survival. Imagine the personal horror of an extreme identity crisis: how can you be sure of who you are when you find nothing in you is necessary for the Beast to go on? A non-Vampire PC can have the hell scared off him just by witnessing the creature shrugging off all kinds of descriptive damage.

                And all that without any balance issue, because unless you want to make decapitation your PCs favourite method for problem-solving, the main source of death in the system is, still, damage. And damage calculation won't be changed by decapitation. Or, at least, it shouldn't.

                Of course, you can let the vampires with as much as humane functions as you like. It can ALSO play well on some chronicles. Be it for the sake of playing sci-fi biology or to work the metaphisical properties of a human body.

                Just let things open. Use your preferred answer, if you like, but there are no reason at all to argue its better than anyone else's. Or to close your mind so much about eventual changes.

                The effect of decapiation with Resilience for me could be just the loss of sight, smell and hearing, rendering the vampire only able to perceive his surroundings by touch or preternatural senses. Oh, and he obviously can't talk.

                    In a lot of folklore it can be easy to prevent a vampire from rising (Torpor) but difficult if not impossible to permanently destroy them (final death). Typically, the way I play it is that any vampire can be brought back from "final" death via certain rites if you have access to their remains (even ashes) and enough blood. This may require mass human sacrifice, the sacrifice of one or more of the vampire's childer, or even draining their sire, but it's always possible. Vampire society is not generally aware of this, hence the concept of "final" death, but it is a truth of the Kindred condition. This creates options for bringing back PCs and important NPCs.

                    Giles: Buffy, I've never heard of a revivification ritual being successful.
