                        Originally posted by Ventrue Life View Post
                        Question: Since there's a penalty for using big ranged weapons in melee, what stops a person from walking away at the start of their round and then firing without the penalty?

                        My guess is that the penalty applies when you had to use your defense during that round. Even when you walk away, you still get the penalty if before walking away you had to use your defense. Is that a correct assumption?

                        I think the correct assumption is common sense. You don't walk back 9 meters, fire a machine gun then the other guy walks up to you and tries to stab just to have it repeat turn after turn.
                        If a PC was in melee and tried to get rid of the penalty for that by walking back a few meters within their Speed, i'd still apply the penalty. Combat is messy and trying to get a small feel of that even within the formal structure of turns is usually a good thing imho. It makes sense that if you had to use your Defense then the penalty applies. However I do think that CofD doesn't give us a clear definition of what the rules views as in melee range. I would say though, if you are within the enemies Speed so they can reach you on their turn without them spending an action to do so, you're in close combat for most instances,otherwise: common sense™ applies.

                        This reminds me of the old Spear argument in D&D fights with free movement, and Reach weapons combined with the traits that gives unlimited Attacks of Opportunity and just walking back and have enemies come to you, or still attack them.

                                    Normally, a vampire's player can trade the acquisition of a bane for the removal of a Humanity breaking point from consideration from then on, and this can be done up to three times; Ziva 4 changes this number to the character's Willpower rating, which is usually a larger number than 3, enabling the character to acquire more banes and thus trade out more breaking points.

                                    Ziva does weird things in the course of hacking your Humanity, considering that each bane you take makes Detachment rolls harder and the preceding Coils' effects are essentially "reduce the effects of all your banes," "add bonus dice to Detachment by spending Vitae," and "replace a Condition you get from Detachment rolls with the one that lets you ride the wave easier."

                                        Originally posted by Ventrue Life View Post
                                        Question: Since there's a penalty for using big ranged weapons in melee, what stops a person from walking away at the start of their round and then firing without the penalty?

                                        My guess is that the penalty applies when you had to use your defense during that round. Even when you walk away, you still get the penalty if before walking away you had to use your defense. Is that a correct assumption?
                                        That isn't a penalty per se but a bonus to the defending player defense. I tend to rule on favor of the defending player. For that, my take is that if at the start of the turn, the ranged attackers were at melee range of the defending player, he gets the bonus defense. Otherwise multiple attackers could be surrounding one player in melee and only the attacked one would get the penalty. The ranged players could still walk away, but the melee player would just have to act after all of them acted to close the distance at the end of the turn so that he could get the defense bonus again the next turn.

                                        Originally posted by Raktus View Post
                                        What if your speed is nearly twice that of the speed of the person you are backing away from. In which case their movement wouldn't take them back into melee combat even if they were somehow moving out of turn.
                                        In that case, the next turn the defending player wouldn't have the bonus with my take. But you have to remember that if the speed is only twice of that of the defending player, he may still attempt a charge attack, which doubles his speed and sacrifice his full defense for the turn, to melee-attack the ranged character. Another option would be sacrifice your action to move at twice your speed to get close to him, thought that wouldn't have much point. Yeah... with that much speed, it's almost impossible that the defense bonus enters in play.
