    Neither covenant is "good" enough for me to root for; both preach horrific doctrines of inhuman monstrosity. As for why it's easier to side against the Sanctified, I think there's a bit of real-world bleedover in that plenty of people have had unpleasant experiences with Christian faiths, so having an evil vampire Catholic church is easy to hate. Comparatively few folks out there have been victimized by matriarchal evil pagan cults, and beyond that lots of the CofD fandom can either enjoy the exotic nature of such a thing or might actually be neopagans themselves.

        Originally posted by Maina View Post
        However, given the content of the game, I can imagine it has to do with the oppressive Christian trappings of the Sanctum hitting home a bit more to many players. Vampire is the exact sort of game conservative Christian parents might tell a teen is satanic and forbidden. I mean, happened to me. Most people don't really grow up around pagans. Really, I think that's the root of it. We tend to root for the oppressed over the oppressors, and the meta-narrative both IC and OOC is that pagans are the ones that get oppressed, while the Christians do the oppressing. Not making blanket statements about either, but that's the typical narrative.
        Mania's hit the head of a pretty important nail, for me. The basic fact of the matter that the Sanctum orientates itself around being "good Christian vampires" is naturally going to alienate those who aren't interested in or actively reject a Christian view of the world.

        Speaking for myself, there's also the different perceptions fostered by the different lores underlying each Covenant's credo.

        The Circle's philosophy is that vampires are natural in their own right; being a vampire is a normal thing, nothing to be ashamed of - indeed, it's something to be proud of. Crones accept who they are and make peace with their nature, trying to live what they consider a "good" unlife without hypocritically denying that their nature requires them to prey upon and sometimes kill human beings. A vampire isn't really human anymore, and the Circle accepts that; but just because a wolf does not cry when it eats a bunny does not mean it can't try to be a responsible wolf.

        The Sanctum, on the other hand, seems to build itself around some of the worst perceivable aspects of Christianity; excessive self-flagellation ("woe is me, for I am cursed by god and will never be shown his merciful light again"), hypocritical self-righteousness ("in my damnation, god has empowered me to act as his scourge upon the wicked") and aggressive proselytizing ("all man must heed unto god, and my depredations will punish those who stray from the flock"). It paints the Sanctified as, ironically, more monstrous than Crones; these are vampires who will justify all the evil they do as "God's Work", adding the least appealing traits of a fanatic's mentality to the already unpleasant aspects of life as a vampire.

        This, to me at least, paints a mental portrait of the typical Sanctified as either a cringing, passive-aggressive beast with no pride in itself, or as a cruel, hypocritical fanatic, whilst the Crone's portrait is of a proud, self-assured and stable vampire who accepts what they are and owns it. I don't know about anyone else, but to me, this makes the Crones far more interesting and personally appealing.

        It's actually one of the reasons why I like Requiem far more than Masquerade. No explicitly canonical calcified Christian crap built into the very backbone of the setting.

                        There really is no way to answer the question beyond it is your personal bias. If you have a problem with Christianity then you have a bias against the Lance. People see what they want to see. This comes up with the Invictus and the Carthians too.

                        Too many people miss the mark. Every covenant is morally bankrupt. Each one contains the worse kind of assholes you can imagine, in fact they are the ones in charge. Each covenant is meant to represent the worst stereotype of a certain belief system. The hope is that a player can see enough light in a covenant to thread the needle and not be forced to become the worst kind of asshole imaginable.

                        The Ordo Dracul are actually the ones that accept that they are no longer human and embrace being a vampire. What that means is up to the individual. The Circle have a specific definition for what it means to be a proper vampire, monster. In typical covenant hypocrisy, the covenant without rules has one of the most strict views on how a vampire should act.

                        Having said that, there is nothing wrong with rooting for any of the covenants. Sometimes people root for the villain in a story even when they are written without any redeemable qualities. I think people at least recognize that they are rooting for the villain though. It is important to recognize what the covenants are and the role they play in society.

                        There is a reason joining one is a breaking point. It isn't a good thing to do. Covenants are antagonists, even to a covenant's members. Covenants are bad, players don't have to be. They were all written to be equally bad so if you don't see the bad then you might want to look harder.

                        The only thing that keeps the Circle from committing genocide is their lack of organization keeps them weak in most places. Generally, every covenant has a "join or die" attitude when they have the strength to pull it off.

                                                            I think that more people root for the Circle because of a few biases. Not only because of the RL Christianity angle (though I personally find that the LeS has several key redemptive features that make them my favorite covenant philosphically), but also because of the clear favoritism of Cruac over Theban.

                                                            To put it bluntly? Cruac gets all the best rituals. In 1e, Cruac ended up with roughly double the amount of rituals. From the beginning, Cruac's gotten more random rituals in books than Theban. Sure, the covenant and core books had the same amount of rituals, but Cruac got more from random appearances like in Gangrel clan book, more Cruac rituals than Theban in Cotires, etc. And, even in the case of those rituals, most of the Cruac ones were considered by many to be more useful than many Theban ones. Especially the divination rituals. Theban generally ended up being used for combat magic in my experience, and little else.

                                                            And don't get me started on the sacrifices. Some sacrifices are so heavy that makes the effect of the ritual not worthwhile, or exceedingly narrow in application. Others are simply difficult to arrange. One player complained to me that the sacrifices made him feel like he was a D&D wizard carrying around a spell component pouch. And the difficulty of the sacrifices stacks on top the usual willpower expendature. Meanwhile, Cruac is a point of vitae, and done (the new mantles need willpower as well, but I think that's more of an exception than rule).

                                                            And then, there's the themes of the magic themselves. Cruac, despite the CotC's pretence at vampire-neo-paganism, tends to have rituals that tend towards powers that vampires classically have in fiction. Vampiric gardens, storm raising, dark divinations, gargoyles and homoculi and other horrors, etc. Meanwhile, Theban is pretty much just "Abrahamic + ancient Egypt" themed magic. While cool in its own right, Theban tends to support more narrow character concepts/flavors than Cruac. At least, that's my opinion.
