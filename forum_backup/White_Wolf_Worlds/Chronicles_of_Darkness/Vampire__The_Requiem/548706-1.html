        It's worth noting that in Second Edition, membership in multiple Covenants is fully permitted, with a few limitations. As long as your total dots in Covenant Status doesn't exceed 5, you can divide them however you want between multiple Covenants. Though, you can't have more dots in any other Covenant Status than you have in your primary. Basically, the Ordo Dracul doesn't mind you attending Sanctified Mass, and the Lancea et Sanctum doesn't mind having you there. But, the more involved you get with the Sanctum, the less the Ordo will be willing to trust you with prominent position. And, of course, if you teach any Coils to other Sanctified, or Theban Sorcery to other Dragons, they will kill you if they find out.

                City, Covenant, and Clan (why they specify Clan, as you can't have Status in other clans, is beyond me) are all capped the same way. You can have dots in more than one faction (City: San Francisco and City: San Diego, as an example), but the total for each type is capped at 5 (like, say, City: SF 3 and City: SD 2). You also cannot have more in an affiliate group than you have in your main one.

                The book does not explicitly spell out why, as far as I can find. I do think there is a good and likely reason: if you are dabbling in other organizations, your own is going to trust you less. Split loyalties do not make for leadership material. Likewise, the organizations that you're dabbling in will take supporters where they can, but without your primary loyalty, they are not going to share the majority of their resources with you.

                I suspect, though I cannot prove, that it's just an abstract representation of the social effects of splitting your loyalties. The Ordo may tolerate you taking part in Lancea services, but they'll never trust you quite like someone who dedicates themselves fully to their craft. Who knows what secrets you might spill after taking communion one too many times.

                                                        Allies is not Contacts, so the number of dots you have in a single instance of Allies is the level of cumulative favors you can ask of them without having to roll. Once the favor level exceeds your total dots in that instance of Allies, I believe the dice pool is Presence + Persuasion + Allies - Favor Level as a roll. On a success, you temporarily lose access to one dot of that instance of Allies, which returns at the end of the chapter (session).

                                                        You can have multiple instances of Allies. Example: I have Allies 3 (Local Precinct) and Allies 2 (Los Zetas). I can ask for up to 3 1 dot favors of the Local Precinct without rolling. If I ask for a level 4 favor, or more than 3 level 1 favors, I have to roll. Each of these is a seperate instance of the merit, and costs 1 Experience per dot.

                                                        What your Allies can do is narratively determined. For examples, Allies might be able to perform the Services listed on page 185-186 of the 2nd edition. These are examples of services that Allies could potentially acquire, but it shouldn't be the limit. Storytellers should hopefully feel comfortable improvising the level of cost of a service, based upon how hard it is for an organization to do, and how much they like the character using the merit.

                                                        As a sidenote, Services might be acquired through other social merits as well. Status for instance could also do it, though it might be harder to convince them to render the service if its higher than that level of Status affords. Services are a neat little thing.

                                                        The cost of some things might move up and down depending upon how easy it is for an organization to do it, or how you want it done. Doing something illegal might be a much higher favor from the Police, and might incur some complications, for instance.
