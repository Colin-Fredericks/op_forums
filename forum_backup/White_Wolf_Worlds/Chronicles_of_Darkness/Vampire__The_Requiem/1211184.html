    Homebrew - the Dementation Discipline in 2e

    04-24-2018, 06:29 PM
    I'm not sure if the Dementation discipline has already been converted to Vampire The Requiem Second Edition (VTR 2e), but if not, I gave it a shot! I'd like to hear people's feedback! if you do decide to give feedback, please be constructive and give me arguments as to why you think something needs to be altered/changed.

    Before we dive into what I came up with, I want to explain my approach when I designed this.

    For those of you who do not know what Dementation is; it's a Kindred discipline unique to the Malkavians. The Malkavians used to be a clan in Vampire The Masquerade (VTM) and got reintroduced in Vampire The Requiem (VTR 1e) as a bloodline or an affliction.
    The Malkavians are Kindred who suffer from severe madness and are capable of harnessing their madness and wield it as a weapon against their enemies. Every Malkavian suffers from a mental disorder is some way and it's never pretty, but some Malkavians are capable of hiding their affliction enough for them to blend in with the other Kindred unnoticed.
    The first mention of Malkavian was in the VTR 1e core book, where they were named Malkovian with an 'o' instead of an 'a'. The Malkovians were a Ventrue bloodline, something a Ventrue could become after reaching Blood Potency 2. These Malkovians would get Auspex, Dominate, Obfuscate and Resilience as their in-clan disciplines.
    When the Ventrue clan book 'Lords of the Damned' was released, they renamed the bloodline to Malkavian, similar to the name of the VTM clan this bloodline is based on. They also changed the Malkavian's disciplines and reintroduced Dementation, the unique discipline of the Malkavians in VTM. However, while thematically similar, Dementation in VTR 1e was mechanically very different from Dementation in VTM.

    When I homebrewed my VTR 2e adaptation of Dementation, I looked at 3 things:
    1. The mechanics and lore of Dementation in VTM.
    2. The mechanics and lore of Dementation in the VTR 1e Ventrue clan book, Lords of the Damned.
    3. The mechanics and power of the Auspex, Dominate, Majesty and Nightmare disciplines in VTR 2e, trying to make sure Dementation would be in line with these disciplines.

    I knew when I adapted the discipline from VTR 1e to VTR 2e I had to change a lot to fit with the VTR 2e design philosophy. Dementation desperately needed to be streamlined. As well, the mechanics needed to change in order to account for the lack of derangements in VTR 2e. Conditions replaced derangements, so I redesigned Dementation to work with conditions instead of derangements.
    While creating this redesign, I did not hesitate to tamper with the lore and mechanics. In some areas, my version of Dementation is vastly different from what you'd find in the VTR 1e Ventrue clan book. Sometimes when I was tampering with the mechanics I decided to dive into the VTM lore of the Malkavians as opposed to the VTR lore. That being said: my version of Dementation should be usable by anyone who wishes to play a Malkavian in VTR 2e without encountering gross contradictions between the Malkavian lore established in VTR 2e and the Dementation discipline I cooked up.


    Without further hold up, here is what I came up with:

    Dementation
    Dementia grants Malkavians a vision of the world that even the sane can appreciate – when that vision can be pried from the trap of their insane minds.
    Dementation is much the same. With it, the Demented can see the world in new ways, and share that vision with others. Alas, the Malkavian perception of the world is erratic, uncomfortable, maddening and frightening. Not all who get flashes of images through Malkavian eyes ask for the experience, and many who do go on to regret it.
    Many more Malkavians learn powers of Dementation than realize it themselves. To many Kindred, the vision and provocative powers of Malkavia are simply further symptoms of the Affliction. Few Malkavians manage to master Dementation. Fewer still share their secrets with others of their sorry ilk. Unlike Kindred of the same clan, a Malkavian can't trust his fellow Afflicted. The seemingly trustworthy Kindred who is guided through the secrets of Dementation one night may be replaced by a murderous sex fetishist the next evening.
    Greater mastery over Dementation does not equate to any greater mastery over the self. Quite the opposite. Dementation's power provokes madness, foments chaos and affords glimpses of the overwhelming, overpowering secrets of the cosmos's dark machinery –– or makes the Demented think it does.
    Dementation benefits the demented, not the sane. A Malkavian with a mastery of this Discipline can be a boon to other Kindred, but no one should trust an insane vampire when he offers to "show you what I see."

    Passion •
    With this power, those who share a room with the Malkavian will get their minds thrown into disarray, experiencing a piece of the Malkavian's delirium or psychosis. The Malkavian projects a piece of her own unstable mind onto the minds of her victims. As a result, the victims are overwhelmed with a wave of powerful emotions related to the Malkavian's state of mind. Either the victims experience an extreme sense of euphoria, or perhaps a sense of intense stupor washes over them. In any case, the victims have difficulty controlling their behavior and are left confused.
    This power, more than any other, seems to be invoked by Malkavians without their conscious intent. To be sure, Malkavians can use this power maliciously, by their own design. But it can also be activated by a Malkavian’s own emotions. The Malkavian’s own psychosis can simply radiate out into the minds of other poor souls nearby, making them feel a piece of the Malkavian's own suffering and leaving them confused.

    Cost: None
    Dice Pool: Presence + Empathy + Dementation vs. Resolve + Blood Potency
    Action: Contested; resistance is reflexive
    Roll Results
    Dramatic Failure: The Malkavian cannot ignore her own delirium, and gains the Confused condition (p. 302 of Vampire The Requiem).
    Failure: The Malkavian fails to project his own delirium far enough to effect the target, or fails to reach down deep enough into her own emotions to affect the target.
    Success:The Malkavian is able to succesfully overwhelm the victim's mind with her own powerful emotions and leaves him in a confused state. The target gains the Confused condition (p. 302 of Vampire The Requiem). For 1 round (3 seconds) the target cannot take any instant actions.
    Exceptional Success: The Malkavian overwhelms the victim to such an immense degree that it doesn't just confuse him, it turns him into a broken mess. In addition to the Confused condition, the victim also gains the Broken (Persistent) condition (page 301 of Vampire The Requiem).

    Nothing about this power makes it immediately obvious to the target where these feelings are coming from. Depending on the Malkavian’s body language or actions, it may be obvious. At the Storyteller’s discretion, a character may be entitled to a Composure + Empathy roll to identify the user’s intent. When successful, a Kindred target may choose to Lash Out (p. 92 of Vampire The Requiem) against the Malkavian using Passion. When doing so successfully, the target loses the Confused condition. Otherwise, the target presumes something else to be the source of his stress.


    Gaslighting ••
    When a Malkavian has mastered this ability, she is able to plague a victim with a lingering madness that withers one's poise and self-control. A short while after being exposed to this power, victims start to wonder if they're going crazy. Later, they become certain that they are.
    With Gaslighting, a Malkavian twists the victim's own mind into either questioning itself, or experiencing a false sense of superiority. The target might doubt his own perceptions and knowledge, or be overconfident in his own abilities, severely overestimating his own abilities, or severely underestimating the situation he's in.

    Cost: 1 Vitae
    Requirement: The Malkavian must have inflicted the Confused condition on the victim.
    Dice Pool: None
    Action: Instant
    With an Instant Action, the Malkavian can choose 1 target who is affected by Passion and use Gaslighting on him. Gaslighting can be used in one of two ways.
    The first use is to trigger madness into the target's mind. He believes someone hired an assassin to murder him, or he is utterly convinced he saw his wife cheating on him. The Malkavian inflicts the Madness (Persistent) condition (p. 289 of Chronicles of Darkness) onto her victim. Additionally, the target can not spend nor regain Willpower for the amount of days and nights equal to the Malkavian's Blood Potency.
    The second use is to trigger mania into the target's mind. He believes he is a trained professional who has the situation under control, whether this is actually true or not. The target gains the Inspired Condition (p. 304 of Vampire The Requiem). Additionally, the target regains all of his Willpower if the Inspired Condition causes the target to roll an exceptional success.


    Mad Insight •••
    As a Malkavian progresses in her abilities with Dementation, the power of her blood tears down her preconceptions, her assumptions, her expectations and her trust that the world is what it appears to be. The Malkavian's perceptions are heightened and freed, her mind becomes receptive to inputs both sensory and extrasensory, and she finds the Blood channeling barely comprehensible signals in the way that dental fillings pick up freak transmissions.
    The benefit of this penetrating madness is the ability to sense and act without shame or doubt. The consequence of this wicked power is that no small part of the telemetry the Malkavian receives is hallucinatory bullshit – static and nonsense generated by her cursed Blood and psychic interference from the same place that torpid nightmares come from. To make sense of her visions, a Malkavian must pick carefully through the sensations she experiences – the sights, the smells, the sounds – for those repeating details that, over time, he can learn to depend on.
    But to do that, she must also come to admit that her own mind and her own senses cannot truly be trusted.
    Kindred legends claim that this power taps into some vast communal consciousness of the Malkavians – a consciousness as demented as the worst of them. Dementation savants claim that Malkavian blood is constantly giving off signals which interact with other Malkavian blood and are, together, modulated by the thrumming frequencies of the universe. This power makes the Malkavian a receiver for those frequencies. By making sense of the telemetry, a Malkavian can gain insight into the rhythms of the material world, and beyond.

    Cost: 1 Vitae
    Dice Pool: Wits + Occult + Dementation
    Action: Instant
    Roll Results
    Dramatic Failure: The Malkavian is besieged by a rush of overwhelming hallucinations made up of her nastiest, most debauched and immoral thoughts combined with warped perceptions of the world around her. She can do little more than scream or weep until she spends a Willpower point to push the vision away. Until then, the Malkavian is incapacitated and unable to respond to outside stimuli. The Malkavian gains the Broken (Persistent) condition (p. 301 if Vampire The Requiem) as a result after spending the Willpower to recollect herself.
    Failure: The Malkavian receives imagery with no genuine insight. The Storyteller might choose to give the Malkavian a vision still, but the Malkavian does not know whether her visions are true or a fragment of her imagination. The vision might still be true, but neither the player nor character can know for sure.
    Success: The Malkavian experiences a vision punctuated by flashes of genuine insight. The effect of this power grants the Malkavian a supernatural vision containing valuable information about her surroundings or even the future. The exact nature of this vision is up to the Storyteller, but virtually any kind of supernatural perception is fair game.
    Here are two suggestions:
    • Grant the character momentary use of one of these Auspex powers: Beast’s Hackles •, Uncanny Perception ••, The Spirit’s Touch •••; but unlike with Auspex, the player does not choose the questions asked, the Storyteller does.
    • Grant the character the ability to see (and maybe even communicate with) ghosts or spirits in the material realm for one scene.
    Exceptional Success: The Malkavian is inspired by her newfound insight and regains 1 point of Willpower in addition to the other effects of Mad Insight.


    Voices of Madness ••••
    As the Malkavian further progresses her madness and Dementation, she starts to hear voices. She isn't sure if these voices are her own, or one of her other personalities, or perhaps they are the whispers of her Kindred ancestors who's essence runs through her cursed blood. These whispers hardly ever make sense. Most of the time they come in the form of incoherent ramblings, while other times they try to provoke the listener into doing something stupid she would later come to regret. These voices are rarely kind and most Malkavians try to ignore these whispers, for listening to them would surely further taint their unstable minds. Some Kindred scholars specialized in the occult might notice this phenomenon is remarkably similar to the Taint experienced by diablerists, Kindred who comitted Amaranth. Indeed, a Malkavian who speaks true about the voices in her head might be mistaken for a diablerist; all the more reason for a Malkavian to stay silent about her voices of madness.
    However, a Malkavian who progressed to this point in her Dementation will soon discover she can wield these whispers as a weapon. When the Malkavian connects her mind to that of her target with Passion, she can transmit the voices in her head to her victim's, further overwhelming the victim's mind and pushing him towards utter insanity. The Malkavian's voices will haunt the victim and try to force his destruction.
    The Malkavian can choose whether she wants to channel her Voices of Madness to one victim, or all victims affected by her Passion.

    Cost: 1 Vitae and 1 Willpower
    Requirement: The vampire must have inflicted the Confused condition on the victim.
    Dice Pool: Presence + Occult + Dementation - victim's Resolve
    Action: Reflexive
    Roll Results
    Dramatic Failure: The Malkavian fails to keep her own voices at bay, they start to take a hold of her and she can no longer ignore them. The Malkavian gains the Tainted condition (p. 306 of Vampire The Requiem) and is incapable of using Voices of Madness as long as she has this condition. For determining the strength and duration of the Tainted condition, use the Malkavian's own Blood Potency. When other vampires uses Auspex on her they might see symptoms similar to someone who is marked by the taint of diablerie.
    Failure: The Malkavian fails to harness the voices of madness in her head. Her target might hear whispers in his head, but the voices don't get a hold of him and do not further drive him into madness.
    Success: The Malkavian's voices of madness take a hold of her victim's mind. The target hears whispers of destruction and he cannot ignore them. The victim gains the Tainted condition. For determining the strength and duration of the condition, use the Malkavian's Blood Potency. When other vampires uses Auspex on the victim, they might see symptoms similar to someone who is marked by the taint of diablerie. Additionally, the voices of madness instil complete and utter dread into the Malkavian's victim. It frightens the victim to his core. On top of gaining the Tainted conditon, he also gains the Frightened condition (p. 304 of Vampire The Requiem).
    Exceptional Success: Double the Malkavian's Blood Potency rating when determining the strength and duration of the Tainted condition.


    Dementia Praecox •••••
    At its pinnacle power, Dementation finally fulfills its namesake. This power (literally, "premature dementia") imposes dementia on the Malkavian's victims, inserting a misconception, hallucination or outright lie into a target's mind and snapping it off. Victims of this power are left believing things – about themselves, about their loved ones, about the world at large – that simply are not true. Their memories are scrambled and most victims become senile. Agitation, depression, anxiety, aimless wandering, delusions and memory loss are all symptoms of dementia.
    Though the length of the delusions imposed by this power is not long when compared to the years of dementia suffered by the aged living, a few nights of demented behavior can be enough to ruin careers, break up marriages, and bring down powerful figures.
    Victims of Dementia Praecox have broken into strangers' houses thinking they were simply locked out of their own homes. They have been found naked in the park, homeless on the street, and covered in blood behind the wheel. They've been found miles away from home, in bed with hookers and high on crack. They've killed spouses they thought were unfaithful.
    They've gotten themselves killed thinking themselves capable of impossible feats.
    A few nights spent in the crucible of madness that makes of a Malkavian's eternity is more than some minds can handle.

    Cost: 2 Vitae
    Dice Pool: Presence + Empathy + Dementation vs. Resolve + Blood Potency
    Action: Extended and contested. The rolls to activate this power and the rolls to resist it are instant actions. Each roll represents one turn (3 seconds) of psychic conflict. The user’s target number of successes is equal to the subject’s Resolve + Blood Potency. The subject’s target number of successes is equal to the user’s Composure + Dementation.
    Roll Results
    Dramatic Failure: In the unlikely event of a dramatic failure, the user is rendered catatonic and helpless for the remainder of the scene. The power fails and any Vitae spent on the contest is wasted. In addition, the user loses a large portion of her recent memory and suffers from the Amnesia (Persistent) condition (p. 301 of Vampire The Requiem).
    Failure: The Malkavian fails to break down her target’s psyche with her maddening onslaught. The power fails and any Vitae spent on the contest is wasted.
    Success: The Malkavian succesfully inflicts dementia upon her victim. The victim's memories are scrambled and he suffers from the Amnesia (Persistent) condition (p. 301 of Vampire The Requiem). In addition, the dementia manifests itself in the victim's mind. The delusions resulting from the victim's dementia are more powerful than any delusion brought forth by the Nightmare discipline. The Malkavian has some control over how the dementia manifests itself in her victim, and chooses a condition. The victims gains this chosen condition in addition to the Amnesia (Persistent) condition. Any condition is fair game as long as the Storyteller allows it. Feel free to be creative with this, but keep in mind that the condition has to be related to dementia in some way. For example, the victim might come to falsely believe he is the loyal servant of a vampire, and gains the Enthralled (Persistent) condition (p. 303 of Vampire The Requiem). Or maybe the victim remembers something that never happened, and gains the False Memories (Persistent) condition (p. 303 of Vampire The Requiem). These conditions last until they are resolved.
    Exceptional Success: When rolling an exceptional success during the conflict, the Malkavian gets to choose 3 conditions for her victim in addition to the Amnesia (Persistent) condition.

          I see where you're coming from. Funny enough, Mad Insight is actually the ability I changed the least. It was the 2nd dot ability of Dementation in the VTR 1e Ventrue clan book, I simply moved it up to be the 3rd dot and added the additional clarification that unlike with Auspex, the player using Dementation has no say over how the Mad Insight manifests itself, instead it's up to the storyteller.

          I think the reason for it being there is because the Malkavians in VTR do not gain Auspex as an in-clan discipline. The Malkavians have this weird ability to gain remarkable insights and their utter madness sometimes allows them to see things more truly or more clearly than other vampires. For this reason, the Malkavians in VTM had Auspex. The Malkovian bloodline in the VTR 1e core rule book gets Auspex as in-clan as well, but not the Malkavians from VTR 1e as found in the Ventrue clan book. They do not get Auspex, instead they get Dementation, which the Malkovian bloodline didn't get (they got a slightly enhanced version of Dominate instead, because Dementation didn't exist yet in VTR when the core rule book was published).

          So if I had to guess the design philosophy behind VTR's version of Dementation, I'd guess they wanted to keep the Malkavian's insight ability and rolled it into their unique Dementation discipline, keeping the Auspex discipline uniquely to the Mekhet. With that in mind, I decided to keep Mad Insight pretty much the same when I tried converting Dementation to VTR 2e.

                  This is a good lift for Dementation. Very serviceable.

                  I think the collision between Auspex and Mad Insight is inevitable, because Auspex is just such a huge blanket when it comes to paranormal investigation tools. The differences here are: you always pay 1 Vitae, and you have no control over which questions/response comes back.

                  My suspicion is that this power would be a major headache for an ST, in that they would have to not only come up with something off-the-wall, but disguise some useful tidbit inside it. Otherwise it'll function identically to Auspex, which itself suggests disguising the responses to questions in the form of impressions and sensory input the vamp has to interpret. Any time you drift into information-gathering powers you'll run into this problem: that it's close to Auspex and you're either duplicating function or form.

                  Solely for comparison, my attempt at a Temporis re-do suffers a bit from the same issues.

                  So, ok. What you have is fully functional. It provides flashes of, um, mad insight. The fluff text seems to imply that the user embraces the madness and lets it wash through them, then out the other side comes insight. That could also be a full scene power that instills Conditions on the user--an inversion of the other powers that all inflict negative conditions on their victims. You could model this by imposing both a negative and positive Condition on the user (resolved without a Beat at scene end), and perhaps give 8-again to a type of investigative skill check? Pondering ways to get away from the "questions" mechanic.

                  Point being that the power should be troublesome for the vamp who uses it. Should be a signature item that is desirable from the perspective of the player to use virtually every time they are in a social or investigation situation--much as a Mekhet will use Auspex (for Free!) every scene. Mad Insight could serve that same purpose--and ensure the Malk is wandering about mumbling, peering at people and clearly off almost all the time.

                  Things to ponder.

                  On Passion, suggest that on an Exceptional Success the victim may be assigned the Inspired condition instead at the vamp's discretion. That would tempt the Malk to use her power to push allies as well as enemies. It would sometimes work... when the emotion happens to align with what's going on in the victim's head just right.

                  Question: is there a physical prereq to use of Dementation? Such as eye contact in Dominate or ability to see the vamp as in Majesty? Something to consider.


                  Good stuff!

                      Thanks for the feedback!

                      About Mad Insight:

                      The original version of the power from the Ventrue clan book states that it's basically a tool the players and storytellers can use to give the Malkavian plot-relevant information mixed with some nonsensical jargon. The book suggests this plot information can come in a lot of different forms, and listed Auspex as an example, but also listed some Mage: The Awakening powers, the ability to talk to spirits, the ability to gain a vision of the future, etc. etc. etc. It's basically up to the storyteller to be creative and choose what info he wants to give the Malkavian and how he's going to do that. The book says the storyteller should be creative with it, but also keep the usefulness of Mad Insight at a reasonable level.

                      I agree that Mad Insight should not be able to outperform Auspex, or at when it does it should be rarely so. Hence I decided to make sure Mad Insight always costs 1 Vitae (to prevent abuse from the player) and to give the control over what Mad Insight does to the Storyteller (as opposed to the player asking questions the storyteller has to answer honestly).

                      Mad Insight could be a chore for a new storyteller for sure, but I know I'd have great fun with it and I'd personally be dancing with joy every time my player uses Mad Insight.

                      While I like your suggestion, I think your alternative would be too weak and not impressive enough. If I were to use your suggestion, I'd move Mad Insight to being the 2nd dot power, and move Gaslighting up to the 3rd dot and perhaps make it a bit more powerful.



                      To answer your question: I don't think the Malkavian needs to have eye contact with her victim. The way I see it, the Malkavian merely has to focus on her victim when she consciously tries to use Passion as a weapon. In that sense it's more similar to Majesty's Awe or Nightmare's Dread Presence, except Passion only targets 1 person instead of everyone in the room.

                      I was thinking about upgrading Passion to allow the Malkavian to inflict the effects of Passion on everyone in the room, meaning everyone, her allies would be affected as well. I think that could be a cool alternative use for Passion. What do you think?

                              Coolio.

                              I'm always looking out for the edge cases, but you're right to point out (and the Ventrue book as well says so) that Dementation in the hands of a PC means the Storyteller has determined to let an advanced power set come into play, and is/should be therefore ready and excited by the twists offered.
