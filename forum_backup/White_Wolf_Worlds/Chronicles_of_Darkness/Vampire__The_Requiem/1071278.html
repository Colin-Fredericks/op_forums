              I've pretty much expressed my thoughts about the Era in the backer PDF thread, but I'll reiterate here.

              - While I was initially most excited for the vampire part of this Era and unsure how Promethean would fit in, upon reading it I found the Promethean material far more compelling. The vampire material was interesting as well, but it wasn't really anything unexpected, whereas I was very pleasantly surprised by Promethean. As Lex mentioned, reframing the Pilgrimage as a quest to gain a soul rather than a quest to become human is a stroke of genius, especially when presented alongside the conflict of knowing that without a solid you are safe from the plague and with one you will almost certainly die of it in a very short time. It actually made me wonder if this might be a better way to present the pilgrimage to folks who are less-than-hung-ho about Promethean's overt "humanism yay", even in modern games.

              - It's interesting to see the Gallows Post make a return as a sort of thieves' and travelers' guild instead of a courier service. This take on them is pretty cool and makes sense both as a precursor to Requiem for Regina's Gallows Post and potentially as an evolution of aspects of Requiem for Rome's Perigrine Collegia. This may be a bit nitpicky, but it feels kind of contrived to me that this earlier incarnation of the Covenant would have the same name as its predecessor. The pun makes sense if the name was invented when the covenant was actually postal service. Less so if the name was originally for a thieves' guild and they coincidentally became a postal service over time.

              - I'm actually really disappointed how similar the Morbus are to their 1e incarnation. Their full Clan-style writeup was cool, although it does break the structure in a few ways (doesn't begin with "something dead approaches", no "see the [example]" paragraphs, little to no use of first or third person, and I'm not sure what fairy tale the "once upon a midnight darkly" is referencing but I'm pretty sure it's not Snow White.) Cachexy looks identical to the 1e version at a glance but on closer inspection has been modified a little bit. I guess there's nothing about it that doesn't work for Second Edition, I had just been hoping for a more significant restructuring like the Khaibit got. I'm fine with unique Disciplines being some Bloodline's Gifts, but the thing is, Cachexy just doesn't feel like a 2e Discipline to me. 2e Disciplines up until now have all been extensions of the Beast - specific uses of the Predatory Aura, or enhancements of the vampire's body with the power of Vitae. Cachexy feels like a return to the 1e style where Discipline was just a catch-all term for any superpower a vampire happened to have.

              - The Amirani are awesome. Kind of the opposite of the reaction I had to Morbus here, I was skeptical of the idea of inventing a new lineage just to fill the role of "the lineage associated with fire" in an era that predates the Frankensteins, and unsure how Prophets would fit that role. But upon reading them, I was sold, they're brilliant and very thematic both to the Era specifically and to Promethean in general. My only critique is that I'm not sure what myth or story their creation story references, or if it's an original story. Honestly, the mythological roots of the various Lineages' creations and being able to pin their original Demiurge to a specific mythological or fictional figure is one of the things that makes Lineages worth keeping as a concept to me, especially in 2e, so it would be nice to see that more for the Amirani. Luckily they stand well enough on their own that this is just a minor quibble for me. Overall they're really cool, and one of my favorite lineages now.

              - All three of the example scenarios are great. There's one vampire-centric, one Promethean-centric, and one crossover-focused. The crossover one is by far my favorite, featuring an Alchemist (yes, that kind of Alchemist) in the employ of a wealthy family which also happens to be the Herd (or maybe Touchstone?) of a vampire who poses as an ancestral guardian of the family. She Ghouls the Alchemist in exchange for him finding a cure for the plague to keep the family she preys on alive. This is the kind of nuance I had hoped to see more of in Alchemists, as he's looking for a cure to the disease that's literally killing everyone in Europe, but not exactly for altruistic reasons, and with undoubtedly unethical methods. Also I love that he's both an Alchemist and a Ghoul. It's nice to see 2e getting bolder with template mixing where it makes sense.

              Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.

                                                  It mostly just doesn't feel like 2e design to me. I wold have expected a power like Diagnose to just work in 2e - if you can perceive someone with a disease, you know they have a disease, just like how if you can perceive another vampire, you know they're a vampire. Then maybe have the ability to spend 1 Vitae and make a roll to analyze exactly what the nature and effects of the disease are. Contaminate is fine - you put germs on stuff and people get sick if they touch it. The fact that it makes every disease transmittable by skin contact has always felt weird to me, but whatever. Inflame is just a floating penalty, which in 2e should really be a Condition. It also feels extremely underwhelming for a 3rd dot power, especially in 2e. Plague Bearer has always felt super odd to me. It's vague on what the constraints are - do the people you infect have to be in sensory range? Do you choose who you infect? Also, how is the disease being transmitted? "Magically" feels like a really unsatisfying answer. I thought we were moving away from Disciplines just being magic powers vampires can do and towards them being extensions of the Beast or the Blood. Accelerate is fine, but ho-hum, and conceptually very similar to Inflame.

                                                  As a whole, it feels like a grab bag of five different powers with a loosely related theme, which feels very 1e. 2e Disciplines I thought were supposed to be single, coherent powers with five levels of increasing mastery. What I would have liked to see for a real 2e version of Cachexy, as oppposed to just a conversion of the 1e version, assuming it was going to be a Discipline, is a power about spreading disease through the Morbus' tainted Vitae. Start small - almost Quietus style, allow the Morbus to spill Vitae and convert it into an infectious agent that must be consumed or otherwise introduce to the bloodstream. Maybe even allow it to be spread through the Kiss. At higher levels, allow it to be spread through indirect contact, like with the current Contaminate. At even higher levels, make it spread not only from the Morbus, but from those infected by her. For a capstone, make it airborne. Just as examples. And again, that's all assuming it takes the form of a Discipline. I could also easily see it as a Devotion chain. Add Auspex for a Diagnose-like effect, Celerity for an Accelerate-like effect, Obfuscate for a Contaminate-like effect, maybe Resilience for an Alleviate effect that sends the disease into remission, or Vigor for an effect that makes the disease more difficult to resist, or more damaging, than normal.
