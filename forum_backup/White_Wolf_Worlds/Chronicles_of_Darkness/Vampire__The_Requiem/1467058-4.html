    A good Monday to you all! Let's talk about the Brigmans.

    In a technical sense, the Brigmans are a First Edition ghoul family, but they never had any mechanics, or even much of an existence outside of their regnant, Solomon Birch, the signature Daeva character. In a nutshell, the family is Birch's attempt to breed a race of perfect humans who could deny the temptations of vampires and lead the rest of humanity to God's holy light. They get a few name-checks throughout various sourcebooks, including the three Chicago Requiem novels (or, well, two of them). Even then, their origins aren't all that concrete. The most we get about them specifically is in Night Horrors: Immortal Sinners, where Birch is detailed as a potential antagonist.

    Originally, I didn't plan on doing the Brigmans, partly because I didn't even remember they existed. I was going to do the Children of Nirriti, and the author who ended up doing them was going to do another new one. However, as one does, I fell down a rabbit hole of Requiem lore. I was also trying to shoehorn in an update to Birch's stats in Immortal Sinners, and putting some numbers on his horrifying ghoul family was a good place for it, especially as a way to talk about Birch in the context of the 2E Daeva bane. This was part of the reason we have four ghoul families in the book as opposed to the usual three. I didn't want to have the lore-heavy ghoul family take up the space of one you can plug into any setting. Not that you couldn't transplant Birch and the Brigmans (or just the Brigmans) into another setting, but I like to make sure readers get as much bang for their buck as possible.

    The Brigmans also presented a chance to emphasize the Daeva's physical side. It's easy to lean on the social aspects of the Serpents, but that Celerity + Vigor combo packs a nasty punch. Birch also famously had no Majesty, so that was another reason to make them a bit burly.

    This family also gave me an opportunity to expand on just a smidge of setting detail from Chicago. One of the sample characters is featured in The Marriage of Virtue and Viciousness, and to my knowledge, her fate was never revealed (apart from a minor mention in Vampire: The Requiem for Dummies, of all things). If you're a fan of the Chicago novels, that's my little treat for you.

    So, let's meet the Brigmans. Tomorrow, let's set the ghouls aside for a moment and look at another antagonist: The Flowers of Innana.

    Brigman: The ones who stand above you

    "Mercy? No. If God needed you alive, you would’ve won.'

    Where we came from

    Let it never be said that Solomon Birch takes half measures. Like all good Sanctified, Chicago’s Black Bishop believes vampires are a race of Devil’s Advocates, one that tests the faithful and takes the sinful as payment. Still (and not to question God’s plan) it’s not the most efficient system. Not all vampires adhere to the Longinian creed, and most don’t care about the difference between sacred and profane feeding. This troubles Birch. It’s a contradiction in the Almighty’s perfect order. Or rather, it seems like one. But God can’t make mistakes, so that means it’s a riddle to solve — and Birch thinks he’s found an answer.

    Enter the Brigmans. They are the Elect, a beacon that will one day guide humanity against the darkness of the Kindred race, injecting true holiness into the flock so the wolves won’t be necessary. Maybe that skirts some Christian dogma, but Solomon Birch is no Christian.

    The Brigmans were a fixture in Chicago’s upper crust long before Birch got his golden claws in them. They invested early in the city’s vast infrastructure, making a fortune with Brigman Transport, Inc., and this simple fact attracted Birch in his salad night. He needed the cash, and he had a Serpent’s lust for the family’s beautiful (married) daughter, Elena. He’ll say now that he was drawn to them due to the purity of their bloodline, but just how he came around to this understanding is obscure, and Birch isn’t one to show his work.

    Some loose tongues at Elysium say Elena refused his Vitae when he first tried to court her; others joke she slapped him for getting fresh. Whatever the catalyst, Birch found a spark in his ghoul that coalesced into a Requiem’s work. The Black Bishop is no geneticist, but he is a craftsman, and he believes he can apply his artistry to the human genome in ways secular scientists don’t have the imagination to conceive.

    Of course, the problem with eugenics, apart from being a pretty clearcut example of evil, is that they’re nonsense. Birch might argue his blood makes the difference, and that mortal genetics have little to do with his project beyond metaphor, but when has Vitae done anything but twist the human soul into more contorted shapes?

    That’s a question he’s not prepared to answer. No matter how hard he pretends otherwise, Birch’s fixation on humanity is just as potent as any other Serpent’s, and he’s as dependent on the Brigmans as they are on him. All he’s managed to do is remake the Elect in his own image, and one night, they may be unlucky enough for him to realize it.

    Who we are tonight

    The Brigmans are a family in the organized crime mold, albeit with fundamentalist vampires cooking the books instead of gangsters. Like the mafia, not all are directly related, but they’ve all tasted the Black Bishop’s Vitae, and that’s more than enough to make you Elect. Their internal structure is stringent yet ever-shifting, with different factions vying for power and Kindred favor. A few things are set in stone, though: The dead are better than the living; the living are less than Brigmans.

    Wherever Birch extends his influence, the Elect are on the vanguard with money and muscle, but that doesn’t mean their service is exclusive. In recent years, Birch has allowed a degree of autonomy as long as they keep the faith, so Brigmans can be both hired help and guns for rent. Every last one of them is bound to him, so it doesn’t really matter how much sinful Kindred blood gets in their veins. Plus, it’s good to throw some new DNA in the gene pool from time to time. It’s also good for his donation plate, and the Brigmans provide soft power to a Lick known mostly for the lash.

    The Elect specialize in anything that requires muscle. Often, that’s taking a baseball bat to some lowlife’s knees, but these ghouls weren’t bred just to be goons. Their construction company front is a good way to access their brawn, but sometimes the appearance of strength is useful too. If you have the cachet to enter a meeting flanked by Solomon Fucking Birch’s brutes, it says a lot about the direction your Requiem is taking.

    The other reason he rents them out is to breed resentment. Seeing the depravity of other Kindred firsthand, yet having to bow to them, isn’t great for the superiority complex Birch has bred into them. That’s the point. One night, he wants them to betray him; not just to quietly disobey when they think he’ll never find out (he always does), but to aim a stake at his vile heart. If the Brigmans are Elect in truth and not just name, they need to break his grasp. Only then can they make the Kindred obsolete. With each generation, a defiance burns in them like flame working through a taper. When the wick is ash, Birch will know if it was all worth it.

    Benefit: The Black Bishop has spent decades molding his Elect into Godly beings, and while his plan is far from complete, he’s produced some interesting specimens in the meantime. Brigmans gain three additional dots at character creation to spend on Physical Merits. In addition, due to their regnant’s spurning of his clan Discipline, few Brigmans know any Majesty. Instead, they gain access to Dominate.

        Let's dig up the Flowers of Inanna!

        The Flowers are humans who've suffered a vampire's feeding and come back different. Claiming to be blessed by a primordial goddess, they return from near death to sap away Kindred Blood Potency in exchange for immortality.

        I don't have much to say regarding development of this one, really (I also accidentally deleted my post draft, so I don't feel like rewriting all of it!) Mostly, the changes to the Flowers were about making them more player-friendly and more efficient. For the former, I made their Afterglow period into a Supernatural Tolerance Trait, similar to Magnitude and similar traits for other mortal(ish) monsters; I could picture them in Hunter chronicles, or even as allies for vampires. For the latter, I cut some details about them producing dhampir more easily from sexual encounters with Kindred. It wasn't a bad idea, but it felt rather redundant with material in Half-Damned, and it was honestly one too many things for an already system-heavy character type.

        Enjoy the excerpt! Tomorrow, I'll be back with one of our more abstract "antagonists": Solace.

        Flowers of Inanna: Love's labors revenged

        "Is this real? Well, tonight it is."

        AKA: Ishtari, Astarte’s Tears

        She’s watching you. She has been since you walked in the club. She’s not your normal type, but she’s got something about her that feels just right for tonight. And maybe tomorrow night. You planned your evening around a quick hookup and a meal, but you just can’t get her out of your head. Maybe it’s something genuine for once. Maybe…

        Or maybe you’re being played.

        The Daeva are nothing if not arrogant, but perhaps they’ve earned the right. They have the power to create city-spanning herds and are endowed with a Kiss with pleasures deeper than any orgasm. It’s only natural that these Kindred would attract their fair share of hangers-on and stalkers. But hidden among that clinging throng is something vampires so rarely encounter: predators.

        Background

        The Flowers of Inanna say they’re blessed by a scornful goddess, one with an endless hunger for the pleasures of dead flesh. By playing at being mere victims and blood dolls, the Ishtari worm their way into the All Night Society, providing potent pleasures for even the most jaded Lick. To love one of the Flowers is to know (an approximation of) true love, but such joy carries a steep price, one they’re ordained to collect. Each caress thins a vampire’s blood a little more, and eventually, it leaves her a helpless whelp.

        The Queen of Heaven

        As their name suggests, the Flowers trace their origins to Inanna (sometimes called Ishtar, or Astarte, or even Athena), the Mesopotamian Queen of Heaven. Inanna was a greedy and spiteful creature, known for her many attempts to steal power from other divine beings. According to legend, one day, she decided to conquer the underworld, the domain of her sister, Ereshkihal. However, when she stood before the gates demanding entry, Ereshkihal struck her dead for the blasphemy, for the living can never enter death’s domain. Of course, gods can’t truly die, and even in death, Inanna still lusted for power. So, Ereshkihal hung her sister’s body on a hook to suffer the fate of living death for all time.

        As Inanna hung on that hook, she was visited by Nergal, her sister’s husband and Lord of Death. The two had long lusted for each other, and so in death they consummated their desire. Three times he went to her, and each time he whispered a grand promise: he would free her; he would abandon his wife for her; he would give her the underworld as her kingdom. And three times he abandoned her as the morning came, left to hang on the hook.

        When her father’s servants finally came to free her from this mess of her own creation, Inanna returned to her heavenly throne, wiser but more vindictive than ever. She had heard the promises of the dead and now knew the lies behind their pretty words. And so, Inanna took the seed Nergal left within her and sowed it within the souls of mortals, ones who had suffered the touch of the death as well, and blessed them with a divine purpose.

        Justice from Above

        Every Ishtari has been the victim of vampiric violation, and all bear scars of their encounter, be they psychological or physical. The Flowers believe their suffering calls out to Inanna and her own experiences in the underworld, and that their goddess helps them balance the scales. Whenever a mortal is afflicted by a vampire’s bite, there’s a small chance they’ll be visited by a vision of a beautiful woman radiating warmth and life. These visitations are most common among feeding victims close to death, as the proximity to the underworld is said to draw Inanna’s attention. When the being appears, she offers the victim a choice between a gentle, kind death or a long life filled with power — and justice.

        If he accepts, the new Flower awakens bearing the Mark of Inanna, an eight-pointed rose symbol that resembles a tattoo. The Mark grows as the Ishtari ages, spreading vines across his skin. With each Kindred victim left in his wake, a thorn sprouts along the snaking vine, and legend has it the oldest Ishtari have marks that spread over every inch of exposed flesh.

        Necromance

        An Ishtari’s presence is invariably captivating to Kindred, and her touch is as addictive as any narcotic. However, these encounters come with a price to the victim: the potency of their blood. By leeching a vampire’s death energies, a Flower can prolong her own life, revitalizing her body and gaining access to immense reserves of strength and will.

        To prey on their victims, Flowers masquerade as blood dolls and groupies, or even cultists if they know where to look. In particular, their ability to shrug off Majesty allows them to pass freely through Daeva circles where a normal mortal would find himself ensnared, entranced, or dead.

        An Ishtari is indistinguishable from a normal human in all ways except one: Her presence is intoxicating to Kindred. This passion isn’t one-sided, though. As an Ishtari makes her way into the All Night Society, she begins to feel a sympathy for the dead. Most Flowers are aware of the threat their affection presents their lovers, but the connection need not be hostile. Many choose not to share the truth of their nature, but it’s as often for fear of abandonment as it is for their own safety. Perhaps the most damning gift of all is not that the Flowers of Inanna must feed upon the curse of vampirism, but that they’re fated to love the ones they devour.

        Systems

        The Flowers of Inanna enjoy several inherent powers, as well as abilities granted by the Afterglow, the period where their bodies digest the power borrowed from their Kindred victims.

        Innate Powers

        The Queen of Heaven’s blessing provides the following traits:

        Allure

        By spending a Willpower, an Ishtari can make sure she stands out in a crowd. For the rest of the night, if she falls within the range of a vampire’s Kindred Senses, he gains the Swooning Condition toward her. To resist, his player can spend a Willpower and roll Resolve + Blood Potency vs. Presence + Manipulation + Afterglow. If he prevails, he’s immune to the Ishtari’s charm for the scene, or the night if it’s an exceptional success.

        Death Sight

        All Flowers gain Unseen Sense: Kindred. In addition, with a Wits + Occult + Afterglow roll, an Ishtari character can learn a vampire’s rough Blood Potency. On a success, the Storyteller must reveal if the trait is greater than one (if it’s five or less) or greater than five (if it’s six or more).

        Flowers of Inanna can identify Daeva on sight, and any other clans they’ve fed from before.

        Emotional Control

        Ishtari are immune to blood bonds, and they cannot be Embraced or turned into ghouls. They are also resistant to the Kiss and the Assault, though they can still find the Kiss pleasurable. Furthermore, as an instant action, a Flower can choose to dismiss Majesty effects if they apply to her, ending (but not resolving) any relevant Conditions. However, for each scene she allows the Discipline’s effects to stand, she refreshes a Willpower.

        Feeding

        Once a night, if a vampire spends more than a few minutes in skin-to-skin contact with an Ishtari, he temporarily loses a Blood Potency dot, refreshes a spent Willpower, and gains the Half Dead Condition. If he has the Swooning Condition toward her, it resolves and becomes Dependent, which fades when he restores all Blood Potency; if he can avoid contact for a full 24 hours, dots start to return at a rate of one every (Humanity) nights. If further trysts would reduce Blood Potency to zero, he instead gains the persistent form of Half Dead and refreshes all Willpower.
        Such connections rarely run one-way. When a Flower drains a Blood Potency, her player can choose to accept the Swooning Condition for a Beat, but only if she doesn’t have it toward the vampire already.

        A Flower can suppress her feeding effect until dawn, but it costs a Willpower to reactivate it. She cannot feed on vampires in daysleep or torpor.

                    Anyway, let's pass around some Solace.

                    This is going to be another short dev note, as updating Solace came pretty easily to me, and it didn't see a lot of changes once the first draft was finished. The big change, as noted above, was that it was originally going to tie in with the Erzsébet bloodline. I had this whole multiple-choice backstory of how it related to them, and it just...didn't work. It wouldn't have hurt the Cavaliers to have a connection to Solace, but I don't think the reverse is true. Plus, what I had made the S a little too definite, even if the backstories were optional. Solace works best as something that's a little unknowable, and I think the write-up I ended up with maintains that obscurity without making it opaque. In any case, I was able to appropriate a lot of that material from my Erzsébet version for the final one. It's always nice to have half the work done already.

                    Originally, Solace appeared in Mythologies, and the mechanics there are...complex. However, I did appropriate the idea of it having a purity rating from that. Otherwise, it's fairly mechanics light. As for what it really is, I feel like that's been revealed in other books if you read between the lines (I'm pretty sure Rose even said it outright somewhere), but I wanted to maintain some ambiguity for this. I'm especially fond of the final rumor I included...

                    So, here's Solace. Tomorrow, being Thanksgiving, I think we'll focus on a (ghoul) family: The Tribute Society.

                    Solace: Liquid nostalgia

                    "First taste's free."

                    Background

                    The Sanctified call it sin. The Carthians say it’s bourgeois decadence. The Invictus want to market it. The Ordo Dracul are performing clinical trials. And the Acolytes? They’ll burn every last syrette given the chance.

                    Solace (Vampire: The Requiem, p. 73) is a drug that provides Kindred with a day of pseudo-mortality. This effect is akin to the blush of life, but the feeling is far more intense, and it comes with a spiritual sense of wholeness. That’s as much as anyone knows for sure; those who claim otherwise are just repeating hearsay and folklore.

                    The S is rare enough that when it does show up in a domain, it’s usually only for a few hours. If a dealer’s stock lasts even a night, it’s notable. From a practical standpoint, its rarity means it’s not exactly habit forming, but if you’ve done a hit even once, you’ll never want to miss another chance.

                    Serpents are especially vulnerable to Solace addiction; it’s an easy way for them to indulge both their narcissism and their humanity habit. Daeva high on the S can almost convince themselves they’re not just wolves among sheep. The idea that mortality could be a needle prick away is thrilling, and many Serpents are willing to forget it’s an obvious lie as long as it’s a soothing one. The prince gets to play pauper for a day, and when the sun falls and his blood turns blue again, he goes back to his velvet-lined coffin convinced he’s learned something.

                    It never remains so comfortable. Getting hooked on Solace is like being addicted to a dream, something that’s real and clear and true for a moment, then cloudy like a cataract in the light of day. Indeed, trying to reclaim that high is a great way to meet the sun. The worst S addicts crave the warmth of sunlight as much as that sweet pinch beneath their tongues. When they lose their supplies, they play chicken with the dawn, hoping to spark something other than fourth-degree burns.

                    Hearsay

                    As for what it is, no one with that information is willing to share it. One rumor suggests Solace is distilled from the blood of women who commit self-harm, though every vampire who’s tried to prove that theory has come away with little more than intense shame. Some Dragon biologists say it’s the blood of Inamorata, beings who resemble a vampire’s long-lost mortal lover. Drinking Vitae from Inamorata has a similar effect to the drug, but these strange creatures are far rarer than even Solace, and highly individual. A similar theory ties it to the Flowers of Inanna (p. 134), but the Ishtari consider it their one true bane, disdaining its presence in a vampire’s blood. A few fundamentalist Sanctified say it’s the work of the Devil, and that, somewhere, Satanists are bottling the blood and tears of seventh sons to tempt Kindred into the Adversary’s arms.

                    The only consistent rumor states that the drug has something to do with blood, and this at least holds the weight of evidence. A hit of Solace provides a bit of Vitae, and it’s hard not to speculate on who bled for it. As a general rule, vampires are pretty cool with bloodletting, but how much varies from Lick to Lick. Maybe a lot more Kindred hold the truth about Solace than they let on, and the reason they keep it obscured is because of the hunt that would inevitably follow its unveiling. Or maybe they just don’t want competition.

                    Folklore

                    Tracking the history of Solace is as byzantine as finding its source. Needle drugs didn’t exist until the 19th century, yet fragmentary tales mention similar potions as far back as the Camarilla, sometimes calling it Ambrosia or Manna, or any other host of divine food. However, these anecdotes never really form a cohesive narrative, and most are poetic to the point of obscurity. Some might just be talking about plain old opium.

                    Most Kindred believe Solace is a modern phenomenon, or at least that its increased availability is. Not that you can find it on any street corner, but the S is more common tonight than even a decade ago, still dismissed as myth in some corners of the All Night Society, but spreading further and further with every year.

                    Rumors (Excerpt)

                    “It’s the Dracul. Evidence? It’s always the Dracul.”

                    It’s not the Dragons. Definitely, probably not. It’s not Dracula either. Dracula bottling his blood? Come on. Dracula bottling his blood and creating thousands of Vincula interlinked through the Coil of the Voivode? Wow. Got any spare tinfoil hats? Don’t let anyone catch you spouting that nonsense — seriously, they might be listening…

                        A very late update today, but a fun one!

                        The Tribute Society is one of two social ghoul families in the book. I've tried to have more of those kind of ghoul families, as they didn't really exist in First Edition, and I think it's one of the more interesting concepts that came out of Half-Damned.

                        The Tributes are vampire groupies, or vampire stalkers if you're being unkind. They exist just on the edge of the Masquerade, not completely part of the All Night Society, but not wholly apart from it either. They gain Vitae through their mysterious club president, who has...secrets, let's say.

                        This is another family that didn't have too many fundamental changes through development. Their benefit was tweaked a bit, but mostly the changes came out of adjusting their backstory. Originally, it was a bit clearer what was really going on, and I felt it was better to leave it more ambiguous; ambiguity is always good for plot hooks! I also beefed up their benefit a bit, which was originally just the tracking ability you'll find in the book. They needed a little more agency, and I think we struck a good balance on that with the final version. I also like to beef up the benefits of social ghoul families, as they lack a lot of the perks of the biological families.

                        On a sidenote, unrelated to the text, our usual logo artist wasn't available for this book, so any new symbols for bloodlines and ghoul families you'll see are mine. I was particularly pleased with the Tributes' logo; those teeth came out just about perfectly.

                        The Tribute Society: The ones who crave your attention

                        "I've done everything for you."

                        Where we came from

                        The Kiss brings euphoria. How could we not want that? Between unrealistic expectations for heteronormative happy endings and puritan cultures that repress our sexualities, there’s massive appeal in the idea of an underworld where unfettered sensuality reigns supreme. Vampires represent everything real life doesn’t: nuance, freedom, and fulfillment. From Christabel to The Giaour, from Carmilla to Dracula, these twisted figures have captivated humanity for generations.

                        To we devoted few, they were more than Penny Dreadfuls or opium-fueled poetry. We felt the truth in these tales as others felt the word of God. In 1730, we formed an epistolary club known as the Tribute Society, and we’ve been operating ever since. In the beginning, it was mostly letters exchanged between a few bored vampirophiles, sharing knowledge of the “Kindred” and their bloody society. Can’t cross running water. Allergic to garlic. Burned by silver crosses — we made sure to purge such deterrents from our lives.

                        Then we wrote pamphlets, took memberships. By 1893, a woman named Angela Chapman became president of the Society. Known best by the pen name Thalaba, she built many of our organizational and structural supports, but — more than that — she got her hands on some genuine blood. The details of this acquisition are somewhat obscure, but however it started, for a fee, you could order a vial of your own and get a little edge in your pursuit. That’s when we Tributes went from observers to active participants.

                        Those initiates who reach the rank of the Unveiled are permitted to consume this precious Vitae and become full-fledged members of our little “family.” There are three official ranks: Veiled (a newcomer), Unveiled (one who has become a true Tribute), and Bitten (one who has been fed upon by a vampire). Tradition says that each Unveiled member takes on a literary hero or heroine’s name during their acceptance ceremony, as well as a Society-approved surname. After all, if you’re willing to come this far into our little club, you know it isn’t just a hobby: It’s a lifestyle.

                        Who we are tonight

                        Pop culture has always had a vampire in the limelight, and weirdos on the fringes have always been tantalized by the idea of some sexy monster sweeping them off their feet. Vampires — or so film and literature paint them — have seen more of the world than any mortal cold fish date ever could, and offer pleasure beyond the wildest imaginings with just a prick of their fangs.

                        We Tributes have grown up longing for that bite, believing with all our might that vampires are real. Knowing it. They’re out there, in the shadows, drinking the blood of the living. And, if so, why not our blood? What makes those others so fucking special?

                        So, we dive down the rabbit hole of message boards, fan theories, slash fic, and counter-culture sex clubs. We work through endless conspiracies, trying to discern what’s true and what’s trash. The challenge is, with so many “clans,” it’s hard to discern the noise from the signal. All we can do is make educated guesses and hope our digging unearths a real vampire.

                        We gather through social media and community centers, and even some progressive churches, sharing our woes with fellow seekers. Sometimes a member shares a lead: a bar purported to be run by a dead man, a location of multiple feedings, photos and paintings of identical faces from across distant decades that must belong to a single individual… or one of their ghouls. Some of us will drop everything just to chase these threads in the vain hope they’ll bear fruit, exposing ourselves to the dangers of the night for the chance some Dead Prince Charming will decide to take us out for a drink.

                        The lucky work our way up the ranks with a patron vampire, but most of us have to do it the hard way. However, if you’ve proven yourself to the organization, Thalaba herself will ship you your own vial of genuine Vitae. There’s a strict regimen recruits need to follow for the “turning” to work: microdoses taken on the hour, consistently, for a month. The change comes over slowly, but you’ll know from the sudden, irrepressible craving for more that you’ve become properly Unveiled.

                        For us, once bitten, twice shy is just admitting defeat. That kind of talk’s not for closers. After one rises to the Unveiled rank, the need for a vampire patron escalates. Maybe Kindred can smell the change in us, because it’s easier to attract a Lick’s attention the higher up the ladder you go — that’s part of the appeal, really. Look hard enough, present yourself on a platter to a blood-sucker, and sooner or later, you’ll get nipped. And the Kiss? It’s everything we dream of and more. A true Tribute will track her first vampire’s every movement, learning everything she can about him, bribing whomever she has to, funneling all her resources into her obsession. She lives, sleeps, and breathes her beloved. Usually she prevails, chumming the water until he can’t resist her. Because if we show them how much we love them, how can they not love us in return?

                        Three Seekers (excerpt)

                        Thalaba Wolfsbane (AKA Angela Chapman), was born in 1871, and it took her thirteen years after beginning her tenure as club president to meet a proper vampire. The tragic Marquise was a Daeva clinging to aristocracy in a world ravaged by the Great War, and she gave Thalaba the idea of creating ranks for her club. Together, they began peddling the Marquise’s Vitae — willingly, allegedly — to Thalaba’s most devoted subscribers. Of course, the Marquise’s true role in this is hotly debated among the Tributes, especially after her untimely torpor some hundred years ago. Gossip aside, Thalaba still can’t really explain how the Society’s methods work, like how the Vitae she ships turns her followers into ghouls, or why it doesn’t seem to form a blood bond to… anyone. The rank and file’s general ignorance of Kindred physiology has helped her dodge more awkward questions, but who even cares? Nothing sinister is going on, according to Thalaba. No one would want a gang of emotionally-stunted snoops keeping constant tabs on vampires. What use would that be?

                        Benefit: Ironically, Tributes are true independent ghouls. The feeling pulls at her like a compass, firing her addiction for more and giving her laser focus. Unless she has a regnant, a Tribute’s starting Discipline options are Celerity, Majesty, and Vigor.

                                                A good Monday to you all!

                                                As promised, let's talk about the Scorned.

                                                This was one that went through a lot of change in development. The concept didn't change too much (i.e., people who get overwhelmed with Majesty and then can feel nothing but pain), but a lot of the assumptions shifted. Originally, there was a scientific component to their suffering, but I pretty much immediately axed that. One thing I try to do with these books is not to ground supernatural phenomena in the real world, and I felt like making it a tangible neurological disorder made the concept too restrictive, and just felt like a space we weren't equipped to wander into in general. Thus, in the final version, they're entirely supernatural.

                                                There were also pretty comprehensive mechanics for how to "produce" Scorned. That wasn't a bad impulse, but in the end, decided to leave it for STs to decide. These characters are always going to be Storyteller characters, and I didn't want to have a system that made the Daeva's signature Discipline into a liability. Partially that was a narrative choice, but I don't think there was a very good middle ground to define such a system mechanically. Either it would happen so often that STs would never want to use it, or it would be so rare that the Scorned could never come up in a reasonable way. Even if there was a happy medium, it felt like a bummer to say that, of all the clans, the Daeva have the Discipline that glitches out the worst. I considered making it something Daeva could potentially do on purpose, but that ultimately felt like it was against the theme of the write-up. So, my compromise was no mechanics (though I did leave a suggestion if people really need them). If you want to introduce the Scorned into a player character's plot, go for it, though make sure that's something they'd be into.

                                                The Scorned: Hell hath no fury

                                                AKA: Ravagers

                                                "You can't hurt me anymore."

                                                Through Majesty, the Daeva do more than enforce reality. They can plum the deepest secrets, sway the unwashed masses, and reap the adoration of fanatical herds. The cruel and uncanny nature of their Discipline perverts a victim’s thoughts and bends their emotions to whatever ends a vampire wishes.

                                                But emotions aren’t as simple as most Serpents believe. They’re fragile mechanisms tied to a patchwork glob of flesh laughably called a mind. Humans aren’t designed to withstand constant and overwhelming desire.

                                                In very rare cases, repeated exposure to Majesty can push a mortal to the breaking point. When the fleeting bliss of the vampire’s affection vanishes, the victim realizes in horror that it took every other feeling with it. Suddenly, she no longer feels pleasure in any form. She’s unable to feel anything at all — except pain. These unfortunates rarely live long, whether taking their own lives or meeting the sharp end of the Masquerade, but a few accept what they’ve become and embrace the only outlet they can. They call themselves the Scorned, and pain is their only pleasure.

                                                Background

                                                Pain isn’t quite the right word for what Scorned experience, but it’s close enough for government work. Does it feel good? Certainly not. But it does feel, and that’s sufficient. Like wanderers dying of thirst in a desert, Ravagers are willing to experience anything — no matter how agonizing — to feel anything at all. This adaptation warps their souls, converting desire to masochism and kindness to sadism. Reborn in agony, Scorned find truth, and in that truth, they find a gospel: Pain must be shared. True joy can only be found in mutual agony. It’s the Golden Rule, after all: Do unto others as you would have them do unto you. So pass that claw hammer; they need to show you how much they care.

                                                Of course, evangelism is only a side concern. Before the Scorned can bear witness, they must take revenge. Most hunt vampires as a fulltime gig, particularly Kindred of Clan Daeva. A Ravager’s first victim is usually the Lick who ruined him, and if he succeeds (or at least survives), it serves as proof positive that he’s taken back the reins of his life.

                                                Not all “scorned” humans take up this philosophy. Indeed, in the rare cases where a Daeva ruins her plaything with Majesty, the poor soul usually spends the rest of his short life on copious amounts of anti-depressants, not some revenge quest. True Scorned are rare, but genuine ones always come around to the above belief system. Some Daeva speculate that an outside force must be at play here, or at least that this ideological prevalence is more than coincidence. However, every Ravager who’s been willing to extol her ethos hasn’t said jack about where she got it. To all appearances, it comes to them apropos of nothing, and if that’s the void Majesty opens in the human soul, then the Daeva may have bigger problems coming over the horizon.

                                                Rumors

                                                “You can cure them. A colleague of mine knows this Night Doctor who took a series of MRIs of one back in the 90s, and the results suggest physiological repair is possible. Their brains are basically riddled with benign, tumorous growths that act as patches over traumatic brain damage localized in the pleasure centers in the anterior cingulate and prefrontal cortexes. In theory, laparoscopic surgery could break up enough of the tumors to allow for stem cell treatments on the damaged tissue. Of course, being as you can’t even anesthetize them, that would be quite the feat.”

                                                A fabrication, but it’s a story that gets passed around from time to time, tagged with varying degrees of neurological accuracy. No one knows the exact mechanism that breaks Ravagers, and few have gotten close enough to find out. Yet the question remains: Can they be cured? Yes, but it’s not something any vampire is going to be down for. The taste of Vitae invigorates the Scorned, offering emotional clarity if consumed in mass quantities. A Ravager is created when a vampire breaks her heart, but all it takes to fix her is returning the favor. If one of them consumes a torpid vampire’s heart, the damage to her soul begins to mend. This healing process isn’t instantaneous, but it’s the only known path to recovery.

                                                    Histrion: The ones who perform

                                                    "But… what if I’m no hero? It might hurt you if you got to know me."

                                                    Where we came from

                                                    The Histrions emerged in Rome’s wake, as the Lancea et Sanctum’s Traditions became Kindred law. For Christianity to outlast the Empire, it needed exemplars, and if the Twelve Apostles couldn’t do it, propaganda would work in a pinch. The first Histrions (literally, “actors”) were drawn from circus troupes and other entertainers, selected for their talents in holding a crowd’s attention. With morality plays and other didactic theater, the dead Church would show the kine how to live up to Christ’s standards, then “quiz” them on their piety. The regnants of these ghouls were mostly Daeva, ones who understood the dividends of high production values even when it comes to faith. They also recognized that their illiterate flocks couldn’t be tested if they didn’t know the rules, and so the Histrions went about making them crystal clear.

                                                    Those Shepherds who brought the most souls to the Lord were blessed with Vitae and power. Actors were often viewed as beyond redemption in these times — little better than whores and thieves — so the Sanctified had few qualms about damning their messengers even as they let them guide the masses to the light. The Gospel from a sinner’s mouth is still the Gospel, and a reprobate is far easier for a vampire to control than a saint.

                                                    Perhaps that was the problem. As successful Histrions lived it up in the lap of luxury, they began to resemble their dead masters, and not just figuratively. The more they were rewarded, the paler their skin became, and the more depraved their decadent tastes. They say imitation is the sincerest form of flattery, and if so, these ghouls were exemplary sycophants. Eventually, little difference remained between the monsters and their servants.

                                                    The name of the first assassinated Histrion is lost, but later written accounts claim it was an ignoble end. During the final performance of a shockingly sexual production of Ordo Virtutum, an audience member stormed the stage and gutted the player portraying Chastity. The assailant proclaimed he’d slain an agent of Hell and urged the crowd to cleanse the remaining taint. A riot ensued.

                                                    This was not to be an isolated incident. The Serpents behind this endeavor became laughingstocks, myopic fools who’d created a bunch of narcissists in their own image. The project was dead.
                                                    However, an actor can never leave the stage until the curtain call. As more of these ghouls fell to violent “fans,” the Serpents saw a brand new angle. Maybe their Shepherds couldn’t make anyone keep the faith, but they sure could provide cover. After all, the difference between a real vampire and a patsy who’s groomed to act like one is academic to the man with the stake. Eventually, these Daeva would turn their blunder into an asset, and even tonight, the Histrions play on. They just don’t realize who their real audience is.

                                                    Who we are tonight

                                                    Histrions are designer ghouls, bespoke insurance policies for paranoid corpses. Modern Shepherds are ethereal beauties with melancholic, pale complexions, subtly guided into filling all the various stereotypes pop culture has bestowed on the living dead. Some Patsies are even altered physically, made to look similar to their regnants or otherwise enhanced to fit certain aesthetic markers. Not understanding the Kindred condition, some are even tricked into believing they are vampires. Histrions are blessed with good looks, resources, and even new passions. The cost, of course, is that nearly all of them wind up dead.

                                                    Placed in large social environs, their unique traits draw both positive and negative attention, luring out stalkers, aspiring hunters, and other undesirables without risking a vampire’s Masquerade or Requiem. By pampering the ghoul and building up her ego, putting her in positions of vague authority becomes a layer of protection. Suddenly all the vampire’s bank accounts are in her name, and all the records list her home address. The entire purpose of a Histrion is to act alone upon the stage, and compared to the eternal servitude of other ghouls, this can be quite a blessing. And if their lives end tragically as they take a firebomb meant for someone else? Well, then they’ve performed very well indeed.

                                                    Most Shepherds fulfill this duty within a few years. Fueled by the ceaseless power fantasies seeded by her regnant, a Histrion rarely realizes she’s a designated meat shield — after all, how could creatures as generous and powerful as the Kindred allow such cruelty? Up to the moment she takes silver blade to the throat, a Shepherd is a pampered, beloved lapdog, and even in dire straits, she’ll play her part to the hilt. She’s special, dammit. No human can dodge a bullet, but a Histrion just might try.

                                                    That doesn’t mean Patsies never piece together their morbid purpose, especially if they survive one of these misguided assassinations. What was once delight in their power turns to ash, and when the ghoul tries to confront her regnant, she’s either struck down for outliving her purpose or cowed by the seductive words of a slave master. Or maybe the event is just tossed in the memory hole, and she’s left to wonder where she got these burns. In any case, many Players wind up justifying these attacks as happenstance, or come to believe their regnants really didn’t know.

                                                    Ultimately, Histrion lives are brief fireworks in an endless night, pretty sparks designed to fizzle away and be forgotten. Just more noise for the Cacophony.

                                                    Benefit: The Histrions exude a natural receptiveness, making them alluring as lovers, confidants, and decoys. In addition, they lose a touch of color in their skin and a bit of volume in their heartbeats, enjoying a +3 on any Expression, Persuasion, or Subterfuge rolls to convince uninitiated mortals (intentionally or not) that they’re “vampires.”

                                                        VLAD: Our little problem

                                                        "This has never happened to me before!"

                                                        AKA: The Withering, KED

                                                        Sometimes stress, anxiety, and life in general mess with your head. The quagmire of living in this world can lead to… things… just not working the way they should. It’s a natural part of the aging process, and it happens to everyone eventually.

                                                        Performance issues aren’t limited to the bedroom, either. Everyone from professional athletics to chess masters knows about the yips and the twisties. Sometimes you just choke. There isn’t a person alive who hasn’t experienced a bout of strange and sudden discombobulation, or just random incompetence related to something they’re good at. It’s all part of being alive — though even that isn’t a given.

                                                        A strange disease has arisen among the Kindred population known as Vampiric Labile Abnormality Disorder (VLAD). Those suffering from the disease find themselves increasingly unable to use their innate abilities: Calling on Disciplines turns into a crapshoot, lashing out becomes arduous, and eventually, even the Kiss doesn’t work.

                                                        Background

                                                        If it’s hard enough to diagnose conditions that only affect a small segment of the human population, and diagnosing creatures as aloof and paranoid as Kindred is nigh impossible. Most vampires refuse to share details of their psychology with anyone, lest they reveal a weakness to their rivals, and even those who are open about such things have little recourse. Sure, there’s always that one Mekhet who attends weird support groups after hours, or who turns their analyst into a ghoul, but these solutions are short term at best. Maybe that’s why VLAD wasn’t properly identified until 2009.

                                                        When Dr. Andrea Heinrich finished her PhD in psychology from Stanford, she was Embraced within a week. Her sire, Maximilian, was an Invictus magnate who wanted to turn her education to his own ends, modernizing his company’s glitzy ad campaigns to keep consumers ignorant of the vampire selling to them. It was demeaning, demoralizing work, but Andrea had little choice. Max was an intimidating, tyrannical, seemingly all-powerful elder — until one night, he wasn’t.

                                                        Max’s Disciplines first failed him at a First Estate soirée during an attempt to victimize a ghoul. Instead of being cowed, the unphased minion shot back a humiliating retort that filled the gathering with mocking laughter, directed at the near-frenzied Max. From there, his condition continued to decline until he was reduced to hiding away in his haven and feeding from blood bags his servants stole from the hospital.

                                                        Max needed help, and Andrea was happy to get back to her roots. After months of grueling therapy and study, she developed a treatment plan that incorporated clinical behaviorism, exposure therapy, and a hefty dose of Dominate. And it worked: Max was cured. Of course, in his desperation, he’d allowed himself to be blood bound to his childe, and she wasn’t above slipping in a few self-serving mental triggers and memory alterations.

                                                        With her sire’s fortune at her disposal, Andrea has created a foundation for extra-normal research, and its first focus is her sire’s former disorder. It was Dr. Heinrich who came up with the acronym, though she was tempted to name it after Max.

                                                        Infection

                                                        VLAD is as much virus as psychological disorder. Bizarrely, it doesn’t pass through any physical means, though it appears in a Kindred victim’s blood like any other pathogen. The disease instead spreads when a vampire witnesses a carrier strike out using one of her Disciplines, or any other deathly power.
                                                        While it doesn’t move through shared vectors like Vitae, it does have an easier time passing among vampires who share blood ties. Kindred of the same clan have a much better chance of catching it from each other, and the closer one’s relation to another vampire, the more difficult it is to resist

                                                        Systems

                                                        Whenever a vampire witnesses another bloodsucker exhibit an obvious symptom of VLAD, such as failing a Discipline or flubbing his Predatory Aura, she must roll Resolve + Composure minus her own Blood Potency. On a failure, she gains the Shaken Condition, fearful that something like that could happen to her. This roll is further penalized by her degree of Blood Sympathy to the other vampire (Vampire, p. 98), but takes a +3 if the characters are of different clans. If the vampire suffers a failure on any Discipline or attempt to lash out before she resolves Shaken, it becomes the VLAD (Stage One) Condition.
