        I probably missed it... or I'm just misremembering something, but wasn't there a character in Chicago who basically felt that the Circle's ideology was to stab people in the back because "trials make you better"? Can't recall at all how much that tied back into core Circle dogma, but there is that whole ultra-Darwinian (if not Randian) undercurrent.

        By way of contrast, the LeS/LS are (presented) as a Church, and part of their typical practice is helping new vampires transition "into the night". Superficially/generically, the Church offers greater support network for the faithful.

        Granted, any particular instantiation of either covenant could likely turn those aspects around. Plus, the once-comforting support network might wind up forcing betrayal of scruples - and woe to the one who the group believes refused to properly give back. (etc., etc.)

        My 2 cents on the main topic? A lot of people who are attracted to this genre harbor negative views of Christianity - and either downplay or disbelieve the positive aspects. For various reasons (exposure via media scandals, resident hypocrites and/or not living in an area that really needs much 'help' from churches), it's easy to automatically assume that a Christian is a hypocrite - that the LeS/LS are just self-righteous whining jerks. In contrast, these biases are flipped for the Circle. The negatives are underplayed - possibly in part because the faiths which would be embraced/corrupted by the Circle don't have as much exposure in our culture - except as victims of Christian overreach. (Particularly in the US, with the whole freedom of religion thing that is constantly under siege...)

        "Chronicles of Darkness" games insinuate that we should paint darker shadows around the games' institutions - and we have so many examples of hypocritical perversions of Christian principles (thanks to history, books and current events, etc.) that it is so easy to just paint the LeS/LS as irredeemable. But, I feel like that's a bit myopic - after all, the heroes of the CoC are Loki and Non-Sorbo-Hercules; but the mythic heroes of the LeS/LS can include Galahad and St. George. Personally, I feel like something is lost if one approaches a covenant as if all members were just hypocrites - as if there were only shadows, and no light. Because I think the thematic horror is made more meaningful when, instead, that light is embraced - and then twisted.

        Wow. That wasn't so much a run-on ramble as a train wreck of language. Sorry for the ... well, that. I'd fix it up, but must work. And/or sleep.

            For me it comes down to a freedom vs restriction, both in character and in making characters.

            Crones are really a catch all covenant, which means you can get some really diverse and interesting characters and cruac styles coexisting. In my current game, we have a sect of very clinical, almost scientific cruac users and another sect who are more traditional wild and sacrificial cruac. Every individual has their own spin on it, very easy to get really cool themes going.

            Lancea on the otherhand is strict and unbending. There is room for some individualism, but it still feels constraining when designing a Lancea character.

            Not saying Lancea doesn't have its place, just that for PC's, not exactly the most tempting of choices.
