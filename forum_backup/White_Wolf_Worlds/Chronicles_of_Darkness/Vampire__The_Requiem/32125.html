                                                  Originally posted by Rani Neferet View Post

                                                  Those are official 2e versions. Not fan-made. Hopefully there will be a book with them before too long.

                                                  Had another look at it. I'm not happy with the idea that they are Strix beatsticks, but then, I'm not a big fan of the Strix. They were really interesting in Requiem for Rome, but after that, they get worse and worse, as the idea is seized upon, taking them from being the reason one of the Clans was created (and destroyed,) to one that hates vampires, but is secretly trying to become them as well. It starts to muddy the waters of the idea that each Clan has a separate origin and creation. If it wee a case that they were haunting the Ventrue, saying to them that they are the children of their fruits, that they are the founders of their clans... then you have something interesting right there, and something that works. Are they really the creators, or just saying it for their own goals? And if they are lying... what are their reasons for doing so? If they are telling the truth, why emerge now? And what are they wanting from the Ventrue... and can they afford to refuse any demands from them?

                                                  But enough of my issues with the Strix... The Khaibit have an extra layer to them now. If you have other monsters and supernatural threats in the City, then they might be able to fight them off. They then have a purpose for existing, and that would strengthen the bloodline.This is something they lacked in the original write-up, a sense of direction and purpose. And they make sense as bodyguards and protectors, and I feel there is a huge amount that can be done with them, untapped potential. How do they recruit their childer? Is it just an Embrace, or is there a period of training, moulding and shaping their minds and bodies to fulfill the potential their would-be Sires saw in them originally? Maybe she is just an average looking, plain office worker who is in a dead end, and not really going anywhere... but if she is taken, Ghouled and made to exercise and train... after a year she is a honed weapon, her mind looking at everyone as a potential threat, assessing the people around. Are they threats? Are they unwitting hindrances to the mission her Sire-to-be gave her? If she fails, would he punish her... and how? Spending the night in an bathtub filled with ice cubes, whilst reciting a mantra over and over, having to ignore the freezing temperature? Or maybe it is something else, more... inhuman? And after her years of training is over, and she realises she is nothing like the plain office worker she once was, does she look in horror at what she has become... or does she feel pride and accomplishment, and pleasure that her Mentor saw the spark of potential in her, and he saw fit to turn it into a fire?

                                                  So much is there, and what we have is just the tip of the iceberg. Some bloodlines are only really going to need ten or so pages to flesh them out. The Khaibit are a bloodline that could have an entire book dedicated to them, and you would find that there were still plenty of questions to ask after reading it. I suspect this is why they are so popular, and one of my favourite things from Requiem.

                                                  Then there is Obtenebration... It goes int "hunt the Strix" mode a bit, and we all know by now what my sentiments on them are by now. That said, you can rework them to get rid of those elements... and they have cracked the nut. If you look back to VTM, and see the Lasombra's version, you see that it was something that could be deeply frightening and powerful... but was quite clunky. V20 has improved it quite a bit... but you end up feeling that there is something missing from the equation. They have found the answer, and have given a brilliant framework. Why only five Devotion powers, though? There could be lots more! Like turning a person's shadow into a spy, going around and gathering information on those being observed... as an example.

                                                  In short, there is a lot given to us, but also a lot more potential unrealised.

                                                  Just my take on it.

They don't "want to be" vampires, they believe that vampires are just like them, but with physical bodies of their own. And yet, the vampires deny this fact. Instead of indulging in all the debaucheries afforded by physical form, they just act as much like regular humans as possible. That's why the Strix hate them, for being a waste of potential. They're The Joker, they want to prove that deep down everyone (or every vampire, anyway) is just one bad day away from being just as monstrous as they are.
