    Fanmade Dark Era: Liberté, Égalité, Vitae

    02-17-2015, 11:20 AM
    I've been inspired the be work on Dark Eras to produce my own, and I'll be starting a game set in the period next week.

    You can find the PDF attached, but here is the full text for those who prefer to read it in browser:
    Libertié •Égalitié •Vitae

    A city on the edge of chaos, Paris is a capital of squalor and suffering as the 18th Century draws to a close. While for hundreds of years the regular procession of plagues kept the peasant population in check there has been no great sickness in generations, and the numbers of the underclass swells in the city. Now Paris is home to almost 650,000 people, and only a bare ten thousand are of the first and second estates, the clergy and the nobility. The rest call the third their home and make up the peasantry. As the population has swollen the balance of production and consumption has become ever more unstable and dangerous, the poor roads and tracks making it impossible to import grain to the city at anything other than extortionate prices, and so the populace relies totally on the agriculture of the city surrounds themselves.
    Only the regulation of bread prices keeps the city from starving and even then, when a bad harvest occurs, the peasantry riots nearly tearing the city apart, lynching bakers, stealing bread and burning down buildings that have stood for half a millennium. The clergy and nobility pay no taxes and take all that they want, and are impeachable in the courts of the land. The young king and queen, Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette, luxuriate in the palace of Versailles far from the heavy masses, totally isolated from the pain and suffering of the lower classes. King Louis, though most incompetent, refuses to appoint important ministerial figures claiming his right to rule the country directly and believing that it is his duty to do so.
    His father, and his father's father, wasted the riches of France on a series of petty wars and disputes, the lands and monies of France slowly eaten away. The rich have gotten richer, and the poor poorer as they are squeezed more and more to pay for the excesses of the first and second estates. The people call for a three house parliament to represent the common man, but such pleas are ignored. France teeters on bankruptcy, a matter which is not helped by Marie Antoinette's lavish spending, and the vast sums Louis sends to aid the American Wars of Independence.
    It is June 1787, and the hungry gap is beginning, when the harvests are yet to be brought in but the grain stores are running low. Bread is scarce and the city boils and fumes in the heat and the poverty, a powder keg that somehow never explodes.
    As above, so below.
    The three estates of the dead struggle against each other just as the kine do. The nobility of the night and the followers of Longinus claim the first and second estates, with the rest relegated to the third. Those kindred who are not embraced into noble lines, or who cannot worm their way into the church, are left to rot, forced to pay the blood taxes of the nobles and given nothing in return. A bare handful of bloodlines hold all the power and the rest scramble to just survive.
    As the kine population bloats as do the leeches that feed from them, hundreds of the dead hanging like parasites from the city. With so many deaths through crime, starvation and a thousand other evils, the peasantry of the city are a never-ending buffet for the kindred whose predations are always overlooked. Yet fury and resentment ferments amongst the downtrodden masses of the dead, just as they do amongst the living. The kindred might have blood aplenty but they lack safety in a city which burns on a yearly basis, and which is ripped apart by riots almost monthly. They look at the sturdy chambers and manors of their betters and then plot amongst themselves.
    Just as the pamphleteers of the mortal world fuel the ever-encroaching revolution, so do the radicals of the dead urge their fellows to action. The name Emmanuel Baptiste Carth is upon the lips of the many, and soon they will be bound together into a great movement whose actions can only result in blood and fire. Theme and Mood

    The theme is Divided Loyalties. Pulled in different directions by loyalty to one’s Clan, Covenant and place the social hierarchy of Paris, all kindred must face up to the fact that no choice is simple, and no choice does not betray someone.
    The mood is Pushing Back against Tyranny. The city’s mortals are wracked by constant riots and uprisings, and though the dead must hide their fury more carefully it is there nonetheless. Every collection of corvée, every judgement of the Prince, and every excess of the nobility makes that anger deeper, harsher and burn fiercer. What Has Come Before

    Paris has stood for more than two thousand years in one form or another and the kindred have been here through every one of those years. Their history is complex and closely intertwined with the lives of the mortals they rely on. Raised from Nothing: Roman Lutetia (52BC – 486AD)

    There has been a settlement on the banks of the Seine for longer than mankind remembers, and one it was the home of the Parisii who ruled the lands they settled before the birth of Christ. The true history of the city begins, though, when the Romans came and made it their own, naming it Lutetia. Vercingetorix, the most famous of the Gallic chiefs, was defeated and Titus Labienus claimed the city for his own. They rebuilt it, the streets aligning to a regular grid in the style of Roman settlements further south, and it was soon an important place of the occupying forces. Where the living go, of course, the dead are soon to follow.
    The Legio Mortuum of the Camarilla were the first kindred to arrive according to the legends of the dead, an extension of the will of Senex who ruled the whole world of the dead. The Legion found the native vampires, the rough barbarian tribesmen, to be amenable to their rule, and it was not long before they made their way back along the trade routes to the city where all roads lead. For Lutetia it was a time of prosperity both for kindred and kine as the military outpost became a hub for trade and then a place in its own right. The amphitheatre on the outskirts served mortal entertainments during the day, but hosted kindred gatherings at night. It seemed the power of the Camarilla could not wane, and neither could its living child Rome.
    But all things change. Whisper of troubles back in the eternal city began to reach the ears of Roman kindred in Gaul, and soon a steady stream left the security of the small settlement and headed back to Rome to gather allies and shore up old debts. The living were the same and the power of Rome was fading, soon to fall away completely as the native peoples asserted themselves once more. By the late 4th century Rome was no longer the power it was and the city was renamed Paris, taking its name from the people who once lived there. The Julii were dead and the kindred of Gaul once more ruled themselves. Glory of Charlemagne: The Early Christian Period (AD 509-885)

    Time passed and the remains of the old empire began to reform and rebuild and Paris once again rose to prominence when Clovis the Frank, the first kind of the Merovingian dynasty, made the city his capital. The native Gangrel were no longer alone in the city by now, joined the Ventrue who had travelled from the east, and the worm Nosferatu who had clambered out of their dens in the soil and stood blinking in the light of the moon with their fellows. The city had a feeling of growth, that great things awaited it, and the kindred were no different. The first Prince of Paris rose to power and ruled with the support not only of the burgeoning Invictus, but also the Lancea et Sanctum whose faith had spread north in recent decades.
    The kings of the Franks ruled well for the most part, but Paris remained small and constrained to the islands that were the best form of fortification against invades available. Slowly work began upon building a greater place to live, but they came to little until Childeric III was deposed by the Pope and Pepin the Short was made king, the first of the Carolingians whose greatest scion was yet to arise.
    Under Charlemagne the lands were united, his armies conquering great swathes of territory and though Paris remained important it waned in influence as the emperor moved his capital to Aachen. The kindred looked upon his works and were jealous, and worked even harder to dominate their fellows, their influence spreading out from their traditional holdings across all of northern Europe. They were joined for the first time by the Daeva who hailed from the south and east, and the first noble lines of Paris were founded, tradition dictating that each new Prince would be drawn from one of these noble families.
    The death of Charlemagne led to a time of fading fortunes as the great empire that had been claimed collapsed. The influence and links the kindred had made to much effort to establish began to falter and within decades the influence of the Invictus was once more localised and isolated. The kindred of Paris withdrew, consumed by petty squabbles and infighting, and for kine and kindred alike the dark ages closed in. The Third Race of Kings: The Rule of the House of Capet (AD 987 – 1328)

    After many years of confusion and conflict a new King of the Franks rose to power, and the House of Capet became the dominant noble force in France. This “third race of kings” returned power to Paris and the city swelled with people and wealth, soon becoming the most prosperous city in the nation. The kindred’s numbers grew as well and soon a full and formal court was established, and the modern noble lines emerged: the Rome, the Fortier, the Chastain and the Lapointe. By the end of the 12th century Paris has become a city of wonders and lights.
    Notre Dame Cathedral was built and the University of Paris established as the power of the city grew, and it became a centre of philosophical and theological learning. Commerce and finance blossomed and money flowed into the city with great speed and the league of river traders was formed to control the transit of goods. The Louvre was built and the golden age of Paris began.
    The kindred flourished too and by the beginning of the 14th century nearly a hundred vampires made their home in the city, feeding off the swelling ranks of mortals and their influence in kine affairs growing year on year. France became not only a great nation of thinkers and merchants but one of soldiers and sailors, and the kindred spread out with the armies of France taking their model of stable society with them. Many courts of Europe came to follow the model of Paris, with a handful of noble lines maintaining control over a population, aided by the church of Longinus. Plague and War: The Period of Uncertainty (AD 1348 – 1594)

    But not all was smooth and easy for the city, and when the Hundred Years was erupted the city was captured by the English and Burgundians. With religious tensions increasingly troublesome France was wracked with strife, and even when the city was reclaimed almost a century later it was still torn apart by sectarian violence. The kindred could not escape the intrusion of Protestantism either, and as it became every more dominant amongst the kine the church of Longinus was forced to respond. Religious strife weakened the rule of the nobles but they managed to cling to power, trying to ride a wave of discontent that fractured alliances of all kinds.
    France was literally torn apart by the conflict but Paris remained a stronghold of Catholicism, and the St Bartholomew’s Day massacre where thousands of Protestants were killed was a sign of the devotion not only of Catholics but also of the traditionalist wing of the Lancea et Sanctum, their internal conflict spilling out in the lives of mortals as so often was the case. Slowly the city began to rebuild and the kindred reunify and by the time Henri IV converted to Catholicism and ended the wars of religions, the dead had already resolved their own issues. The traditionalists and the Catholics had won, and Paris was whole once more. Palaces and Parlours: The Years of Excess (AD 1671 – 1774)

    With France healing the kings were determined to show that the nation was still proud and unbroken, and worked hard to build up their capital as a symbol of their strength. The city was remodelled into something more beautiful than ever before and statues, academies and theatres all sprung up in great numbers. The dead could not avoid such a change and embraced the aesthetic spirit of the city, becoming great patrons of the arts and sciences and investing their own influence to make the city ever greater. L’Academie du Serpent was established at this time and became known throughout the courts of Europe as a centre for kindred learning.
    With the great projects of the age draining the coffers of France the nation began to find itself relying more and more upon their status as traders and soldiers, and soon their colonial efforts bore fruit. Their holdings overseas, however, were a drain on the finances more than a benefit, with the nobility taking the profits of such ventures for themselves. Paris itself became a place of imbalance and division as the population grew at a phenomenal rate and the city expanded with great speed, but the working classes became poorer and poorer. Despite being the financial capital of Europe the city housed huge numbers of suffering peasants, their poverty crushing them and never letting them better themselves. Louis and Marie: Recent Years and the Rule of the King (AD 1774 – 1787)

    The ascension of Louis XVI to the throne was a turn for the worse again. Crowned at the age of 19 and seen as young, inexperienced and timid by both the nobility and the populace in general, his was a rule that encountered difficulty from the very beginning. Even before he became king he had been unpopular, in part due to his difficult marriage to Marie Antoinette, who was seen as a puppet of Austrian influence, and their inability to consummate their marriage or produce children.
    Despite this things seemed a little brighter when he did become king as he began to push for reform and move towards enlightenment ideals, something that resonated well with both the impoverished lower classes and the ambitious middle classes. However, his proposed reforms caused fury amongst the nobility and he was forced to drop them, something seen as a betrayal by the people and as a sign of his weakness as a ruler by his political opponents.
    Determined to make a name for himself, Louis sunk much of the nation’s remaining finances into supporting the North American colonists in their attempts to win independence from Great Britain, which made the country popular in the new world but prompted a disastrous financial collapse. This combined with his indecisiveness and inability to push reform led to the people of France increasingly seeming him as a symbol of the tyranny of the Ancien Régime, the social structure of the Estates that led to the poor having few to no rights at all.
    Despite the upheaval of in the mortal world, the kindred of Paris were little affected by these excesses and the unpopularity of the royalty. With the population of Paris growing ever larger the kindred found themselves in possession of a seemingly limitless supply of blood, and the increasing chaos of the city and discontent of the poor served as a perfect aide to the masquerade. However, this increase in resources has led to a much larger kindred population, and in response a much more unequal society amongst the dead. The younger kindred, almost all of whom are relegated to the Third Estate, railed against their lack of power and the increasingly harsh corvée demanded by their regents. Motivated by mortal ideas of political change and enlightenment concepts of equality and justice, they began to form loose alliances of kindred radicals looking to change their own government just as the mortals long for change themselves. The brutality and sometimes inept leadership of Le Requin did not help matters during this time. What is to Come

    With the storming of the Bastille only two years away, France hangs on the edge of a precipice. Though Louis XIV is hugely unpopular and the country is in turmoil, enough power remains entrenched with the nobility for them to hang on for some time yet. The populace retains some hope yet that things will improve, however, as comptroller Charles Alexandre de Calonne is in the process of proposing tax reforms that would ease the burden on the poor and introduce some equality to the system. It won’t be long, however, before the nobility and the church begin to fight back, and action which will eventually result in the summoning of the Assembly of Notables. When they refuse to act to resolve the crisis the King will call the Estates-General, a sign that the monarchy was losing control.
    The meeting of the Estates-General in 1789 would fall into chaos quickly – using procedural tricks to tie up the much larger Third Estate the First and Second Estates took control of the meeting. In response the Third Estate will declare themselves the National Assembly and began to attempt to conduct the nation’s affairs with or without the clergy and the nobility. The King would attempt to shut down the assembly but this only strengthened their resolve, and soon riots would break out across France. In Paris the mobs stormed the Bastille, a symbol of oppression, and the revolution would truly begin.
    With unrest only growing and peasant uprisings common, change would come quickly – the national assembly will reject feudalism, reduce the power of the church and nobility and attempt to force the monarchy to become constitutional. Fleeing Paris and Versailles only highlights in the eyes of the people the guilt of the King, and he is arrested. Though for months the assembly argues back and forth about what to do with him, they eventually disbanded the monarchy and established a republic. A year later, in 1793, he will be executed for conspiracy against the public safety. War breaks out between France and England, and counter-revolutions are attempted. The reign of terror will grip the country under the guidance of Robespierre, before he is arrested and executed by his opponents.
    The country will stutter and struggle for ten years of revolution as decrees are passed and the fortunes of the nation rise and fall. In 1799 Napoleon Bonaparte will stage a coup and install the consulate, before becoming dictator and then emperor. And France will never be the same again.
    For the kindred the years are as bleak. With chaos and terror gripping Paris their unending food supply turns into a terrible threat, and fires and battles destroy havens and risk the masquerade. The kindred struggle between themselves, but if the radicals manage to overcome those who dominate them as the mortals do… that is lost in the mists. Paris Tonight

    Paris is a large and complex city with many unique or unusual institutions. Though the kindred share many of their political and social structures with other domains, a number are either unique to Paris or have special significance in the City of Lights. Noble and Common Lines

    Though clan is still of importance to many in the city the real division is by bloodline – the direct lineage of a kindred and who their sire and grandsire were. Those who are unaware of their lineage are considered to not just be lesser than other kindred, but verging on monstrous, as a sense of one’s place in society is imparted by the vitae that flows in their veins. These families of the dead can be divided into noble and common lines depending on their historical status in the city, but exactly why any particular line is considered noble and another base has been forgotten for centuries.
    The noble lines have distinct rights that the commoners do not, and it is an important facet of the city’s politics. Only those of the noble bloodlines can control territory in the city and have tenants, meaning that they are the sole beneficiaries of corvée and have the rights of the landlord of their tenants. Nobles are perfectly within their rights to use disciplines on common kindred, but not each other.
    Commoners find their rights much more restrictive. Not only must they have a landlord and thus pay rent, and are banned from owning territory outright, they may not move between territories or relocate their havens without permission. Travelling to visit others in the city is frowned upon though many still do so via the necropolis. Commoners must petition their landlord for the right to embrace, and in theory to create ghouls (though this is often overlooked in practice). For a commoner to use a discipline on a noble kindred is a breach of the law of the city and punished harshly if discovered. It is also illegal for a commoner to blood bond a noble kindred, and if this is discovered the commoner is usually placed in torpor or executed for the slight.
    Put together these restrictions mean that the noble lines have almost total power over the commoners in their territories, and this causes considerable strain. The driving force between much of the tension of the city is caused by the discrepancy in rights between noble and common kindred. Districts and Barons

    The city of Paris is divided up into a number of districts, each of which is controlled by one of the noble bloodlines and is made up of a number of territories ruled directly by a member of that bloodline. By convention only members of the Second Estate own territory, but there is at least one exception to this (as the Bishop Chastain has his own territory and has tenants living upon it). Each district is ruled by a regent (a senior member of a noble line) but they can in turn parcel out smaller territories to their underlings to rule as their own (and often owe them corvée in turn).
    Those kindred who own territory are known as Barons or Propriétaire (“Landlords”) and have certain rights of their tenants. The most important is, of course, the collection of corvée, but they also have the right to request their aid in times of crisis or danger and have them report any possible masquerade breaches within their territory. In return they are supposed to assist their tenants with any threats to the city, and protect them from interlopers and poachers (how well they actually do this is up for debate).
    Île de la Cité is one of the two islands at the heart of the city, and is the very oldest part of the city. It is ruled by the Chastain, and the regent is Bishop Chastain. The Carmichael number amongst their tenants and the relationship between the two is tense. The territory is home to La Sainte-Chapelle (a gothic chapel which houses part of the crown of thorns), La Conciergerie (a royal palace and prison), and Notre-Dame de Paris (an ancient cathedral of some note).
    Île Saint-Louis is the other island in the Seine, and is primarily residential, and the richest area of the city by some way. It is territory claimed by the Rome, and the regent here is Amaury Rome, the eldest kindred of the dynasty. It is home to the Sarkozi brood, who are the most loyal servants of the Rome. It has no major landmarks, but instead is home to many elegant manors and houses.
    La Rive Droite is a large territory covering much of the city to the north of the Seine, and contains much of the more substantial residential districts and a large number of the historic landmarks of the city, and much of the royal presence in the city. Its regent is Georges Fortier and the tenants include the other wing of the Carmichael, and there are strong and amicable bonds between the two families - it is one of the few territories where the tenants can count on the support of their lords. It includes the Royal Library of Paris, the Louvre Palace, the Hôtel de Bourgogne, and Temple (a fortress once run by the Knights Templar).
    La Rive Gauche is the largest territory within the city itself, and covers almost the entire south side of the river. It is by far the poorest area of the city and is rife with crime that makes it very difficult. It is also the locations of the majority of the slums of the city, and it houses a huge kine population. As such, though it is hardly elegant, it is prime territory, and the prize holding of the Rome family - its regent is Comtesse Giselle Rome, and its tenants include the Moreau brood and the fallen Lapointe.
    Quartier Latin is a small but select district which surrounds the University of Paris, and is named for the fact that Latin is very commonly heard throughout the territory. Its major landmark is the University itself, which dominates the territory. The Quartier Latin is a strange territory for it falls between the control of two different regents and is ruled instead by the Courtmanche (who are not noble). This strange land of outlawry is allowed to continue both because of the difficulty of enforcing the law on the Comte Courtmanche, but also because of the usefulness of L’Academie du Serpent.
    Champs-Élysées is the newest area of the city, built around the comparatively recently laid down road that bears its name. It is a lively and bohemian district, known for its theatres and cafes, and for its rowdy atmosphere. The regent of the district is Ethan Fortier who is relatively convivial with his rule, and his tenants include the Roux brood, which makes for an exciting if unpredictable mix.
    Les Marais is a small residential district located to the north of the river, and on the edge of the city. It is traditionally the residence of the aristocracy and contains a plethora of churches and mansions. Its regent was once Christophe Lapointe but since his execution has fallen into the hands of Anna Chastain. The Favager are among the tenants of the territory, though exactly how many dwell there is uncertain, and Anna has found trying to extract service from them infuriatingly difficult.
    Versailles is the largest territory in the whole domain, for it is not truly part of the city - instead it covers all of the countryside around the city, the small towns and villages therein, and of course Versailles itself. It is ruled directly by the Prince himself, and no common kindred are allowed within, the Prince taking corvée instead from the nobility. The court itself is the most prominent landmark in the territory, and all that really matters to its lord. Corvée and Laisse

    The Second and First Estates maintain their rule through the collection of corvée, or service. Corvée is collected on an annual basis (usually at a fixed time in the year, but technical a baron can request it at any time if it has not yet been collected that year) by barons from those of the Third Estate who live in their territory, a sort of “rent” which all of the peasantry are obliged to pay to their lords. This tribute can be collected in a variety of different ways, with the baron who is collecting it dictating the form it takes. According to the law of the city, corvée must be collected as one of the following:
    Travail pour le sang (“Labour for the Blood”), which is any service which calls upon the vassal to collect vessels for the lord to feed from, or which requires them to clean up after a lord’s feeding. This is rarely used by the nobility for the purpose of sustenance, but rather as a way to get access to important or influential mortals in order to blood bond or dominate them, and also as a way to have masquerade breaches or threats dealt with. As it is the fault of their vassal if a masquerade breach occurs due to them not “cleaning up” well enough after their excesses, this is often used to shift blame away from a sloppy aristocrat and onto the commoners in his territory.
    Travail pour la puissance (“Labour for the Power”), which his any service which calls upon the vassal to behave according to his low position. This can include acting as things like a messenger, porter, or a valet, or being loaned to another lord in any capacity, and is in effect indentured labour. Some nobles are lenient with the use of this form of corvée, or deign not to use it at all, but others keep their tenants in almost year-round servitude by requiring their labour in perpetuity.
    Corvée sanglant (“Blood Service”), which is any service or act which is humiliating, gross, or foul, whether or not it involves blood. This is such a broad category of activity that it can be twisted by a suitably motivated noble to mean almost anything they wish. However, common activities include violence (including murder of mortals), information gathering in unpleasant places or even just cleaning up after mortals or animals. It is used most often as a punishment with the intention of cowing the commoner into behaving better in future.
    Loyer (“Rent”) is the final form in which corvée can be collected, and involves a regular payment of some kind made to the vassal’s lord. It usually takes the form of goods or money (and this can be an excellent revenue stream for the nobility if their vassals are wealthy), but can also be less physical things, such as information, knowledge or tuition.
    This system of taxation is the most common form of exchange within the city, but it does not handle exchanges between kindred of an equal social standing, or those who wish to take additional services from their vassals beyond their due corvée. In such circumstances, the standard transaction is in Laisse (or “Leash”, as one is leashed to the debt). Laisse are official favours owed between kindred which must be recorded in the presence of a court official, who records it in the city’s books, or Comptes. Laisse which are not registered are not law abiding, but most members of the Third Estate choose not to record them anyway and are generally very hesitant about not paying their debts. Laisse can vary greatly in value and can be kept for any length of time before being called in. Refusing to pay an officially recorded Laisse is a crime against the city, though it should be noted that this law is applied considerably more harshly to members of the Third Estate than the First or Second. There are four types of Laisse: Banal, Mineur, Majeur, and Fatal.
    Laisse Banal (or “Trivial) are the meanest sort of favour, and take only a small amount of invested time and effort, such as a loan of equipment, or an introduction to another.
    Laisse Mineur (or “Minor”) are actually of considerable note, and represent a whole night's effort and work to fulfil, or lesser effort over a full month. Examples might include supporting another's political goals, a lesson in a common power of the blood, or protecting someone's possessions for a period. One Laisse Mineur is worth three Laisse Banal.
    Laisse Majeur (or “Major”) are considerable debts which a massive effort and potentially considerable danger to pay off. It can call on a kindred's service for a full year, and might include adopting a kindred into your bloodline, giving up a valuable retainer, or accepting a partial blood bond to another. A Laisse Majeur is worth three Laisse Mineur.
    Laisse Fatal (or “Life”) are rarely exchanged, and cannot be imposed on another - it can only be taken on voluntarily. It is the supreme show of devotion and debt, and can be called upon to do almost anything, including sacrificing oneself. You cannot trade lesser laisse for a Laisse Fatal, or a Laisse Fatal for other types.
    Laisse can be exchanged and traded freely, with a set exchange rate. All such transactions must again be recorded with the court to be legally binding, but trades of Laisse Banal are very common without any records being taken. Some bloodlines make secondary records of Laisse to ensure they are kept. The Clans

    Blood and lineage is of great importance to the kindred of Paris and as such the clans make great efforts to work in a unified fashion. Despite this it is truly the bloodlines and lineages themselves that divide kindred more than actual clan, and as such few embrace the clan as a political entity. No clan has agreed upon a priscus for almost four hundred years and that seems unlikely to change in the near future. Instead the patriarchs and matriarchs of a clan come together to discuss matters when it is necessary. The Ventrue

    Dominated by the two noble dynasties of the city, the Ventrue are a powerful clan but a divided one. The Rome and the Fortier rule supreme wielding a disproportionate amount of power considering their small numbers, and some Ventrue take pride in this victory. Others are jealous and grasping, and some few in their desperation have even turned to the radicals – after all, winning is all that matters, not how you go about achieving your victory.
    The Rome are the most powerful dynasty in Paris, if not all of France, as they count amongst their number Le Requin himself, Henri Rome. What is more, the prince is not even the patriarch of their line, and Amaury Rome remains a potent if mysterious figure in city politics, shepherding his childe through the travails of political office. The line is very small, barely a dozen members in total, but a full third of them are elders with at least three centuries experience, and the dynasty actually manages to cooperate with remarkable efficiency. As a combination this is devastating, and it is perhaps no surprise that the Rome have a stranglehold on the city. Though they have extensive territories in the city they also favour the town of Versailles for its royal connections. Those not of noble blood are banned from entering the town, and of course that is where Henri holds his courts. The Rome family have considerable influence in the kine courts at Versailles and across the mortal nobility more generally giving them tremendous influence. Some even whisper that they have ties to the King and Queen themselves, though such influence would surely be skirting the limits of the masquerade.
    The other noble line in the city are the Fortier who can trace their lineage back six full generations, making them one of the oldest dynasties in the city. Their founder claims that he was embraced by a kindred of Rome, which would make them direct blood relatives of the Dead Julii – the truth of the matter is up for debate, but few dare to reject the notion in hearing of the Fortier themselves.       The Estates

      The real division in Paris come in the form of the Estates. Le Requin is the ruler of the city and is considered most prominent, but below him are the Estates. The First Estate, the clergy, is the next most prominent as they are God’s representatives. Below them are the Second Estate, the nobility, who exert the secular power of Le Requin. At the bottom is the Third Estate, the peasantry of Paris, and those who give service to their betters. The First Estate (The Church)

      As the mortal clergy are ordained by God, so the damned have the ranks of those who are God’s hand upon the earth. Behind dripping candles and fogs of incense smoke these monsters lurk, powerful beyond measure and bathing in the blood of those around them. They sometimes refer to themselves as the Lancea et Sanctum, and certainly they would be considered such by kindred from other cities, but in truth they are their own beasts unique to Paris alone. They are known simply as le clergé, or première état, but they divide themselves further amongst their ranks into élevé (the high clergy) and faible (the low clergy). The high clergy are those drawn from the families of the second estate (noble bloodlines) and only they may rise to the rank of bishop (with one rather notable exception) whilst the low clergy are drawn from the lines of the peasantry and make up the priests, monks, nuns and friars of the city.
      Le clergé are the smallest of the estates and have no more than two dozen members of which only a handful are of the high clergy. Despite this they remain hugely powerful and influential, and hundreds of kindred attend their midnight masses with regularity and devotion. They tend to the religious needs of their congregations, devote themselves to the teachings of Longinus, and test the mortal herd as is their duty. Traditional and, one might say, catholic, they are a brutal hard kernel around which the rest of the city forms. Though they have less overt power than the nobility, they have the ear of the Second Estate and of the Prince, and as such cannot be underestimated. The First Estate does not traditionally receive corvée as they do not acts a Barons, but instead receive a tithe from the Second Estate as a sign of respect.
      The First Estate is led by Évêque (Bishop) Chastain who is the formal advisor of the First Estate to the Prince.
      Members of the First Estate may learn Theban Sorcery with the storyteller’s approval, though doing so can attract the ire of the Bishop. They are also considered members of the Lancea et Sanctum for the purposes of qualifying for merits, etc. Sects

      The old guard rule almost supreme in Paris, and as such the members who identify as Catholique dominate the estate. Though many were indeed Roman Catholics in life, the term does not refer to the Church of Holy Rome (which is, of course, superseded by the Temple and Spear in death) but instead uses the word to indicate a continuity of faith and practice, drawing on the rituals and observances of the earliest days of their order. They indeed trace their lineage to Rome, claiming to have established themselves in the city during its time as Lutetia, and their sheer influence makes it believable. All of the high clergy in the city consider themselves catholique, and it is not unpopular amongst the low either.
      Some do throw off the shackles of history, though, and declare themselves part of La Réforme. These individuals still value the teachings and traditions of their faith, but wish to break away from the constraints of the Estates seeing them as an imposition by the secular world on God and his work. The handful of kindred who identify with this faction would seem to be excellent allies of the burgeoning Radical movement but in truth they want to sweep away all secular governance regardless of its type and replace it with an overt theocracy. Despite this they sometimes pander to the underground movement and pamphleteers in order to gain advantage in the city.
      The last, and smallest, of the sects found amongst le clergé are Les Protestants who reject the Catholique stance of honouring the religious past completely, and instead advocate adopting a new more puritanical approach to their religious duties. They either were embraced from families which followed mortal protestant faith, or decided to go against the grain after death, finding the pomp and circumstance of the mainstream distasteful. Though few in number Les Protestants continue to survive due to their influence beyond the boundaries of the city, and in particular have a strong relationship with like-minded individuals in Britain and Germany. This means they can sometimes accomplish things that others cannot, and so their positions remain secure for now. Despite this, the idea of any of the high clergy being openly protestant is laughable. The Second Estate (The Nobility)

      In the ballrooms and dining halls of the City of Light darkness lurks like luxurious velvet, slipping through the cracks into the light of thousands of candles. The mortal nobility feasts and dances and in the shadows lurk their dead compatriots, the Second Estate of the Damned who rule the city with an iron fist. They hold all the land and they receive the corvée of their tenants, and in turn pass some of that on to the clergy and Le Requin. Made up of the noble kindred of the city who do not choose to join the clergy, the nobility is the real power in Paris.
      Though there is no official “leader” of the Second Estate it is clear to all that Marquis Amaury Rome is much more powerful and influential at court than Comte George Fortier despite the latter’s extensive network of spies and agents. As guardians not only of stability but also the masquerade, the Second Estate prides itself on keep the city peaceful and the peasantry under the domain of those kindred who know better.
      Members of the Second Estate are considered members of the Invictus for the purposes of qualifying for merits, etc. In addition, they gain a free dot in one of the following merits of their choice for each dot in Status (Second Estate) they purchase: Resources, Herd, Retainer, Staff. Sects

      The Cavaliers (or Royalists) make up the overwhelming majority of the Second Estate, to the point where it is barely considering there to be a difference between the two. Royalists give their loyalty to the Prince, and believe that only with the continuation of his rule, and the continuation of the Estates as they stand today can peace be maintained and the masquerade remain unbroken. They see it as their duty to keep the Third Estate in line as they know the commoners are the most likely to overstep the mark and, through poor judgment, make the presence of the kindred known to the populace at large.
      The tiny minority of those who do not fall in line with this opinion are sometimes referred to as Compatissant, as they are seen as being sympathetic towards their lesser and thus somewhat soft of heart. They still maintain that the rule of a prince is natural and right, and that the Estates serve a useful purpose, but feel that the harsh rule of the Requin is serving only to risk the masquerade. They believe that by lessening the burden on the peasantry, and giving them more freedom, violence and thus a potential masquerade breach is less likely. The Third Estate (The Peasantry)

      Made up of the roiling mass of those kindred who are not embraced into the right lines, or who do not feel the call of the church, the Third Estate or troisième état is by far the largest and yet least powerful of the three. Outnumbering the first and second estates five to one at the very least (counting the peasantry isn’t necessarily easy) they are the tenants of the territories of the nobility and the payers of the hated corvée. Fractious and prone to infighting, the major reason they have not seized power to date is that getting kindred to look beyond their own interests and work together is a herculean feat. Sects

      In the streets of Paris the peasantry align themselves into a confusing arrangement of alliances, loyalties and communities, with considerable overlap and lots of partisan hatred. The following are just the major groups not based upon bloodline (for the most part); there are dozens of other tiny short-lived organisations and sects that are too numerous and insignificant to mention here.
      The real mover and shaker of the underclasses is of course the Radical movement, who are also known amongst themselves as sang rouge, cartien, révolutionnaires, pamphlétaires, and a dozen other things. A ragtag bunch of discontents, liberals and radicals they are less an organisation and more a tendency. Made up almost exclusively of neonates they are agitators for political change in the same way that their mortal counterparts are. Their goal is to tear down the Prince and his estates, and replace the status quo with a more egalitarian and equal system. What this system would look like is a point of great contention amongst the radicals, and this lack of unity is endemic to all their activities.
      Despite their confusing message and lack of structure, the Radical movement is growing at an impressive rate as more and more young kindred become disillusioned with their place in kindred society and yearn for something where they are not forced to serve their barons in such a denigrating manner. Though some are full-time radicals most are members of other sects as well though to date there are no members outside of the Third Estate.
      Members of the Radical movement are considered members of the Carthian Movement for the purposes of qualifying for merits, etc. Storytellers may restrict access to Carthian Law if they see fit.
      Known interchangeably as Bas Rouge and Beauceron (both of which are also names for a breed of particularly tenacious dog known for its loyalty and bulk), it would be more accurate to describe this rough alliance as a tendency akin to the Radicals rather than an organisation in the strictest sense. If the Radicals wish to tear down the estates and the nobility, then the Bas Rouge take the opposite point of view, and work hard to preserve it. In almost all cases this has nothing to do with loyalty or philosophy but rather self-interest, and as such many of the ancilla and elders of the third estate can be found to be members of the Bas Rouge. Many of the third estate are increasingly seeing these traditionalists as traitors, abandoning their own to suffering and misery in favour of their own selfishness, which only heightens the increasing divide between the young and the old in the city.
      Members of the Bas Rouge are considered members of the Invictus for the purposes of qualifying for merits, etc.
      La Sous-terre are an important part of everyday life for the dead of Paris as they control the extensive labyrinth of tunnels and chambers that run underneath the city. Verging on apolitical they are willing to barter for almost anything, and they are a reliable source of shelter and sustenance if all else fails you, though such things do not come without a cost. There are a handful of other clans amongst their ranks but the vast majority of La Sous-terre are Nosferatu, and there are whispers of grander structures hollowed out beneath the city, a necropolis only open to the Haunts.
      Due to the restrictions on travel for members of the peasantry beyond the reach of their Baron’s territory La Sous-terre act as something of a gatekeeper to wider cooperation amongst the third estate, which acts as another barrier to organisation of the radicals as access to their tunnels is never free. This may be changing, however, as some of the more junior members begin to fall in line with Radical philosophy and supposedly some of them will let their fellows travel for free if they can get away with it.
      Members of La Sous-terre may take the Necropolis merit if they wish, regardless of their clan. Nosferatu not of La Sous-terre may not take the Necropolis merit.
      One of the few respected covenants to find a place amongst the ranks of the peasantry, the Children’s Crusade has a little more leeway than most of the third estate, though for how much longer it is hard to say. Those who are embraced before adulthood in the city are swiftly adopted by the crusade regardless of their allegiance to the estates, though noble kindred have long-since learned not to embrace children for this very reason. Once the covenant was militaristic and feared, but it has been a long time since it held much real power, and these days it is more akin to a support network for those who can never grow up than anything else. Members of the crusade look out for each other, and attacking one usually means attacking them all – a survival tactic they’ve had to adopt as their reputation is no longer so potent.
      All members of the crusade, despite the fact that they no longer march to war, are trained to fight by their peers. Martial prowess is considered an important part of their identity, and internal power squabbles are usually dealt with swiftly and brutally with a show of strength. Scuffles are common amongst the children of the covenant, and other kindred are sometimes surprised or shocked at the bloodiness of their fighting. When a problem plagues the city the crusade is almost always first to answer, though not all problems are solved easily with bloodshed and youthful fury. This can sometimes cause discomfort for those in power, or those who would prefer a more peaceful resolution.
      Members of the Children’s Crusade only pay 2xp for dots of the Brawl, Weaponry and Firearms skills, and 1xp for specialties of those skills.
      L’Académie du Serpent is one of the most unusual organisations in the city and has a small but influential membership. Based in the Latin Quarter which has become something of a “free state” (due to a conflict over territory boundaries between two of the nobility) they dominate kindred interaction with the intelligentsia of the city, having a stranglehold over interference with L’Université de Paris. Its philosophy is complex and in places contradictory but it draws influence primarily from the relatively new ideas put forwards by the Ordo Dracul and the French interpretation of freemasonry.
      For L’Académie the matter of prime import is the perfection of self in service of ultimate truth. They do not seek to throw down or reject God as members of the actual covenant do, but instead hope to commune with what they refer to as the “Godhead”, a concept drawn from hermeticism and freemasonry, through rational inquiry and spiritual development. This heresy makes their existence contentious amongst the Ordo Dracul in a broader sense, who see the adoption of the practices but rejection of the philosophy of the order as an insult to its founder. Despite this no “true” Ordo Dracul have managed to gain anything but a tenuous foothold in Paris as they find the political and social constraints of its court to pose a unique difficulty to them, and the uncompromisingly religious attitude of its residents incompatible with their views. As such, the Académie manages to maintain its independence despite teaching what the dragons see as “their” secrets.
      The inner circle of L’Académie operates much like a mortal fraternal organisation, with a hierarchy based upon knowledge and spiritual wisdom and many arcane rituals and a secretive system of “hidden truth”. Far closer knit than many groups, a sizable proportion of its members actually live together in a communal haven known as the loge (“lodge”) found underneath L’Université de Paris, where they can study and teach without distraction. Outside of this core membership are the many students and apprentices that L’Académie takes on, its membership acting as mentors and tutors for kindred drawn from all three estates in return for various kinds of payment. Such tuition ranges from genuine academic teaching such as of the classic or philosophy, to medical and anatomical knowledge and skill, or even mystic education and training in the use of disciplines and devotions. Under no circumstances, however, are students taught the secrets of the Coils, which are reserved for the inner circle alone.
      Members of L’Académie du Serpent may take the Coils of the Dragon with storyteller permission.
      Notables of Paris

      There are many important individuals in Paris, and not all of them are kindred. The following are a select few who may be of interest. The High

      Le Requin

      Henri Rome is in theory the most powerful kindred in the city. Embraced in the mid-16th Century in the city itself, he rose to power less than five decades ago after his grandsire, Adrien Rome, fell into torpor and left the position of Le Requin (“The Shark”, the equivalent of Prince in other cities) open. He was appointed swiftly and with a minimum of fuss and has a strong level of support amongst the First and Second Estates.
      Known for his brutality, his rule has heralded if anything a further increase in the divisive politics of the city, and his approach to troublemakers has been terrifyingly bloody. Where his predecessor was content to follow the will of the First Estate and condemn criminals to torpor, he has pushed hard for executions amongst the peasantry, seeing them as inherently worthless. This approach may in part be due to his isolation from the city proper, as it is known that he keeps his haven at Versailles so as to be near to his preferred herd and his court. In recent years he has even gone so far as to ban any kindred of the Third Estate from setting foot in his favoured town.
      How much power Henri actually has is up for some debate as he almost certainly rose to power at the behest of his sire, Amaury Rome, who was generally thought to be next in line of succession. Amaury decided, however, to step aside and allow his childe to take the title and this has caused no end of consternation amongst his political opponents. Jean Baptiste Chastain

      Évêque de Paris

      Évêque (“Bishop”) Jean Baptiste Chastain is the Prince's closest advisor and between the two of them they hold the vast majority of the city's power structure in their grasp. Bishop Chastain claims an improbably long personal history and has publicly spoken of his embrace during the reign of Charlemagne. If true, this would make him by far and away the eldest kindred in the city, and he certainly has a terrifying degree of personal power, which makes the boast considerably more believable.
      His views are extremely traditionalist, and his links to the Roman Catholic community in the mortal world make him extremely influential. Those who wish to attract his favour would do well to ensure regular attendance at his masses, and to give plentiful alms to the First Estate. His only unusual piece of policy is that he dissuades his followers of delving into the secrets of Theban Sorcery. There are several explanations for why this would be - publicly he has stated that such devotions are a distraction from the true work of the church, but most whisper that by doing so he leaves himself almost totally unchallenged in terms of mystic power for he himself possesses considerable skill (supposedly learned before his "revelation").
      A shrewd political animal and also a personal powerhouse, Jean Chastain cannot be overestimated. Many have speculated that if he wished he might claim the city for himself, but none would dare to say such in his hearing for he becomes most incensed at the idea of the church taking secular power. Sir Ethan Bertrand Fortier

      Shérif de Paris

      Sir Ethan Bertrand Fortier is the Shérif (“sheriff”) of Paris, and yet known more for his wit and social graces than for his strength of arms (relying heavily upon the Sarkozi to provide muscle in cases where it is needed). It is his role to enforce the laws of the city, investigate possible breaches of the masquerade, and bring the perpetrators to justice. In truth, he usually only bothers in establishing guilt when the accused criminal is of the First and Second Estates, and so is more a political tool than a legal one.
      That said, he is not to be underestimated: embraced in the early 17th Century he is an experienced kindred, and the head of the Ordre de la Couronne Épineuse within the city, a knightly order of the Second Estate who hold honour as the highest virtue. They are known for their fearsome duelling abilities, and Sir Ethan is one of their finest swordsmen. George Fortier

      Le Tisserand

      Georges Fortier is the patriarch of his dynasty and is a true monster, albeit a viciously cunning and inhumanly intelligent one. Once he was a notable socialite and womaniser who used his charm and influence to win political favour and defeat his opponents. For decades he was the majordome of the court, tending to its day to day affairs when it was ruled by Adrien Rome, the grandsire of the current Le Requin. From there he established a network of connections, mortal agents, and kindred allies or servants that earned him the nickname Le Tisserand (“The Weaver”).
      When Henri Rome claimed praxis over the city, however, George Fortier and his line more generally saw their support and influence wane. His reputation for being dashing and entertaining soured into one for contrariness and social gaffes, and he quietly withdrew from personal appearances in court so as not to disturb the actions of his brood any more than necessary. Since then he has secluded himself and for the last thirty years or so he has not been seen in public at all. He continues to manipulate the city from shadows, pulling strings to find whatever advantage for his dynasty he can, but he is no more a personally alluring figure. According to rumour he has shed his pretensions of humanity entirely and is now a heaving bloated hulk who consumes both blood and flesh to slake his appetite. Anna Chastain

      La Langue Acérée

      One of the most junior members of the Second Estate, Anna Chastain is the grandchilde of Jean Baptiste and a driven and vicious social climber. In particular his flouting of the Bishop’s rules on the learning of Theban Sorcery have made him something of a pariah. Period Firearms

      The ascension of the gun as the main weapon of war has totally changed violence, but firearms in the late 18th century are not without their drawbacks. Almost all personal firearms of this period are muzzle loaded (the powder, wadding and ball being inserted down the barrel ready for use) and flintlock (which produces a spark that ignites the powder when the trigger is pulled). This combination makes firearms both slow to reload (most soldiers would be able to fire 2 or 3 times per minutes) and prone to misfires and other accidents (such as premature firing, powder explosions and so on). Despite this firearms are used extensively as they cannot be rivalled for sheer stopping power. Reloading

      How long it takes you to reload a muzzle-loaded firearm depends on you skill and experience. Reloading always requires an Intelligence + Firearms check. A failure indicates the packing was bad and you must start over. A dramatic failure usually indicates a misfire, powder explosion, or other serious issue – the storyteller should decide the outcome. Reloading require and instant action each round during the entire reloading process. If you stop reloading during this process then you must start all over again from scratch when you resume.
      An untrained marksman (0 dots in Firearms) will struggle to load the weapon at all. It will take them a full minute (20 instant actions). With 1 or 2 dots of Firearms a character can reload in 30 seconds (10 instant actions). With 3 dots of Firearms the character can reload in even less time – 7 instant actions, or just over 20 seconds. With 4 dots of Firearms the character can reload very fast indeed, in about 15 seconds (5 instant actions). With 5 dots they are incredibly capable, and can reload in just less than 10 seconds (3 instant actions). Misfires and Other Problems

      Whenever a flintlock weapon is fired, it might misfire – on a dramatic failure the powder reserve explodes early, catches fire, splits the barrel or the weapon otherwise misbehaves badly. Such misfires almost always damage the weapon or at least mean it requires cleaning before using again, and can sometimes injure the user.
      As a flintlock weapon is used black powder builds up in the barrel slowly clogging it and making it less accurate and also more prone to misfires. For every shot made with the weapon all Firearms checks using the weapon (including reloading) suffer a -1 penalty. For rifles this penalty rises to -2 per shot, as the rifling is easily clogged. This penalty is completely removed when the player spends some time cleaning the weapon, but otherwise continues to accrue regardless of how much time you leave between shots.
      Flintlocks also unleash a flurry of sparks when fired. For most this is just distracting and occasionally results in a few minor burns to the hands and face, but for kindred this can be terrifying. The storyteller should require a frenzy check as appropriate when firing a flintlock.
      Flintlocks also create clouds of thick smoke when fired. For the round they are fired and the round after this acts as a barrier to visual acuity, inflicting a -1 penalty on all appropriate checks. This penalty can be cumulative if multiple weapons are fired, or if weapons are fired on each consecutive round.
      It is also possible to pack and fire a flintlock so that instead of firing a ball it spews flaming black powder. You must choose to set up a firearm to fire in this way when you load it. Its ranges become 3/6/12 and its damage remains the same, but the same it inflicts is in the form of a small fireball. This might set targets on fire or have other effects (like on vampires).                       And again as promised, some Bloodline workups. They're a little sparse in background material (they're all being made for the players in my game and we've decided to work out some of their history in game) but they're mechanically sound.

                      The Fergris

                      The Fergris are a bloodline with strong connections to both the French military and to northern Africa. Founded in the early 1680s by one of the first French kindred to travel to the colonies in Algeria, it has roots in the iron-rich country that was his adoptive home. Rumoured to have been a man known as Louis Villetrous, tales within the bloodline suggest that their founder had been a military engineer and sapper who made connections with some of the native kindred of the region, learning from their mystic insights to gain a better understanding of his own vitae and the structures he dealt with every day. He achieved a transformation of that vitae whilst still an ancilla, an unusual feat, and his inexperience might explain the somewhat unpredictable results that arose.
                      Parent Clan: Gangrel
                      Disciplines: Auspex, Celerity, Protean, Resilience
                      Weakness: The Fergris, upon entry into the bloodline, find their skin and flesh to become pale and grey almost as if they were kindred of much greater age. In truth this cosmetic change is due to unusually high concentrations of iron in their blood. Their real weakness, however, related to that blood’s unique properties – attempts to feed others their blood (whether mortal or vampire) results in horrific injury and pain to the subject as the vitae solidifies and crystallises whilst in their throat and stomach. Anyone who drinks Fergris vitae suffers a point of aggravated damage for each point of vitae ingested, their body wracked with pain and convulsions as their digestive system desperately struggles to cope. Whilst most can survive the process it is not subtle, and symptoms involve vomiting blood, intense stomach pain, fever and hallucinations.
                      Gift: Known amongst the bloodline as the durcissement, Fergris vitae has an extremely unusual property: upon leaving the body it hardens within a minute or so into raw and rough iron, nearly indistinguishable from the metal apart from the deep reddish hue that makes it look rusted. The bloodline has found many uses for this property of their blood.
                      • When a Fergris spills a point or more of Vitae (usually by cutting upon the body) the vitae will harden into solid iron after roughly a minute and will not disintegrate until exposed to either fire or sunlight, the touch of which will turn it to ash in short order.
                      • Soaking clothing in their blood allows a member of the bloodline to turn it into crude armour. It takes 1 vitae to produce a single point of armour for a single location through this method, making it expensive but useful in a desperate situation.
                      • When struck by a weapon that breaks the skin and bites into the flesh of the Fergris they can reflexively spend one or more points of vitae to let their blood harden around the weapon. This traps it within the body and it can only be removed with a successful Strength + Stamina check with a penalty equal to the number of points of vitae spent beyond the first. When the weapon is removed it inflicts a point of bashing on the vampire. If left in the body and not removed it will fall out during the kindred’s next daysleep.
                      • Almost any other application of this strange hardening property is available to the inventive kindred, but the storyteller is within their rights to require additional vitae expenditure and/or appropriate checks if they rule it is reasonable in the first place.
                      Le Garde du Sang

                      Le Garde du Sang are a bloodline of martial and mercenary kindred with a chequered past and a poor reputation amongst members of the Invictus in particular. Founded in the 1740s by Amélie Dusand a former Invictus Knight who was ejected from her order after the political intrigues of one of the senior members of her covenant left her as a useful distraction. Furious at her ignoble treatment and swearing that she would never betray anyone else for personal gain in the same way that her lords had betrayed her, she devoted herself to acting on the behalf of the downtrodden and disenfranchised kindred of Europe. Travelling from city to city she made a name for herself as a mercenary to be trusted, and one that was not available for hire by those who ruled a domain. She attracted a number of acolytes and inducted them into the bloodline she had developed to ensure trust and honour amongst those who served the underclasses.
                      Parent Clan: Daeva
                      Disciplines: Animalism, Celerity, Majesty, Vigor
                      Weakness: Le Garde du Sang pride themselves on their skill and honour, but they cannot handle even the faintest whiff of betrayal. If they are betrayed this is an immediate trigger to frenzy. In addition, if a kindred with the Dominant Condition makes any action that acts against the character or their interests then this is also a trigger for frenzy.
                      Gift: Known as supplier (“supplicate”) by Le Garde du Sange, their unique ability is a form of blood oath that is enforced by their very beast.
                      • A member of Le Garde du Sange gains a new aspect of the beast: The Servile Beast. Using the Servile Beast works just the same as any of the standard Bestial Triad.
                      • The Servile Beast is Beast-As-Follower. It needs an alpha to follow, a master to obey. It struggles to enforce its own will on others, instead seizing upon the will of its betters, and giving into their commands. It wants to help those it serves, and better then through its actions. It evokes the need for leadership, and causes the Dominant Condition.
                      • Use all the standard rules for predatory aura except that if the kindred is using Servile Beast as the aggressor and their target chooses Flight the supplicant must make a convenient excuse or escape to pull away, and it is the supplicant who looks inferior or foolish.
                      Condition: Dominant
                      The character is infused with a feeling of power and success over the supplicant (the vampire who imposed this situation), and you instantly become reliant upon them and wish to defend your new servant. You get a -2 die penalty to any rolls you make when the supplicant is not present. In addition you suffer all the effects First Stage blood bond with the supplicant (they count as your regnant) and you will therefore intrinsically trust them more than you otherwise would. This is not a true blood bond and ends when this condition ends and is not subject to any of the other rules concerning blood bonds.
                      This Condition fades naturally after a number of nights equal to the Blood Potency of the vampire who caused it (the supplicant).
                      After resolving Dominant, you character cannot be subject to this Condition again for a full month.
                      Possible Sources: The Servile Beast
                      Resolution: You are betrayed or harmed by the supplicant.
                      Beat: n/a

                      The Lapointe
                      The Lapointe dynasty spreads its influence in less direct ways than many others, but its chians once forged are like iron. They are specialists in using the blood bond to control others, making them into soldiers for their armies. However, they are cunning, for the blood bonds they create do not bind their thralls to them directly, but rather to their cause.
                      Weakness: The Lapointe's unique blood means they are more susceptible to the blood bonds of others. You cannot resist a blood bond, even by spending Willpower.

                      Gift: The Lapointe's blood is particularly invigorating and compelling, and has a strange and potent effect on those who consume it.
                      • When an individual would normally suffer a blood bond to a member of the Lapointe, they instead suffer it to a particular goal or ideal determined by the Lapointe. The Lapointe must specify this ideal at the time the blood bond is first created, though if a bond fades and is remade, they can choose differently the next time. It could be something as general as "Justice" or as specific as "the slaying of Bishop Marcus". These bonds, other than the changes noted below, operate exactly as normal bonds do.
                      • The First Stage blood bond now represents excitement and enthusiasm for the chosen ideal. It doesn't matter what they normally want, suddenly their focus shifts. When anyone takes a Social maneuver against the thrall where the end goal is to further that ideal, cause, or concept, their impression is considered one step higher. In addition, they gain a +1 die bonus on all Social actions and Discipline rolls against them, assuming against the end goal is in service of the ideal. Causing direct or indirect harm to the ideal or cause constitutes a breaking point at Humanity 5/Integrity-2
                      • The Second Stage blood bond now represents obsession and devotion to the chosen idea. Not only is it exciting, its fundamentally a good idea - it becomes and important facet of their belief in the world around them. The impression of social maneuvers (as above) is now considered two steps higher, and the bonus increases to +2. Causing harm to the ideal or cause is now a breaking point at Humanity 3 or Integrity on a -3.
                      • The Third Stage blood bond is simple and absolute zealotry, a totally consuming and obsessive need to further the cause. The impression on social maneuvers is always considered perfect, and the bonus rises to +3. Causing harm is now at Humanity 1/Integrity -5. Finally, all Discipline effects applied to the target that further the cause gain additional duration - scene becomes night, night becomes week, week becomes month, month becomes year. At the third stage it shatters all other bonds (even normal ones) and prevents further bonds from forming.


                      For some reason I got very hung up on the Lapointe, and ended up writing a whole bunch of different version of their bloodline. Here are the other versions I worked up but ultimately aren't getting used (I used some of the ideas from the last option in Le Garde du Sang instead):
                      The Lapointe dynasty has made a name for itself by being superb spymasters and secret-keepers. Learning from their cousins the Nosferatu the skills of subtlety, they combine it with their raw social power to extra hidden information in a variety of ways.
                      Weakness: The Lapointe are almost paranoid about revealing secrets, even to each other, and each one ends up with a treasure-trove of hidden information. Revealing any secret where it is not absolutely essential to do so requires them to overcome their own instincts and blood, and constitutes a Breaking Point at a level determined by the Storyteller (based upon how trivial the reveal is).
                      Gift: The Lapointe have developed the arts known as Caché which play upon their ability to uncover and keep secrets, as well as their natural inclinations towards the arts of Obfuscate and Majesty. All Lapointe gain the first devotion (énigme) upon joining the bloodline, and can purchase any of the others for 2 experiences each (edit: I will write more devotions if this is the variation we pick)
                      Enigme
                      The most basic gift of the Lapointe is able to hide something so thoroughly that they themselves do not know it until a specific circumstance triggers the release of memory. This ensures that a secret remains a secret, no matter the powers brought to bear against the individual.
                      By focusing on a particular piece of information or memory the Lapointe can spend a point of Vitae and conceal it even from himself. A memory of this kind cannot be accessed by the kindred themselves, or read using any form of discipline or sorcery, whether the mind-reading of Auspex, the commands of Dominate, or the divination of Cruac. When you activate this power, specify a trigger for the memory to be released - a particular circumstance, event, or period of elapsed time. When this occurs, the memory returns, and can be accessed as normal. You can choose to add no trigger - in which case the memory is simply eliminate forever. You cannot use this power to "undo" breaking points, or regain Humanity.
                      Faire Courir
                      With this power the Lapointe can cause a secret to become extremely public knowledge in a terrifyingly short period of time. The Lapointereveals a secret to another (which may be a breaking point as normal) and then spends a point of Vitae to add the power of his blood to his words. The individual thus spoken to gains the Gossipy Condition (see below). Prerequisite: Majesty 2
                      Bavard
                      Some secrets are painful to hear, and this gift allows the Lapointe to give them even greater edge. You can use this power when you reveal a secret to someone who is personally hurt by it, spending a Vitae point to unleash the secret in such a way that it seems to scream out to them in fury. They, and any others who hear it and who are upset or hurt by it, must immediately make a Frenzy check at a penalty equal to the Lapointe's Blood Potency. If they fall into Frenzy, then the Beast's (first) desire is to harm those who have hear the secretother than the Lapointe.

                      Condition: Gossipy

                              The First Estate (The Church)

                              As the mortal clergy are ordained by God, so the damned have the ranks of those who are God’s hand upon the earth. Behind dripping candles and fogs of incense smoke these monsters lurk, powerful beyond measure and bathing in the blood of those around them. They sometimes refer to themselves as the Lancea et Sanctum, and certainly they would be considered such by kindred from other cities, but in truth they are their own beasts unique to Paris alone. They are known simply as le clergé, or première état, but they divide themselves further amongst their ranks into élevé (the high clergy) and faible (the low clergy). The high clergy are those drawn from the families of the second estate (noble bloodlines) and only they may rise to the rank of bishop (with one rather notable exception) whilst the low clergy are drawn from the lines of the peasantry and make up the priests, monks, nuns and friars of the city.

                              Le clergé are the smallest of the estates and have no more than two dozen members of which only a handful are of the high clergy. Despite this they remain hugely powerful and influential, and hundreds of kindred attend their midnight masses with regularity and devotion. They tend to the religious needs of their congregations, devote themselves to the teachings of Longinus, and test the mortal herd as is their duty. Traditional and, one might say, catholic, they are a brutal hard kernel around which the rest of the city forms. Though they have less overt power than the nobility, they have the ear of the Second Estate and of the Prince, and as such cannot be underestimated. The First Estate does not traditionally receive corvée as they do not act as Barons, but instead receive a tithe from the Second Estate as a sign of respect.

                              Though organised differently to the Lancea et Sanctum in most domains, the First Estate still consider themselves to be part of the Church of Longinus, and indeed claim a continuous tradition from the earliest days of the movement in Rome. They believe it is their God-given duty to weed out the unfaithful and torment the innocent to allow them to prove that their devotion to the Lord is strong. Indeed la clergé are so convinced of this that they have embraced the ideal on a larger scale than is usual, influencing mortal policy and activity directly in order to spread misery and torment thousands. According to local legend (and the First Estate refuse to confirm or deny such rumours) the cycle of plague that wracked the city and kept the population in check, but caused horrors untold was created by la clergé themselves. Supposedly they had a kindred with unique powers to spread filth and infection, and it was only when they realised they could create greater suffering by letting the population grow that they stopped utilising his services.

                              The Testament of Longinus remains the foundational work of the devoted kindred in Paris, but it is joined by the so called Letters of Germain which are seen as nearly as important and circulated amongst the dead widely. These letters, supposedly penned by the first Bishop of Paris in the 5th century were half of a long and extremely involved discussion between the Bishop and his counterpart in Rome who was at the time the supposed leader of the church, and who had personally overseen the fall of the Camarilla. Though many kindred have looked for them, none of the replies have ever been found, but the philosophy and theology put forwards in what does remain has been very formative. It is an emotive and spiritual appeal to the duty of those who follow the Temple and Spear to cleave to traditional above all things, as to preserve the holy truth as revealed through struggle is to understand the work of God. Many members of the covenant in the city have taken these words to mean that they should push themselves even when it would be more prudent to conserve strength, as by doing so they reveal their true faith as they reveal the faith in others through torment.

                              The other theological oddity revealed in the letters has caused some consternation in member of the Lancea et Sanctum in nearby cities as it relies upon a metaphysical concept that is not universally accepted. Specifically, members of la clergé (or at least those of a traditional persuasion) believe that the torment of mortals is not only useful in that it weeds out the unfaithful, but also because it reduces the amount of time that they must stay in purgatory. By working off their sins through punishment in this world they avoid such lengthy punishment in purgatory in the next, and thus can ascend to heaven more quickly and without shedding so much weight of sin. This has even formalised amongst some kindred into a belief that the kindred on earth are in fact the direct agents of God who act as God’s representatives do in purgatory, and that their torment is not simply holy, but actually divine. Members of Les Pureté find this stance to be unfathomably offensive, and it is one of the major sources of division between them and their more traditional associates.

                              The First Estate is led by Évêque (Bishop) Chastain who is the formal advisor of the First Estate to Le Requin. The position of Bishop is one steeped in history in Paris as they are second only to Le Requin in power. According to tradition, should Le Requin be unable to serve for a short period (but not release his praxis) such as when visiting another city, or if engaged in other matters, it is the Bishop who rules in his stead. As such Chastain has a variable amount of power: at times he is little more than a glorified vizier, but at others he is literally a tyrant whose word is law.

                              Despite this closeness the relationship between the First Estate and the office of Le Requin, the relationship between Chastain and Henri Rome has soured over the last few decades and the power structure of the city is not nearly as unified as it was under his predecessor. The major reason for this division is a differing attitude to final death and to diablerie. Chastain takes the traditional sanctified stance favouring torpor as a punishment and rejecting final death as it prevents kindred from doing God’s work by ending their eternal suffering, whilst Le Requin is much more liberal with death sentences, imposing them on any law breaker he sees as a threat to his rule. In addition, Rome has on occasion ruled that a criminal who is treasonous in an extraordinary way be subject to amaranth (usually carried out by himself or his sire Amaury Rome), and the diablerist be exempt from all repercussion or criminality for their act. The last kindred to be executed in this manner was Christophe Lapointe, an action which caused considerable division between Le Requin and Évêque Chastain.

                              Members of the First Estate may learn Theban Sorcery with the storyteller’s approval, though doing so can attract the ire of the Bishop. They are also considered members of the Lancea et Sanctum for the purposes of qualifying for merits, etc. Sects

                              The old guard rule almost supreme in Paris, and as such the members who identify as Catholique dominate the estate. Though many were indeed Roman Catholics in life, the term does not refer to the Church of Holy Rome (which is, of course, superseded by the Temple and Spear in death) but instead uses the word to indicate a continuity of faith and practice, drawing on the rituals and observances of the earliest days of their order. They indeed trace their lineage to Rome, claiming to have established themselves in the city during its time as Lutetia, and their sheer influence makes it believable. All of the high clergy in the city consider themselves catholique, and it is not unpopular amongst the low either.

                              Some do throw off the shackles of history, though, and declare themselves part of La Réforme. These individuals still value the teachings and traditions of their faith, but wish to break away from the constraints of the Estates seeing them as an imposition by the secular world on God and his work. The handful of kindred who identify with this faction would seem to be excellent allies of the burgeoning Radical movement but in truth they want to sweep away all secular governance regardless of its type and replace it with an overt theocracy. Despite this they sometimes pander to the underground movement and pamphleteers in order to gain advantage in the city.

                              The last, and smallest, of the sects found amongst le clergé are Les Pureté who reject the Catholique stance of honouring the religious past completely, and instead advocate adopting a new more puritanical approach to their religious duties. They either were embraced from families which followed mortal protestant faith, or decided to go against the grain after death, finding the pomp and circumstance of the mainstream distasteful. Though few in number Les Pureté continue to survive due to their influence beyond the boundaries of the city, and in particular have a strong relationship with like-minded individuals in Britain and Germany. This means they can sometimes accomplish things that others cannot, and so their positions remain secure for now. Despite this, the idea of any of the high clergy being openly puritanical is laughable.
