    Gangrel Discipline Advice

    07-19-2022, 03:35 PM
    I am making my first Requiem character, after a long time with Masquerade, and I know sometimes things look good to a new player but don't play as well as intended. I will be playing a Gangrel in a group of largely more experienced characters and wanted to hold my own. He is not a combat/physical oriented character starting (might grow into it) but he is tough and I want him to emphasize survivability. So, I was looking for advice on how much Resilience would be a good starting point or when it becomes truly effective. Is Protean as strong as it looks and worth investing in? Also, for when he does develop more disciplines eventually, which between Celerity and Vigor is a better pairing with the Gangrel Clan Disciplines?

      Resilience is one of the most cost efficient survival tools across all supernatural splats, it increases your ability to fight large numbers by an order of magnitude. Regarding combat builds, I think the quick aiming focused marksman can be interesting if you go for the angle of eliminating foes as quickly as possible. In terms of optimizing for combat and long term goals, I believe this topic on Gangrel combat builds goes into all the detail you might want. Protean is pretty good for hand to hand combat, especially since the barbed hands can inflict lethal damage even to vampires, but its important to think of how your character approaches combat when building the feral transformation. Since grappling and Brawl are related, it means a vampire can attack with Protean and strategically drain targets while benefitting from Resilience.

          From my admittedly limited knowledge (since my Gangrel was a cerebral Ordo guy taking Protean all the way for entirely in-story reasons)and just my take on this, with trying to keep the emphasis on survability in mind :

          Vigor helps better with hitting harder, Celerity with not relying on Resilience as much.
          Vigor's primary use (to me, might learn something new here) is higher strength dice pools, and thus increased likelihood of suceeding, e.g. causing damage, as well as increasing the caused damage.
          Celerity increases your defenses, so it's less likely to be hit: a boxing world champion has an attack dicepool of around 11-16, I suppose. With Wits3&Dex3, Athletics2 and Celerity 2, your defense would be 7 (without dodging) so the world champion of boxing would still role 4-9 dice for an average of 1-3 Dmg. Spending Vitae gives you the ability to relocate very quickly (getting out of danger = survival) or a chance to interrupt actions before they happen.
          Resilience primarily increases your health pool and downgrades serious damage. Spending Vitae gives you a variety of effects, among them Armor, which might render succesfull hits irrelevant.

          The main differences in Celerity vs. Resilience, when it comes to defense, are:
          1. Celerity requires you being aware of the threat, resilience applies even when you're knocked out.
          2. Resilience (active effect) reduces damage after the fact and lets you take more damage (passive effect), whereas Celerity reduces the chances of being hit beforehand and lets you avoid damage by running aw- ahem, I mean relocating.
          3. Celerity reduces long-range weaponry's chances of causing injury.

          Celerity and Resilience in combination would make you much more likely to survive, in combination with Protean you might even still deal some decent damage.
          Personally, I prefer some Protean/Resilience/Vigor, but that is very combat focused.

          Since you're looking for survivability, Protean 1 should be a must have, as being stranded outside somewhere with the sunrise coming is a death sentence to most Kindred, but with just this, "I'll lure them outside the city to the spot where we prepared a trap and leave them stranded for the sunrise" becomes a viable (if risky) strategy. You could also save Dots in you Haven Merit with this, if so inclined.
          Protean 2 gives some versatility, although those are occasionally circumstancial and depending on your choices. I like the Claws and usually pick at least one of the senses.
          Protean 3 once again gives you tools for a "can fight without having it as the focus" type of deal- consume a bear, and you can basically Beastboy yourself into it. Take in addition a small and a flying animal, and you have a lot of wiggle room for movement and fighting. Maybe even look into the "Swarm" Devotion.


          Hope I could provide some help.

                  Reflexive Resilience healing is always more efficient than the standard 1 vitae per 1 lethal or two bashing because it removes Resilience rating +1 of all damage up to aggravated.
                  Resilience 1 allows you to mitigate successful stake attempts because you can negate 1 success. Unless 8+ successes are commonplace in your setting, Resilience 4 makes successful stake attempts on you virtually nonexistent and Resilience 5 laughable. But the question becomes, if you need this much Resilience on a regular basis to justify forgoing improving other skills or getting other merits and disciplines, your chances of survival long term aren't promising. If avoiding or escaping combat is your M. O. then 2 ranks usually sufficient. If getting in someone's face once they push your buttons is your thing, then go to at least rank 4.

                  Animalism gives you a steady food supply and an army of spies and messengers. And the animals only get deadlier and more combat/security useful as you progress. There is something to be said for Gangrel survivability coming from the fact that anyone attacking him often ends up ripped apart by a swarm of labradors and ravens. Or unapproachable unless you will it at the highest levels. Animalism 2 is a must have for survivability. Anything beyond that should be based on playstyle.

                  Protean gives you a place to sleep nearly anywhere natural weapons and/or armor undetectable by scanners, and unusual movement ability. Depending on your setting, the most exotic combat type predator that may be available for you to eat and transform into is most likely going to be a coyote or maybe an alligator down south as bears aren't usually in urban settings. But don't discount the usefulness of being able to transform into a deer (vermin), house cat, vulture, bat or other setting specific animal. Turning into mist enables you to enter or escape from almost any restraint or locked container.
                  Protean 1 and 2 are must haves for survivability. Having a reputation of final deathing someone if you enter frenzy makes attacks on you unlikely but its socially a double edged sword so I would not say agg dealing claws improves your survivability so much as enhances your lethality. I usually find safer and more reliable methods of doing aggravated damage than prerequisite merit that requires frenzy and can't be turned off ever again. That comes down to play style. Turning into a mist is situationally useful for hiding or attrition fights if your opponents are dumb enough to not move out of your radius but I don't recommend prioritizing it unless you have something else in mind for it.

                              Originally posted by Caelican View Post
                              For the Armor Piercing aspect of Protean 4, I am assuming it does not cut through Resilience?

                              Nope, Resilience is direct damage mitigation from all sources. It reduces damage from vampire claws, from werewolf bites, even mage damage spells lose damage. When it hits high levels, its terrifying to watch, because it does nothing to diminish the aesthetic effect. The vampire has a bullet through the head, looks like a charred corpse after emerging from the car crash and is getting more bullet wounds with every round. You see them moving with unstoppable momentum, the feral threat clear in their bared fangs. You know that not only will they survive this ambush, but everyone in your team will become a bloodbag for them. A Gangrel with good dots in Brawl and Professional Training can use the barbed hands from Protean to drain vitae very quickly whilst protected from attacks by Resilience. Knowing when to use the psychological effect to aid survival (refill the blood pool in this case) can be a powerful tactic.
