                                    Okay, feedback time! Some of my thoughts!

                                    Crusade - I join the Weaponry Dots Feels Weird Chorus, but for slightly different reason. Its is that I don't like seeing it restricted to Weaponry - guns, archers, and claw-brawlers shouldn't be entirely dismissed from benefiting. I would suggest personally turn it from Weaponry to "Dots in Crusade" or "Crusade + Status, capped at 5" or something else more generic with the other vampires. That gives it a more of a faith-based, spiritual discipline feel to it, I think. Or go with "appropriate physical skill."

                                    I would like to point out that I'm at a bit of a loss at its use during a vampire game. The first dot is practically limited to Cruac or fellow Thebans (Belial heretics!) - I don't see how it can apply to Ordo Coils/Scales, which should be a thing, as well as potentially the Oaths and Law. Same with the third dot - extended disciplines aren't exactly common in a combat situation, and introducing any combat tends to disrupt them. All the disciplines are all Instant or Reflexive activations, even if some of them require set up.

                                    It feels like it just doesn't work against everything except Cruac and Theban. Even the Strix and VII, major antagonists, don't offer much in the way of sorcery used against the vampires. All the other covenant Fighting Styles relied the character acting certain ways. This relies on specific antagonists being chosen. So, short of hunting the Circle or the occasional heretic plot, this just sits, which I think is a shame.


                                    Flock -A better herd merit. The willpower bit of the herd is strange, since we have no mechanics for the Herd doing anything; I was under the impression that was Retainer. There's also the question of how it interacts with Lorekeeper. Does buying Library qualify as Herd dots for purposes of buying Flock? Unsure of the mechanics behind this.


                                    Laity - Okay, similar to other covenants. Moving on.

                                    Sanctuary - A curious twist on the old tradition. Its reactive, where you'd need to buy it as a crime happens. The merit assumes that the criminal is going to behave, however. Is that your intent? That they be good little souls and not try to sneak out like rebellious teenagers, and the like? I'm almost thinking that it might be better if the character is maintaining a Temple? Traditionally, you take refuge on holy ground; at least, that's the pop culture reference, not someone else's home that I'm familiar with.

                                    Stigmata - Unsure as to the timing of the bleeding here. Are we talking about set times, where you can plan it out on every Sunday and Tuesday? Does the willpower mean you're forcing it beyond the usual dots-per-week? Or just when it bleeds on what day? Feels like an alternative to the Undead Mense merit in the core book. Cool for the Lance.


                                    Temple of Damnation - Holy, this is interesting. I especially like the ranged Theban bit. Its a very sexy merit that I would adore to have in any game.


                                    Apple of Eden - Fun little ritual, not sure what everyone is saying its a powerful effect. Its essentially a plot hook for dealing with NPCs. I especially like the potential to use it for scaring people with wrath of god type stuff. I suppose you could say its powerful if you have a rank mortal in your game, but that's not the norm in my experience. Unless the merit is meant to work on ghouls as well as regular mortals? I am used to thinking of ghouls as not-mortals.

                                    Marian Apparition - Am I the only one that finds it odd that we have a visit from Mary in the Lance? Putting aside the whole Vampire-Catholic connection thing I thought the Lance moved away from, wouldn't Livian be a more appropriate image here? It feels tied into the Livian Heresy, with that Cruac benefit. Are you using Mary so other vampires can excuse the ritual with that reason, while still denouncing Livian?

                                    Another mechanics question - how do you contest an extended roll when the ritual is already complete? Normally, you contest it as the rolls are made, no? An equal number of rolls to the original rolls? Compare at the end when we hit target numbers? How does that compare to a single roll? Or is it like the new Cruac rituals where you prep before hand, then execute another roll when activated?

                                    Relevatory Shroud - Has a lot of overlap with Auspex 3, though it is different.

                                    Apparition of the Host - Now that's fun! Reminds me a bit of the old attacking owl ritual.

                                    Bloody Icon - I flat out hate Humanity as a cost, especially with Theban needing higher humanity for their magic. I was under the impression of Cruac users being heavy into low humanity, and Lance vamps into accumulating lots of banes.

                                    Walls of Jericho - While I like the effect, the low humanity requirement is very odd to me, and feels against what I thought the Lance was growing towards, thematically. The ritual says you can't have above 3 to cast it as a level 2 ritual. You need higher ratings of Theban in order to make it work. That's mind boggling. As well, I don't know if I like the variable cost, forcing you to keep a low humanity to cast a ritual like this, where as Theban as a whole needs higher Humanity to learn. I am not a fan of the odd requirements here.

                                    Aaron's Rod - Fun times. Lots of potential, and the combination with Animalism has some yummy possibilities.

                                    Miracle of the Dead Sun - Another Sacrifice-Humanity one. Again, not a fan.

                                    Pledge of the Worthless One - Yay Belial! What's not to love? Actually, I'm liking the idea of more "heretic rituals" for the different heresies. Livian, now Belial...

                                    Great Prophesy - Lots of potential interesting effects. Reality writing at its finest.

                                    Guiding Star - More catholic-christian imagery. I wonder if a level 4 ritual for what amounts to a safe haven for a night is a bit high.

                                    Apocalypse - Break the Masquerade!

                                    Judgement Fast - Judgement on the city, which is painful for Elders that are running around. Very neat and powerful for the Lance moving to take command.



                                    There's two things I notice with the rituals. A lot of love for heretical spells here, which is wildly awesome. Says a lot about the covenant and Theban as a whole. The second is there seems to be a comeback of "sacrifice your humanity" themes, which I feel is at odds with core book descriptions of Theban. I suspect that might be the reason, a whole "sacrifice what's most precious to you for desperate times," but I feel like its way over cost. Or establishing some kind of Sanctity rule for using it this way.

                                    I'd also like to see some more Jewish and Islamic symbolism in some of the rituals instead of predominantly christian.

                                                        Originally posted by MCN View Post
                                                        It feels like it just doesn't work against everything except Cruac and Theban. Even the Strix and VII, major antagonists, don't offer much in the way of sorcery used against the vampires. All the other covenant Fighting Styles relied the character acting certain ways. This relies on specific antagonists being chosen. So, short of hunting the Circle or the occasional heretic plot, this just sits, which I think is a shame.
                                                        This I have to agree with. As I mentioned before, Cruac and Theban rituals take generally somewhere between 15 minutes and an hour to do, they're not something you're going to break out in the middle of combat. Then, rituals are already considered a failure if they're interrupted, so you're not just having to find the ritual being enacted, you're going to have to not interrupt it before your big attack. Which leads us to the big attack itself. People performing a ritual have no defense, which is great for the Crusader, but they're using a willpower and still attacking at a -2 penalty. I think, given the cost and that the targets, until we get some further combat ritualists, it would make more sense for the penalty to be gone, or even the Willpower point to help out with the roll. I prefer the latter, with their zeal helping them make the attack.

                                                        But then you get to the repercussions, and you've done some extra damage which is probably bashing, and turned a failure into a dramatic failure for them. But what does a dramatic failure do for a ritualist? In both cases of blood sorcery, you get a penalty to your next ritual, but if the ritual is that of Theban sorcery, they get the Humbled condition, which gives them a penalty to their Resolve rolls and makes them unable to gain Willpower through their Requiem. This can be pretty good, even the description seems appropriate, 'Your character has felt the touch fo the divine and trembled. She feels unworthy and wretched.' But if you look at Cruac if you get the dramatic failure you're hit with the Tempted condition, which gives you a penalty to resist Frenzy, making the attack somewhat like poking a bear, since Frenzying vampires can be very dangerous. Looking over at Werewolf, you get the Shadowlash condition, which gives you a penalty to interacting with Spirits and the Shadow, certainly not as dangerous as a frenzying vampire but low in payout for the Crusader.

                                                        I think the problems lie in its limited use, and its cost for potentially low payoff. I have no idea what to do for the limited use, but there may be rituals more likely to be used in combat as time goes by and mage or other games come out. Or further books might have extended Disciplines. For the investment, which isn't exactly high but does require 3 Merits and a lot in the way of prerequisites, I think changing how the willpower cost works would be a nice step. But the big thing would be the Dramatic Failure. I think it would be thematically and mechanically more advantageous if instead of this it should simply afflict the Humbled condition, or a variant that allowed for other supernaturals, since Vampires are so far the only ones gaining Willpower through Requiems and Masks.

                                                        I dig the rest, though. I especially like the idea of the Flock, Temple and Sanctuary merits.
