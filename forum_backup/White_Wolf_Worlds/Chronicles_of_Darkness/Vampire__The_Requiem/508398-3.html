            Originally posted by Diggs View Post
            Outside of a literal interpretation, yes a shared haven prohibits a person from having their own space. It's the "share" part. You can't kick everyone else out. You can't have locks and traps to protect you from everyone else. There are real people that want to live alone. They want to go to a home where they will not hear or see anybody else. There should be vampires like that too.
            Its not a question of some vampires wanting to be alone. Its a question of what the norm is. No one will argue that the norm for a werewolf is the pack, and yet we still have a notable number of lone wolves. That some will be alone, and some in a group, was never in doubt. Its a question of what percentages.

            If a hunter breaks into a vampire's haven, will they generally find a group of some kind, or a solitary?

            I've actually started to consider it an academic question that's only answer is "It depends on what the ST and individual players want."

            Originally posted by Nyx View Post
            I dunno, my play group usually laughs off Hunters, because of the cotorie strength and Discipline awesomeness. My players noticed a mid-tier hunter cell was on to them, and went after the Hunters. Even if they only took a single hunter captive, a squad of 4 kindred have access to all sorts of ways to pry out secrets, even from unwilling minds. Hunters who opt to use fire and bombs risk bringing cops and possibly Federal attention down on themselves. Hunters have to be as quiet as their quarry, otherwise they'll end up locked up in a psychiatric ward, or going out in a blaze of glory.

            Unless the hunters are military grade, or otherwise well trained and ready for this sort of violence, which the vast majority aren't, a vampire with the same experience will win pretty easily.
            You know, when talking about using humans as enemy opponents for a supernatural, I feel like we're talking about a Hunter: the Vigil cross-over. Not sure how much I like that. I like my vampire hunters to organically rise out of mistakes that the characters make with the Masquerade. That holds true for all the games, really. The feeling that we have the Night Watch already established and just waiting for a hint to send humans after vampires isn't necessarily something I want in a Requiem game. That's what rogue ghouls and VII are for, imho. At least, that's what I use them for in my games. Something tied more intimately to the vampire condition.

                    I consider it an academic question, as you put it, MCN. That was my purpose. There are no known percentages except in a particular city, therefore what the group decides. A hunter that breaks into a haven should not know what to expect. There might be one very powerful vampire or a handful of vampires or a brood. Haven merits work best for NPCs. For players, they are just flavor because the truth is all the defenses can be overcome when it works for the story. If players feel they need to band together to have the best defenses in the world then they have an antagonistic relationship with their ST. If players have characters that feel they need to band together for mutual defense then that would be the norm for that particular setting, extremely dangerous. Is that every setting? No. The number of vampires in a haven will be determined by covenant, city, and age of the vampires. We could probably agree on some relative details, but there is no real reason for vampires to share a haven unless they wanted. The ratio is then undefined.

                            Personally the greatest weakness humans face, in my opinion, is during the intelligence gathering stage. Okay, so IF they find the vampires' haven they can win, right? Except they have to find out where a notoriously secretive creature is sleeping without giving themselves away. So you have to first learn how to trail after them and if you give yourself away, they only need a dot of Dominate, Obfuscate, Animalism or Majesty to get you where they want you (in the middle cases following you home) and then blood bonding you into servitude. So unless the hunters want to publicly splash someone with fuel and set them alight (a risky proposition in most spots as cops tend to frown on it), they're going to have to coordinate a surveillance effort that requires a lot of training against regular humans who can't see perfectly in anything but pitch blackness. Unless you're going with well-trained Vigil Hunters, this is not likely to be a skill that they start off having while it is something that those paranoid vampires should quickly develop (in terms of spotting a tail) since their vampiric enemies are just as keen to know where they sleep.

                            So you've got a creature that can see much better in the dark than you can, whose heartbeat can give away their hiding place if the vampire passes through a couple meters (and if they're female, possibly a couple dozen yards depending on your time of the month), whose blood can enslave you and cause you to give up your allies (even the first level of blood bond makes it a Breaking Point at 5 to let them get hurt), and who have several disciplines that allow for fantastic levels of surveillance to follow you. Dominate can be used in combat to turn one against the others, Obfuscate makes them hard to notice, Protean lets them disappear into the soil, Celerity lets them seemingly disappear (if they end up behind cover you won't know where they went) and Resilience makes even fire lack much of a punch. Also all damage dealt to them is bashing unless it's fire or sunlight. Oh, and unless it's aggravated damage, the vampire can spend blood to heal up the damage those hunters are laying on. And if they frenzy, they add their BP to all combat rolls.

                            And sunlight? Well, if they have low BP and high Humanity (which means up to 100 years old if they have regular BP), they can survive ten minutes in the sun which is more than enough time to kill everyone and go hide in the fridge until nightfall.

                            On the other side, you have a group of humans who take lethal from everything other than fists, pass out if they fill up with bashing, can be blood bound into giving up their friends within three nights (and may not even need to be kidnapped if a coterie-mate with blush of life spikes their coffee), and can't see very well in the dark. And that's not adding in the difficulties of getting heavy duty firepower or high level combat, surveillance and counter-surveillance skills. Also, if the human is injured they wear it longer and may even need medical help -- which raises questions and can draw the attention of the authorities if they have stab or bullet wounds. Finally, the larger the team the harder it is to stay hidden and keep a secret. Plus the odds of a single breach convincing a half dozen humans who know each other to risk their lives and fight back are pretty low and any attempts to find other hunters to group up with risks them coming to the attention of other hunters. So you're probably looking more often at two, maybe three, hunters rather than six to eight.

                            Even a relatively weak vampire can take enough punishment from two to three hunters and grind them down in return.
