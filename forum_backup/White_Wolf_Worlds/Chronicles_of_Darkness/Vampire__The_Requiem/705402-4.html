                        Originally posted by Claire Redfield View Post
                        This story is true.

                        You go back far enough, all you find is blood and smoke. It's dark, hazy, and when you see any light at all, it's red. It feels like rising from an ocean of blood, taking the first tentative steps onto land, when the moon was full and pale, unclouded by smogs and blotted out by skyscrapers. We knew Thirst then, as we know it now. The further from the ocean of blood we crawled the thirstier for its warmth we became. The People were there, the Kine, their hearts beating with such enticing vitality. Other animals could slake our thirst, but not like the People. The sweetness of their blood eclipsed all others, a wine that raised us from ravenous beasts to refined hunters. As their civilization grew, so did ours. We were there, in the shadows, watching, hunting, choosing from among them to become like us. Perhaps that is what damned us so, or maybe we were always damned. We arose with our own demons, the dark ones, the Birds of Dis. As we haunted the Kine, the Strix haunted us. As we hunted the People, the Strix hunted the Kindred. In those early days we fought with them, for they would have had us tear down the People and give in to our predatory urges. They whispered with wicked voices, spoke to us as children, as pups learning the art of killing from their mother. We were born not to live among them but to devour everything the People made. Tear it apart, shatter it, plunge it into eternal night.

                        Some of us gave in. The Strix took others by force, possessing their bodies and driving them to bloody madness. We gathered together with others of our kind and we fought the possessed ones, the mad howling revenants, and the disembodied spirits of smoke and blood. Dark times, indeed. Even we feared the night, but dared not gather too near to the fire. Those of us who in desperation butchered villages soon met their ends at the hands of the Wise. The Lupines were there even as we were, hunting their favored prey, heedless of our struggles just to survive. We slept in the earth to escape the sun's grasp. At night we crept out to find what mortals were too foolish or unlucky to be caught away from the fires and talismans of their villages. Sometimes the Strix were waiting then, reanimating those we had slain and sending them against us. The most desperate of our number sought help from the Wise, even beseeched the Lupines to help drive away the Strix. Most found disappointment, many Final Death.

                        In the end, we had but one option to survive: adapt or die. Like the Kine, we found ourselves in a dark and dangerous world, full of predators outside the walls. Humanity drew strength from its community, in numbers and teamwork. So would we. The greatest of our kind gathered into flocks. Bloodlines spread. The greatest of these bloodlines became the clans we know today, but back then there were many more. Each its own species within an unholy genus, these "proto-vampires" banded together with a singular purpose: raising up the Kine, that the Kindred might live among them. We aided the People by night where they needed us, tirelessly tilling soil and guarding them from the predators that stalked them at night. From observing their ways we refined our own magic, and by feeding from them we grew stronger still. When the Strix came to set our villages to ruin, we had the People with their fire to help drive them away. As we grew stronger, our blood magic more powerful, we too came to fight back. We lured them into the bodies of the dead buried in unguarded graveyards, our thralls trapped them and put them to the torch. We adopted the talismans of the People and so used them against the Birds of Dis. In the larger settlements the Strix became a grave threat only in numbers, while the Kindred continued to grow with the People.

                        We had a chance at last to become something more than savage revenants. With art and burgeoning civilization came opportunity to develop philosophies during the night, to learn crafts besides those of Disciplines and the art of killing. Some of us met to discuss those philosophies, or to form cults, or simply to socialize with others that shared our burdens. For a time we forgot what it meant to be the hunted, save for those of us foolish enough to provoke entire settlements to torch-wielding ire. We grew, and we prospered, and though few settlements could sustain many of us, we established hierarchies, traditions to help us survive. They would become the Traditions in time, but the world then was different, and we had different needs. If we were to be the Kindred, bound together by Night and Thirst, we needed some form of structure. Thus were our Traditions:

                        To the Shadows Cleave
                        When the People knew of bloodthirsty monsters in their midst they would destroy us. They would turn their rites and their Wise against us, they would find us where we slept and expose us to the sunlight. The People knew the terrible world and its many dangers, and they accepted them. Spirits and other beasts from dark realms hunted them, terrorized villages as did the mortal beasts of the time. When we were hidden, a danger lurking in the night, we could survive, for the People did not wage crusades to wipe out the creatures of the night. Those of us who did walk openly among the People did so as godlike figures worshiped by entire village cults. We found that feeding in the shadows, or upon the monsters that would prey upon the People, kept us alive. Out of sight, out of mind, or at least away from the end of a burning brand.

                        Raise Only Your Own Dead
                        In the Neolithic Era we had the power to Embrace even corpses. Many Kindred poisoned the waters of the People with Vitae, slowly influencing them. This meant that many mortals had tasted Vitae, however diluted. The Kindred discovered that they could then raise up these corpses as their own by feeding them Vitae. Cults formed around this, entire vampiric lineages born out of rituals of unhallowed rebirth. Not all corpses stayed dead, or rose at the whim of vampires playing god. Revenants clawed their way from the grave, murdering their living kin before dying to flame or fang. Some foolish vampires were themselves destroyed when a revenant killed the entire village, leaving the Kindred to die in the wastes or fall to one another's fangs. Eventually among our more civilized kind grew an understanding: only Embrace those who live or die directly at your own fangs. Poison not the waters or risk the undead rising and shattering lives in berserk rage.

                        Know Humanity
                        We have an old saying: "Even the heartless can be staked." In the Neolithic it was perhaps easier than in modern nights to forsake Humanity. Society existed as far as the pastures and crops. Law vanished as did the unwary into the wilds. Beyond the River, perhaps the Mountain loomed in the distance, and the world beyond that remained a mystery. The Kindred did not have this byzantine structure of modern societal laws to rely upon for guidance. If a vampire desired a maiden for lust or for blood, she could simply take that maiden. If a fool offended her, she could simply tear out his tongue to silence him. Those who formed cults that worshiped them as deathly gods lost sight of who they once were, perhaps one of those very villagers that now sacrificed their own to sate the vampire. Piece by piece we could fall into bestial madness, when we had worked so hard to rise above that. We needed to cleave to Humanity so that the Beast did not rule. It was said in those times that when the Beast won, it cast off dead flesh and went unshackled, becoming a creature of smoke and evil. To remain in control we had to retain a sense of refinement. We were better predators because we were the Kindred, not the mindless killers.

                        You might notice that there was no Tradition against Amaranth. Blasphemy, some say. Lunacy. It was simply assumed, a base truth from which all other Traditions sprang. I say differently. For we were predators then as we are tonight. While often predators avoid direct battle with one another, competition is a part of the cycle of life and undeath. In conflict we grow. Our desperation to escape predation by the Strix made us stronger, more advanced. A thousand different "clans," or what we might call such, existed then, each seeking to further its line. Not all of them could survive, just as not all species survive in the wild. We killed one another. We ate one another, and we changed because of it. Bloodlines merged and developed new traits, while others vanished into extinction. The strongest persevered. Some of the clans hit their peak, becoming diluted, their powers of the blood thinned by successive generations. Time only strengthened others. But in the end, the greatest of the clans survived into the modern night. Don't be fooled: many more are still out there, remnants of the Neolithic slumbering in earthen graves or waiting to reemerge in modern bloodlines.

                        Being a vampire in the Neolithic meant many things. Above all it meant trial against great difficulties. Alcohol, drugs, raves with pounding music and stupefied inhibitions, none of these things existed. Popular culture painting the Kindred as sexy and mysterious was millennia from its emergence. The People were not packed in like sardines in a tin, waiting to feel the pleasure of the Kiss. We fought to survive, to protect our mortal families, to establish laws and societies in a world that would not accommodate us. We were rugged, and we were potent. The Kindred of the Neolithic had no sacrosanct feeding grounds, no blood dolls. They went out and hunted, fed upon one another, stalked the beasts and the spirits that wandered the dangerous wilds beyond the River. We survived. In time, we even thrived. Though the youth (and all too many of our elders) today forget it, once we were the prey as well as the predator.

                        Now, the Kindred are apex predators, but only thanks to the legacy of our Neolithic forebears.

                        Creating a Neolithic Kindred Character
                        When creating a Neolithic Kindred character, use the following changes to the vampire template presented in the Vampire: The Requiem Second Edition rulebook.

                        • Clans: The clans as recognized by modern Kindred do not yet exist, or at least dominate vampire-kind. Many clans exist, including those whose evolution will transform them into the modern day clans. Instead of choosing a clan in the book, players of Neolithic vampires define their own bloodlines that have given rise to the character. Because the vampires of the Neolithic were more hardy by necessity, they gain one dot in each of three Attributes: one Power, one Finesse, and one Resistance. The player may choose all the same category, or spread them among the Physical, Mental, or Social Attributes.

                        • Disciplines: An extension of defining one's own clan includes choosing the clan Disciplines. Each vampire may choose any three Disciplines as her in-clan Disciplines, with one limitation: one of the Disciplines must be one of the physical Disciplines: Celerity, Resilience, or Vigor. The Coils of the Dragon and Theban Sorcery do not exist, and so vampire characters cannot take these powers.Crúac exists but it is not widely known (only vampires with Occult 3+ may learn it), and its usage is tied to the Strix. Too many Crúac rituals often tend to bring the Birds of Dis to an area, with disastrous results.

                        • Humanity: Kindred begin with Humanity 5. The struggles to survive amid a backdrop lacking in the more advanced society of modern eras means that a vampire faces greater challenges in reclaiming her Humanity.

                        • Feeding Restrictions: Vampires of this era can feed on spirits and werewolves, though both prospects bring with them the risk of Final Death. Spirits are common in the mortal world, crossing through the Border Marches to live among those of flesh and blood. The People accept this as a fact of life, and so do the Kindred. Essentially, all vampires begin with two free dots in the Unnatural Affinity Merit, allowing them to feed from spirits and werewolves. Pangaea is a realm in which few vampires survive for any length of time, and so developing further Unnatural Affinities for Pangaeans is unlikely but not impossible. Feeding upon these gods of Pangaea is likely to result in something arcane and strange happening to the vampire.

                        • Merits: The following Merits are not available to Neolithic vampires: Status (Clan) or Status (Covenant), and any Merits that use them as prerequisites. The Altar Merit is an exception, which does not require Circle of the Crone Status, instead requiring Occult 3 (as does Crúac). The use of the Altar Merit is sure to draw the attention of the Strix sooner or later. Dream Visions is available to any vampire with Auspex as a clan Discipline. Kiss of the Succubus likewise becomes available to any vampire with Majesty as a clan Discipline, Pack Alpha becomes available through Animalism, and Unsettling Gaze through Nightmare. City Status is replaced by "Village Status," functioning largely the same, even if it doesn't cover quite so populous an area. Carthian Law Merits and Invictus Oaths are unavailable.

                        • Clan Banes: Players should work with Storytellers to define a suitable "clan bane" for their character. Any character may use the banes listed in the book as inspiration or as their personal bane. Because of their starting Humanity, all vampire characters spend their unlives with their clan bane active. Storytellers may wish to allow vampires who reach Humanity 10, rare as it is, to shed their clan banes and only regain them if they drop to Humanity 6 or lower again during their unlives.
                        Bump. I know it's not perfect, but it's a start, with Dark Eras out now. Some folks may want to play vampires in the Neolithic, or use them in their chronicles, so having at least a stopgap measure is always good.

                                        ​I would have had at least one primary source of vampires being the retreating waters of the Ocean Beyond Life. As they lower, they reveal strange things on the rocky, barren islands that now rise up from the sea. Perhaps there are strange ceramics, odd pots marked with signs like those of the Vinca yet even older, and when a Sea-Wise exploring the Ocean returns to the World of Life carrying some, he breaks their seals and from them pours forth choking, smoky captured breath that winds its way into the lungs of hapless humans and takes them as puppets even as they are caught on the threshold of death. Perhaps on some of the lifeless shores, bodies are revealed; dead who never sank all the way into the waters and have laid there for centuries, perhaps millenia, in lifeless comas. In the cold air of the Ocean's surface, they find some semblance of sensation returning to their numbed, sodden bodies, and a fierce craving for the heat and fire of life; stumbling confused around the islands, unable to sate their hunger on other dead denizens, some are drawn to Avernian gateways and into the World of Life. Perhaps huge monoliths rear up out of the receding waters, surrounded by lethal, memory-eating brine and their lengths carved with eldritch symbols; as the waters recede, more of the secrets of immortality can be read. Some hubristic Wise, unwilling to see if the monolith's full secrets will be revealed during their lifetimes as the waters slowly drop, decide to try and work rituals only from the passages visible above the waterline; they gain the immortality they crave, in a manner of speaking, as they become conduits, siphons that are allowed a fragment of the life energy they must consume from others to feed into the Ocean Beyond Life.

                                                            I've been pondering the Neolithic Vampire question myself, and have come up with a few ideas and points, because I'm considering running a small chat for the big three in the Neolithic, though it doesn't nearly compare with the detail Claire seems to already have gone into. Regardless, here's what I have:
                                                            • Clans don't yet exist. Instead, you have "Birthrights", that is, the way a given Vampire came into being, which defines ONE of your three "in clan" aka affinity disciplines (Edit: one of which should probably be physical), and also grants a free dot in it, and ONE of the two attribute bonuses you'll get. You then choose two more in-clans and one other Attribute. My ideas so far for birthrights are as follows:
                                                              • Strix-born, those corpses or people that were possessed and later synthesized by a Strix. They start off with Dominate and Manipulation.
                                                              • Spirit-born, those corpses or people that had some heavily sympathetic or physical tie with blood or even actual VItae before being Claimed by a hunger-spirit or blood-spirit, creating something altogether different from a typical Claimed. They start off with Blood Tenebrous and Wits. EDIT: Alternatively, they might simply be the creation of a high ranking blood/death magath's Influences.
                                                              • Horror-born, those that were killed by something they heavily feared, but upon their death received a vision of what some call a "Dark Mother", who imbued them with their Beast, a primal entity manifested from humanity's fears of hunger and the night. This does imply modern Beasts are right... about SOME Vampires' origins. They start with Nightmare and Presence.
                                                              • Spontaneous Embraces, those that died a bloody, violent death, potentially after already having tasted Vitae (or maybe just human blood), and whose soul was warped by the process, then 'repossessing' their former body. They start with Protean and Stamina.
                                                              • Sired, those few that were actually the childe of another Vampire, with the typical process, rare as it was for them to want to multiply in this time period. They start with two out of three of their affinity disciplines being chosen from those their sire has an affinity for (but only get 1 free dot), as well as both attributes needing to match their Sire's. However, they may immediately gain their sire's bloodline benefit (mentioned below).
                                                            • Banes don't tie into Birthright, you simply choose a Bane appropriate to the character, following the normal Bane rules; this doesn't count towards your normal maximum, though. Spirit-born generally keep the ban or bane of the spirit involved as their own Bane, and Sired usually have one of their sire's banes, but that's not a hard requirement.
                                                            • Humanity starts at 7 as normal, but Kindred don't start off with any Touchstones; they need to expend effort to ground themselves that way (by purchasing the merit). If they lack one entirely, this is counted as all of them being 'Detached', rather than like they've lost all of them, mechanically.
                                                            • At Blood Potency 3, most Vampires learn to refine their blood a bit, coming into their own. They may create a Bloodline at that point (it's much easier in an era when Vampiric nature is still solidifying), with a Gift of the character's design, and a fourth affinity discipline. Bloodlines from the "future" may work as inspiration for the gifts; the fluff won't match up, but it's a good place to start, mechanically, or one can just create an entirely new one from scratch.
                                                            • Sired Vampires may gain their sire's Bloodline at Blood Potency 1 as normal, be Avus'd at 2 by anyone, or may wait until Blood Potency 3 to make their own. On a less mechanical note, 'bloodline' doesn't really exist as a term yet, nor are they individually named by most.
                                                            • Most Vampires have difficulty siring before they have a bloodline, but there's no specific mechanics for that. (Edited)
                                                            • Blood Tenebrous is one of the core disciplines, and can be chosen as affinity by anyone; even Vampires were capable of interaction with spirits in an era so heavily tied to them. To note, Shadow gates of course lead into the Border Marches, rather than directly into the Shadow or physical, or into either the physical or shadow realms if already in the border marches.
                                                            • Similarly, any Vampire can learn Cruac (at normal out of clan type costs), though it's not exactly called that yet, and in fact can exist in a different form for any given vampire; some might channel spirits, others might steal the power of the Strix, and others might venerate and beg assistance from Pangean gods. Even blood sorcery is also a bit more fluid at the time; the Themes system from Blood Sorcery is the default assumption, but without the prevention of affecting spiritual realms. However, codified rituals are incredibly rare; most Blood Sorcery is improvised.
                                                            • Theban Sorcery and Mysteries of the Dragon don't yet exist, nor do Oaths. Bloodline-based Sorceries might be possible, though, and Devotions are VERY common.
                                                            • Feeding restrictions are two higher; Vampires of up to Blood Potency 4 can subsist on animals, for instance. Early vampires hadn't yet become so reliant on human blood. There's also no risk of "shadow sickness" if a vampire feeds on spirits, as that's similarly common in the timeframe.
