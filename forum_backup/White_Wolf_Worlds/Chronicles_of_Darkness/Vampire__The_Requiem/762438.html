    Help me brainstorm this character

    11-10-2015, 02:09 PM
    Essentially what it says above. I'm still fleshing this character out.

    The basic concept of the character is that he's this guy who in life was just a really nice guy. His parents always instilled in him the importance of doing the right thing, so he spent all his time doing charity work, and political activism, and just trying to make the world a better place. Then in a twist of fate, he got turned. I'll spare you all the details, unless anyone really wants them, but essentially he woke up to a note from his sire that said "Hey, you're a vampire now. Also vampires are real. I can't really help you figure this shit out because of reasons, but here are all the basic rules (Both the traditions and the rules of how vampires work). Also, I can't tell you where to find the prince because reasons, and he'll probably kill you for not presenting yourself to him, so try not to draw attention to yourself.

    So this guy who all he wants to do is just do the right thing and be helpful and kind has woken up to find that he's now this creature that, by its nature, needs to prey on others in order to survive. So he's utterly unable, on a moral level, to feed on other humans because he can't bring himself to do it. And even when he tried to feed on animals, he couldn't bring himself to do that, because in his mind, they're completely innocent and while he was able to eat meat in life, it was another thing when he had to hunt down these actual creatures and force himself on them.

    What he found he was able to do to justify this was that he couldn't bring himself to feed on innocent people, but non-innocent people? That was a slightly different prospect. He still didn't like it, but he could justify it enough that he could bring himself to do it. Of course, vampires aren't so tough that they can get into fights with humans and win without using their special powers. So he had to violate the masquerade. And thus draw attention to himself. To mitigate this, he's adopted a disguise and an alternate identity, so that the vampire powers that be are forced to chase after a specter, and he can hide himself away otherwise.

    Essentially, through a series of accidents and natural logical leaps, this mild-mannered, well-meaning guy has become Vampire-Batman.

    At first, it wasn't too bad. Vampires occasionally have to make use of their disciplines to feed and the authorities that be (The setting is this kind of amalgam of Requiem and Masquerade, where it's the requiem clans, but with the Camarilla and Sabbat and Caine and all that good stuff) are used to dealing with it. But over the past years, more and more reports have been filtering in of a hooded figure hiding behind a mask, defending the weak with inhuman powers. Human authorities have been writing it off as local superstition, but the Camarilla knows better. And our story is opening right as they're getting ready to crack down.




    Onto the advice I need.



    The biggest thing is that I'm still torn on is a clan for this guy. I have two immediate inklings



    1. Nosferatu

    Pros: The Nos really play well to the fact that this guy has been transformed into a monster against his will, and from a character perspective it makes a lot of sense to give him a curse like that for him to rail against. I like the image of this guy still trying to be kind and generous to the people around him and being unable because they're all reacting to him with sheer terror. From a mechanical perspective, he gets the 2 disciplines I know I'm going to need to rely on, Vigor and Obfuscate, as clan disciplines. Nightmare also works great with the concept as something he can unleash against the baddies beyond just wailing on them.

    Cons: This might be petty, but I really like the image of the guy with the hood and the mask and why would he even need the mask if his actual face is horrifying and monstrous looking. At that point he doesn't have an identity that he needs to hide. He can't go outside anyway. He may as well just walk around in jeans and a t-shirt. I know in requiem Nosferatu don't have to be ugly, and they just have this "menacing aura" but eh? I wouldn't fault another player for it, but for me it feels like a cop-out to make a Nosferatu and not fuck up his face at least a little bit. They're the ugly vampires, damn it. They're the Anti-Rice! On a mechanical note, I only need the one dot of obfuscate, and it's a generic discipline so I can take the one dot at character creation and not deal with it anymore. And if all I need is vigor at that point, I may was well get a clan or a bloodline that'd give it to me and get some other disciplines I'd like to fill out more. I also don't know how effective Nightmare really is in play. Does anyone know how good a discipline it is in practice? (Post blood and smoke)



    2. Daeva

    Pros: Essentially that he doesn't have to deal with the cons of being a Nosferatu, and still has vigor. I also know from experience what a great discipline majesty is and it seems like it would be all-around more useful. Also the idea of dressing up in a costume is ridiculous and flashy enough that it seems like the kind of thing a Daeva would do. I'm also planning on making this guy as much a social character as he is a fighter, to represent his human life in addition to the skills he's had to pick up to survive, and that works a bit more with a Daeva, who's a lot better equipped to be a social character.

    Cons: Doesn't have all the flavor that comes with Nos mixing in with his character. At least no more than any other generic vampire. Also planning on making this guy more brute strength than grace and speed which doesn't totally gel with my concept of a Daeva.



    Nosferatu seems like the better fit overall, but there's enough there making me hesitant that I'm tempted to go with Daeva, which I have more experience playing with.



    Also not sure on what would be some good merits for him. I put down a few dots of fighting style: martial arts and the obligatory dot in resources, but beyond that I'm not sure what would really be good for the concept.

                                                  I just saw this, but I feel like pointing out that A Hunger Like Fire by Greg Stolze is a Vampire: The Requiem novel which deals with this exact issue at one point in the book, and shows some of the consequences that arise from going after people you think "deserve it."

                                                  It can make for great dramatic play, and your CHARACTER almost certainly won't see how this doesn't end well, but as a player, you should definitely keep in mind that it's a dangerous practice to feed on people you have contempt for.

                                                  Kindred feeding is not an exact science, and if you hate someone for something they've done and it makes you angry while you're feeding, you are really at a fair risk of draining them without realizing what you're doing and frenzying and killing them. Ironically, feeding on people who don't deserve it is probably easier, because you're motivated to not just drain them dry.

                                                  Mechanically, your character will also suffer breaking points for assaulting these people while feeding. Violence like that is dehumanizing. You might find the character's Humanity descending as a consequence of his practices, which might even start to negate the reason he fed that way in the first place.

                                                  If this is coming across like a warning, please let me clarify: This is awesome. This is the stuff Requiem is made to be about. Examining how your character engages with this idea and the pitfalls of it is perfectly suited to Requiem. That's a cool direction to take your character in.
