    "Is gameline X dead/dying" isn't a particularly rare question/topic of discussion. With OPP's inability to produce dedicated books for more than a few of the literal dozen CofD games at a time, and at a glacial pace for the games OPP is actively focusing on, it's no wonder that a lot of core fans (including some who actively participate here and/or the official Discord) have such doubts.
    That list includes books for Mage, Changeling, bluebook CofD, Deviant, Mummy, and Hunter, totalling half of the games. It does not include books for Vampire, Werewolf, Promethean, Geist, Demon, or Beast. For all we know, neither of those will get another dedicated book. Especially Vampire's future look grim (even though Spilled Blood is one of the more recent books) since material by alumni writers that could've been compiled to a full book has been released on the STV instead. And even if we do get more books for them as early as next year (doubtful, considering they haven't even been announced yet), that means there would've been a four year gap between books for Promethean, Demon and Beast.

                    I'd like an eventual 3d edition of Requiem to be more ST-beginner friendly.

                    This is a more general consideration that applies to other gamelines as well. You have a Storytelling chapter with all kinds of advices on how to handle specific themes and scenes and questions to ask players etc., and yet I have still no clue on how to run a Requiem chronicle.

                    By reading the book I think an aspiring ST should have an idea of how Requiem stories may look like.
                    If I'm about to run a D&D for the first time, I have a good idea of what a typical adventure is supposed to be, and I know exactly where to start: make a dungeon, fill it with monsters, put some magic loot, end with a boss fight, done. Of course, this is useful as a starting point: as the DM grows more experienced and confident, stories will probably deviate more from the standard dungeon-crawl.
                    At least in 1st Edition you had some suggestions on the types of conflicts you could focus on, some examples of story hooks, chronicle ideas etc. That's something I find lacking in 2E.
                    You might say that CofD gamelines tend to have a more sandbox approach: in that case, the book should provide ST advices and tools like Apocalypse World does.

                    Another thing that would need to change in 3d ed: less Strix.
                    Yes, they're a cool addition. But come on.
                    Too many Strixes.
                    There is a staggering 19, NINETEEN different Strix NPCs, and not even a SINGLE sample Ghoul or Kindred NPC.

                    So I guess a bit more variety and wider range of ready-made stuff for beginner STs would be nice.

                                I was just thinking about something, from my understanding diablerie is supposed to be akin to consuming a vampire soul and thats scary but why only vampires? why doesn't it work for humans too for example? or even other supernaturals? I think that a system similar to the blood resonance from v5 would be a nice addition, by "diablerizing" a human you would get a "resonance xp" that can only be spent on disciplines, this would be conceptually the vampire getting stronger by consuming the souls of other living beings (of course animals wouldn't count) at the cost of their humanity (humanity 1 breaking point), this would of course be different from draining someone without diablerizing them. Now, other supernatural would offer the same benefits of diablerizing another vampire, you would get a dot of blood potency and a dot in one of their skills that are higher than yours (of course you wouldn't get dots in your disciplines). I think this would make vampires more scary without making them too OP, since, aside from the humanity cost, you would still have to overpower another splat (this might be feasible with a changeling or even an unprepared mage but a werewolf, a demon or even a promethean? good luck with that).

                                    In Requiem the explanation is that vampires are the ones with their souls strongly attached to their corpses to an unusual, that is what makes it possible to be attempted in the first place. Trying to consume souls was possible, but would require either a power based upon enhancing basic capacities-conditions of kindred nature, like the apocryphal and very frowned upon "Coil of the Locust" within the Ordo Dracul, or some sort of obscure bloodline power, like Requiem's Tremere bloodline apparenly did. And if memory tricks me not, neither case it was as permanent as "vanilla" diablerie, due to differences in nature of the souls consumed.

                                    Simply put, what passes for the soul within a vampire's undead carcass - and is consumed through diablerie - is not quite the same as a human soul anymore, and the capacity for diablerie within vampires is directly connected to whatever changes in the soul when one becomes an eternally ravening but thinking, speaking & walking unspoiled corpse.


                                    Also remember how other supernaturals are specially incompatible with vampirism in the first place, as demonstrated by how messy attempts to embrace Garou or Awakened can be.

                                    Wraiths-risen and the "Wan Kuei" (that might not be canon in V5 for all i know) might make for interesting edge cases in that they are thinking, speaking & walking shades unusually attached to the mortal world and/or their past, through either fetters & passions (wraiths), corpses (risen, though far from unspoiled in many cases) and "wan kuei" (that even though they fit the whole bill, by the book could not commit or suffer diablerie, for reasons).

                                    Aditionally, in VtM's case, there's also the extra complication - at least if one believes in the noddist/caine narrative - that "consumption of the Heart's Blood" was supposedly one of the angelic-divine-whatever curses bestowed upon Caine, to make all its undead "descendants" hunger for him and each other, suffering much conflict & misery for it and if taken as fact, as direct a statement as possible of Diablerie as a cainite-only thing. but that one completely depends on "all kindred descend from Cain, who was cursed by the biblical god/its angels" as a true fact, so take it with a gram (or whole sack, in my case) of salt.

                                        Originally posted by Newb95 View Post
                                        I was just thinking about something, from my understanding diablerie is supposed to be akin to consuming a vampire soul and thats scary but why only vampires? why doesn't it work for humans too for example?
                                        There are at least two pieces of developed talent on the books for exactly that. It's optional for a reason and tends to have fewer returns than committing the Amaranth on another vampire. It's more of a curiosity than anything, because if a vampire wants to make a mortal a part of themselves they usually start by feeding on them, making them a ghoul, and/or making them a vampire; you can't get more of them if you drink them down to the roots, after all.

                                        I think this would make vampires more scary without making them too OP, since, aside from the humanity cost, you would still have to overpower another splat (this might be feasible with a changeling or even an unprepared mage but a werewolf, a demon or even a promethean? good luck with that).
                                        I will point out that the Player's Guide to the Contagion makes plainer the fact that vampires share the distinction of "can straight-up yank out your soul and destroy you" with Unchained demons, who can't wrestle it out of your unconscious spirit and instead need a willingly-given (albeit coercion is fair game, but mind control is not) signature (in blood) on a pact whose terms lay out when they're allowed to repossess your identity.

                                        The next-closest alternative is the Tremere, who get the ability to literally pull your soul out of your body at sufficient levels of power but don't destroy you by doing so and can put it back or supply a replacement; particularly powerful ones can do so with the souls of vampires, werewolves, and changelings in addition to mages and mortals, but the Tremere are a particularly ruthless group of mages who've altered their template in the specific pursuit of mastering The Soul.
