    Social penalities for losing Humanity

    07-29-2017, 10:11 AM
    Hi folks,

    I am really not liking that penalities. It reads you get the penalities when "familiarize" with humans. How would that work, I mean practically in game. To give the penality in all mortal interactions seems way too punishing for Vampires who are build for social interactions. Even considering that vampires can in my mind be really convincing and seductive even with low humanity. When it only means you can not "get" humans, it comes up not often enough and would be an "Empathy" penality in a nutshell and a bit boring. Additionally, even psychopath are fully able to analyze you in full, even if they don´t familiarize with you. They don´t "get" the feelings, but they understand what makes you tick.

    So how about this:

    You roll humanity in the following situations:

    a) When a mortal suspects you not to be human. If you fail, he knows...
    b) You roll if a mortal pleas for your compassion, maybe if he asks you to help him or if he is in grave danger. If you fail, you are unable to care.

    Would that not be better?

          The specific phrasing in 2e is "relate." This isn't a blanket penalty to all social rolls for human interactions. It's a penalty on rolls that involve putting yourself in the other person's shoes. Vampires with low Humanity are going to have trouble with Soft Leverage (since you need to relate to the target to know how to appeal to them), but be just fine with Hard Leverage (where you can just use the instinct of self preservation against a target by threatening them with violence).

          There's no penalty to Discipline rolls there, and these penalties are (until Humanity 1), easily countered by using blood to thrall individuals.

          So socially built vampires can do just fine. The struggle is trying to be nice to mortals without also keeping your Humanity up. Being a cold manipulative bastard? Humanity isn't going to get in your way.

                  I'm not really sure where psychopaths figure into this. Being a vampire isn't a personality disorder -- it's an unusual series of life experiences that induce profound alienation. The problems a low-Humanity vampire has relating to mortals (and "relate" is, indeed, a key term) have to do with the huge gulf in experience between an average person and one of the living dead. Don't think "psychopath" or "sociopath" or any of those kinds of terms.

                  Instead, think about sitting down in a Starbucks with a kid you knew in high school... somebody who made the opposite of every choice you ever made. Somebody who got all the chances you didn't. Somebody whose daily routine is 100% composed of things you probably don't even do once a month. And then that guy wants advice on how to bridge the gulf with a family member you don't even have. And you just sit there, drawing a blank, because you cannot imagine what his life is like anymore.

                  That's low Humanity.

                  (EDIT: Also, in this metaphor, you don't like anything on the menu at Starbucks.)

                              Originally posted by Narr666 View Post
                              But how I see humanity is not really the point.
                              Sure... but that means you have to put your views on it aside and accept the RAW Humanity if we're just going to focus on if the RAW penalties are good/house rules are better aspect of things.

                              Right now if feels like you're "not convinced" more because of how you see Humanity, not because of any points people have made.

                              I search more for a alternative that is clearer and more fun than the penalities, where you in my opinion have to think every time it is "relating" to a human or not and feel a bit boring.
                              OK, so focusing back on this:

                              1) Humanity is now your "don't break the masquerade pool." this house rule creates a lot of mental hoops to jump through as a Humanity 10, Manipulation 1, Subterfuge 0 character is far better at covering up the signs that they're a vampire than a character with Humanity 5, Manipulation 5, and Subterfuge 5. I don't see how this generates fun at the table, as it divorces being convincing about not being a vampire completely from character proficiency at being convincing in general. It's also not an improvement on situational clarity, because you just replaced, "is this a roll that involves relating to a human," with, "is this a situation where a human suspects they're dealing with a supernatural." The ST has to constantly be deciding when in the ambiguous process of suspicion a human is suspicious enough to warrant a roll.

                              2) Rolling to care is decidedly boring and unfun. My character being unmoved for empathetic reasons doesn't mean they don't care. If my vampire thinks the human is a valuable pawn, he's going to rescue him because of that even if any appeals to morality or humanity were pointless to start with. Whether or not my character cares, and why they care, shouldn't be up to the dice. If my character as two Touchstones, both of which are also protected by the True Friend merit... why the heck should I ever have to roll to care about them? I built that character to explicitly care about them. It's not really engaging to play to have to roll to help them because they asked. That also brings in a lack of clarity. When I have to roll for this isn't clear. What's a plea for compassion vs. a plea for something else?

                                              I have an unofficial rule or guide as a Storyteller. When I portray an NPC, I try to include two qualities, Role-play their dialogs and some action or mannerism description. But when vampire players are interacting with Humans NPC, I drop this qualities along with Humanity; first the descriptions and then the role-play dialog, i.e. I use my poker face. This encourage them to rely on dice to read the situation or the NPC, calling on the Humanity penalty more often (its a bit tricky with a party with a very diverse Humanity). I'll try an example: If a vamp player with Humanity 3 is questioning a Human, instead of me role-playing him slightly raising my voice and saying, "I don't know anything!" and add that he did so while avoiding to look at their face--- I just tell them in a calm voice, "He says he doesn't know anything." A vamp with Humanity 4 would get the role-play dialog but no the description. Don't know if this helps in anyway.

                                              Additionally, Awe, first dot of the Majesty Discipline, voids a lot of penalties which is compatible with low Humanity vampires easily relating to Humans, so socially build vamps have no problems.
