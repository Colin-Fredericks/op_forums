    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Whether or not Auspex can detect lies is another point of contention, although not one that has gotten much focus in this thread.
    Well the wording says "allows to ask a question" and the examples cover things such as knowing someone's intent ("Who is about to attack me?"), value judgements ("Who is weakest"), inanimate objects ("what is most dangerous here?" example answer: "a car crusher"), mental state ("Who is most afraid?"), probability (several of the previous). There are no explicit limitations on the power as written and the examples show it to be able to access multiple categories of information and be sophisticated enough to make logical comparisons between them. I'd say lying is definitely a contender for a legitimate question.

    I think the intent of the rule is to be limited to fear / threat questions and for judgements to be based around the user's own mental processes ("I find the car crusher more dangerous than the police officer"). But it really needs some tightening up to actually reflect that.

        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
        I hate to open this can of worms again, but Auspex can only be verified by other Auspex users, which puts it into the same category as eyewitness testimony. Which is to say, unreliable evidence on its own, but a good reason to start looking for more concrete evidence. How your Kindred Court handles it is of course up to you as ST, but I would have most Kindred be hesitant to admit Auspex accounts as evidence, not the least of which because they know it could just as easily be used against them.
        I think this thread overlaps rather too much with the Diablerie and Auspex thread and could do with a merge if such things are done here, but I'll reply here as well. There's an important difference between Auspex and eye-witness testimony. Eye witness testimony is limited by happenstance and you have to hope you get a suitable set of eye-witnesses. Auspex can add eye-witnesses as needed (in theory) and the pool of them is likely to be a significant portion of your community. You can also draft in respected eye-witnesses post-fact as well. And eye-witness testimony is not a weak thing even in our world. People are routinely convicted from eye-witness testimony. Let alone when you can keep adding further eye-witnesses to see if they agree.
