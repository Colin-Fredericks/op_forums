      All of the background and setting material from the Clan books is still valid and fully compatible with 2nd edition. They're even listed in the 2nd edition core book as recommended inspirational reading. The rules are still usable as well, but may require some minor house ruling to fit into the 2nd edition rules. But they are fairly easy to incorporate if you want to. There are not currently any plans to remake the Clan books for 2nd edition, so you definitely don't need to worry about having to buy new versions of the same book in a year or so.

                              Originally posted by essen_ko View Post
                              Okay, then one more question :

                              Does buying the Danse Macabre book still makes sense now that the new version is out ?
                              Personally, I'm going to say it depends on what your'e trying to do. Danse Macabre came out at the same time as other Chronicler Guides were being made, but Requiem already had a book named that, so I think of it as a kind of Requiem Chronicler Guide 2. The big thing is that its a lot of hacks to the game - first, we should ask if you want to make a bunch of changes to the game?

                              The first part of the book talks about how to make different "tier" games work - at the street (covenants become philosophies), city (default), and global level. If you're happy with just the city level stuff, there's no change; while I like this section, its very short. Later on, there's also some new covenants to chew over, if you want them; personally, I think they act a lot like just variations on existing covenants, and have used them to inspire factions within my existing covenants rather than use them as their own thing.

                              The hacks to the clans are outdated, I think. Masks and Dirges likewise have turned into the new vampire virtue/vice already.

                              Then, we start getting into system hacks. Now, I like the Edge from social combat in theory, but I've found that players tend to avoid the social combat parts; meanwhile, we've had a blast with social maneuvering. No one's ever been interested in "Mental Combat," though I've had success with Discipline Combat, ripping off the Duel Arcane. Down and Dirty Combat? Tilts? Part of 2e by default. Dead Echoes: Manifesting Disciplines gives bonuses to synergetic skills. Which, while interesting in theory, is just flat bonuses to skills; kind of redundant with things like Vigor or Majesty already granting bonuses; given the boost in power of disciplines, you really don't need skill bonuses. Not quite outdated, but certainly not worthwhile in my opinion.Hell is Other People feels like the Touchstone mechanic. Banes are part of core rules now. Dearly Devoted gave some rules for making devotions (outdated with how the disciplines work now) and the idea you can get devotins from a quest instead of XP spending (don't need book for).

                              Meanwhile, Atrocity still works if you want an alternative Humanity system to work with. Its completely different in theme and feel from both what the new Humanity reflects, but if you want to really play up the atrocious monster, this is for you. The Gargoyle stuff is still relevant.

                              Last chapter of the book deals with some sample chronicle ideas, nothing really jumping out that isn't covered elsewhere.



                              So, in the end, Danse Macabre really only helps out with different tier games, suggested new covenants, some setting ideas, the Gargoyles, and maybe an alternative Integrity system if you like it really dark. Something between a seventh and a tenth of the entire book possibly relevant, and most of that depends on how much you like going through books like this for story ideas instead of making them on your own. If you prefer doing your own stories, or have other sources, then that just leaves Gargoyles, which isn't enough of a draw to buy the book.

                              I personally do not suggest the book - I'd recommend Damnation City first.
