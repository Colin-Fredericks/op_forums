    Clarification about the Beatdown condition?

    07-14-2016, 12:59 AM
    I need some clarification on the beatdown rule, following an encounter in my group's most recent session. I'll explain the situation, and hope to get a clearer understanding of how it should have resolved (and hopefully I explain this clearly):

    We have Characters A, a vampire with low Humanity (certainly he has no qualms with committing murder), attacking Character B.

    Character B, however, hits Character A with more damage than his Stamina, thus giving him the beatdown condition. At this point, if Character B states he just wants Character A to get lost, the conflict is resolved by Character A fleeing, correct? Or is character A immune to beatdown because he's too low humanity? I would think Humanity only applies to Character B, to ignore the effect of Character A's condition.

    Would anyone mind running through this with me?

      If murder would not be a Breaking Point for character A, character A does not gain the Beaten Down Tilt and can continue to attack character B without spending Willpower or facing a Breaking Point, even if character B surrenders. However, character A is still free to surrender to character B if he feels the fight is going poorly for him and he doesn't want to draw it out any longer.

                                                  The mechanic is really only supposed to be considered when all sides of a fight don't really want to kill each other.                                                       Originally posted by Blitzburger View Post
                                                      Can anyone explain to me the logic of having humanity have any bearing at all on whether a defender is beatdown or not? Why would low Humanity indicate any less of a survival instinct?
                                                      It's not so much a matter of lacking survival instinct as it is a desensitization to violence. These characters are still perfectly capable of deciding "hey, this guy is a lot tougher than we expected him to be, and just wants to be left alone. Let's do that." They even gain a point of Willpower for surrendering in that way. They just don't get the fight knocked out of them to the point where even defending themselves takes significant effort and costs Willpower.

                                                      Also, I would argue that Humanity loss alone isn't enough to inure anyone short of Draughur to Beaten Down. As I mentioned, since "heinous, spree, or mass murder" is a Breaking Point right down to Humanity 1, I would personally rule that they're still subject to the Tilt. Unless they get rid of that one last qualm about killing with a Bane. And at that point I'd say they're sufficiently alien that it makes sense for them to be able to shrug off a savage beating.

                                                      Since you asked in your other post, I believe that's the philosophy behind certain characters being immune to Beaten Down. In a game where ordinary people confront actual monsters, it's thematically appropriate for the monsters to take no quarter and fight to their last breath. It would be pretty weird if the slavering, bloodthirsty beast turned tail after being punched a few times, or couldn't bring themselves to continue tearing into their prey when it surrendered. "Doesn't consider murder a Breaking Point" happens to be the standard metric in CofD for when you cross the line from person to monster.

                                                      And again, these characters aren't mindless. They can still make the rational decision that a fight isn't going in their favor and the most prudent course of action is to just give the other party what they want and walk away from the conflict. But if they're inhuman enough that they would have no compunctions about killing their victim if the tables were turned, they are also inhuman enough that they never reach the point where they can't fight back. Surrender becomes a choice, rather than something they do because they can't take any more beatings.
