                                If I were an undead ruler and I needed someone to keep Werewolves in check in my territory I would NOT rely on a Gangrel brute, that is a dangerous one shot crap weapon. I would rely on a tough and intelligent agent capable of actually filling the role (could be any clan really). Vampires who tend to use brawn are not going to live long enough and are just going to make matters worse.

                                As in all other facet of life and violent situations in particular it is all about stacking the card as much in your favor. This means that knowledge about the situation and opponent are by far more important than your dots in Vigor or Protean. Knowing what is enough manpower and/or controlling the environment or when to strike is whats going to make you a successful enforcer. A stupid enforcer go in and fight without knowing they will win no matter what.

                                You can discuss what power will do what in a physical confrontation, but if you enter into such a situation without being prepared and armed to the teeth you are just being stupid. Relying on personal prowess are just throwing the dice and hoping for the best. This will eventually get you killed once you encounter an unexpected factor.

                                Creating a character with the intent of physically beating a Werewolf in single combat are in my opinion the least effective way dealing with the problem. I have no problem with a vampire being a physically powerful character so they can survive an encounter with a Werewolf. But if you are physically threatened your first priority should be retreat and not offense, you never know what other surprises awaits you of you engage with no means to escape. You will in this instance need to retreat and come back tenfold in strength. You NEVER fight with equal odds, that is just stupid and a borderline naive way to solve a problem.

                                Players in my campaigns are very reluctant to engage in physical confrontations since they are so uncertain (unless the outcome are clear)... anything from the dice rolls going against them to not being able to controlling the situation most of the time. This is true no matter what role-playing system we play... I usually do not reward wanton violence just to spice up the game... to me violence need to make sense and be dramatic and realistic to some degree. A player character that tend over-commit will often also die... often sooner rather than later... this is very realistic.

                                Enough ranting on my part...
