            Sorry. I just realized this was a thread about the origins of the Strix.
            Hey dude, I enjoyed the tangent. I got the CtL book for my partner and haven't read it because she's planning to run a game, so I'm really starved of lore.


            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
            the reality of their position in regards to either is minimally important. It takes something outside of context to really make the origin change the way you look at them... the origin of the Strix primarily matters in regard to the flavor of horror you want to deliver. Their more-human origins causes self-reflection,.. I mean, otherwise, the origin of the Strix is a non-point, since their Gordian Knot seems to just be inherent most of the time, and the narratives they present justifying it are just tales to tell the kid before putting them to sleep.
            So, the problem that the Strix present in-game is "serial killing possession demon", and in terms of conflict, their origin doesn't change this problem. No matter what, they're serial killing possession demons. You're right that the choice of origin advances certain themes in the VtR game, but I think the choice is also important for how STs portray the Strix. Like, if I'm playing a spurned human spirit, I'll be giving a different mood than if I am playing a sentient void (kind of like the Spectres from His Dark Materials). And I'm going to make different choices as well.

            They're hungry hateful things, but we(I) need an origin for characterization purposes. In my game, I have a Strix targeting one of my PCs' younger siblings. But I literally could not figure out how to guide it's actions without an intelligible motivation. The story-planning problem, presented by the Strix was Which particular choice does this hateful, sadistic, jealous thing that directs cruelty towards children and their caretakers make next? If your Strix doesn't have some master plan, and is only acting out of sadistic jealousy, we(I) need more information to determine the next action. This was before Rose's origin had come out, and I came up with a plague that wiped out a village's children.

            I love Rose's origin, but I think that any choice of origin needs to serve the role that the Strix play in your particular story. For my story, it was the emotional residue of a thousand babies dying painfully. It was the right answer, relative to this particular story, because it helped me to coherently guide the character's actions in a way that wasn't just meta-gaming. With a particular origin in mind, I can drop a lot of smaller facts and actions that hint at some larger picture. Even if the origin never comes out, the story ends up being much fuller.


            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
            I feel it's worth pointing out Rose's old "vampirism is a wound" analogy
            I really like this conception. I'd been thinking of it as "something that happens when something goes very wrong". But it brings in a weird, almost Aristotelian essentialism that I don't really like. I think framing it as a wound is much better than as a deviation.

                                It's also possible that Lilith/Owl simply was shattered by Wolf before his end, making the Strix a Host.
                                Strix are thematically and mechanically similar to Hosts, with how they take over humans, and how they can eat each other to get more powerful as well as split up into multiple lesser beings. What differs mechanically is their traits, but most of them can be explained as being adjusted for VtR instead of WtF. Even their shadowy Vitae could actually be a type of Essence of vampiric resonances and made visible as a quirk of this particular Host. Thematically speaking they differ from most Hosts in that they have nothing to do with the Gauntlet, but nothing says all Hosts need to focus on that. The connection to a Lower Depths realm is also atypical of Hosts, but that's something Owl might've found before her shattering, and the resulting connection between her Shards and Dis might be the reason behind any differences between Strix and other Hosts.

                                It's not my preferred take, but it makes a lot more sense to me than Strix being the spirits of humanity. Though I have to admit I haven't read the text and don't know if or how Rose explained Strix not being ephemeral, not being in the Shadow, not using Essence, etc.

                                        Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                                        Strix not being ephemeral, not being in the Shadow, not using Essence, etc.
                                        I know absolutely nothing about Werewolf or the lore surrounding spirits. So this is my take:

                                        There was an Enlightenment philosopher, Rousseau, who thought that humans literally changed their natures by entering into civilization. That the ability to change our own natures is an essential part of what it is to be human. I like to think that in the Chronicles, Rousseau was correct.

                                        If that's the case, it would make some sense that our spirits would have the ability to change their natures as well. So when we changed our natures, our spirits said "What? No, fuck that. And fuck being a reflection of you," and literally became something else.

                                        It would also explain one of the features of the Strix that really struck me when I first read their chapter; it's almost impossible to bind them to anything (to their words, to some action, or an object) Their natures/essence includes immediate short-term freedom.
