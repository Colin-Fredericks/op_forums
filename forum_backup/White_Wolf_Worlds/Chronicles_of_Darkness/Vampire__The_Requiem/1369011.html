    The Everlasting- Crossover Thoughts

    02-19-2020, 04:47 PM
    Once upon a midnight darkly, there was an empire, great and terrible. Guided by sorcerer kings created in the image of the gods, it grew and conquered and devoured all it could, dedicating itself to laws of suffering and the endless hunger of oblivion. Slowly in surely, it feasted upon the misery of its people, until on night, when the stars were right, it devoured itself with its hunger, leaving no name behind and scattering its remains across space and time. And for the sorcerer kings? In their pride and lust of power they descended to the depths of the earth, where they demanded from their cruel gods the gifts meant for human souls. The gods, however, denied them- and left them to rot in their prison, feasting on vinegar and ash miserably ever after...

    Or did they?

    It is easy to notice that Spilled Blood has its fair share of crossover references. Werewolf, Promethean, Hunter, Geist, Demon and Beast are all easy to track down, either through mentioning a certain individual or actually having a deeper connection between a splat or antagonist and another gameline. However, some references may be more subtle than it seems in first impression. Like, the Nereids for example- are they bound by Sea's eternal hunger, or perhaps they are touched by the depths of the Ocean of Fragments? Heck, why not both? Each could have its own potential for a crossover game, after all...

    The Everlasting are even more subtle, IMO.

    I mean, think about it for a second- we have immortal beings originated from the ancient Middle East, sometimes viewed as gods, which can barely be destroyed and even then their heart is indestructible, which also have power over time itself and have a golden, sweet liquid flowing through their veins called "Ichor"...

    Yeah, just change Ichor to Sekhem and tell me it does not sound familiar.

    Now, while having a connection to Sekhem may explain a thing or two about the Everlasting- what if it is not all? After all, many immortals have connection to Sekhem, from Blood Bathers to Harvesters, from Body Thieves to the Purified, from the Visitors to the Reborn- and, of course, the Arisen. Compared to all but the last one, the Everlasting are a few levels above them. No meager trick at immortality can achieve what the Everlasting has- and that means something. The Everlasting are not simply Timeless. They are Deathless.

    But you can't be Deathless without a sacrifice.

    I mean, even if we were to examine the Minor Guilds, no other form of immortality is as complete as that of the Everlasting. Cursed with plague, bound to weapon and stealing the lives of a mortal are nothing like the powers of the Rite of Return, and even the Deceived had their gods broken and bound to their own flesh. The Everlasting's immortality is one level beyond all of those, and while it does not carry with it the raw power of Utterances and Affinities, nothing can deny that their immortality is compared to those of the Guilds of Irem.

    So- what are they?

    They are not mummies, that's for sure. They may even not be Iremite- as they are all of seemingly Mesopotamian origin, not North African. They also seem to have some similarities to vampires, with their Ichor being so addicting. Maybe they were another experiment done by the Shan'iatu- yet compared to the mistakes they had in the Lifeless and the Minor Guilds, it is hard for me to imagine it as their failure. I mean, the Shan'iatu may have the potential for doing great things when they succeed- but when they fail, they fail miserably. I would have suggested them to be vampires exposed to Sekhem, but we already know from Dark Eras 2 that a Vampire + Sekhem = Bloodless, that is, nothing as magnificent as the Everlasting. Once, I had a theory that the Judges of Life has sent more than one group of 42 demigods to the world- instead, they created one per each Cradle of Civilization, including Sumer, Egypt, India, China, Mexico and Peru. Sure, there is nothing to support that theory and there are many things against it, but the concept was still intriguing, IMO. Wherever there is civilization, there is Sekhem, and where there is Sekhem, there are those sent to guide it, right? And who knows what kinds of wonders and horrors did those shepherds of civilization spawned from their own cultures..

    Heck, what if the Everlasting were not the creation of the Shan'aitu- maybe they are the Shan'iatu?

    Sure, the Shan'iatu were described as very mutable beings, with the words of creation on their lips. However, as time progressed, they have slowly got bound into form, and started to forget the language of the gods. Oh, we should know what have happened to them- they are feasting on vinegar and ash, pretending at being gods. But not all of them- the Namers got broken into pieces, after all, and the Healers (if they indeed has Shan'iatu, which I think so) were consumed by the Contagion (or similar occult plague). What if a few have found escape from Irem, taking the faces of mortals and slowly falling victim to their habits, getting stuck in time? Maybe the Everlasting are indeed the gods, bound in their own power...

    While that theory is fun, it does not actually fit the theme of the book- Spilled Blood is about vampires, and more than that, is about how the Blood changes and transforms. So yeah, presenting the Shan'iatu as antagonists is something more for Mummy's Night Horrors, not Vampire's. And again, Spilled Blood is about the changes of the Blood- and as such, the Everlasting should be connected to vampires, and changed by it.

    Maybe there was a separate group of Shan'iatu in Sumer who did experiments upon vampires. Perhaps there wasn't, and after Irem conquered the Ki-En-Gir some of Mesopotamia's vampires were gifted with the golden ichor of the gods, drinking deep from the Shan'iatu themselves. And maybe there were other gardeners bound by Sekhem unrelated to the Nameless Empire and its cruel gods, and those were the ones who either made the vampires, or perhaps made the Everlasting from vampires. Who knows? Ages ha passed since then...

    So what do you think? Are the Everlasting indeed a reference for a Mummy Crossover? And what kind of theories do you have about whatever connection there is between those elusive demigods and Irem's eldritch immortals?
