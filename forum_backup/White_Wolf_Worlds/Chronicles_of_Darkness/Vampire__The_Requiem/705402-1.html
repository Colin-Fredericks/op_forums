    I think the Ancient books, as well as the clan books, go a long way towards building the lore and keeping it completely different from Masquerade. There's a lot of lore in vampire. The main thing that keeps it distinguished from Masquerade is that it is not all-encompassing of all vampires, nor is it all known or agreed upon.

    The idea that neolithic vampires created human civilization is something a vampire in Requiem would totally imply, if not outright state. The more arrogant might even claim to be descended from the vampires that did it, while the more savvy would wave that off as statistically unlikely while also, cannily, implying that they might be.

    But for the most part, new World of Darkness sticks to more low-key and realistic depictions of things. If there were groups of vampires, they would be scattered and disparate and unorganized. In one place they may manage to pull things together and get a blood farm going, in others a vampire might try it only to find that the humans left while they slept. Just like one group of people didn't create agriculture and spread across the globe instilling it into others, one group of vampires wouldn't create static settlements and travel around trapping humans with it. It's the kind of thing that would require insane amounts of resources and foresight, as well as motivation.

                                                            It's somewhat ironic that vampire is one of the splats least anchored in the past*, while we've gotten the most historical information about it, through the Rome books and Ancient books. I think the Camarilla formed in Rome was considered by vampires at the time, in Rome, to be the height of vampire organization, while older vampires in the know would sort of roll their eyes thinking, themselves, about some great vampire community of the past that had fallen. Almost every chapter in the Ancients book describes some great feat of community once you go back far enough, with En Isiratuu ruling all of a nation for a tremendously long time as En, or Lord, of vampires. While both the Ancients books and clanbook Mekhet delve a bit into the Egyptian past and their strong influence there, where they even worked alongside a hunter conspiracy to defend Egypt from supernatural threats.

                                                            But like human legends and human places, these things fall with time, the survivors scatter, specifics are lost, holes in memories open and fill with hopes or hatreds. The Lancea et Sanctum is called out for actually hiding or destroying historical evidence of vampire 'civilization' from long ago. It's all very interesting.
