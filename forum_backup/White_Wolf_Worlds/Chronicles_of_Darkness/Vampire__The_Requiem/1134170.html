    How do you Vampire in a world full of Mind Control detection?

    08-26-2017, 01:08 PM
    We, for a moment, going to set aside the ST responsibility of building a world that allows the players to individually shine. Lets assume that there is a world where Changelings and Mages are are rampant, meaning that Kenning, Mage Sight, and Mind 1 spells are also commonplace. How would a potential Majesty or Dominate focused Vampire leverage their talents in this world against those supernaturals without getting half the setting turning on them?

    The only way I can think of includes a custom Obfuscate Devotion to hide Mind Control.

      Well, vampires should think twice before pissing off most other templates. They're really only on sort of equal footing with Changelings (they were in 1e anyway), unless we're talking fire elemental or shit like that. But werewolves ? They don't even really need to detect such attempts, they just need to become angry or suspicious enough that they decide they've had enough of you. And mages, well, mages had the potential to pretty much crush everything else in 1e, so the only answer I'd give would be to just leave them alone. But let's be honest here, nobody really likes vampires in the WoD. attempts at mind control or the such are sometimes just icing on the cake. You're still a dead thing that eats people to survive. Maybe some templates can relate, but they're pretty likely to mostly consider you a threat and maybe something to kill just to be on the safe side. Plus, to me, when you're a Ventrue or a Daeva, you'll tend to use your powers on those weaker than you, just in case you fail the roll. Rarely will you attempt to dominate someone who can wreck your shit, mostly because you should avoid being in a position where it's your only choice (granted a gaming will tend to feature those situations, but you know what I mean). Normal humans are the best target, as they're the easiest and also the least likely to understand what happened when it's over.

      Anyway, my answer to the issue, and that's mostly because I've only DM'd vampire games so far, is to consider that vampires are simply the most common creatures of the setting, followed by the werewolves, except I tend to put werewolves closer to non-urban areas, while vampires dominate those. For me, they also tend to try to keep out of each other's hair. My vampires also get pretty involved in the mortal world and their governments try to have some measure of political power at their disposal that they can control with blood bonds and disciplines. That means that while they're not the most powerful creatures individually, if you start causing them enough trouble that they notice something's up, they'll be able to drop the hammer on you without even necessarily needing to go after you themselves.

      I tend to assume Changelings are less numerous than Vampires, more interested in keeping a low profile than them, and that they deal with each other more, and so don't really cover as much territory. They don't hold as much power (maybe the idea of making people their puppets someone doesn't appeal to them as much for obvious reasons), so they can't really challenge vampires in that area, but they're also harder to get a hold on since they don't have to sleep during the day and can escape into the Hedge. Mages, for me, are a rarity. You'll only find a few in a given city, and have preoccupations beyond what vampires tend to care about. Of course, I don't know the mage book that much, so I mostly don't use them, really. My take on it is : they exist, probably, but you've never met one, and you don't really know what they might be up to.

      Generally, the core books for each template tend to assume that the other templates don't exist. They're barely mentioned, more as some kind of pointer for if you want to involve them, but you're really going to have to come up with the inter-template dynamics by yourself (I'm a player in a werewolf game, and in the city we're in, werewolves have the upper hand, and vampires are tolerated as long as they don't kill people and cause trouble). If you were to include all templates and try to come up with a believable situation in a given large city, chances are, you'd have more a situation where you have a global underworld with the various creatures interacting in different ways depending on what's in their interests. The Invictus might try to make money by renting ghoul footsoldiers or political favours, perhaps causing the Carthians to compete with them in that department so they don't fall behind. The Lancea et Sanctum might be singled out as they're very vampire-centric and few people would like the idea of professional monsters causing suffering among the humans for some divine mission. The Circle of the Crone could end up hanging out with Werewolves for their spiritual aspect (or they could clash with them if they interact with spirits in a way werewolves deem irresponsible), while the Ordo Dracul would maybe try to deal with changelings and mages for knowledge and artifacts from other worlds (although in some interpretations, they tend to dismiss everything non-vampire, thinking it can't help them do what they want), and either deal or compete with werewolves for access to places of power (which may or may not be loci and/or wyrm nests). Some changelings could actually welcome all that stuff and be perfectly happy to live alongside dozens of really dangerous and nasty people that could see a true fae as an invader.

                                  Their point still applies. Unless there's some kind of self-preservation or personal motivation involved or setting specific information I don't know about, I don't see Vampires running around and deliberately mind controlling normal people, much less specifically targetting other supernaturals. It is a waste of their most valued commodity, blood. Let's take a look at the most likely way a Vampire is going to encounter another supernatural he's not specifically looking to find for personal reasons. That's going to be in the process of hunting for food.

                                  A Vampire uses Dominate to lure a bar patron away for a few points of blood disguised as anonymous sex. This particular bar patron is a Changeling but the Vampire doesn't know that and it's immaterial. That Changeling is successfully Mesmerized and Iron Edicted out of the bar and down to a motel room half a block away. He doesn't get to independantly decide he's going to turn on Kenning and identify that Vampire is using Mind Control and refuse. He's going to obey the directives given him, have the anonymous sex while the vampire takes 2-3 points of vitae. And this next part is important: The Changeling is not going to remember anything about that encounter. By default, mesmerized victims don't remember anything that happened while they're acting out directions given while they are mesmerized.
                                  Maybe the vampire will use Entombed Command or The Lying Mind to attach some details about the encounter depending on how long it takes (And may also involve including a description of the encounter with a person who does not look like the vampre at all) to cover up the missing time. If it's less than an hour maybe the vampire will just tell the person to go back to the bar where they met that Changeling who will simply think they lost track of time and go about his own business - which is probably just looking for a human whom they can sponge glamour from anyway.

                                  Same scenario, but this Vampire uses Majesty. Awe turned on when they walk in the bar. The Changeling notices the Vampire. The Vampire decides the Changeling gets to be their meal. The Changeling decides they aren't going to use Kenning because they're more interested in ripping the clothes off that Vampire. It may cross their mind that they'll try and get some glamour off the Vampire. They'll remember the encounter and they'll remember that they freely decided to go. Unless the Vampire makes additional requests, they aren't going to think twice about it unless they encounter the Vampire again later.

                                  I'm not going to muddy the waters any further by pointing out that Predatory Auras and the Kiss allows every vampire do these things with varying degrees of skill as well. My point is, the very thought of mind control by someone else is scary. You don't know if what you believe, think, or remember is what happened or even if your actions are of your own volition or because it's all what someone told you. Other supernaturals aren't going to just go willingly screw with someone who can do that because unless they're metagaming out the ass, they don't automatically know the full extent of a vampire's capabilities and limitations on such powers. And if they're the type who uses Mind Control as well, then they have no moral ground to justify a witch hunt.

                                  The Supernatural Prime Directive Continues.

                                          I'll give you my opinion or the matter, because I like the subject it touches on, and I'll give you an option I used in the past.

                                          My opinion is, as others have said, that each splat should be played mostly as if others don't exist (I know, "should" is subjective, but you get my point). I play Vampire, and the game is very vampire centric. So far in the chronicle, no other supernatural has been encountered. However, playing that way (as if...) doesn't mean other splats don't exist. I actually used werewolves in a past chronicle that was very vampire centric.

                                          And that's when my option comes into play. When I introduce another splat in a vampire centric game, I am not trying to apply the rules, powers and game mechanics from the other splat. I just don't care. I might get inspiration from it, but mostly, I set up that splat so that it fits in my vampire centric game the way I want it to. In my previous example, I used the werewolves as physical killing machines that could disappear into some "other world". The explanation stopped there and that's exactly how I used them. They where a physical threat and I didn't dwell on the other aspects werewolves have in their own game.

                                          My point is, if you try to incorporate each splat exactly as they are in their own set of rules, you will run into a multitude of balancing issues that will cause you a headache for no good reason. So if your game is vampire centric and you want their mind powers to be relevant, just decide their mind powers are the most powerful of the lot and find one or two strengths for each of the others splats depending on how much of a threat you want them to be. If you like, you could even decide one of those plats actually has counters to vampiric mind powers to make them the main threat. It all depends on what role you want to give them.

                                          In the end, each of these added splats should be incorporated and defined not based on their own game line but on the role they will play within your vampire centric chronicle.

                                              Vampires as individual entities are just weak enough to be overlooked in a casual passing by, but just strong enough to make violence against them risky. You can pull your spell together before it can cross the crowded room. Right? What will the Guardians of the Veil think if you make a scene in front of this crowd?

                                              As an entrenched power structure? Cleaning an entire city is a daunting task. Sure, they don't operate on the same metaphysical level as the heavy hitters, but they work on an inherently human level. Cops, lawyers, janitors, private investigators, and mercenaries. Armies of them. Entire libraries of dirt on the local influential families extending back a couple hundred years or more. That Mage who has spent all his time hiding his true name to protect his mortal family and himself? Oops. I got his face on camera while he was pasting a few cronies outside a 7-11. A ghoul on sixth recognizes him as a regular in the supermarket. A packet of information based on his credit card purchases is dropped off on my desk right next to a full credit report. Turns out his financial situation is full of odd holes and there is a warrant out for his sister's arrest.

                                              To be honest? The best way to deal with them is probably to leave them alone (which is how I see the other supernaturals operating). They don't really muck about with spirits any worse than the seedier elements of humanity. They don't have a place on the table in the ascension war. The All Night society sticks to and polices itself with a machine like efficiency. It might even help clean up -your- messes on occasion.

                                                      In a fight between a vampire and a mage, the vampire needs to be able to get the first hit in and at least knock the mage unconscious in one hit. Otherwise, I think the fight favors the mage, especially if the mage has 3-4 dots in an Arcana. Mastigos can shrug off mental Disciplines, Obrimos can easily set a vamp on fire, Moros could have all sorts of fun with an undead creature, Thyrsus can basically nullify a Gangrel's advantage with Protean and Animalism, and Acanthus could have all sorts of fun with Time. I agree that a vampire's best bet would be to let their mortal network bring the mage down, and even then, I would give an Acanthus the edge thanks to Fate and Time.

                                                      So yeah, if your local vampire domain throws down with the local consilium, I think the consilium wins out in the end, but the vampires would really make them suffer for it. So if the Prince and the Hierarch know about each other, they are definitely telling their subordinates to stay the hell out of each other's business: the Prince to avoid certain destruction, the Hierarch to avoid a really costly and painful headache.
