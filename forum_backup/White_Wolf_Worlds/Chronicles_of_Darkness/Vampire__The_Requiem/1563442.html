    Newly Embraced Kindred - Do they get Bloodline bane?

    08-24-2026, 09:06 PM
    Newly embraced kindred do not suffer from their clan bane until they fall to humanity 6.

    Is that also the case for bloodline banes in the case of a kindred embraced into a bloodline?

    I can't find anything official.

    I'm not sure what i would do.

    On one hand, having an extra in-clan discipline at creation, which often include one of the five restricted discipline, is powerful enough to warrant an extra penalty.

    On the other, if clan banes are excluded, i don't see why bloodline banes wouldn't be either.

      Considering that the book states in concrete terms that a Kindred may join their sire's bloodline at BP1 and "Joining a bloodline" is on the list of things that grant Blood Beats (if you're using that system) that includes a bunch of stuff in the same genre as Detachment risks, I would say the intent is definitely that the Bloodline Bane is added regardless of whether your Clan Bane has set in yet, but also that, given how some Bloodline Banes specifically add to the bloodline's parent Clan Bane, a case could be made for joining a bloodline being sufficient buy-in to the Curse that even if you've never dropped below Humanity 7 before it can probably kickstart the permanent addition of the Bane to the character.

      Clan Banes are the only type of Bane that sets in automatically without the player making the decision after the loss, the mechanics allow characters to develop personal Banes from losing higher levels of Humanity, and Clan Banes setting in (and worsening for elders) are part of the same ludonarrative systems that are supposed to allow a character to better pretend they're still human for a while at the start of their Requiem. Bloodlines are secret societies and mutations of the Blood whose more particular gifts tend to require training from a more established vampire or diving deep into powers that all their upbringing rightly says humans aren't supposed to have.

      Even if the book doesn't directly say it like it does for Avuses, the way bloodline powers and deliberate alterations to one's vampiric state work indicates to me that being "Embraced into a bloodline" is typically a matter of your sire preparing you for the process without needing special dispensation to join it rather than it being something you can do automatically on your own. On the other end of the spectrum, for comparison, I wouldn't allow a vampire who somehow reached BP6 without ever having a net loss of Humanity to create a bloodline unless that happening was the entire hook of that bloodline — it's the semi-natural next step in a Kindred vampire's personal evolution after they've detached from Humanity enough to have a harder time relating to the living, not a module of monstrosity bolted onto the side of their character sheet.

          I must disagree here.

          As much as Bloodlines in Requiem are mutations rather than mere inheritance and all that stuff, their not being a Breaking Point for Humanity tells me this engagement is not what is meant for either what a Bloodline represents or what the Banes do.

          On the Bloodline Bane kicking in, I'd have it do so only if the Bane in question is too important for the Bloodline or the Bloodline has too many benefits besides a fourth in-clan. The fourth Discipline itself isn't a big deal: the Bane is still there waiting to kick in anyway, and you did use up your single "Bloodline slot", so that's enough of a price in my book.

          The creator of a Bloodline may be an exception as the Bane is then part and parcel of what you are doing to your curse, but wouldn't also start the regular clan Bane and maybe even then I would let it pass.

          By my understanding associating every focus in the vampire condition as intrinsically inimical to one's Humanity is beyond the intended dynamic. The Breaking Points related to being a vampire are for a reason related just to lack of mortality, as in being unmoored of time and society, and direct predation over the mortals starkly marking you as something that is ecologically distinct, not mystically.

          Rather, the "mere" magic distinction is a fact you are reminded of way more frequently, but also not a negation of your human experience, only an addition to it.

              Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
              By my understanding associating every focus in the vampire condition as intrinsically inimical to one's Humanity is beyond the intended dynamic. The Breaking Points related to being a vampire are for a reason related just to lack of mortality, as in being unmoored of time and society, and direct predation over the mortals starkly marking you as something that is ecologically distinct, not mystically.

              Rather, the "mere" magic distinction is a fact you are reminded of way more frequently, but also not a negation of your human experience, only an addition to it.
              Requiem occupies a spiritual metaphysic where mortals risk damage to their spiritual health from exposure to the supernatural in any form and newly-Embraced vampires risk Detachment from physical trauma and isolation. Vampires have as much reason not to talk about magic with mortals as mages and changelings do even without an explicit printed breaking point attached to the event and part of that is because mortals do not engage with real magic without drastically altering their understanding of the way the world actually works. Hard Leverage works as a mechanic in Social Maneuvering based on the character's morals because Integrity is imperiled by things a mortal character's society tell them are wrong as much as by things that personally push their buttons for moral injury and because the All Night Society, callous though it may be, recognizes that adhering to certain standards of Humanity is beneficial for them and being prepared to break that social contract has implications.

              If joining a covenant to the point of gaining Status in it — a thing with no attendant costs besides those narratively implied by the upkeep of Status Merits in general and no inherent occult impact on the character by itself — and learning a dot of Crúac — a Discipline whose first dot merely prevents you from maximizing your Humanity beyond conventional levels — can be breaking points, aligning yourself more strongly with the power-at-a-price refinement of your monstrous abilities that may also come with extra secret society sauce is absolutely fair game for making your ability to put yourself in mortal shoes that little bit more tenuous. The stat exists primarily to be threatened and lost down to a character's level of comfort and begins applying drawbacks immediately below the level of a starting character even without Clan Banes; it may not necessarily do so depending on the game and character, but joining a bloodline is in the genre of events where it would be weird if you were going for it early enough to not have already compromised your Humanity in the first place.

                  Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                  it may not necessarily do so depending on the game and character, but joining a bloodline is in the genre of events where it would be weird if you were going for it early enough to not have already compromised your Humanity in the first place.
                  If you assume deliberate, conscious decision, that may be the case, though I'm unconvinced this would be certain enough to be argued as a likelihood rather than an option with no particular leaning.

                  But I personally think the notion of Bloodlines being this kind of purposeful Blood alchemy is overemphasized sometimes. Requiem has as a theme the unstable, unpredictable mutability of the Curse, and some of the traditional theme of inheritance and familial bonds.

                  With that, it's perfectly within theme the notion of a Bloodline being either adopted out of sire's pressure or spontaneously, especially if it is the Bloodline of the sire. In fact, the character may have never been out of one. Those are all well within situations where the character didn't get to engage the Curse in a way that compromise Humanity.

                  That said, I do think the transmutation into a Bloodline and the adoption of the social stance are both important things and the later could carry the weight of a Covenant. But I also think those must be kept as distinct things, because there's no mandate otherwise and not every bloodline has the same weight in this secret society, or have none at all. I find it useful to keep the possibility of a character developing such powers without buying in to their social circle, knowingly or not, as a potential source of drama.

                  Anyway, to answer the opening question: the rules are open and that's a legitimate gap, there is no hint to what was intended and both takes follow some premises and violate others. But also neither is particularly problematic, so any option works.

                  For the specific worry that it may feel too much to get a fourth in-clan without the extra Bane, in practice that's minor, the main price is actually the opportunity cost and the dangling risk of the pretty much still there Bane.

                  Going for applying it or even activating the clan Bane along with it isn't much of a problem, either, just be open with the player beforehand.

                      Thanks!

                      I think you both make interesting points, which, unfortunately, it doesn’t help me decide one way or the other, lol.

                      I’m not especially worried about the extra power awarded at creation; it’s indeed a very temporary advantage. Staying above humanity 6 will be pretty hard in a typical vampire game. (And in this specific case, it’s actually for an NPC that’s going to be a player’s childe.)

                      I do agree with Satchel that joining a bloodline should be breaking point, even though it’s not textually mentioned in the book. That’s something I would either classify as an ‘’alienation’’ type breaking point (feeling less human) or a ‘’Requiem’’ type breaking point (engaging in kindred politic).

                      Although, I also agree with monteparnas; Bloodline can represent a twist in the blood, and there is a precedent for a player’s choice, not necessarily being a character’s choice. Non-clan Banes are good examples.

                      As for my current chronicle, I’ll settle with what I find the most interesting.

                      Given that the player has told me that they intent their childe to have their bloodline, I think it would be more dramatically appropriate to have the bane take effect as soon as the embrace happens.

                      The exhibitionist curse of the Von Schrekt bloodline requires the kindred to be observed by mortals while feeding or become capped by half humanity dots for each vessel.

                      This will make for a fun scene where the newly embraced Von Schreckt Nosferatu feels like it’s already pretty hard to feed with the added restrictions, only to realize some time later than after her first humanity dropped that, no, actually, the real curse hadn’t even begun yet;she had it fairly easy before that point.
