    Bringing the Fog of Eternity Back

    05-26-2017, 06:11 PM
    so I've thought about the subject a lot- and by "a lot", I mean since the 2e core has came out- and I've finally came to the conclusion: while I love almost everything about the new edition, I miss the Fog of Eternity. Yeah yeah, I know, "senile elders" and "vampires don't need to lose their minds to avoid having a known origin- they can just lie", but I really liked that aspect of the game. I mean, it made torpor terrifying. You were afraid from getting staked or starved not only because it made you helpless, but because it threatened to devour your mind. Sleep, which is meant to be something relaxing, turned into something dark and terrible. Vampires have invested their efforts to escape that sleep, or try to preserve their memories. The ideal of "perfected vampire" wasn't a blood-god as much as a vampire which actually remembered everything about their life and death. It added another level of personal horror, another level of detachment from humanity. Vampires, which are usually defined by being static and immune to the passage of time, turned into something which feared time- or at least, the madness it carried. They couldn't even trust their own memories- for who knows what truly happened and what was a product of their own nightmares? Searching for why and how the Fog works and how to protect against it was even the goal of number of few bloodlines and plot hooks. In sort, yeah, it was a big deal in the 1E setting, and I really think it was cool.

    so in sort- how much adaptation do you think would be required to adapt the Fog to 2E in a way which works in the new setting material and mechanics?

                                  I am well aware of this, and that most people dislike the Fog as a "thing"- and its ok. Everyone have their own preferences about the game they play. I also know that the vast majority of people support that opinion (after all, I find it hard to believe that 2E would have removed/reduced the Fog is it was the most popular aspect of the game, right?). I, however, think that the Fog of Ages gave the game a certain flavor I really liked. Like I've said in the opening post, it is something I've thought about since 2E has just came out. It is not that I think that without the Fog (as a rule, not the exception) the game is "RUINED FOREVER"- 2E is awesome, and I really enjoy it. I just also miss the Fog- not as just a concept, but as something with teeth, which can bite you down. Be it mundane effect or a supernatural one (after all, getting shot by a gun is also a mundane effect- it still has mechanics), without mechanics the core assumption is that unless the player has decided that their character forgot something during torpor, everything they remember is, more or less, how things happened.

                                  In sort, I like the concept of the Fog as something which actively devours the minds of sleeping Kindred. I know that most people don't agree with me, and so I don't try to convince anyone that the Fog of Ages should be a thing, but for advice about how to add it to the 2E setting and which modification may be required. Vampire is a great game, with or without the Fog- but until Onyx Path would Kickstart their Edition Ninjas Project in order to force everyone to "play the game the right way", I'll probably still like the Fog as a "rule and not exception" and think about adding it to my games and setting.

                                              Here's an idea, more or less inspired from the atrocity dice thing or the paradox from mage. Let's say sleeps for a given number of years. How about for each set number of years (every year, every five year, every decade...), they earn some kind of "torpor point". Then, whenever they make a mental roll to recall something, be it remembering a certain fact or a certain type of knowledge (Science, Academics, Politics) that they learned about before they went into torpor, the GM can decide, if it's appropriate, to make the character use up one (or more) of those torpor dice. Maybe they have to roll it themselves with their dice pool, or the GM rolls it behind the screen.

                                              A success on the die is fine and changes nothing, a failure removes one success, a 1 adds gives a misleading result to the roll (ex : How do you make gunpowder, Roll intelligence + science or academics, torpor die ends up 1, "Well, gunpowder explodes, so you should add something spicy like pepper") and 10 gives an additional knowledge the character didn't learn back then, but was granted to him via some torpor induced insight (it could also effectively change their memory so they remember learning about it, but the information is real or at least helpful).

                                                          If you need a mechanic, memory loss sounds like a derangement to me.

                                                          Maybe one of the functions of torpor in your game could be to clean out the old detritus of derangements that have been accumulating. When vampires experience a full torpor, of a length appropriate to their Humanity, they may trade out another, more troublesome derangement for the loss of memory of a period equal to the length of their torpor. Just describe the derangement as being functionally identical to the Amnesia Condition.

                                                          There could be two forms of the derangement: Repressed Memories (mild), which clouds the memories, but allows the sufferer to spend an XP to ask the ST a single Yes/No question about the period lost, and thereby slowly rebuild the memories; and Infantile Amnesia (severe), in which the memories are gone. Period.

                                                          Whether it is Mild or Severe could depend on whether the derangement it replaces is Mild or Severe.

                                                          Ex 1: A Humanity 4 vampire entered torpor for one year, they could trade out, say, Schizophrenia (severe) for Infantile Amnesia (severe), removing the memories of the one year period in which schizophrenia was originally acquired. The memories are simply gone, and this derangement cannot be removed... though certain disciplines might bend this rule. Go ask the Agonistes, maybe?

                                                          Ex 2: A Humanity 3 vampire entered torpor for five years. They suffer from Suspicion (mild), and choose to trade that out for Repressed Memories (mild), which cloaks the memory of the five year period in which he gained Suspicion. They later need to know if they were in France during that period. They spend an XP to piece together the scattershot images they still have and ask, "Was I in France during that period?" The ST will answer accurately.

                                                          This would make the Fog positively correlated with low Humanity and an unstable mental state. It would also tend to remove the most notable periods of the amnesiac's life, when derangements are mostly likely to be acquired. Plus, torpor is supposed to be filled with dreams and nightmares that recombine elements from memory to re-interpret events. That sounds believably therapeutic enough to allow such a trade.
