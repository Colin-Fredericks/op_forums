    CofD has a very cinematic way of handling damage. It's not very detailed. It's not intended to be. Getting bogged down in realism isn't really the design philosophy of CofD, and you're probably better off playing other games if that's what you want.

    Radiation is an environmental hazard in CofD, so I'd just use those rules as-is. I thought that, somewhere or other, there were tweaks for Vampires using environmental hazards, though I can't find them. So I'd just use Environmental Hazard rules with the guidelines in the book (heavy radiation is level 4, for example).

                Hurt Locker, p149.

                Radiation poisoning, desert exposure, and toxic fumes are of little concern, save for vampires and the desert sun. These beings suffer the same effects of heavy snowfall, rain, and high winds as mortals do, but their powers give them an edge. The undead suffer no ill effects at all from extreme environments up to level three, and suffer level four environments as mortals do lower-level conditions (i.e., bashing damage equal to the environment’s level once per hour, after a number of hours equal to the character’s Stamina). Even then, their undead bodies resist such conditions to an incredible degree, subtracting their Supernatural Tolerance trait from the damage sustained.

                                                Clash of Wills is how you resolve an action. It's not an action in itself. Only when you've used Auspex in a way that opposes another character's Obfuscate does Clash of Wills apply. The ST will then tell you that you need to roll Auspex + Blood Potency (or just ask for your Auspex + Blood Potency if they're rolling for everyone like my beast of an ST).
                                                You can never just say that you want a Clash of Wills. You need to actually do something that triggers a Clash of Wills.
