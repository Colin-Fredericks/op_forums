    Removing Touchstones (or at least downplaying them tremendously)

    03-15-2016, 01:38 AM
    I've been running a game for a couple of months now, and I have been noticing that, well, touchstones just aren't a thing. Everyone picked a touchstone at creation, of course, and we talked a lot about who would be a given character's touchstone and why, but, there has been zero interaction whatsoever between any of the PCs and their touchstones, except for the PC who chose his dog and made him a retainer (I never liked that one from the start, honestly, felt like cheating to make it both useful and effective in game, and a non-human to boot) and the circumstances of what they've been doing would make it truly odd and obviously forced for their touchstones to come to them.

    It could be the circumstances of the game we are playing in particular. The first session covered the PC's very first night as vampires, for example, and only about a week of in-game time has passed over, I would estimate, 10-12 sessions, rather than having characters who have already established themselves as vampires for several years before the start of a game, and making liberal use of time elapses as it seems is the norm.

    Frankly, having seen it in play, now, I'm wondering how, exactly, a freshly awoken vampire's touchstones first develop, how long they have to form a bond before they start feeling the penalties, etc., but rather than make that stuff up, my group and I discussed it, and we'd just like to downplay touchstones significantly. I said that if the PCs actually make a real connection to an NPC such that it's obviously a touchstone, I will still allow some of the touchstone mechanics to apply. They can get willpower from defending them and their touchstones can ignore their Nosferatu penalties or talk them down from frenzy, but they don't get a +2/+3/-2 to detachment rolls regardless, and I am not going to charge merit dots or tie them to Humanity lines or anything, either. It'll basically be a lot more hand wavy, and less hard coded, "You better have some touchstones or else!"

    Which brings me to my main problem: the Ventrue clan bane. I have to admit, I was already unhappy with it. "You lose 1 XP more than a member of another clan at the same humanity, until you're borderline unplayable at 1 humanity anyway" just doesn't have the same, well, anything, that the other clans get. I actually liked the old Ventrue flaw and thought it to be the best one of the bunch, but here, in the new edition, they fix everything up, make all the flaws better (Mekhet went from the lamest, basically non-flaw, to the most interesting!), and kind of drop the ball on the previous best.

    My stop gap solution is simple: -1 to detachment rolls, and on a detachment roll dramatic failure, gain a 1st edition style derangement (in addition to the jaded condition and other effects).

    However, I was wondering a few things. First, does anyone have some other ideas for a good Ventrue flaw? I read quite a few threads complaining about it, but the majority of the fixes I saw also tied into the Touchstone system, which, obviously, is something I do not want. Is what I outlined above a decent flaw? I did -1 because I figured -2 would be too severe (the die pools basically range from 1-4 with maybe a few bonus or penalties based on circumstances), but there have been very few detachment rolls so far (is it me or is the 2nd edition chart ridiculously more forgiving?). The derangement is mostly for flavor and fun rather than balance--I figured it wouldn't ever come up unless someone purposefully wanted one or they were already fairly low humanity committing fairly terrible acts, probably with one or more Banes in play.

    Has anyone else ever done a Blood and Smoke/2nd edition game downplaying/removing touchstones like this? How did it work out? What did you have to adjust that, maybe, I'm not thinking of, etc?

      So, my initial reaction here was "So why are their Touchstones still attached?", but after reading further, it seems like the problem you're having is stemming more from the fact that, as you mention, your characters are all recently embraced neonates. I'll address those circumstances more specifically later in this post, but first I just wanted to comment on this point in a more general sense, outside the context of your game's unique concerns. Cause the thing is, even if your characters had all been around longer and were more entrenched in vampire society, Touchstones aren't going to fit naturally in the flow of what the characters will generally be getting up to. This is a feature, not a bug. The point of Touchstones is to give vampires an anchor to humanity (that's small-h "the human race", not big-H stat on the character sheet). Keeping connected to a normal mortal, with a mortal life, and mortal problems is hard for a vampire. They really have to go out of their way to maintain a relationship with someone who, quite frankly, doesn't fit into their unlife. It would be far more convenient to just ignore them and go about your business doing vampire things without having to check in on your human friend every once in a while. But if you do that, you lose the one person in your life who still reminds you of what it means to be human, and as a result, it's much harder to keep a grip on Humanity (that's the capital H kind there). A vampire should always be torn between the needs of their Coterie and the needs of their Touchstones. And if they chose to prioritize the Coterie, those Touchstones should become detached. That's all part of the drama of Vampire the Requiem.

      Side-note: Yeah, I wouldn't have allowed a dog as a Touchstone. Making them a Retainer could be interesting, but a dog is definitely not going to anchor you to the human experience.

      I agree, the particular circumstances of your game are definitely going to make Touchstones seem less important. If your characters were all recently embraced, they probably don't yet need to maintain a relationship with a human to remind them what it was like to be alive - after all it was only a week or so ago. The default rules kind of assume that the characters have been vampires long enough to at least gain 3 dots of Disciplines, 3 dots of Merits, and to drop to Humanity 7. If you're playing characters who were embraced that recently, I would also start them at Humanity 10, because they shouldn't yet be used to things like lying in defense of the Masquerade, going over a day without human contact, possessing superhuman abilities, and using those abilities to steal the lifeblood of the unwilling, or to deprive them of the agency to attest their unwillingness. That's valuable stuff to play through if you're starting that early. Don't bother with Touchstones yet, have the players acquire them in-game, at a time when it feels dramatically appropriate. When they start forgetting things like the feeling of a sunburn, the rhythm of a heartbeat, and why exactly they're supposed to care about consent. When they start to experience that cognitive dissonance of "wait... I should feel guilty about this. Why don't I feel guilty about this?" that's when it's time to find a human you can confide in and who can serve as an example of what humans are like and why that's important.

                  Let's say your vampire has Bob, his old roomate from college, as his touchstone. So, once a week, say, you and Bob catch up for an hour or something. Maybe your tradition was bowling, so, the table options are:

                  You: "I go hang out with Bob. We go bowling."
                  Everyone at the table is bored to tears as you and the GM have a conversation, mostly about nonsense, that has nothing to do with the rest of the game whatsoever.
                  Touchstone interrupts and suckifies the game for a while.

                  or

                  You: "I go hang out with Bob. We go bowling."
                  GM: "Ok, you do that. It's fun. He and Christine are in a rough patch, but the [sports team he likes] are on a winning streak. What does everyone else do for a couple of hours? Feed? Hang out with their touchstones?"
                  Everyone kind of rolls their eyes at this pointless "obligation minute," because they know it's silly, but they don't want a 4 die swing on their detachment rolls.
                  Touchstone is successfully glossed over, which diminishes its value as a roleplaying tool.

                  or

                  GM: "Your touchstone is in trouble!"
                  Everyone sighs. It's super forced and obvious--this does not fit into the game whatsoever, but, it's necessary to give this mechanic that, othewise just suckifies the game or gets glossed over teeth. And everyone knows their turn will come as these random people have bad shit happen to them, not because they know a vampire, but because they know a PC and interesting stuff has to happen to them in order to keep the mechanic interesting and worthwhile.

                  I don't know, I'm just not a fan of that. Touchstones work in theory--they work when talking about the game. It works great in other media--how many times do the heroes have a scene with an otherwise uninvolved person (their family, or a mentor or something), and achieve some kind of insight into the current plot from them? But, that only works for two reasons: everything, from the problem, to the lucky lesson learned from an old friend, is scripted (and, in my opinion, nothing should be in a roleplaying game), and because the other protagonists aren't sitting around a table watching this one character get his insight, waiting for their turn to do something.

                  So, the short version is, I don't think I would like Touchstones even if my game didn't have special circumstances. Though, my mind is definitely changeable on that front. How have they worked at the actual table for others? Like, not just a flower prose version like you get in "actual play" posts, the real version, where solo scenes are full of side comments and bored groans from other players.

So, if the player orders food at the diner, you can either wait until he is vomiting to roll detachment or until he reverses his choice. The latter carries bonus modifiers for being less physically intense. The other side of this though is where, upon hearing they will be meeting at the restaurant, the players resist. Meeting in an ally to avoid humans is more detaching than seeing others. It doesn't leave much in the way of winning rolls because at such a high level of humanity and for new vampires, they will detach at some point. (Which is why I reckon the 9-10 is wrote as it is. To be a fully human vampire, you have to acknowledge your distance and try anyway. Call it the last visage of the ethical-humanity and its original sin overtones.) However, if they go and don't make any choices that would be poignant, then don't detach. The essence of the seeing others eat is the sense of alienation. If they don't notice, they can't feel it. To me, making that happen is no more railroading than asking for vitae expense when you use blood buffs. Like blood buffs, you can only put them in situations where activation is their fault. :P

                                                          Honestly, I've had the most success with Touchstones that also serve as retainers. Or part of their haven. Or something else that integrates the Touchstone as part of the one (un)life. I find that trying to make Touchstones as something completely separate creates a kind of artificial divide between PC-as-human, and PC-as-vampire. If anything, this makes them push away their human side, instead of integrating their humanity within their vampire life. I made a Daeva PC that had a ghoul (also part of the herd) as a Touchstone. You say that having a dog Retainer as a Touchstone felt off, almost like a cheat. Personally, I say that's working as intended. Well, one way that they're intended to work - Touchstones should be something fluid and fit almost anything.

                                                          Perhaps the core of the problem here isn't that the characters are too young, but rather that the players picked innapropriate ones? PCs don't always act out the way we think we do at char gen. They change and evolve rather drastically, even if only over the course of a single session? One reason I think its important to integrate Touchstones into one's vampire life is that one of the major functions of a Touchstone is to talk their vampire down from a frenzy. The Touchstone can't do that if they're safely in their white-picket house across town. In fact, I think that if your Touchstone is something that's not part of your life, something that you want to cut out for safety reasons? That's the opposite of a Touchstone. That's reinforcing that you are not human, and pushing your Humanity further down.

                                                          How do Touchstones develop? Again, this is something flexible. The bare minimum for being a Touchstone is just that it reminds a vampire that they're still human (at least in part). It could be something as simple as a song that reminds them of simpler times, happier times that they hear for the first time while hunting. I find it rather evocative that a vampire would hear some music, maybe a musical jewelry box, that reminded her of her mother, a century dead, do anything to get that little trinket, and obsessively listen to the song. And maybe, just maybe, one of her ghouls would record the song on their phone, and the next time, in Elysium, on the verge of frenzy and attacking someone, that song starts playing, and cuts through the red rage like a sharp knife, bringing the vampire to her senses. All this, from just wandering around, and snap, instant connection.

                                                          What about for neonates? Fresh fledgelings? Maybe it should be the car they're still driving around from their living days. Maybe its going to dinner every Sunday with their family, and eff any vampire that says they shouldn't. Its any number of things that mean they're maintaining their still-human lifestyle. Its something, anything, that they had a connection with as a mortal. The Touchstone should become a mechanical thing the moment they first rise hungry after Embraced.

                                                          I honestly don't think that being freshly Embraced is the issue here. I think that its just a case of picking bad Touchstones, maybe misunderstanding what Touchstones represent. Your PCs still engage in things from their mortal life? So, those things that they're still using and part of should be the Touchstones.

                                                          Now, the Ventrue clan bane. Its always been a bit fudgy, but to be honest? I think the Mehket and Daeva ones are insanely easy to ignore as well. Its insanely easy to pick a Bane that is rare enough that it doesn't impact your character at all, and a Daeva's weakness only comes into play if one's herd somehow becomes relevant to the plot. I've had games where neither one has had an impact in the story. You claim the Mehket is the most interesting, but it still comes down to the same thing as the 1e flaw - "same weaknesses as everyone else, but worse!" The new Ventrue flaw is actually meant to reflect the Julii flaw - they tend to become detached faster than the average vampire. The whole Touchstone thing is supposed to reflect them having a harder time holding onto their Humanity. They kind of dropped the old Ventrue flaw (madness) because too many people found that the whole immorality = insanity thing to be insensitive, and some of the derangements do honestly overlap with the general Banes that a Mehket might pick up.

                                                          There's a couple options here for a new Ventrue bane. One is to just copy the Julii clan flaw. -1 to all detachment rolls. Represents the same thing as the current flaw, imho. Another is to just take a derangement if you like the idea of them being a bit unbalanced, though again, we get into Unfortunate-Implications territory. I would absolutely not do the derangement on the drop - not only because of unfortunate implications, but it makes less sense with the current Humanity than it does with the old.


                                                          Have I ever downplayed Touchstones in a game before? Well, that one is a bit debatable. I consider Touchstones to be something that's Opt-In from a player's standpoint. As in, I leave it up to the players to decide what they're goign to do with them. I've had palyers that simply ignore their Touchstone and just go on with the game, and eventually had nothing to do with one. I've had others that made Touchstones a huge part of the character.
