            Werewolves, mages, changelings, and Prometheans all have canon answers to some degree (which have been given). My headcanons for the others are:

            Sineaters: Similar to werewolves; the vampire gets twice the normal vitae (additionally, each health level the Sineater loses costs her a point of plasm, as some of it is in the blood), but the vampire gets a -2 to resist Rotschreck for a number of nights equal to their vitae.

            Mummies: If the mummy doesn't have a Sahu going, nothing happens at all. No blood, no vitae, no Kiss, no ill effects. A vampire needs the wherewithal to feed on ghosts in order to feed on a mummy with Sahu, but if they do, it's Christmas: the vitae they drink is multiplied by half the mummy's current Sekhem (round up), and is not limited by their normal maximum pool - on the downside, if they do drink more vitae than they can normally hold, they are drawn to the mummy's first and final purpose until it drops to within their pool size.

            Lucifuge: Normal vitae, but it tastes fiery; the Beast twitches when they taste it, but not enough to require Rotschreck.

            Ascending Ones: Normal vitae, but tastes a bit on the icky side.

            Beasts: If the Beast is Sated or Starving, the vampire gets twice the Vitae (either the fullness of the keenness is good). If the Beast is Starving or Ravenous, her Hunger infects the Beast of the vampire - for 1 night per vitae, the vampire's Beast craves satisfaction through the Begotten's Hunger instead of blood - the vampire still needs blood (and doesn't need to satisfy the Hunger to survive), only what might trigger Wassail is affected. Needless to say, this can be a weird experience, but some vampires like it.

                                I tend to like expanding the effects of what the blood of other supernaturals does to give a more concrete benefit in addition to the drawback, making it a very mixed blessing. Taking into account 2e rules, I'd do something like:

                                Werewolves: 2 Vitae per lethal, -1 to frenzy rolls for a night per -vitae- gained, but increase your effective BP for the sake of bonuses in frenzy (only) by 1 during that time.

                                Mages: You have access to the active mage sight of the mage you've taken vitae from for an hour per lethal caused. But that includes the penalties it causes, and you can't turn it off through any means, and it still taxes your willpower for each of those hours despite not being able to shut it down (if you run out, it doesn't stop early, either). Might also impose a condition related to the mage sight if you weren't expecting it.

                                Changelings: Will depend on 2e mechanics, but the perception penalty still applies for the night (-1 per vitae taken). On the bright side, you can see through the mask for the duration, too... if you can figure out what's a changeling and what's a hallucination superimposed on a normal person.

                                Sin-Eaters: The blood of Sin-Eaters brings the Vampire that much closer to their undead side. Utilizing vitae to mimic life costs 1 extra vitae for 1 night per lethal caused. On the bright side, you gain access to the SIn-Eater's death sight abilities, and if you have a way to feed on ghosts, through a merit, gift, or discipline, you gain 2 vitae per lethal done to the Sin-Eater.

                                Mummies: I don't like Mummies. Do whatever.

                                Demons: You get no Vitae from a Demon if they don't have a Cover active, though it causes damage and otherwise as normal. If they do have a cover up, you feed from them normally. Nothing too special here; any specific effects that could become recognizable make little sense given the ability of cover to spoof and similar.

                                Beasts: The Vampire's feeding habits become tainted by the Hunger of the Beast. For one night per lethal caused to the Beast, the Kindred needs to spend a willpower to gain vitae from a source that doesn't at least peripherally align with that beast's hunger. On the bright side, when it DOES align, you gain double Vitae, and that includes every vitae after the first taken from the Beast themself.
