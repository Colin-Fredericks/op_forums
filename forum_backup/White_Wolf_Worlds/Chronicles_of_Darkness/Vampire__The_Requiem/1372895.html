    errata for Secrets of the covenants?

    03-05-2020, 12:40 AM
    I know Onyx Path doesn't do official errata or faqs, so I was wondering if anyone had some unofficial errata for Secrets of the Covenants. The more I look through the appendices the more I wonder if this book was ever playtested. I mean the Zirnitra Coil should just not have been allowed to be a thing, and who actually thought the instant death black hole part of the Opening the Void Cruac Style was a good thing. It's gotten to the point where I'm considering banning this book outright from all of my chronicles. I mean CoD isn't the greatest when it comes to balance (professional training, for example), but this book just goes above and beyond. So, has anyone actually managed to fix this book, or would it just be better to ban certain sections, or even the whole thing?

      Professional Training was included because of how well received it was in Hunter 1e. It does wonders to make characters stand out in their chosen field. The only thing we've done to balance it is to remove 9-Again and Rote Action from attack rolls because otherwise not choosing your main combat Skill as one of the Asset Skills would seriously unbalance the party in combat heavy games. Other than that my only problem with PT is that its effects aren't baked into character creation. Kinda sucks that you'll always want to put 3-5 of your starting Merit dots into PT.

      Coil of Zirnitra on its own isn't that powerful. You get Supernatural Merits that generally aren't better than Disciplines but they cost huge amounts of XP and it says directly that all Supernatural Merits needs to be discussed and judged on an individual basis. Just don't allow Pyrokinesis and the Coil is absolutely fine. Grafting Unholy Flesh, on the other hand, is a powergamer's wet dream and should definitely be banned if you're worried about balance.

      Other than that single Scale, Coil of Ziva is the worst offender. Unlike Zirnitra (and its Scales, I might add) this was immediately banned at our table. It's also a horrible mess of mechanics so if you for whatever reason don't find it too unbalanced I'd recommend checking out the rewrite in my forum signature.

      Just remove the oblivion gate from Opening the Void and it should work fine.


      I can't think of anything else that is unbalanced in that direction. The Crusade Style in the LeS section is close to worthless against blood sorcery (which it is supposed to counter) because you'd never want to cast blood sorcery while in combat anyway. The second dot works well against uses of Dominate and possibly Majesty in combat, but that's basically it outside of crossover games.

          Agreed with Tessie

          Shadowdragon I know other people have errata'd or attempted to fix the Coil of Ziva, so by any means you should check their work out too, but here is my attempt at fixing the Coil of Ziva without changing too much. I'll see if I can fix Coil of Zirnitra as well (though I feel that Coil doesn't need as much fixing, it just needs a firm ST who doesn't allow power gaming to say "no" when a player with that Coil tries to pull off a game-breaking combo like Pyrokinesis).

          My take on Coil of Ziva:

          Coil of Ziva

          Denying the Bane •
          At this initial level, the character may shrug off some of the more heinous effects of her degeneration. Add her Coil dots to her Humanity for the purpose of determining the effects of her Banes. This includes her clan Bane, acquired Banes and bloodline Banes. This does not, however, affect the vampire’s weaknesses to fire or sunlight. This also doesn’t affect the Ventrue clan Bane. The Mekhet's Tenebrous Curse caps their effective maximum Humanity at 9 even if this Coil would push it above nine.

          Enliven the Anima ••
          With this Coil, your character becomes resistant to the psychological strains of encountering Breaking Points. She finds overcoming her soul’s trials invigorating. Any time she succeeds a Detachment roll and doesn't already have the Raptured Condition, you may choose to take the Raptured Condition instead of Bestial, Competitive or Wanton.

          Buttress the Soul •••
          When facing a Breaking Point, the Dragon may now choose to spend Willpower on the resulting Detachment roll. This works similarly to spending Willpower on resisting frenzy. Each point of Willpower spent adds a +1 die bonus to the Detachment roll.

          Embracing the Banes ••••
          Your character can sustain a number of acquired Banes equal to her dots in the Coil of Ziva (instead of three) and her acquired banes no longer inflict a die penalty on her Detachment rolls.
          The player may choose to acquire a Bane for a particular breaking point even when succeeding the Detachment roll.

          Siphon the Soul •••••
          This ability acts as a sort of pseudo-Amaranth which can be used on humans and vampires with an Integrity/Humanity rating higher than the character's own Humanity rating. The vampire drains a human or Kindred victim completely and then continues to do so further. She spends Willpower and rolls Strength + Resolve as she normally would when committing diablerie (see Vampire: The Requiem, Second Edition p. 101).
          When the victim dies, the vampire gains a dot of Humanity, but also rolls for Detachment for premeditated murder (Humanity 2), possibly losing the stolen Humanity dot, and suffers the Tainted Condition. While under the effects of the Tainted Condition, the user of this Coil might register as a diablerist to Auspex users.
          The vampire can end there, or choose to complete the Amaranth if the victim is Kindred, with all the consequences of doing so (this counts as a separate breaking point at Humanity 1).


          Scales of Ziva

          Shedding the Beast’s Skin
          Prerequisite Coil: Siphon the Soul
          Procedure: The vampire distills a bit of her own Humanity into a decoction that can turn any vampire into a human again, for a brief time at least. Preparing the decoction takes a full hour and costs the vampire one Vitae and one Humanity dot. This Humanity loss is unavoidable and cannot be shielded with a Bane. The end result is a decoction in a vial or syringe, that can be consumed or injected by a vampire to briefly turn human again.
          Outcome: The vampire who receives the decoction undergoes rapid changes. His blood starts to boil, his skin starts to burn, flake and fall off. The Beast goes berserk and the vampire has to roll to resist frenzy with a -5 die penalty. This whole transformation process takes 5 turns, during which the Beast cries out in agony and the vampire turns human again. After these 5 turns, the vampire passes out, but will soon wake up, breathing, with a beating heart and completely human in every single way. No doctor in the world could see him as anything but human. This also means all Disciplines, Vitae, Banes and everything that made the character vampire disappears for the time being. The character's Humanity rating becomes his Integrity rating for the time being.
          While human, the character can engage in sexual intercourse and possibly impregnate or get impregnated, but such a pregnancy is unlikely to produce a normal, healthy human. A dhampir on the other hand...
          The character stays human until the next sundown. During sundown, for nearly a half an hour, she suffers agonizing pain as she slowly dies. This leaves her functionally incapacitated during this time. This might inflict a breaking point after the fact. When the character is completely dead, she will immediately wake up as her former Kindred self, being one of the unliving once again. She can hold off the transformation for one minute with a point of Willpower. Once she’s changed back, roll one die to determine her new starting Vitae.

                          Dice tricks stacks. You can have Rote Action, 9/8-Again and even Advanced Action on the same roll.
                          Multiple sources of 9-Again does not stack to 8-Again unless one of those sources explicitly says so (like this: "if you already have 9-Again from another source, you instead get 8-Again").

                          I've seen arguments based ln the phrasing of Rote Action that you wouldn't even roll the regular 10-Again, but that's overly literal bullshit.
