                            You do know that Christianity's history of martyrdom is predominantly a falsehood, right? The vast majority of stories about martyrs and persecution are either outright fiction, drastically manipulated away from the truth to support the myth, or so dubious that we can't figure out which they are. The comparatively tiny percentage of martyrs that actually existed have been drastically expanded in numbers and prominence.

                            For the 300 years between the purported death of Jesus Christ and Emperor Constantine making Christianity the dominant religion of the Roman Empire, they were persecuted for perhaps 10-12 years, and even then, those years weren't concurrent and enforcement of the official persecution was very haphazard; some regions, usually those that had seen considerable Jewish uprisings before, were strict as the stories claim, but most did everything they could to avoid having to execute Christians.

                            In fact, the early Christians were so eager to martyr themselves and thusly win a guaranteed place in Heaven, that they would openly confront Roman officials and beg to be put to death. A plea that the officials would do everything in their power to not grant, up to and including simply telling the Christians to bugger off and refusing to pay any attention to them.

                            Before you take the obvious route and point to the very site linked in my quote, keep in mind that this is the same organization I am telling you repeatedly committed fraud to promote their own agenda. "Lying for Jesus", they called it. For example, the infamous passage in Tactitus has been proven the result of forgerous additions to the original texts by an 11th century monk Actual historical evidence points very contrary.

                            For example, in 112 AD, Pliny the Younger sent a letter to Emperor Trajan asking how he should deal with Christians. He was told, in response, not to seek them out, not to believe anonymous accusations, and to give them every opportunity to agree to be good Romans, at which point they should be released unharmed.

                            Even when Christians were legally punished, the cause was practical and secular, not religious. Christians were seen by the Romans as alarmingly superstitious, rumored to engage in cannibalism and incestuous, and more practically being at best disrespectful and at worst outright seditious; the Romans took a dim view to doomsayers actively calling for the downfall of their Empire, and even Pliny's famous letter mostly dwells on his fears that the Christian cults are hotbeds of anti-Roman sedition. That's generally when the Christians came to be actively pushed; when their refusal to respect the emperor and thus show solidarity with the empire coincided with strong external strife.

                            The actual truth of Christianity is that, for the vast majority of three hundred years, they were either completely ignored or granted special privileges. Emperor Trajan very much set the mold for his fellow emperors in that he did everything he could to grant them special favors and reasons to ignore them - even Emperor Hadrian, who absolutely hated all Judaic sects after the three years war he spent fighting against fanatical Jewish uprisings, was content to live and let live towards Jews, Christians and all the other Abrahamic sects of the day.

                            Anyway, as for whether or not knowledge of this colors players against the Sanctum and towards the Circle, I don't know. You can point to just about any Catholic or Protestant preacher on the net and you can be sure they'll disavow these facts, asserting it's simply anti-Christian prejudice and persecution, so I hardly expect them to be known amongst most Requiem players.
