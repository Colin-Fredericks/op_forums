    Blood Sorcery Hack

    02-08-2022, 02:44 PM
    It sure has been a while, huh? I was thinking about the discussions regarding bloody sorcery that were had a while ago in the hypothetical 3e thread, and was struck with sudden inspiration. So, here is a completely unplaytested hack for the blood sorcery system in the Requiem 2e core. It isn't anything revolutionary, more of a patch than an alternate system [for that I would probably remove the Disciplines entirely, and focus on the rituals alone, similar to Carthian Law or Invictus Oaths]. Hopefully, it makes blood sorcery a little more attractive without losing the ritualistic feel. In particular, I think moving the ludonarrative "ritual" away from rolling repeatedly and toward deciding how much time you are willing to spend and the difficulty of affecting your target, and then using that to build the pool makes for more active feeling play. Knowing exactly how long it is going to take should hopefully make it easier to weigh the benefits of casting a ritual versus accomplishing something similar through other means. I also buffed a couple rituals that I felt where too weak to bother with, because I cannot be stopped. Let me know what you think, or if you have any ideas of your own!

    Using this hack, learning and using blood sorcery works much the same as it does in the Vampire the Requiem 2e core rulebook, with the following changes:

    Casting Rituals
    All rituals are instant actions instead of extended actions. They still take a while to cast, but the process is condensed into a single roll. The precise casting time varies from ritual to ritual, using categories based on the target number of successes they normally require. This scale ranges from 5 minutes to a full night, though only special circumstances can normally reduce it below 30 minutes. The default categories are listed below.
    • Rituals that require 11+ successes take a full night to cast.
    • Rituals that require 10 successes take 3 hours to cast.
    • Rituals that require 7 or 8 successes take 2 hours to cast.
    • Rituals that require 6 successes take 1 hour to cast.
    • Rituals that require 5 successes take 30 minutes to cast.

    Time is a precious resource to the Kindred, limited by the night as they are. To make sure all their business gets done, ritualists may accept penalties on their casting rolls to reduce casting time. Every -1 penalty accepted reduces the casting time by one category. Similarly, a ritualist with time to spare may extend their ritual, gaining a +1 bonus for every category they increase it by. It is not possible to increase a ritual’s casting time above a full night.

    Possessing more dots in the Ritual Discipline than a ritual’s rating decreases its casting time by one category. If a ritual normally takes 30 minutes to cast, then this makes it only take 15 minutes, instead. Likewise, an exceptional success on the casting roll can reduce the casting time to 15 minutes. If the casting time is already 15 minutes, then an exceptional success reduces it to 5 minutes. Finally, it is also possible to reduce casting time to these levels by accepting penalties, but characters can never reduce a ritual’s casting time below 5 minutes, no matter the method.

    A ritual must still be completed in one attempt, and fails automatically if interrupted.

    Defending Against Blood Sorcery
    All rituals are contested or resisted, even when targeting willing participants or the ritualist herself. The conversions below list whether a ritual is contested or resisted and by what, but typically, if a ritual is not already contested, then it should be resisted by whatever Attribute makes the most sense to you. If a ritual does not target a character, such as Gift of Lazarus, then the ritualist instead subtracts the ritual’s dots from her pool, or if it targets an object, its Durability. This represents the world’s resistance to being altered by the Blood.

    As rituals are instant actions, their results are decided with a single roll, which can be confusing if a character tries to stop a ritualist in the middle of casting. However, keep in mind that a ritual’s effects do not take place until it has been fully cast, and it can be interrupted. Regardless of the ritual’s roll result, characters will still have a window of time in which they can disrupt the ritual, if they can find the vampire targeting them…

    Potency
    If a ritual has effects that scale off of Potency, then treat its Potency as equal to total successes rolled.

    Duration
    Unless otherwise noted in its description, a ritual’s effects only last until the sun next rises. However some rituals have had their durations altered to increase their utility, such as Rigor Mortis or Touch of the Morrigan. Full details are in the conversions, below.

    Roll Results
    Since ritual rolls are no longer extended, use the following roll results:
    Success: The ritual is successful, and its effects immediately take place.
    Exceptional Success: The ritualist exceeds expectations. The player chooses one of the following benefits, in addition to successfully enacting the ritual.
    • Increase the ritual’s Potency by her dots in the Ritual Discipline.
    • Halve the ritual’s casting time, completing it faster than normal.
    • Take the Ecstatic [Crúac] or Raptured [Theban Sorcery] Condition.
    Failure: The ritual fails, and the sacrifice is wasted.
    Dramatic Failure: The ritual catastrophically fails. Not only does the ritualist waste her sacrifice, but she gains either the Tempted [Crúac] or Humbled [Theban Sorcery] Condition, and she suffers a -2 penalty on the next ritual she attempts.

    ALTERNATE RULES: Even Simpler Casting Times!
    For groups that want even simpler casting times, simply have a ritual take 15 minutes to cast per dot. So a two dot ritual takes 30 minutes to cast, a three dot ritual takes 45 minutes, etc. If a character gets an exceptional success or has more dots in their Ritual Discipline than the ritual’s rating, then reduce this to 5 minutes per dot. If a character gets an exceptional success and has more dots in the Discipline, then reduce the casting time to 1 minute per dot.

    This approach will not always create the most faithful conversions of existing rituals [after all, by the time a character is able to cast Stigmata, it probably isn’t going to take them an hour, RAW], but will make calculating casting times quick and painless.

    Corebook Crúac Conversions
    Make the following adjustments to Crúac Rites in the corebook.
    • Rigor Mortis - Penalty applies to a number of actions equal to Potency or until the night ends, whichever comes first
    • The Hydra’s Vitae - Resisted by Stamina
    • Deflection of Wooden Doom - Resisted by Composure
    • Touch of the Morrigan - Resisted by Stamina; can be used up to Potency times instead of once, and deals lethal damage equal to Crúac dots, instead of Potency
    • Willful Vitae - Resisted by Resolve
    • Feeding the Crone - Resisted by Stamina


    Corebook Theban Sorcery Conversions
    Make the following adjustments to Theban Sorcery Miracles in the corebook
    • Blood Scourge - Resisted by Composure
    • Vitae Reliquary - Resisted by the sacrament’s Durability
    • Malediction of Despair - The victim suffers the penalty a number of times equal to Potency, instead of once
    • Gift of Lazarus - Resisted by the ritual’s rating
    • Transubstantiation - Objects resist with Durability
