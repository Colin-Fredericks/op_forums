    City Of The Damned: São Paulo

    10-24-2016, 07:16 PM
    Hello guys! First time posting here, hoping to be active!

    I'll try my best to keep this short and concise:

    Me and my friends never played pen-and-paper RPG. Since we're all almost on our 30s and have our own lives and stuff we decided to meet every 15 days on a Friday night to bond together and play some game that we never did. After some months we finally decided to play RPG for the first time and we all liked the Chronicles of Darkness setting and Vampire the Requiem. I was elected to be the GM on a drawing straw. So, we got the Second Edition book and delved into the mechanics. Damnation City.

    The catch: they wanted to play as themselves, today, in our city. Like if they were bitten and turned on that night. So, I got the Damnation City book, read some posts and forums and got some ideas and went worldbuilding. I went crazy and enjoyed the process of telling the tale of a city behind the curtains of the Chronicles of Darkness. So here it goes:





    City of the Damned: São Paulo

    Starting Point
    I based the initial setup of the story on the Mary's Child, the official demo, since as a first timer I didn't had any idea into how to throw a group recently changed into the Kindred society. The players, play as themselves in the reality they live – they were free to choose their clan.

    Now I'm motivated to develop this scenario into a full-fledged city someday.


    Scenario
    The city is São Paulo. The biggests cities Brazil – with a population of 11.32 million – it's one of greatests metropolis of the world. Started as a small religious village em 1554, it transformed into a trading post, a coffee and sugarcane farmland, an industrial center and eventually the financial heart of the country. Adaptability and transformation is the name of the game. From a simple church to be the city behind wheels a country – every resident feels that they can make it there.
    But every story has a bleak side. Corruption, rampant violence, xenophobia, religious conservatism, right-wing violence, left-wing propaganda and health and educations systems almost of breaking down. This is the São Paulo that we live in.

    Since 1802 when the Portuguese Crown escaped Napoleon to live here in Brazil, the city found itself amidst a power struggle between many factions:
    • The Lancea et Sanctum and Invictus representing the aristocracy and the church of Portugal and England.
    • The Carthian Movement, upholding the ideals of French Revolution and the independence of the country from Europe.
    • The Circle Of The Crone, that gained strength with the mulatos, natives and slave population that found their customs repressed by the Europeans.

    From 1900 to 2000, the country and the city went through several coups d'état, a raging civil war, presidents assassinations, a military dictatorship and political scandals. That what we know, but behind the curtain they were the reflection of the intense blood war between this factions. Nobody would let the others hold the entire city on its hands.

    Everything changed in 20th century: the Priscus from Lancea Et Sanctum finally brokered a deal between the factions and clans that split the city into four fiefdoms. Together they formed The Overlord Council: an unstable alliance enforced by the Prince – a council member elected by the others. As long no one interfered with each other's domain things would last... Or wouldn't?



    Things started to fall down from 1992, when a series of mysterious attacks put the safety the status-quo. A mass assault into theCarandiru Penitentiary by vampires ended forcing the brutal execution of all the survivors to protect the Masquerade. The mortal medias was outraged and the Kindred felt it. They hunted the culprits without any meaningful success. Things kept going south with a series of arsons and a open war between the Police and gangs. On the eve of 2010, a name took responsibility by the attacks: Black Mask.

    Who he was? What he was? Nobody knew, but every Kindred felt a chilling fear long forgotten since they were alive. The uncertainty of tomorrow.

    But no one would expect what would have happen in 2015. Right into the City Core, one of well establishef fiefdoms, the Council Overlord and his entire coterie were burned into ashes inside his secret haven. The Council broke down. Suspecting each other and even their underlings, their members tightened the grip and isolated themselves in their fiefdoms trying to purge any spies and traitors.

    Today, the City Core lacks any kind of formal law. Street-level leaders and gangs see that as an opportunity to gain power. Death dealers and influence traders are triving. The Prince and the Seneschal arrived on site and are trying their best to pacify and restore the fiefdom, so the entire structure won't fall apart. But who will inherit it the vacant crown?


    The Chronicle
    The game starts with six friends going to a club downtown for the birthday of one of them... but things go wrong when they wake up in a abandoned park – dead and hungry – without any memory of their last night. The unauthorized newborn end up being dragged into Kindred society, attracting attention from the streets and even from the Prince itself. Who made them and why? In a domain that every inch is an advantage several groups try to leverage the newly formed coterie for their own goals. Fresh meat are usually the best tools.



    So here it is.
    This is the setting that I created. It's my first time as a Storyteller and I still have to develop the major characters and their agendas – beyond just naming it.
    Any help and tips in how to do that I would appreciate: I am a first timer.

    If anyone managed to read through it all, I would gladly appreciate if you give me tips, ideas and even poke holes that I need to patch in this city.

    Thanks!

              This is an awesome scenario and you look to be off to a great start. I just wanted to register my praise and get it out of the way. :P

              I play a lot of InRealLife-made-ChroniclesOfDarkness stories for some reason: My player tend to love the conceit. I will try to come back to this with more later but two tips: One, try to immerse the player in the Vampire world as soon as possible and Two, play through each tier.

              The first tip is not because the vampire world is great but I find it hard to simulate my friend's friends for too long, so I try to let them live out a fantasy or two, then have the vampire world catch up with them. This allows them to play themselves, occasionally revisiting their lives, without forcing you to debate how a person might act. Important because, well, people are more complex than characters and therefore, willing suspension of disbelief easier to knock. I've had to retcon a few times because my players refused to accept that 'Steve' would react like that. -_-

              Second, play by tiers. If you are unfamiliar with tiers, make a trend here and ask. It is a great storytelling tool. The reason you should start at tier one is that it let's them play up the untangling from human life, explore some power fantasies, and answer the hard questions ('how do you feed?') in real time. By the time they obtain stability, you can move them to tier two and go from playing 'night to night' in game time, to playing with downtime and zenith moments.

              Hope you have fun. If you are the documenting type, I'd love to read the story you and your friend's tell. (just...for your sake...blur your identities. :P )

                  I wish you the best of luck, and yes, it is wonderfully developed.

                  What I can add to the above tips is to replace names as much as you can.

                  This isn't our world. It is the world of Requiem. Changing things like the name of a brand to something else seems silly, but it helps a lot to keep things straight. It is too easy to forget that the setting can and does deviate from our world. Using real-life names creates petrifying expectations, and you loose a lot of time doing unnecessary research and/or arguments.

                                  It goes more or less like this:

                                  Tier 1: Local. The focal point is everyday life and more personal problems. The characters deal with their neighborhood, with relatives and close acquaintances, be in the mortal or undead world.

                                  Tier 2: "County". A larger picture start to draw itself. There is mundane politics, even if of a supernatural nature, maybe minor conspiracies. It can range in scope from a couple districts in a large city to an entire country, but the feel of the greater works is still relatively mundane. The characters can save lives, but they still work withing the general status-quo.

                                  Tier 3: Global. The Great Design, Conspiracies and World-Shaking Powers. The true scope doesn't really matter, but the sense remains that the status-quo itself is at stake. The characters can change relevant laws or institutions of the setting. Even if they decide to not change it, they know they have a role in keeping the ways of the world. They can deal meaningfully with the movers and shakers of the world around.

                                  Tier 4: Universal. The very laws of reality are at odds in their hands, or in their hands to be saved. The answers to the meaning of life and the source of life, the beginning of the Curses and the ever greater truths of the universe lie just ahead, if the characters can muster the will to grasp them. What's at stake is the very destiny of entire supernatural races or humanity as a whole.

                                  As a rule of thumb, you can default your chronicle to beginning at Tier 1, with a gradual transition to Tier 2. Maybe someday, if the chronicle goes far, Tier 3.

                                  But it is relevant to have in mind that the Tier System, though a great tool for story-devising and chronicle-management, is just about scope, not power nor timing. A typical chronicle will change a little between them back and forth once in a while without any problem, and some chronicles can change between then in any order. Some go from BP 1 to 10 without ever changing Tier. Both the power of the PCs and of their enemies and allies won't matter too much for the definition of Tier (but the expectations of the players will).

                                      Excellent summary.

                                      Based off of this, you can see why I recommend moving from Tier 1 to Tier 2. Start local, where little questions such as how you feed, where you get your money and the exact relationship with your touchstone is, and when you have exhausted all those questions and settled into vampire life, move to Tier 2 Vampire-as-Written game, with its politics, horror, and intrigue.

                                      In essence, when 'Steve', one of your players, successfully gets to a point where he can describe a day in the life of his character, you are ready to take an aspect of said life and use it to weave in the Sheriff. For example, Steve might be a banker in a big office. After speaking with management, he is permitted to work from home and come into the office as he needs. He uses this to farm out his work so he gets a steady stream of cash to live off, and he goes to the office and chows down on two or three office clerks when he gets hunger, careful to keep them alive and in the dark as to his true nature. As Steve has won a temporary normality, it is now time to threaten that normality as a hook for moving into Vampire politics. Naturally, it comes in the form of the Invictus, rulers of the West, to enforce their rule. He'll have to pledge himself to a local power-player if he intends on carrying on his activities. Once you move to this point, the scope widens and the ennui of vampire life can take a backseat. Hunting, unless it would be dramatic to introduce a complication, runs as he has established, so it can be a simple dice roll every now and then. This way, attention remains fixed on parrying rivals!

                                      Disclaimer: This is not a script for a perfect game. It is meant to inspire ideas and be a useful tool to fall back upon. Remember, if you are all having fun and want it, you can fight to preserve the vampire race while doing hunting in detail.

                                          Quite a simple way to do it for starters, and not a bad idea at all for any game.

                                          I would just add one thing. Play out the transition for some time, doing the supernatural stuff and first uncommon conflicts in Tier 1. It is local, but doesn't have to be everyday life, so it will work well to start the more advanced stuff still in a safe environment. Bring the vampiric society to argue their rights for the hunting grounds they're using, but keep it personal. A rival, a menace, their first encounter with a ghost. A lot of stuff can happen without getting the players out of their neighborhood.
