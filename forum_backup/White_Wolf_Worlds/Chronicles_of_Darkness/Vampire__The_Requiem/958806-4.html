                        Sorry for the multi-post, but going to add this bit of idea in here.

                        The Lancea Sanctum is primarily about self loathing. They make it very clear to you that you are damned and nothing you do will ever cleanse you of your sins.                                 I think there are countless ways to view any covenant and interpret their credo (and that's what I like about them, you could justify both being a very nice person and a complete psycho with the right arguments). In its worst light, the Lancea et Sanctum is a bunch of fanatics who build their existence around the goal of finding the purest persons and messing with their lives to see how long it takes them to snap. They're nihilistic jaded assholes who don't have anything to lose anymore and will do anything to live up to their beliefs and ensure other vampires do the same or die.

                                But logically, that description should only concern a minority of the covenant. That stuff is a lot to accept and it's not realaly anything to look forward to. A few extremely violent people will dive into it and use it as a means to act without moral restrains and be admired for it. Still, I'd think that kind of person is rare. Unless they're very intelligent, they would attract attention sooner or later. The Lancea forbids killing other vampires and that might be a means to ensure that these people don't trigger open wars with other covenants, but such extreme individuals aren't constrained by rules very long. If they need to, they'll find a loophole, make excuses that it doesn't really count in a variety of cases (the Catholic Church didn't have any problems with "thou shlat not kill" when they called for the crusades). Point is, a covenant composed of too many of those people would make for a very unstable political scene and they probably wouldn't live long for most of them.

                                The more positive aspect of the Lancea is that it gives the same thing other covenants do. You may say it's about self loathing and resigning to damnation, but that's not entirely true. From what I think I remember, the Lancea doesn't rule out hope of redemption, but that's a distant hope and only if you fulfil your role. The Lancea Sanctum, even more than other covenants, is about acceptance of one's fate. You are damned, you survive by preying on other living beings and the sun kills you. There is no way out of there, but that doesn't mean you have to lose hope. Even in this condition you have a place in the world. You are needed. You can go crazy and become a beast, but you can do and be better than that.

                                The Invictus teaches you that being undead doesn't mean you have to give up your dreams and ambition. You can still accumulate power behind the scenes or get behind someone who has that power and is ready to give a small portion to you. The Carthian movement teaches you that the vampire society is unjust and that you can do something and fight the elders' power. The Ordo Dracul teaches you that you don't have to be content being what you have become. You can be more if you're willing to put in the hard work, and the sky's the limit. The Circle of the Crone, I assume, teaches you that it's okay to be a vampire. Monsters are just another type of being. What happened to you didn't put you outside the world and you're definitely not alone. Just accept what you are and do what comes natural. The Lancea Sanctum teaches you that all those teachings get old after a while. Power can only last so long and you'll get bored eventually and make a mistake. Fighting elders and the Man is a vain and endless struggle. One day, you'll get careless and die or you'll become the elder and then what ? The quest to better oneself is an illusion. Some things are better left alone and you don't need to be better, you already are exactly what you are supposed to be. Finally, yes you are a monster, but you are not just a normal part of the world anymore. The beast will eventually betray you and nothing will hold you back from becoming an abomination.

                                The Lancea gives lots and desperate vampires a goal, something immaterial to look forward to. It gives them a faith to cling to when their requiem gets tough. When you doubt, you can turn to a priest or bishop who will honestly listen to you. They won't demand loyalty like the prince or give you a bomb and tell you to blow up the elyseum like the firebrand. Sure, the principles are kinda violent, but you're there for a reason, you're making a difference and you're working for the greater good. You're doing what needs to be done, what nobody else can or should do.

                                That's the vibe I get if I try to paint them in a positive light. I'm thinking most Lancea members aren't very active in their activity of trolling mortals. That's actually kind of hard and a lot of work. I'd imagine most of those who try either select one person and see where they can go with them (within the limit of how far they are personnally ready to go) or go from mortal to mortal, doing bad things to them like once or twice before moving on. A good number of sanctified, mostly recent members would probably want to hold off for a while, pay lip service to the faith, but not really doing much and just being happy with having church buddies they can talk to when they get lonely. A lot of time would also probably be spent discussing the faith, the texts, doing mass, confessions, and stuff. Finally, there's politicking. A cult has power dynamics. Status, recognition (inside and outside the covenant) attract most people. The Invictus is associated with power and money, but powerful priests and bishops have power over other sanctified. They can get money easily and have a pretty good life. And it's not necessarily a bad thing for other convenants. A bishop who values their expensive cars, luxurious apartments and private receptions will think twice before making too much wave or going to war against a rival covenant. These are vampires who may be bargained with. Some of them may also see the Lancea for what is is good for, not only a holy mission and/or a private army, but also a home for younger vampires, a way to make them feel better about the whole deal and stop them from rampaging left and right out of spite.

                                    Nitpick - its actually "thou shall not murder," which is a much different definition. From the earliest texts, self defense and military action have never been covered by the commandment. I mean, hells. The hebrews immediately went to war to take Isreal immediately after getting the tablets! That's not a loophole, its a modern interpretation of an ancient text.

                                    Some people are fond of saying that words mean things. What those people don't mention is that words mean different things to different people, and that what words mean changes over time and location.

                                    Now, lets apply this to talking about the Sanctified's relationship to their commandments, the Three Traditions. Masquerade, Progeny and Diablarie. The Curse wants to spread. Vampires come with reproductive urges to make more like themselves. This is also another LeS understanding.

                                    Vampires don't have strict laws. Their covenant wouldn't survive with it - exceptions must exist by the vampire's very nature. So, the LeS have guidelines instead of a code. That makes things far more fluid without the need of excuses or loopholes.
