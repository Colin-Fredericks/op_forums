    I always considered Dominate the sledgehammer for forcing people to do things whether they want to do it or not, subsuming their will pretty much entirely, while Majesty makes them want to do things by contrast.     The tricky part there is that the most definitely Majesty allows you to make someone do things is that it makes it easier to get them to listen to you and it lets you steer their emotional state — it's more useful for engendering loyalty than precision on its own, which is why the archetypal Daeva manipulator is a creature of actual social engineering rather than just a funnel for Majesty and the Vinculum.

    Dominate, by contrast, has a more discrete impact on its victims and does not build from a root power that by-design attracts attention; its capacity for use as a blunt instrument doesn't change the fact that a master of Dominate can get people to do what she wants without even knowing she exists, which is the trade you make between a Discipline whose primary target is a person and a Discipline whose primary target is a crowd.

                                Basically this. When an NPC succeeds at a social roll against a PC, I give them a Condition. They can then decide to follow it or not.

                                For instance, an NPC trying to talk a PC into following a course of action he had not intended on could put a Condition called "Conflicted" on the PC. The PC was sure what the right path forward was, but because of the NPC raising some very good points in an eloquent manner, there's a little feeling of doubt at his core. The condition would probably penalize Resolve, and would resolve when the PC decides to go along with the NPCs plan. It would fade naturally without granting a beat once the dilemma is no longer relevant (so if you stick to your original plan, it vanishes once you complete that plan).

                                Conditions like this offer various little penalties/incentives to the players for buying into the idea that, yes, NPC X or Y really is that good of a social manipulator, in a way that is generally fun for everyone involved. Also lets you play with Conditions like that more outside of the Disciplines, which can be a bit of a declaration of war to employ against PCs, while a good old social roll rarely is.

                                        This is what Rose said about Majesty and whether vampires can figure out they've been Awed. It's in the FAQ, about that very question.

                                        Not automatically. They just feel what Awe wants them to feel. However, they retain their faculties enough that if a Daeva seems too amazing to be true, they might be able to turn around and defy it with the predatory aura.
                                        Saying it breaks the Masquerade is a bit an of an overexaggeration. A vampire who knows Majesty exists may or may not decide to go with it (on its own, the lower Dot abilities are not harmful, and Lashing out can cause a scene, or fail and just leave you Awed and suddenly on the Majestic vampire's bad side). This doesn't mean that an ordinary mortal, suddenly seeing someone interesting, is going to think to themselves that they must be under a magic spell and it's clearly from a vampire. The person just seems overly charismatic or attention-getting, similar to how Striking Looks can work.

                                        There was an example of Majesty (or whatever the VtM version of it was) in one of the Setite novels that was well done, the perspective character was talking to another vampire, that they liked, and noticed that they enjoyed their company overmuch, considering the situation. They were being too amiable. They didn't Lash Out, but asked the other vampire to tone it down so they could think clearly.

                                        Keep in mind that mortals can't really Lash Out and don't have a Clash of Wills to activate against this stuff, so it doesn't matter if it is out of the ordinary, that's just part of being in the Chronicles of Darkness.

                                        The question of whether mind control can be used on PCs is about as old as 'charm person,' and I don't believe anyone has come up with a single answer that encompasses all solutions. Some people will just walk right up to the Dominator of Mind-Command and punch them in the face even if you describe how their character doesn't want to. Others will get the gist of things just by a description. Sometimes you need to back descriptions with mechanical oomph. It depends on the group. I think it's unreasonable to completely take it off the table, especially with gamelines that are effectively about mind control in one form or another. Some people might not want their characters to be usurped in any way, but when dealing with blood bonds and long-term changes you can talk to the player beforehand and see what they think about it and perhaps reach a compromise.

                                                    And exactly because Majesty is something you can 'know', it has to be a bit subtle. When you cheat someone out of something important using one of the higher dots of Majesty, they remember what happened and may retaliate when the feelings wear off. When you do that with Dominate, they may not even remember ever meeting you at all. That is a huge boon of Dominate. And while people keep saying Majesty is AoE, that's not true for most of its dots: Confidant, Green Eyes, Loyalty... all single target. So for the most part, Majesty competes directly with Dominate in application.

                                                    Making Majesty both more knowable, more counterable (once you're Dominated, good luck) and more obvious than Dominate is clearly painting one as just plain inferior to the other. Dominate is the obvious one: people can tell the one affected is not acting naturally most of the time. But the victim will rarely if ever remember anything about what happened. It is less knowable. Majesty is more subtle, its very effects make you want to not believe something supernatural is going on. But the higher dots past Awe are very knowable after the fact.

                                                    It's hard to think anything bad about a vampire using Awe and that should include thinking they're using Awe in the first place. Like Rose said, Awe makes you feel what it wants you to feel, and it doesn't want you to feel that what is happening isn't entirely normal. So yes, Rose says that you can sometimes figure it out. Her sentence has the structure "If... might..." That's two degrees of doubt in a single sentence. If you turn around and use that to say half of all uses of Awe in a social setting with Vampires is detected and foiled, I really think you're reinterpreting a statement that says "It's possible, maybe, sometimes" to be "Vampires be Lashing out against them Daeva errydayyyyy".

                                                    It really depends on the context. If you get affected by Majesty, you'll probably rationalize it:

                                                    "I was lonely."

                                                    "He was funny."

                                                    "She was really on fire last night."

                                                    Depending on what you did under the effects, you may think you were being dumb and be too embarrassed or annoyed to want to associate with the vampire. Or, you may remember it as a good time, and even though you don't feel the same crush or trust or whatever that you did last night, you remember it and are more likely to be friendly to the vampire.

                                                    If you've frequently experienced Majesty, you're likely to have some weird feelings:

                                                    "Some nights I need to let go."

                                                    "Dunno why, but around her I completely lose it."

                                                    "He's a guilty pleasure."

                                                    Without Majesty, you probably vary on how you respond, depending on what else is going on. You might seek the vampire out, because you know you like their company. You may avoid them, because you know you do stupid shit when they ask.

                                                    It's also worth noting that since the bigger powers build on Awe, other people you're with are likely to back up your impressions of the vampire:

                                                    "You've got a thing. I can kinda understand it, but it's a thing."

                                                    "Yeah, he was hot in those jeans."

                                                    "Her bullshit always sounds profound until you actually think about it."
                                                    Notice how nothing in here is described as being some form of supernatural attraction. Awe and Majesty feel just like high levels of mundane charisma and social skills, and maybe some personal magnetism. The best way to think about whether or not someone would Lash Out against Awe, is to determine if they'd Lash Out in this situation if the vampire just had higher attributes and skills. If the answer is yes, go for it. If the answer is no, there needs to be some very clear reason why you're doing it against something that looks and feels exactly that way.

                                                    Similarly, fallout against Majesty after the fact is mostly identical to fallout from being completely mundanely talked into stupid shit.

                                                    This one is also good:

                                                    Originally posted by Rose Bailey View Post
                                                    Lashing out to throw off Awe works the same as lashing out without provocation. It just has the additional effect of throwing off Majesty. You pick an aspect of the Beast, you roll, and the vampire you're lashing out at chooses fight or flight. Willpower is spent and Conditions are imposed as normal.

                                                    This almost always represents an escalation of an encounter, and it can get pretty messy, since lashing out against someone trying to Awe you doesn't change their effect on anyone else nearby. At the very least, you run the risk of looking like an asshole.

                                                    Also keep in mind that nobody besides the participants perceive the effects of lashing out... they only see the physical actions the vampires take to lash out and resist. So when the Gangrel resists the Daeva's Awe, people see her growl menacingly or sidle up with a distracting and sharp-toothed smile, and they see the Daeva's response. They don't perceive the supernatural contest that underlies that interaction.

                                                    The predatory aura is called an "aura" because it's an ambient effect that allows vampires to recognize each other. Lashing out is focusing the aura on one person and cracking it like a whip.

                                                    Other vampire capabilities are actually manifestations of the aura. For example, Awe itself is "tinting" your aura to show you in a good light, while Face in the Crowd is turning your predatory aura inwards so that neither peer nor prey notices you.
                                                    Seems pretty clear, exactly what I said before this quote: Lashing Out against Awe is exactly like Lashing Out without there being Awe, but just happens to end Awe. Because usually the provocation will be something else happening, not just "Oh, there's Awe going on." And yes, it can easily get messy.
