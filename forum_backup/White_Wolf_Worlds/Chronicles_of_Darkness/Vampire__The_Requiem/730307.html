    The God-Machine in Requiem

    10-26-2015, 07:30 PM
    When I first heard about the 2e version of the WoD, I was under the impression that the God-Machine was intended to be a spine element that drew the main lines together. After digging through VtR and briefly flipping through WtF, that doesn't seem to be the case. I'm fine with that. It's a neat idea but one that seems best suited for a Demon game. I don't have much interest in fitting it into my Vampire game.

    That said, has anyone used it as a plot point in a VtR game? Also, for those who have been around the game a bit longer than me, was GM intended to be sweeping cross-book plot glue, or did it just happen to be the story choice they made for the rules update?

          Originally posted by dxanders View Post
          That said, has anyone used it as a plot point in a VtR game? Also, for those who have been around the game a bit longer than me, was GM intended to be sweeping cross-book plot glue, or did it just happen to be the story choice they made for the rules update?
          It was part of the chronicle book setup, from before CCP gave the go-ahead to call the new rules a second edition; it was just that the mortal/blue-book line was going to have a signature chronicle antagonist that was the same antagonist from Demon, just viewed from a different perspective. The God-Machine was meant to fill the same role as the strix or the idigam: a fancy antagonist that doubled as an obvious, convenient answer for people who needed something to build a story around. (I believe they also batted around the idea of a Mothman Chronicle for the bluebooks before settling on the GMC.)

          So no, the God-Machine wasn't supposed to be a big fixture in Vampire any more than the strix are a big fixture in Demon. It's a thing in the World of Darkness, but so are the True Fae and the Seers of the Throne and all that. They're just not very Vampire-y, so Vampire books don't talk about them that much.

                      I actually have this as a big chunk of my current mixed book session since we have a very mixed group in my game (Vampire, Mage, and Changeling). I added in the God Machine because I had a diverse group, and the players in my game really like heavy investigation and enigma like stories. I threw in a Demon who is an NPC guide while the team is in New York looking to take out an over all threat posed by the Machine.

                      To be honest though, unless you really want to go for a long haul, I really had to wrangle it into the story to give the characters reason. The vampire was easy, the thing is taking in potential food. Mage? He just wants to investigate it and see if there is anything he can learn from it (Tech based mage) and the Changeling sees it as a disruption. Besides notoriety in the game for taking down a branch of the Machine, I did bribe them with a bit of money via the Demon.

                      Note my players as I said like a lot of enigma based stories and lead them into dead ends sometimes, and they have to actually think out what is happening. If you do not have players like this, I have a feeling they would have a tough time with it. Ran solo with mortals, sure, but adding in the additional races, you need to really give them incentive.

                              The San Francisco write-up in VtR 2e mentions a Wyrm's Nest somewhere in Stanford. In my setting, that Wyrm's Nest turned out to be Infrastructure. It was a very weird place where space and time did not act as one would expect, making it rather perilous. The God-Machine was using it to have one of his Angels experiment on Vampires from all across history. He only needed one more to complete his plot and he'd let the other PCs go free, but the players didn't take that bait.

                              I vaguely recall one of the interpretations for the G-M in GMC is that it can only account for mortals in its plans, which leaves it to generally ignoring supernaturals. The point of the Infrastructure was to try and let the G-M adapt to include Vampires in its plans, too.

                              I think it was a pretty good plot line for Ordo Dracul characters and their allies.

                              So that's how I integrated some G-M into my campaign. Hope that was interesting for you.

                                      Yeah. I tend to feel that a Vampire focus benefits best from crossover when it's sparse and distant. Vampires are some of the most practical gribblies of the World of Darkness in my eyes. They're not blind to sharing the world with foreign shadows, but they believe good fences make good neighbors. They put up some cautionary defenses in case trouble starts, and then as long as trouble doesn't start and the darkness stays out of their business, they're happy to stay out of its business in turn. (Until some clever asshole neonate or dragon decides to try to drag the darkness in as an X-factor weapon against rivals, anyway.)

                                      As long as those fences stay up (and I think they stay up pretty well in the long run), crossover elements take the form of urban legends, unanticipated dangers and complicating factors. The monster in the cave isn't a beast for the vampire to slay, but a problem to avoid when seeking cover from an enemy. A mage might get unexpectedly caught up in the harrowing of a spirit from the Hisil, and that'll spark an adventure to figure out the nature of the spirit and its motives for running wild, and then to seek to control, banish, destroy or transform it. The vampire gets unexpectedly caught up in a bizarre spirit harrowing, and that's just a bad day to remind you that unexpected shit happens to complicate your Requiem. Maybe you end up having to cash in some favors to recover that put you in a spot later, or somebody dies in the confusion and their death is pinned on a Carthian assassin. But if the spirit doesn't keep following the vampire, the vampire just doesn't go back to that place. There's no need to learn what its deal is or where it came from. Mark off the lost territory on the map and go spend your time somewhere safer, around people who can more easily be manipulated. Let some other sap play hero.

                                                          Interestingly, to me at least, Theban Sorcery actually makes sense as a kind of Occult Matrix output. Theban is holy magic that requires unique sacrifices to work, and we know that sacrifice is how the God Machine generates the essence to perform its techno-miracles (at least for angels). And Theban generally is explained as channeling the power of God, Amoniel, Vahishtael, or a different angel (at least since the LS book came out), and Theban rituals are learned in a similar manner to how demons learn their Embeds.

                                                          So, if you want to bring in the God Machine into Requiem, I'd highly recommend actually going for making Theban Sorcerers (or maybe anyone with Lancea et Sanctum status, even non-sorcerers) be part of a unique Infrastructure. That means we could have angels hovering over vampiric shoulders, or demons seeking to destroy the local Lance (or subverting them somehow into providing aether for the Unchained). Something along those lines.
