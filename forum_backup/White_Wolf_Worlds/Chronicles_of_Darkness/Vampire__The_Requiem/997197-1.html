    A mortal can buy up to 9 dots in Attributes. It isn't likely, but means that with +9 dots a person can raise from her usual state to her peak performance. A vampire, preternaturally buffed by the power of blood, needs +3 BP just to do the same, and only then it starts to surpass their mortal potential. With BP granting +3 dots in Attributes, the characters are likely to resort to this way for increasing them anyway, with the sense of this increase being powered by the power of blood without being just to keep it up with mortality.
    That's why for this to work, I needed to limit the number of Attribute dots a mortal could buy with XP, to emphasize the difference between a mortal and a vampire. If we allow mortals to purchase as many Attribute dots as they want, then I agree vampires should be allowed to do so as well. It's either one or the other, nothing in between or it stops making sense.

    Do not underestimate the value of spending more Vitae per turn, and of keeping more Vitae at any given time. A character with Celerity can spend Vitae to win Initiative, move fast, and yet as any vampire get a Physical Boost or heal injury. But only a vampire with enough BP can do all of that at the same time. Since Masquerade, it is really one of the strongest benefits of BP.
    I don't, but again it goes back to the fact I liked the idea of torpid elders to be weakened if they wake up at a low Blood POtency. That's why they lose attribute dots and Discipline powers along with Blood Potency. I will admit it served my narrative for my current Chronicle. I needed a very old vampire, formerly powerful, to wake up but represent an even match to my three PCs. If Discipline levels were not limited this way, it would have been a bloodbath and they couldn't have negociated their true Embrace (they were revenants, one was uplifted, and then they embraced each other). That being said, it's not a major aspect of my rule set and it's easily (re)houseruled.

    On the other hand, the impossibility on raising Humanity can create some nasty corners, both making it impossible to play the trope of the Good Amnesiac and others of Elder Redemption, and by making it a sink impossible for the players to get out if they get stuck. You can make it very hard to raise, but "impossible" have too many implications.
    That's a very interesting point. I guess it comes down to my interpretation of what Humanity represents, in my own setting. It's not so much a reflect of your moral or how you react as a reflect of how corrupted your body is by the extent of the curse. I actually try to have the PCs' interpretation of their character completely free from their Humanity score. It means you could be a Humanity 9, Blood Potency 1 and be a complete monster but you could be a Humanity 2 and BP 8 vampire, look monstruous, yet have a moral. Your Humanity is more about how you look than how you think. The more powerful you want to become, the more of your Humanity (physically speaking) you have to sacrifice which makes it harder for you, and more costly, to hide among the flock.

    I still love the inner conflict trope, but I just don't let it depends on Humanity level. For example, my current players' characters have just been recently turned, but their choices and circumstances are making them fall into a downward spiral. They just did something monstruous. Yet, they remain at Humanity 8 or 9 because the power of their curse (BP) is still low. I also like the fact that once you corrupt your body with power, you cannot recover what's lost. You can still pursue redemption, but you cannot recover what you lost. I admit it is brutal, though, but being a vampire is a curse.

    Now, if you prefer to have Humanity to be linked to the PCs' actions, values, or behavior, then I do agree this rule would be very limiting and you underlined an important point.

        So, to divide this up into the interconnected "chunks" you can plop into a campaign:
        Originally posted by Lestat View Post
        Attributes
        In my setting, mortals are limited by their genetic potential and cannot increase each attribute by more than one dot. Vampires cannot increase Attributes with experience.

        [...]

        Each time a vampire increases her Blood Potency, she gains one free dot in a Power Attribute, a Finesse Attribute, and a Resistance Attribute for a total of 3. [...] Also, for each BP point a vampire loses due to torpor, she loses one dot in each Attribute category. I also wanted to highlight how Blood Potency impacts a vampire's current power level, thus making torpor an even bigger decision.
        Originally posted by Lestat View Post
        Humanity

        [This replaces the normal Humanity and Blush of Life rules.]

        I wanted to make Humanity something more physical, for lack of a better term. In my setting, any human has a Humanity of 10. A vampire's Humanity's maximum value is determined by 10 - Blood Potency. Therefore, the more powerful a vampire gets, the less human she is. Humanity can never be recovered, even if Blood Potency decreases. For each Embrace or Diablerie, the vampire's Blood Potency is considered 1 higher for the purpose of determining Humanity. As a result, the Embrace becomes an even more important decision. I will admit that my setting has a very low vampire population, and this system was adapted with that in mind.

        Just like in the original material, the lower Humanity gets, the less human the vampire appears. It is just a bit more extreme here. It's a -1 at Humanity 9, and it increases by one as you go down the ladder, until it reaches -5 at Humanity 5. After that, the vampire cannot pass for human. At humanity 4, she looks dead; at humanity 3, some monstruous features appear, and at Humanity 2, she is a monstrous humanoid (a bit like what the nosferatu used to be). At Humanity 1, she is more monster than human, and at Humanity 0 she looks nothing like the human she used to be.

        [Additionally, any time you gain a point of Blood Potency, you may choose to gain a Bane, lowering your effective Blood Potency for the purposes of determing Humanity by 1. The rules for Banes are otherwise unchanged.]

        A vampire can spend blood to look more human for an entire night. Each vitae point used this way increases Humanity by one for the purpose of determining the appearance of the vampire. The more inhuman a kindred is, the more blood is needed to look human.
        Some people object to the "humanity doesn't return" bit. Both ways work, honestly - leaving Humanity permanently lower means it serves as a marker for Elders (and justifies getting XP for lowering Humanity during chargen!), and generally adds to their mystique, but having Torpor "wipe the slate clean" could also be thematic.

        So here's an optional houserule to the houserule:

        Any time you would lose a point of Blood Potency (e.g. through Torpor), you may choose to either regain a point of Humanity or lose one Bane. This cannot raise your Humanity above the cap imposed by Blood Potency.
        Originally posted by Lestat View Post
        Final Death

        [This replaces the rules for what causes Final Death.]

        I came up with the idea that a vampire who is destroyed [in the ways specified in the original Final Death rules] can always come back. The vampiric essence is free and can reform (in my setting, the Strix are actually vampires who were destroyed). The higher their blood potency when they died, the less time it takes for them to reform. Then, they can possess a human and become a kindred once more (BP 1) or a vampire (BP of the vampire, if equal or inferior to the Strix's).

        The only way to cause Final Death is through Diablerie, which justifies why it is so badly regarded.
        Originally posted by Lestat View Post
        Blood Potency

        [...]

        - Blood Potency cannot be increased through experience. It is increased by actively concentrating one's blood. Mechanically speaking, it means players have to use blood points for the specific purpose of concentrating their blood, or gain "Concentration Points". Once you reach a target number of Concentration points, you increase your Blood Potency. The target number is determined by the maximum number of Vitae points a vampire can store at the next Blood Potency level, times 10. So a Blood Potency 1 vampire has to concentrate 110 blood points to increase his blood potency. This number can be adjusted up or down to fit the number of nights your players actually play. If you play a night to night chronicle, the rule can be used as is, but if you tend to just play three nights a month (in play), then you may want to lower the target number. At first, it might seem a bit complicated, but so far it's worked very smoothly. The goal is that a vampire who wants to become more powerful has to feed more, which takes more time and attracts more attention. In practice, it doesn't take much game time either because the hunt can still be resolved by one dice roll. The consequences of frequent feeding though, are at the GM discretion.

        - After torpor, it takes three times less Concentration points to regain the lost Blood Potency. So, powerful vampires tend to regain their power quickly. I like the idea of vampires feeding actively after waking from torpor to regain their lost power. It's also an opportunity for their enemies to take them down at their weakest. To me, this is highly thematic.

        - A vampire's level in any Discipline is limited by her Blood Potency. Thus, you need to be Blood Potency 5 to fully develop a Discipline. When Blood Potency decreases, the higher levels in any Discipline are not lost but cannot be used until the vampire regains enough Blood Potency.
        Originally posted by Lestat View Post
        Blood Potency

        [...]

        - A vampire spends his Blood Potency/2 (rounded up) in blood points to wake up every night. As a vampire becomes more powerful, he must feed more. Power at a cost. This alone can create tension between the vampires of one city because as they get more powerful, their presence is felt more and more, and the Masquerade is put at a greater risk. It can lead to tension and conflict that are directly caused by the vampiric condition, and the competition for blood which is very thematic.

        - A vampire is not limited to kindred blood once Blood Potency 6 is reached anymore. Instead, a vampire needs more blood to get one vitae point when he feeds from a human. At Blood Potency 6, she needs 2 to get 1, same a BP 7, then it's 3 to get 1 at BP 8, 4 to get 1 at BP 9, and 5 to get 1 at BP 10. Kindred blood remains at a one for one basis though. I prefered the idea of humans remaining the primary source of sustenance for kindred, but at the same time I wanted to keep some restrictions. This effect is cumulative to the increasing vitae loss every time a vampire wakes up. Getting more powerful is costly, for the vampire herself, as well as for her kind because she needs more blood and competition becomes more fierce.
        EDIT:
        Originally posted by Lestat View Post
        In my setting, I don't allow Willpower to overcome any restriction because the restrictions are what makes the theme.
        Originally posted by Lestat View Post
        I should add that a staked vampire is not in a true torpor, but a stasis. Thus, her Blood POtency does not decrease, which makes staking vampires dangerous but necessary so they don't come back.

                That's good enough. The greater problem isn't Humanity being linked to behavior or not. Is it not being possible to raise, becoming a sinkhole.

                You have three ways of lowering Humanity: to raise BP enough to lower your cap, to Embrace, and to Diablerize. Even considering that raising BP only lowers your cap, do not actually lowers your Humanity, and not considering yet any way to raise it, it means that between Diablerie and Embrace, you're limited to 9 for all eternity, and that's it. If you never raises you BP soon enough to actually loose Humanity because of that, you can Embrace or Diablerize up to 9 times, and that's it. Reaching BP 10 or doing just one more Embrace, and that's it. If you raises you BP before you ever do one of those, what's very likely, then you'll have even less chances.

                It isn't about Humanity being what it used to be. It is about you creating an inescapable corner.

                    Don't forget Banes; I mentionned how they could be used to stop the loss of Humanity dots, three in total as you can only have three banes. Personally, I did not have a problem with Humanity being an "unescapable corner". At least, it's not unescapable if you don't try to reach BP 10 everytime. ANd what would be the point given how difficult it is to sustain ? Most vampires don't reach super high blood potency and there is a reason. Now, I understand Diablerie and Embrace can be a sink which is why Vampires don't indulge in these a lot. Power comes at a cost, as well as creating another vampire or causing Final Death through Diablerie.

                    But then again, I completely understand how it can be too extreme for some, and the solution presented above is a great alternative. THe middle ground could also be to allow torpor to only remove banes. This would allow a vampire to Embrace again and engage in Diablerie. Sure you would have to limit your Blood Potency but mitigating all these variables makes for interesting choices and trade offs.

                    Let me illustrate this with an example:

                    Octave is a vampire with 4 in Blood Potency. He never embraced nor commited Diablerie. Thus, his Humanity is 6. Now, let's say he wishes to Embrace but does not want to lose more Humanity. He can take a Bane, Embrace and not lose any Humanity dot. He can do so, or commit Diablerie two more times without losing any Humanity by taking a bane each time (maximum of three). After that, if he wishes to Embrace or commit Diablerie, then he has to go into torpor. For each "period" of torpor, he can remove one bane. Potentially, he can do all of it all over again, while still gaining his BP back without losing a single more Humanity dot.

                    Now, I am not saying it is easy. It's not. But so is the nature of the Curse. And no, he cannot increase his Blood Potency without losing more Humanity unless he takes a bane for it. So the questions are: is he willing to lose more of his Humanity to become more powerful ? Or is he going to take banes to mitigate the loss ? And if so, he only has three banes he can take, so how is he going to use them ? Raise BP ? Embrace ? Cause final death to his worst ennemy while gaining his power ?

                    Either way, potentially, he can exist for eternity, and embrace or commit Diablerie several times during that Eternity thanks to the use of Banes and torpor.

                            They do. But maybe I did not express myself clearly. I am not saying Humanity does not influence how vampires act, I am saying Humanity is not influenced by how vampires act. It's a subtle but important difference. That is, as her humanity lowers, a vampire will tend to focus more on what's important to her survival and everything else will start becoming secondary. So it does have an impact on a vampire's behavior, but the behavior does not have an impact on Humanity.

                            As a maybe not so great comparison, if you act like a crazy person, you will not get rabbies. But if you get rabbies you are going to act as a crazy person.
