      It depends on what materials you're using. In the Ordo Dracul book, it was not unheard of for a Dying Light member to use their authority once they obtained it to strike back at those who gave them shit or hindered them in the first place. (Be careful who you piss on climbing the status ladder because it could come around and bite you in the ass.) They were the final word, so you have no recourse once a decision is rendered by a Twilight Judge. Even in the current description, they're supposed to be considered individuals who have already earned not only the respect, but a measure of trust from all the other Ordo Dracul mysteries. You don't get someone entrenched like that removed easily short of murdering them outright.

      Are you asking about their removal from standpoint of the the scope of mechanics or from a roleplaying perspective?

                  I would say that once reached, this kind of power is precisely that powerful. It was earned by trust, but now the Judge is free to abuse power, what is very in-character for a vampiric institution, even with a necessity for trust.

                      When you think of a Twilight Judge you should think of Stephen Hawking not Antonin Scalia. A Twilight Judge is, first and foremost, a scientist. They are drawn from the ranks of the Sworn of the Dying Light, whose task is to continue research into the coils of the dragon so as to find a way to transcend the curse of vampirism. The credibility of a Twilight Judge rests on their mastery of the coils and their creation of new coils and new scales. Even when they are personally disliked they can still command the respect of their peers because of their accomplishments in the lab.

                      For example:

                      No one in the Ordo actually likes the Nosferatu called "Black Gregory"; he's creepy and he smells bad. However, they respect him because he completely mastered the Mystery of the Ascendant and developed "scales" in the form of alchemical stimulants which can allow any vampire to maintain the blush of life all night long. The trade in these stimulants has won the Ordo great influence with the other covenants and brought in a lot of resources. Between those accomplishments, and his well honed abilities to attune wyrm's nests, his election to the office of Twilight Judge was assured.

                      One could argue that a Twilight Judge is trusted with the power they are given because their scientific successes prove they are intelligent and objective. However, it could also be that the Ordo cares more about results than it does personality when doling out political influence.

                              Originally posted by Scutarii View Post
                              So Twilight Judge goes to the people doing the research? I thought it would have gone to the political wing of the Ordo.

                              Where are you getting this information on how it operates in setting by the way?
                              In first edition, all of the sworn oaths were political. Each one is assigned an area of concern where their particular order has primacy in regards to everyone else. The Axe was in charge of covenant security and when/if the covenant is at war, The Sworn of Mysteries were in charge when matters entailed some kind of formal policy of involvement with non covenant members or representing the covenant to the city at large. The Sworn of the Dying Light were in charge of directing covenant research, formal sentencing for violations of the covenant's rules, adjudicating disputes between members, and deciding which branch has primacy in situations where authority is unclear or it overlaps with multiple groups. (Nepotism and conflicts of interest weren't an issue then either.) The Twilight Judge was a seniormost member of the Dying Light determined by how many coils they knew.

                              In the scenario Vyrkolash outlined, Black Gregory may have created a new scale and offered to sell them to individuals outside the covenant but 'technically' he would not be allowed to do so without approval from a Mysteries Sworn. Obviously this would entail more deal brokering. Likely in the form of ruling in favor of something the Mysteries Sworn would want or maybe authorizing them to study a subject formerly considered taboo.

                                  The Ordo Dracul book has the info. The reason it isn't in the "political wing" is because those Sworn are dedicated to directed the Covenant as a whole and dealing with outsiders. The researchers are the ones that deal with internal matters affecting an individual. The researchers direct individuals in the covenant as it is sometimes required during their research. The Sworn of Mysteries are actually more like legislators and are prohibited from making judgments for single members, so the Dying Light are more like the research/judicial branch. Dying Light interprets the rules handed down by the Sworn of Mysteries. Technically, the Sworn of the Mysteries should be able to "overrule" a Twilight Judge in some cases, but it wouldn't be retroactive and so it would fall more under ST fiat. If a Twilight Judge starts making judgments that seem out of character for the Ordo Dracul then you could assume the Merit is lost.

                                      Originally posted by Scutarii View Post
                                      So Twilight Judge goes to the people doing the research? I thought it would have gone to the political wing of the Ordo.

                                      Where are you getting this information on how it operates in setting by the way?
                                      Sworn of the Axe were obviously the warriors, but they also got first pick of any spoils, even if it was the spoils of investigating a haunted house where they did nothing. They are in charge during times of war. Sworn of the Dying Light are not only the researchers, but also the judges of the Dragons, and have the ability to settle any disputes. Sworn of the Mysteries are the socialites and politicians, and they get to decide the course of the overall Order when not in war or research.

                                      In short, each of the Sworn is either Mental, Physical, or Social focused. Being a judge is actually a -mental- aspect, since it requires study of law and precidents, and not social. It seems like a social thing in the US, because we elect our judges, but many other countries appoint their judges based on qualifications, and not social ability.

                                      This information comes from the Rites of the Dragon and Ordo Dracul books, who've covered the Sworn in more depth than we've seen in the core.


                                      As for its use? I actually haven't seen it come up in play, but that's because I haven't had a game that's been Ordo focused yet. Ordo politics haven't really come up in such depth where the PCs have considered it. Some games make use of such merits more than others. Now, if we were in a heavy Ordo Dracul game? I could see a lot of potential. Right now, not so much.

                                                      Academia and scientific research communities are extremely political. The people running research have very little experience with the research they oversee. If you state an unpopular opinion as a university professor, your career is over at that university. So for the real models that Dying Light would be based on rising in prestige and authority is all about how well you lie and kiss ass. It makes sense for one of the highest positions to be a political position that decides without regard to impartiality or conflicts of interest. That is exactly how the scientific community works. A member believes what they want despite little evidence or evidence to the contrary and as long as they convince enough of the others that they are correct then it is considered fact. Want a scientific study that asbestos exposure has no link to the development of cancer? Write a check. With enough zeros on that check you can get the whole world to believe it.
