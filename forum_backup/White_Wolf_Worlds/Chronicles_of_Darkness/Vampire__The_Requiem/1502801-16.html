        Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
        I know CoD has a new sunlight-damage system based on your humanity and Blood-Potency; now, just how much more effective, in game-play is that system vs. the 1e Requiem sunlight-damage?
        In 1e, sunlight damage was immediate and consistent and aggravated regardless of how potent or humanlike you were, with specific exceptions like the Mekhet clan weakness, scaling only with degree of exposure and intensity, such that as soon as someone turns into a vampire they would be vaporized in under ten seconds if thrown out on the street in the middle of the day without a shirt.

        In 2e, Kindred vampirism is meant to facilitate a more gradual slide from human to inhuman, as demonstrated by a new Humanity 7, Blood Potency 1 vampire needing ten minutes of exposure to sunlight to take a point of lethal damage and being able to avoid even that if almost totally covered; the same principle is why Clan Banes don't kick in until you first drop below Humanity 7 and elders develop a further Clan Bane at Humanity 3.

        In this model, it makes sense for 1e's level of sunlight damage in 2e to be the stuff of notably old and monstrous Kindred who use neonates for their greater surface mobility, in addition to bringing the Kindred a little closer to the rest of the supernatural world's denizens after ten years of power creep and a marked effort to more cleanly distinguish Requiem's vampires from Masquerade's without those attempts being to Requiem's detriment.

        Same goes with Forsaken, harmony, and silver-damage.
        Werewolf 2e didn't change the way silver damage works in any significant way — "taking aggravated damage" and "taking damage from silver" are nearly identical in practice — and the main distinction is that 2e makes Death Rage a two-stage process scaled from Harmony and Primal Urge where 1e had no buffer and the consequences of failing the roll to resist simply lasted until the end of the scene.

            The problem is that simply deterring them isn't good design.

            Harsh damage just means you have fewer avenues for telling stories because they're completely blocked by insta-kill, and it also fails to deliver the point of having a scale to begin with.

            Now most vampires suffer less damage from the sun, and have more means to mitigate it. The result is that doing things by day becomes feasible, if still not easy, as long as you plan ahead of time and don't try anything too crazy. That's also great for crossovers, but it's good even for pure Vampire games.

            And now you actually feel the difference from loosing it. You feel that things you could do before become too hard or impossible as you become more monstrous, instead of just having a few less or more seconds of life under the big ball of fire on the sky.

                                Originally posted by Snailslayer View Post
                                I also understand they aren't catholic or even Christian but do believe they are divinely appointed.
                                The first thing is this. The Lancea IS Christian, and mostly Catholic, although they have elements of some early sects and some schismatic churches (Christianity had a lot of schisms before the Protestant Reform, since Nicea and up to the 12th century).

                                And that is the key to understand them. Not because this is in some way representative of actual Christianity, but because vampires already have beliefs when Embraced. And after that, they'll still live in a society with a dominant belief system.

                                You don't chose what you believe in, so analyzing those Covenants through a logical examination of what they offer misses the whole point. If you're the kind of person that's picking and choosing what to live and fight for, then you're not the kind of person that'll go far in a religious group to begin with, you may like, but you don't believe. And by the way, that applies to RL religions, too, as many of the criticism you make at the Lancea isn't too dissimilar with many of the criticism against actual religions that a lot of actual people believe in.

                                So, there are actually two possible reasons for someone to follow the Lancea. The first and probably stronger in many cases, especially in the past, is that this person is already a Christian, the single most common religion (or group of religions) in the world. You may have been pious when mortal, or may have taken the Embrace and Kindred existence as a personal revelation, but you do believe in Jesus and the Bible. For those people the Lancea offers meaning within a framework that makes sense to them. Any advantage from the Circle gets negated by the fact they're heathens and witches, and work for other Covenants doesn't seems more important than the work of YHWH.

                                Now, certainly characters can still think the Lancea does not reflects their religious beliefs. But they can't simply decide not to believe. If they want to live by the Work they have very few options, and they may face persecution if they try those that involve heresy in the eyes of the powerful. For the player the religious Covenant is a choice for a fictional character, but for said character it feels like something else, especially when it comes to religious Covenants.

                                Religion is also about community, about feeling part of a group, and about showing piety to feel validated in the group and by the group. It's about liturgy and rites and stuff not because they give you direct benefits, but because they build shared experience and meaning. So the second reason is with whom you socialize. And here we have one of the strongest advantages of the Lancea over the Circle (and I say that as both a fan of the Circle and a pagan IRL): it creates and manages public spaces and rituals. It builds community with everyone, not just Lancea members, where the Circle usually has only closed affairs.

                                Building community by itself is a powerful thing because the Lancea is frequently a "second home/common ground" for a lot of Vampires. On your "typical" city members of the Invictus and the Unaligned Kindred frequent the church, Ordo and Carhian may or may not, but are still possibly there, and it's even possible that some of the Circle go just because it's the main weekly social gathering in town. Even without that, though, if the local community is mostly Christian, then most new vampires will end up Christians if they weren't already, if you socialize your whole life with the Catholic rites and community, you'll feel at home with Catholic-like rites and community, and so on.

                                Many other reasons may apply for someone to be a Lancea, and for the player to want to explore it. More than the Circle, they are used to partnership with political power structures and direct secular power. Some people want to live the idea of martyrdom, of suffering either because they feel guilt or because this massages their ego in the right way. Lots of people like the "holier than thou" attitude, and/or to preach and tell others how those others are wrong while they're saintly and right. Many like to have a reason to justify their desire for violence or cruelty, maybe because justification brings good feelings, maybe because justification makes doing so easier. Sometimes people don't really have a choice at all, the religion is mandatory in the city, or at least by their sire, and when they have the means to leave it behind they're already too entrenched and empowered by it to go ahead.

                                Of course, from the Five Covenants I think the Lancea is the most likely one to not work on a lot of places. It makes a lot of sense in mainly Catholic areas, and then less sense the less influence you have from hardcore Christianity, or even worse, more influence from other religions. Similar but at the same time very different Covenants should replace the Lancea at many places in the world, matching local religions. But overall in Europe or the Americas, especially southern US and LatAm, I think the Lancea works pretty well as a point of familiarity, a dark reflection of mainstream religion.

                                        Originally posted by Snailslayer View Post

                                        Genuine question: Why are you bringing up the afterlife for a religion that ministers to immortal corpses whose stated Earthly goal is making sure good people don't turn bad and bad people get punished and the rules of society are upheld?

                                        They revel in being assholes. They feel more like a bunch of losers who wanted an excuse to be cruel to others and don't have the spine to admit it like the Circle do.
                                        They have a nominal purpose that relates to keeping the world from changing so much that they don't have a place in it. If they were Embraced into the covenant it was because someone saw something worth preserving in them to the end of stress-testing the virtuous, making examples of sinners, or steering society toward maintaining its traditions. Not having any hope of redemption and thus leaning into a constructive form of their damnation — damnation likely levied on them by another vampire for their actions as a mortal — is notably different from "reveling in being an asshole," just as the Circle having an ideology that treats the supernatural as part of nature does not mean they are the Chill Vibes Covenant.

                                        This just doesn't seem appealing. If I'm joining one of the two religious covenants, and one is having parties and telling everyone they shouldn't be ashamed, while the other is doing the same with eyes wide shut parties but with added hypocrisy and acting like they are better for pretending to be just monsters instead of the monsters they are.
                                        The Sanctum is organized. They owe some of their big break to early Christianity hitching its wagon to the Roman Empire, and this organization and their lorekeeper/relic-hunter mode mean they are one of the major covenants best equipped for travel. The Ordo Dracul's niche includes squatting on real estate that normal people typically avoid and the Circle of the Crone is sufficiently sedentary that they're one of the primary textual use-cases for "what happens if you make a garden full of ghouled plants?"

                                        The Sanctum is (nominally) responsible. Their magic relies on intelligence, non-fungible sacrifice, and the cultivation and maintenance of a level of self-control and human comprehension typical of the average neonate to optimally work, so they are further incentivized to avoid the pitfalls of allowing their elders to fall out of touch with the herd. The Condition their blood sorcery instills on an exceptional success (which lay members can also provide with a proprietary Merit directly comparable to Inspiring) makes them better at controlled frenzy. Even if their claim that the Traditions came from them is false, that still demonstrates that they are invested in those laws of the All Night Society. Hypothetical Responsible Acolytes and Dragons do not outweigh the fact that Crúac stunts Humanity and runs on spilled blood and Coil research involves no small amount of unethical humanoid experimentation.

                                        The Sanctum is big. They're typically deliberately playing second fiddle to the Invictus in most domains because it turns out that it's real easy for the covenant with strong incentives to control information and supernatural resources to get in bed with something that calls itself The Conspiracy of Silence. Not for nothing does Hunter 1e's writeup of the Malleus Maleficarum's note on the gaps in secret ghoul Ambrogio Baudolino's pet witch-hunting conspiracy's knowledge of vampire society still boil down to "it's useful to know that the Invictus, the Carthians, the Lancea, and the Brood exist in broad ideological strokes, but the rest is irrelevant." Whether they're fairly or unfairly persecuted by other groups (including the Lancea), the Circle and the Ordo are the smallest of the major covenants and are institutionally characterized as cultish or disinterested in participating in city politics.

                                        The Sanctum is not about having an orgy and saying it's for the greater good, it's about selling a form of vampirism that gives its bearers something to agree on to do that isn't going to step on too many important toes and has an ideological reason for it that they can take some comfort in while they carefully acclimate to losing their heartbeat and reversing their sleep/wake cycle after breaking the wrong taboos too many times. It's about vampirism being a moral punishment for sins whose sting is reduced by participation not because participating makes the sins go away, but because punishment following sin is the world working the way it's supposed to in a world where many things, particularly magical things, do not seem to work the way the world is supposed to.

                                        For people that are not satisfied with "You're just a vampire, nothing else to it" as an explanation for their condition, the Sanctum provides a support structure, a broad directive, and a way to make sense of a chaotic universe. If you have a problem with faith and/or people doing nasty stuff in the name of it, the Sanctum is not going to be for you. If you are interested in playing a character who has to deal with the principle tension of What A Covenant Is in relation to Humanity, the Sanctum is there precisely because of the thing you are describing as hypocrisy — this is a group that is specifically interested in maintaining their self-control and understanding of the humans around them while firmly institutionally dividing themselves from humanity, and that means strong odds on many of them having developed banes to slow their descent (and, like with the Mekhet clan bane, this means having more secrets to safeguard).
