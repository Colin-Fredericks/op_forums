        Under the Solace drug sidebar - "They say the drug makes the dead alive, or close to it, for one night. The body warms. The food has taste, and the wine makes you drunk again. You shed your supernatural powers, and the hunger dies away. For one night." And under Blush of Life. "Normally, a vampire looks the part of a corpse. He’s pale. He registers no pulse. He exists at slightly below room temperature. Food and drink taste terrible, and immediately cause violent, bloody vomiting. However, with a bit of Vitae, he can fake life temporarily. By spending a point of Vitae, Kindred may invoke the blush of life for a scene.... They can keep food and drink down, ejecting it later in the night."

        The implication is that, when fully "dead," food tastes nasty and can't be kept down. When under some mystical effect to act more "alive" you can consume food and drink normally. I think its notable that the Blush of Life section doesn't mention food tasting good, so it could go either way, though "eject" does imply throwing it back up, and the whole "food is unnourishing" theme is rather central to Vampire, so I lean towards it being unappatizing in either case. Its a breaking point for a reason - to highlight that you no longer are sustained by real food, but only blood. So, not only not a Masquerade-ism here, but actually something that's more important to Requiem than Masquerade.

        Also, you don't have to spend vitae to throw up the food, or hold it down. You just spend 1 vitae for the Blush and get the effect for free.

                A lot of the talk about souls in this thread is kind of confused, given that souls in the CofD are not the seat of the self or consciousness. Beasts have their souls devoured and replaced with a "Horror" and are still the original person. In mage, there are mages who steal souls but leave the person alive. They lose drive, motivation, etc. until/unless their soul is replaced (and isn't necessarily their original soul).

                So if the embrace destroys your soul and replaces it with something else, that doesn't really mean "you died and are gone and something else is driving your body like a car." It just means you've changed.

                Anyway, I don't find the question all that interesting as such things tend to suck the drama (and fun) out of Vampire. What's the point of trying to hold onto humanity you never actually really had?

                As a hypothetical discussion amongst kindred, it's fine. As something proposed as the truth of the setting, it's terrible.

                    Originally posted by Aleksay View Post
                    I wondered lately why so many people wuldn't mind to become vampires, considering vampiric weaknesses and banes quite a reasonable price compared to eternal life, and was struck by one simple idea. And this idea was so cool and terrible, that I'd like to share it with you, for maybe in your setting you could put it into mouth of some Dragon scientist or Sanctified preacher (well, may be it already was in some manuals, I didn't read them all).

                    When you are Embraced, you get immortality, right? Wrong. You just die. Forever. You lie still, cold, and your soul -- if you ever had one -- is forever gone.

                    Then your Sire drops some Vitae in your mouth, and this Vitae holds some kind of mystical parasyte, that has no memory, no experience, just self-awareness. And as it fills your body, your brain, it devours your memory, your experience, your personality. It's not that you retain yourself, but get Beast into your soul. But as this Vitae-parasyte has no memories and personality of its own, it believes it being you.

                    But you personally, you are dead, and gone forever. That's why spreading the Curse is not giving immortality. It is a murder.

                    So. Do you still want to be a vampire?


                    Buffy had a similar concept in that vampires were actually demons possessing human corpses. Most of them didn't have souls, until they used witchcraft to restore Angel's human soul, while still leaving him physically as a vampire.


                    Still if you implement this interpretation of vampirism, then it means Belial's Brood is absolutely correct. The vampire is supposed to be the Beast incarnate, they just don't realize it.


                    And also if you use this method, then I'd have to say that no, I wouldn't want to be a vampire. What's the point of becoming a super powered immortal if I don't get to enjoy it? If all I'm doing is handing my body off to something else, then it's not a worthwhile trade.

                                                Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post
                                                A lot of the talk about souls in this thread is kind of confused, given that souls in the CofD are not the seat of the self or consciousness. Beasts have their souls devoured and replaced with a &quot;Horror&quot; and are still the original person. In mage, there are mages who steal souls but leave the person alive. They lose drive, motivation, etc. until/unless their soul is replaced (and isn't necessarily their original soul).
                                                I think a lot of people are larching onto the "replaces your soul" part and ignoring the "you are dead and the thing that animates you is a separate entity that happens to have your memories" part. The latter makes whether or not the soul is the seat of consciousness irrelevant to the discussion. Vampires are, physiologically speaking, dead. The actual seat of consciousness, the brain, no longer functions. If anything, the fact that the soul has nothing to do with consciousness is evidence in support of the theory that the human's consciousness ceases upon embrace and the vampire just doesn't realize it isn't the same person who died.

                                                Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post
                                                So if the embrace destroys your soul and replaces it with something else, that doesn't really mean &quot;you died and are gone and something else is driving your body like a car.&quot; It just means you've changed.
                                                No, it doesn't, but the fact that all of your biological functions have ceased does mean that you have died. If the soul isn't what keeps "you" anchored to the body, what is? If it's the brain, then you absolutely are gone and something is driving your body like a car. Personally, I'm inclined to suspect the personality must reside elsewhere in CofD, neither in the soul nor the brain. I do have a musing, which I'll spoiler here cause it's kind of a big tangent from the topic.

                                                Perhaps there is more to the soul than Mages conceive of. Maybe the part that is made up of the five subtle Arcana and causes the Soulless Condition when removed isn't the seat of consciousness, but it's only one part of the "soul." To the ancient Egyptians, for example, the soul was made up of five parts. The Ib was the seat of thought and emotion, and was contained within a single drop of blood, passed down from the mother, and contained within the organ we call the heart. Sounds a little like Vitae, no? Diablerie does consume the "soul" of the victim, and the Third Tradition does refer to Amaranth as "devouring the heartsood of [another vampire]". Doing so does cause the Diableriest to hear the intrusive thoughts of the victim. I'd say that's pretty strong evidence that the Ib, one crucial drop of Vitae, could be where a vampire's consciousness resides.

                                                Another interesting note from the ancient Egyptian concept of the soul is the duality between Ka and Ba. The Ka was thought to be the vital spark, the part of the soul that made the body alive, sustained by food and drink. When the Ka left the body, the person died, and the funeary rites described in The Book of the Dead were performed to allow the Ba, the personality, to leave the body through the mouth and rejoin the Ka in the afterlife, forming an Akh. An Akh could go forth on its own by day, independent of the body, but at night would return to the body, which served as its connection to the underworld. This is why offerings of food and drink had to be placed consistently in the tomb, to sustain the Ka and make sure the Akh didn't die again, permanently this time. We know from the Mekhet Clanbook that vampires, or at least Mekhet, can be created by interrupting a human's funerary rites, causing a separation between the body (which becomes a Hollow, a vampire with no echo, no reflection, and no shadow), and something else, which the Mekhet call a Ka, (though honestly it seems to be more like the Sheut to me, literally the shadow), which resembles the missing shadow and reflection of the Hollow, and feeds on the flesh of the living. It could be that a vampire is a person who's Ba survives within their body despite the lack of Ka, perhaps sustained by the Ib of their sire. In the case of the Hollow, the Sheut for some reason accompanies the Ka instead of the Ba.

                                                Perhaps most disturbingly of all, the Ba, the thing that might be the aspect of a soul that makes a vampire "you", is typically depicted as a bird with a human head...


                                                Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post
                                                Anyway, I don't find the question all that interesting as such things tend to suck the drama (and fun) out of Vampire. What's the point of trying to hold onto humanity you never actually really had?
                                                I think it is interesting, but in a different way. It's a major thematic shift for Requiem, but examining what really is consciousness can be fascinating. Just because the human who occupied the body you are in died, does that necessarily mean "you" aren't really that person? You have all the same memories, thoughts, emotions, personality. For all intents and purposes, "you" may as well actually be them. Does it really matter that "they" died and were replaced by "you" if "you" are completely indistinguishable from "them"?

                                                Has anyone here played SOMA? Fantastic game that explores these ideas in really cool ways.

                                                Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post
                                                As a hypothetical discussion amongst kindred, it's fine. As something proposed as the truth of the setting, it's terrible.
                                                I don't think I would want to settle on a definitive truth of the setting. I think any theories on the true nature as vampires are best as unprovable hypotheticals for Kindred to discuss. All that said, I think we're largely missing the point of the OP's question by discussing it in these terms. The OP seemed to take as a given that most people would want to become vampires if given the chance, despite the drawbacks, and was proposing this as "would you still want to become a vampire if there was a chance it worked this way?" To that, I say no, but I wouldn't want to become a vampire anyway. When I was in high school I would have jumped on the chance, but nobody said teenagers have good judgment.
