          Lashing Out is basically just posturing. It's not inherently violent, but it is a challenge, and different people will react to a challenge in different ways. The Monsterous Beast is by far the most likely to lead to physical confrontation, since it's the vampire equivalent of taking off your shirt and saying "come at me, bro!" But even that is just a nonviolent display of dominance more often than not. The Seductive Beast is like an agressive come-on. Might get you laid, might get you an eye roll, might get you laughed at and a drink thrown in your face. Probably won't get you physically attacked, but you never know.
          The Competitive Beast is like a "bet I can [do a thing] better than you." It's far more likely to lead to a [do a thing] competition than a physical fight, unless [do a thing] happens to be bareknuckle boxing. But, you know, some people don't take kindly to boasting and some people don't deal well with losing a challenge, especially to a braggart.

                                  On NPCs making your PCs doing something: remember that the Social Maneuvering system is explicitly -not- for use against PCs, so unless the ST really starts pushing social mechanics then Awe can't -force- you to do anything, it just makes someone extremely charming and persuasive. If it gets further up into Majesty where it -is- forcing you to do something, I think it's entirely in line to Lash Out. No fair judge would hold that measure of self defense against you, but then again if it's an elder it might not -be- a fair judge.

                                  If your players still feel it's an issue, then direct them to the Composure attribute, and maybe consider temporarily upping the beats per session so they can grab Composure without feeling like it's taking up all their xp, or give them a cost break, or hell, just give them some free dots if you want. Totally up to you.

                                              Mm. The PC resolutions in GMC are okay, but very much optional. In the meantime, I'm going off the explicit message of "this system is meant for PCs to use on NPCs" found in GMC, VtR2 and WtF2.

                                              I largely agree that roleplaying efforts shouldn't be "ignored", but what that means differs from table to table. It's the old question of "does using the Diplomacy skill make a PC" do something" that's been around since social mechanics were introduced in RPGs, and at my table I very explicitly separate mundane charm and mind control. So while I can try to make a roleplayed offer as compelling as possible, describe the NPC as being smoother and sweeter than ice cream and otherwise argue about it until I am blue in the face, the PCs have the option to say no.

                                              Does that give the players a somewhat meta level of rationality that people in the real world often don't have when accosted by charmers, salesmen and flim flam artists? Well...yes, and I would tell my players that's how I handle social stuff before the game even begins, because it's a big deal to me. Ideally I want players to give up some of their agency but I'm not going to take it from them.

                                              Once you get into Majesty, you're falling into the softer side of straight up mind control, which is another story entirely and, for me, falls in that grey area in Kindred society where you can legitimately argue that it's use is offensive or at the very least aggressive in nature and that measures like lashing out is a matter of self defense.

                                              Now, I am not saying that this whole wall of text is the best way to play, but I am offering it up as food for thought in a thread where OP's PCs are struggling with how Majesty makes them do stuff since I put a lot of thought into it for my own games.
