    Carthian-Ruled Domain

    10-11-2016, 05:50 PM
    So I posted about this in a separate thread, but in the chronicle I'm running soon, the Carthians overthrew the Invictus, after the previous prince's excesses and other issues I still need to hash out.

    The Carthians themselves are somewhat numerous in light of their success, and are primarily divided into three factions (and yes, they're all leftist): Anarchists, communists, and social democrats. These three groups are fairly disparate and have plenty of reasons to distrust and dislike each other. A big problem for the communists is that it's 1976 and the cold war is going strong. Another issue for them is the split between "tankies"* and those who are not.

    The anarchists have issues with establishing an authority - even a Carthian authority - vs. other alternatives. This group is the least homogeneous and most argumentative.

    The social democrats somewhat cynically and definitely ruthlessly got the cooperation from the other factions in staging the coup simply because they represented less of a threat than the Invictus (and exaggerated the threat the Invictus represented, although the prince was downright terrible and many would agree he needed to go).

    I plan the chronicle to cover a few decades (hence starting in 1976, 10 years after the coup itself). At first, the Invictus will represent an external threat to the Invictus that guarantees some level of cooperation. As time passes, however, this spirit of cooperation is likely to fade, and the social democrats will throw their rivals to the wolves should matters come to that.

    The Invictus are relatively weak at first, and at least give lip service to the notion of fealty to the Carthian prince. In the Invictus' eyes, the biggest thing the Carthians had going for them was that the Invictus was ready to depose the prince as well - and said prince was responsible for weakening the Invictus in the first place.

    So there's going to be a lot of tension between the Invictus and the Carthians, and among the Carthian factions themselves.

    I'm still debating how I want to do the whole thing, but the overall city government will be the prince, a technically democratically appointed council (not sure how big - I had 10 members before but six or probably eight seems reasonable). The reality is that the Carthians will have the most seats, while other seats are appointed from within the other five covenants (the extra being House Tremere, who threw in with the Carthians when it looked like the Invictus were going to lose and used that to leverage themselves more power in the domain).

    Looking at 1e the positions are:
    • Prince
    • Seneschal
    • Herald
    • Primogen
    • Priscus
    • Whip
    • Harpy
    • Sheriff
    • Hounds
    • Master of Elysium

    Not going to fill those roles precisely as described and it looks like a lot of it was dropped in 2e. Just making a note for later.

    * A hardline Stalinist. A tankie is a member of a communist group or a "fellow traveller" (sympathiser) who believes fully in the political system of the Soviet Union and defends/defended the actions of the Soviet Union and other accredited states (China, Serbia, etc.) to the hilt, even in cases where other communists criticise their policies or actions. For instance, such a person favours overseas interventions by Soviet-style states, defends these regimes when they engage in human rights violations, and wishes to establish a similar system in other countries such as Britain and America. (courtesy of Urban Dictionary)

      Something to think about: what is the Carthian council responsible for? Distribution of limited resources? What do they have authority over? Havens, feeding rights? Do they seek to influence mortal politics as well, or is this a "socialism in one domain" situation?

      There's a couple of potential breaking points here depending on how much you want to draw on real-world leftist politics. As you say, one threat would be the social democrat faction throwing their rivals to the wolves- EG, European social democrat parties endorsing WW1. An alternative risk could be the communist faction tacitly supporting an Invictus takeover (or some really wild outsider faction like the Crone or the Tremere) to punish the social democrat faction, hoping that the purge of their rivals and the renewed tyranny would inspire a true revolution where the hardliners can take power. And/or, either faction could turn on the anarchist faction and expend their resources trying to control their nominal allies.

      What does anarchy look like in a vampire domain? Is there just no Prince or ruling council, and organization starts with voluntary coteries and grows upward? Do they care about the Masquerade?

          I'm still working on what the anarchists' ideal domain would look like, but it'll be very far from the reality.

          The Council is basically the primogen council. They serve as advisors to the prince, have their own power bases, are all elders or ancillae themselves. In theory, the council members are appointed from within the ranks of whichever covenant they're from. In practice, the council has considerable say over who gets to stay on the council. In practice, the most powerful and influential kindred end up there. They have a lot of say in policy, who gets assigned subdomains, etc. but the prince is the final word.

          Many kindred have influence over mortal agencies - whether political, corporate, etc. This isn't something that's necessarily specific to anyone with a title, although those with titles can use their status and personal power to encourage others to use resources on their behalf.

          The Lancea et Sanctum had some losses of their own when the Invictus was deposed. They're definitely not happy about that, esp. with the Circle's fortunes improving at the Lance's expense (or at least that's how they view it).

          The Ordo Dracul is mostly investigating splinters, and aren't heavily invested in the local politics. Basically as long as they're left alone to do their thing (and no one really knows what their thing is right now) they'll be happy.

              I think the one threat of revolution is filling that vacuum. Everyone has different perceptions of what the ideal government is and how they fit within it. If you look at revolutions, the aftermath usually follows one of two models. Either a strongman seizes charge and establishes himself as a dictator by using the necessity of martial law a pretext, or factionalism creates a violent and rapidly shifting political structure in which no one individual or party manages to stay in power for very long.

              Obviously the Carthians, at least early on, will have to maintain some sense of solidarity, since the other covenants are real and present threats, but I wouldn't be surprised if political positions change hands quick and often over the next few decades. I'd imagine it's only after the revolution that things really start to get ruthless.

                      I would note that the listed positions and titles are very much an Invictus structure. I know the 1e core listed them as the default and suggested alternate titles that the other Covenants might use basically the same positions, but Requiem has gradually moved away from such a standardized structure, to the point where 2e didn't even list those titles.

                      Now, that's not to say you shouldn't roll with them. It could be that the social democrat Carthians adopted the existing political structure, perhaps as a way to make the transition less jarring for the citizens, or to assuage the Invictus who might otherwise have opposed them more vehemently. But it's undoubtedly going to be a point of internal contention. I'm sure many Carthians, especially the anarchist faction, would likely see a structure that mirrors Invictus roles as more of the same with different assholes in charge.

                      In fact, this is something I have seen come up in and out of character in Requiem LARP all the time. A city is predominantly Carthian, but uses the "traditional" structure, maybe with some small tweaks to who is responsible for what and some titles swapped out, and everyone whispers about how they're not real Carthians, they're just Invictus Lite. In character this usually takes the form of plots to overthrow the current prince or prime minister or president or whatever they call him, more often than not by other Carthians. Out of character it takes the form of people gossiping about how so-and-so doesn't know how to play a Carthian, and how they probably just wanted to play an Invictus but have access to Carthian Law and their proprietary Devotions, or debating about what a well-roleplayed Carthian domain should look like.

                                                  Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post

                                                  That definitely gives me something to think about.

                                                  Off the top of my head, I'd probably put together something like this.

                                                  You have a Prince who's the nominal representative of the Carthian Movement. He could be either a paper tiger assigned to placate the feuding Carthian factions or a strong man who uses intimidation and purges to maintain his power.

                                                  A central committee of eight members is in charge of assigning ministers. You have the Prince, a representative from each of the Carthian factions, and one from each of the other covenants. Probably one or two of those leaders are toadies for the Carthians. Nominally, these are democratically elected positions, but it's likely each covenant has their own standards for what "democratic" means.

                                                  As for ministers, I'd imagine secretary of the interior would be the coveted position. She's more or less the Sheriff of the city, but she holds a lot of power. Bureau chiefs beneath her are in charge of dealing with inter-covenant and inter-vampire struggles, keeping an eye on threats from hunters and other supernaturals, and maintaining a ruthless secret police force in place. You probably have a labor minister in charge of a vast department that maintains pawns in the various mortal unions and city hall and a cultural minister promoting the mortal arts and governing the rules and status of elysium. Transportation minister would closely screen who enters and leaves the cities and keeps the public transit system free from internecine squabbles and safe for kindred to travel on.

                                                  Given the cronyism at play in Soviet-era politics and the small population of kindred in any particular city, I'd assume that anyone without some sort of position in one of the bureaus is being singled out as a pariah. I could see a bunch of departments being created for the express purpose of placating other vampires. Maybe the Ordo Dracul has been calmed by being given control of a ministry of research, more or less offered their own expansive domain where they can do whatever they want as long as they support Carthian rule.
