                      I am going to have to agree with Michael's statement. The entire post that links to - and indeed, the complimentary posts which follow - all seem to stem from a failure of imagination. I'm going to go ahead and give this some advice, and hope that it helps without missing the mark of what it is you as an ST are trying to go for. With that said:

                      Okay - so storytelling for a character with Dominate, that will work if that character also has friends: Expect them to get their way - they are going to be the kind of character who when dealt with directly is used to winning - to being heard and obeyed. If they are going against mortals, they will be superior when it comes to social constructs. Don't tweak Dominate at all - Dominate is useful, Dominate is intoxicating, Dominate is dangerous.

                      Instead, tweak how an antagonist is going to approach an individual with Dominate. Are you dealing with a group of hunters? They are likely trained with the knowledge that this is part of your capacity. Maybe they have tricks and tools to counter it. Imagine a member of Task Force Valkyrie being Dominated to go off the rails. Their friend is on a computer back home, and in response to signs that they have been compromised hits a key that inflicts a point of lethal burning damage to clear their heads. This informs the Dominated Hunter, breaking their mesmerism. Another hunter group might allow it to happen and just... watch. Studying you and what you do, how you do it. Learning more about you.

                      Are you dealing with a group of people or even a single person who saw too much? I imagine at the point that that becomes a threat, the individual or group has already inflicted damage - enough to let you know that there is a breach of the Masquerade. Dominating that individual will solve the problem of -them- knowing too much, but how do you recognize how big the damage to the Masquerade is when your target might not even know? A video posted on youtube, a word to an unknown friend in an all-too-public bar about the crazy shit they saw, or even the individuals making a scene when they see you - can you really draw up dominate faster than a person can start to panic?

                      Are you dealing with other vampires? Old or new, these vampires are going to act in ways to prepare for you and your group. They probably have dominate, and if they don't - they have blood, and betraying your thrall is a breaking point. They probably aren't even competing against you directly - you start dominating to get the funds of the head of the CEO of some business - only to find they've beaten you to it. That's the benign, weak option too. I want you to imagine the shock your players feel, when they go to Dominate someone only to immediately learn when they have a bullet in their head that that individual is set to trigger when he's dominated by someone other than the person who came before you.

                      Now - my solutions here can easily be confused with counters. In a way, they are - but keep in mind they aren't supposed to be counters that the storyteller utilizes against the players, but counters that the NPCs would utilize against the PCs. In order for a counter to exist, there has to be evidence of an action taking place - so allow the PCs a little bit of enjoyment in the sheer power of using someone and then tossing them aside like a rag doll. Run stories meant for them to use these power to the fullest of their extent, and win with these powers.

                      Then enforce consequence, to expand their retinue and cause them to adapt to new situations.

                      In regards to the powers of Dominate being used on the player, there are a number of solutions to that as well. Let's take the post there - a person goes into a club, and the bouncer is a ghoul or vampire who has Dominate. The bouncer recognizes the lone individual, and dominates them.

                      Set the player to the side. That person is currently witnessing the game, not playing in it - at least for now. If they come up with creative ways in which their character might exit said dominate (such as stabbing oneself in the leg, which the ST in question there seemed against) listen to it, but barring that don't make them play through being controlled. Instead, describe the actions of the player and the bouncer to the other players who see that person being Dominated. It is the ST's responsibility to make things seem odd, or strange, if they are. It is the ST's perogative to decide if he -wants- things to seem odd or strange, or if he wants this scene to leave an impact on all of the characters. This is now an opportunity for the ST to control the flow of the story.

                      If the STs goal is to make the conversation seem weird, then do so. Have the character make casual movements - maybe he even points at the players, like he's selling them out. If the players are familiar with them, mention little things that he's not doing that he usually would. Paint a picture and make it seem surreal or alien, and let them latch onto that and feel comfortable doing so. And if all else fails and they miss all your hints...

                      ... Or your goal is to leave an impact, gloss over the scene. Inform the player of the Dominated character what they remember about the scene. If they remember it fully, use that to drive a wedge of fear into the players character. If they know about the capabilities of dominate, hint at certain things like 'triggers' that seem to keep popping up. And if they don't remember it fully or correctly, emphasize the elements where their allies know something happened - but the character doesn't.

                      I don't know about you, but I feel that's both horrifying as a character, and provocative as a reader or witness.
