    Changing a few merits into positive conditions

    12-02-2016, 01:59 PM
    Namely:
    - Retainer
    - Contact
    - Allies
    - Resource
    - Mentor
    - Status

    It occurred to me that the way i was using those merits fell more in line with conditions than the way they are written. By that i mean that if the player do something that would logically earn them the merit I don't bother with the exp spending. Also i've not been following the mechanics of these merits too closely, a player tells me what they want to accomplish and i judge if its feasible with the merits they have.
    I also really like the ... flowing nature of how conditions work, the way you gain and lose them.

    I'm thinking of awarding the condition following appropriate story circumstances (same as any other condition). I'd also add something i call 'strings attached' which is what the players need to do in order to keep the condition.
    You earn beats for gaining or losing the condition.

    I'd also like to add something in the vein of scales. Perhaps even giving beats when that happens.

    So far i think i'll keep the merits and using the conditions for temporary gains.

    What do you guys think?
    Is it feasible?

    Completed campaign: Scion 2nd Edition. Les Légendes Currently playing: Being a dad for a 3 year old daughter and a 2 years old son and now a beautiful new baby.

      I personally use the "keep the Merits and use Conditions to supplement," method. A character can have Resources to represent a reliable source of extra cash, or win the Lottery and get the Windfall Condition that they can resolve by spending a big sum on something.

      I think one of the big problems with moving completely over to Conditions is that (1) you pretty much have to have Persistent versions of some of them to make sense which just turns these things into Beat machines on top of the benefits they provide, and (2) you remove the Sanctity of Merits aspect of them.

      Now, for your group, those might not really matter, but in general it makes a big difference.

              This seems pretty common for folks who are new to 2e. My first session running 2e, a player got the Spooked Condition (CofD core book), read it, gave me a quizzical look, and asked "is there anything stopping me from just not Resolving this?" I told her no, but if she didn't Resolve it she wouldn't get a Beat from it. The whole table gave an "Ooooooh" and from then on we never had any problems with reluctance to resolve Conditions or take dramatic failures. But I guess Iucked out, a lot of groups take more time to get used to the idea of accepting IC drawbacks in exchange for OOC rewards. And some folks just plain don't like that play style. Are you using Group Beats? I've heard a lot of STs have found that more successful, as it creates a perception that accepting negative effects from Conditions benefits the whole group, and a lot of players are more willing to take a hit for the team than to trade personal drawbacks for personal rewards.

                      Originally posted by Maitrecorbo View Post
                      Tho i didnt know there was a Windfall condition. Its pretty much what i was looking for.
                      Where is it written? I have looked in the vampire corebook and its not there.
                      Sorry, as guess, it's a personal custom Condition.

                      I use it for things that are more abstract/complicated than just a pile of cash (in the form of Cash or Contraband equipment).

                      The basic way I have it function it that the character can make a purchase as if they have Resources (or an appropriate other Merit) + 2, max. 5 for a single purchase of equipment or services that does not count towards the usual limitation of purchases with Merits.

                      The Condition Resolves when that purchase is made, or the character's rating in a Merit that represents an economic asset (usual Resources, but also Merits like Safe Place) is increased with XP.

                      The second half basically lets you use it as a discount on increasing those sorts of Merits if you want to "invest" instead of making a big purchase.

                      Would there be something similar for the other merits?
                      There are a few things already in the books. The Connected and Informed Conditions (CofD 2e core book) work well for a lot of the organization focused Merits you listed in your first post.

                      A number of Conditions that could be put on NPCs could be used as well, but don't really seem necessary between Merits, and things like services. You don't need Retainer or a custom Condition to hire a limo driver for the night.

                      As for the beats, i'd actually like for my players to have another way to earn them. They are new to the system and they're not earning as much beat per game as i expected (2 at the most each game). My guess is its still a bit counter intuitive for them to put themselves in negative circumstances to earn exp. So far none of them have changed a failure to a dramatic failure and they are very cautious about not being in situation that puts their humanity at risk. Also its my first 2nd ed game and I don't know the conditions sufficiently yet i think.
                      Keep in mind that more Conditions doesn't really equate to more Beats. Cashing in a bunch of them at once isn't as good as spread them out more.

                      To an extent, you've basically got to wait for something like what happened for Charlaquin when it finally clicks. Though something you can do to try to instigate this sooner rather than later are Conditions NPCs can put on the characters via Lashing Out, Disciplines, and even if you're worried about them feeling "attacked" have a friendly NPC use the Inspiring Merit on them.

                      It's important to remember that while Beats mostly come from negative situations, a lot of Beats also come from positive things. It's not "have something bad happen to your character, take a Beat," it's, "have something interesting happen to your character, take a Beat." Remembering to look at it from the perspective of having a given Beat opportunity stemming from something interesting makes it a lot easier to spurn player engagement with it.
