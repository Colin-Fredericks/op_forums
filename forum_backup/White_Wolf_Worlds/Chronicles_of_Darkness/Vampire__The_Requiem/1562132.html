    Hunger System Homebrew - Feedback Welcome

    07-24-2026, 04:45 PM
    I’ve been playing Requiem with Hunger dice rules for quite a while now.
    The basic idea was very similar to v5, with some minor tweaks :
    • If you have a 1 on a hunger die and no successes, you’d have a bestial failure, or if you rolled a 10 on a hunger dice that exploded into another success, you’d get a messy success.
    I’ve enjoyed it for a few reasons:
    • It gives a visual representation of hunger.
    • It makes the beast’s urges feel a lot more present.
    • Disciplines and blood surge now involve a risk rather the calculated decisions that the vitae pools grant.
    The system isn’t without ‘’jank’’, however.

    Indeed, I think the frequent penalty that requiem imposes on tests makes it a bit awkward when hunger is high. Indeed, a character with a decent skill pool has between 4 and 6 dice. As soon as I applied a penalty, it became a bit awkward, as most dice ended up being hunger dice, so any exceptional success carried the taint of the beast. It didn't feel good.

    I also feel like bestial failures were a bit too frequent to my taste.

    I decided to refine the system into a single dice. I called the ‘’Bestial dice’’, which I’ll have to test in my next session.

    My idea was to reduce the number of hunger dice to a single die handling the bestial urge, while still keeping the hunger and rouse check system.
    Bestial Die
    When a vampire makes a test, treat one die from their pool as their bestial dice; it functions as a regular dice, except for the extra effects detailed below. If their pool is only one dice, roll the bestial dice apart from the last dice. Ignore any success it might give.

    When a character rolls a 1 on their bestial dice. Roll a d10. If they roll a number that’s lower than twice their current hunger, their test becomes bestial.

    Bestial Result : The beast has wants that rarely align with a vampire’s goal and the hungrier they feel, the louder it screams from the back of their mind. A bestial result means that the character has let their beast’s influence take its hold.

    This is usually represented in one of the following ways :
    • They might gain any of the bestial triad conditions (Bestial, Competitive or Wanton) for the rest of the scene. Based on what they were trying to achieve.
    • They might gain any other conditions, such as obsessed, swooning, etc. They also last for a scene.
    • They make a flat rouse check if their hunger increases.
    • They might need to make a frenzy test if they are already hungry, or in a stressful scene.
    • Or their success might be influenced by the beast : like successfully picklocking a door by ripping the handle off, persuading someone by relying on intimidation instead of careful arguments, etc. In the case of an empathy test, the beast might focus on ways to exploit the target instead of their emotional state.
    • A failure might provoke a frenzy.
    A player is also free to come up with ideas.

    Satisfying the Beast
    If the character acts in accordance with the beast’s want, they can regain a point of willpower, once per scene.

    For example, if the beast wants to frenzy, a character regains a point of willpower by opting to give in. If the beasts inflicts the Competitive condition, the character regains willpower if they assert their dominance, etc.
    If the beasts need are unclear, such as turning a success messy. The storyteller can award willpower for good roleplay.

    Ghouls and Bestial Dice
    Ghouls also rolls a bestial dice. On a roll of 1, instead of rolling against their hunger, if their d10 result is lower than their resolve + composure, they suffer a complication. Usually manifesting in conditions or behaviour that represents their vitae addicted nature.
    Hunger
    Hunger replaces vitae reserve. It has a score from 0 to 5. Feeding cannot reduce hunger below 1 unless it’s from vitae, or from killing a human.

    At hunger 4, a kindred is considered hungry (-2) and at hunger 5 they are considered starving (-4).
    Unless mentioned otherwise, if hunger would increase past 5, the kindred suffers a point of lethal damage instead.

    Rouse Check
    A rouse check is a single d10 roll. On a roll of 5 or lower, the kindred’s hunger increases by 1. A vampire can’t voluntarily trigger a rouse check at hunger 5.

    Vitae Cost to Rouse Check conversion
    Vitae Cost Rouse Check
    1 1
    2 Roll an extra die, keep the worst.
    3+ Half vitae cost, rounded up
    Feeding
    Satiating one point of hunger requires inflicting 1 point of lethal damage per vitae potency, with a feeding move, which inflicts 1 lethal per turn.

    Waking up
    To wake up, a vampire needs to make a rouse check. If they fail that check while at hunger 5, they fall into torpor.

    Stealing & Sharing Vitae
    When feeding on another kindred, the vampire that’s feeding need to spend 1 turn feeding per their vitae potency score to reduce their hunger by 1. Each hunger reduced that way count as 1 vitae for the purpose of blood addiction & Blood Bound.

    The vampire that’s being drained increase their hunger by 1 after a number of turns equal to their vitae potency, if it’s lower than their vitae potency, they simply need to make a rouse check. If a vampire is already at hunger 5, feeding on them inflicts them with 1 lethal per turn spent feeding, which does not satiate hunger. Which can lead to torpor, or diablerie if the vampire keeps on sucking. (See diablerie VTR: 101)

    Ghouls
    Can store a number of rouses check worth of vitae equal to half their stamina, rounded up. Each month they need to make a rouse check to maintain their ghoul status. They cannot make a rouse check if they have no more vitae stored.

    Blood surge
    You canmake a rouse check once per turn, you gain Blood Surge value as extra dice on all physical action for a turn. At BP 3 or higher, you can use the lesser number to make two rouse check instead of once, only increasing your hunger if both fails.

    Discipline Reduction
    With high blood potency, reduce the number of rouse check needed per discipline. If this would reduce the number of rouse check below 1, roll 2 dice instead of one, if one succeeds hunger does not increase.

    Vitae Potency.
    Vitae potency represent how must vitae and kindred can store and how much blood they need to take before feeding slated.

    It also represents how many nights they can wake up per single rouse check; this does not mean they stay awake during the day, simply that it doesn’t tax their reserve enough to be relevant on a single night.

    When feeding on mortal a vampire needs to inflict a number of lethal damage equal to their vitae potency before reducing their hunger by 1.

    Damage Mended
    A vampire can mend damage by making a rouse check. They can heal a number of bashing equal to the first number and a number of lethal damage equal to the second number.

    Healing a point of aggravated damage takes a full day of sleep each 5-point worth of lethal healing heals 1 aggravated damage.

    Feeding Restriction.
    Feeding restriction makes a kindred unable to reduce their hunger bellow a certain threshold. Cold Blood, Animal blood can only satiate the beast to a certain degree.

    A character can ignore their feeding restriction, but this cost 1 point of willpower per hunger reduced below their restriction. Hunger cannot be reduced to 0 this way, unless a human victim is also killed.
    Some answer, to some question I anticipate receiving

    Why does sunlight damage scales differently than the book?
    simply felt like having a linear progression. Additionally, since BP 2 in my rework becomes a lot better by increasing the amount of rouse checks they can take per turn to 2, I wanted BP 1 characters to have a little edge in that aspect.

    Why did you change feeding restriction?
    I made it a bit more linear. It’s a bit more lenient than the base book also, since Willpower is spent per hunger reduced instead of per vitae taken. For two reasons:
    • Willpower tends not to regenerate very quickly in my games.
    • Elders already have so many ways to avoid blood bonds I didn’t feel like lowering the restrictions a bit actually changed anything meaningful. Worse case scenario, an elder with no kindred connection can feed on humans by spending nearly all their willpower, it ain't free.
    Does it make disciplines more expensive to use?

    Yes, in a way, and no in others.

    Since a rouse check has a 50% chance of increasing hunger, but a vampire’s hunger threshold is a lot less than vitae maximum, I estimate that 1 vitae is approximately worth 1 rouse check. Starting at hunger 1 is the equivalent of starting at roughly 8 vitae, so just short of 10, which is the maximum of vitae for BP 1 character. I went with V5’s way of making it harder to reach hunger 0; I want it to have cost or a risk. So, in that aspect, it’s a bit more expensive to use disciplines, since your reserve is harder to fill compared to the base game. Once a character gets some discipline cost reduction from High BP, it starts to matter a bit less.

    In my experience what it truly does, is that player thinks a bit more before using their discipline, because it involves risks. For each fail hunger check increase their chance of rolling a bestial failure or success.

    What’s are chance to roll a Bestial success at any given hunger?

    At hunger 1, the chance is 1%, 3% at hunger 2, 5% at hunger 3, 7% at hunger 4, and 9% at hunger 5.

    Under the previous system with hunger dice, the chance to have a bestial failure would be a bit too weird for my math abilities, but it definitely felt too high for my taste. As for bestial success the chance was 3% at hunger 1, 6% at hunger 2, 9% at hunger 3, 12% at hunger 4 and 15% at hunger five.

    Feel free to ask if you have any questions!

    Feedback is also welcome, as long as it's constructive!

      What do you want Hunger to represent?

      The first issue I see here is a lot of choice effects without much clarity on what they're trying to build.

      Requiem is not Masquerade, much less V5. V5 Conditions and effects for Messy Criticals are a reflection of a concept of the Beast as moral degeneracy. In V5 it's not only a hungry thing, but a combination of malice and vice that induces the Vampire towards depravity.

      Legacy Masquerade has an amoral Beast. It is not interested in concepts of clan, power or vice. It's a violent animal, but ultimately moved by basic needs. Beneath the skin, all Beasts are equal, and it doesn't provoke the vampire into evil as much as represents loss of Ego: losing Humanity isn't about evil, but about losing your grasp on your own identity, slowly reduced to a mindless automaton.

      In Requiem the Beast is an alien intelligence that may or may not be linked to the Strix. It is driven by hunger, but it's ultimately Other. It is fed by the loss of grounding in this reality and seeks a new life where the concerns of mortals and Vampires alike don't have meaning.

      With all differences and nuance, the three games remain consistent in their vision of the Beast, with all mechanics feeding their main concept, be them restrictions, Conditions or what a Wight is.

          My goal is to make Vitae management a riskier endeavor than typical "Blood Points."

          I want the players to feel the weight of their decision when they call on their special points; calling on the Beast's power is always going to be risky.

          Having playtested Hunger in V5 and my previous version of Hunger Dice, it works for our group. I tested it both as a Storyteller and as a player.
          As for the nature of the Beast in Requiem, nothing I have read from the core book seems to suggest to me that it's an alien intelligence. I interpret it more as raw impulse. I went and reread the information on the Beast, but maybe I missed something from the rest of the book, or in some other source somewhere. Anyhow, that's how it manifests in my chronicles.

          I don't attribute the Beast's wants to moral degeneracy, simply to those of a violent loner predator that wants to feed, dominate, and continue its existence. The Beast is short-sighted and can't plan for the long game, but it's insidious, and it feeds on certain feelings: Alienation, Anger, Arrogance, Callousness, Despair, Grief, and Isolation. It's definitely something other, but I see it as good enough to sometimes hide itself in the vampire's intention. At least, that's how I interpret it.

          Hunger is supposed to represent the increased danger of letting the Beast seep in. The hungrier it is, the louder it is. Or the hungrier you are, the more susceptible you are to its influence.

          The Bestial Dice determine if the Beast gets to influence you in a significant action. The conditions are supposed to represent the Beast's bestial urge in that moment.
          Examples of bestial results:
          • On a successful Wits + Empathy test, you realize that your rival is lying to you. The Beast wants you to put them in their place; you gain the Competitive Condition for the scene.
          • On a successful Presence + Persuasion test, you successfully seduce your mark. The Beast's influence seeps in; it doesn't only want you to bring home one mark, it wants all of them. You gain the Wanton Condition.
          • On a successful Manipulation + Intimidation test, you successfully frighten your target into leaving you alone. The Beast likes that; you gain the Obsessed Condition as your Beast wants you to repeat the exploit again and again.
          • On a successful Strength + Athletics test to climb a nearby wall and spy on the rooftop, you jump 6 meters high as your Beast pushes you to your maximum thanks to your Vigor. Someone from across the street definitely noticed; hopefully no one will believe them.
          • On a failed Intelligence + Academics test, as you try to recall some important obscure information about the law relevant to the murder case you're trying to solve, your hungry Beast is assailing you with images of bloody murder; make a Rouse Check, as all you can think of in this instant is blood.
          • On a failed Dexterity + Larceny test, you fail to disarm the alarm system and it goes off. Panic sets in. The Beast smells your panic; roll for fear frenzy.

          Maybe a caveat I should have added: I sometimes treat some rolls as "trivial," the classic "roll to see if you notice X detail." Those rolls are not subject to the Beast's influence. If the Beast's influence would have absolutely no dramatic effect, I'll consider the roll trivial.

          A vampire can also ignore what their Beast wants, to an extent, as getting a condition from a bestial test does not imply that they have to follow their urge.
          For example, in the last game I took part in, my character became Competitive as I realized on a Wits + Empathy test that someone was hiding something from me, and the Beast was annoyed by it. My character was aware that it was her Beast's urge and not her own, so she tried to channel those competitive feelings into something more productive. Since she was partying on a baseball field with a bunch of other people, she took part in a ball-strike contest, spent some Willpower, and thankfully managed to win, proving to everyone she was the best around. The Beast was partly sated.

          Finally one thing to keep in mind, is that i didn't want to have to rewrite the whole book. I like homebrewing, but i also like to play, lol.

          When i designed the hunger system, i wanted it to be usable with the base game. As it stand it does in our gaming circle. The idea is that you can pick a discipline from the book and know how many rouse check you need to do simply by reading the amount of vitae necessary.

              My first suggestion, then, is to focus more on general usage when asking for feedback. I see a recurring friction in your threads where you propose a rule, but you're not interested in hearing what people have to say on their use in situations that don't pertain to your group.

              That won't do. I can discuss the rule within the parameters and game goals you set, not within the specific culture and homebrews of your group. Either you engage broader conversation or you get more succinct in your questions.

              Your 1 Hunger Dice only take seems good. I can see issues with it requiring the extra roll, but nothing too worrying for now. Another approach may be to move the Hunger score from modeling the likelihood of interference to the intensity or duration of interference. That way no second roll is needed, and Hunger has a more nuanced effect where its value matter during the Condition.

              For the possible results, I'd remove the Rouse Check and revise how it may influence the roll. The Beast is hungry, not wasteful, rolling a Rouse Check for no benefit doesn't look at all as something it would do. You may instead homebrew a Hungry Condition where it pushes the Kindred to feed with abandon, instead.

              Vitae cost of 2 I'd convert to no roll rather than two dice. More mathematically sound and less complexity. Otherwise just do as V5 and calls more Rouse Checks with individual resolution.

              I don't like the rules that force more damage or killing the vessel to benefit from feeding. I don't like them in V5, I don't like them here, I think it's not an interesting constraint when it forbids you from building logistics and strategy just to say you have to be a killer to feed. I think doing a similar approach to the total slaking of the night is an acceptable compromise, forcing to cause X damage to a single victim is not.

              On to my point on goals: I think you may have misunderstood my description. I'm not saying VtR Beast is alien as in inscrutable, but as in a more overtly extraneous influence. A character experience the Beast as a thing that's clearly distinct from their Self, as intrusive sensation rather than a change in personality.

              And this directly affects what and how those Conditions and roll influences should work. I see a take that's still very similar to V5, but V5 does the moral degeneracy thing: the Beast is affecting complex emotions and thoughts as it is driving the Vampire to change personality into a worse version of themselves.

              With this in mind, I'd really avoid manifestating the Beast within intentions, at least in a more complex way. With Conditions, I'd consider the character getting more direct versions that feel like this extraneous influence, or at least as an impatient, crude goal. A vampire sometimes do think of those feelings as just their desires going intense, but ultimately it's recognizable as other, as dissociated from what they want.

              I'd similarly avoid the current take on influence roll to make for a more additive thing: instead of the Beast changing how the character goes with the roll, it adds a secondary if minor action that's only contextually related: a target notices the hungry eyes, the lock is picked but the tools are broken, an Aura flares without intention. I'd move from corrupting the action itself (which just succeeded or not) to adding anything else that shows the other, internal Beast coming to the fore opportunistically.

                                                      Blood powered superhero was a simplistic throwaway line; it wasn’t intended as an insult. Hence why I said ‘’lean in’’ and specifically mentioned that there was nothing wrong with that play style.

                                                      That being said, I realize how it can be taken as offensive, has it has often been used to demean Vampire players certain playstyle over another. I apologize if it was offensive. It wasn’t meant to ridicule or diminish your idea.

                                                      What I meant was that a limited resource expenditure will inevitably lead to power being used more frequently and it could lead to some unintended consequences for characters as soon as BP 4 using the system I made.

                                                      However, it’s true that it could be used for very high-power elders to great effect.

                                                      In the end, though, I don’t think there’s any gameplay incentive that will prove one to be superior to the other; it all comes down to the style of campaign you want to play.
                                                      For the ventrue using dominate, ‘‘All the time’’ was a slight exaggeration, but let’s say they had done so every time it was advantageous to do so.

                                                      In the end the player did end up paying for his abuse when his poorly worded entombed command created a big masquerade breach. He ended up discarding the character when he got tired of dealing with the consequences of his character’s actions; for such player a direct mechanical deterrent is generally a better way of restricting power abuse.

                                                      Creating limitations through narrative consequences is interesting, but it’s extra work for the storyteller and it might not be applied evenly between the players.

                                                      I personally prefer when there’s a clear resources expenditure/risk involved with using a power.

                                                      Anyway, my point wasn’t that Requiem specifically isn’t equipped to deal with it, it’s just that magic in general create extra work for the storyteller and cut some angle where you can challenge players. Now that isn’t necessarily limited to magic, but I’ve found magic to be the most prone to generate headaches. Hence the love & hate relationship. Anyway, it wasn’t my main point, only an RPG tangent in general.
                                                      Continuing the tangent

                                                      Let's take an actual recent example to illustrate my love & hate feeling with magic.

                                                      My player is currently in the middle of a semi-abandoned Cheiron Lab Complex with SCP-foundation vibe mixed with Umbrella Corps and a side dish of Stanley Parable/Portal with an evil Ai/Artificial Spirit thing i homebrewed. It was essentially running a dungeon with Vampire the Requiem rules, not optimal, but it made it functional. However, because discipline is as powerful as they are I had to make sure that :
                                                      • Walls were solid enough to avoid Vigor to punch down any wall.
                                                      • Not all monsters were susceptible to Mental Discipline or Obfuscate.
                                                      • Players couldn’t escape by any means before completing their objectives.
                                                      All the while Making sure to reward disciplines usage in the opportune moment to acknowledge that my players did indeed invest experience point in those powers and it should be relevant, because well it sucks to your powers invalidated every time you want to use it.

                                                      The Hermes System (the Artificial Spirit) came through later in the creation process where i was ''how can i make sure the lab is secure against spiritual intrusion?'' Then came the idea of a Artificial Spirit super computer with access to spiritual wards, elevator control and a quirky personality. This is actually also why i like supernatural stuff, where the extra considerations i had to take when designing the ''dungeon'' ended up sparkling creativity. The AI was actually the thing i ended up enjoying the most, as it essentially allowed me to act as an in world narrator; super fun.

                                                      Why go through all that trouble one might one ask?

                                                      I spend a little over 30 hours, no exaggeration, designing new monsters, physical prompts like the 75 pages of files materials players could read that include references to current NPCS their life, other they hate, secret information on how to beat the spirit, design the map. Paint new miniatures, etc.

                                                      So, I wasn’t ready to have a moment a la ‘’oh fuck I didn’t plan my player with Vigor 3 to punch down a steel door with Durability 3. ‘’ or ‘’Oh fuck, I didn’t plan my Nosferatu player could skip the whole Super deadly encounter with the Giant Flesh Ooze Monster thing designed to make the fighter feel badass by using Nightmare 4. ‘’

                                                      In the end, the players and I had a lot of fun. They really liked all the physical props. I'm really glad it worked, I would have been devastated to have gone through all that work to have the player sidestep 3 hours of content simply because they were able to burst down a door that had access to the final area immediately.

                                                      It has happened to me in the past and I always take it gracefully, but it sucks so much.
