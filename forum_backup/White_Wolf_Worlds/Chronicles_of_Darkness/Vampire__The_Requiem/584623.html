    Touchstone-based Clan Banes? Feedback wanted.

    07-09-2015, 09:36 AM
    I've been mulling over the Clan Banes for some time, now. Reading through several threads that touch that topic got me thinking about what I want from a Clan Bane and what a cool common underlying theme or mood might be.

    I came to the following conclusion:
    1. A Clan Bane should symbolize a certain difficulty that members of the Clan share (no surprise here )
    2. It should leave a character with two options: give into it and cope with the consequences or struggle against it
    3. The latter option (and possibly the first one, too) should result in role playing opportunities: the character changes his/her behaviour to struggle against/cope with it.
    4. The underlying theme should correspond to what my players and I want it to be in our game. For us, this should be the struggle to uphold Humanity.


    Touchstones

    Ironically, the Ventrue Bane, while being widely regarded as a failure, fits
    this (almost) perfectly. With a few tweaks to the touchstone rules, it was fine.
    Then I tried to draft touchstone-based clan banes for the other clans, as well.

    First, the tweaked touchstone rules:
    1. Slots for touchstones go from Humanity 6 to 1 and are filled from the top.
    2. Touchstones have two properties that can be true or false: "Active" and "Attached".
      1. To be active means the touchstone currently fills a slot. To be active, the touchstone must be available (e.g. not dead as part of a story) and maintained by the character. Maintaining a touchstone requires regular meaningful interaction with it. What "regular" means in this context is up to the storyteller. Once per fortnight might be a good guideline.
      2. To be attached, the character needs to have a dot of Humanity at the slot of an active touchstone.
    3. The free touchstone every character begins with is protected by the "Sanctity of Merits", too.
    4. Unattached touchstones need to be maintained, too, if a character wants to benefit from other touchstones.
    5. If a touchstone becomes inactive, other touchstones residing in lower slots move up to fill the gap.


    Example: John Doe has two touchstones (in slots 6 and 5). As long as he stays at Humanity 6 or above, and maintains them, both are attached and active, so he benefits from the +3 bonus to detachment rolls. Eventually, his Humanity drops to 5. Now, both of his touchstones are still active, but only one is attached. He now only benefits from the +2 bonus. To keep that bonus, he still has to maintain regular contact with [em]both[/em] touchstones. Should he neglect his touchstone at 6, it becomes inactive and vacates the slot at Humanity 6. John's touchstone at 5 moves up to fill that slot. Now one of his touchstones is inactive (currently not filling a slot), and the other one is active, but not attached. So he loses his bonus. Should John make up with his inactive touchstone or establish a new one, this newly activated touchstone fills up the highest free slot (5). Now, John has an attached touchstone again and benefits from the +2 bonus. If he manages to raise his humanity to 6 again, he benefits from the +3 bonus.

    Ventrue Clan Bane

    With the rules above, the Ventrue Bane can remain as written in the book. The Beast of a Ventrue is an alpha. It is arrogant and feels superior, especially towards humans.

    This essentially means that the slots for touchstones don't go from Humanity 6 to 1, but from 7 to 2. That means, to gain the same bonuses to detachment rolls, a Ventrue must maintain one more touchstone than other clans.

    The effect of this is that due to their superiority complex, Ventrue must invest more experience and time into upholding their Humanity. Because they tend to view humans as beneath them, they have to struggle not to lose their contact to humanity.

    Ventrue either work at staying in contact to Humanity or they will detach more quickly than other clans.

    So, to align the other Clan Banes, I'm looking for Humanity-related effects that cause trouble but can be "contained" by buying one additional touchstone and maintaining it.

    Gangrel Clan Bane

    The beat of a Gangrel is a particularly feral one. Once Gangrel hit Humanity 6, their dice pool to [em]resist[/em] frenzy (not the dice pool to ride the wave) is penalized by -2. For every attached touchstone, this penalty is reduced by one (but it doesn't turn into a bonus...).

    Example: Jane Gangrel has Humanity 6 and two active (and thus, attached) touchstones. She is not suffering any penalty on rolls to prevent frenzy. If her Humanity drops to 5, she either suffers a -1 penalty or she can try to build a stronger connection to her Humanity by establishing a third touchstone.

    Gangrel either work at staying in contact to Humanity or they will frenzy more often than other clans.

    Nosferatu Clan Bane

    The Beast of the Nosferatu is a terrifying thing. Once a Nosferatu hits Humanity 6, their effective Humanity for rolls to relate to humans is considered to be two steps lower. For every attached touchstone, this penalty is reduced by one (but it doesn't turn into a bonus...).

    Example: John Nosferatu has Humanity 6 and two active (and thus, attached) touchstones. For rolls to relate to humans, his Humanity is considered to be 6. No penalty. One of his touchstones becomes inactive (maybe he or she dies). Now John's Humanity is considered to be 5 until he can reconnect to humankind by activating his second touchstone.

    Nosferatu either work at staying in contact to Humanity or they will become unsettling towards humans more quickly than other clans.

    Mekhet Clan Bane

    The Mekhet Beast is a thing of the shadows. Once a Mekhet hits Humanity 6, his Humanity is considered to be one step lower in regard to Humanity-based banes (including sunlight and torpor). As long as the Mekhet has more than one attached touchstone, this penalty is neutralized.

    Example: Jane Mekhet has Humanity 6 and two active (and thus, attached) touchstones. She is exposed to the sun. When determining the damage, her Humanity is considered to be 6. No penalty. Had one of her touchstones been inactive, her Humanity would have been considered 5.

    Mekhet either work at staying in contact to Humanity or they will become more vulnerable to sunlight and other Humanity-base banes more quickly than other clans.

    Daeva Clan Bane

    The Daeva Beast is a thing of compulsion. Once Daeva hits Humanity 6, they tend to become obsessively attached to his or her touchstone and acquires the Persistent Dependent Condition toward the touchstone. Should the touchstone die while the Daeva is under that condition towards it, the Daeva immediately gains the Languid Condition.

    If a Daeva has more than one touchstone, the obsession is weakened enough to lose (not resolve) the Persistent Dependent Condition.

    Example: John Daeva has Humanity 6 and two active (and thus, attached) touchstones. He might be a little obsessed with his touchstones, but it doesn't have any effect in terms of game mechanics. If on of his touchstones becomes inactive, his obsession towards the remaining attached touchstone intensifies. He gains the Persistent Dependent Condition toward the touchstone.

    Once he reactivates his second touchstone, he loses the Persistent Dependent Condition. However, it is not "resolved", it might come back, anytime. So he doesn't take a beat.

    Daeva either work at maintaining a broad contact to Humanity or they will become obsessed with their touchstones.

    --

    What do you think? I'd be grateful for feedback.

      I like the thought process going on here, but I'll admit I sort of dislike the idea of having Touchstones mitigate Clan Banes. For one, I think that giving that in addition to the dice pool bonus they give to Humanity checks makes them almost too valuable for players, and makes it less likely that those banes come into play. Like the Nosferatu Clan Bane for instance is basically a non-issue with 1 Touchstone for 7-5, combined with the dice bonus it offers which will bulwark a lot of characters against degenerating unless you introduce additional kinds of modifiers (not a bad idea IMO)

      I think the philosophy of having touchstones interact with Clan Banes is a valuable one, but IMO the best way to get Clan Banes that aren't ignored constantly by those with Touchstones that beat Humanity rolls and is to find something that is either a common component of the vampiric condition (ex. the Mekhet Bane or the Gangrel Bane) or one that interacts with the character of the Clan (Nos are penalized when interacting with humans in most constructive ways BESIDES using fear and revulsion, like through Nightmare).

      I think the one issue with the Ventrue Clan Bane or the Daeva one is that they can feel a bit more passive than the others, even if the idea or the theme of it is compelling for the Clan.

      I think the Daeva Clan Bane is interesting and a creative way to re-do their dependency, while the Mekhet one loses out on the coolness of each Mekhet having their own weird little Bane. Ventrue makes sense within your framework, but I still wish there was some more substance there. I like the Gangrel one, sorta. Particularly the notion that their bane doesn't affect Riding the Wave. If anything, it feels like the Gangrel intimacy with the Beast should somehow make it easier, but the Bane isn't the place to discuss that.

      I think the Nosferatu one is the only one I really dislike, on account of it mitigating the penalties they suffer to social stuff (it makes no mention of the dramatic failures issue, which is another part of the bane)
