                        Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                        Ancient Mysteries quote, from page 155, seems to solve my question on Byzantium.



                        So if Imperator is Embraced before fall of Rome, it means it's late 5th century, so he should be from Julii Clan.

                        Not true. You misunderstand the Julii in this regard. They were the Clan of Rome. Not Romans. Rome. The "Queen of Cities". Even as the Roman Empire decayed and the capital of that Empire migrated to more defensible locations (Ravenna, etc.) the Julii remained buried in Rome like ticks, pulling the strings of their Camarilla and acting like it was the heyday of the Republic.

                        They didn't like to leave, and certainly didn't like to travel. Doing so put them at risk of being influenced by ethereal critters. Doing so took them away from what mattered: Rome and its power manipulations. They could easily leverage their position and contacts to send "lesser clans" out to accomplish the administration of the Camarilla's vassal princedoms, and did so. Thus, assuming that a minor regional city such as Byzantium might get a Julii honcho just because the latest upstart human emperor renamed it Constantinople is to ascribe human motivations and sensibilities to human power on top of these ancient monsters.

                        Yes, you did have some Julii who went into exile away from Rome--perhaps for a time or voluntarily, but they tended to come back or be on assignment until an objective was reached. The Legio Mortuus--paradoxically one of the safest ways for Julii to travel, was the adopted home of more martial clans, as well.

                        So no, the Imperator doesn't need to be Julii. And further, the assault on the Julii themselves was not limited to the City of Rome: they were ALL targeted by the Strix. It was a great conspiracy and the Ventrue might have been intimately behind it. They were certainly the powers behind the barbarian tribes at the time.

                        --Khanwulf

                                    Generally speaking we can assume the Ventrue were more associated with migrating tribes prior to the rise of feudal states such as the Franks. Constantinople was affected by but never fell to such tribes. Most likely the Prince (or even King of the ERE) was Daeva (hedonistic power) or Mekhet (famous schemers). I'd side with Mekhet, though WoD created a triumvirate between a [Daeva], Nos and Tzimisce. You could do something similar (Daeva, Mekhet, Nos) and rotate the roles of Prince of the city, King/Emperor of the East, and torpic elder voice between them. Give them each a set of favored "speaker" lackeys who carry the elder's favor and (when they are in torpor) speak with the elder's voice.

                                    I'll probably use some structure like this. It would be pretty stable so long as you treat it as a PC coterie that turned NPC--in other words they actually liked each other, once.


                                    --Khanwulf

                                    PS Edit: A few additional points on the Ventrue. They were lords of the migrating barbarians. They may have been lords in Troy, come to Rome (and Carthage), and been expelled as the Julii became what they are. The Ventrue may be an offshoot of the Julii, or the Julii an offshoot of the Ventrue (this is more likely in my opinion).

                                    In any case, the Ventrue were banned from Rome itself but existed as "lords" among the barbarians in their core peoples, washing back and forth around the Empire as it became progressively less stable. The question you may want to answer, as it will explain origins more, is whether or not these Lords knew to stay out of Rome thanks to oral tradition, or simply did so because the Julii made it quite impervious to their presence?

                                    If the former, then you have a thousand-year conspiracy between the/a Ventrue progenitor and his or her extended line with the Strix, culminating in the Julii's well-deserved murder. In the latter you have the Ventrue and their Gangrel allies gathering a fatal strike at the heart of the tottering Camarilla, and the Strix riding (literally) along for their own pleasure and revenge.

                                                    I'd like to point out that as of A Thousand Years of Night, the Julii and Ventrue have different clan banes AND different clan disciplines. 2nd edition has chosen to emphasize the differences between them. That said, if at your table, the Julii turned into the Ventrue, that is okay.

                                                    That said, I imagine they two clans were very competitive with one another. It would come as no surprise to me that the Camarilla banned the Ventrue from Roman territory as they did the Traditores, but i don't think that was ever confirmed. In fact I used to think the Ventrue were the Traditores until one of the writers/devs said they were meant to be the Akhud.
                                                    Which is what Piff asked about, so I feel comfortable sharing that here.

                                                    On the topic of Byzantine Julii, the last one is said to have vanished in 418 CE, with a scream. At least according to Fall of the Camarilla, page 196. So yes they existed, but they certainly aren't in the public eye when the Great Night Imperator showed up centuries later. That said, you can change that at your table and there is nothing to say the Byzantine Julii did not go into hiding. There were rumours of Sanctified assassins offing them around that time.
