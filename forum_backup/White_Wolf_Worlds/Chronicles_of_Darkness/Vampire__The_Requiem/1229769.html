    Circle of the Crone worshipping things from Changeling/The hedge rather than spirits?

    06-24-2018, 05:52 AM
    Was just musing recently, as I read through the Circle of the Crone covenant book. So the Circle often worship Spirits, and they have a big interest in the natural world. So if a Hierophant got a glimpse of the Hedge in some way, what are the chances it could lead to a Circle cult that worships fae entities the way others worship spirits?

    I don't really know anything about Changeling other than what some friends and a quick browsing of the wiki have taught me, so I'm wondering if any of you have done something like this in the past, or if those of you who have actually played/read Changeling could see how it could fit together?

    (Maybe this should have gone in the Changeling sub-forum? Really hard to tell with a topic like this.)

      It's a thread about a vampire thing that also requires knowledge of a changeling thing so I think it's the right forum.
      As for worshipping hedge denizens, I think that could work. My interpretation is that spirit worship is relatively uncommon (partly due to VTR never including spirit rules so it can't be the default way of playing the game) and stumbling upon the hedge is even less common. But if Acolytes with a penchant for worshipping entities beside the Crone stumbles upon the Hedge, then there's definitely grounds for at least veneration of the realm.
      The question (that I don't feel comfortable to answer due to my own lacking experience with CTL) is exactly what type of entities that would be fitting to worship. True Fae seems somewhat farfetched since they're all the way in Arcadia, and any second hand accounts won't likely portray them positively. Or maybe it's exactly their negative portrayal that makes them enticing to the Acolytes. Perhaps even considered an alternative take on the Crone herself.

          You've got my creative juices flowing. The realm itself could be the subject of worship. Perhaps they could not enter it physically, but could reach out to it with their consciousness by performing certain rituals at a particular doorway into the hedge, and enter spiritually, treating it as a spirit journey, a divine "vision", interpreting what they see as oracular wisdom and cryptic messages from the beyond.

          Considering how dangerous the Hedge and its inhabitants can be, it paints the theme of "Tribulation is Enlightenment" in a rather literal and visceral way. Perhaps on these voyages the consumption of certain Goblin Fruits could lead to actual visions or temporary insights. The glade where these grow could require a dangerous trip from the point of entry to where this particular type grows (eating the wrong type could be... bad/interesting), all the while a ritual is underway around the one doing the spirit quest to keep the connection up (this ritual being interrupted would leave the central ritual subject's spirit lost on the other side and needing to find its way back on its own, which would be... bad/interesting). It could be the ritual you need to take for Initiation out of the Chorus. One example of one such initiation in the book is a trip through the woods to a certain glade, with the woods being werewolf territory. This seems like an interesting parallel.

          This can just be one ritual practice the Circle undertakes, with perhaps one or two more centred around the Hedge out of their total package of ritual practices, but it doesn't need to be specifically a "Hedge Cult". Just like spirits tend not to be the whole of a cult's worship, but rather one of their many practices tied into their specific form of worship. In the case of this cult, it would be one with a high level of devotion towards nature, seeing the Hedge as a sort of mystical expression of nature itself, perhaps.

              I'll start by admitting that my knowledge of Circle practices is thin. That said, the impression is that they are 1) omnivorous when it comes to "pagan" worship practices regional or otherwise and, 2) venerate more broadly the thread of maiden/mother/crone female goddess motifs with special focus on the Crone part. These are also not exclusive points, nor are they ones that VtR comments on as to their "correctness" as a results-oriented belief structure--Circle adepts get results because of Cruac at minimum and no one really knows why Cruac works.

              That said, the practices of Circle worship and Cruac are BOTH likely to attract the attention of supernatural Others, who could benefit from the release of energy (Vitae/blood/Glamour) and/or take an interest in the clear avenues for manipulation a coven might present. Thus: spirits. Also, Hedge Denizens and even True Fae.

              Keep in mind it's not that the True Fae don't like "Ironside"--they just need a good reason to get involved and bear the cost of either manifesting or drawing creatures closer to them (Hedge, at minimum). They also have very, very long associations with the themes and iconography that flow from the triune-goddess myths and especially so in the British Isles where it seems the Circle codified itself first. (I'm perhaps wrong on this point.)

              Were I a bored True Fae, manipulating a coven of ecstatic vampires into serving me would sound like a fun diversion and potentially useful source of mortals (Changelings) and/or tools; you could easily employ them as a Loyalist troupe, or a wrecking ball against a Court that offended. Plus all the delicious story and emotions vamps generate....

              Side note: In my own campaign arc there are several very old characters who purposefully are blurred between vampire and Fae. Notably this includes The Morrigan, a vampiric manifestation, and within the Fae court the entities Anand, Macha, Badb and Fea/Nemain. In other words, I want to have my story cake and eat it too. The handwaving involves these as genuine people at one point, who ended up cursed vampires AND passed under the hills with the rest of the Tautha to become part of the Fae; diablerie may or may not have occurred to create The Morrigan. Really, defining this stuff down too far takes the spook out.

                                      I had a long and elaborate response typed out that evaporated and haven't the heart to recompose at the moment.

                                      Suffice to say that True Fae can and will imitate the legends of, well, faerie. They will order themselves and compete among themselves according to rules that they set (no challenge can be completely impossible, for example), and they need the real world to fill the creativity gap that they have. There is a lot of conjecture as to where the True Fae came from, other than "Changelings with high Wyrd", but to me it makes sense to assume that mortals/supernaturals that dabble in the Hedge long enough could slip into their roles over time--literally becoming the gods and legends mortals know.

                                      If you really want to become all-powerful and eternal (on par with ascending), becoming True Fae is the way to go. It's not that simple of course, but it is nearly so, and you'd not understand the difference until it's too late.

                                      Prince of the Night in relation to the thread, the Blood God ritual would be perfect for creating a vessel to be filled by a coven's specific spiritual or otherworldly entity. In that respect the point is not to create some super-powerful monster you only vaguely control, but to create something into which the monster you bow to can call home (temporarily, anyway). This doesn't exclude enacting the ritual for other purposes, of course.

                                      Second Chances how about the Welsh legend of Blodeuwedd? She was literally made by magic out of flowers and such to be the wife of someone, who quite casually betrays him after falling into lust with a visitor, and then is ultimately (re-?)turned into an owl. This is one that I'm going to use as a secondary antagonist, tapped by an unwise NPC Weihan Cynn member in a bid for revenge against a PC. Following the rules of "define lightly" all I know is that Blodeuwedd has the ability to create Hedge gates and once there sports access to the shape-changing magic of Math fab Mathonwy, has contacts with Fae, wants a body again, and is linked to vices of lust. She's probably a Goblin Queen, but I'm waiting on the upcoming Changeling 2e release to define further.

                                              Originally posted by Unahim View Post
                                              Weihan Cynn is from Requiem for Regina, right? Funnily enough, I have just earlier today learned this exists and just picked it up to see if I want to run in Elizebethan times instead.
                                              Yep. The Weihan Cynn are interesting, and I'm sure that there would be a few around in Elizabethian times--though most would have moved on to more vital Covenants.

                                              My understanding of the Cynn is that they represented a web of occulted knowledge of supernatural contacts: what exists, roughly where, how to contact them, what they wanted and what they might offer. They shared this knowledge more-or-less freely within the Covenant (boons aside), and then members went out and cultivated relationships with supernaturals (e.g. werewolves, spirits, Changelings... True Fae...) in order to accomplish things they couldn't or wouldn't do through their fellow vamps.

                                              It's an interesting idea, and certainly could if exercised judiciously leave ones' fellow, highly paranoid vampires feeling like touching the Cynn amounted to buying trouble in large, weird doses.

                                              Fun bit: the name appears to mean "Holy/Sacred Tribe/People", which could both refer to how they saw themselves, as sacred intermediaries between mortals/vamps and "other" entities, and as their assumed function as a type of vampiric priestly caste in the Isles. Isles, or beyond, to Germany. I say this because the words are proto-Germanic and NOT of British origin. This implies that perhaps the Weihan Cynn came over with the Germanic foederati of the 3rd and 4th centuries, or the "Saxon" invasions of the 5th century. If they did not, then by elimination we could surmise the Covenant was reorganized by either invading vamps or at least those embraced from the Germanic invaders--probably around the late 6th century when British resistance largely collapsed.

                                              --Khanwulf
