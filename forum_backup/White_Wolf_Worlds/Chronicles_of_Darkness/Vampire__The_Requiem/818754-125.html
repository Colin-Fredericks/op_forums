                                                I get the feeling Vitalis 's perceptive on the Beast and frenzy is mostly derived from how they are portrayed in VTM and VTR 1e, correct? I definitely see where you're coming from, but I do think VTR 2e changed the Beast and frenzy a bit.

                                                The Beast in VTR 2e definitely isn't stupid and it definitely isn't as dumb as an animal. I get the feeling you're too hung up on that one sentence you keep quoting over and over again. I get the feeling you take that one sentence way too literally. Understandably so, because the Beast in VTR 1e most definitely was an aggressive, destructive primal bestial force. But the Beast is more than that in 2e.

                                                Might I remind you of the Predatory Aura? Read that chapter again, it literally proposes 3 types of Beasts (or 3 states the Beast might be in). There is the bestial Beast to be sure, but also a competitive Beast and even a seductive Beast.

                                                I also suggest reading the 'Moving Up' chapter starting at page 56. It suggests that Beast is more or less our primal instincts and not a literal animal. Let me propose something radical. This might sound off-topic, but hear me out:

                                                If you're familiar with Sigmund Freud, you might know he proposed the human psyche can be split into 3 categories: the Id, the Ego and the Super-Ego, or the unconscious, conscious and preconscious parts of the psyche.
                                                The Id is the humans basic drive to fulfill his primary needs. The Id seeks shelter and protects us from incoming danger, but it also seeks pleasure and immediate gratification.
                                                The Super-Ego is the moral compass of the human, the standard the human sets for himself, the desire to be something greater, to follow long-term goals.
                                                The Ego is the combination of the Id and the Super-Ego and forms our core. The Ego is the mediator between the Id and the Super-Ego, is tries to keep both the Id and the Super-Ego satisfied and tries to keep both in balance. The Ego is often the driver, but it has to steer two horses with fundamentally opposing interests.

                                                One could easily argue that the Beast is the Id, Humanity is the Super-Ego and the vampire himself is the Ego. Indeed, reading through page 56 and 57 of VTR 2e seems to draw that comparison as well.

                                                It might be tempting to say the Id (and by extension the Beast) is the animal part of the human/vampire brain, and indeed it seems logical to say so, but the Id never operates alone and it certainly doesn't forget about everything the Ego has taught the Id. Indeed, the Id will use everything the Ego knows about the world to get what it wants. An animal seeking pleasure doesn't know that heating up crack on a spoon and smoking it gives him the best high he ever experienced, but a human might. The Id of the human knows that crack might give him what it wants, because the Ego of the human knows. The Id and Ego work in tandem.

                                                One could argue the Beast and the vampire are no different in that regard. There is nothing in the book suggesting the Beast cannot utilize the knowledge the vampire has gathered through his life/unlife to get what it wants.

                                                Of course the argument made by Marcus holds up as well: it's the Beast that gives the vampire his Disciplines in the first place. The Disciplines are the powers of the Beast, not of the Man. So wouldn't it be weird then if all of the sudden a frenzied vampire or draugr can't use Dominate or Majesty anymore? That notion seems to go directly against the established lore of VTR 2e.
