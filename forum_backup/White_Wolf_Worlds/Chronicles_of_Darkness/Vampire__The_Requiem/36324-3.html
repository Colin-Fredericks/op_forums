            This is why I haven't touched the Pit of the Nameless since I first read the book, until someone (un)fortunately reminded me it existed.

            It didn't feel like every other story hook provided where the ST is the one who decides how things really are or has the freedom to chose the actual truth behind events. I could tell there was a story there, I could tell that it was written with something in mind, and I could tell that there was an actual truth there. I don't feel comfortable using the setting when all that's going on in the background but so close to any characters within is completely obscured like that. I wouldn't feel comfortable making my own interpretation or deciding the truth in my version because it would be like falsifying it. I wouldn't be able to invest myself in doing whatever I want with it, because I'm too invested in wanting to really know what's going on.

            It understand it may sound like going against the spirit of the World Of Darkness. But this isn't a "setting mystery" like the origin of the Strix, or the Supernal War and the Lie, or the death of Father Wolf, or the truth of Irem and the Rite Of Return. This is something exclusive to Montreal, and extremely personal to every Kindred who lives there.

            I can't bring myself to care about creating subjective explanations to the Pit of the Nameless, when I know there's an objective one that is simply hidden from us. It's this close to the characters of the setting, so it doesn't open me to following speculations or accepting the distant vagueness as it is. It just makes me anxiously tear my hairs out at trying to understand it and frustrated that we're given so little to go on.

            The Pit of the Nameless isn't just a setting or a story hook to me. It's closer to a sinfully incomplete chronicle.
