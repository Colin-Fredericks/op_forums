    The Tzimisce - a Ventrue Bloodline

    07-06-2014, 12:55 AM
    So I've basically finished the write-up for these guys, I'm editing this first post to reflect that a lot of the issues I had at the start are no longer applicable. So for those interested in the Tzimisce as control freak, flesh-sculpting occultists, here you go:

    Bloodline: Tzimisce

    I'm using the version of Vicissitude that NeoTiamat created for his Nagloper write-up (special thanks to him and Charlaquin for that!) and I've decided to make their Gift reflect around controlling their Ghouls. The Szlachta are enhanced by the Privileges to make them better Ghouls, but the Tzimisce also gain the benefit of being able to keep track of them through blood sympathy. They also get a hefty bonus to flesh-sculpting their ghouls, so Tzimisce Ghouls are a thing to fear.

    To explain some of my thought process here, I'm working with an eye towards NeoTiamat's Nagloper write-up, which already includes a weakness that's similar to the original Tzimisce one. I really like his reasoning for it, and I was looking for ways to differentiate the Tzimisce from the Nagloper.

    I was also doing research and found out that the supposed reason the Tzimisce have this weakness is because of Kupala. Seeing as there is no Kupala and no koldun (yet?) I felt like it was an unnecessary bit for the Bloodline. I thought it would be nice to focus on my favored parts of the Tzimisce, those being fleshcrafting and the occult. They're further differentiated by not having a particularly strong tie to the home of the line. They've moved before and they've now moved again.

    I'd definitely be interested in seeing a Bloodline which might go the direction of the Old Clan Tzimisce, too, though I'd probably make them not share a history with the Tzimisce.

              Now, I'm not a fan of Masquerade. Never played it. So, I'm coming at this as someone who's only heard about this through the grapevine.

              The Tzim in Masquerade were an ecclesiastic mix of themes and powers to fit their particular backstory. They were fleshy horrors, wearing the beast on in their skin like the Nosferatu are. No, they're eastern europe's Ventrue cousins. No, they were an alternate gangrel style shifter. No, they're... the list goes on. That's a lot of mixed things that won't translate well over into a single bloodline, because, as a clan, they needed to allow to cover a wide range of concepts you can fill in as a character. Bloodlines, however, are the distillation of a single clan concept.

              You are all over the place with things. Your theme is "feudal lords" but you give them shapeshifting powers and note that they're primarily Ordo, who tend to not get involved with the whole neo-feudal structure. They search out scholars - notably people who aren't known for ruling over others, but for study.

              Your blood magic, the whole raison d'etre of the bloodline, is heavily tied to medicine, which again is a very small subset of specialists - that's something that would make good doctors or nurses or even back room drug making biochemists. This lends itself less to scholars or feudal lords again. I really think you need to tighten down the concepts here first priority, then work on the weakness.

              Speaking of magic.... your write up is basically giving the Tzim two new bloodline powers. First, they get protean, and THEN a blood sorcery. I would recommend either eliminating protean, or leaving Protean and making new Obtenebration style devotions to do what you want. Or make bloodline-specific scales that use Protean instead of a Coil as its base. If you are going to keep the blood magic stuff.... then I suggest either deciding if you are going to be allowing Themes or not. Because you're making reference to Hanging Spells which is only possible via Themes. Either make it a special exception to how this blood magic works, and explain how Hanging Spells will work for this stuff, or take a long look at all the other things that the Themes will allow.

                      Well now. This is flattering. Looks solid overall, though could use tightening, yes. Miscellaneous thoughts:

                      1) Theme is key and should inform all other things. I think Lords without Lands is solid, though I also think that it can be refined further. I rather like the idea of the Tzimisce as the ultimate control freaks, using Dominate to take over your mind and Vicissitude to take over your body.

                      2) Obviously, I think the Discipline and Bloodline Sorcery is fine, but then I would. Medicine is there for the purely practical reason that if you are going to be warping flesh and bone, you should know how not to accidentally impale your ghoul's spleen with a bone spike in the process, which requires knowing where the spleen is.

                      3) I would be careful about tying the Tzimisce and the Ordo Dracul too tightly together. That is to say, while there's a definite historical connection, I'd also make it clearer that the Tzimisce can and do go hang out in other Covenants. The Invictus is an obvious choice, the Crone is quite plausible. One of the more irritating habits, I think, of earlier VtR Bloodlines was to treat them as though every bloodline member meets the stereotype of the clan and joins the same covenant.

                      4) As far as weakness goes... perhaps look to the Bron Weakness, from one of the early Bloodlines books (Legendary, I think it was?). The idea is that whenever the Tzimisce is in charge of something, they take a (10-Humanity or 10-Humanity/2) penalty to rolls related to maintaining and using that power. Make that powerful and eloquent Tzimisce the Prince, and all of a sudden, they start tripping over their tongue. A Tzimisce becomes a mentor to a younger student, and their minds cloud up whenever they're trying to explain the Coils.

                      How to explain it / justify it? Well, at least with the Nagloper, I had the idea that Vicissitude is taught, first of all, by the Strix. The Tzimisce appear to have gotten on the bad side of the Strix in a bad way, and they claim to have stolen Vicissitude, so... perhaps a curse?

                      In any case, the weakness is both markedly painful if you are trying to rise high in the ranks, and also very aggravating. It makes people with an extremely strong sense of ego into making the choice between being a follower and being constantly sabotaged.
