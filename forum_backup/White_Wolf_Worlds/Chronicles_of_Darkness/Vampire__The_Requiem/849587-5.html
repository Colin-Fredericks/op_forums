    Originally posted by The Storyteller View Post
    I don't find that interesting, I find that...disconnected from the game world. When a character rolls intimidate, they are determining how intimidating they are. They are not determining what kind of person the guy they're intimidating is.
    Again, this is the disconnect. 2E is agenda based, not action based.

    Not,

    PC: "I roll to see how intimidating I am."

    Rather,

    PC: "I want to get information out of him."
    ST: "How?"
    PC: "By Intimidating him. Also, I shoot his partner first. Does that give me a bonus?"
    ST: "Hell yes. Gonna be a humanity check also, but definitely a bonus."

    The agenda is specifically getting the information. The player has a number of ways (actions) to do that. Trick him. Persuade him. Seduce him. Blackmail him. Intimidate him. Torture him.

    The action is second to the agenda.

    Again, look at all the new per-your-definition 'story-mechanics' - even combat, with the beaten down and agenda based system is moving that way. 2E is infused with it. Hence my initial suggestion that you may be better off starting with 1E, and pulling over the systems you do like (Combat, Disciplines, the other crunchy systems). Rather than start with 2E and try and unweave the as-you-say story-mechanics from everything.

    And let me re-iterate, none of this is an indictment on your playstyle. I myself have had really great and fun games run very similar to everything you do, and even run games that are similar in the past. I just would never run that kind of a game using the 2E rules. And I hit this dilemma directly when thinking about a 2E LARP. A lot of the concepts don't translate easily (Though to be fair David Hill did post some cool discipline conversions for a potential LARP), but it's going to be an intensive project to convert the whole thing.

    This is crazy to me:

    PC: I got zero successes on my intimidate
    GM: The guy is so scared of you, he becomes a gibbering mess
    PC: But, I failed
    GM: Yeah, so, he doesn't talk, he just gibbers.
    PC: So, my intimidation skill is about...helping my prisoners talk coherently?

    Sorry, that just doesn't make sense. These skills should mean something and have predictable effects on the world. A PC's roll to determine how scary they are should not change the other guy's personality. If anything, those things should be the result of a successful or failed Resolve based roll of some kind on the NPC's part. The idea of failing an intimdate roll and then the other guy suddenly is too scared to talk, or succeeding and suddenly he can't lie, or any of the stuff the both of you have suggested here makes about as much sense to me as you asking a random passerby for help lifting something, and if you get an exceptional success on Persuasion, it turns out this guy is actually a ghoul with 5 Vigor and can easily lift anything you need.

    The mechanics and game world need to be connected in a logical way, or we won't enjoy the game.
    Hopefully my description above re: agenda-based vs. action-based play helped clarify this. And again, there is nothing wrong with not liking agenda-based play, or director/writer-style play at all. It just seems like you really want to believe 2E isn't what I would define as agenda-based (if other lurkers have a better term for what I'm trying to explain, please hop in). I think I, and a lot of people who have sort of posted counter to your ideas, are seeing that agenda-based style as integral to the system. And in fact, find it one of the more compelling aspects of the system.

    Beats, Aspirations, Breaking Points, Touchstones, Social Rolls, Dramatic Failures - It's difficult for me to see much of a 2E game when you strip all those things out.

    But, moving forward - that's what you want to do, and that's great too. I would just say be prepared to have this conversation a lot, 'cause I really think you're swimming upstream against a lot of the 2E current.

    For example, maybe a better question would have been, "I'm running 1E, but I want the Clan Flaws to match the new 2E versions. Here's my ones for the first four clans. But I'm having trouble with the Gangrel. Any ideas?" Instead of, "Hey I want to change the Gangrel Flaw in my 2E game, but I don't want it to have anything to do with beats, aspirations, breaking points, touchstones, social rolls, dramatic failures, frenzy or any other agenda-style play." : )

        The mechanics and game world need to be connected in a logical way, or we won't enjoy the game.
        But the thing is, skills never auto-succeed. Putting a gun in someone's hand does not give them infinite Intimidate dice. A pump-action shotgun, when racked, adds +1 to Intimidate checks because, as Gabriel Iglesias puts, "when I hear chik-chak, the joke is over." If there's any chance that the action might fail (such as when someone else is opposing you, as Intimidate does by default), you have to roll. And if you fail, you don't successfully intimidate the target into doing what you want. It's actually very easy to scare someone so much that they can't tell you what you want, even if they dearly want to. That's where there's a "good cop" in the classic "good cop, bad cop." And we're not talking fundamental changes in a person's personality here. We're talking how, in this instance, a hypothetical person with a gun to the head of another hypothetical person and the obvious willingness to use it might still fail an Indimidate check, and how that result might be described in that particular instance. The target of Intimidate doesn't suddenly gain bunches of Willpower, representing that they now know they're made of way sterner stuff than they ever suspected, they just found, in that moment, the courage to hold to their convictions in the face of certain, terrifying death (though a player who found their character in such a situation, and passed such a loaded against them Intimidate roll, I would easily allow to spend Experiences on Willpower, Resolve, and/or Composure, precisely because of that.) The dice inform the story and vice versa, failing a roll can add to the drama as effectively as succeeding, and saying that a roll can't fail (or succeed) in a given circumstance due to modifiers (essentially what you're talking about) undercuts the roll one plays in constructing the other.

        For this hypothetical Intimidate roll, I'd add a capped bonus of +5 (if I recall, miscellaneous bonuses to rolls still can't exceed five dice in 2E). Between the gun and the obvious willingness to use it, the Intimidating character is about as scary as they can possibly be. But the roll can still fail. In which case, it's my job as ST to determine why that happened. Maybe the other person believes that the Intimidator isn't actually willing to kill them, because they can't get answers from a corpse? Maybe they do find that ability to spit in the face of iminent death. Maybe they're so terrified they can't answer. Whatever works best for the NPC in question.

                                                Originally posted by Marcus View Post

                                                The roll come up just in some situations. I mean: if you point a gun and ask for the wallet, ok, no roll is needed.
                                                Not necessarily. I remember hearing in line in passing (think it was from some Law and Order incarnation or other) where a known hold-up artist was explaining that he couldn't have shot the victim of the week, because he uses a hand cannon while the victim was shot with a 9mm. He explains that he uses a really big caliber gun because "some people are stupid enough to argue with a 9 mil." Even if you've got a gun, someone might be brave enough or stupid enough to not give in, or might have something in their wallet that they feel is worth dying for. Think back to Ghost, where Sam the Stock Broker was perfectly willing to hand over the cash in his wallet, but refused to hand over the wallet itself because it had sensitive information for his entire company's fortune in it (which is why he was being robbed in the first place.)

                                                The motivations of the NPC are just as important at the motivations of the PC. The NPC might be answering to someone even scarier. A guy with a loaded gun to your head can only kill you, a Nosferatu with Nightmare can make you live in an eternal hell, and a Ventrue with Dominate can make you murder your own family. They might be clinging to whatever code of honor or ethics they have that tell them not be a rat. They may just be a contrary asshole who gets more resolved to deny something the more someone else obviously wants it. Quite simply, there should be no such thing as a guaranteed success as soon as another character, Player or Non, gets involved. Every attempt to get someone else to do what you want should have a chance, no matter how slim, to fail. Stating that "under X circumstance, there is no logical way this cannot work" is a fallacy, because humans are fundamentally illogical. Even in real life, threats of violence and torture (or actual violence and torture) don't always yield the desired results. Human beings are complex animals, their reactions are not always predictable, and responses to stimuli can vary greatly even within the same individual.

                                                But this is getting increasingly off topic. I'd suggest, if we have more to say on the topic of Intimidation rolls and so forth, someone start a new thread to talk about it, because this has nothing to do with Gangrel Clan Weaknesses.

                                                        Hy, storytellers!

                                                        I've been reading your posts, and I think that finally found a houserule for the Gangrel's weakness good enough to my group.

                                                        The Feral Curse

                                                        "Gangrel Beasts boil close to the surface. A Gangrel can run with the devil inside her, but has difficulty fighting it. All her dice pools to resist frenzy related to hungry or thread are limited by her Humanity dots. This weakness does not affect dice pools to ride the wave, or frenzy estimulated by fire or sunlight. As well, the predator within calls for a prey, so, everytime when a Gangrel feed using the Kiss - instead of the Assault - she gains no nourishment from the first few vitae taken . This amount is equal to (10 – Humanity)."

                                                        That's it. Now we have Gangrel characters encouraged to act like the predators they are. Or they will frighten their victims terribly, as Eli from "Let Me In/ Let The Right One In" (a true gangrel to me), or exhaust them almost completely seeking to satiate their hunger predator, as Lestat (Interview with the Vampire, the only book in which he does not look like a superhero, but a unforgettable degenerate character).

                                                            Originally posted by The Storyteller View Post
                                                            (...)

                                                            The Gangrel flaw is just not to my preference. The Gangrel are supposed to be wild and bestial, but their flaw, the way you avoid it, is by doing exactly the opposite of that, by maintaining humanity, avoiding anger and anger inducing situations, being extremely polite and nice to everyone to avoid provocations, feeding carefully to keep from hunger, etc., etc. It's everything a Gangrel shouldn't be.
                                                            No offense, but I think there are two things you've missed in your reasoning:

                                                            1. Every clan bane is linked to Humanity in some way, and thus maintaining high Humanity is a way for everyone to avoid or mitigate their bane. The Gangrel aren't exceptional in this regard.

                                                            2. Gangrel bane caps their frenzy dice pools, but not their Riding the Wave pools. Hence, the way to avoid the bane without taking the moral high road and maintaining high Humanity is to ride the wave as often as possible. Going this route, you end up with a character that frenzies more often than other vampires (because she rides the wave where other vamps would have just suppressed the frenzy) but with less random side effects (because she frenzies on her own terms), creating the impression the character is more in tune with her Beast. Isn't this exactly what you want out of clan banes - avoiding them by embodying them?
