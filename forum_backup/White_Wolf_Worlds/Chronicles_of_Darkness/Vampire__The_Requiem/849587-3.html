    Originally posted by The Storyteller View Post
    While I wouldn't describe the state as "acting like frenzied," didn't my suggestion basically do what you're saying? You have to act directly to escape the situation or lash out at those that you perceive as having confined you, or else you spend willpower and suffer a penalty.
    This was more about being direct in all actions, not just when under the conditions of "being confined," which I think you yourself stated might have some trick definitions for the players to grasp (if not, I apologize for misreading.) Instead, the idea is to act in the most straightforward manner at all times. Don't like the way someone is behaving? You don't snicker at them and talk behind their back, you walk up to them and say "I don't like your attitude, change it or I change it for you." Want blood? You don't play the dating game, you grab them or drug them or Dominate them. Don't like the Prince's latest decree? You don't lobby and debate and try to convince him to change his mind with rational discourse, you find a bunch of others who don't like it and tell the Prince to change his mind or deal with you and your friends.

    Granted, "most direct method" can also be somewhat nebulous, but it was the best I had at the time.

    As far as frenzy itself, even in Masquerade it wasn't "kill until there's nothing left to kill." The only thing even close to that I remember is "if blood is available, the vampire will drink until they're full." Which could kill one person or shitloads of people, depending on the vampire's Generation and how much blood they actually had. Other than that, exactly what a frenzy was remained pretty nebulous, and I think Requiem has been trying to hone in on what a Frenzy means more and more though 1st and 2nd Edition. I do recall reading the thing about the Beast having a goal in a Frenzy, and pursuing that goal to the exclusion of all others. A vampire frenzied by fire wants to get away from it, it's not going to attack anyone unless they try and prevent it from escaping, and then the attack is only so they can continue to flee. Whether they just shove someone aside or literally go through them depends on the individual vampire and what they have at their disposal. A vampire humiliated by a rival may certainly have as their goal "kill that asshole," but they won't indiscriminately kill everyone else. Just their target and anyone who tries to get in their way. Less like sharks in a feeding frenzy, more like The Terminator. A goal pursued with single-minded purpose. At least, from my admittedly half-remembered reading way too long ago.

    Originally posted by The Storyteller View Post
    That pretty much goes against my clan bane paradigm, though. I want the flaw to be avoided, not embraced. I don't want it to come up much at all. I want there to be a way for the Gangrel to mostly avoid their flaw by acting in a certain, stereotypical-for-the-clan way. In this case, Gangrel would prefer out doorsy areas, with less corners to be backed in. They'd avoid fancy, formal affairs with suffocating rules. They'd avoid sitting in the corner, being always aware of exit points. They'd hunt on the rooftops instead of alleyways and haven in the dirt outside, rather than in elaborate labyrinthian underground complexes. You know, that kind of stuff.
    Ah, I see. Forgive me, I get the Gangrel from Masquerade, 1E, and 2E all muddled in my head (they are pretty much my favorite Clan, though), so I wasn't quite sure which of the various Gangrel stereotypes you were aiming at. The problem I see with this is that "avoiding fancy, formal affairs with suffocating rules" basically means "avoiding vampire society entirely," since vampires do love themselves lots of fancy formal affairs with suffocating rules. It also essentially bars Gangrel from three or four of the five Covenants (depending on exactly how formal and suffocating you think the rules of the Ordo Dracul, Invictus, Circle, and Sanctum are.) Perhaps a penalty based on how they approach the parts of the lives that are fully under their control? Joining a Covenant (or being part of Kindred society, or any society for that matter, inherently means giving up some personal freedoms to the greater whole). A Gangrel who's signed on to the Invictus knows exactly what they're getting themselves into, and so has no problem with the rules and etiquette of that group, because something about that must have appealed to them on some level to join. Rather, when they're "on their own time," Gangrel have certain ways of doing things that apply a penalty when not done. Could be as simple as "must keep a Haven in an outdoorsy area" or something like that.

    The problem is that the Gangrel kind of embrace a pretty wide stereotype, so honing in on what, exactly, the Clan means is likely going to be different for everyone who reads them. "Primal, animalistic vampire" is far broader than "Aristocratic power-hungry vampire" or "modern sexy vampire" in a lot of ways. Maybe that's the stereotype that should be embraced. "Primal animalism" has a lot of different connotations, so maybe an array of Banes that the player picks one of, to reflect how the primal, animalistic nature of the Clan manifests in her.
