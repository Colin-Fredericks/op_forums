    killing blow with firearms

    10-11-2015, 04:28 PM
    Hi,
    I have a question:
    A PC has the merit "murder expert" that convert every surprise attack in a killing blow.
    Now, he successed in a surprise roll hidden in a second flor flat, and shot in the street below.
    For a close combat I don't see any problem in letting him deal a killing blow, but for a long distance (17m) shot??? I mean, he could miss the targhet or not be so precise. I want clarify that he have just 1 dot in firearms so he is not so skilled.
    Ho do you handle this situation?

      I'm hoping that's 17 meters, not miles. But to be specific, the skill doesn't convert surprise attacks into killing blows, it requires some premeditation.

      Slasher, p127, 'Drawback: You must name your target on the turn before you attack — you can’t choose to slaughter whoever is slowest that turn.'

      That said, if you let the character have murder expert, they are a murder expert and guns are very good murder weapons, even at range. So providing they met the prerequisites for the usage of the merit, they successfully murdered a person with a gun.

                  With one dot of firearms, his killing blow can't be all that powerful either. Top he is doing 7-9 damage. If this is against a Kindred, that is immediately bashing, and more annoying. If it is against a kindred with resilience, it is almost laughable.

                  Granted XP expenditures will make it more deadly over time, but this where you get creative as a GM.

                  (Assuming Kindred) This person is getting known for their ability to quickly bring down any targets from hiding. Having attention brought on you for being an effective killer suddenly thrusts the character into the possibility of being a hound for the city. Hounds have interesting jobs and suddenly your character who is exceptionally good at surprise combat finds himself dealing with situations that don't always require surprise combat.

                  Perhaps he is investigating the disappearance of a young invictus Daeva and runs across the Daevas ghoul, mind broken and gibbering about nonsense(Thoroughly dominated).

                  Perhaps he is sent as backup to ensure peace at negotiations between two influential Kindred. If violence breaks out, how does his surprise attack help when everyone is aware he is there?

                  In the end, if you allowed the merit, you have to come up with creative solutions to limit it if you feel it is to powerful. Normally I would say allow it, Kindred have for more powerful abilities, and this merit hardly breaks the game.

                                                      The real deal here is to understand what the Killing Blow Rule represents, and forget about murder entirely. Yes, the merit had the intent to facilitate kills, and the Killing Blow Rule has such name for obvious reasons.

                                                      But it isn't instant kill or a treatise about optimal killing circumstances. It is just about: in an optimal condition to use your abilities, you have your entire dice-pool as successes. That's all to begin with. So anything affecting your dice-pool is already affecting your killing blow. That includes weapon range. That includes lack of skill. That includes anything at all that reduces your accuracy, because any reduction in accuracy will reduce your dice-pool, given that it is how the system models accuracy. That aside, anything about actual ability, equipment or the possibility to access the head or the heart is completely irrelevant, not because they aren't issues, but because they are already taken into account.

                                                      It doesn't matter if the rule have Killing in the name. If your character have just 3 dice to hit and +2 damage from the weapon, the "Killing Blow" will cause just 5 Lethal and won't even cause penalties to a normal human with Stamina 3.

                                                      So, just use it as it is. The character is a superlative dealer of destruction with surprise. It is a little strong, but is the benefit of the merit, and shouldn't be limited to melee combat. It isn't as strong as it seems, since you actually needs 14 damage to insta-kill the average human (7 Lethal and them 7 aggravated). And to be so superlative at dealing damage shouldn't be that relevant on the game, either.

                                                      Of course, if you're having actual problems with this, for whatever reason (including your campaign being too combat-oriented for this to pass), then just ask the player to change the points for another merit. It is easier and more reasonable.
