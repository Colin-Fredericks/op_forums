                    I think this thread illustrates very well why the narrativism/gameism/simulationism divide is waffle. Some folks like some mechanics. Some folks don't like other mechanics. Lots of folks want to be able to label and categorize the things they like and don't like, so it feels more objective. But ultimately, it's still totally subjective which mechanics fit into which category, so the categories themselves just end up causing confusion when people say things like "I don't like story game mechanics" and then everyone has a different idea of what "story game mechanics" means, and we end up giving advice on how to fix mechanics that the person never actually had a problem with.

                            So, this thread is basically dead, and that's fine, no need to further respond, but I thought this was the best place to share an awesome experience I had in my game this past week that exemplifies why I like Humanity.

                            The group attacked a bunch of drug dealers protecting a stash in their territory. Three of the four who made it to the game had Obfuscate 1, and the fourth had Majesty, so she talked her way close, then everyone attacked. It was brutal. In the end, two thugs were bleeding out dying, two had surrendered and were led into the storage lockup for holding until they sorted out what to do, and the fifth, a ghoul, was being interrogated. Most of the party was hungry at this point, and some tough decisions had to be made. They actually tried to pin and drink the ghoul, but amazing, desperate rolls on his part prevented it, while he successfully pleaded for his life, offering to trade information about the vampires running things if they let him go.

                            After that was settled, one character was sitting over the dying men, contemplating whether she wanted to murder them or not, since they were probably going to die anyway. She was basically talking out loud to herself. "It's a mercy, right? They're going to die anyone, so it's ok?" She ended up choosing not to kill them, but as a result, she's going to be imminently hungry and at risk of frenzy (she's sitting at 5 blood).

                            Another who had just barely held off a frenzy during the fight since he was actually hungry (he ended up with only 3 blood) asked the group's grappling specialist to hold on to him while he drank one of the conscious guys. In case he frenzied, he wanted the other guy to hold on to him and prevent him from killing him off. He managed to fight it off again and dominated the guy to forget what happened.

                            The fourth needed to drink as well, so, he forced the final guy to take some of the hallucinogens they were dealing so he wouldn't remember what happened while he drank.

                            Everyone rolled for detachment. Nobody died (the only guy not hungry even moved the bodies and called 911 so they could get help), but there was still impassioned violence all around, and most of them hurt people for blood. But, they all succeeded, and everyone felt awful about it. It was such a great moment when we talked about it and they were like, "I can't believe I just had to debate whether I was going to mercy kill someone or not." "Yeah, well, I needed someone to hold me as assurance in case I flipped out and murdered someone." "I just drugged a guy so I could eat him and he wouldn't tell anyone..." It was deep and awesome.

                            That's why I love Humanity. Without it, nobody would have cared about what happened. They would just would have killed the guys for expediency and walked away with their information not giving a damn about their condition or, well, anything but themselves.
