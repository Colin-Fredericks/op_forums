                Originally posted by Hastur00 View Post
                I'm saying that regardless of where their power comes from, the mindset that drives it would be accurately represented by a Humanity cap.
                There's no way to mechanically represent that for the wide variety of mindsets. What if someone is just following someone else's notes? At the risk of Godwin's Law here, but the whole thing with unethical testing is that it does produce results faster than not. We've pardoned criminals in exchange for notes that save millions of lives in the medical field. That information, once discovered, isn't harming the integrity millions of doctors world wide. Its saving lives.

                The point is - while developing a Scale is likely bad to your Humanity... if you're just taught the Scale? Steal its knowledge via diablarie (which has its own rules), use Dominate or Auspex, something like that? Doesn't make sense. And, even if you go with the evil mad alchemist route, we still have the point that not everyone engages in inhuman acts equally - some acts are more inhuman than others.

                The fact is that, for the most part, there is nothing inhuman about using a Scale. There's not enough of a proximate causation to justify anything related to Humanity here. You want to learn scale, the ST can require you to make inhuman experiments, then hit you with breaking points as you engage in said experiments. Its the actions to justify buying the scale that's the issue, not the scale itself. Because said actions can be so many different things, trying to paint a one-size-fits-all doesn't work.

                        I disagree, I think it is a poor way. First, I know you said this offhandedly, but everything is NOT being equal. Coils and Scales aren't Cruac. Cruac is innately tied to the demonic strix, to communion with the Beast and a primal mind set; I see Coils and Scales as controlling the Beast through academic knowledge and higher brain functions, not primal instinct. Its implied that low Humanity = beastial, not inhuman, and I don't think equating the Ordo to beastial is appropriate, while it is appropriate for the Circle.

                        Two, scales have no reason to restrict Humanity once they're known. Learning one from a book or a teacher doesn't require torture to learn (only discover the first time). Hells, even if you wanted to assume using a Scale is detrimental to Humanity by making each use of a scale a breaking point, lets say, not all scales are equal - one that removes blood bonds can sit unused for large swaths of play-time, while one that is used to go out in the sunlight finds constant use. Two people with two different Coils acting in vastly different ways being capped in the same manner? Makes no sense.

                        Three, appropriate Banes are more fun and thematic, I think, than a cap, which would discourage players from taking Banes (which happens in my experience). And taking a bane is often far more inhuman in practice, even though it ironically preserves Humanity.

                        Instituting a cap has a lot of effects that go just beyond forcing Scale users to degrade in Humanity that I think is clumsy and could hurt the way the covenant is seen and played.

                        Let me ask a question. Why do you need to reflect a Dragon's shift in mindset in such a different manner than any of the other covenants? The Invictus change, the Carthians change, the Lance change. If VII includes Kindred and not something stranger, they change too. They don't need a humanity cap, even if they commit as many inhuman acts as the Ordo in their own ways? I mean, really, the Movement is the only covenant that isn't associated with a couple different VtM Paths. All the covenants lead to inhumanity one way or another. Why does the Ordo specifically need one and the others do not?

                                                        Well, in my initial post, I did call out the Scale for breaking blood bonds as been "acceptable torture." Because, in theory, you're doing it to free someone from a Blood Bond they don't want. You certainly could use it on someone who's quite happy being bound, break them free so you can take them for yourself, but that would be a whole separate issue.

                                                        You do, indeed, raise a good point that none of the Scales need to be used to do evil. However, using them almost requires you to do evil. Several of them, specifically the ones I called out in my original post, require (often large) amounts of tissue from humans. While it has been rightly pointed out that there are means to acquire those materials that don't rely on killing people for their body parts, it would still generally be a lot easier to kill one person and get all the stuff you need, instead of haunting the local liposuction clinic for a week. One of them specifically requires a large quantity of fresh blood, no more than one minute old, meaning you have to have a "donor" on hand, likely more than one if you don't want to leave dead bodies around. And, of course, few people are willing to part with large quantities of whole blood, so you need to do something to enforce their co-operation.

                                                        Now, I get that this could easily be called "feature" instead of "bug," but it just rubs me the wrong way. I get that there should be temptation to do "evil" for vampires, but, as I've tried to explain, the presentation leaves me with the impression that the Ordo are less tempted to do evil then they are evil by default. I agree that none of the Covenants are particularly "good," but I don't like playing them as particularly evil, either.
