    All of that feels to come from the fact that the Masks/Dirges are wrongly chosen. For Mask/Dirge you shouldn't go out of your way, it should be how you want to roleplay. As for player cues - after a while, the players will get a feel for when they deserve WP and when they don't, so you won't be interupted needlessly

    Anyways, perhaps, the right solution here, instead of looking for something "correctly", talk it over with the players, and to hell with Touchstones/Masks/Dirges and whatever else you don't like? They're supposed to be there to reward good roleplay, not to shoehorn something somewhere.
    It doesn't come from mask/dirge being wrongly chosen, it comes from real people being complicated and diverse. Even if you can call someone a jokester, they're not literally making jokes all the time in all situations. They're also not forcing jokes at weird times because they just were in a tough fight. But when you get willpower for cracking a joke? That's what happens.

    A real Monster isn't cruel to everyone all the time. That's a great way to get dead. But if you get willpower from it? Guess who is trolling the neighborhood looking for someone to bully whenever they do anything difficult?

    These rules are in place to encourage good roleplaying, but for them to result in actually good roleplaying, you need people who aren't good roleplayers normally, forgtting to act like their masks and dirges until they go, "oh, right, I should say something snarky!" (or whatever). If you have people who are conscious of their mask/dirge, they're going to act weird, not actually in character, but as a flanderized* version of their character.

    *If it's not known what I mean, Ned Flanders is Homer Simpson's neighbor. In the early episodes, he was just a really nice neighbor to provide a foil to Homer. Eventually, his niceness and other quirks (okily dokily) grew and grew until he ended up nice and devout to the degree of insanity. It's basically what happens to all side characters in long running shows. They start as normal, 3 dimensional people with a few quirks, but all anyone cares about or respond to are the quirks, so, they grow and grow until the character has actually become a 2-dimensional board holding up a few quirks.

    Getting willpower from Mask or Dirge every scene will lead to that, I fear. Normal vampires do not act as their mask/dirge 100% of the time, anymore than normal humans constantly indulge in virtues and vices. These things should be occasional, not a driving force. If you have people who are actually good roleplayers, playing in character, mask/dirge will only come up every once in a while. But if its the only way to get back willpower, they're going to purposefully stop being good roleplayers for a bit in order to make back some quick willpower.

    And seriously, to me, this is a roleplaying game. Good roleplaying is my default assumption. I'm not going to give anyone rewards for doing what they're supposed to do. That'd be like getting $100 in monopoly just for rolling the dice.

    Anyway, yeah, I am 75% sure I'm just going to keep running it the way we've been playing.

                        Couple thoughts, Storyteller:

                        First - It's true that the initial implementations of Mask / Dirge / Touchstone tend to be clunky when players are first starting to get used to them. Part of your job as Storyteller is to be the gatekeeper on what a meaningful expression or interaction with their anchor is. Whenever I feel like it's becoming a rote expression, I straight up ask the player, "Share with me how this is meaningful to your character. Did they learn anything about themselves, or did you as a player experience anything new about the character?" If they come up with an honest answer, that's fine with me. After a few of these, assuming your players are in a zone more skewed toward the dramatic vs. mechanic, they shouldn't have trouble coming up with stuff.

                        Second, I also had trouble getting Touchstones to 'work right' at my table when I was first transitioning from 1E to 2E until I took a much more thematic approach. I found more success when I started looking at Touchstones as a representation of a specific relationship the character has, through which the lens of humanity would be focused for that character. To distill that into more relateable terms, I come up with sort of a relationship archetype to represent the Touchstone. So, 'My young niece who might be showing latent psychic abilities' becomes, "The Prodigy" - and 'The streetkid who I helped get her life together after her parents disappeared' becomes "The Orphan." I then do my best to make that relationship archetype a theme of the character's interactions in the world. The Orphan is causing one character to deal with a lot of abandonment themes, while the Prodigy shades the second with themes of unrealized potential.

                        I put it this way to my players now. "Every Vampire has to personally experience the conflict between man and beast, human and kindred. Think of a type of relationship or archetype (Patron, Matron, Child, Bully, Con Man, Lawyer, Sibling, Lover, Enemy, Rival, Pet, Saboteur, etc.) that you want to represent on a thematic level that struggle or experience for your character. Now, think of a person who is important to your vampire who represents that type of archetype as a character. That person is your Touchstone." And, if the player more just has a stock character like a bartender, then I'll just reverse engineer a couple of options like, "The Confidant," "The Enabler," "The Party Boy," and let them choose which one they like the best.

                        Finally, let me second the good idea that MCN and others mentioned about representing Touchstones with other Merits. Retainer, Herd, etc. Anything which draws the Touchstone closer to the vampiric orbit, only increases the chances that the Touchstone will be more involved in the night-to-night stories. I'm currently toying with the idea of even giving an XP discount to such purchases. (1 XP = 2 Dots of Merits) to encourage this even more.

                        But, at the end of the day, If you are keeping the 1 WP refresh per night, I do think you are eliminating a big mechanical function of Touchstones. That, combined with it maybe being a little too proactive ST / Reactive PC - I don't think you'd lose too much if you just cut them out. Rejiggering Breaking Points and the Ventrue Flaw being the obvious ones which you're already addressing.
