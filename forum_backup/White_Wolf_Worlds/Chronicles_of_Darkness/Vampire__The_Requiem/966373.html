    (1E) What was playing VTR like in its early days?

    09-09-2016, 03:18 PM
    I often see threads and discussions on various websites asking what Vampire: The Masquerade was like when it first came out and how people played the game back in 1991 and 1992. It's kind of obvious as to why, VTM was a major turning point in RPG history and the early 1990's was over two decades ago. Plus oWoD ended up radically different from how it started in many different ways.

    ​But what about Requiem? Now that the game is in its second edition and has also been changed significantly, along with the rest of the New World of Darkness (which is now called Chronicles of Darkness, though for sake of simplicity, I will refer to 1e material as nWoD and 2e material as CofD), I want to spark discussion on the early days of Requiem and the New World of Darkness.

    I know 2004 was more recent than 1991, but it's been over a decade ago now, and I didn't get into World of Darkness until 2010, when I was in high school (I was born in 1993). By the time I started playing WoD, the First Edition of nWoD had more or less completed its run, barring a few minor PDF updates here and there. I want to know what this early period in the gameline's history was like, with an emphasis on 2004-2006 (pre-Changeling, which was when the nWoD fully discovered its own identity before forming a new one as the Chronicles of Darkness later on).

    I was on the old forums from 2010-2012, and it was different from what it is now, but I'd imagine things were even more wild back then. I want to know what the speculation on nWoD was like following the demise of oWoD, and what people thought of Requiem. I'm talking about those who actually played it of course, and not those who dismissed it and just stuck to Masquerade. I know a lot of people did that, including most of the gamers in my area, which is why I played Masquerade for so long, because nobody but me wanted to play Requiem. Nothing wrong with preferring Masquerade over Requiem or vice versa, I like both equally, but I'm seeking info from long-time Requiem fans.

      First and foremost, 1E Requiem seemed to fix a lot of the things that rubbed me the wrong way about oWoD. The switch to Blood Potency from Generation alone I feel would have made the switch worth it. I loved the Fog of Eternity (sad to see it mostly go in 2E) so that there was no way to prove what creation myth was real, actual reasons for not everyone to be playing some famous historical figure (maybe they thought they were, but odds are on fevered torpor dreams and they were just some rando.)

      I really enjoy the culling down to five basic clans based on fictional archetypes, then the broadening to Bloodlines to represent regional, cultural or philosophical differences. I do remember being sad that my favorite Clan had Presence replaced with Animalism, but I came around to the theme.

      Breaking away from the metaplot was also good for stuff I liked. Giving each setting/city its own focus as opposed to them just being foot-notes on the Grand Sabbat/Cam/Anarch war appealed. The Covenants as factions that worked against each other more cold-war like than the all-out ones from Masquerade was another in that vein.

      There wasn't as much as a narrative shift like 2E has, but the seeds were obviously planted which was great for where I wanted to go from oWoD. I did LARP as well, and there were real discussions about whether to switch over or not. I had no interest in a game that was going to stay Masq, but gave the Requiem one a shot.

      I was fascinated by the Ordo at first, but then came to settle with the Invictus as my home-base Covenant.

      I like the kind of separation they made, keeping each line kind of it's own strain, with the assumption that crossovers would be rare or just very customized, so in a Vampire game, the Vampires had the numbers, etc.

      I have mixed feelings about Paradox and 5E. On one hand, I'm glad to see the IP actually doing stuff again. On the other hand, going back to Masquerade from Requiem feels like a retrograde on some level, so we'll see.

                      I started with Requiem shortly after it came out, having never really played any old World of Darkness. I only joined the online community fairly recently. 2013, if my join date wasn't shifted by the site change.

                      I found the pack of a metaplot refreshing, but some design decisions were strange, such as the frenzy upon encountering vampires. It's the kind of thing that could wipe out a party if you introduce them to the wrong antagonist and suffered some poor dice rolls. The Fog of Eternity, too, seemed sort of restraining.

                      A problem for me that only really surfaced later was the atmosphere. While new World of Darkness was meant to be its own system where the darkness was about mystery and Gothic Punk wasn't a 'thing,' the Requiem book still clung to a lot of Masquerade's conceits, where the World of Darkness is dark because more people are evil. P202 says, 'Exaggerate the negative in a real-world city to turn it into a hellhole,' in order to fit your location into the World of Darkness.

                      While I found the new bloodlines and the lack of internalism in vampire society a great advantage, so that a covenant was not the monolithic entity a clan could be in Masquerade, there weren't a lot of examples. The fiction trilogy that started things off was good, but it really only portrayed the Invictus and Carthians and a bit of the Lancea et Sanctum. The books could be a slog to read through, too, without examples or fiction to break up the monotonous drone of lecturing text. I've found the fiction of old World of Darkness really indicates a lot of things for people, though maybe I'm wrong, and the lack of it for new World of Darkness, as well as clinging artifacts from the past, really seemed to suggest you should just bring personalities, attitudes and such over. But then there wasn't too much support for that, either.

                          I like to think of classic wod as a massive wiki edit war. Now that being a bold statement. It was good for its time and really broke ground. and I've had many enjoyable games with it.

                          The first few ideas and books of maqs were small time and a lot of the focus was more local and you can understand motives and direction. It created a very solid set world mythology. That english readers could grasp. Then you got small one off ideas to explore more narrative space, but because of the previous set in stone narrative, you got a lot of chaffing and unwieldiness to open new ideas. I'm pointing to the Generation Stat. How can any other ideologically / presentable narrative be right when you make it such a part of the stat/mechanic block. There was no well maybe I'm wrong? There was, No you are wrong and you're getting slapped for not knowing.

                          Right when the Sabbot/Cam War became a thing into the thing it really just consumed the world and all the stories into this awkward cross country war plot. It was bad because it made good vampires by difference. Can you really say you're the bad guys when the other side murders families like its nothing? and when you've pulled the pin out of the grenade there was no going back on that ethical question. and made all the small plots irrelevant and silly. How can we argue and fight about feeding grounds when the barbarians are at the gates?

                          These two things really pulled the game from a focus of damnation and unliving to the heights of gonzo, its famous for.

                          And don't get me started on the publishing new powers to counter the previous powers you accidentally made. Requiem felt like a fresh start and a return to the beginnings of Masqu ideas. And it actually took a bunch of the really cool and interesting idea from oldworld and really let them flow. Look at the Sactum and the Circle, those ideas of monsters for chirst and monster we are. are taken directly from the sabbot, and because it's not tied to a war zone narrative, you can highlight good point and bad points. no one is the designated good guy or bad guy. I remember reading the Lasombra book and thinking, monsters for chirst. thats an interesting idea and could be played around with. And pagan vampires is kind of a trope all on it's own.

                          Because you see the sabbot had this very weird thing about it. It had more freedom to do what it wanted in terms of narrative space. So really you had like two different sabbots. The higher up ideals and the common murder hobos. and marring these factions together instantly made them the wrong bad ideas when really they were just different viewpoints to the common problem. But because of the narrative stone from before. you couldn't change the setting's narrative.

                          And then you had the massive wiki edits as I like to call them. each new writer had to have their own little spin on their pet clan/bloodline/sect. which felt like a big No you're wrong, because you don't have these books. I really liked a lot of the fiction that got run, the historical and family line plots were interesting and pretty good. but the meta plot just killed it.

                          With the nwod's focus on the local city, you can have each city run a little differently and play with the different themes. And it doesn't destroy the verisimilitude of the setting.

                          The biggest sell I've made for Nwod over old is simple. You can play old world style games in New Wod. But you can't play New wod in Old world. You still run sealed evil in the can. master manipulators. and good guy fighting the greater darkness. The one thing I will bring up is that; in an attempt to not gonzo the nwod game they made the splat a notch too weak. They really scaled the power back a bit much. But I know what they were going for. which is why I'm happy about 2ed really taking another crack at it.

                          Fog of ages was interesting. but I never played too much with it. but I understood what they were going for. You can't remember everything you done for forever. It's too much of a headache. but you definitely remember the important stuff. Blood Potency was a god sent. It massively decoupled the previous meta being completely right.

                          oldwod edition to edition are really just more of the same. I never felt like the mechanics really saw much of a difference. More the same, with a world update for tech sake.

                              I agree with the above, especially the part about the 'power levels' being different. You felt incredibly fragile as a vampire in nWoD 1e (e.g. a small group of mortals could beat the crap out of a centuries old vampire).                                   1e's fluff gave me the feeling that pretty much every city was off on its own, a beacon of relative safety in the wilderness for both humans and vampires, but vampires especially were cut off from all contact with other cities; the idea of traveling to another city seemed like a major undertaking. 2e has scaled back on this theme of urban isolation: cloistered domains are still possible, but they aren't necessarily the rule, and you get the sense that vampires who have some level of regional community or organization.

                                  The Fog of Ages was a creepy aspect of the game, and it was definitely interesting, but I wasn't personally a fan of it. To me, it seemed like it was the writers' way of explaining why the setting was so vague: "Why don't vampires have a perfect record of their history? Because they get amnesia or have their memories scrambled when they are torpid!" There are other ways to explain that stuff away, like conspiracy, or destruction of records, or death of witnesses, or stuff like that. That said, I like the idea of the Fog of Ages being a consequence of, say, going into torpor due to violence, and then it messes with memories in relation to the event that sent them into torpor: the further away from the incident, the easier it is to jog your memories; the closer to the event, the harder it is, and you may never get those memories back.

Etc. 2e was able to let go of some elements that existed more to differentiate Requiem from Masquerade than to serve Requiem per se. It's grown into completely its own setting that doesn't care what Masquerade thinks of it.
