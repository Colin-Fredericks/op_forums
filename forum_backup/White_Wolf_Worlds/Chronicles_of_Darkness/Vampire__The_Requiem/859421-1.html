        The Invictus will ruthlessly adapt whatever suits them in order to maintain the order and hierarchy of Kindred society. That could be neo-feudal traditions or it might be a corporate style setup, a criminal family's organization or a 'democratic' government whose functions happen in accordance with their desires.

        They're all about maintaining structure, but it doesn't matter what structure that is as long as it reinforces hierarchy. That's the key to understanding the Invictus in 2e.

        It's also important to think about the subtle ways that the Covenants were changed in 2e. 2e reflects a paradigm where not every Covenant in every city is vying to rule said city. The Sanctified as a philosophical position generally prefer someone else to rule while they advise them, for example. It both explains the relationship they have with the Invictus while also giving them a hook for interacting with the other covenants in a way that isn't just competitively trying to assert control.

                                            It's from the first edition core book, and not nearly that simple.

                                            The truth is, Kindred numbers vary from city to city. In most small cities, the proportion of undead to mortals tends to be relatively low: One vampire per 100,000 or more mortals is not uncommon. In large cities, the ratio is usually nearer one vampire per 50,000 mortals. In some cities that seem to draw the Kindred for whatever reason — cities such as New York, London, New Orleans and Chicago — the ratio can be substantially higher. Most Princes don’t particularly keep track. As long as the population doesn’t grow so high that people take notice of the predators among them, numbers don’t matter.

                                            If one were to take a worldwide average, the ratio is probably around the one-to-50,000 mark. It fluctuates so thoroughly from domain to domain that one should never assume that any given population corresponds to that figure.

                                                    So, this is how I think about vampire politics; your mileage will, of course, vary.

                                                    Vampires don't like to talk about it, but they're scared. Like, all the time. Which makes sense, when you think about it. Imagine a world in which, for something like half of every 24 hour period, it rains white hot fire from the sky and the only way to survive is to seal yourself away in a cold dark place and pray that the walls stay standing and nobody opens the front door. On top of that, the entire planet is populated by exactly two types of people: a huge population of fireproof idiots who would have every reason to want you dead if they had any idea you weren't one of them, and a tiny cadre of literally bloodthirsty cannibals who are one bad night away from turning into six-foot tall rabid dogs with super powers.

                                                    The worst part is, you know this is true because you've had nights like that yourself.

                                                    Vampires are like people in all the bad ways. Not only in the bad ways, but in all the bad ways. They can be petty and jealous and conniving and vengeful, and they get into fights for stupid reasons and hold grudges for something resembling eternity.This is only exacerbated by the fact that every vampire domain is basically one of those tiny villages where everybody knows everybody else, and when you do something stupid or embarrassing you can never, ever live it down. And it's not exactly easy to leave because, again, the world is on fire half the time.

                                                    Because of all this interpersonal bullshit, not to mention the risk of frenzying or full-on turning into a soulless monster, vampires can't be sure if they can trust one another not to sell them out to the herd. That joker you humiliated last week at the bar has mind control powers, and he could probably arrange for the vice squad to make a daylight raid on your haven that would put a swift end to your so-called life. He might do it, too; he's looking pretty corpsey these days, and he has a tendency to stare at you the way you've seen hyenas staring down a baby gazelle on nature shows.

                                                    The problem is, if that sort of thing starts happening often enough, pretty soon the mortal population, slow as they collectively are, will start to put the pieces together, and you can bet they're not going to like the picture they see. Somebody has to keep the undead on the straight and narrow, or at least come by every once in a while to smack them upside their collective heads and remind them to keep a low profile. And, of course, there will always be social climbers, people who want to have power and who like to be in charge; in fact, there's at least one whole clan full of them.

                                                    Naturally, this creates a whole lot of new problems, because wherever there's somebody in a position of authority, be it an elementary school teacher or a cop or the President of the United States, some people are going to resent that authority. And if you thought it was bad just hanging around with your fellow vampires, just wait until you're trying to tell them what to do.

                                                    So, what are the leaders of vampire-kind to do to protect them from the people they're trying to lead? Well, one solution is to take a page from monarchs and despots throughout history, and turn your rabble of questionably loyal underlings into a formal court. Make up rituals and rules of etiquette that make it inconvenient for people to plot or strike against you. Reward the biddable with affordable gifts or meaningless honors and titles. Make rules restricting the movement of the powerful in your domain, or which require them to attend certain formal functions that prevent them from spending too much time out of your sight.

                                                    Louis XIV and Tokugawa Ieyasu both used this strategy to great effect, and it may help explain some of the seemingly pointless posturing that goes on in many Kindred courts.

                                                            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                                            Potential new question: is the average Requiem setting overpopulated?
                                                            Define "average." Not only is the average number going to change based on city, but what each individual table will consider overpopulated. The simple truth of the matter is that this is going to depend on what your ST's and group's gaming needs are.

                                                            There's that old 1:10,000 number I always see tossed about unsourced, meaning that a fairly large city like San Diego has ~140 Kindred in it, while Portland can only have ~61. Sure, London can have nearly a thousand vampires under this metric, but that's at least thousand mortals getting fed upon per week; surely this goes noticed?
                                                            That number came from the 1e Requiem core, but it also came with a caveat - the distribution is not uniform. It was entirely plausable to have 15 Kindred total in the Denver area, but ten times that in New Orleans, and absolutely none in Washington DC. The writers quickly realized that people were using the number as a hard cap on what population "should" be, which was not the intent, and quickly eliminated it from all future games. The number had little meaning in 1e, and absolutely none in 2e.

                                                            I can't help but wonder if a greater scarcity of Kindred might be more realistic and more entertaining in play.
                                                            *shrugs* I had players that found a "scarcity" of vampires to be less realistic. All depends on the desires of your table and setting in question. What do you want out of the game?
