    Alternate Vitae Pool Idea [Crazy? Dumb? Ill-Thought Out?]

    01-15-2016, 08:45 PM
    Note: These rules solely apply to Kindred. Ghouls work like they do normally.

    Under this schema, you have a Vitae pool of 10. That's it; your Blood Potency doesn't increase it at all.

    • There's no limit on how much Vitae you can spend at once. Blow your entire pool if you want!
    • When you take your daily "nap", you lose Vitae in excess of your Blood Potency. So if you go to bed with 7 Vitae in your pool and only have Blood Potency 3, you lose all but 3 points before you wake up. In other words, you'd wake up with 2 Vitae (4 lost from falling asleep, 1 spent to wake up). This is referred to as Sleep Burn.
    • You may choose to spend Vitae as Sleep Burn in excess to the amount you lose due to getting some shut-eye.
    • You may heal one level of Aggravated damage for every 5 Vitae lost to Sleep Burn.
    • Revenants effectively have Blood Potency 0. They only wake up from Daysleep if someone feeds them blood or Vitae.

    Now, what does this change?

    • "Young" Kindred are going to starving and vulnerable when they wake up. Expect to start every night with a feeding scene.
    • As Kindred gain more Blood Potency, they have the luxury of being able to go longer without eating.
    • Herds and Feeding Grounds probably become all the more vital - an Elder letting you use one of their Feeding Grounds or giving you someone to chow on when you wake up is going to be one hell of a bargaining chip.
    • Personal power is going to be more about skill and less about having a huge Vitae Pool to toss around. A lot of "Elder Devotions" become accessible to the common Kindred.
    • Kindred with low Blood Potency have a strong incentive to run around and get things done before they essentially waste Vitae. If they have a Herd or a Feeding Ground, expect to see a lot of conspicuous spending.
    • Probably a bunch of other things! This hasn't been playtested yet (who am I kidding? Nothing I write is playtested!), so there's probably some really weird extra thing that pops up when you do this.

    I have decided, after some thought, that I don't really feel happy on these forums. I might decide to come back to post. Who knows - but right now, I'm gone.

    So good bye, good luck, and have a nice day.

      Letting you blow all your Vitae in a single round definitely has some unbalancing risks. If you can equal the expenditure of any Elder out there (who has the same blood pool and expenditure rate) then you'd better hope the Elder in question has other ways of defending themselves, because getting caught off guard by one bad tempered neonate who suddenly dumps 20 extra dice into that sudden attack may wreck their day (and if multiple people get involved and are spending it all at once, it's all but literally a bloodbath).

      Additionally, the Sleep Burn thing kind of conflicts with at least one bloodline, the Neglatu, who have this sort of issue as their bloodline Bane.

      Revenants in this scheme are also screwed; they don't even get a single chance to wake up, which means you have to basically keep them as blood-fed pets (and feed them a LOT if you want to keep them viable at all, which cuts into your own feeding if you have limited resources).

                  As far as I can tell... I don't think this accomplished the intended goal of "flattening" the setting. Neonates have zero power now. This is now "Vampire: the US Student Loan Crisis taken to 11." except instead of just endless debt, there's increased risk of murdering anyone you care about, death, and insanity.

                  In the default, Neonates aren't entrenched, but have much greater flexibility for it. This makes them dangerous, because it's harder for the Elders to control them. And few vampires are so powerful, that they can realistically rely on their personal power to avoid getting offed in a rebellion if they're not careful with their powerful.

                  With this... Neonates have jack-shit. They wake up with zero vitae until they hit BP 2. If they're lucky, they survive long enough to deal with their Humanity dropping like a rock, and the giant debt they've racked up to other vampires. Most will be killed after one too many frenzies. Or maybe locked up for some amount of time until their BP goes up.

                  This doesn't really do much for evening the field. They're still going to be way behind with Attributes, Skills, Merits, Disciplines, and etc. "I can drop 10 BP for +20 dice to attack!" vs. "I can drop 1 BP and use Celerity so you miss... and I still have 9 BP left to put you down." Not to mention that young vampires aren't really going to be able to maintain Ghouls easily, so Elders will come to most situations with personal power and numbers now.

                  As is, I wouldn't want to play this. It's like Mage the Ascension, and pretty much needing to drop at least 1 into Avatar, and raise Arete to 2, or be insanely weak at start. There is pretty much zero reason to not drop Merit points on starting with BP 2 or 3 so you can actually expect to survive more than a couple sessions.
