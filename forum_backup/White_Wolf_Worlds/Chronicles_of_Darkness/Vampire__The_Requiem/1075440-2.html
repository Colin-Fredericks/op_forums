                                                    When D&D comes out with a new edition of the game the approach is to reinvent the game. The ST system did that with the old and new Worlds of Darkness. All the editions after that shift are revisionist in nature. Lots of games create new editions by revising the current edition. A revision can be significant enough to be considered unique.

                                                    Spanish, French, and Italian are all derived from local dialects of Latin. They became separate languages long ago. Italian spoken in Northern Italy is different enough from the Italian spoken in Southern Italy that sometimes Italians can't understand Italian. On the other hand, British English and American English are not distinct enough to have any real issues. Somebody might say something odd every now and then.

                                                    Romance Languages. Italian. English. Reinvention. Revision. Edition Revised or N.X Edition. It is possible to complain about any changes but comparisons begin to fall apart the more extensive the changes become. Since the COD editions are progressive revisions in nature, you would not find much more than an odd rule or two to go backwards if you already are at 2e. Going backwards would be the accurate way to describe switching from 2e to 1e. Going from Requiem to Masquerade is sideways. Feel free to pick the lane of your choice.

                                                        While both games have grown apart from each other this part still holds true, having recently gotten to re-read masquerade is like all roads lead to masquerade in many design choices that still carry from them, specially those plain just dont work with the current games.

                                                        Yes both games are different but reading OWoD and NWoD/Chrod is an interesting though exercise as you can see many of the hows and whys of many things almost in a anthropological way. If you see something that dont quite work in requiem, forsaken or mage chances are is a holdout from the previous edition that works quite well-ish there but just feels odd in the NWOD.

                                                        Egregious examples that come up in my mind are:

                                                        Gargoyles from Requiem whose name makes little sense for what are and should have being name "golems" but instead they got an ill fitted name for their Masquerade counter parts.

                                                        Forsaken has the term gifts that even the line has a section in Lore of the forsaken explaining how that name makes no sense in the context of Forsaken.

                                                        And where characters start in chargen from all games that only truly made sense in Masquerade but was used and re-used in all lines to the detriment of all of them.

                                                        Yes they are different lines/games with both pros and cons but the comparison would still be made because they are similar games made by follow up companies and sometimes even the developers continue that line of thinking.

                                                        ​
                                                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                        Werewolves were nerfed compared to what? 1e was the first edition, there's no previous version for them to have been nerfed from.
                                                        I would argue they were nerfed compared to their own fluff in 1E. Like the authors had Apocalypse power level in their heads when writing the fiction.

                                                        As for the 1st edition vs 2nd, as others have point out there is the issue of 1st edition having more books that require no conversion while second does need a little, not much mind you but it does have a problem.

                                                        Also 2nd in general has a certain power creep in their splats which some people might have problems with and others dont. But 1st edition vampire were much weaker at the start than their 2nd edition counterparts. And a well made human could stand a chance against them one on one to a certain level.

                                                        And finally is the issue of freedom vs vision. 2nd edition mechanics gear towards A style of play while 1st edition have a bunch of mechanics that clumsily carry the game with little to no synergy between them. The latter makes ignoring certain mechanics for an alternative style of play easier while 2nd edition´s mechanics interface with its themes which at the same time makes it difficult to play it when you wanna play vampire but dont quite buy into those themes.

                                                        Example 2nd puts lots of mechanical emphasis in Vampires dealing with their human lives, some people like to play that. Others like to play characters completely immersed in their supernatural lives without it being a "thing" as dealing with human life, friends or family is boring to them or their foregone conclusion is not something they care about. 2nd edition lets you play that character but it makes it a "thing" with consequences while in 1st it didnt.

                                                        So someone that would like to play a vampire, like the setting but dont necessarily buy into 2nd edition themes could choose 1st edition.
