          As far as I know there's little official material for pre-Christianisation Scandinavian Vampire, but I'll compile what little I do know.

          First off, vampire vikings would be incredibly rare due to the difficulties of going viking on ships without roofs with a sun allergy. Any Norse Kindred who wish to go raiding would have to do so on land.

          The Mekhet Bloodline of the Norvegi (detailed in the Mekhet clan book) is said to have come from a Norse Clan called Grettir. There's a few homebrewed versions of them on this forum. Bringing them up because they were known for "inbreeding" and would thus form their own social structure, akin to a one Clan Covenant. As the name implies they'd mostlybe found in Norway.
          As a side note, another lost Clan that may be interesting is the Mikhaili of the Siberian wastes (Half-Damned). Canonically way too eastern to be relevant, but you could just move them to Northern Kievan Rus'.

          It's likely there were some proto-Circle Covenant, and their magic would be known as Seiðr (or possibly Blóðseiðr). They would likely venerate Odin/Óðinn and/or Freyja as the main deities connected to Seiðr. Seiðr was considered a very feminine practice and taboo for men, but that's not to say men didn't practice it. Especially in a dead society where taboos may lose their meanings.
          Female members could be known as (singular/plural) vǫlva/vǫlur, seiðkona/seiðkonur or even dís/dísir. Male members could be known as seiðmaðr/seiðmenn. But since it was so strongly associated with femininity, it's fully possible male members still used traditionally female titles.

          If there's a more authoritarian Covenant (from hereon known as the Invictus-look-a-likes) they'd have to cover a larger area in order to have any power (considering the low population density and tiny domains), and thus they'd regularly hold Þing, a type of parlamentary assembly of all the regional rulers and elders. During þing a lot of various disputes were settled (as to not create chaos in society) including feuds between clans, inheritances, conflicting claims, and even legal cases if they couldn't be settled locally. Laws were written and chieftains elected. All of this would be of massive use for Kindred society.

          Priests (goði/goðar, gyðja/gyðjur) would likely hold important roles even in Kindred society, but if they have a organisation it'd probably be as a subgroup of the Invictus-look-a-likes due to the lack of numbers.

          If there is a grouping that chooses who gets to Embraced or not, they're definitely called Valkyries/Valkyrjur. Choosers of the slain, the mythological valkyries would both chose among the fallen from a battle who'd get to go to Valhall and join the einherjar, as well as chose who'd to die in battle. If they exist in your game they'd most likely be a subgroup of either of the above suggested Covenants. If in the proto-Circle, then they'd be the fighting force as well as granting the larger group political power. If in the Invictus-look-a-likes, they'd represent some of the religious nature of the þing together with the priests.

          And as mentioned, they wouldn't be Covenants in the modern sense. They'd lack the unity of the Camarilla and the modern Covenants, and few would consider them rival organisations as they all fulfil different roles in Kindred society. The whole division of Covenants in modern nights have always felt partly artificial to me, and doubly so when populations are small and the level of organisation is low. The Invictus-look-a-likes would not even be considered an organisation by Kindred. It would just be society. Or possibly a higher social stratum. Kinda like how the actual Invictus should've been seen during most of medieval Europe.

              I imagine them divided more along clan lines than covenants.

              Grettir
              Currently the most common vampires in Norway and also much of Sweden. They don't have their own organisation. Rather, they associate with their own living families, embracing within. They're still developing a concept of "vampires" beyond themselves. They know that there are other dead beings that take blood. Some peculiar commonalities that they've noticed between more than one kind, are the presence of animal servants or allies and biting to extract blood.

              Draugr
              Human sorcerers have developed a means of granting immortality using blood necromancy. They have no way to control these draugr and the subject has to be alive during most of ritual, so it's used for trusted associates and loved ones. They commonly exist as pairs of necromancers and draugr. If they can, the necromancers often arrange for the ritual to be performed on themselves in return, but they don't always find a suitable donor to sacrifice. The ritual appalls those other vampires who know of it, so draugr avoid them. Few are left in Scandinavia, but they've managed to colonise Iceland before any other clan.

              Vikings
              The southern vampires have a lot of names, but they're mostly known by Grettir and Draugr for their aggression, their ability to take the forms of animals and their apparent ease of travel. They're in settlements all over Denmark and are spreading across the coasts and beyond. In fact, they're much more cosmopolitan than the northern clans, having encountered more exotic vampires and cultures. While their relations with other vampires of Scandinavia have been mutually hostile, they're integrating into multiple clan societies, usually to be hired as soldiers, mercenaries and runners, but sometimes to share occult lore for use against the great Lancea et Sanctum cult. Recently, some daring Grettir and Draugr have attempted to follow their example.

              [Nosferatu]
              Some Nosferatu have been found, but most of them are sireless and not identified as single type of monster. They're freaks of nature that usually either get destroyed as soon as they're discovered or escape to places where kin understand and accept them.

              Others
              There are wild speculations about other undead and blood drinkers in the eastern Baltic. Even Gangrel aren't sure if they're vampires or what kind magical powers they hold. The most common rumour is that they hide behind fanatical, human followers. If there are vampires in the far north, they're even less understood.

                  I put some thought to this recently, and came up with some rudimentary covenant seeds.

                  Firstly, a Legion of the Dead-like order of soldiers, the Barrow Guard. They are the housecarls and gravekeepers of vampire society, defending tombs in both the figurative (the households of vampire lords) and the literal (actual tombs) senses. Of course, they expect payment for their services, and in the case of actual burial sites, they see no issue with robbing the graves themselves. As a result, their covenant benefit would be Grave Goods: cursed swords and shields, haunted silver, and other bleak relics.

                  Secondly, a lordly covenant of lawmen, priests, and sorcerers. They would have aspects of the Invictus, the Circle of the Crone, and even the Ordo Dracul, serving as both the keepers of eclectic holy places (Wyrm's Nests, in modern parlance) and the mediators in disputes among the dead. Their blood sorcery, one of the roots of modern Cruác, would be focused on sealing oaths and drawing power from the sacrifice of others.

                  Finally, I considered a Christian covenant that would have formed independently of the Lancea et Sanctum, the Red Faith. Arisen from Christianized Scandinavians, without continental vampire influence, they are a cult of ecstatics and perpetual martyrs. Given to unhealthy, incestuous blood bonds and great acts of self-sacrifice, they would be a short-lived covenant that is ultimately replaced by the more stable Spear. Their covenant benefit, if anything, would be a unique kind of semi-controlled frenzy that they claim to be born out of faith and the terrible love of God.

                                                      I've been running an early Viking Era chronicle for a few months now. Current in-game year is about 815 CE, in central Norway, and the crew's all wound up in a plot by a Strix-possessed renegade L+S elder to unleash a draugr plague on the northern lands as a seed-bed for a wave of vampire doom over the Invictus and L+S realms in Europe.
                                                      The way I see it, at this point in most of Scandinavia there really isn't the kind of population base to support much in the way of complex covenant vampire politics. I've got two proto-covenant/clans, the Mound-dwellers (Nos) and the Gravewolves (Gangrel). The Mound-dwellers are the remains of a much older network of Nos necromancer-witches that used to extend across most of Neolithic Europe, living off of blood offerings to the dead and extorted human sacrifices in exchange for ghost-wrangling and divination and that kind of death priestess/volva schtick. They live in the old gravemounds and howes, hence the name.
                                                      The Gravewolves are pretty much just a really loose network of sire-and-childe scavengers and woods-monsters that occasionally get together for moots and brag-fests or to fight over hunting rights to specific areas. A lot of them spend most of their time in animal form once they hit Protean 3. The Mound-dwellers sometimes try to manipulate them into lending some muscle to some scheme or other, but it rarely works out for long.
                                                      The Legio Mortuum have been sending recruiters up here to bring skilled warriors south for a few centuries, but the Invictus are mostly too involved in wrangling over post-Charlemagne political squabbles to care about some broke northerners, and the L+S are mostly focused on England and Ireland. Once the Viking raids kick into high gear in a few years, that'll change.

                                                          As others said, there isn't much lore on vampire vikings, but there is a bit.

                                                          The vampires back then were collectively called the draugar, not to be confused with a Humanity 0 vampire. They're also called aftergangr by the werewolf vikings.
                                                          They were few and far between, especially in Scandinavia. York in England has a domain ran by the Lancea et Sanctum, but most settlements can barely support one coterie. Thus, there aren't any Scandinavian covenants to speak of. Perhaps some Norwegian and Danish coteries come together to perform blood sorcery rituals, those vampires could be an early incarnation of the Circle of the Crone. But that's about it.
                                                          Most vampires however, can't go on raids, thus they aren't vikings. Instead, they turn human vikings into their ghouls and send them plundering in their name instead.

                                                          This information and more can be found in Dark Eras, under the 'Wolf and the Raven' chapter.
