      The honest answer is: it depends on the domain.

      Each domain is a potentially unique entity in both composition with regards to Clan and Covenant, and governing structure between them all.

      For instance, Tokyo's only example of one of the five conventional Covenants is the Lancea et Sanctum. Everyone else is either unaligned (a minority) or part of one of three vampire-run Zaibatsu corporations (the majority).

      Swansea, Wales on the other hand sorta has Covenants, but they are mostly irrelevant. Everything is controlled by a Clan Council made up of elders from each of Swansea's four Clans. I say four because they exiled and outlawed the Ventrue decades ago due to Ventrue association with oppressive industrial interests that the mostly working-class vampires of the other clans were not fond of.

      My best general advice would be to refer to the section starting on page 65 titled 'The City as State' in the Vampire the Requiem: Second Edition corebook. After digesting what you find there I would look at the example domains in Chapter 6, and then if none of those fit what you like you can take the material from them and the overview given under The City as State and come up with your own way of doing things.

              These stereotypes are very 1st edition. The Covenants have changed somewhat as of 2nd edition.

              The Lancea et Sanctum (now featuring grammatically correct Latin!) do retain the "Damned by God to tempt the faithful" aspect, but have also picked up a role as historians and lorekeepers. Now that the Fog of Ages is no longer a major concern, they have to work much harder to make sure their version of history is the one that people remember. They seek out and hoard historical documents, making the ones that support their doctrine available and well-known, and keeping those that do not under lock and key.

              The Ordo Dracul have a renewed emphasis on their defiance of the curse. Whether an individual Dragon believes the Kindred are damned by God, afflicted with some kind of supernatural virus, or something else entirely, they endeavor not just to cure themselves of the curse, but to turn it into a blessing. Not to rid themselves of the weaknesses inherent in the vampiric condition, but to use those weaknesses to their advantage.

              The Circle of the Crone have a much more specific identity now, with clearly defined origins a little under 200 years ago in Scotland and Ireland when the Lancea et Sanctum was ruthlessly persecuting pagan coteries. They banded together for mutual safety, fighting back against their oppressors. Tonight, they stand not so much as a single organized religion, but as a coalition of spiritual vampires who do not conform to the dominant Lancea et Sanctum ideology.

              The Invictus give no shits for tradition or the old ways. The Camarilla fell because it refused to adapt to changing times, and the Invictus, while they consider themselves the spiritual successors to the Camarilla, are not going to make the same mistake again. They are about power and control, by any means - old or new. They have also picked up some new themes as the self-styled enforcers of the Masquerade. One of their new nicknames is "the conspiracy of silence." They're the ones who make the people who know too much disappear, and the people who say too much public examples.

              The Carthian Movement is analogous to the Circle of the Crone in that they are not one single political ideology, but more of a support network of political dissidents of various leanings united against the dominant Invictus power. They may not all agree on what to do once that power is overthrown, but to The Firebrands, that's secondary. What matters most is what's right here, right now, and right now, the Kindred need a revolution.

                  It really does depend, but in first edition there were a lot of options made available with the covenant books. I don't have them all and I don't remember everything from the ones I have read so this may be not entirely correct.

                  I think the Ordo Dracul went into the most detail. New members usually start off all alone but at some point are ideally picked by a mentor, at which point they become a more vital member of the clan. The mentor and apprentice relationship seems pretty tight, and something they take seriously, its really good for storytelling opportunities as well. An Ordo Dracul Prince is called a Master. They are divided into, primarily, three groups. The Sworn of the Dying Light, who are the scholars, the Sworn of the Axe, who are the warriors, and the Sworn of the Mysteries, who act as diplomats to the other covenants and politicians in their own. There are a couple other types of Sworn, but I think they're considered dead or heretical in modern nights. The Sworn of the Locust is one. The Ordo also have these long, encoded titles that tell other people in the know how many coils they've mastered and which ones they're the best at. There's also this one member of the covenant who knows all the secrets, I think they're called the Kogaion. They know what everyone is studying, the locations of all the wyrms nests and members of the covenant in the area. Because of this they aren't allowed to show up at big meetings where the heads of the Sworn are, because losing the Kogaion and the heads would be devastating to the covenant.

                  I wasn't sure if this was how their politics worked or just how they loosely organized, but I think the Circle of the Crone based themselves off of archetypes. I think the leader of the Circle of the Crone is called a Heirophant. I forget all the archetypes, but there's the Hero, who takes up the heroes of old archetype where they're not necessarily a good person but more of a loner who does what needs to be done. The Father, which is an evolution of the Hero, who has learned from their experiences and settled down to be a mentor and advisor to the young. The Scarlet Woman, who sort of stands outside their normal social statuses and cleaves to her own ethical and philosophical beliefs. The Fool, who is sort of like the trickster, they poke at rules and rulemakers, sort of acting as a devil's advocate so crones remember to see other points of view. And the Maiden, who is the 'virgin,' in that she has not killed a person, she's still untouched by that part of vampire life. There's more that I can't recall offhand, but it was an interesting read. There was another position that was sort of like the Ordo's Kogaion, the Rex Nemorensis, who are the keepers of forbidden knowledge.

                  Other than that, the 'standard' vampire city has a Prince, who rules, and a Sheriff, who enforces the law. Ideally they're neutral, more loyal to the law than the Prince but how likely is that? There's a primogen council, who are supposed to be the advisors to the Prince though they may not share the Prince's goals. Some might be given jobs similar to a Minister in some government systems, such as Norris in Chicago,who acted as the Prince's spymaster. Alternatively they could be the true power behind a puppet Prince. Clans might have a priscus, a particularly powerful member of a clan that may sort of act as leader or spokesperson for it, I think that would be traditionally separate from covenant activities but I guess a priscus in a covenant would be a good way to get a lot of similar vampires in it. There's a Master of Elysium, who runs, organized and maintains the Elysium. Harpies, who are sort of like gossip mongers, they get the word out about orders from up top, but can also be used for spreading rumors or gathering information, they tend to have their finger on the pulse of vampire society, again, ideally. The Prince and the primogen might also have Hounds, who are sort of like, I guess, adventurers? They do the gruntwork, whether it's roughing up people, spying, spreading lies and staking liars, they tend to be loyal to their master and the rest of society might not know if they're Hounds or who they're working for.

                  I've probably left out a lot and got a lot wrong but I'm sure someone will add and correct things.

                      Specific titles and duties time! In all cases, keep in mind that these are only examples of ways a Covenant might (stereo)typically be organized, and may be completely different in any given domain.

                      Carthian Movement:
                      The Prefect is the official representative of The Revolution. Usually elected, the Prefect's job is to play nice with the other covenants, present a respectable face for the movement so the other members will at least be tolerated within the city, and to act as a sort of figurehead so the other factions have a face they associate with the Movement and feel safer for it.

                      The Myrmadon is the arbiter of the internal disputes that inevitably arise within a covenant of Kindred who basically only work together because they all agree the current system doesn't work. When you've got a Marxist member and a Neo-Platonic member at each other's throats, it's the Myrmadon's job to break it up.

                      That's about as much official structure as most Carthians are comfortable with, though some domains where the local Carthians' ideals are unusually closely aligned may have more. Look to human political structures for examples.


                      Circle of the Crone:
                      The Heirophant or The High Priestess is the lead ritualist. He or she is the spiritual leader of the local Acolytes, offering guidance to those who seek it, and presiding over most Cruac rituals.

                      The Chorus is a catch-all term for new initiates who have not been taught the secrets of Cruac.

                      Rex Nemoranses is an outdated title that rarely ever sees use in modern nights. Historically, it was held by the eldest member, and it was this Kindred's job to act as an un-living repository of forbidden knowledge.

                      Like the Carthian Movement, the Circle of the Crone is big on personal freedom and not so big on structure and organization, so this is the extent of their "official" titles. However, they often hold ideas of philosophical roles. They have no associated duties, and are not held by individuals, they are more like descriptors of where a member is at in her un-life. Every member therefore fits into one philosophical role or another. These vary greatly from one domain to the next depending on local beliefs and customs, but one common philosophical structure is the family roles.

                      The Fool is an Acolyte, typically male, who is unenlightened in the ways of the Circle. Their naivete is valued, as it offers a certain insight that the jaded elder members might miss. Fools are occasionally called upon to come up with ideas so crazy they just might work.

                      The Maiden or The Virgin is the typically female counterpart to the Fool, often young, and always ignorant of any Cruac. They are valued for their purity, as Cruac takes its toll on a ritualist's Humanity. Virgins are often called upon to act as Touchstones for degenerating ritualists. (Ok, ok, the Touchstone mechanic didn't actually exist yet when the book this is from was written, but they serve as anchors to keep the low-Humanity members from going off the deep end, and that's exactly what Touchstones do.)

                      The Hero is the spiritually adolescent role aligned with typically masculine ideals. Heroes go on quests to prove themselves and to earn favor. They're the go-to foot soldiers of the Covenant.

                      The Whore is the typically feminine counterpart to the Hero. Whores challenge tradition and make their own way. They are seen as a much-needed counterpoint to the elders who can get stuck in their ways.

                      The Father is a spiritually mature masculine figure who guides less mature Acolytes, typically through discipline.

                      The Mother is a spiritually mature feminine figure who guides through praise.

                      The Hermit is an elder member, usually male and always of high enough Blood Potency that they must feed on vampires, and was historically associated with the role of Rex Nemoranses. Typically keep to themselves and are sought out when you need advice the Mother can't or won't give. Offer him your Vitae and he might be willing to tell you things you didn't know you didn't want to know.

                      The Croneis an elder member, usually female and always too potent to sustain herself on humans, exists to destroy. She consumes those who have transgressed against The Circle.


                      Invictus:
                      The Invictus have a TON of titles to denote all sorts of things, from esteem to rank to knightly orders to dynasties to anything else you might want an epithet added to your name for. But as for titles that come with specific duties, they largely conform to the standard positions of power familiar to anyone who's played Masquerade.

                      The Prince is the guy in charge.

                      The Seneshal is the Prince's second in command.

                      The Primogen are a council of advisors to the Prince, often but not always representatives of each prominent covenant within the domain.

                      The Prisci (plural: Priscus) are the collective heads of each prominent Clan within the domain.

                      The Harpy is a social leader and is sometimes called upon as a propagandist.

                      The Sheriff is the guy in charge of enforcing the Prince's laws.

                      The Hound or The Scourge is the Sheriff's second in command.


                      Lancea et Sanctum:

                      The Bishop is the religious leader of the local Sanctified.

                      The Archbishop is the title used by a Sanctified who rules over a domain (instead of Prince).

                      The Cardinal is an extremely rare title used when the Bishop and the Archbishop are the same person.

                      Priests perform Theban Sorcery


                      Ordo Dracul:
                      The Ordo Dracul have a ridiculous number of titles and positions, but unlike the Invictus who seem to do it to promote their own self-importance, the Ordo Dracul is just deliberately trying to be as unnecessarily complicated as possible. They're like one of those secret societies like Skull and Bones that pride themselves on being mysterious and arcane. I won't go into all of the roles here, but it basically boils down to:

                      The Sworn of the Axe: are the soldiers and militants.

                      The Sworn of the Dying Light: are the scientists and mystics.

                      The Sworn of Mysteries: are the judges and politicians.

                      Each Sworn has its own system of roles and hierarchies, and each group acts in relative autonomy from the others, though the Sworn of Mysteries are generally the arbiters when conflicts between the different Sworn do arise.

                      The Kogaion: is the most important and respected position, but it's almost more of a punishment than an honor to be named Kogaion. They're in charge of mapping leylines, tracking and protecting Wyrm's Nests (locations of mystical power that are of interest to the Order), and generally keeping the Covenant's secrets. They tend to be driven mad from the terrible things they're expected to know and to keep secret.


                      They're all pretty basic descriptions, and I may have missed a few titles. If you want to know more, the Covenant books are full of this stuff, and most of it is still relevant to second edition.
