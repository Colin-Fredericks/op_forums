                        No, I very much am not. They can eat the flesh of living humans and wolves. This requires consuming their blood, too. They don't commit this sin by cooking human steaks, nor can they magically eat the flesh without also gulping down mouthfuls of blood. It's the way biology and predation meet through the vector of lupine jaws. I didn't mean to imply they get Essence from blood, but this isn't ambiguous. The very act of cannibalizing humans and wolves for Essence demands that they consume blood in large quantities as well.

                        Werewolves don't consume blood and flesh without directly meaning to, except for minute amounts of blood that they probably swallow while biting—not enough to matter for Vitae purposes. Vampires explicitly don't bleed like living creatures, and even with the blush of life active they bleed, but don't lose Vitae like it's blood. This is clearly stated in the book. Drinking Kindred Vitae is also a distinct act. It doesn't happen by accident, even if a werewolf bites a vampire. The vampire might force the issue by attempting to pour her Vitae down the werewolf's throat, such as in a grapple, but this requires an insane amount of risk for almost zero unlife-saving payoff. Vinculum isn't going to stop the werewolf from killing the vampire, and aside from a very specific power isn't going to be established fast enough to make a difference. The werewolf is tearing your face off now. Maybe she'll feel a Vitae-influenced guilt later, but that doesn't matter. The vampire's dead, the blood bond means nothing, and life goes on. Except for the vampire, of course.

                        Does that clear up my stance on this matter? My earlier post inadvertently suggested that I meant you could gain Essence from blood alone; clarity is my burden (more so since I'm working on Changeling), so here I have attempted to clarify my thoughts.

                                    Originally posted by Rani Neferet View Post
                                    So what your saying then is vampires can't extract vitae at all, because obviously if they don't bleed they can't give vitae? So dripping blood and such doesn't work? Spiking a drink with vitae within seconds of it being sipped? A few drops on the tongue?
                                    I'm saying that extracting Vitae by will or by force is a conscious act. It doesn't happen just by biting a vampire, at least for werewolves. They don't actively choose to gulp down flesh and blood with every bite. Vampires don't bleed Vitae and don't automatically force their Vitae down the gullet of everything that bites them. When a vampire bites someone, whether the initial bite attack or using the Feed move, they do drain Vitae, because it's what they do. In fact, the book actually says that mortals take one lethal damage per Vitae taken with the Feed move, or from the 1 Vitae taken automatically (which one assumes is voluntary) when they bite someone. The same section calls out werewolves as being immune to the effects of the bite where the word "mortal" is used, so you could even argue that they don't take lethal damage from being drained of Vitae.

                                    Either way, the stuff you're talking about is all a conscious decision, or the result of same, like forcing someone to drink. It doesn't happen automatically on a bite. Werewolves don't automatically ingest Vitae, even if the vampire would want them to. The book outright says that they don't bleed Vitae like they do normal blood, even with blush of life active. Sure, the vampire can go into the fight intending to bleed. It doesn't mean it automatically drains her Vitae to go into the werewolf when the latter bites her. The books seem to support this. You can't automatically blood bond a werewolf biting you unless the ST rules otherwise.

                                    I'm pretty sure most werewolves, if they know they're biting a vampire, aren't going to want anything to do with the stolen, mystical blood in their veins. If they bite, they won't be doing so with the intent to ingest anything.

That's not an insignificant investment for a vampire for putting a bond on someone and THEN still needing to spend a few turns to remove their Doors and talk a single werewolf down while in the middle of combat. With the werewolf's pack right there, watching, and the Cahalith likely knowing something funky going on and telling the entire pack to murder-kill the vampire messing with one of them.

                                                Yes, the blood bond has a "don't attack me!" clause, but that works through Humanity / Integrity breaking points. Werewolves, at worst, would suffer a breaking point towards Spirit from ignoring that compulsion. Which, frankly, is par for the course with Werewolves. It happens all the time and, honestly, you can balance that out by going out and eating pizza after the fact. At best, nothing happens.

                                                I think having the bond and addiction is dramatic, because it adds a certain level of stories after the fact. Think about it. Werewolves addicted to vitae after killing a cotorie. This is going to push the pack into hunting down more vampires, because of the addictive rush of tearing into vampires. Its a dangerous, double edged blade that threatens a wolf's balance, which is important to Werewolf stories in the first place. More drama is good. But, in mid combat, its at best a desperate act that has very little chance of success.

                                                Make no mistake, werewolves are probably the physically most competent of all supernaturals in the CoD right now. Killing on the hunt is their specialty. But mind control, drugging, and infecting others is a vampire's forte.
