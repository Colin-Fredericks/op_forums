    A Few Questions about VtR 1e

    04-12-2015, 12:22 AM
    I'm fairly new to WoD in general and am currently running my first Chronicle, which mixes the vast majority of the supernatural factions. Most of the game lines aren't giving me a problem because all of the characters in those games are starting off as brand-new characters. But my co-Storyteller is trying to make a recurring Vampire NPC for our Chronicle that is basically supposed to be a sort of mentor to the party. The co-ST claims to know VtR really well, but he seems to be pulling a lot of things out of nowhere that the core VtR 1e book doesn't have any info on. If anyone has any info on these topics, I thank you in advance for your help! Book and page number references are helpful, but not totally required.

    First Question: The recurring NPC was Embraced 200 years ago (1815 AD) and was 22 years old when it happened. What kind of experience should this character have? Looking at the default options on page 92 of VtR (0, 35, 75, 120, 180-300+), I suggested 120exp because I didn't want this NPC to be to TOO godly. But I'm wondering if that would be accurate, or if we should be looking at more/less EXP.

    Second Question: In VtR 1e, are there any rules regarding a female Vampire's ability to become pregnant and/or a male Vampire's ability to impregnate either a female Vampire or a mortal woman? The reason I ask this is because the co-ST is also asking me to help him write the NPC's history, which involves a now-defunct bloodline of Vampires who gave birth to children and then Embraced their own children at a specific age (21-25 seems to be the general area). This practice stopped a couple generations before the NPC came along, but the question of whether or not that bloodline was possible is something I'd like answered before I agree to its canonicity within the lore of the Chronicle while holding true to my statement of "as few house-rules as possible."

    Third Question: This is one of the big ones here... What kind of ramifications would the total annihilation of a single clan have on Kindred society? The NPC my co-ST is trying to make is basically an advanced version of his PC from the Chronicle he played in several years ago, in which his ST seems to have enacted a bunch of house-rules that have severely confused my co-ST. In that Chronicle, the PC was the only surviving member of the Daeva clan. Basically for no reason at all, other than the ST thought it'd be a good idea to make that one PC the last of his clan. And he's remained adamant that the NPC should still be the only Daeva left in the whole world. But since I haven't touched on Vampires within my Chronicle yet, I want to get an idea of what would be going on in their society following the annihilation of the Daeva clan. I have a feeling that this kind of thing isn't covered in official rulebooks, though...

    Fourth Question: This NPC's concept is a high-class lawyer with an extremely high win ratio. The co-ST made sure to set up this character with max Manipulation, Intimidation, and Persuasion in order to get whatever verdict he's gunning for (because he can be a defense lawyer OR a prosecutor, so his goals switch often) pretty much every time. He did increase his Blood Potency to 2, but didn't buy any Rituals or Devotions. Should he have? Or did he make the right choice by ignoring them?

    I think that's all of my questions for the time being. Thank you in advance to anyone who can help me!

      Hi SilverOkami,
      Firstly welcome to the forum And good luck with your first Chronicle! I'll be happy to try and answer your questions, but I'll bow down to those with a superior knowledge of VtR:

      1. I suppose the amount of exp he has should depend on his background - how active has the NPC been over those 200 years? has he spent any time in torpor? - that sort of thing more than anything. After all, a player character gets exp quickly as s/he's very active each night, whereas an elder who doesn't get involved much may not be as experienced. At the end of the day it's up to you as the ST.

      If you want some mechanics though, the sourcebook Ancient Mysterious (which deals with elders) gives ways to work it out. I don't know how much I can put up from the book, but you basically decide for each decade if the character has been in torpor for a while (The Sleeper), passive where they haven't had much to do (The Spider) or has been actively pursuing his Requiem (The Wolf). For every decade the NPC has acted in one of those, they get either 0, 10 or 15 Exp respectively. That could mean up to 300 experience admittedly, but you make him buy the Blood Potency 5 he would be (1 started, +4 for the 200 hundred years of being awake), which works out as 40 points. As you mentioned in your fourth question that he is only Blood Potency 2, roughly 75-100 of those years would have been spent in torpor to lower it down to 2, or down to 1 giving him roughly 50 years before the start of the Chronicle to establish himself.

      2. As far as I know, basically vampires cannot become pregnant or impregnate a woman. After all, they are essentially dead bodies mystically animated through Vitae. I suppose you could make a magical rite of the Lancea Sanctum or the Crone to do it, but for the most part they won't be able to.

      But I know in one of the sourcebooks (Night Horrors - The Wicked Dead I think) has rules for Dampyr, who are half-vampire, half-human. Don't know the exact rules as I don't own the book, but I know that they are very rare and they're not like Blade lol. Instead they are sort of a mystical deathtrap for Kindred of the same clan as their parent, being oddly alluring to draw the vampire in and their blood holds a strong curse or Doom. Hopefully someone else on the forum can tell you more though.

      3. I suppose it depends on how long ago it happened and how it happened. If it was for example some magic spell that suddenly killed all Daeva off in the year 1850 apart from the NPC in question, then I'd imagine that by now only elders would remember the clan. But I doubt it would make much difference in the night-to-night runnings of the Requiem. As an example, in Requiem for Rome they had the Julii instead of the Venture. They were essentially killed by the Strix quite suddenly with barely any survivors and who may by today experienced Final Death, living in secret pretending to be Ventrues or even believe they are due to the Fog of Ages.

      But it would open up some possible story plots for the character - why was this NPC the only survivor when his entire clan died? Maybe the more investigative PCs can look into it. Maybe an elder with an obsessions for collecting rare objects decides that the only Daeva in the world would make a fine addition to his collection, and the PCs need to protect him. Just some food for thought

      4. It would be difficult I would think being a high-class lawyer as a Kindred, thanks to all of those day-time court sessions. Not only that, but he won't be able to stick around for long periods as people will start to wonder why he never seems to age (and the excuses of plastic surgery and Botox can only go so far lol). Personally I would maybe go for a famous legal advisor or something, where he make appointments for people to come to him for legal advice, or someone whose made it as a senior partner of a firm and just makes money from his underlings. But that's just me, so I'll give some advice for being a lawyer.

      For attributes & skills, those sound like good choices. Personally I would raise Intelligence and Wits as well, and maybe have a few of points between Presence, Composure and/or Resolve for Attributes. Lawyers tend to be pretty smart and able to think under pressure after all, and having a bit of determination, composure in the courtroom or the sheer presence that fills the room helps. As for skills he will definitely need Academics with a speciality in Law, as well as other ones such as Empathy (to read the person), Subterfuge (should he have to be "creative with the truth" in the courtroom) and Investigation (to make connections between evidence). Maybe even a couple of points here and there for things such as Medicine (if he's had to read medical reports he could understand them a bit better) or Larceny/Streetwise (picked up a few things from his clients over the years). A few handy specialities for skills such as Motive (Empathy), Courtroom (Persuasion) or Cold Stare (Intimidation - for scaring the witness in the stand). All these are just suggestions though to make the NPC a bit more rounded, just wanted to give food for thought.

      Mystically, if he's a Daeva then Majesty will go a long way towards helping in the courtroom as well as Dominate perhaps to change what a witness remembers (although with signed witnesses statements and things that might not work as well). I don't know many devotions of the top of my head, but in the 1e Rulebook perhaps Verdical Tongue will be handy (page 153).

      Sorry for the wall of text, but I hope I've helped a bit

          On vampires having kids: The wicked dead book gives four rituals (one that works on each gender in both cruac and theban sorcery) plus a devotion that requires celerity 2, vigor 2, and I presume resilience 2 (but it actually says fortitude, which may explain why almost nobody knows it). So on that side of things the bloodline works. Unfortunately where it falls down in that attempting to embrace the resultant child is a very bad idea. Depending on the clan the bloodline is a part of it will have a different effect, but in all cases feeding on a dhampyr of your own clan will generally remove any capacity to function at all as one of the damned.

              I would agree that somewhere between 120 to 150 exp is a good amount to grant them. especially if they spent the last 200 years developing their skills. Humans get weaker over time, but vampires only keep improving.


              I could have sworn the 1st edition book has a section dedicated to talking about myths and facts for WoD vampires. But anyways, no, vampires can not produce children. When a male becomes vampire, his sperm turns into vitae, along with most of his other bodily fluids. As for females, I would imagine their own body stops producing eggs, since the vampire's organs begin to atrophy and only requires vitae to maintain themselves.


              As for wiping out an entire clan, that would require a hefty explanation. Unlike Masquerade, there is no such thing as antitribu. The clans are scattered in every faction and they have over a dozen bloodlines each. Short of globally organized genocide, I don't think wiping them all out is possible. It sounds like he just wanted to be the only sexy vampire on the planet. It's possible that the Ventrue may have staged a coup to destroy their potential rivals (they did it to the Brujah in masquerade). The Daeva have the power to stir the passions of others, which is dangerous for the Lords who rule their subjects with an iron fist, so it's possible to believe they would try to wipe them out before they could spark a rebellion. If he were the last surviving Gangrel, I could imagine the game being something along the Chronicles of Riddick story.


              In masquerade, the Tremere managed to "wipe out" the Salubri by convincing the rest of kindred society they were demon worshipers and soul eaters, and even then the Salubri still managed to exist on the verge of extinction by maintaining a rock bottom profile. There were also the Bhaali who were believed to be simply boogeymen to scare young Fledglings away from demon worship. Just because the world believes a clan is dead and gone doesn't mean it is.



              As for the fourth question - 200 years is a hell of a long time to remain BP 2 and not get any new disciplines. I think your BP increases by 1 point every 50 years or so (somebody correct me if I'm wrong), so the character should be around 3 or 4 BP. Enough to the point where they need to feed on humans, but not yet high enough to be considered a blood god. Blood potency drops by 1 point every 25 years in torpor.

                              As has been noted, there are Theban/Cruac rituals which allow vampire-pregnancies. It wouldn't take much to port those over to mechanics for a custom bloodline that does the same thing. Having the the lines' weakness be something akin to only being able to embrace their own offspring would also make sense and fit a theme on why this power was developed. Could be an interesting story-exploration if this line were wiped out because of the practice leading to a glut of kindred-destroying-Dhampyr (if you wanted to tie those two mythologies together).

                              My concern is more that, and while I'm sure there are a lot of details about the NPC we aren't seeing, the NPC in question seems to be geared a lot toward your co-STs enjoyment. Everyone should be having fun, that's well and good, but I've seen conflicts brew when too many 'special things' are frontloaded into an NPC. Examples:

                              Old PC to NPC: If the NPC is truly supposed to be a mentor for the player characters, there should be more hooks to your current players than hooks to your co-STs PC. In games with multiple Storytellers, it's very easy for an NPC to get labeled as a Storyteller-surrogate. Especially if it seems like drama or player-protagonism is being sidelined for the benefit of the NPC/co-ST.

                              Maxed-out Socials: I worry that this could be one of those cases of 'too-good-to-fail.' A sort of attempt to make sure that social conflicts with the NPC cannot be won, and certainly not by the starting players. While not spending a lot on Disciplines or Blood Potency means the NPC probably has a large weakness in terms of combat, that could be another vector of PC/ST strife. Basically, make sure that horrible NPC death isn't going to upset your co-ST, or that if it will, make sure there are social avenues to mitigate / manipulate / trick or outmaneuver them.

                              Last of the Clan: Unless you are making this a deliberate cornerstone of your campaign, I would be concerned about wiping out the Daeva because your co-STs PC was special in the previous campaign. Player characters are special, and removing a Clan could be an interesting story to explore. But again, by doing this you are saying that in your campaign no future Daeva can be created as Player Characters, basically for the benefit of one NPC's backstory. You could easily retain an aspect of that loneliness / uniqueness by saying the Bloodline, rather than the base Clan, has been eradicated.

                              All in all, one or two of these issues might not be a big deal, but when I see a few of them grouped together it sets off my radar. Even if your co-ST is perfect, it can still give off the appearance of favoritism or a focus more on the 'surrogate' or 'Mary-Sue' NPC than on the actions of the players. It's like the legal concept of Conflict of Interest. Even if you have been proven to have done nothing wrong, you can still have a Conflict of Interest.

                              Anyway, it seems like you have already heightened your scrutiny of the NPC, so I'm probably preaching to the choir, so I would say trust your instincts on this and in further conversations with your co-ST, really put him to task on how each concept or aspect of this NPC will be directly enjoyable by your current players. If it seems to more be an indulgence for his benefit than an active force in enhancing the drama for your players, ditch it. A good first step in divorcing the NPC from the previous PC would also be the name, don't let him be named the same. And the more physical aspects you can change (sex, nationality, etc) - the easier it will be for the co-ST to separate the NPC from his previous PC.

                                  Well said, Holy. I agree that while all of this stuff sounds possible within the rules, it smacks of DMNPC shenanigans. Especially the part about being the last of their clan and the having tremendous amounts of experience above and beyond the PCs. Make sure that all of these decisions are in the best interest of the players, not just the co-ST. Think of it like a group of 1st level PCs in a D&D game and the DM wants to run Drizzt or Elminster as the party's mentor. While it's not unheard of for this kind of thing to lead to fun games, far more often it's just a case of the DM wanting to play Drizzt and using their DMhood as an excuse to do that, totally out-shining the characters who are supposed to be the actual heroes of the story. I'm not saying that's necessarily your co-ST's intent, but you should definitely talk to him about how these specific character choices are going to help make the game more fun for the players, and ask him to consider changing aspects of this character to better facilitate an experience that everyone will enjoy.

                                      Keep in mind that being the last member of a vampire lineage is not as big a deal as being the last member of humanity. Vampires reproduce through transmigration, meaning they turn other people into more of themselves. All it takes is 1 drop of vitae (and a bit of willpower) introduced to a fresh corpse, and poof: the Daeva can repopulate. There may be a looming sense of dread telling the character they need to embrace as soon as possible if they wish to not go extinct, but you could hardly say there won't be anymore Daeva ever again.
