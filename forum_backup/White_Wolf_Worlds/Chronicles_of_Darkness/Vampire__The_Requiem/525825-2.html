                                                There's the old stock-standard answer of "vampires don't understand the mechanics of breaking points."

                                                That's true to a point, but they recognize the results of callous brutality on their psyche, I'm sure. Particularly a covenent full of parasychologists, researchers, undead psychiatrists, and torpor-reading telepaths.

                                                The other thing you have to keep in mind is that Humanity fluctuates. It drops, it rises. Vampires struggle to hold on to what keeps them anchored in themselves as opposed to sliding down into bloodlust and survival focus. There's even a role within the Ordo Dracul known as the Confessor (to risk treading on Sanctified ground) to keep Dragons on an even keel.

                                                Originally posted by Ordo Dracul pg 58
                                                The nature of their experiments and studies frequently calls into question their morality as well as the extent of their ethical boundaries. The more they cross those lines in order to effect some hoped-for scientific accomplishment, the more they risk demolishing what remains of their humanity. By having a Confessor to watch for those dangers, counsel the Dragon how best to avoid them and be there to help her when things go south, the Dragon's Requiem will be marked less by tragedy and more by achievement.
                                                Things like this are why the 1e covenant books are still worth their weight in gold.
