    A new perspective

    05-28-2015, 08:46 PM
    While I was making some food and thinking of vampires (I can't be the only one), I recalled the clan buff in Danse Macabre for the Venture that gave them a bonus to political rolls. As a simulationist, I like the mechanics to be thematically present. Ergo, I wondered how this could be. From this, I formed the notion that, just as the default beast would urge one to consume those nearby and flee the sun, each clan had a different beast. Not so radically different to be a different genus, but a new species.

    I'm curious if anyone has any ideas of how to furnish this idea? How can I thematically convey the character of each clan as if it was the Beast's urging.

    I've to go but I'll check in soon to see what people have come up with.

                              Heck, in B&S, it is suggested that both are offshoots of each other iirc.

                              But let's keep it simple for now. Five clans, origins not of the highest importance. I wasn't looking for mechanical buffs but as with everything, to each their own. I was interested in simply discussing the experience of each Beast, assuming each beast had different urgings. Kinda like how each mage sight comes in several flavors.

                              I've two examples I think hammer home this point.

                              The first is just looking at the Beast in its raw form. To do this, we simply have to look at the Draugr, as it is a Beast without Man. As it is in RAW, it seems that all vampires as Draugr have the same routine and this implies that they behave the same. What I'm suggesting and want to develop is that each clan has a specific Beastly aspect. If vampires share a common source, clans diverge as much as bloodlines. If not, this difference can be fundamental. What is important is is the idea that a Draugr represents the archetype of each clan. While Gangrel and Nosferatu Draugr might look like the type suggested by RAW (viciously stalks and kills its living relations), a Mekhet would be a shadow stalker, a Deava would lure and ensnare, and a Venture would brood and trap. When there is the Man, these preferences creep out. It is why so many Venture become manipulative and political; it is their beastly desire. Variation can occur, of course, but the Beast acts as a prism for your undead experience and the strength of this prism is the inverse related to the strength of your humanity.

                              With a perfect humanity, each vampire is an individual. Peter the Deava can enjoy fishing by the moonlight and Liza the Venture is a supportive person in the lives of those closest. However, as the humanity drops, the Beast colors their experience. Fishing ceases to be meaningful and more vigorous pursuits are needed. Support and modesty turn to dominion and pride. What I am trying to suggest is that the Beast has a viewpoint and within the union of Man and Beast that is a vampire, this viewpoint needs to be expressed to the player. The ST needs to remind Peter that his daughters play is a trivial and boring affair, while he reminds Liza that those around her incompetent morons. Indeed, with low enough humanity, the ST shouldn't bother with an objective perspective. Don't say 'As you try to accept your sisters apology for being late, the Beast spits at her weakness', say 'Your sister is late. Again. She seems to curl up under your gaze and timidly makes a flimsy excuse even she knows is false.'

                              My second example is how I made sense of this while thinking about it. For those who know the game, this will seem vague but hopefully, that just makes it approachable for those who don't. Suppose five League of Legend gamers are embraced to the different clans. As their preferred past-time is an online video game, they use it as an escape from the unpredictability of their new life. However, though the Beast wouldn't play League normally, it is chained to the whims of the Man as much as the Man to it. As such, the players notice they now play different. The Deava suddenly finds it an easy affair to chain deadly combos together and out-play opponents. What they intend to do and what they do now line up with frequent regularity. The Gangrel finds himself pulling back from an attack moments before an ambush, and knowing -just knowing- which path to take. His survivability shoots up. He also finds himself preferring tanks and melee. The Nosferatu starts to play with his opposition. He just knows which champion and items will give his opponent a hard time and how to play up this advantage. He also starts to use chat to infuriate the enemy team, unnerving daring advances and splitting defense against shadows. The Mekhet starts to notice tells and give aways. He always seems to be aware of cooldowns and mana reserves of the enemy, as well as deriving the location of others from small and subtle details. The water splashing through fog of war. The bush moving. The enemy suddenly having dragon buffs. It is as if the game is an open book. The Venture stops playing a personal game and develops a wider perspective. He never plays against opponents who defend properly anymore; there is always a weak spot. Additionally, he rarely goes a game without directing his team. Previously, he only spoke to them to badmouth their preformence but now, he instructs and his instructions pay off. After a few minutes, he has his team coordinated in a way usually reserved for the pros. And that's in solo que.

                              Okay, second example got away from me but it is basically the first in different garb. I'm going to cease writing now before this wall of text makes the Chinese envious.

                                  I like differentiating clans in terms of how they experience hunger.

                                  With the Serpents, it's an intimate, almost sexual thing - you desire to be close to specific people, feel their touch, share your emotions and your pain (all Daeva know pain). You need a connection, and the best way to find that is the Kiss - the perfect expression of physical intimacy.

                                  The Savages are the most honest clan in that their hunger is just that - hunger. A burning desire to feed, to gorge yourself in the blood of prey you have hunted down yourself using nothing but your own claws.

                                  The Shadows grow cold and dark and silent when the're hungry. And there are all these whispering voices, telling them to do bad shit, and they get louder and more insistent and demanding as a Mekhet gets hungrier and colder, and sooner or later you'll have no choice but to do what they tell you.

                                  The Haunts are monsters. To be Nesuferitul is to be part of a lineage that is inherently inhuman and removed from all things natural and pure - far more so than all other vampires.
                                  It gets lonely being a monster - lonely and scary. The only way to make it stop is to try and be a little more human - and to do that, you need to have something human inside you.

                                  The Lords experience their hunger as a desire to assert their dominance over other people. Lords are patriarchial abusers who cannot help but feel the strong need to lift themselves up by putting others down. The Ventrue Beast tells them that others must obey; and what better way to put them into their place than by feeding on them?

                                              There's several origin stories floating around, with lots of different possibilities. No one's mentioned the "we all come from strix!" one, though.


                                              But, to answer the OP: Irregardless of clan origins, there are still several methods to differentiate the clans. With the new Blood and Smoke book, my favorite way has been to play up both the clan banes, and make the "clan merits" very prevalent among the clan's NPCs, and giving the PCs the option of getting them for free at char gen. Interestingly enough, the four merits in the core book just kind of happened without planning - convergent evolution of merits! After the book came out, people asked for a Ventrue one - I don't remember where it is, but one of the writers made one up somewhere... Ah, found it!

                                              Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
                                              Heart of the Challenger (••)
                                              Prerequisites: Ventrue
                                              Effect: Your Lord's Beast stands as a gargoyle perched upon a parapet, ready to strike against any that would assail her. When any character unleashes the Predatory Aura on your character, that character gains the Competitive Condition, unless he achieves an Exceptional Success on the roll. If he already has the Competitive Condition, it becomes Persistent, and can only be resolved by besting your character in a competition where the character lost a Humanity/Integrity dot.
                                              Once per scene, you can take +3 dice against any single character with the Competitive Condition, as if you'd spent a point of Willpower. You cannot spend Willpower to add additional dice to such a roll. This counts toward the Competitive Condition's requirement for spending Willpower.
                                              Note that this Merit supersedes the normal monthly limitation on the Competitive Condition.
                                              Drawback: Your character suffers the Competitive Condition at all times. It cannot be resolved. However, when it would normally be resolved, gain a Beat.

                                              So, there you go. Merits and banes to emphasize clan. I also have seen people make the clan signature disciplines into clan-unique disciplines to help. I like keeping things simple, and those are the best way I know how.

                                              You can also steal the ghoul clan-specific merits and give them to full vamps, I suppose, which could help, though only do that in a ghoul-less game, I'd say.

                                                      I think that the "roboticness" of the Mekhet's beast beast is meant, in part, to simply be that individual vampire's perception of things. That said, it makes sense that the beast of the Mekhet would be colder and less wild than that of other vampires. Thematically, the Mekhet are associated with darkness, silence, and stillness; the ancient Roman Mekhet even rationalized their supernatural perception as stemming from a unique ability to stil their minds and bodies. One wouldn't expect a Mekhet in the throws of her beast to hiss or scream; rather, she might slip into the shadows, striking silently at her prey with a swiftness and economy of motion like that of a striking cobra.


                                                      With regard to the origins of the clans, I think it's noteworthy that all five clans appear to have been present in the Americas prior to the arrival of Europeans. The Daeva were the dominant clan among the cities of Mesoamerica, but shared the night with the Gangrel and Mekhet. The Nosferatu clanbook contains an account of ancient members of the clan emerging from an unearthed grave in the San Fernando valley, while the second edition core mentions that Montreal hosts a brood of Ventrue that predates European settlement.

                                                      Considering how difficult it is for most vampires to travel long distances, particularly in regions where the kine are few and spread sparsely across the landscape, it seems unlikely that Vampires (except perhaps the Gangrel) could have entered the Americas by the same routes that humans did. The most likely explanation seems to be that the clans somehow arose independently on both sides of the Atlantic.

                                                      One possibility is the Strix, of course; the nemeses are better suited to long-distance travel than the Kindred since they can move about during the day while possessing a host and potentially subsist on a wider variety of prey, like spirits. Using the Synthesis embodiment, a Strix could potentially turn a corpse or a living mortal into a vampire of any clan, which could then embrace others as normal and spread the curse. Other possibilities exist, however; legends claim that corpses can sometimes spontaneously arise as Nosferatu, and there might be some similar process that can turn those denied proper burials into something like a Hollow Mekhet. Other stories claim that the first Gangrel were somehow the product of bestiality, and that the Ventrue were originally household spirits tied to particular families, who were subsequently cursed to become vampires by a goddess.

                                                      Regardless of the truth, it seems to be the case that, wherever mortal humans go, the kindred are never far behind.
