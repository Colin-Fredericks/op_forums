                                I'll take this on since it's largely my writing.

                                A) Keep it separate. Improvised Law is the Carthian's Power, Pacts are taking it from others. Similarly, even if you were JUST using A Deal With Darkness, and using the sidebar for Invictus to forge their own Pacts, I'd let them both empower and seek Pacts entirely separately.

                                B) As the section on strengthening or weakening Establishing Precedent goes into, increasing the Laws that can be upkept is a perfectly expected way of tuning the power level. If you want to do that with Specialties instead of a given Skill, absolutely go for it.

                                C) As in, does it need to coincide with an explicit tome of laws? Depends on the city. I'd imagine in a city run by the crones there are a lot of "implied" laws that are good enough for Precedent to hook onto, but in an Invictus-run city you likely need to go by the book.

                                D) What do you mean? Privilege, by its nature, is exceptions, unless I'm misunderstanding what you're asking.

                                E) It works normally. If a Carthian is in a position to declare official laws, they can, and then can Establish Precedent around it. If not, they need to forge Precedent around the laws of those in charge (or in opposition to, for Privilege, which might be better thought of as "Rebellion" in cities where Carthians are at odds). Establishing Precedent is meant to be exactly that; you're not creating a new rule, you're explaining what happens when you break/follow one in a specific way.
                                The basic idea with Establishing Precedent is you take an existing law or rule for the city, then attach the benefit or drawback to it. You can't Establish a Precedent of "Don't kill the prince or else you take aggravated damage" unless the law of the city already says no killing the prince. You can always get MORE specific, though (like the example of "don't kill the prince" when you're just not meant to cause Final Death at all). Privilege, of course, references the existing law but only to say you don't need to worry about it.
