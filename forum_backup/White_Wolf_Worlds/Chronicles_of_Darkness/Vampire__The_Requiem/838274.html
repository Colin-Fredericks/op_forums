    Red Surrender Revisited

    02-27-2016, 07:47 PM
    The gates of hell are open night and day;
    Smooth the descent, and easy is the way:
    But to return, and view the cheerful skies,
    In this the task and mighty labor lies.

    —Virgil, Aeneid VI:126-129 (Dryden translation)

    I've been re-reading the Red Surrender section of Savage and Macabre, as well as MachineIV's own houseruled take on the subject, and figured I'd take my own stab at it using those two sources. Any constructive feedback would be appreciated.

    I like the idea of the New Moon version of MachineIV's conditions as well as the original "Leashing the Beast" sidebar, but both of them are a little too permanent-ish for me. I like the idea of trying to keep the Beast locked up, but it's bound to break loose sooner or later, or the Kindred might decide that they need—or maybe even want—it loose again. So, let's begin with that concept.

    Restraining the Beast is a constant mental battle; holding it so tightly saps the will (more so for less humane or more potent Kindred—no one ever said it was easy for a monster to not be a monster). To paraphrase Savage and Macabre, denying the Beast feels awful, as the Kindred concentrates on the harm that she has done. Restraining it can be beneficial in trying to interact with others, but doing anything that threatens one's sense of Humanity is even more taxing to the psyche. The same applies to letting it out: It means acknowledging that monstrous part of yourself. In addition, just because it's held down doesn't mean it won't bleed through at times anyway—and Riding the Wave is even harder due to both the mental effort required to leash the Beast. (Thus, failure is more likely, too. Oops.)
    *
    RESTRAINED (Persistent)
    With an effort of will, the Beast has been leashed by the Man. It’s more controlled—mostly—but it feels awful and unnatural to do so, and the Beast still tests its bonds.

    - Gain 1 die on rolls to resist Frenzy.
    - Subtract 1 die from rolls to Ride the Wave.
    - Effective Blood Potency is decreased by 1. (If the Kindred in question is already at Blood Potency 1, treat their Vitae pool the same as a Revenant's: 5 + Stamina / 1 per turn.)
    - Lose 10-Again to Athletics, Brawl, Intimidation, Stealth, and Survival rolls.
    - Upon waking, choose to gain 9-Again on Empathy, Expression, Persuasion, Socialize, and Subterfuge rolls; if so, take a -1 to Detachment rolls for the night.

    Source: Lose (Blood Potency – Humanity) Willpower Dots (minimum 1).
    Resolution: Experience a voluntary Breaking Point at current Humanity rating with a 1-die penalty, regain lost Willpower dot(s), and immediately take the Impulsive condition. The Kindred in question may only do this once per Story—the Beast is not so easily ushered into and out of restraint.
    *
    I liked the five 'stages' of Red Surrender originally, as well as the multi-stage conditions by MachineIV, but I noticed that Socialize was not one of the skills penalized in his version, so I've added it to the list for a total of five skills penalized by the Surrender to match those that receive a bonus. (After all, the more the Beast shows its face, the less sociable overall the Kindred in question will be.)

    Both the Savage and Macabre and MachineIV versions of Red Surrender allow the Kindred to 'snap out of it,' but it feels like it should be a more nuanced struggle with the Beast. Letting it out of the cage means it's that much harder to confine it again; thus the list of conditions here are a sliding scale, as someone in Red Surrender either descends further, fights for stability, or claws their way upward again one night at a time. (No one ever said it was easy—to paraphrase Virgil, it's a seriously slippery slope.)
    *
    IMPULSIVE
    Like the full moon shining above, the Beast calls to the Kindred, urging them to give into their baser desires.

    - Take a 1-die penalty to resist Frenzy or Detachment.
    - Effective Blood Potency increases by 1.
    - Gain 9-Again on Athletics, Brawl, Intimidation, Stealth, and Survival rolls.
    - Lose 10-Again on Empathy, Expression, Persuade, Socialize, and Subterfuge rolls.

    Source: Red Surrender.
    Resolution: Upon waking, roll Resolve + Composure – 1; if successful, remain Impulsive or leave Red Surrender entirely. Failing or forgoing this roll results in the Reckless condition instead.
    *
    RECKLESS
    The Beast begins to steal over the mind of the Kindred, like the shadow of a gibbous moon, as its instinct become more and more appealing.

    - Take a 1-die penalty to resist Frenzy or Detachment.
    - Effective Blood Potency increases by 1.
    - Gain 8-Again on Athletics, Brawl, Intimidation, Stealth, and Survival rolls.
    - Lose 10-Again on Empathy, Expression, Persuade, Socialize, and Subterfuge rolls.

    Resolution: Upon waking, roll Resolve + Composure – 1; if successful, remain Reckless or take the Impulsive condition. Failing or forgoing this roll results in the Wild condition instead.
    *
    WILD
    Almost balanced against each other like the two sides of a half-moon, Man and Beast struggle to take the reins—or destroy them.

    - Take a 2-die penalty to resist Frenzy or Detachment.
    - Effective Blood Potency increases by 2.
    - Gain 8-Again on Athletics, Brawl, Intimidation, Stealth, and Survival rolls.
    - Lose 10-Again on Empathy, Expression, Persuade, Socialize, and Subterfuge rolls; cannot achieve Exceptional successes.

    Resolution: Upon waking, roll Resolve + Composure – 2; if successful, remain Wild or take the Reckless condition. Failing or forgoing this roll results in the Feral condition instead.
    *
    SAVAGE
    A sliver of rationale remains, like the faint light from a crescent moon, but the Beast has largely assumed control.

    - Take a -2 penalty to resist Frenzy or Detachment.
    - Effective Blood Potency increases by 2.
    - Gain 8-Again on Athletics, Brawl, Intimidation, Stealth, and Survival rolls; achieve Exceptional success on 3 successes.
    - Lose 10-Again on Empathy, Expression, Persuade, Socialize, and Subterfuge rolls; cannot achieve Exceptional successes.

    Resolution: Upon waking, roll Resolve + Composure – 2; if successful, remain Savage or take the Wild condition. Failing or forgoing this roll results in the Monstrous condition instead.
    *
    MONSTROUS
    Like the darkness over a new moon, the Beast has all but eclipsed reason, and the Man struggles to even be heard at all.

    - Take a -3 penalty to resist Frenzy or Detachment.
    - Effective Blood Potency increases by 3.
    - Gain 8-Again on Athletics, Brawl, Intimidation, Stealth, and Survival rolls; achieve Exceptional success on 3 successes.
    - Lose 10-Again on Empathy, Expression, Persuade, Socialize, and Subterfuge rolls; cannot achieve Exceptional successes, and failures are automatically Dramatic.

    Resolution: Upon waking, roll Resolve + Composure – 3; if successful, take the Savage condition. Failing or Forgoing this roll means the Kindred remains in the Monstrous condition.
    *
    A few notes:

    Where there's a will . . .
    The original method of caging the Beast cost a Willpower dot, for a permanent effect; my version takes into account a balance between Humanity and Blood Potency as the 'cost'; for example, a Blood Potency 8, Humanity 4 Kindred would have to pay 4 Willpower dots to restrain the Beast. It feels a little clunky, but it's the best I could think of to illustrate just how hard it would be for someone especially monstrous to try and control their impulses.

    Our name is Legion, for we are many
    I'm wondering about separating the Red Surrender into three categories to match the aspects of the Beast (as a few have discussed here before), but I keep thinking that it might be overly complex to do so, or at the least very different from above—what skills would receive which bonuses and penalties for each Aspect? Would the aspects cancel each other out or complement them? Should the proposed version have as many conditions/stages for each Aspect? Etc etc etc.

    There must be some kind of way out of here . . .
    Both the previous versions I've been studying allow the Kindred to leave Red Surrender entirely all at once; one by making the resistance roll, the other by resisting Frenzy (which immediately locks the Kindred into a hyper-rational condition that can't be resolved except by extraordinary stimuli). I like Red Surrender as a spectrum, but perhaps leaving it entirely if the Kindred in question experiences a Breaking Point, whether they succeed or fail (with all the associated condition penalties), might be a possible option.

    The Pull of Red
    Effective Blood Potency adjustment means everything related to it increases, but I wonder if that might be too overpowering. Then again, considering all the various penalties associated with Red Surrender, maybe keeping it as-is works. Alternatively, perhaps taking a few things out might be better, such as the increased Blood Pool or some other bonus.

    I Deserve a War Hell Ride
    I considered making the structure for Frenzy, Detachment, and Resolve + Composure rolls a 1/1/2/3/5 or 1/2/3/5/Chance-Die setup instead, but a character with some seriously unlucky rolls might see their Humanity rapidly decay as they find themselves in the depths of Surrender and uncontrolled Frenzy. If this were the framework, then I'd suggest either (A) allowing someone to make another Resolve + Composure roll (appropriately penalized for whichever Condition/s the Kindred is in) to flee Red Surrender if they hit a Breaking Point; (B) allowing someone to shift 'upward' one step after failing a Detachment roll; (C) allowing a Resolve + Composure roll (again penalized) to shift 'upward' at a Breaking Point; or (D) just buckling up and getting ready for a possibly short but definitely hellish ride.
