        To be honest, once I started hearing about changes that 2E made, I really didn't like the sound of them. Like the entire concept of touch stones as a form of game mechanic, instead of an organic role playing component. I get it, touch stones are supposed to keep you tied to the remains of your humanity. But really, why did that have to be written up with a rule system, instead of letting it flow naturally as part of the story? I don't need a dice roll to tell me if my character goes into a rage frenzy over seeing their biological child being murdered!


        The Strix were also an addition that I don't see much point in. The entire premise of vampire was that they are solitary, back stabbing predators by their very nature. Giving them an outside threat to rally around really detracts from that concept of personal horror and gradual descent into a cold, uncaring monster. Besides, I thought they already had enough enemies to compete with, without adding a unique villain tailored for them. The Strix also tried to impose some level of origin point or time period in which vampires emerged into the world, which ruins the ambiguity that 1E had in which it's left up to the player's imagination on where vampires came from.

        There's probably some other changes I didn't like the sound of, but I can't remember them. But the Strix and touch stones stand out the most in my mind.

            Touchstones got rules because without rules you don't really have any incentive to have Touchstones. The rules also reinforce the themes of having to cling on to humanity or descending into becoming a monster. It's the same reason Humanity has a rule system.             Strix are optional. And were added in a 1E supplement first.
            Also, I don't get why you think of vampires as solitary creatures when they're all about forming coteries, joining covenants and participating in their own societies filled with other vampires. Backstabbing is incredibly accurate, but solitary is not.
