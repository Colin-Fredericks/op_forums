    Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
    I understand that there are no schools for thugs and Vagrants, but think of gangs and mob hierarchy, a lot of them have mentors or people to work with to show them the ropes. Could I just read a book on how a gang is suppose to work then say that I am a professional gang member? That was my point. Can I be completely self taught and take this? How would I get contacts if I just ran to the library or watched how to videos? Think of it this way. if I wanted to be a professionally trained engineer and I learned on my own. What are my chances of getting a real job as an engineer without the schooling to reflect that? Or even a Gang thug, I cant just walk up to them and expect to be part of their world. Even if you were a professionally trained con artist, I still see having the need to have a mentor or some sort of Other person teaching you.
    Well, hopefully people's explanations for having Professional Training is a bit more involved than just reading a book anyway. We can't give you guidelines for every situation, but I think a good general rule is that a Profession has to be or have been a big, significant part of your life to translate to having those merit dots. My character with Professional Training (Thug) has been loyal to the crew for a long time. My character with Professional Training (Lawyer) went to Law School and worked at a firm for years. That sort of thing. If you're taking Professional Training for something, it, in my opinion, should be embedded into your background solidly, because it means you're a professional in that arena. Spending many years on the streets as a homeless individual, just scraping by with occasional help from your fellows might entitle one to Professional Training (Vagrant) for instance, which isn't as formal. There's just room for that sort of interpretation.

    Just, y'know, again, whatever it is, it should be something that a character has spent a lot of time with. You don't get Professional Training (Lawyer) from being a hobbyist that watches Law & Order. You get it from a not insignificant amount of time with hands on experience.

    Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
    I am not saying All, but I am saying A good deal of hackers went to school or in high school took classes for computer programs. Also, I would think that actively communicating with other hackers, asking advice how to ect is getting some sort of mentorship and not going at it alone.
    I think you guys are agreeing here.

    Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
    If you were walking alone down a street and someone else was walking alone down the other side, wouldnt you watch them, look at them and wonder why they are there alone? Even more so what if they had a bazooka? How can you be just another face in the crowd if there isnt a crowd? I would think that being on a deserted street, in a park or building calls attention in and of itself. Or perhaps that is my human curiosity wanting to know why the heck the other person is there all alone late at night. wondering if they are wondering the same thing about me?!
    That'd be why I wouldn't make it automatically succeed, I'd require that Perception check, penalized by their Obfuscate dots. If you fail the check, you just don't really notice the person. If you do, you notice them fully, with the range of possible action that might entail. It's a supernatural power, so the notion that it might supernaturally affect your natural instinct to intensely scrutinize the only other person walking down the street doesn't seem too far out of the question, with absolute invisibility within two dots reach.

    This way, Obfuscate 1's utility isn't solely limited to being in a crowd, while it's simultaneously not the perfect stealth offered by Obfuscate 3. It serves a different purpose. If you're using Obfuscate 3 you can just walk into the house. Obfuscate 1 would then make it easier to sneak in, but still require that sneaking.

    Let me stress also that *this is a House Rule* and is a comfortable one for my table. What works for yours may not be the same.

                                                    From a game standpoint, it works best to just say nothing transfers, from there Storytellers know they can bend the rules where they want. Otherwise you could say your vampire was a member of the Aegis Kai Doru and so he has artifacts, or she was a member of the Ascending Ones, so she can make ointments or whatever, which a Storyteller wasn't ready for and now has to tell the player no, and the player can point at the rules and say there's no rule against it.

                                                    But from a story standpoint, when you're running the game and feel fine with it or playing the game if you know your ST, and hopefully they're open minded, and your characters are growing (or being killed as they get Embraced) it's not unreasonable to let them keep their abilities or pick relevant other abilities. I don't particularly like the transferring of telepathy to Auspex myself, and would probably just let them keep their telepathy, especially considering how cool the idea of psychic vampires is.
