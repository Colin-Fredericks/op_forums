                                          Stereotypical presentation and dehumanization aside, Chronicles of darkness characters being too good for the type of story it usually seems to want to tell is something I also struggled with.

                                          The base game makes it nearly impossible to start with overqualified PCs with the base stats & skill spread.

                                          Originally, coming from a DnD background in which the games put a hard cap on your capacity based on your progression, which CofD doesn't do, I often have to remind some of my players, and sometimes even myself, that we play characters that are within human standards and 4 to 5 dots in a skill is pretty exceptional. When left unchecked, that causes tonal clashes with the tone of my campaigns.

                                          Hell, even 2 dots is assumed to be professional training.

                                          CoD character creation gives you the illusion that you can build a relatively normal character you could meet on the street, like ‘’a bored medical student’’ or a ‘’single parent trying to get by’’ but honestly… Unless those two have exceptionally well-hidden skills, you won’t succeed at creating them without being at least overskilled in one area or two.

                                          Tangent about recreating myself as a CofD Character.
                                          More than a decade ago, something that got weirdly popular for a little while in my gaming circle was to recreate ourselves for a chronicle.
                                          I’m under no illusion that I would ever get close to the power level of a CoD PC, but if I were to try to approximate my lived experience, I don’t think I could even spend all attribute dots.
                                          Maybe I could justify intelligence 3 and resolve 3 to fully spend my 5 dots on mental attributes, but I’m not fully convinced.
                                          And I certainly don’t think I would ever qualify for more than 2 dots in a single skill. Let’s take a look.
                                          • Academics : I have a bachelor’s degree, but I never been a PHD student. That’s between 1 and 2 dots. 1 dot with some specialties might be more accurate.
                                          • Politics : I can justify 1 dot, since I’m keyed into most political issues in my area and did some political philosophy classes when I was at uni. And I work in a corporate environment; I can somewhat navigate the corporate office structure with relative success. I simply choose not to do so because I hate it.
                                          • Other mental skills : At most hobby skills, as a millennial, I could justify not having a penalty on computer skills, but I certainly do not have any programmation skills to justify 1 dot.
                                          • Drive : One of the rare physical skills I could justify, but only since I know how to drive in the snow as a Canadian and I’ve successfully dodged 2 very risky scenarios that could have got me killed. (Or I could have been super lucky with my chance die, twice! It was pretty scary).
                                          • Weaponry : The only other physical skill I could justify, as I played larp a lot back in my younger days, I could justify one dot, or buy off the penalty. I haven't played in a while; I’m pretty rusty, not going to lie.
                                          • Other physical skills : Maybe I could justify not having a penalty on athletics, since I’ve been trekking a bit lately, but I’m not constantly playing sports. I’m above the North American average, but that’s not saying much, to be honest.
                                          • Empathy : I think could justify 2 dots. I had some social working classes and worked part-time in a youth centre for a few years and I work in public. That makes you pick up social cues pretty quickly.
                                          • Expression: I've dabble in a few artistic hobbies. As a virtue of being really invested into roleplay, i could justify 1 dot.
                                          • Other Social Skills : I might be able to justify 1 dot in most social skills as a virtue of working in public and living downtown and I have a general knack for at least ‘’belonging’’ in a social setting, but that’s about it. Intimidation would be a stretch, Animal Ken is definitely out of the window.
                                          With a generous interpretation, that would leave me around roughly 11 to 10 unspent skill dots of 22. With generous interpretation, I might be able to spend all my attribute points, with something like Intelligence 3, wits 2, Resolve 3, all physical 2, Presence 2, Manipulation 3, and Composure 2.
                                          Damn, I’m an NPC.

                                          I think the difficulty of creating a grounded character in CofD comes from the assumption that the game runs on movie/book logic, rather than realistic assumptions. The game is viewed through the lens of chapters & scenes. In books and movie characters tend to be generally skilled, unless their incompetence is a plot point.
                                          That being said, that’s for PCs, and NPCs generally shouldn’t follow PC creation rules.
                                          In the case of Isabel & Isiah, I will admit some of their skill dots are a bit eyebrow raising. With as little context as I’m provided, I would probably change the following both characters :
                                          Isobel : I will assume that she mostly lives on the streets, in a dangerous city.
                                          • Attributes : Strength 2, dex 3, stamina 1, rest is fine.
                                          • Mental : Craft 1, Investigation 1, Medicine 1, Science 1
                                          • Physical : Athletics 1, Brawl 1, Firearms 2, Survival 2, Weaponry 1
                                          • Social : Empathy 2, Intimidation 3, Persuasion 1, Streetwise 3, Subterfuge 2
                                          Merit : I’d go with contacts in the underworld, allies, etc. Striking look 2 is high, but it doesn’t necessarily mean hot; she could be intimidating.

                                          Alternatively : make her older and she could be a vet that ended up on the streets, a sadly too-common fate, that would probably justify her high firearm skills.

                                          Isaah : As others have mentioned, his expressive skill + specialty in acting is odd for a failed actor, but he could be failed because of multiple reasons. Skill is not a guaranteed factor of success; unfortunately, plenty of people succeed because they are well connected and plenty of skilled people do not because they never had the chance to prove themselves. Hopefully for him, he can fix that in the future when someone realizes his talent.
                                          As for using autobuilder, that’s… certainly a choice. It will leave you with oddities for your NPC concept. Not to tell you what to do, but you don’t need to build a full sheet for an NPC if you lack the time to do so. Some simple writeups like the NPC you can find in the core books are detailed enough.

                                              The thing isn't just that the game works on movie/book logic, but a related yet slightly distinct fact: it's about exceptional people.

                                              Stories are about exceptional people. CoD isn't about making ordinary characters, it's about making exceptional grounded characters. Looks the same, but isn't. A character with Science 4 isn't a regular person, is an exceptional person you have the reference to understand within a grounded context.

                                              We have a tendency of assuming the character creation rules generate a weak beginner. There are several reasons: is the baseline we actually experience, is the baseline we share with other players, looking at other chronicles/characters give the impression that those are common within the game world, and our eyes set into the promises of advancement make the initial state seems weak.

                                              In a sense it even is, as stories are also about exceptional circumstances that will challenge those exceptional people beyond their already impressive feats.

                                              Not just a CoD problem, either. It's easy to forget in D&D that your 1st level adventurer is already stronger than 90% of the population. Your regular farmer has between 8 and 13 in each Ability Score, average 10,5 and has no special ability. That's the baseline the story is made to assume (authors committing similar mistakes compound the problem in every game).

                                              Another thing most players and many STs don't get is that NPCs can freely use rules that are minor for PCs. CoD has Specializations and Merits and rules to invest on them, reaching higher stats without investing in the Skill directly.

                                              For a PC, with a player thinking in terms of point cost, it's mostly meant for special cases or to hit above the trait limits. But for NPCs? A big chunk of them probably don't have a single Skill above 1, rather having specializations that allow the professional levels in whatever they actually do.

                                              Because, you know, not many professionals, even exceptional ones, are absurdly skilled in every single natural science known to humanity or every manual skill whatsoever.

                                              NPCs rarely need more than two or three stats. I give them a few more just in case I need to rule through weird player decisions, but overall even that won't extend beyond 7, 10 if you want to be absolutely thorough on derived traits and resources.

                                              Isabel and Isaiah could be explained out with little effort and no change to traits if they were treated as actual people, even if NPCs, rather than shallow stereotypes, and keeping the concepts to boot.

                                              Being a veteran would help a lot with Isabel, but she could also be genuinely an exceptional gang member that nonetheless get shit because she's a woman.

                                              There are gang members that, upon raising by being better at something, do start to learn other things as they are, you know, people. Also sometimes college students and even young professionals get down on their luck enough to end up in such a wasted state.

                                              She's good looking, given the actual benefits I don't buy Striking Looks as being an intimidating presence, although it really doesn't have to mean beautiful, just pleasing to be around. There's a lot to explore there and then how she ended where she is.

                                              For Isaiah I can easily picture someone who was really good in theater school, talented and promising, yet simply pushed by sabotage from more powerful people fearing competition, and then his life starts to go bad very intensely and fast.

                                              More importantly, though, the very stereotypes could have been relied upon without flattening. She IS a Chicana and he IS black, and plenty of bad stuff happen to those people in the US because of prejudice and racism.

                                              Which is precisely the ignored reason why they are disproportionately found on such situations for real.

                                              Anyway I'm half curious, half dreading how Iguazu would react if he saw the color of my skin, or if my country already colors his reactions.

                                              Not saying it does, just that I wonder.

                                                  While certainly common, stories are not exclusively about exceptional people; they can also be about exceptional situations. In my French literature classes back at university, we read a lot of books with very mundane protagonists with no particular set skills worth mentioning. Very often they would actually be worse than average and that's what made the story interesting.

                                                  RPGs, including CofD, will often focus on action/pulp style, or generally skilled protagonists. For multiple reasons; I suspect that it makes for easier storytelling and players would rather play skilled characters than mundane corporate office clerks. Stories also tend to come to a halt when a player fails and they usually run for longer than a book or a movie by ‘’content length’’, at least it’s true in my case.
                                                  That being said, I don’t disagree with your assessment.

                                                  We can also agree or disagree on whatever a grounded exceptional character means, but my initial perception of the game was definitely skewed toward understanding starting the character as barely above average. However erroneous that might be, for the simple fact that the first character examples that are given are ‘’single mother scrapping by’’ and ‘’bored medical students’’. My introduction to the game certainly had more of a street-level feel to it too.

                                                  I won’t lie; coming from DnD probably skewed my perception as well.

                                                  However, I’m not sure it’s possible to truly end up with a bored medical student who also won’t be a genius or very skilled at a collection of skills, in a way that makes them formidable in their own right. Not to downplay motherhood, as all single mothers are their own version of badass, but a CofD version of that ‘’archetype’’, for lack of a better word, will also be better than average.

                                                  I, personally, don’t see that as particularly grounded. They are simply less fantastical or exceptional than most rpg characters at first glance.

                                                  Like most point-buy games I've played, the game doesn’t hold the player and the storyteller by the hand. Any questions of relevance and justification are purely left to the player and story teller’s discretion.
                                                  • Does my character, who hunts twice a year, really have that many dots in firearms?
                                                  • Does my medical student character really have much more than one dot in medicine? But if they don’t, won’t that make them much more spectacular in another field diluting the whole concept…
                                                  There are no inherent guard rails, which aren’t necessarily a bad thing, but it’s especially made worse by the fact that all traits have a flat XP cost. I very much disagree that the difference in the efficiency of resources 2 and 5 is worth 3 XP, but I’ll cut myself there, as I’m already pretty far into that tangent.

                                                  You are also right; skills are very versatile. One has to interpret the game’s skills as an approximation of real life abilities and roleplay and interpretation has to fill in the blank if they wish for an experience that's closer to reality. An expression 5 world-renowned fantasy writers can technically play piano like Bethoven, but it doesn’t mean that the player and storyteller can’t stop for a second to ask themselves if it makes sense given the character and the type of story. That’s the price to pay to avoid having a crazy amount of skill lists like GURPS.

                                                  Fact is… It’s pretty hard to make a struggling down to earth character. You have to really restrain yourself and think in advance, or they will end up ridiculously overskilled in an area or two. I wish the game had a weaker starting character with a clearer XP bracket for the style of campaign one would like to run. As I, personally, find it very fun to play regular folk in difficult situations, I often find myself distancing myself from characters that are too exceptional.

                                                  For NPCs stats block complexity. I actually agree; full sheets aren't necessary. I’m a bit crazy and I like to make the full sheet because I run a sandbox game, but that’s only for what I’d categorize as NPCs rather than extras.

                                                  In my games, NPCs have goals that go further than the immediate and while they certainly do not receive as much spotlight as players, they have agency and will not only react but also act. I design NPCs in a way that a player could technically take control of their sheets and read their background and I’d trust the player to actively be able to pick up and do a fairly good job at portraying them.
                                                  Extras are people I create on the spot for a scene, or cheap antagonists, like cops, gangsters for an action scene. That won’t deserve the full attention of an NPC as they will most likely never be relevant beyond that one scene they appeared in, unless players become very fond of the personality I created on the fly for some reason. For those I generally make a dice pool on the spot ranging from 2 to 6 based on what feels right.

                                                  For Isabel and Isaiah, yeah, the descriptions provided were certainly too short to humanize them.

                                                  For Isabella, I was mostly trying to charitably make sense of the sheet or the character concept, which clearly appears to have some inherent dichotomy that needs explanation.

                                                  I agree that she could be a skilled gang member, but it would be weird for her to have streetwise 1, which would be a skill she would most likely use all the time as a gang member and have a nearly world class elite assassins skill in weaponry and firearms. This, to me, screams more ‘’Someone recently down on their luck’’ than ‘’Hardened gang member’’.

                                                  As for striking looks, are you talking about Isabela’s case or striking look in general? Because the striking look merit does include ''repulsive'' and ''commanding'' which aren’t what I’d call pleasant. In Isabella’s case, given that we’re only given a hero’s forge miniature to judge, I don’t know. A homeless person or a drug addict can appear intimidating in their mannerisms and appearance, in the sense that they make most people feel uncomfortable. (For numerous reasons that aren't really their fault.)

                                                  That being said, you’re more keyed in than I am on the stereotype as a French Canadian and I can certainly see how the concept comes across as offensive. I’m not as alert to that kind of stereotypification as you are, as I’m not dealing with it on a regular basis.

                                                  I’m reminded of Elizabeth Anderson’s book The Imperative of Integration, which aptly describes the issues with the colour-blind approach; namely that it obfuscates sources of inequality and oppression. The conclusion of her book was… debatable if not maybe a bit clumsy in some aspects, but that’s another subject.
                                                  You’d think I would be shielded from racism and sexism with my white and male privileges, but those folks often get too comfortable telling me what they should keep to themselves.

                                                  The other day at work, I had an elderly lady complain to me that the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, which is a holiday to honour the native residential school survivors & victims, was going too far, as ‘’We already did enough for them.’’

                                                  Always awkward when behind the counter to have to politely explain that no, actually, it’s the bare minimum we can do.

                                                  I’m not having it nearly as bad as those that are actively oppressed, but it is an annoying side of looking like the in-group sometimes.
