                                            Yar, I'm not a fan of the term Bipolar. Sure it's easier to say (and kind of makes more sense), but Manic Depressive just sounds more suitable for a gothic style game. Same with Psychopath. Ya, it's almost a taboo now. Anyone who is labelled with that is almost instantly ostracized in society. But still, a more fitting term. Also yes, they do feel emotions and not empathy; however, they also tend to not show much in the ways of emotions. Sociopaths do, and they can even get along with like-minded individuals. But not so much for the latter (unless it suits their needs, which being a vampire who was in torpor for an incredibly long time, he didn't want to be around anyone/trust anyone. So he showed no emotions, or if any, disdain to most individuals).

                                            Also I completely agree. Mental/social disorders are not taking as seriously as they should be. I am glad to finally see some physician's taking it seriously now. But we are a long way off from what we should be. It is also difficult to play them out as well, especially if you aren't trying to offend anyone (which is rather hard to do these days). Which is why I went for those two. Either incredibly happy/sad/angry, or no emotion. Randomly roll a d10. 1-5 = Manic. 6-10 = Psycho. If manic, 1-3 = angry, 4-6 = sad, 7-10 = happy (yes, there was one extra for happy, but it was easier to play!) It was incredibly fun to play. Added extra depth. Mood swings like a muffin though. Especially if angry. So many frenzy checks 0.o

                                            Also, this is all from my understanding of the illnesses.
