                  I'm not going to call it "broken" or anything, but I think Obfuscate 3 can become a serious problem to a game. It's less overwhelming power, though, and more a ridiculous tool for spying and murder, even on vampires. If you know for sure that someone is a worse fighter than you, you just win if you can catch them with it and nobody without Auspex really has much of a chance to stop you. It requires so many countermeasures to prevent a player with it from just following any vampire home around dawn to discover their haven, and if they can stay awake (either with a roll or Coil of the Ascendant), they can just freely stake whoever they want...it almost feels like scry and fry in D&D.

                              Originally posted by tsusasi View Post
                              You don't require Auspex to pierce Obfuscate anymore, anyone with a decent perception rating can do so. The player just needs to get in the habit of actively searching for clues to indicate an obfuscated person may be nearby not just the obfuscated person. Granted the Obfuscate rating of the user penalizes the perception check on the character but merits like Trained Observer (especially the 1st. Edition version) and Enhanced Senses offsets it.
                              You don't pierce the Obfuscate, though, you just know that they're around. And yeah, having every vampire looking for clues constantly just in case is exactly the kind of nonsense preparation I'm talking about, like making sure every wall in higher level dungeons are dimensionally warded or contain teleport traps or something or that all bad guys walk around constantly nondetected and mind blanked, etc.

                              There's no resistance, that's the problem. Only unusual, specific countermeasures do anything, and, well, if the invisible guy is a better fighter than you, he can just abduct you into the abyss and own you one vs. one where nobody can help unless they have Auspex (since just knowing they're there doesn't really help).

                              It's actually always bothered me that Obfuscate isn't opposed--it works explicitly with mind control after all. You're literally manipulating people's minds into not paying attention to you/not perceiving you. How is that not call for an opposed roll? In my future games, I intend to house rule it to be an opposed Int + Stealth + Obfuscate vs. Resolve + BP roll, I think. I had the same problem in 1e with Obfuscate, because it was totally free. I thought charging blood in 2e would make it better, but then they added the fact that you can abduct people and fight while completely invisible and it went just right back to the same power level--it might even be more powerful.
