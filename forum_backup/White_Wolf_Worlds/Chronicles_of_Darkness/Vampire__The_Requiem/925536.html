    Allies and XP

    07-03-2016, 06:42 PM
    So the situation os this: a player of mine asked me to find a police officer, after a while he found one and dominate him to meet him alone and let him drink his blood. With the trigger of dominate 3 he close the vinculum in the successive 2 nights. Now he has a policeman as a trhall without the xp expense for the merit and want to go on with other higher ranks. The question is: is acquires Merits like allies legit even without xp expenditure but just with the roleplayng? If not, how can I manage this?

          This is what 2e's Sanctity of Merits refund is for: that's what you get for buying Merits with XP rather than just in-character effort. Your player put in the work: great! They "earned" their thrall, as much as vampires earn anything by the Vinculum. If they don't cash in XP to put him on their sheet, though, the thrall is just like any other non-Merit possession, like a gun or nice clothes at their haven: it's something that can be destroyed by circumstance or even bad luck. Easy come, easy go. Perfectly legit.

              Another thing you can use as an interim step between "the cop just exists as an NPC," and "the cop is bought as a Retainer," is to use Conditions, which can put some mechanical restrictions on how much the cop can do for the PC.


              One advantage of this is it empowers the player a bit more than ST dictated events that cause the cop to go away; since resolving the Condition (or as a Persistent Condition) can help go towards racking up those Beats to encourage the player to pick a time to "use up" their sway over the cop (even if they don't get the cop killed and decide to go right back to getting the Condition reapplied ASAP), rather than just having a friendly NPC around for "free."

                      Hmm, I handle this pretty differently.

                      The sanctity of merits doesn't provide any plot armor, nor does it make the NPC ally or contact any safer than they'd otherwise be. It just means, as per the book, players can re-spend the lost dots into something else like a replacement ally, or whatever.

                      In situations where the player has expressed an interest in doing something like in the OP, I'll either hand out un-sanctified merit dots (freebies which they can't re-spend), allow them to do it but not allow the NPC to factor into the story until they spend the XP to reflect it mechanically, or tell them that the idea doesn't actually occur to the character until they have the XP to spend on it (at which point they can go ahead and do it).

                          What needs to be kept in mind is that XP is an entirely Out of Character resource. Characters don't spend beats or experience points, players do. When someone bloodbonds a member of the police, that serves as justification for buying up Allies or Retainer. All of that happens at the meta-game level, not an in-character perspective.

                          So, without the player spending the XP, one really doesn't gain the effects of an Ally or a Retainer. Even if someone is bloodbound, its not a reliable person - the vinculum is easy to resist by simply running away. Its much like the difference between using Resources and Larceny to meet something's Availability - you can steal things every time, but its dangerous and unreliable without the invested Resources.

                                          It's also worth remembering that most such Merits are not "I have a buddy at the precinct," except maybe the first dot. Allies, Contacts, and Status are supposed to represent sway with large groups of people that you don't get by getting one guy in your pocket.                                                   Well, you can run it that way if you want - golden rule and all - but that's not how the Merit is actually phrased. It's called "Allies" not "Ally." It gets you influence in a group, not influence with a person who then uses their own advantages on your behalf (which is a Retainer).

                                                  The FBI director doesn't handle most things. Hell, most of the 35,000+ employees of the FBI have probably never met one of the directors - at best they might have been at an event with the director and shook a hand - leaving most of them having no idea who the hell you are and why they should do anything for you. And the FBI director isn't going to call up a field office to tell them to give a friend access to the crime lab for their own personal investigations; because that's pretty much a guarantee that they'll get fired for corruption.

                                                  That's why Allies is about groups. Having influence with a group explains why you can get favors from their whole organization. Members/employees/etc. know who you are, and why people do you favors.

                                                  In the same post, I explain how that's exactly what has happened so far in my current game, so no I'm not saying they are mutually exclusive, I'm just presenting two opposite views and stating which one I like more if I have to choose.
                                                  Phrasing it as "two opposite views," is basically saying they are mutually exclusive by making them a binary issue. Sure, 1e had holes in it that made it harder to judge. That's why 2e added the Sanctity of Merits rules, and has Conditions/Equipment that can overlap with Merits in concept (the Connected Condition is pretty much exactly for the OP's issue of working for influence in a group but not yet having the XP to buy Allies, and there's Cash Equipment for when you find a pile of money instead of having to buy up Resources).

                                                  You don't have to worry about giving way free Merit dots, because Merits aren't meant to be free like that. You have other things to act as an interim between in-character actions and putting XP into the effort (if you ever do, since you can just work Conditions if you want); players have a reason to invest time in the RP justification for Merits. It can even help with the ST's pacing/plot because, again, there's a middle point you can use so players can feel like they're getting stuff for their efforts, even if the ST doesn't want them to get to the point of investing XP on it.
