                        Let's talk about the Shepherds! (I will second that Baaldam's remix of the Iltani sounds awesome, though).

                        This is a bloodline that was very low on my radar; Gangrel bloodlines are not my expertise, and I actually forgot they were in the book before I started writing my outline. I actually think they have a really cool concept, especially for Gangrel, but they end up being outshone by the Sta-Au, who are by far the cooler of the two Montrose Party bloodlines.

                        With that said, the big direction here wasn't radical change so much as adding more meat to the bone. I really liked the "gray hat" mood of their original write-up, and that they're very explicitly not made out to be the "good guy" vampires. As such, this was probably the most straightforward update in the book, with a few more plot hooks in their backstory (I'm a big fan of the Origins section on this one). The biggest change is their bane: Their original weakness pretty much went straight out the window; it wasn't bad in concept, but we really didn't feel it fit for this particular bloodline. So, the author added a little twist to the Bestial Curse, and I think it ties in much more strongly with their themes and their reputation. The author had a Twist in the first draft offering an alternate bane that conformed in spirit (if not mechanically) with the old bane, but I felt that the one we ended up with was leaps and bounds more interesting, and I didn't feel a need to retread the previous version.

                        The other big adjustment was expanding their power set, giving them an inherent gift as well as some Merits and a new Devotion. Some of those are based on the original material, but we gave everything a 2E shine.

                        Not too much else to say about the Shepherds. They're a solid bloodline in Ancient Bloodlines, so we didn't feel a need to rework them too much beyond giving players more options.

                        So, here are the Shepherds. Tomorrow, let's look at a new bloodline: the Vetala of Clan Nhang.

                        Shepherds: The Ones Who Corral You

                        "Behave, or this'll get unpleasant right quick."

                        Many Kindred exist in abundance, which breeds recklessness. And when junkie bloodsuckers get reckless, this often leaves the sorts of messes behind that spook mortals. When the herd is spooked, all Kindred lose. Thus, the so-called Shepherds take on a responsibility akin to their namesakes among the kine, protecting human flocks as a herdsman might do with cattle and sheep. This isn’t normally so literal; the Watchers don’t keep pens of humans they occasionally let out to graze, but many of the same concepts apply when standing security in the city’s hottest nightclub or directing a convenient flash mob — although some Shepherds do get involved in “animal husbandry,” but this isn’t common.

                        Just as with mortal shepherds, what these Savages do isn’t out of kindness, but pragmatism. Shepherds (both mortal and dead) need their herds for sustenance, and it simply won’t do to butcher your cattle when you can first take milk. Mortals have much more to offer than their blood. Of course, that doesn’t keep the Watchers from developing deeper relationships with parts of their herd, just like farmers sometimes have prize heifers.

                        The commonly accepted founder of this lineage is one Greta Devenpeck. She was the only vampire who escaped the destruction of the Montrose Party, a group of settlers traveling along the Oregon Trail that was accompanied by Kindred hoping to establish domains in the West. Great misfortune befell the Party, but Greta escaped, and with the lessons she learned and the change these challenges wrought in her blood, she soon gathered the first coterie of what would become the Shepherds.

                        Being Savages, Watchers are both the shepherd and the sheepdog. While they typically make for gentle guides, they can lash out with shocking violence against any wolves who would threaten their herds — as well as at any disobedient “sheep.” Corralling humans requires a special kind of social engineering that involves many tools. A little fear of the dark here; the comfort and safety under the Watcher’s gaze there. But even when a Shepherd must be harsh, she must always appear as an anchor of safety to the kine. She must be able to gauge how any disturbance could impact her flock and how to counteract threatening influences. For some Shepherds, this turns into all-consuming obsessions as they manipulate relationships and even whole family trees.

                        The attitude they bring to their work doesn’t help their reputation, however. In many domains, other Licks ridicule the Herders as spoilsports and softies. This doesn’t usually lead to any institutional harassment, but altercations between Shepherds and rowdy Kindred aren’t unheard of. In some cases, these conflicts grow to encompass the domain as a whole, and the local Shepherds are forced to move on, sometimes taking their herds with them.

                        For this and many other reasons, self-righteousness is common to Shepherds. Whether they feel they’re the only ones capable of long-term thinking, or genuinely see themselves as heroic, Herders often have trouble leveling with their Kindred. Occasionally, a Watcher styles herself as an avenger of the kine, taking her sanctimoniousness too far for even other vampires to bear. Few domains will suffer vigilantes for long.

                        Why you want to be us

                        Kindred rarely get to feel like they’re saving lives and keeping order in the world, but we do. Yeah, the others talk shit about ulterior motives and pragmatism, but is it so bad to keep the apple cart running and the body count low? Didn’t you ever wanna be the Good Guy going Savage on a bully? Well, guess what: There are a lot of bullies in the All Night Society.

                        Why you should fear us

                        Livestock handling isn’t just beer and massages, like you’re some prime Kobe beef. The herd needs to be kept docile and oblivious. That’s what we do, and we have to do it subtly. The only ones who should fear us are our shortsighted kin. It’s all fun and games till you have to tear up some motherfucker who doesn’t check himself. The kine are way quicker learners.

                        Why we should fear ourselves

                        Does the milk cow appreciate being slaughtered later than the steer? Maybe. Not like she gets much of a choice. Seeing mortals that way dehumanizes them more than the occasional “feeding accident,” and being the designated killjoy in a world of dead yahoos doesn’t always make for a great time either. Just all the more reason to let out the inner Savage after all.

                        Bloodline Origins
                        • The Montrose Party were one of many groups of settlers traveling the Oregon Trail in the 19th century, and with them, a coterie of young Kindred. It quickly became clear that protecting every single mortal was vital for survival, as each life lost was a major cut in the blood supply. Ultimately, they failed. Like many migrants who took on the arduous journey, many died to hardships, and those who were left were picked off by the Sta-Au (p. 87), remarkably monstrous vampires native to the region who haunted the Party over its last few nights. At least one vampire survived, however: Greta Devenpeck. While sharing the adversity (and blood) of the Montrose Party, she developed a special link with her herd. This brought a deep understanding of how important it is for Kindred to not unduly harm or spook their food supply, and from there, she found likeminded Savages who would protect the kine for the long-term benefit of all Kindred.
                        • There never were any vampires in the Montrose Party. What an idiotic idea, that Kindred would cram themselves among just a handful of mortals over miles of wilderness! And even if some were stupid enough to try, they surely met Final Death in short order through accident or discovery. Maybe the reason why you can’t find the Montrose Party in any history books is because it never existed at all. A Kindred fiction to teach an oblique lesson — or to hide a darker truth. Whether or not the Montrose Party existed, the Shepherds certainly do. As do the Sta-Au. The truth is, the Sta-Au call themselves “twice-dead” for many reasons, both poetic and bloodcurdlingly real. The first Shepherds were what was left behind when a Wicked One decided to die her third death. This separated any human decency still left within her from her monstrosity, allowing the Sta-Au to become one with the Land of the Worms. The Shepherds are still monsters, but they’re the better part of what was left behind.
                        • The Montrose Party did exist as the Kindred tell it, but the important thing is that the “Conquest of the West” wasn’t really a mortal project at all, nor even a Kindred one. The Great Will, in its immaculate calculations, determined that the settling and industrialization of the American continent were of vital importance. That adventurous bloodsuckers would try to hijack this project wasn’t accounted for, but It understands how to fit an errant cog into Its Grand Design. Through Its agents and portents, It effected a bonding between mortal and immortal, and created servitors who would forever help maintain sublime stability and order among the unruly and erratic dead. Humans who selected to further Its design should not fall prey to the fickle variable of the dead, nor should they have reason to fearfully look at what’s concealed around them. The Watchers fulfill an important role, but there aren’t enough of them yet. They must spread. Carefully.


                        Parent Clan: Gangrel

                        Nicknames: Watchers, Herders

                        Bloodline Bane (The Vigilant Curse): Shepherds protect their herds and, by extension, all mortals with a watchful ferocity, and their Beasts have adapted to protect this precious food source against any threat. Any overt violent action toward a mortal by another vampire or supernatural being is a frenzy trigger with a modifier commensurate with the severity of the transgression. This does not apply to self-defense or subtle feeding, but only if the act doesn’t inflict the Drained Condition. If the victim is part of the Watcher’s herd (e.g., represented by the Herd or Feeding Ground Merits or determined by domain law), [REDACTED].

                        Disciplines: Animalism, Obfuscate, Protean, Resilience

                        In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

                        The Great War — 1914–1918 CE (DE2, p. 390): It’s only natural the Shepherds follow their herds wherever danger awaits them. While only a handful of Watchers (mostly from the Canadian prairies) make the journey across the Atlantic, that’s vastly more than any other group of North American Kindred. They take on masquerades as medics and nurses, or even drill sergeants who wake their charges at night for “special” training. Many serve the war effort as hidden presences, angels of the battlefield who whisper in young men’s ears to get out of the way just as the shelling starts. Some Embrace posthumously in order to bolster their numbers, and many of these neonates remain in Europe after the war, hoping to shepherd the herd into a lasting peace…

                                    Alright, let's look at the Sta-Au. From a distance.

                                    This is one of the very, very few "any/multi-clan" bloodlines from First Edition, which for some reason was a thorny issue in early Requiem. Infamously, the Moroi bloodline write-up includes a sidebar that goes out of its way to declare that they're in fact two bloodlines that are exactly the same. To a lesser extent, then Mortifiers of the Flesh made you jump through several hoops if you wanted to play a non-Daeva. At any rate, that reluctance seems to have tapered off by the time Ancient Bloodlines came out, because there don't seem to be any caveats about who can join. At any rate, this is all to say that I like all-clan bloodlines. I feel like a future project might be to do a book of them, although I have to admit I'm a little bloodline'd out at this juncture.

                                    Anyway, my big directive for the Sta-Au was to make them more playable. Their original weakness was that they could never buy back Humanity which, uh, certainly scales with that trait, but is maaaaaaaybe a little harsh; direct quote from my outline: "Boy their bane goes hard." It's a weakness that's interesting in concept but in practice (and with apologies to the sidebar on p. 35 of Ancient Bloodlines) mostly relegates them to Storyteller character territory, in which case that particular weakness doesn't really matter anymore. I'm sure one could play a Sta-Au with their original bane, but even ignoring how narrow it is, it's a tad boring. As such, I really like their new bane. We have a few more "externality" banes in this book (e.g., the Brothers of Ypres, the Nehebkau), in that they don't directly affect the character, but still influence behaviour. This bane also integrates the Land of the Worms more intimately with the bloodline, making them direct vectors for its spread.

                                    Speaking of the Land of the Worms, I'm really happy with how our version turned out. It's not too heavy mechanically, but it's a bit more modular than the original. I'm a big fan of Mysterious Places, so I'm always happy to fit them in where we can.

                                    I don't have much to say about the development process here, as not too much changed from the final draft apart from a couple of tweaks here and there. This was one of the bloodlines that was really solid right from the start. My only major addition was adding an additional Dark Era (the Lifting of the Veil one), as well as the "Who We Are Tonight" list. My biggest contribution was mostly the art.

                                    Anyway, I believe the author wanted to talk a bit about the writing process here, so I'll end on that note.

                                    So, here are the Sta-Au. As always, if you like the book, consider leaving us a review. Tomorrow, let's finish the week with the chilling Caporetti...

                                    Sta-Au: The Ones Who Slaughter You

                                    "Wolves to them; sheep to us."

                                    Across the Great Plains, Kindred tell tales of the Sta-Au, ashen-skinned boogeymen with jagged teeth like rows of knives, covered in trinkets and trophies of human bone. This bloodline makes its home in the inhospitable badlands of the region, where all trespassers are dealt with accordingly. “Normal” vampires in the area cleave to humans as much as possible, spending their nights in cities and only traveling on heavily trafficked roads if possible; anything to avoid facing the Sta-Au and their hunting parties, or coming across the skull-adorned borders of the Land of the Worms.

                                    The Wicked Ones live up to their moniker. They have no regard for morality, instead celebrating depravity and cruelty as if they were virtues. The Sta-Au act as if they’re the Beast incarnate, seemingly giving in to every dark urge and instinct with abandon. Yet unlike the draugr, they’re not mindless monsters incapable of higher reasoning — which only serves to make them more insidious.

                                    The Sta-Au take their name from the ghosts of Blackfoot belief, the wicked dead who choose to harry the living instead of passing on to the Great Sand Hills. Unlike their incorporeal counterparts, the Wicked Ones believe all vampires have failed to properly die, reprobates eternally stuck in their corpses. To break free from these shackles, the Sta-Au die a second time, bringing themselves ever closer to a true death. After that, it’s just a matter of finding the passage to an afterlife befitting such vile creatures as vampires and, finally, proving worthy of entrance.

                                    The Sta-Au’s afterlife is said to link to the locations collectively known as the Land of the Worms (p. 91). It’s within the Land of the Worms that Sta-Au traditionally dwell, reinforcing its ruinous resonance and preparing themselves for what lies beyond. By deliberately and carefully shedding each remaining scrap of his mortal life, a Wicked One shall become a pure monster, unfettered by any inhibitions yet not reduced to a simple animal. When he’s freed from the limitations of his Humanity, the Twice-Dead sheds his human body and enters his new hunting grounds.

                                    True monsters don’t abide by any rules, but until the Sta-Au can escape their immortal coils, rules serve a crucial purpose. As a group, the Sta-Au would’ve self-destructed long ago were it not for the traditions and customs they developed centuries ago. One seeker’s path to transcendence shall not interfere with another’s, and a threat to one’s road is a threat to all. While violence is the natural way to settle conflicts between Wicked Ones, it’s ritualized, and the victor is expected to respect their opponent’s defeat. Even bitter rivals cease their attacks once the other lies in torpor, and they even band together should another Twice-Dead break one of their few taboos, or fail their quest and fall to the Beast.

                                    However, not all Sta-Au are committed to anarcho-primitivist deaths in the wilderness. As the American frontier drew to a close, towns and cities boomed. During the Great Depression a few decades later, hunting parties discovered the Land of the Worms could manifest in built-up areas, such as derelict industries and abandoned residencies. Since then, more and more Wicked Ones have made their homes in urban environments, though not without adaptation. Rather than existing in bands separate from the All Night Society, they hide by intermingling with the uninitiated — joining mixed coteries and even participating in the covenants.

                                    This is not to say traditional Sta-Au are rigid and unchanging. Elders expect new blood to adapt to their brutal ways, with scarification and the filing of one’s teeth being common ways to show dedication to the goal of transcendence, but individuality is otherwise encouraged. New ideas just mean new possibilities to explore — and ultimately, the goal is for you to become the best monster you can be.

                                    Why you want to be us

                                    The first death cursed you. You’re caught in the world of the living, unable to break free of your invisible bonds and move on. You play make-believe and hide behind petty schemes. It’s pathetic. We discard all these meaningless pursuits, fetters that would keep us from reaching our true potential. We know what we’ve become, and we’ll show you how to become more.

                                    Why you should fear us

                                    Your second death is a promise. The world is violent, and sooner or later, everyone dies for good. Sooner if you give us a reason. But we aren’t beasts who attack on sight. We act deliberately. With reason. Just make sure to always treat us with respect, stay out of our way, and never, ever show weakness. If you can do that, you’ll be fine.

                                    Why we should fear ourselves

                                    Our third death lies ahead of us. We know the path. We only need to reach beyond the Land of the Worms and grasp our reward… but the path is narrow and treacherous. Violence begets violence, and many of us perish before we ever reach our goal. We celebrate our dark desires and denounce our vestigial Humanity, but without restraint, they will surely destroy us, and only darkness will be our reward.

                                    Parent Clan: Any

                                    Nicknames: Twice-Dead, the Wicked Ones

                                    Bloodline Bane (The Ruinous Curse): The Sta-Au are creatures of defilement and destruction, both physical and moral, and wherever they go, they bring this nature with them. Whenever a Wicked One suffers a breaking point or causes another to suffer a breaking point against Humanity or Integrity, roll her Humanity. On a failure, the location around them suffers the Spiritual Infestation Tilt.

                                    Disciplines: Auspex, Obfuscate, Protean, Resilience

                                    Spiritual Infestation (Environmental Tilt)

                                    Description: The curse of the Wicked Ones has infested the area. It eats through the spiritual foundation of the world, perforating its structure and leaving nothing but refuse behind.

                                    Effect: The area takes on the traits of the Land of the Worms. When this Tilt is applied, the area suffers the Spiritual Blight Aspect (p. 93). Additional breaking points suffered within the same area reset the Tilt’s duration, and if a breaking point leads to the loss of Humanity or Integrity, the area gains an additional minor Aspect (Potency 1, if needed).

                                    If the area would gain a fourth Aspect, it’s major. At this point, the area is at the cusp of deteriorating into a true Land of the Worms. Provided the location is not otherwise unfitting (such as being populated or heavily trafficked by humans or wildlife) a single significant push will end the Tilt and create a Land of the Worms at Potency 2.

                                    Causing the Tilt: A Sta-Au suffers or causes a breaking point.

                                    Ending the Tilt: The Tilt ends after a number of nights equal to the Sta-Au’s Blood Potency times the number of Aspects that currently apply to the area.

                                    In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

                                    A Handful of Dust — 1933–1944 CE (DE1, p. 470): Amid economic hardship and ecological disaster, the Sta-Au became yet another scourge afflicting the Midwest. Barren fields, abandoned factories, and storm-struck towns allow the Land of the Worms to grow at an unprecedented rate. The Sta-Au are quick to follow, and they find unlikely rivals. Animated piles of dust and detritus, also known as Dust Devils, have no aversion to the Land, and they stalk the same human victims as the Wicked Ones. They’re also strangely drawn to the dead who dwell in Twilight…

Ghosts in the state of Twilight (see Chronicles of Darkness, p. 124) appear as if physical to her — only their muted colors and lightly distorted voices betray their true insubstantiality. Other entities and phenomena of ghostly ephemera are similarly visible.

                                        Alright, today, let's talk about some new blood: the Vetala.

                                        All the new bloodlines went through a bit more development than the old ones; the major benefit of working from previous material is that a lot of the groundwork is already done for you, so making something wholly new is always a different beast, especially when we're talking about lost clans, who don't have the literal decades of background the standard-issue ones have. So, it took a bit more work to get these new bloodlines where we wanted in comparison with the "Ancient" bloodlines. The Vetala were no exception, mostly because the Nhang, while a really cool concept, are one of the weirder lost clans. "Water vampire" is definitely a trope, but it's just narrow enough that we had to work a bit harder to find a natural expansion of that concept.

                                        To that end, I think we ended up with something really cool and thematic with the Vetala. It's not a long walk from water vampire to swamp thing vampire, and the author had a really interesting idea in framing them as classic movie monsters. Unlike, say, the Nosferatu, where they represent your deepest personal fears and fundamental prejudices, the Troglodytes are the externalities of human hubris (like so many movie monsters), evolving with technology and pollution and culture. They're what happens when you dump toxic waste in the lake and it wakes up hungry for blood.

                                        Obviously, that steps a bit into Gangrel territory, but we thought of the Vetala as being a very modern phenomenon, whereas the Gangrel are more primordial and (in a sense) natural. That said, it's hard to escape the archetypes, and if you wanted to play them as a Savage bloodline, I don't think that would be a stretch. You could pretty easily map all the new bloodlines to the basic clans if the lost ones don't fit into your chronicle, which is partly why we did five; not just because there are five in Spilled Blood!

                                        The big dev changes were their mechanics. Making vampires hard to kill is a surprisingly delicate dance; you don't want things to just be unfair, but you don't want them to be only nominally tougher than regular vampires. Protean and Resilience also cover a lot of "this isn't even my final form" territory, so we had to get a bit creative. Their bane also went through a few versions. Originally, they had to take skins with the Nhang Flesh Form Devotion to prevent themselves from mutating, but I'm ultimately not a fan of having to harm your Humanity to fulfill your bane, and literally skinning people was just too much for a weakness. I also didn't want to make buying the Devotion a prerequisite to joining the bloodline at all, so we went with something that narratively suggests they're going to mutate no matter what, but mechanically that only affects them in certain circumstances.

                                        Anyway, I'll let the Vetala do the rest of the talking. Tomorrow, let's have a look at the Anvari, the only First Edition bloodline not from Ancient Bloodlines, and the one I've literally been trying to figure out how to fit into a book for three years...

                                        Vetala: The Ones Who Make You Shudder

                                        “Patience! Lurk in the dark, dank places. Nurse that hunger. Evolve.”

                                        Drip, drip, drip goes the chemical drain. Toxic pools stink and burn and let off a gaseous odor that keeps even the rats away. NO TRESPASSING signs aboveground on the barbed wire gates seem almost comical by comparison. The corporation that keeps this place hidden from regulations and inquests turns a blind eye, pretending it doesn’t exist. Suits you fine. It’s home sweet home.

                                        After the first decade down here, as your hair fell out to every last eyelash, as your teeth rotted and your lesions multiplied, you had moments of doubt. The silence was nice, but it left you starving for a morsel of fresh blood. No one comes around here except the rare inspector, and if too many of those go missing, you’ll be attracting attention you don’t want. But when your flesh sloughed off in ragged strips and you felt yourself becoming, you knew you had it made.

                                        Then that tabloid reporter showed up about rumors of a mutant or a radioactive mongrel lurking in the slurry. You gave him a little glimpse. Just enough to piss his pants and write his story. Now the hunters come — not many, not enough to be dangerous, but enough to keep you fed. You play with them, stealing their forms and walking among them… for a while. It breaks up the monotony, right?

                                        Say what you will about pollution; it’s great for your neighborhood monster.

                                        The Nhang are the stuff of vampire nightmares, but that’s because the Kindred forget to count the Vetala. Even the dead can evolve, and the Troglodytes are the glorious, grotesque proof of that, ever-shifting and ever mutable, sprouting whatever proto-organs their environments demand of them. “Humanity” is just another weakness to be expunged.

                                        The Strix think Kindred are abominations, walking corpses who pretend at being people that need the delusion excised by force. No such force is needed against the Troglodytes. Their very existence eats through Humanity like acid rain, eroding them into something inhuman at all times. Take on a human face and slow the distortion of the body — and hope your victim won’t be missed. Perhaps because of this atrophy, the Vetala have an understanding with their Beasts. It boils against their flesh rather than lashing out, and an Extremophile’s frenzy is not the messy rampaging of less-evolved Kindred, but a caustic boiling over. Watch in terror as their bodies swell and distort, jaws wrenching wide, many hands grasping: a nightmare risen from a watery grave. Just another cryptid to add to their urban mythology. The Nhang might be forgotten, but the Vetala are here to stay.

                                        The Troglodytes don’t make a habit of hobnobbing with their Kindred. They surface only when they need something: a bylaw changed, a development project derailed, a fresh rumor to drive prey into their domain. But with their habit of wearing others’ faces, you likely won’t know you’ve dealt with an Extremophile at all.

                                        The first substantiated sighting of a Vetala was during the industrial boom of the early 1900s, but it wasn’t until the 50s with its ramp-up of space-age technologies that this bloodline really found its foothold. The Troglodytes have always been reclusive, but as industry expanded and the world shrank, these hideous Nhang were forced into ever more extreme conditions. Sulfuric hot springs, active volcanic craters, acid lakes — these are the places they make their lairs. They become customized to survive in the most inhospitable locations on Earth. And there, in places anathema to humanity, they wait for the Strix to show them the next step in evolution.

                                        The isolation means they remain largely unmolested, and perhaps this is how they’ve managed to survive. However, it also means they’re forced to venture out on forays to find their food, and their clan's ability to steal appearances becomes crucial. Behind these temporary masks, an Extremophile’s true form is hideous and often plagued with spliced limbs, bulbous growths, and distorted features. They cannot pass as human, living or otherwise. But they can survive anything.

                                        Why you want to be us

                                        You aren’t human anymore, so stop pretending. Those old limits mean nothing to you now. Slip on human forms to lure them in, then discard their husks to tangle in the flotsam. With us, you need not sacrifice sunlight — just society.

                                        Why you should fear us

                                        We’ve got tricks you’ve never even imagined needing to counter, and there’s no one who can make us bend, no one who can challenge us for our domain. We’re always hungry and we’re always hunting — yes, even you. We drown the meat to steal its face, so you won’t even know we’re among you… until it’s too late.

                                        Why we should fear ourselves

                                        Our flesh sloughs off, and we take a new face. We walk in it for a while until it flakes away, and we take another, and another… Each time, we’re worn a little smoother, a little less. No matter, we’ll take another! But soon, we see our reflection and don’t know this boiled, cancerous, wriggling thing peering back, cold and lonely in the dark depths. All those myths about monsters reduced to their watery lairs, their ravenous hunger — they’re all true.

                                        Parent Clan: Nhang (Night Horrors: Spilled Blood, p. 94)

                                        Nicknames: Extremophiles, Troglodytes (derogatory)

                                        Bloodline Bane (The Contaminated Curse): The Troglodyte Beast doesn’t know when not to adapt, even when it’s to nothing. As the Blood thickens and Humanity declines, a Vetala’s body reacts by forcing a violent and disfiguring mutation: tumorous growths, new organs with dubious functions, etc. This degeneration is inevitable and worsens as the vampire ages, but sometimes the body overwhelms even itself. To keep this overgrowth in check, roll an Extremophile’s Humanity penalized by Blood Potency when she rises for the night. On a success, nothing happens; on a failure, she gains the Maladaptation Condition. As long as this Condition lasts, her bane remains dormant.

                                        Disciplines: Celerity, Protean, Resilience, Vigor

                                        Maladaptation

                                        [REDACTED]

                                        In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

                                        Fear and the Golden Promise of Tomorrow — 1938–1946 CE (DE2, p. 428): The sudden surge in horror books, comics, and films makes “the monster” a common feature of many science fiction works, and this fascinates the Troglodytes, who see in these creatures their own likeness, whether to be lampooned or made into tragic heroes — though never with a happy ending. As their faces fill movie posters around the world, some retreat back into the shadows, only for their evolutions to morph in new and extreme ways as they fight to adapt to the coming nuclear age. On the frozen ice floes of Severny Island and the dry wastes of New Mexico, a few Vetala get a dose of a very modern terror.

                                        Bloodline Gift: Adaptable Physiology

                                        Vicissitudinous flesh provides the Vetala greater protection against environmental factors. By default, the standard Nhang traits are no longer hindered by the purity of the water the vampire lives in. A Troglodyte’s color-changing scales and underwater vision are unhampered by pollutants, alkalinity changes, chemicals, saltwater, etc. In an extreme environment [REDACTED]. However, this gift does not apply if the vampire is exposed to sunlight or the effect of her clan bane is in effect.

                                        Prerequisite: Kindred, Fame •

                                        Effect: Your character has acquired a local legend. This niche brings cryptid hunters and other looky-loos to her territory searching for proof of her existence, providing potential sources of food. Treat each dot of Fame as a level of Herd or Feeding Grounds, distributed as you see fit.
