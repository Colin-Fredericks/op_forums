                Oh, I gotcha. My experience with 1st edition Requiem was entirely through LARP, so I don't know if this was different in tabletop, but the Mind's Eye Theater book for Requiem had a sidebar stating quite explicitly that Dominate required the victim to look the vampire in the eye. Not the nose, not the forehead, not the chin. Naturally, dark or mirrored sunglasses were a great defense against Domimate because if a would-be Dominate user can't see your eyes, they can't know if you're looking into theirs or not. This was a huge pain in the ass for everyone, and presumably has something to do with the decision to change it in 2e to say that it works as long as the victim can see the vampire's eyes, even through mirrored sunglasses. As for mirrors and cameras, I agree with your interpretation. I could see an argument for mirrors working though because it's the same light reflected from the vampire's eyes, just reflected again in the mirror, as opposed to their eyes on a video screen, which is light generated from the screen and merely an artificial representation of their eyes. But personally I rule that it's got to be direct, no reflections and no recordings.

                            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                            Nothing, really. The Lost Visage works on both. But in 2e it can be voluntarily turned off at no cost, so that shouldn't be a problem.

                            Personally, I don't allow self-Dominate using mirrors or cameras. You could theoretically mesmerize yourself, but then you'd be mesmerized, which would make it kind of hard to give commands.
                            Mesmerized only makes you quiet and subdued, it doesn't explicitly rob the victim of their ability to act, so I figure they'd be able to talk, albeit quietly and in a rather reserved manner. Enough to give yourself a command anyways.

                            Moving on, let me first say that I think that auto-hypnosis via Dominate is a cool concept that allows neat tricks that reward creative thinking, and that as far as I can tell the game does nothing but benefit from its inclusion. Now as for why I would let a mirror would work while something like a video camera wouldn't, I justify it by the degrees of separation between the two. A mirror gives what I'd feel is a much more direct connection to the victim than something like a video feed, even a live one. Though even that could be diluted. While I would allow a single reflection to allow Domination (maybe at a penalty), once you get into hall of mirrors level of separation a mirror is no better than a video.

                            That said, this is pretty much all personal opinion. The books as far as I know have nothing to say about this kind of thing.
