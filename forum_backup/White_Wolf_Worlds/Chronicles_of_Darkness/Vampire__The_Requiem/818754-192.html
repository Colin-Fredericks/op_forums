    * Defense is only hampered by the hold maneuvers (neither grappler has defense during a hold)

    When you are immobilized there are two types
    Immobilized with Bindings
    Immobilized with Grapple
    (No MV. No Def, No Actions)

    * Yes character D suffers - 4

    * Character A gets all the benefits and rules of Defense unless denied his defense (Invisibility, Immobilized, Fast Effects,)

    * Yes Character A gets a Defense of Gun Size +1

                        Use the range penalties. Firing at medium range is -2 and firing from beyond the gun's long range reduces the attack to a chance dice.

                        Firing from close range to avoid penalties in most cases means someone can still move close enough to hit back.

                        Implement penalties for firing while moving proportional to how quickly they move. (Despite Hollywood bullshit, it's actually extremely difficult.)

                        Have the melee character pull out a gun and shoot back.

                        Have the melee character use their brain and move the opposite direction or take cover. Then you can use concealment penalties too.

                                            If the target is stationary then there is no kiting involved. If the target is chasing the shooter then the target is not stationary.
                                            Remember that the turn system is intended to make real-time combat manageable at the table. If this is becoming a problem at your table then I would suggest that until the chasee gives up following the shooter, then they would still be considered in melee combat for the purposes of defender getting to use his defense.
                                            Of course these aren't whiteroom fights so the shooter would likely run out of space after a short time.
