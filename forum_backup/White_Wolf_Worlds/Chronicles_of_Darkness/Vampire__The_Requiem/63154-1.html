                        As a general rule of thumb, I don't mix merits with Disciplines/Contracts/etc. That said, there is...

                        Ritual Area (_______) (● to ●●●+ each)
                        Effect: Your character has set up an area of their lair in such a way as to benefit uses of some manner of arcane power. Choose a style or theme of magic (Necromancy, Memory Editing, Relic Crafting, etc). On any extended roll involving the magical theme in question, add the dots in this Merit. Ritual Areas are almost always extremely noticeable. A necromantic Ritual Area will have a floor of grave dirt, lots of black candles, skulls, and walls covered in black silk and so forth. A Relic Crafting area will have purifying baths, alchemical tools, tiny bonsai trees, jars of meteoric iron, and so forth.

                        -----------
                        Basically, it's a modification to your Haven/Safe Place. So a Ritual Area (Vicissitude) would give you a +1 to +3 bonus to your Vicissitude rolls and would probably resemble some kind of twisted doctor's office. On the flip side, a Ritual Area (Gilded Cage) would have models of the city, collections of maps, probably the Vesica Pisces engraved in the floor, etc.

                        The + at the end means that it is possible to have Ritual Areas at greater than 3 dots, but it needs some heavy in-game explanation (say, the Nagloper took over an abandoned hospital and has somehow managed to keep it). The Each means that you can have more than one Ritual Area, provided you can fit all of them into whatever your Haven/Safe Place is.

                            Mechanically, using stolen flesh, alive or dead, is straightforward. You consume X points of Size to add +1 to your Strength, Dexterity, Stamina, or Size.                                             Hrm... I'm running into a bit of logistical hiccup here.

                                            Alright, so here's the powers as I have them planned so far:

                                            Incorporation of Quiescent Flesh
                                            Add +1 to Str, Dex, Sta, or Size to the target body; with a further +1 per additional three dots of Potency (so +1 at Potency 1, another +1 at Potency 4, another at Potency 7, etc). The donor body (or body parts) may be alive or dead.

                                            Amalgamation of the Alien Minds
                                            The target gains +1 to Int, Wits, or Resolve, though no score can be raised higher than the highest possessed by target and donor (in other words, if the target has Int 3 and the donor has Int 2, you aren't making an Int 5 genius here). However, each time this is used, the target gains a serious Madness Condition. (I group my Conditions, so Madness Condition here means: Broken, Fugue, Madness, Obsession, Amnesia, or Persistent Paranoia, Delusion, or Depression).

                                            The hiccup I am running into is 'how do you cap it'? Previously, the cap was XP spent, basically. Even if you moved stats around, you're still limited to how high they can go because you are limited by your original Experience. A Nagloper or Tzimisce could use Mandate of Flesh and Exaltation of the Primordial Mind to get their physicals up very high, but the XP for those physicals needs to come from somewhere.

                                            But here, what are the limits on combination? Take a base as a Revenant, so it doesn't heal these things. Wave away for the moment the control aspects -- vinculum, Conditioning Devotion, Majesty 4, there are ways to get pretty firm control, though it's pricy for a Nagloper. What is stopping the Nagloper from simply using Incorporation time and again until they get a Vozhd with 20 Strength who eats werewolves for breakfast? Or for that matter, a Vozhd with fifty Strength? It just requires a lot of dead bodies. Or live ones. Alternatively, sure you end up going crazier than a hatter, but you can use Amalgamation and Exaltation (bump your mentals, then transfer those mentals to physicals, then restore mentals once again) to do this entire process rather more quickly.

                                            So clearly, some sort of cap is necessary here. But what cap? A global limit to Vicissitude to the effect of 'You can't raise Strength or Stamina or what-not with this above (Normal Max+Vicissitude?)" Or perhaps limit the number of times Incorporation and Amalgamation can be used? (Incorporation is the more mechanically problematic one, I suspect, since repeated Amalgamation makes the target very crazy -- but still problematic).

                                            Tricky, tricky.
