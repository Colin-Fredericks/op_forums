                                    Originally posted by Veindark View Post

                                    I think reason for Dracula being super animalistic in the book had more to do with deeply seething caricaturist or rather racist polemics against foreigners or "The Other" who were 'taking away their women' which is what Dracula originally represented which was sadly common in the late Victorian era, well just like F.W. Mernau's "Nosferatu" suspiciously looks like a anti-semetic caricature especially being associated with Rats and Plagues which was also common anti-semetic imagery at the time (oh even the whole "burning in sunlight" death concept from it which I highly suspect is also anti-semetic imagery as well if you really think about it).
                                    Those may have been conscious or unconscious influences on the originals, but I don't think that requires us to always have to view those characteristics through the lens of "racist caricature." Dracula could just as easily be reinterpreted as representing predatory sexuality, or the Id in general. The Requiem clans are broad enough that they read as analogues for what Freud would refer to as thanatoi, those things that we know can destroy us but we long for regardless. The Masquerade clans were far more "ethnic," which could a little tactless at times.

                                    In the book, Dracula isn't really plotting to take over the world, or looking for a mortal to make his lover. He's an old, tired monster who senses that his time is running out, so he's going to get a change of scenery for a change, and cause as much mayhem as he can before he goes down fighting. That's Gangrel AF in my book.

                                    And if you're still having trouble imagining him as a Gangrel, take a look at some of the bloodlines. Want to focus on his depiction as a nobleman who treats his land as his territory, and his subjects as prey? Take a look at the Annunaku. Want to focus on him being the ancient, aged menace living on the fringes of civilization? He's an Oberloch. Want to channel the sex symbol depiction? Maybe he's a Kerberos, trying to transition from Medieval nobleman to Victorian gentleman.

                                    The point is, the clans are broad enough that you can get a lot of mileage out of each one, and end up with some pretty diverse characters and concepts.

                                                        I had an idea I used in my last campaign for (sort of) clanless vampires. One way to interpret the strix is that they might have created vampires. Their power of synthesis allows them to kill a human host by infusing them with shadow potency, turning them into a vampire (possibly at the cost of their own "life"). Normally, the clan is same as the last vampire the embodied strix took vitae from or, in the case of a possessed revenant, the vampire who created the revenant.

                                                        The book then says that a random clan is chosen if the host hasn't had any contact with vampires. I always found that to be a bit strange and meta, but whatever. What you could do is say that such a case of a mortal who's never been in contact with vampire blood and is the subject of the synthesis power results in a vampire without a clan. In my case, those vampires weren't so much clanless as they had unusual combinations of clan disciplines and weaknesses, making it impossible to say if they were from one particular clan or not. One of the PCs, who had only played masquerade, liked the idea of not having a clan, so I gave him that possibility (only revealing much later who/what his sire was). Until he met other "clanless" vampires (who were called "orphans" by other regular kindred), he pretty much introduced himself as gangrel since he had their weakness and the Resilience discipline (he also had Vigor and Celerity, so he could have said he was a Daeva too I guess). Basically, those random power and weakness distributions were an attempt by some strix to start the experiment over and create a new vampire breed who wouldn't be a failure like the first one.

                                                        Or, and that might fit a self serving Dracula, why not a revenant who survived and became more and more powerful until he somehow managed to gain blood potency on his own, without outside help. Maybe he simply used his disciplines a lot and drank vampire blood and magic happened, or maybe that's the true origins of the Mysteries : a way to uplift oneself from revenant to kindred. Then, that particular mystery was lost because it's useless to a vampire and nobody really likes revenants enough to actually teach them.
