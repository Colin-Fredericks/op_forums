    Originally posted by Algarik View Post
    Fact is… It’s pretty hard to make a struggling down to earth character. You have to really restrain yourself and think in advance, or they will end up ridiculously overskilled in an area or two. I wish the game had a weaker starting character with a clearer XP bracket for the style of campaign one would like to run. As I, personally, find it very fun to play regular folk in difficult situations, I often find myself distancing myself from characters that are too exceptional.
    I'd suggest just changing what the dots represent. Like, 1 dot stays the same, 3 dots uses the 2 dot description and 5 the 3. If a player wants to represent higher skill, then you have specialties, area of expertise, and the thing the charts really ignore: Professional Training. Like, the core might say that a character with 5 dots in Science is our era's Einstein, but that's not how it really works mechanically. A character with 3 Science and 5 Professional Training is going to be far more impactful on the game.

    The problem with making the stats lower is that you're going to have to offer a lot more bonus dice to avoid the game grinding to a halt from constant failure.

        Originally posted by Michael View Post
        The problem with making the stats lower is that you're going to have to offer a lot more bonus dice to avoid the game grinding to a halt from constant failure.
        That's actually the point, getting more bonus dice.

        Seriously, what PCs do most of the time? They do stuff on the spot with whatever is around. Frequently they do really hard stuff like that.

        What do regular people do?

        1. They stick to doing things they know how to;

        2. They do their things properly equipped and in a properly prepared environment;

        3. They stack the advantages they can;

        4. Or else they either achieve a poor result, a really easy task still with difficulty, or just fail.

        In a game of common people, the point already is to feel they're common, not hiper-resourceful and doing crazy stuff.

        Current rules already afford for quite a lot. Let's say you have an Attribute at 2, skill at 1 + Specialization, a properly equipped workshop and specific tools for the exact craft you're doing, this is at least a combined +2 equipment bonus.

        You got 6 dice. That's an average 2 successes per roll for tasks you have to roll at all. Chance of failure small even if not negligible.

        And what are you rolling for? The last thing is to remember what kind of tasks even should require a roll at all, or receive a bonus because of being particularly easy. This isn't necessarily the ST giving more bonuses, although that can happen, but the players staying within simpler actions they know they can succeed easily, which is the actual point.

        Giving more dots for a given description risks being self-defeating: you have characters that are nominally weaker doing cinematic stuff and being over competent at everything.

            For character creation and progression I remade XP cost, all but merit now cost incremental XP. At the top of my head, the XP costs are :
            • Attribute or disciplines : New Dot x2
            • Out of clan disciplines : (new dot x2 )+2
            • Skills : New Dot +1
            • Merits : 1 per dot
            • Supernatural tolerance 3 x New dot
            Characters start with 140 total XP and must buy everything from scratch with only 1 dot in all attributes and 1 dot in supernatural tolerance if it applies. This gives roughly the same XP as a CofD starting character, with the exception that dots above 3 are pretty expensive at creation.

            I could also lower starting XP if I wanted to have less exceptional character, i'd have to recalculate what would feel appropriate, but going of vibes, i feel like 120 wouldn't be too bad.

            I also do agree with Michael solution. I usually recontextualize a bit what dots will mean within the context of the game. Professional training, areas of expertise, and cross-disciplinary specialties are all wonderful merits to achieve greater specialties.

            Interpreting dice results in the context of the setting environment rather than trying to fight the game system too hard is usually my preferred method. It’s also, I think, more fun for players to have dots; they can increase in skill. Character mechanical progression is also valued in our circle.

            Monteparnas is not wrong; however,the system already accounts for this using equipment.

            However, I’m not a super fan of equipment beyond the odd circumstancial bonus. Indeed I found the equipment bonus to be really high when mixed with the starting character competencies. Penalties being capped at 5 also means that basic equipment + willpower trivialize the hardest checks. At 2 merely 2 dice, a character already crosses the 50% chance of success.
            I think cash as social equipment is the most egregious case. For 5 XP spent on dots of resources, a character can spam +3 social equipment for free. To be fair, this is consistent with reality; money is indeed quite OP.

            I’m going to stop myself there. I’ve already derailed the thread enough and I made it clear enough. I have some grievances with the base dice system, even though I quite like the game’s overall structure. Hell, this is what inspired me to design my own game and even then I always come back to it in some fashion, so CofD is clearly doing something right in my book.

            monteparnas , as for the harmful stereotype, that’s unfortunate. I hope it’s involuntary on Iguazu’s part. I try to stay charitable but that’s easy to say when I’m so far removed.

            Iguazu your computer juggling sounds painful and time consuming.

            Why generate random dots if you modify them after? Do you use this as a way to come up with an idea?

            If not, why not simply assign them dots how you see fit?

            I personally use a word document with tables I organized to look into a character sheet for NPCs with space for their character image, connections & backstory. This allows me to have all my NPCs in one big folder. I find it easier to manage than 150 PDFs (I’m not exaggerating what I say 150 NPCS).

            For a quick generation, I also made myself a table with a dot budget, with specialist or generalist stats & skill spreads. Sometimes, I also just go by feeling.
