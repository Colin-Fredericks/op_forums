    And it's a loss condition because frenzies are 90% avoidable. Unless someone is using powers like Animalism 4 on you, or someone is particularly cruel for basically no reason and tries to humiliate you just for fun, frenzies are totally within your grasp to stop. You can just not get hungry. Major source of frenzy closed. Avoid fire and sunlight--again, duh. Don't let your friends/lovers get hurt. Don't cause waves such that people target you for mockery and humiliation. Don't screw up so that your superior insults you. Don't screw up so badly that your inferior does. And when all else fails, roll Resolve + Composure, so, you know, have a bunch of dice in that pool. The sources of provocation are mostly in your hands--so when you frenzy, it's because you blew it.

    The whole purpose of frenzy is that it's a bad thing, Thematically, it's the reminder of what happens when you screw up, it's the beast rising to the surface when one of the goals of the game is to suppress the beast and hang on to your humanity. Losing humanity, giving in to the beast, is losing yourself, losing you. Of course a manifestation of that is a bad thing.

    That's a better gameplay pattern than the current one, but it's not what I really want. I don't want to force anyone to frenzy, though, in an ideal circumstance. I'd like to keep it a bad thing. I'd like for the Gangrel to be wild without embracing the Beast. Like, their wildness keeps the Beast in check as much as someone else's stoicism.

            Have to agree with The Storyteller on this one. When provoked, you have three options:
            • Relinquish. Immediately enter frenzy with the target/agenda of the beast set by the provocation.
            • Resist. Roll Resolve + Composure with modifiers per sidebar p104. On a failure, enter frenzy with the target/agenda of the beast set by the provocation.
            • Ride. Extended Action, Cost 1 WP per Roll. "Normal" Resolve + Composure roll (I interpret this to mean the modifiers from p104 still apply). Target = 5 Successes. Failure, or running out of Willpower enters a normal frenzy with the target/agenda of the beast set by the provocation. Success allows the player to set the target/agenda of the beast.

                    Gangrel: The Feral Curse
                    Gangrel must run wild and free; they cannot abide confinement, be it physical or metaphorical. Entering a contract or swearing an Oath is one thing--one enters such an agreement of their own free will--but when othewise forced into a corner, Gangrel chafe and lash out, gaining the Cornered Beast condition. Consider a Gangrel "confined" when they are in a situation, against their will, in which simply walking away is not an option. This applies equally to social obligations and politcal traps as physical retraints. The rules of Elysium do not normally trigger this bane, but other, more one sided rules and regulations certainly might.

                    Cornered Beast
                    You feel trapped and must escape at all costs. The closer your Beast lies to the surface, the worse this feeling becomes, as the devil inside seethes with resentment at your confinement. You must take direct action, either to escape the situation or to lash out at your perceived captors. All other non-reflexive actions require a willpower point to attempt and are capped by your Humanity. Rolls to resist frenzy suffer a penalty equal to the penalty you suffer on social rolls relating to humans due to low Humanity.
                    Possible Sources: The Gangrel clan bane
                    Resolution: Frenzy or escape the confinement that triggered this condition
                    Beat: n/a

                            Originally posted by Griautis View Post
                            That's an interpretation, for sure. But I don't think that it's RAW or even RAI.
                            For example, defending against frenzy section speaks nothing about this. all it says is "WP delays your roll". There would be very little point to delay your roll if you could roll for a different objective. (Barring super small dice pools).
                            Willpower 'delaying your roll' is referencing the extra rules for spending Willpower on Frenzy. Instead of the normal (+3) to a rolled pool, spending Willpower gives you a turn of freedom as you struggle against the Frenzy. As soon as you stop spending Willpower, you make the delayed roll with a (+1) bonus per Willpower spent.

                            Note that Willpower is not required to be spent to resist frenzy. You can immediately roll Resolve + Composure + Modifiers to resist without delaying at all. The delay only happens if you want an additional bonus.

                            The point of Resisting vs. Riding is that one stops the Frenzy, meaning you don't have to flee/fight/feed from a target at all. Also, there is only one success needed to resist, vs. 5 successes needed to Ride the Wave. Even with average Resolve + Composure pools (6 Dice), adding any sort of modifiers in there, like for hunger or wound penalties, and it's not uncommon to fail the Ride check on the extended roll. Finally, unless you get an Exceptional Success out of the gate, you're looking at a minimum of 2 WP spent to ride, as you pay the cost each roll.

                                                        Maybe encourage Gangrel to act like they're in a frenzy, without actually putting them there? The new Frenzy paradigm is "get what you want right now in the most direct way possible," so encourage Gangrel players to do that? Maybe something like "in a situation in which the immediate goal has two or more paths to achieve it, Gangrel suffer a penalty to all but the most direct one." For example, they need to get past a locked a door. They can pick the lock, climb up to the roof and open the skylight, or kick the door down. They suffer a penalty to picking the lock and climbing to the roof. The same would apply in a situation where a Gangrel is waiting on someone else to achieve the goal less directly. Rolling to keep their patience while a teammate picks the lock or sneaks in to unlock the door, failure meaning the Gangrel loses their patience and just breaks in.

                                                        That having been said, I somewhat disagree with the idea that Gangrel "primal behavior" equates with "instant gratification." Predators in the wild can be very cunning and forward-thinking. A wolf pack hunting an animal doesn't chase it, if the prey gets a chance to run you've already lost, burning more calories hunting it than you'll get from eating it. Rather, I'd penalize Gangrel for not pursuing their immediate goals. "Immediate" being a more flexible term here than above. If a Gangrel leaves his Haven with the intent of getting some Vitae, getting sidetracked from that goal should be a penalty. They're on the hunt, feeding is their goal, and someone popping up and saying "hey, while I have you here, can I ask you a favor?" is not going to get a warm response. Depending on the Gangrel in question, it could be "I'm sorry, I'm in the middle of something right now, but I'll find you later and we can talk" to "Get the fuck out of my way before I pull out your ribcage and wear it as a hat." Once they've fed, they have a new goal: find the guy who wanted to ask them a favor. And anything getting in the way of that is also going to be trouble.

                                                        Encourages players to step into the mindset of their characters, thinking "what do I want right now, in this scene," and actively work towards it. The penalties could be mounting to certain kinds of rolls while being waylaid from their objective, making it harder for them to talk their way out of a hindering situation, until they either Frenzy from having their objective delayed (or possibly rendered impossible), or directly remove the obstacle, either by physical force or judicious use of Disciplines. The main penalties would be to social or mental rolls, I'd think. Basically, the Gangrel gets increasingly impatient and flustered by this interruption until they do something decisive about it. Of course, nothing prevents them from rolling really well immediately, with little or no penalty, extricating themselves from the distraction with minimal impact. But nothing prevents them from getting increasingly annoyed and inarticulate with the cop who just pulled them over for reckless driving, until their options for getting out of the situation all become equally unattractive. . .
