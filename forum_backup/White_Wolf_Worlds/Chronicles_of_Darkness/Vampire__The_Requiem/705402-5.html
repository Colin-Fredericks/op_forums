            I've been pondering the issue of stone-age vampires as well, and while I haven't come up with an idea anywhere near as complete as the ones people have proposed here, I do have a bit of purple prose that I'm rather proud of:

            We have always been with you.

            Wherever brother turned on brother with club or spear or chipped stone ax, we were there to lap up the spilt blood.

            Wherever man coveted his neighbors wife, or woman her sister's husband, we were there to whisper cruel encouragements in the night.

            Wherever humankind knew death and learned to fear it, we were there to embody that fear.

            We thrived in the shadows of your first cities, but before that we laired in deep caves, in burrows beneath the earth, and amidst the tangled branches of forests so thick that they blotted out the hateful sun. When your kind first learned to bury or burn your dead, it was for fear that they might rise to join us in our nightly hunts. You sought succor in empty ritual and superstition, and prayed for the protection of imagined gods, but our hunger would not be denied.

            Although we have always been your Kindred, we have not always cleaved to the same five families which rule the modern night. And yet, even in the darkest depths of prehistory, one might find echoes of what we were to become.

            Beautiful demons sang beneath the pale moon and lured the unwary toward their serpentine fangs.

            Savage spirits rose nightly from the undisturbed earth to stalk foraging bands in the shapes of beasts.

            Hungry shadows lurked among the trees while their owners yet slept in defiled graves.

            Nightmares in flesh haunted deep caverns and lakes, crooning maddening hymns to the twisted gods that spawned them.

            Imperious shades lorded over cowering tribes, demanding toil and sacrifice to feed their bottomless hungers.


            Were these, then, our forbears; the founders of our lines? Perhaps it is so. But the primordial dark held more horrors than these, and many had vanished into memory and myth by the time the first stones of Jericho were laid.

                Originally posted by CatDoom View Post
                I've been pondering the issue of stone-age vampires as well, and while I haven't come up with an idea anywhere near as complete as the ones people have proposed here, I do have a bit of purple prose that I'm rather proud of:
                The brother turned on brother part gave me a very interesting idea regarding the Cain myth (borrowed from VtM, admittedly). Longinus was said to have been damned, when he tasted the blood of Jesus of Nazareth on his lips. What if Adam, Eve, and their offspring, were not the first and only humans? Others existed as well, who dealt with creatures of spirit, and one of them was lead to the corpse of Abel (who had been risen to sainthood by his tribe's god) and prompted to partake in Abel's spilled blood?

                Another reading, in line with the gnostic outlook of the Chronicles of Darkness, of the same myth (and one that may have given rise to the Cainite Heresy) would have us examine the Cain and Abel story as a conceptual one. Borrowing from the Kabbalah, Abel can be viewed as one archetype of humanity, while Cain as an entirely different one. Abel is the caregiving altruist, the shepherd who leads his flock and gladly shares with the creator for the sole reason of pleasing Her. Cain is the exact opposite of God's ideal for humanity, the selfishness to desire a "bond with the Creator for one's own fulfillment. [...] Abel has pure desires, the desire to bestow, to receive pleasure from delighting the Creator. It would appear that Cain also has the desire to bestow, but in his case he strives to win the Creator’s favor, to merit His attention, to acquire the spiritual world. He wants to receive all the Light and the infinite pleasure it contains, but only for himself." (kabbalahblog.info).

                I have often felt drawn to an explanation of Vampires as a distortion and corruption of the gnostic, neoplatonic form of humanity. In this, tragically, it wasn't Cain's act of slaying his brother, that brought about damnation. It was Cain's motives towards winning the Creator's favor, that cursed him to rise from the grave in the way he did. As with most stories in the Kabbalah, Cain and Abel's should be viewed as semi-metaphorical and conceptual. Cain was the first Vampire, only insofar as it is a name given to a specific archetype, form, or energy within creation. In the same vain, Adam and Eve are the two facets of mortal humanity, echoing God's own feminine and masculine aspects. The reason for the subsequent breakdown of each in the material, vis a vis the existence of individuals who may demonstrate more of one archetypal aspect over the other, of individual vampires, and so on, was necessary only so that Humanity's desire to exist and experience individuality (echoing the Hermetic story of the Human archetype falling in love with material Nature) could be fulfilled.

                The Strix, either as a Pangean entity, or simply an archetype without a material representation (and since matter was made by the Creator, without a vessel imbued with Her energies) might have conspired to make the Vampire archetype its own. Either that, or it was created as Vampires distanced themselves from the Divine Energies emanating in the Material, as one possible endpoint for the downward spiral. Perhaps even as a deterrent for vampires who might otherwise forsake their Humanity.

                        Originally posted by malonkey1 View Post
                        That description implies to me, at least, that Vampires and Striges are or were at some point Abyssal.
                        Yes, and it was what was I was hinting towards. I am trying to break this down philosophically. How does each supernatural differ from Humanity?

                        Werewolves are part human, part spirit.
                        Mages are mortals who have reached the level of understanding on the workings of reality, and its manipulation, inherent in all mortals.
                        They all share a connection to the Supernal, and all humans are theoretically capable of experiencing that yearning (to borrow from Sufism) to awaken, discover, and ascend.
                        Changelings were forever changed by Arcadia and the True Fae.

                        And so on...

                        Now, Vampires... What would their place in the world be? What could have possibly spawned them? We could argue that they are parasitic, but this ignores the depth invested elsewhere in CofD, into the mystical symbolism and gnostic imagery behind things mundane and supernatural.

                        We can agree that Vampires steal blood from mortals, transforming it into Vitae. Okay, blood. Blood is one of the four humors, symbolizes the element of air, inspires courage, hope, playfulness and so on. I could dwell further into what Air symbolizes in Hermeticism, but that's food for different thoughts. Vampires steal blood to maintain their eternal state of undead existence in the Material.

                        The themes and questions relevant here, and excuse my dry, philosophical rumblings, but these are things that I feel need to be considered, if we are to discuss possible origins of Vampires in CofD's world, are theft of life, and why would vampires need to burn Vitae to remain active in the material.

                        I feel inclined to believe that early Vampires, similar to Caine before meeting Lilith in VtM, did not possess the breadth of powers they do now. The Disciplines are an extension of the Beast, and the Strix claim that the Beast was their gift to Vampires. So we can at the very least discern that prehistoric, pre-Strix Dealing Kindred vampires, did not demonstrate any Disciplines.

                        So what would the early Vampires' purpose and reason of existence be? I guess, and here we can also justify why Vampires tend to be assholes even without the Beast's influence, it was because of mortals who died with blood inside their systems. Coupled with what the deceased considered to be unfinished business (which is almost always selfish in nature, even in the case of protecting a loved one by committing even greater hubris than the persecutors), it provided fertile ground for the mystical transformation of blood to Vitae to fuel the Vampire's undead existence and raise them from the grave. Perhaps a sin committed by the deceased, so vile that it enabled the opposite of a human's positive potential (i.e. awakening to Magic) to come into this world. Another answer could lie in the historical Gnostic group of Cainites, which partly justifies the Sanctified's doctrine as well. Vampires are there to do what needs to be done, just as Judas had to betray Jesus because of a deeper understanding of what was at play. Just like the Cainite gnostic group, Vampires must not refrain from any atrocity, for some greater cosmic good.

                        Another question that arises from my trainwreck of thought, what did the Strix get in return? Here, I suspect, is where it matters that the Strix originated from some abyssal, immaterial realm. They bandwagoned Vampires as a means of existing in our realm as well. After all, most non-Material entities in the CofD cosmos have a hard time remaining or returning to realms that are not their own. The Beasts they gifted Vampires with, provided a permanent passageway for them into the Material.

                                Personally I prefer to keep Judeo-Christian mythology strictly in-character, and the events described the Bible at a remove of at least a few centuries in my CoD headcannon.                                 Other proto-vampires would be much different creatures. I really like the idea that the Clans as we known them today came about as a result of some kind of occult convergent evolution or supernatural mimicry, in much the same way that the North Carolina Jiang Shi have grown more "Kindred-like" over time.

That way, every Clan could have a different non-Kindred ancestor that came to intermingle with the Strix-Spawn and eventually became part of the family.

The explanations themselves will be rooted in real-life religion, solely for the sake of good fiction. Just like the Exarchs from Mage are actually gnostic Archons.

                                    And exactly your belief that God may exist and be a singularly hateful bastard, is an issue central to the questions asked by neoplatonism and gnosticism. Why is good drowned in evil? Why humanity cannot go for more than 40 years of peace and prosperity, and even then it is only for a fragment of the world's population? Gnostics lament the Demiurge's imperfections, Kabbalists try to see the emanations of Divinity in everything, despite its unfortunate coating in imperfect matter. Hermetics strive to rejoice at the small miracles inherent in our otherwise bleak universe. Other neoplatonists believe it is the price we chose to pay in order to experience both Everything and Nothing in the Universe. Evil is good approaching near-zero levels like an asymptote, not an existence independent of good. Personally, I don't have any answers so I wouldn't expect my games to, either. But as I enjoy this sort of fiction and roleplaying, I research them so as to enrich my games with some post-classical Hellenistic philosophy (that is already there to begin with, in both WoD and CofD).

                                                        The big elephant in the room that makes the existence of vampires in the prehistory impossible is that prehistoric people had way way worse health than modern people do. WoD vampires require a shitton of blood to function. You need to spend a blood point just to wake up. You can take half a liter of blood from a modern human without serious consequences. But just a hundred years ago people were fainting all the time due to lack of iron in their diet while at full blood pool. Taking 1 or 2 points from the prehistoric human is debilitating and potentially lethal. There is absolutely no way a caveman tribe can support even a single WoD vampire, much less a coterie.
