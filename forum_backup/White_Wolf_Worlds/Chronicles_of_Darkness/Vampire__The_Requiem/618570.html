    Extracting Vitae

    08-07-2015, 02:26 AM
    So, long story short, my ST has our vampires (VtR2e) split and near a few other supernaturals. He asked me to come here and beseech your collective wisdom as to how will he proceed on feeding.

    What could we get from feeding on the following splats, besides the vitae itself (assuming we already have the merit from the book)

    * Changeling
    * Demon (the descent)
    * Geist

    Also: does a Demon actually grant vitae. Does his cover allow for vitae (not blood) generation?

    Historian ~ www.cronistasdastrevasbr.com

    I currently ST a... MtAW 2e campaign called "Axis Mundi - Si Vis Pacem"

      Firstly, you might not even need the merit to gain nourishment from supernaturals that have actual blood. Depending on how you interpret the merit, the merit's effect might only be that it lets you treat the blood of the supernatural as Vampiric blood for the purposes of feeding restrictions. But opinions differ on that on these forums.

      With that out of the way...

      Changeling:

      Originally posted by Rites of Spring, p. 21
      Changeling blood isn’t directly harmful to vampires, but it has a hallucinatory effect on the drinker. A vampire suffers a cumulative –1 penalty to perception rolls for every Vitae worth of changeling blood he consumes, as he is wracked by the same misleading visions that plague those Lost of low Clarity. Faces sprout in the environment and babble nonsense rhymes, colors shift and bleed, the outlines of objects warp and shudder. The effects last until the vampire is able to sleep the meal off, though his sleep may be wracked with particularly vivid nightmares.
      I'd say this still works in 2e, unless anyone has a better idea.

      Demon:

      I'd say that if the Demon is in cover, he is for all intents and purposes human, and has actual blood. But maybe, if the Demon fails to Spoof when the vampire drinks from him, the Vampire gains visions of God Machine related things (in the same way a Stigmatic does) for a number of days equal to the points of blood ingested.

      Geist:

      I would lets the Vampire have intermittent ghost sight (as in seeing ghosts for a few moments sometimes, when it is interesting) for a number of days equal to the points of blood ingested. In addition you could allow the Vampire to open Avernian Gates by annointing them with his blood, because there is some Plasm in it, but this is probably not relevant to the campaign, and doesn't give the Vampire any way of finding said Avernian Gate.

              Well, there's no official rule saying that the old 1e rules of tained blood applies going forwards into 2e. It actually doesn't do anything, if we're going strictly by 2e rules. I personally find that, in a crossover game, it actually discourages interaction with other supernaturals more than encourages it.

              If you absolutely must have supernatural blood inflict penalties, then, generally speaking, the effects were almost always "inflict the supernatural's Curse on the vampire." A touch of werewolf rage. Mages go insane with their magic, thus Derangement. Prometheans blood inflict Disquiet on the vampire. Changeling blood inflicted the inability to tell reality and fantasy apart. Following in the same vein, Geist blood would either encourage a split personality, or make the vampire hounded by ghosts wherever they went, unable to get any peace and quiet. Mummy blood would encourage memory problems. Beast blood should inflict a driving need to fulfill their supernatural Hunger, gorging on blood or dominating over their political rivals.

              Demon blood... should be like regular blood if the Unchained in question successfully Spoofs the vamp; a successful Spoof should be 100% indistinguishable from a regular mortal, to the point you don't even need the merit to drink. Otherwise? Perhaps attract the attention of the God Machine? Or perhaps a temporary loss of social connections?

                  That's why I decided to have no game mechanical negative effects for drinking Geist or Demon blood. Both effects can be potentially useful depending on the campaign and if the vampire know's what to expect.

                  Changeling, again:

                  First edition Changeling isn't really consistent on what Changeling blood does, and Autumn Nightmares implies that Changeling Blood is very addictive, without real negative effects. If you choose to use this because it is more interesting, I'd say the effect described above (or at least the nightmares) is a so called bad trip for the vampire, and only happens sometimes (when interesting for the plot), and not often enough for a vampire to take it into account over the blood addiction. Some flavor text from the book:

                  Originally posted by Autumn Nightmares, p. 50
                  The blood doesn’t do anything for him above what a human’s blood does, but it damn sure tastes fantastic. It gives him a rush of sweetness, and each drink is different — sometimes he tastes flowers, other times melting caramels. Being largely dead, he hadn’t tasted anything like that in years.
                  Mages:

                  I don't remember which one of the devs said it on this forum, but one of the ideas was giving the Vampire mage sight that he could not turn off. I'd use part of the mechanics of the spell apocalypse, but I'm not sure yet about the duration, because it is pretty invasive. Maybe one hour per Vitae ingested? I think the blood having only effects in the short term fits with Mages being essentially human.

                  Originally posted by Apocalypse, spoiled Prime 4 spell
                  Along with this gift [Mage Sight] comes temporary immunity to Quiescence and an instinctive knowledge of how to use Mage Sight. It does not, however, prepare the subject for how to interpret the visions received under Mage Sight, and the uninitiated are likely to suffer Breaking Points from the trauma of the Sight.
                  It could possibly result in Humanity Breaking Points, as it feels pretty inhuman, but I still have to decide at which level.

                          I would go for this:

                          WITHOUT the Merit, the given blood grants the Condition: Supernatural Vitae. While with this Condition, the character have the Unseen Sense Merit for that kind of creature and related phenomena. This condition can be resolved for a benefit based on the kind of blood ingested:
                          - Changeling can perceive Fae phenomena for the scene, including Seeming and Doors to the Hedge. During this scene you can open a single Door to the Hedge as if you had spent Glamour.
                          - Demon perceive the works of the God-Machine for the scene, and you can tap on the mystical energies of Infrastructure to actually feed from the leading Angel through other aspects of the Infrastructure.
                          - Geist sees perfectly in "Ghost" Twilight for the scene, and can sense and locate Avernian Gates in a mile-per-ingested-blood radius, and open one without cost.

                          Also, a Demon out of Cover wouldn't have actual usable blood. If it does have blood of any kind, it is being used as any machine fluid and isn't of use to a vampire.

                          WITH the Merit, the given blood grants the Condition: Supernatural Vitae, as above, and also 1 point of the same energy stolen from the other character. This energy can be used for some of its normal uses, at ST discretion, or to acquire the Condition: Supernatural Vitae again.

                          Also, Unnatural Affinity with Demons let you feed from Demons out of Cover (this IS one of the few stated effects of the Merit).

                          If your ST wants to make this blood also have a cost for the characters, they would also get another Condition:
                          - Changeling grants Condition: Hallucination. The character gets -1 on Wits rolls until resolve this Condition to turn a success right into a Dramatic Failure (two beats).
                          - Demon grants Condition: Paranoia. The character gets -1 on Intelligence rolls until resolve this Condition to turn a success right into a Dramatic Failure (two beats).
                          - Geist grants Condition: Death-like. The character gets -1 on Presence rolls until resolve this Condition to turn a success right into a Dramatic Failure (two beats).

                          Each Dramatic Failure could be tailored by the Condition's idea, and it would be optional to give or not the Condition to a character with Unnatural Affinity.
