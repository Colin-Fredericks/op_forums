Either way, the point I was kinda making was that I dont think we should make such hard-and-fast rules when there are exceptions to it. Same with the Cruac cap. I just don't like making generalities when we have outliers that defy said generalities.

                I personally believe the humanity cap exists because each casting of a ritual IS a breaking point. However, the assumption is that you'll be casting multiple rituals at that level, especially in teh course of learning that level, so instead of bogging down the game with multiple rolls for degeneration, simply assume you'll eventually fail and won't act in a way to raise your Humanity back up (ie, you'll still be using the magic at that level). So, I have to disagree with your idea on how Cruac and the cap work, Charlaquin.

                ​
                Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                1 - Well I was kind of thinking of the Warlocks at the time, but I guess I misunderstood how they work.
                I was talking about anyone casting any spell, be it ghost, astral entity, fae, demon, you name it. All mages, irregardless of Path, would have that same "going insane with power" problem from your examples.

                2 - So far as I know, God Machine doesn't exist in first edition and I wouldn't include it even if it did.
                I simply brought it up as a general "these magics are not all the same." It doesn't matter if you include the God Machine or not. Awakened magic is not the same as any other game's magic, with the arguable potential exception of Mummy magic (a discussion for another thread).

                3 - I don't think you need to kill a bunch of people to use Cruac though. It only takes a few points of Vitae and apparently you can use your own blood to fuel it. I didn't see anything that suggests you're sacrificing people on a routine basis to cast your spells.
                Then, why did you bring up a comparison to murder in the first place?

                4 - I fail to see how you can look at Dominate and not automatically call its power abusive. You have the power to tamper with people's memories, weaken or even completely destroy their autonomy, or you can even possess their body and take over their life! All three of those are crossing some pretty massive ethical lines.
                First, Dominate can be used in positive ways. Helping people forget trauma is arguably beneficial. Using it to protect the Masquerade when the alternative is worse? Possession can be used on willing targets. The old level 4 power was pretty bad, but MET altered it so that it didn't remove personality, so that's another consideration. None of that is crossing any lines I'm not comfortable with. Power is just that - power. A tool. The fact its very easy to use in a corrupt way is a different matter entirely. Vampires want to be tempted to use it, then start slipping down a slide into depravity. Part of the game.

                Two, Humanity is not the same as morals or ethics. Also, who's ethics are we talking about? Ethics are tied to specific societies, and vampires don't have ethics against using Dominate against others. Human society doesn't have mind control on this level, so clearly we don't have the ethics to reflect it. Mage society doesn't apply. So, who's drawing these theoretical ethical lines?

                                    I think Chattnos was pointing out that higher Humanity doesn't give bonuses to Theban. So if you're a Cleaver and only ingest blood of animals mixed in with normal food and you have a night job and eschew politics and don't even learn any disciplines, you have the same ability to use Theban rituals as a vampire who was kicked out of Belial's Brood for being too extreme and who strix envy.
