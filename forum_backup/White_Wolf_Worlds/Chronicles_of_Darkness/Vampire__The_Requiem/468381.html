    New material wishlist

    04-04-2015, 10:47 AM
    Sooo... The Second Edition has already been with us for a while, its changes digested by players and its hooks and seeds have enriched the dark chronicles of Storytellers. We have two great books on the way, one on covenants and one on elders, their conclaves and the conspiratorial networks that expand around them like a deadly spider's web. (Edit: plus the one about ghouls and other half-damned creatures).

    Haven't seen a wishlist in a while, things have been slightly slow on the Requiem front (now we lend our new edition spotlight to Werewolf, Mage and the wonderful others) so I figure why not. List your great ideas, things you wish to be explored in the refreshed setting, or things you imagine would be fun to explore (even if not your cup of tea). Let's brainstorm and hopefully provide useful feedback for the amazing people that develop the games we play.

    I'll start:
    • ​Bloodlines: Too many of them or hardly enough, bloodlines offered a more close-knit experience for players. Most had some purpose, lost and forgotten or newfound. It's time we get one of those. My ideal bloodline book would update some 1e bloodlines that are a good fit to 2e setting, add new ones and explore alternative familial connections for vampires (such as Malkavia). I would especially appreciate added attention to what it means to belong to a bloodline, how an Avus would approach candidates or Sire new members into the bloodline, initiation and embrace rites, traditions including gatherings of members of the bloodline from many nearby domains. Also, how bloodlines fit into covenants, which covenant they are more inclined to join (due to a respectable reputation or some other reason) and how they would treat someone turning their back on the close family.
    • Dark Eras: It was a great kickstarter and some really great ideas surfaced and made it into the final product. Truth be told, however, it'd be nice to go back to the in-depth looks on single eras, a la Vampire Dark Ages and RfR. Favourite era? The Dark Ages of course. Fits Vampire thematically and historically. Gargoyles? Check. Gothic churches? Check. Weird bands of barbarian, Strix or Demon worshipping vampires? Check. The Invictus try to hijack the Holy Roman Empire's rise and re-create the Camarilla? Check!
    • Methuselahs and other things you'd rather not see in chronicles you get to play neonate: Why? Because like many other great plot hooks and story seeds in nWoD, fans were initially suspicious of this, just like Second Editions, Imperial Mysteries, etc. If we hated it 7 years ago, it's probably going to be the best book yet. Seriously though, it's time. Let's see the big bad wolves of Requiem, what makes them what they are, and what are the myths and legends surrounding them. Naturally, a book on this topic will raise more questions than it answers. But it should be interesting and will (finally) allow century-trotting chronicles take place. For those lucky of us who have a group of players dedicated enough to see this through, we can't wait to see our players' neonates grow up and put the entire cosmos in danger with their reckless abuse of 6+ powers.
    • More antagonists: The Strix are nice, but for some groups owls just don't cut it as scary. I'm not one of them of course, as owls frequent my parents' house back home. One night we were playing Requiem for Rome with my friends and one of them just sat on a fence across our balcony and stared at us (true, horrifying story). But yeah, Requiem always was rich with interesting antagonists that were never truly fleshed out. I'm not saying playable VII needs to happen, but another example that comes to mind is that of the Sons of Phobos. Great idea, much potential, no clue as to how they function and interact with Vampires. Mortal hunters dealing exclusively with Vampires, groups of renegade ghouls and even things closer to Kindred. Perhaps the relationship between Vampires and other templates could also be explored, even from the perspective of Requiem (as in unique supernatural antagonists like Hunter's version of vampires and mages).

      Another idea which springs to mind is of the kind of monsters that come into existence when the Strix use synthesis on supernaturals.
    • More setting fluff: VtM Bloodlines is what got me hooked on Requiem and tabletop roleplaying in general. The setting was immersive, coherent and with plenty of potential for conflict. We need more Vampire cults, more vampire monsters with lairs, more examples of how covenants antagonise and fight each other and why they do so. The clanbooks and the 2e corebook did a lot of great work on that front, but I would like to see more from the storyteller's perspective. What powers do these groups invent? How are they different and would the covenants want to hunt them down and either destroy them or steal their secrets, if they found out about them? I'd love more stuff to explore with my players, and tools to create an environment as vibrant and realistic as possible. It's the World of Darkness. Darkness should be all around, all pervasive. The DIY mentality is great, and this is not a challenge to that approach. More fluff leads to more inspiration for homebrews.
    That's what I could come up with for now. Also, I must ask something even though I am sure we are a great community and personally, I have not witnessed anything to suggest that derailments of the bad kind are possible. Nevertheless it needs to be said: This should be a constructive, creative process. No idea is so bad or out of place that should not have been uttered. When commenting on other people's suggestions, please only try and find ways to make their suggestion better for a wider audience of players and storytellers.

    Cheers!

                              I'd like more books in the vein of Night Horrors, but I'd like that across all lines. I think a more devoted city book would be cool, perhaps not focusing on one city but with, say, 30 pages each about several. Apparently location books haven't sold very well, though. It would be interesting, but unlikely, to see a book that followed an entire dynasty. The first vampire of this clan, their childer, all the way down the line until modern nights. A book that goes into more depths about lost clans or clans that didn't make it, with mechanics in case someone wanted to play one and notable figures.

                              Really, first edition covered things very well so it's hard to find places that need to be shored up in second. At least for me. Secrets of the Covenants is doing a clanbook-like take on the covenant books, which were sort of a drag to read. Elders were never really gotten into and we're getting a book on that.

                              The big things I see asked for online are NPCs and motivations and 'what things are like' from an in-world perspective. Right now there's even a thread asking for two of those things for Carthians. And I have to admit, when I was setting up my game the Carthians were mostly an outlier I hoped no one would ask too much about. Though at some point they went to visit the territory of the Premier, a Carthian Prince, and Damnation City was a great guide for depicting what it would be like, in-world. I know I've seen a lot of posts with people having trouble understanding how five covenants could exist at the same time in the city, so I suppose a book showing the shifting alliances and powers of covenants and how they work in different cities might be good, but that could be under my aforementioned Domains of the Damned type book. Though that sounds very Ravenloft.

                              If we're just talking whimsical dream ideas, a series of six books, each going into tremendous depth of a faction of Belial's Brood and each including a city write-up where that faction is powerful and what they do there, how they stay in power and notable figures and info on lesser, splinter factions.
