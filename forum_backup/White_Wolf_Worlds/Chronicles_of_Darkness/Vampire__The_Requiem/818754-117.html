    I'm leaning towards the latter, but not sure how correct that is.
    Bearing in mind the forever-quandary of leaning too hard on semantics, it does specify that the passive effect applies whenever damage is dealt, and generally damage from a single source is modified up or down before dealing it to a subject.

    Resilience dots overlapping their active and passive effects rather than stacking them effectively makes the active effect "spend one Vitae to heal [Resilience] points of aggravated damage as it is received," which is nothing to sneeze at, but the stacking read has the benefit of also making it so that a defensive power that specifically works on things that normally cut through your natural undead defenses enables a vampire who has it to bounce back with less downtime, which is in keeping with the literal name of the Discipline. (But again, semantics might not be the strongest basis for decision-making. Granted, this is also the Discipline that makes you harder to stake and lets you recover from superficial whole-body harm with a single point of Vitae — if anything had a strong claim for cutting five days of recovery down to one, it would be Resilience.)

                Resilence active effects are reflexive so.you can use whenever in the turn. That implies that the passive effect occur before:
                You downgrade [resilence] aggravated damage (since it's not an action. It simply happens) than you can activate reflexively one or more effect at once. I.e. you can spend 1 bp to ignore [resilence] aggravated damage (the remaining one) and another bo to ignore the remaining [resilence+1] not aggravated damage.

                I.e.: you have resilence 2 and take 4 damages from fire or claws of unholy.
                You suffer just 2 aggravated and 2 lethals. Than you spend 2 bp; one to ignore the 2 remaining aggravated and one to ignore up to 3 (but you have just 2) lethal damages. You don't take any damage

I'm not sure if you mean that both types of damage are equal (which isn't true since Claws of the Unholy isn't a bane) or if you mean two damage from each source since that's what the math more closely resembles.
                    Also, BP generally refers to Blood Potency, which can also be confusing for people not used to Masquerade.

                    If your example is four damage from fire (4A), the character first downgrades two points to lethal (2A+2L), and can spend a Vitae to ignore two points of damage (2L).
                    If your example is four points of damage from Claws of the Unholy (4), the character first automatically downgrades two points to lethal (2A+2L) and can spend a Vitae to ignore three points of damage (1L).
                    Doing the math for myself I think you're confused about the two active effects of Resilience. One removes [dots+1] non-bane damage and the other removes [dots] bane damage. Agg damage is not automatically bane damage, and bane damage that has been downgraded is still damage from a bane. If it did Resilience would be able to remove twice as much aggravated damage than lethal damage.

                                                            I definitely think that a vampire invested enough to ask around will eventually find some ritual to "reset" the Cleansing. I don't know of one in published material, and I imagine different covenants have discovered different rituals in different regions; it's good story bait to work out with your Storyteller.

                                                            I'm not sure what the question is, re: Promethean Embrace. Is it whether the Embrace would rise as a Kindred or as a recreated Promethean? Or whether the vampire would rise with the Promethean's memories or with none at all? Points to consider:
                                                            • It might simply be that the Embrace fails: the body lacked even the aftertraces of life necessary for undeath. This may be more likely for Prometheans hewn from nonliving material, such as the Unfleshed.
                                                            • If the Promethean was created primarily from a single living body, one might consider the strange possibility that the Embrace raises the original living human to living death as a vampire, as if the Promethean's existence had been lost time or deleted footage.
                                                            • The Embrace definitely wouldn't raise a body as once again Created simply by using the remains of one. A Matchless exception case aspected to the Blood is possible, but probably requires more extreme circumstances, which might not even involve an attempted Embrace.
                                                            • If a vampire rises with the vestiges of the Promethean's false life, its knowledge is probably especially spotty and limited, like rising from the generative act all over again. The intelligence of a Promethean is partly learned and partly inborn from the Azothic Memory, and once the Azoth gutters with the Promethean's final death, the Blood will not stoke it.
                                                            • The body might rise with no human or quasihuman intelligence at all. Fear this possibility. In the best case scenario, it's a waste of blood. More likely, you've given the Beast a body without the Man to hold it back, a natural-born draugr. Or if it is intelligent, you might have inadvertantly permitted the darkness within the Blood to incubate a Bird of Dis into the world.
                                                            I'm personally inclined to maintain the finality of a Promethean's last death: some manner of conceptual persistence beyond material death is one of the treasures of the soul they seek through the Great Work. So if I ran a game in which this happened, I would probably opt first for the rising of a draugr, second for something strixlike, and third for the undead life of the person whose body was used to construct the Promethean.
