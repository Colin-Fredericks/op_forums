                            Originally posted by nofather View Post

                            Yeah it doesn't seem to be that simple at all. I was asking in one of the Monday Meetings if Werewolf was getting a book, as nothing is planned, and RichT responded, 'Not every CofD line will get a book every year between GenCons, we simply don’t have the space to give all 11 lines that many books. But WtF2 will be getting more Dark Eras, and most likely will get more DEs in Dark Eras 2.'

                            I don't know what the money situation is at all, maybe World of Darkness does make more and they're trying to get as much as they can before White Wolf takes over, but it doesn't really seem like there's more World of Darkness books, the books they have coming out are leaning towards Vampire because there's only three games out, compared to Chronicle of Darkness' ten, eleven if you count 'blue book, which I do.

                            It's frustrating, for those of us who are only really interested in some lines, to not see those lines get support. But, again, I don't know what the business side of things is like beyond what we see from the Monday Meetings, which is admittedly a lot. Some writers can to take a long time with things, because of real life concerns such as 'working to make a living,' and while Mage 20th came out, I think, really fast, Wraith 20 is still being written. Then there's the art direction, which I think only has one full-time worker and someone learning how to do it, dealing with the mercurial and limited pool of artists. It's possible that they just don't have the people to really write and organize and put out more books. There's only so many writers who have the experience and skill to be developers. It's not an easy thing at any level once you get past 'this might be a good idea for a book.'

                            And then there's the shift in attention. Vampire doesn't seem as popular as they once were. I see a lot of more on-topic threads in the Vampire forum where someone expresses their moral distaste or disgust in what vampires are and how they don't want to play that, while other games have gotten surprising amounts of attention. And admittedly a lot of the second edition games already have books out from first edition, and a lot of the material can be transferred over, even if it isn't of the quality or with the developmental mindset of second edition. So it makes sense for something like Demon to have several books out, given its popularity, and the fact that some were promised as part of the Kickstarter. And while they may not be out yet, Vampire does have two books coming down the line, and a third planned.

                            Even understanding that, the frustration's there. It was like during the Dark Eras voting, where you had one vote out of hundreds, and seeing the lines you like pass by Era-less. I got lucky with Werewolf on the second go around, but Vampire didn't get much beyond Regina, Hunter outside of Salem.

                            I think I'd feel at least a little better if I knew how things were going with the whole White Wolf Publishing reincarnation. Will they ever actually do anything? Will they be taking all of World of Darkness properties? If they do, will that mean more steady flow of Chronicles of Darkness books? Or a crunch without the World of Darkness income? Will they be taking writers and artists? There is some overlap.

                            Some of those votes have less to do with Vampire, and more to do with the actual Era that they were competing against. I feel like I could run Vampire in most era's if you give me enough era info. basically they didn't offer me much in the way of Vampire Dark Eras that I was actually interested in.

                                                    I'll quote this while echoing several of the other sentiments here: a bit of history reading (ok, a lot) plus some imagination can bridge vampire into various dark eras, especially if VtR 1e and VtM are mined for ideas. With enough research, in fact, you can pretty quickly see how characters (PC and NPC alike) could roll forward into a cascade of events that would keep them busy for a long, long time to come.

                                                    That's where Thousand Years of Night comes in--or at least I hope it does, in presenting the broad strokes of kindred existence as well as why elders are scary beyond just their ability to put so many plays in the game at once.

                                                    TYoN is, as I understand it, a product that will color in an area for vampires that is--purposefully in fact--left vague by the main focus on modern nights. To me at least the stretch of history and the impact of said history on people (kindred) as everything about humanity collides over time is the most interesting part of vampire as a game idea. Timelining characters through such chop is my idea of fun, twisted though that might be.

                                                    So, bring on those thousand years!
