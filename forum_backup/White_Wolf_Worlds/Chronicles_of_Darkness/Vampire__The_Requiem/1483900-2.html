            Werewolf Primal Urge 5:
            Strength 5 (+3 in Gauru 8) + Brawl 5 + Purity 5 + Crimson Spasm (stamina + survival - the 8'again does not come here - is 12 dice with an average of 4/5 successes which means 10 dice more on the attack) 10= 28 dice
            - they have the 8' again which is a great plus, I agree.

            How did you get 24 dice for crimson? Crimson is Survival+Stamina. Stamina is 7 at max and survival is 5. 8'again here is not applicable. on an average roll it gives you 8 to 10 dices more.
            I said there was a lot of chicanery.

            If you dig into the Werewolf merits you'll quickly find that the PU limits on attributes are at best guidelines. In this case to get 24 dice on the roll we have Stamina + Survival + Purity. Our stamina is 5 + 1 from favored form, +1 using totemic empowerment and +1 from either embodiment of the firstborn or the totem advantage. We'll activate the gift in either Urshul or Gauru for +2 stamina, bringing our stamina total to 10. We then add to that 5 Survival + 1 from totem advantages (so 6) plus 5 Purity plus willpower (3) gives us 24 dice total.

            Through similar chicanery our wolf has 6 brawl (plus area of expertise) and 7 strength going up to 8 in their favoured form (probably Gauru) before form bonuses. This all stacks along with the relevant Gifts to give us 24 dice as a base attack pool with however many successes * 2 extra dice from Crimson Spasm (I went with 7 as I said giving us 38).

            Originally posted by Marcus View Post
            Vampire Blood potency 5:
            Strength 5 + Brawl 5 + BP 5 + Vigor 5 + 8 (4 vitae on strength) = 28 dice (+4 protean = 32)
            - force of nature for a + 8 dice more = 40 dice
            - swarm form: roll full pool ignoring defense and taking only 1 or max 2 damages per attack.
            - can do aggravated with protean 4 and 1 merit ignoring the werewolf regeneration.
            There are a number of assumptions here I feel are unwarranted. For Force of Nature you've clearly assumed the Vampire has 5 Stamina and 5 Dex, I didn't assume this because it costs too much exp and the Vampire was already much more expensive than the Werewolf. If you want to spend another 12 exp getting the Vampire those attribute dots it puts him at 40 exp more expensive than the Werewolf and you have to reasonably start asking what the Werewolf is spending all that exp on (Tongue of Flame is always fun). And if you're going to be using force of nature, that that's 8 vitae and an instant action. Your BP 5 vampire only has 15 vitae and can only spend 5 per turn. In other words, that's 2 full turns before you can even start using the devotion, and then you need to spend another 2 instant actions to shift the dots around, and after all that you will only have 1 turn of full physical intensity blood boosting (assuming you spend blood on nothing else). I think it's fair to say that as your Vampire spends 4 turns "powering up" you're going to be absolutely eviscerated by the Werewolf.

            Secondly, you're assuming a frenzy here. This I think is unwarranted. Vampires can't just choose to Frenzy (unlike Werewolves and Death Rage), and when they do Frenzy they can't choose what the Beast's goal is (and "get the fuck away from the big Werewolf" is more likely than "attack suicidally"). Even if we assume the Vampire is riding the wave, this can take several turns to activate and there's no guarantee that the check succeeds.

            Originally posted by Marcus View Post
            swarm form: roll full pool ignoring defense and taking only 1 or max 2 damages per attack.
            I ignored Swarm form since it doesn't directly boost the Vampire's dice pools and that was what I was most interested in. If we're going down that route though, Tongue of Flame is a hard counter to Swarm form. Also if you think about it Grasp of Howling winds should be devastating to a Vampire in Swarm form but obviously that's getting into the realm of ST fiat.

            Originally posted by Marcus View Post
            can do aggravated with protean 4 and 1 merit ignoring the werewolf regeneration.
            ​
            I think it's pretty clear that you can either have claws of the unholy OR swarm form but not both at the same time. Claws of the Unholy also isn't free, it requires frenzy, which leads to the issues described above. Furthermore, if you take Claws of the Unholy, that means you are not taking advantage of Protean 4's Barbed Hands meaning you lose the + Protean to grappling pools (meaning you lose grapples against the Werewolf and it can auto grapple you on a normal attack roll whenever it wishes). Lastly you're doing + 0 agg damage with claws of the unholy. Once you account for defense etc (be prepared for the Werewolf to have at least 12 defense) and things like Berserker's Might, you're going to be dealing very little if any damage

                                                    Originally posted by Father Enoch View Post
                                                    Personally, I'm having trouble getting my head around Beasts. I'm not sure what they bring that Changelings couldn't.
                                                    In the same fashion as vampires, changelings are built on a foundation of innate insecurity — Clarity means the Lost cannot be completely assured that their perceptions and worldview are appropriate to their truthful context, and gaining more power to repel predators makes it more likely that they will lose perspective and also attract more predatory or opportunistic attention. Clarity is closest to Integrity in what it represents, in that learning more about the supernatural events that made you a changeling imperils it (partially by making it easier to relive those events coincidentally), and gaining supernatural power brings you more vivid memory-dreams of those events. The primary benefit to high Clarity is too fragile to not be replaced by leaning into the development of magically-assisted senses to prop up one's flagging Perception pools, retreating to the Hedge, and getting their fix of human contact from dealing with other changelings.

                                                    The Begotten don't have that problem. Their starting point as a monster involves knowing exactly what they are (in broad strokes, at least — further self-discovery is a major part of the splat's higher-level pursuits) and they have a place in the world set aside for them that's built to feed the dark desires they've acknowledged (which feed themselves, given enough time and power). The mild ego-trip of being a conscious piece of the world-soul making waves in the ocean of human thought shares narrative headspace with the Awakened's acts of blindness to such a degree that the main cultural element of Begotten society-such-as-it-is can be summarized as "be mindful of the consequences of your actions, because the world is watching."

                                                    Separately, even before 2e steered away from the specific angle of changelings as creatures of story, Beasts deal with narrative as a social instrument — the Lost of 1e sometimes encountered narrative enforcement at a personal level through dreams and talecrafting, but the Begotten's backyard is the place where the collective unconscious plays out stories that resonate with people in such a way that being in the Bright Realm of superhero fiction regularly arranges for characters to make choices that steer them toward being a hero or a villain and committing to or struggling with the role, and their most prominent unique antagonist type is fed on the cultural notions that You Gotta Kill Monsters Because Monsters Are Bad and can mobilize groups of people to prop them up in the starring role of the conflict that results from this.

                                                    Where vampires and beasts have comparability is in their ability to mess with society at a large scale, but vampires need to build up a sociopolitical machine for that and beasts have a personal toolbox that is mostly hammers aside from persuading other monsters to lend their assistance on the strength of occult charisma. At that point, it gets a little abstract trying to pin down whether having a bloodline pumping Vitae into and out of guests at the local theme park to plant sleeper agent programming in their brains on the Ferris wheel is more or less influential than running a crew of Morlocks in the rafters of the local sports arena to spot petty criminals and contrive their public embarrassment so you can become a shadow-divinity of surveillance culture.

                                                    (Cultural inertia aside, the reasons vampires and werewolves get compared a lot are pretty obvious: they're both territorial man-eating mainly-nocturnal predators of a fairly specific bent whose supernatural state causes them to occasionally flip out and murder people. You have to get into particular subtypes of the other monsters to get that close of a match. Beasts, changelings, and deviants are highly flexible splats, as HeavyArms correctly notes, along with mages and demons; mummies and Prometheans are specific in a form that isn't really comparable to vampires or werewolves, much as the Bound being very clearly Weird Ghosts isn't.)
