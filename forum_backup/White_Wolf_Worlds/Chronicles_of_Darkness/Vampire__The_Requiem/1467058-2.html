    Originally posted by TrueMrMultiverse View Post
    The Duchagne are very interesting! Like the implication they’re a weird cyclical bloodline that rises and falls in different forms throughout history.
    It was a plot thread that (hopefully) expands the bloodline's potential for stories, especially historical ones, but also still honours the original. I didn't use all of the French aristocracy backstory, but there's enough of it there that you can fit it in with the old material. And really, if someone likes the old lore better, it's super easy to use that and just incorporate the new mechanics.

    Anyway, today let's take a whiff of the Gulikan!

    This is probably the bloodline that changed the least in the book; mostly, we've just streamlined it and made it a little more accessible. They're still perfumer Daeva who owe allegiance to a vast hierarchy back in Istanbul. The author added a few plot threads and strange details to their possible origins, but generally this is pretty true to the first version. Even their bane isn't too different from their (novel-length) original weakness.

    The biggest change came with Ortam, in that it didn't really live up to its concept. The basic power was decent, but its ability to produce Discipline effects through scent was nigh useless. So, we kept that fundamental power and went about creating some proper recipes for it. These are more like "distillations" of Disciplines, and unlike the original power, you can produce them with donations from other Kindred, rather than needing to know every single one yourself, which we felt was a bit too much of a roadblock. We also realized, for a bloodline of characters who sniff things all the time, they weren't particularly good at discerning scents. So, we gave them some inherent abilities to go with Ortam; it's a Discipline that requires a lot of buy-in, so I felt players should get some bennies for making that investment. Originally, that was going to be an inherent quality, but my feeling is, if you've got a unique bloodline Discipline, you don't need an inherent gift as well.

    In development, very little changed for this bloodline. The bane went through some tweaking, but other than that, it was a very clean draft.

    But enough out of me; reacquaint yourselves with the Gulikan. Tomorrow I think I'll take a day off, but Monday I'll be back with my dev commentary for the Xiao and Erzsébet, and then we'll get into Blowouts.

    Gulikan: The ones who breathe you in

    "What shall I make out of you?"

    To smell something is to literally take a tiny bit of it into yourself, adding a sliver of its substance to your own. In humans, this connection is fleeting. With our stunted noses and laughable sense of smell, all but the most obvious scents pass us by, barely perceived. But Kindred aren’t human. They can sense the scab where you cut yourself shaving from all the way down the block, and they can follow its sweet aroma right to your door. If blood is life, then by taking in its scent, are you not capturing a person’s very essence for yourself? The Gulikan certainly think so.

    All vampires excite at the coppery delight of blood, but the Perfumers relish it almost as much as the act of feeding. For them, scent isn’t just truth — it’s the only truth. Originating out of the former Ottoman Empire, but now found all over the world, the Gulikan aren’t just better at smelling blood than other Kindred; they can extract something essential from it, a special quality only they can perceive. This ability to draw out blood’s true essence allows them to manipulate Vitae and infuse supernatural qualities into perfumes, soaps, incense, candles, and more. A Perfumer’s works are of exquisite beauty and quality, and turn an even more attractive profit.

    Would you like to waltz into the VIP section of the club and instantly own it? Just a spritz of Serpent’s Enticement, and all the pretty people will be hanging off your every word. Tired of not getting the respect you deserve? Authority for Lords will have mortals scrambling for the privilege of obeying your every random whim. Sick of being pushed around? A dose of Haunt’s Spite will turn you from milquetoast to terror in an instant. More than anything, the Gulikan bottle power and sell it at a premium.

    With such effectiveness and versatility behind them, the Gulikan’s wares are always in demand. Of course, if other Kindred realized the truth of these heavenly scents, there might be a boycott. Vitae is the active ingredient for everything created by Ortam, and that Vitae is most often donated by the Perfumer herself. Every time you take a deep breath of the exquisite jasmine-and-honeysuckle blend prepared by your local Gulikan, you’re also breathing in her blood. The Perfumers certainly don’t advertise this little side-effect of their wares; they prefer to keep their influence on their clients “subtle.” If a person is more favorable to a Gulikan after sampling her stock, more willing to listen to her suggestions in public, well, chalk it up to customer satisfaction.

    The Perfumers are an organized and haughty family, with high standards for who joins. The Old Masters who guide the bloodline from their seat in Istanbul supposedly have records of every Gulikan ever Embraced, as well as lists of their accomplishments. This regimented structure ensures Perfumers aren’t competing with each other in the same domains (with the notable exception of Istanbul itself), and guarantees the worthiest — read: most obedient — students receive training in Ortam. This decadent tower has stood for centuries, though in recent nights, it’s started to show some cracks along its base.

    Objects created by Perfumers are notoriously beautiful, not only for the exquisite odors they produce, but for the artistry of their presentation. The thing is, they don’t have to be. A candle infused with a Haunt’s blood will ward an area with fear whether it’s delicately carved to resemble a pillar of weeping skulls or just a rough wax cylinder with a wick stuck in the top. In short, as long as it’s functional, the form makes no difference. Realizing this, many neonate Gulikan chafe under the Old Masters’ rules and restrictions, especially those Perfumers half-way across the globe from Turkey. Why should they have to pick up and move to a different domain when they can compete by selling their wares at half the cost? Why should their goods be exclusive to the already wealthy and powerful?

    Like most elders, the Old Masters don’t appreciate being questioned, and their counter-arguments in favor of the traditional hierarchy have been rather pointed — lethally so, in most cases. Still, their reach can only extend so far, and while they may dismiss these radical youths as nothing more than common Peddlers, the movement is growing. Why buy from the master when you can get the apprentice to make the same thing for a song? Sure, it won’t make you weep blood tears with its ethereal beauty, but you’ll still be able to make rent at the end of the month.

    Why you want to be us

    We have it all: wealth, respect, and product that moves. We can bottle fear, spray-on authority, and people will pay us ridiculous amounts of money for just a sniff. And don’t you want to smell amazing for all eternity?

    Why you should fear us

    Weren’t you listening? We have it all. All the money, all the prestige, all the power. We have every weapon of the Blood at our disposal, and with one errant breath, we’ll have you too.

    Why we should fear ourselves

    We have it all, but it’s never enough. We obsess over the next scent, trying to perfect it, to realize it to its fullest perfection, and take it in until there’s nothing left… then we move on to the next one and do it all over again. Eventually, “the next one” is going to be “the last one.”

    Parent Clan: Daeva

    Nicknames: Perfumers, Bloodhounds, Peddlers

    Bloodline Bane (The Hyperosmic Curse): Whenever a Perfumer has more than passing intimate contact with a living person, which can range from an involved conversation, to a nasty ground fight, or even to sex, they must roll Humanity. On a failure, [REDACTED].

    Disciplines: Celerity, Majesty, Ortam, Vigor

    Ortam (Excerpt)

    The end result of the Gulikan’s art and obsession, Ortam is their most carefully-guarded secret. Using an arcane combination of alchemy, chemistry, and blood sorcery, Perfumers can render Vitae down to its purest essence, extracting enchanting and terrifying scents. They then trap these scents within perfumes, incense, candles, pomades, and more. The resulting items are infused with effects that echo different Disciplines, based on the recipe the Gulikan used.

    Ortam works in two parts. First is the basic practice of extracting the essential scent of Vitae, the crafting of an item to house it, and the binding the former to the latter. The second is the recipe, the specific effect the Gulikan is trying to create. Not so much a single progressive Discipline as it is a different way to employ the Beast’s arsenal, Ortam is incredibly versatile. One of its chief strengths, however, is subtlety. Kindred are used to guarding their eyes and ears against the dangers of an imprinted command or dulcet word, but few are vigilant against an enticing scent carried on the breeze.

    Ortam Recipes

    The eleven recipes outlined below are the most common, but variants exist, and rumors persist of more exotic formulas that can mimic unique abilities possessed by bloodlines and lost clans. There are even whispers of secret recipes that use the blood of werewolves, fae beings, and even stranger things…

    Essence Vitale Absolue

    Duration: Scene

    Effect: The essential Ortam ability can be crafted with Vitae from any vampire, creating an intoxicating scent that ensnares minds as it delights them. Smelling Essence Vitale Absolue is the same as having tasted the donor vampire’s blood. It can establish a Vinculum, though it can’t increase an extant one, and it provides no sustenance. Any mortals taking in the scent are helpless against it, but Kindred reflexively resist with Resolve + Blood Potency – Ortam, shaking off the would-be bond with a successful roll.

    Essence Vitale Absolue is the only Ortam recipe that can be combined with others; this requires the vampire to spend an additional Vitae per dose, but he still only needs to make one roll.

    Animalism: Tiger Musk

    Duration: Scene

    Effect: A thick, intimidating odor that tells man and beast alike a predator is in their midst. Anyone wearing this scent enjoys the rote quality on all rolls involving the Animal Ken and Intimidation Skills, as long as the target of these actions can smell them.

        Happy Monday, all!

        Today, we'll talk about the Erzsébet and the Xiao. I was going to include something on Blowouts (what we're calling our social gathering system), but I think that deserves its own post, so we'll cover that tomorrow.

        Here are the original previews:

        Erzsebet

        Xiao

        Let's start with the Erzsébet, so that I can get typing the accented E out of the way for the foreseeable future.

        The Cavaliers went through a lot of revision. The 1E version is really great (iconic, even), but I wanted to offer a few more reasons to join them. They also have a pretty nasty bane, but I wanted that to feel more immediate, as aging can be easy to gloss over in a short chronicle. I also wanted to do away with making them infirm, which isn't a bad idea at all (slowly rotting until you wither to nothing), but I felt like the Masquerade danger of their curse was the more interesting thread to follow in Second Edition.

        That said, it took a long time to get where it where it needed to be. My first idea was that Erzsébet have difficulty relating to other immortals, and that this sets them apart as insular members of the All Night Society. However, the more I developed the piece, the more that felt like the wrong choice. The original Erzsébet are a bit shady in the eyes of other vampires, but they're not aloof. It also felt like too much of a social disadvantage for a bunch of scheming Daeva who seem to have conned much of their fortune out of their Kindred. But, I still wanted something that would matter more night-to-night. So, I took the idea of their aging and put it into overdrive: The Erzsébet can now age rapidly whenever they suffer a breaking point. This is just a year, and you get to roll Humanity to prevent it, but those years can really start to rack up if you've been acting like a monster. They still age slowly, but now that's a given, not something tied to torpor. They can revert their aging now with sleep, but once a Cavalier hits Humanity 6, she'll always be on the clock.

        Because this bane was going to be more immediate, I also wanted their benefits to be a bit meatier. Initially, I kind of hedged my bets, and gave them a few extras for the blush of life...but then I thought, screw it, they always have the blush. The Erzsébet are always just a little more alive than other Kindred, and while that comes with a serious downside, it also allows them to relate to kine in a way other Serpents simply can't. I also gave them a couple of Merits and Devotions to represent their immense wealth and their "enhanced" relationship with humans.

        Another cut element was Solace. Solace appears elsewhere in the book, but originally I'd tied it to the Withered. I'll talk a little bit about how this was supposed to work when I do an entry on Solace specifically, but basically, it just didn't fit in the context of a bloodline.

        Narratively, the Cavaliers aren't too different from their original version. I tweaked their presentation a bit, presenting the idea that their main avenue of entertainment is fraud on their Kindred and the kine (in very different ways), but generally, if you liked their 1E version, I think you'll recognize them in this 2E one. And of course, there is the prerequisite David Bowie reference; some things never change.

        Now, let's talk about the Xiao.

        The Xiao are another bloodline that's not that different from the original. They still run a cult that steals emotions, and they still have a schism of apostates who hate them. Because we made Kingjan into a Style Merit for the Sect instead of a Discipline, we also wanted to give the Apostates some mechanical weight. So, we offer a few "anti-emotional" Devotions for them to pick up in the fight against their cousins and their cult.

        This bloodline didn't change a lot in development, though I did ask that the Apostates be made, for lack of a better term, meaner. While they may abhor the Sect, they're just as much leeches on society, just in different ways. Instead of just being slightly benighted conscientious objectors, they're ruthless loners who do dirty work for the All Night Society, leveraging their muted emotions as a way to handle the tasks no one else wants.

        The other thing we wanted to cover briefly was this bloodline's relationship with the Tianpàn Xiao. Although we don't come out and say "they're related," we did want more of a plot hook for their relationship than Bloodlines: The Chosen offers. They certainly could just have the same name by coincidence (they might not even exist in the same chronicle, if you don't want them to), but we felt that a little bit of ambiguity was more interesting, especially considering each bloodlines' differing relationship with the Ordo Dracul.

        Mechanically, apart from replacing Kingjan with Nightmare, the other major mechanical change was their bane. The original isn't bad mechanically, but we wanted to move away from references to real-life mental disorders. Instead of being "sociopaths" the Xiao now have increased difficulty in relating to humans. Their bane is a bit of a riff on the Nosferatu curse, but both a bit narrower and a bit nastier.

        Anyway, that's it for today. Tomorrow, we move on to Blowouts — for real this time!

                            Hello all! Let's move on from bloodlines and get to the special "thing" chapter of the book: Blowouts.

                            In each of these books, the second chapter has been dedicated to a special subsystem that's associated with the supplement's particular clan. In Strange Shades, this was Shadow Cults, in Better Feared this was Necropoli, and in False Gods this was Malkavia. All three of these systems are based on material in the First Edition clan books. But when we get to Kiss of the Succubus, there isn't really anything like that. The Cacophony is kind of that, but it's covered by a Merit, and one that was updated in the Second Edition rulebook anyway. There wasn't really much to add to the Cacophony (apart from a couple of Merits here and there), so that left us with a lot of room to come up with something new.

                            Parties in Vampire chronicles are almost a given. Elysium is often framed as having a festive atmosphere, and Fall of the Camarilla famously begins with the coterie being assigned to throw a party. Parties, of course, aren't just a Daeva thing, but this is true of the other special systems in the series as well. Malkavia isn't tied to the Ventrue so much as its tied to their Discipline, and Shadow Cults are by far more common for Mekhet to start, but any clan could put one together in a pinch (and trust me, they do). The Warrens system is probably the only one that's really unique to the clan, but even then, we were able to include a few story seeds about other vampires having similar, exclusive domains.

                            Still, parties are probably the loosest connection to the associated clan, overall, so we made a point to focus on Daeva viewpoints (both from within and without), as well as their clan Discipline, Majesty. We also wanted to go into some history: what kind of ragers were the Daeva throwing in the Middle Ages? That became a little bit of a micro Dark Eras.

                            I also wanted to cover Genres. Parties come in many forms, but it's all too easy to fall into cliches with vampires: underground nightclubs or to-dos at the opera. Not that there's anything wrong with those, but I thought an exploration of different "kinds" of parties would be useful to players and Storytellers. The Genres are easily my favourite part of the chapter; the author took my very basic initial ideas and expanded them into something way cooler. There are a lot of really fun and weird story hooks in that section, so if you're running a party in your next chronicle, have a look.

                            That brings us to systems. Vampire has had a few social subsystems over the years, allll the way back to Succubus Club: Dead Man's Party for Vampire: The Masquerade. However, the one thing I didn't want to do was Social combat. A, because Guide to the Night already did that, and B because I don't really like social combat systems. I know Social maneuvering doesn't get a lot of love, but I think it works quite well without bogging down players with a lot of additional traits to keep track of.

                            But, if we weren't going to do Social combat, we still needed something to create conflict. And where does a lot of conflict come from? Comedy.

                            While you can play the Blowout system straight (the example in the book isn't particularly comedic, for instance), it was very much born out of my love of sitcoms. Parties in sitcoms always go horribly wrong. Add vampires to that mix, and they go catastrophically wrong. They have weird interlopers who can turn invisible, they have blood dolls dying left and right because the prince didn't get her O negative, they have thousand-year rivalries coming to a head — Kindred parties are disasters waiting to happen, and disasters are always more fun than when things go smoothly, at least in a role-playing game. And the rewards of throwing an awesome party for a bunch of vampires (even one that derails a bit) can be quite lucrative.

                            Fun fact: this is one of the few times I've done playtesting for these books. I always try to get second opinions on powers and things, but this system needed a test run that I couldn't accomplish in my own head. I think I said this above, but it was some of the most fun I've had running an RPG in quite some time.

                            So, here's a little excerpt from the Blowout system, as well as some of the sample "Social Tilts". Tomorrow, I think we'll start getting into the antagonists chapter, though I might sample a few Devotions from the systems chapter as well...

                            Up All Night: Blowout Systems

                            Throwing a vampire party is easier said than done. There’s a reason Elysium Masters are well-paid positions in most domains, and every covenant has its own specialized roles for handling social gatherings. Even the Dragons have a guy who maintains emergency procedures in case a good time breaks out.

                            Kindred don’t throw parties just to entertain each other, though. Vampires are users. They only put themselves out if they want something, and they never send out invitations unless they want something big.

                            The following is a player-facing system for hosting parties and other social gatherings in the Danse Macabre, a special form of Social maneuvering Kindred characters can employ to climb the cannibal totem pole with a little help from their friends.

                            Stage One: Planning

                            Being eternal, Kindred can spend decades plotting out every minute of a social gathering, but even a spontaneous party needs a little prep.

                            Goals

                            First, decide on the party’s goal: What are the characters trying to accomplish? Do they have a specific agenda in mind, or are they just trying to make a name for themselves in the All Night Society? Has an elder or another important figure tasked them with a particular agenda, or is the party a front for a more devious scheme?

                            Second, each player should decide what their character’s individual goal is. Are they sucking up to a superior? Trying to humiliate a rival? Or are they just putting on a show to be seen?
                            Finally, decide what kind of party it is, and how big it should be (see “Genres” on p. 75 if you need inspiration). Who’s on the guest list: one covenant only or every bloodsucker in town? What do the attendees hope to gain by attending: prestige, protection, or opportunity? And what kind of entertainments might they expect: a feast, a game, or a seduction? What about all three?

                            Cheap, Fast, Good

                            Once the players decide on the party’s goal, they need to figure out how to accomplish it. Are they putting a lot of money into it? How about effort? And what kind of timeline do they have?
                            This step follows the old Cheap, Fast, Good principle, more formally known as the project management triangle. You can have two, but that means the one you leave out is going to make your night more difficult. A party will always have an element you have to work around, and that defines how the characters will go about their planning and hosting. Choose one of the following categories to discard; this choice applies a mechanical and narrative disadvantage to the party (see p. 84).

                            Cheap

                            The coterie can get this done without a lot of resources, though you’ll have to sacrifice speed or quality. A Cheap party doesn’t mean it’s poor quality (that’s what Good is for). You can still put on a rave with Solo cups and a boombox.

                            System: Blowouts that lack the Cheap quality gain the Expensive Social Tilt.

                            Fast

                            Your coterie is equipped to handle whatever time constraint their planning falls under, even if they have to spend a lot of money or patch over the rough edges.

                            The word Fast here is a bit relative. Five hours is more than enough leadup time to cover a small gathering, whereas six months is barely enough to cover a large convention. It’s more that parties that lack this quality don’t have enough time.

                            System: Blowouts that lack the Fast quality gain the Delicate Timing Social Tilt.

                            Good

                            You want the best of the best. That might mean spending a lot of money or taking a lot of time, but it’ll be worth it in the end.

                            Parties that lack this quality aren’t necessarily “bad.” Neglecting Good just means the coterie had to break out a lot of duct tape and prayer. They can still achieve their goal, but maybe only by the skin of their fangs.

                            System: Blowouts that lack the Good quality gain the High Stress Social Tilt.

                            Difficulty

                            Murphy’s Law is the only law at a Blowout, and when you throw Kindred in the mix, its many codicils are on full display. Social Tilts represent obstacles the coterie must resolve over the course of the night, with the exception of the one from Cheap/Fast/Good.

                            System: The Storyteller chooses a number of Tilts to apply based on the coterie’s goal and how difficult it will be to accomplish. Bigger goals mean bigger Blowouts, and bigger Blowouts mean bigger problems. However, they also mean the players have more chances to rack up Beats. A party should never have fewer than three Tilts at minimum or more than ten at maximum; consult the chart below for guidelines (see p. 85 for sample Social Tilts).

                            Each Tilt takes a scene to resolve. To start, the coterie has a number of scenes to complete their Blowout equal to total Tilts, but this will change throughout the night.
                            At this stage, the Storyteller only needs to state the number of Tilts she’ll be applying to the Blowout. When the Hosting stage begins, she reveals them as they come up, though she can reveal them all at once if she wants to give the players a preview of what they’re in for.

                            Prep

                            Once the troupe decides on all of these terms and limitations, each player narrates their character’s main contribution to the Blowout. They don’t need to go into detail; they just need to explain how they accomplished the task. These preparations must be within a character’s financial means, social status, and individual capability, mundane or otherwise.

                            As long as this action is in a character’s wheelhouse, no roll is required, but if the character wants to swing for the fences, break out the dice. Failure doesn’t mean she didn’t contribute, it just means she only scraped things together at the last minute. She cannot dramatically fail or exceptionally succeed; this is simply a pass or fail roll. This action comes into play in the Hosting stage, below.

                            A character can also choose to shirk her responsibilities to focus on herself. If a player forgoes a prep action for his character, she refreshes a Willpower. She performs the bare minimum but doesn’t go out of her way for the rest of the coterie.

                            Social Tilts

                            The following are examples of the kinds of problems that might arise during the coterie’s party. Descriptions are fairly general, with plenty of room to tailor them to the specifics of your chronicle, and Storytellers should feel free to come up with their own if none of these suit the story. You can also treat Tilts as multipart, representing several events with only one narrative thread. This can avoid making the party too fragmented, although some troupes might enjoy the chaos.

                            Social Tilts are written with a lighter tone, but that doesn’t mean your Blowout has to be a comedy of errors. All of these scenarios can be played straight, and are good prompts for serious drama. However, you can absolutely lean into the sitcom tropes if you want to. The Kindred don’t like to admit it, but What We Do in the Shadows is much closer to the All Night Society than they would have you believe.
