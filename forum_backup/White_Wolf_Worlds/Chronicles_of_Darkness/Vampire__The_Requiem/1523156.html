    How do you spot an Ancient-Vampire?

    09-25-2023, 10:27 PM
    They're shorter than your modern-person. They didn't have a modern-diet like we do today. It also would make a difference if the vampire came from an agricultural-society, or a pastoral-society as nomads/herders tend to have a more varied-diet. But, usually, the most exceptional of the society would be made into a vampire, so, expect a height of around 5' 6" to be considered, "Tall".


    They might look, unusual; that is, the human-race slowly changes with time and they might have a facial-feature, that was attractive back then, but as fallen out of favor in modern-times.

          It is easier to eliminate someone as "not ancient" than guaranteeing that someone is.

          Top of my head:
          • Ethnic group. Depending on how ancient belonging to certain ethnic groups can discard people.
          • Language. Clearly if the vampire does not speak anything that has a recently equivalent they are either old or from an isolated region.
          • Tooth health. Tooth hygiene is relatively recent and was one of the things that people did not take care of, their regular human teeth (not their fangs) could be an indicator.
          • Age of embrace. People did not tend to live to old age in ancient times, so an old presenting vampire is unlikely to be ancient (except perhaps as part of their curse or bloodline characteristics).
          • Scars and missing limbs. Ancient times were brutal and not only in conflict, the lack of medical knowledge meant that things are minor inconveniences today could have been lethal or life impairing then.
          • Height. Changes in diet and nutritional content, show that we have progressively grown taller, but there are always outliers AND if the person was prosperous in their time they might have been taller than their contemporaries.
          • Social expectations. Opinion on other ethnic groups, slavery, women rights, children duties, etc, can all be reflective not of a conservative mindset but of a truly OLD society.
          So with all of those, you have a reasonable, but by no means certain, way of eliminating factors based on that you have a better than guessing chance of finding an ancient.

                  Some counter points:

                  Language: The big thing of the matter on this one is that unless you're talking about people suffering truly huge leaps due to torpor, most leaps are going to to be within the reach of a century*, so most of the time the language leap isn't going to be that much. THis is also compounded by the problem that vampires, though not subject to the Fog of Ages as in First Edition, can forget things, and as languages die and there's less need to practice them, it's unlikely that you're ancient vampires are going to be using things like the spoken form of Linear B.
                  Tooth Health: We have proof that as far back as the Neolithic Age (and we're talking early Neolithic, about 13,000 years ago) that civilizations were in the business of doing forms of dental cleaning (albeit in the form of scrapping plaque from teeth with primitive tools), and as far back as 2660 BCE we start seeing the development of mouth washes, specific locals for the gums, and application of antiseptics in Egypt, and on the Indian Pennisula, the practice of brushing teeth with paste and a soft piece of shrub was encouraged by the Vedic texts and proved pretty damn effective, and even practiced a form of braces to support looser teeth. Greece wouldn't be too far behind on these ideas, since Aristotle(philosopher) and Hippocrates(medical scholar) both mention similar practices in their own texts. The Olmec people regularly made pastes for the purposes of cleaning teeth that are proven to be effective to this day. There's lots of evidence for pretty sophisticated dental practices for the time, and while modern dentistry is definitely a lot more advanced than it was in old time, oral hygiene and tooth structure from ancient peoples likely isn't particularly any worse than people of low income today, particularly for any vampires who come from the upper classes of their home place and time.
                  Age of Embrace: Again, sort of depends on the region and time period you're talking about, and also how you define old age. While the modern world is setting records for the average regular old age, certain periods of time had systems of medical support and good circumstances that could regularly get people going to their 60s. Contrawise, there's also the vampiric predilection against Embracing anyone too young or too old because guess what you have to deal with for eternity, which is going to be the larger contributor towards certain ranges of adolescence in vampiric communities.
                  Scars and Missing Limbs: Ironically, what you point out is more likely to contribute to them not missing limbs, because too much of a limb loss more likely just led straight to death. The brutality of the times is also not a given? A lot of places in time were pretty decent for living in, again at particular different classes, but even peasantry (and depending again on the time period and location, even the enslaved) very often didn't face brutality in the way you're implying.
                  Societal Expectations: You're actually dead on about this, but for the benefits of people who might not know better, ancient societies didn't have racism, sexism, or a lot of the other isms in the way we experience it modernly(and some societies at times didn't even have some of the ones you'd think they have, like racism), and a lifetime through the lens of vampirism very likely sands down bigotry, for practical purposes if nothing else. I'm not saying ancient vampries are secretly social justice corpses, in advance, just that a lot of the ideas that social justice issues are centered on aren't universal and ancient features of human society and pragmatism has a way of discarding certain biases.
                  Part of the reason why truly ancient vampires are troublesome on a scale that puts them on par with Strix is that, for all that you might think there'd be certain tells for them, they actually likely to blend in more than not. It's nice to think you could spot the Methuselah Zagreus out of a lineup of Mehket**, but honestly you've got about the same chance as spotting someone ridden by an Owl, and by the time you realize you got the wrong guy, your average Methuselash probably has the domain playing to their tune, if that's what they're interested in.

                  *To really get long torpor sessions requires both low Humanity and high Blood Potency to really get going, where as the stereotypical average for an elder suffering from their hubris or misfortune, usually depicted around Humanity 4-3, Blood Potency 7-8, is regularly going to suffer within the reach of 7 years to 40 years of downtime. Vampires can definitely hit those higher points with Humanity 2-1 and Blood Potency 9-10, but those are statistically likely to be outliers even in conversations about truly ancient vampires, and I'd personally wager most truly ancient vampires who had those sort of massive torpors likely had them long before getting to the modern era-mistakes like that are more the realm of what truly ancient vampires would likely call "youth", even if no one else would.
                  **Figuratively speaking, of course.

                      Ultimately, I think the biggest problem is that any vampire that survives as a vampire long enough to qualify as a Methuselah is going to have to have developed social adaptability skills. Given how rapidly human cultures in a given area can change in a few decades, let along centuries, the longer an undeath, the more vampire needs to develop methods of keeping up with the times (relying on younger vampires to do all that for them is a great way to get screwed by them instead). A vampire Embrace when Rome was still a Republic, and that tries to keep acting according to the mannerism, language, and cultural norm of the day wouldn't have been likely to survive the Roman imperial period without other vampires deciding it was too revealing to the humans and doing something about it. While there's always room to play things off as liking old-fashioned stuff, being a foreigner, and so on the help smooth over minor thing, if you can't adapt socially, you aren't making it that long as a vampire.
