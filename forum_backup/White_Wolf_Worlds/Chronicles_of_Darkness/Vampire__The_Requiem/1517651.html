    (1e) Vampire: The Requiem Nostalgia Thread

    07-10-2023, 12:56 PM
    So, I was lurking through the Gargoyles thread and a burst of nostalgia hit me. So I decided to make this thread as a spin-off of sorts. For context, here's my post from that thread.

    Originally posted by Matt Corleone View Post
    Sorry if this counts as thread necromancy or comes off as too much of a tangent, but reading this thread reminded me of why I fell in love with Requiem 1e way back when I was young.

    There was an open-ended weirdness within the whole World of Darkness at the time, especially when all you had were just the two core rulebooks and your own imagination, which was how my friends and I played for years. Gargoyles were a surprisingly common encounter in our games. None of us knew much about the original WoD setting from the 90's aside from the fact it existed at one point and so we didn't have nostalgia goggles on when we played Requiem for the first time (not yet at least, lol). We didn't get the chance to buy Werewolf or Mage back then, so we just made our own rules for lupines and magi because they were mentioned briefly in the core rules. Our group eventually got Hunter and Changeling and a few supplements like Armory and Nomads a couple years later, but that was it.

    Requiem 1e's corebook had weird stuff like Gargoyles, Homunculi, and Hellhounds that were never really brought up again until way later if they even got brought up again at all. A lot of people just forgot about them completely and some of that stuff didn't carry over into 2e.

    For further context, I first started RPG's in late 2003 when I was ten years old with D&D 3.5, or more accurately, a heavily house ruled homebrew variant of it. My Dad was my first GM and he grew up on the classic Advanced Dungeons & Dragons from the 1980's, and was able to simplify the game for me, my stepbrothers, and my friends to understand easier and he also taught me to be a pretty good GM.

    By late 2004, we were all growing tired of medieval fantasy and wanted something darker, edgier, and with a far more modern setting. Keep in mind that me, my stepbrothers, and my two friends were the kind of kids who loved stuff like InuYasha, Kingdom Hearts, Underworld, Hellsing, and Devil May Cry unironically and a lot of us were also wannabe gun nuts too. I even tried to come up with rules for guns in D&D. Not muskets or arquebuses either, but modern automatic firearms. We didn't know about d20 Modern at the time or anything like that, so we thought all RPG's were either medieval fantasy or sci-fi. We knew about Shadowrun and the Star Wars RPG but never actually got to play either of them, go figure.

    Around that same time, Dad tells me about Vampire: The Masquerade and White Wolf and the basic premise of the World of Darkness games. I instantly fall in love with the basic concept. Even moreso after playing the Hunter: The Reckoning PS2 game around that same time and finding out it's set in the same world as VTM. I also found out about Masquerade's then-recent end and the Requiem reboot.

    So naturally, I wanted to buy this game and check it out. I saved up enough of my allowance money and birthday money from earlier in the year that I was able to get both the World of Darkness corebook and the Requiem book at the B. Dalton bookstore in the mall during their after-Christmas sales. I instantly fell in love with the idea of a game set in a dark modern gothic world. There were a few things I didn't care for, but I'll get to that in a bit.

    I spent the first two weeks of that January obsessively reading the two books and memorizing the rules as best I could, taking notes and trying to come up with some setting and rules hacks to make the game more appealing to me and my group. I'd take the books to school and read them during recess, writing in my notebook as I came up with an idea for a campaign.

    Now, let's get the elephant in the room out of the way. Me and my friends never did personal horror. Back then, we always found that aspect kind of lame and boring. To us, it felt like they were trying to make the game seem more artsy than it was and as we came from D&D 3.5 and were into stuff like anime, action movies, and vidya, we went full "Supers with fangs" as some of the fandom may derisively call it.

    On an unrelated note, we also eventually did a sort of troupe play with me, my best friend, and one of my brothers alternating as the Storyteller.

    Our characters were cool, they dressed like Hot Topic mall goths (or Tetsuya Nomura character designs) and we all looked like anime characters (no joke, we'd print off anime and manga pics we found on Google Images for our characters if we couldn't draw them ourselves) and yeah, our PC's even carried katanas, keyblades (yes, really) and lots of guns. In my defense, I was eleven years old when I first started getting into WoD and was probably too young to be playing something like Requiem,

    The combat system wasn't the best for the kinds of games we ran, but we didn't know any better nor did we care. Lots of mortal criminals, Belial's Brood cultists, VII assassins, gargoyles, and hellhounds got their asses kicked by our characters. It was fun.

    We were aware the other WoD games existed but we never got to buy the corebooks for them nor did we ever get any supplements (we were tweens after all) but we did want to include werewolves and mages in our games to the point I even came up with a crude kitbashed system for mages. Rather than using something like Cruac or Theban Sorcery as a guideline, I actually tried to copy and paste spells from Dungeons & Dragons into the game but arranged them in a five-dot range like Disciplines with the different schools of magic being treated almost like Disciplines. It was janky as fuck, but it worked well enough. Magic was treated as a homebrew four-dot merit that mortals could take and they got two dots worth of spells for it

    I can't remember what I did for werewolves, but I remember I tried to stat them up as an elite antagonist meant for boss battles (we all had seen Underworld and Van Helsing by that point, after all) but I don't think anyone ever played a werewolf. My youngest brother did play a mage with our homebrew rules after his Nosferatu got killed.

    We played on and off for over two years, from late January 2005 up until the spring of 2007. My brothers and I continued playing for about two more years but it was more sporadic. We did get to try Changeling The Lost though and it blew us away!

    I moved out of town in 2007 shortly after I turned fourteen and started high school. I drifted away from Requiem and RPG's in general but later got into Masquerade around the time V20 came out. I did get to play a few one shots Requiem 2e and it's a damn fine system that much better fits the personal horror themes intended by the game. With that said, I prefer Requiem 1e, or more accurately, the weird and heavily homebrewed variant of it I played as a middle schooler. Much like cocaine, nostalgia is a hell of a drug.

    On a more personal level, I think playing Requiem 1e helped me better explore my issues with gender identity as all my Vampire PC's were men. At one point, I even played a Daeva PC who was pretty much the yaoi bishonen equivalent of the infamous "Lesbian Stripper Ninja" meme. In my defense, I didn't know any better and the rest of my all-male group was surprisingly cool with it (so long as I didn't hit on their characters). But I'm not sure I'd ever have worked up the courage to transition and be my true self were it not for those gonzo Requiem chronicles all those years ago.

    Now don't get me wrong, nowadays I actually enjoy more serious dramatic personal horror games like the World of Darkness (or Chronicles as it's now called) was originally intended for. Requiem 2e is honestly one of the better games for that sort of play, especially with the systems of touchstones, banes, and Humanity being more tied to being like an actual human being instead of just a glorified morality meter like it was in 1e.

    At the same time, I'm not gonna lie and say that I don't miss those days of bishie vampires dressed to the nines in Hot Topic chic, wielding keyblades, fighting gargoyles and Brood cultists, and trying to score money and artifacts.

    But enough of my own nostalgia-fueled ramblings, tell me your favorite memories from Requiem's first edition!

              I gripe about 1E a lot because I've had to dig through so many half-baked mechanics and ideas for 2E updates, but even then, it's an immensely playable, accessible RPG. The setting is rich but easy to understand in the broad strokes, the character options are very straightforward and evocative, and it feels very polished and complete compared with its predecessor. As much as I love 2E, it's not quite as easy to pick up, and expects a bit more foreknowledge of the CofD setting. If you want a political game about scrappy, street-level vampires, First Edition Requiem is a great place to start.
