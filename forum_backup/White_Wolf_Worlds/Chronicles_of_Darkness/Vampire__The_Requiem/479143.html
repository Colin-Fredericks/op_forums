    Touchstones and Travel

    04-13-2015, 11:58 AM
    I would like my players to be able to travel, but Touchstones (examples) seem to require that they always stay in the same place. While this makes sense thematically, I would still like them to be able to Travel. Am I misunderstanding this? Or, if not, what should I do?

      Keep on the phone with your Touchstone and accept the possibility that they may be vulnerable, or make sure they're safe before you go anywhere.

      Your daughter doesn't stop being your daughter just because you can't get late night coffee and have to keep it to skype calls, but if you're in France and she's in New York, there's not much you can do when your rival coterie busts down her door mid-call to take her away.

      Touchstones where you have no means of checking in with them will be much tougher if not impossible, particularly before the 21st century.

          Unless the Touchstone is a location/building, it should be able to travel in some sense.

          Now, certain touchstones are going to be harder to get to travel than others, and depending on if you want that to be a focus of your game, you can make it dramatic or not. If you don't want it dramatic, just have players choose touchstones with a travel theme (truckers, flight attendants, nomads, traveling art shows or bands, etc).

          If you do want it to be dramatic, then you can explore issues like a Vampire getting their touchstone fired from the local job, so they have more time to travel. Of messing up their local romance and catfishing some distant romantic contact, things like that.

                                                  Given that some players (especially those who started out with V:tM and other OWoD storylines) grew up in the dawn of the internet era and made friends online (especially if the friend is overseas and/or an MMO gaming buddy), I can envision someone actually having a Touchstone that they've never met in the flesh, having only seen them in photos or video/Skype style chat, and only heard their voice in the same fashion. I actually had toyed with the idea of a character who had a long-time friend and gaming companion as one of his Touchstones in this way.

                                                  Being in touch with them through similar electronic means might make that kind of contact easier, although it's also kind of a two-edged sword; if you don't have access to a means to contact them (especially for extended periods of time), you're not nearly as connected to them. Likewise, it might be harder for an enemy to harm said Touchstone directly, but as time passes and people change, that connection—not the electronic linkage—could also change or fade.
