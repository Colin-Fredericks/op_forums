      I'd recommend also asking this same question in the Masquerade forums. Opinions are undoubtedly going to be biased towards Requiem here and biased towards Masquerade there, so getting feedback from both groups will probably give you the most varied perspectives to help inform your decision.

      Personally, I'd go with Requiem 2e over V20 in a heartbeat. The rules are just much more streamlined and make for smoother gameplay. If it was a question between Requiem 2e and the upcoming Masquerade 4e, it might be a closer call, because M4 will presumably be made with an eye for more modern game design sensibilities, while preserving the nostalgia some folks have for Masquerade, and advancing the metaplot if you're into that. I would still go for Requiem because I don't have the same feelings of nostalgia for cWoD (as I learned nWoD first) and I'm not a fan of the metaplot. But if you do prefer the setting and style of Masquerade but prefer Requiem for the rules, waiting for Masquerade 4e is probably the way to go. They're shooting for a 2016 release too, so with luck it wouldn't even be that long of a wait.

                                  It all depends too much on your personal style, interests and feelings. So its easier to help you if you tell us something of your expectations and feelings toward each game.

                                  But as a first warn, only very, very few people disagree about the rules on Requiem being far better. Even more so in 2nd edition. A lot of people still stick to Masquerade for reasons, or play both (as myself), but even among those most recognize that Masquerade rules are way too broken.

                                  The settings have too much distinctions to talk about. They're completely different games, even if similar at first. Since 1st edition, still too tied to Masquerade in some respects, Requiem wasn't based on the same premises and literary background (old-style Gothic Horror mixed with Punk-style social criticism for Masquerade). So they are vastly different experiences.

                                                  Both games are different enough that you're going to have completely different experiences with them. My preference is for Requiem, but I wouldn't play Masquerade for the same reasons, if that makes sense.

                                                  To me, Masquerade is a good 90s game, where it feels like you're playing a bloody(er) version of The Crow. The cities are all run-down and full of corrupt cops and criminal conspiracies. There's a layer of stylized grime reminiscent (to me) of Shadowrun. Certainly you can play a sordid game of personal horror with Masquerade, but I'd greatly prefer Requiem for that (despite 2nd Edition Masquerade's core book saying that it was a game about internal conflict and personal horror). I guess I've just come to believe that Masquerade is more about exploring a setting than about exploring your characters.

                                                  Speaking of which, Requiem feels more like, say, It Follows or Let the Right One In. The style is dialed back, nothing is as obvious, vampires are not the scariest thing in the shadows. 2nd Edition especially is a game about monsters trying to be human, and the societies that they create to keep up the charade. And what happens when a boogeyman (the Strix) threatens to tear that apart from within. I've got a friend who says that the Kindred don't need a boogeyman, since they already had Antediluvians, but I like that the Strix can be fought, whereas if 3rd-generation vampires show up in your city in Masquerade you're all boned.

                                                  Another subtle difference is that the punk elements of Masquerade lend themselves to class vs class, scrappy underdog stories. I don't buy that a Ventrue and a Gangrel would hang out there, because it doesn't fit the themes (though subversion is always available). Requiem doesn't have that built-in assumption of classism, but since the focus is on exploring the horror of the vampiric condition, and every Kindred experiences that, I could buy a Daeva pop-star hanging out with a Nosferatu middle-school janitor.

                                                  Right now I'm biased toward Requiem, because I want subtler games about people, monsters, and their relationships to each other, but I see what Masquerade has on display and I can appreciate it for what it is.
