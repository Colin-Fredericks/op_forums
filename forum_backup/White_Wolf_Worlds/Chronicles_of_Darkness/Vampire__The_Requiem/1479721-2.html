    Alright, let's get into some of the new blood.

    The Verlice are a bloodline of wanderers, Gangrel who develop a sympathetic-magic-like connection to vehicles and serve the All Night Society as messengers and couriers (when they deign to take such roles). Their "Rides" are vehicles imbued with...sentience, let's say, and these vehicles can help their masters get out of scrapes on their own initiative.

    This bloodline didn't change much from first draft to development, apart from some mechanical tweaks here and there. I especially like how they take the sort of subtheme of "freedom" that runs through the book and makes it far more of an imperative. The Needles are forced to wander by their very blood, and though some make their Requiems in cities, most take to the open road. Another thing I like about this bloodline is that it ties into the idea of coyotes, vampires who can help Kindred move between cities despite the dangers of the sun (hence their other nickname).

    Vehicles come up a couple of times in this book (cf., the Brides of Dracula), which was inadvertent, but it's something that really fits the Gangrel.

    So, here are the Verlice. Tomorrow, let's look at the Wickers, one of the stranger bloodlines in the book...and perhaps the most dangerous.

    Verlice: The ones you can't catch

    "You think those walls will protect you?"

    The headlights blind you as your squint at them in your mirror. No one else should be on the road this late — not way out here — so it’s just your luck you found the only midnight tailgater. You don’t see any flashing colors, so it’s not the cops.

    Just as suddenly as they appear, they’re gone. What happened back there? Did they crash? You pull over and step out onto the old cracked pavement and look back, calling out. The only answer is the headlights, right behind your car again, and a dark blur rushing toward you.

    The world is littered with small towns, villages, and hamlets, lost in a concrete bramble tying all the little points of light together. These places are usually invisible to Kindred; they can’t support even a single vampire long-term, and even if they could, they provide little support to build a Requiem.

    The Verlice see the bigger picture. A small town might not sustain a Lick forever, but it provides enough sustenance for a few nights — even a week. The Needles draw maps and plot routes, hopping from one quiet oasis to the next in search of a meal or a job, sometimes going years without encountering their Kindred. Plenty of places don’t even appear on GPS, but a well-traveled Needle knows them all, collecting those tiny drops of blood overlooked by the rest of the All Night Society. The world is a much larger place than most vampires think, and a Verlice can traverse that void like a wolf.

    When Needles do choose to stay in town, they explore, digging into all the little cracks and worming their way through the secret pathways that lace the city together, but they’re always alert for trouble, ready to set out on the road at a moment’s notice. Either the trouble they cause or the call of their own blood eventually compels them to seek new journeys. They make a habit of begging forgiveness rather than asking permission, especially when it comes to the territory of others. A little poaching is easy to get away with if no one knows your face, let alone your name.

    Travel is a way of life for this bloodline, and members will offer their services as couriers and transports to other vampires for favors owed (if they ever return), or just for the materials they require to keep them moving. They take particular interest in local Gangrel, ever watchful for others they can free from the confines of a city’s gilded cage. Needles look at cities and don’t see the neighborhoods or buildings the way most other Kindred do. No, they see a circulatory system at work, moving lifeblood around to touch every person, place, and dead thing.

    To a Verlice on the road, their Ride is their home, hideout, and fortress. The stereotypical Needle owns a muscle car, an old warhorse that was hardy even before the vampire got hold of it, but many use trucks, vans, boats, or even small planes. Few use motorcycles, though, mainly because of the lack of shelter and storage space. Their whole existence comes with them wherever they go, and it’s a rare Needle who gets by with so little that it fits in a saddlebag long-term. The bond a Verlice shares with her Ride runs deep, and most hold superstitious beliefs about treating them well. They’re often overheard speaking to them as if they were pets, and rumors abound that these vehicles are intelligent to a limited extent, watching out for their owners and working to keep them safe. Stories of a Ride’s engines growling around particular people, smelling faintly of old, rusted blood, or of silently turning to face someone when they weren’t looking are just that — stories. Right?

    Parent Clan: Gangrel

    Nicknames: Needles, Coyotes, Trespassers (disrespectful)

    Bloodline Bane (The Stagnant Curse): The boredom of staying still is blood deep for the Needles, and it drives the Verlice to inhuman things in an attempt to feel connected. All Trespassers suffer from a withering of their Touchstones when they remain in the same place. Each night after the first that a Verlice sleeps in the same (Blood Potency) kilometer radius, her effective Humanity lowers by a dot for the purposes of attached Touchstones. Only resting in a new area for a full day can alleviate this bane, and she cannot rest in the same area for a full week.

    Disciplines: Auspex, Obfuscate, Protean, Resilience

    Bloodline Gift: The Ride

    A Verlice can anoint a vehicle with a point of Vitae and spend a Willpower to designate it as her Ride, bonding with it the way a human might bond with a pet. The Ride responds to this relationship. Small coincidences make it seem like the car wants to help its Needle: The doors are never locked for its owner, its horn goes off or flash its lights if danger is nearby, it subtly nudges the steering wheel during a tense chase to avoid an obstacle, etc. If the Ride’s aid could benefit one of the Needle’s actions, once per chapter, the Storyteller can roll the vampire’s Drive + Blood Potency in secret, applying successes as a bonus to a relevant action. In addition, reduce a Ride’s Handling Modifier by half, rounding down.

                                Happy... post-Wednesday!

                                So, the Wickers are one of six new bloodlines out of the ten in the book (eleven, technically, if you count the Bruja's cameo). As an aside, part of the reason we did so many bloodlines instead of our typical seven or eight is because the Gangrel didn't get a lot of love in Spilled Blood, and only four collectively show up in Bloodlines: The Resurrected and Devoted. So, we wanted to make up for a lack of Savage blood. You might've also noticed that this is the only one of our clan books where we didn't do full write-ups of the bloodlines from the original clan book, those being the Mabry and the Hounds of Acteon. To be honest, neither of those bloodlines set our world on fire conceptually, and neither of them had mechanics that were in desperate need of updating. Which isn't to say we won't do them at some point in the future, but the quick conversions in Bloodlines: The Devoted ought to do for now. And while I'm sure we could've had fun with them, we felt it was more important to add some new bloodlines.

                                Which brings us to the Wickers. The Wickers are sort of what happens when you ride just up to the boundary between Kindred and Strix and then vomit on it. The Flock are sort of antigens to the dooms brought on by the Owls, but this has cost them a great deal. They're sort of anti-prophets, if that makes any sense, using their collective, flock-like collective unconscious to stay one step ahead of the Striges. They don't have the easiest Requiems, but when you need them, they're the only thing that will do.

                                Development-wise, the concept was pretty consistent throughout the process, though we played around a lot with their systems, and it took a while to find a good mechanic for their Murmur power. Their Council of Talons cult was also more prominent in the beginning, but I ultimately felt it was better to leave them more to the imagination. One thing that stands out about this bloodline is their Discipline spread. Normally, there's kind of an unwritten rule that bloodlines shouldn't get more than two clan-unique Disciplines, but the combo of Auspex, Nightmare, and Protean was too fun to pass up, and we felt their bane balanced the, for lack of a better term, power-gaminess. Plus, when you've got ten bloodlines, you start to want to break some rules so things don't feel monotonous.

                                Speaking of their bane, this bloodline might have my favourite curse in the book, though it's certainly one you have to be careful with. Still, I like the idea of the Strix's self-proclaimed worst enemies also being vectors for them...

                                Anyway, meet the Wickers, but don't get too close. Tomorrow, I think I'll do the Baetyl or the Yarilo, though if anyone has a preference, comment below.

                                Wickers: The ones who grip your fate

                                "Hear that — the silence? The birds have stopped singing. Run."

                                “Go ask the Hawk.”

                                “Anything but that…” Scout shudders, blood and soot still smeared across his haggard face. His packmates back away, leaving him groveling on his knees. Everyone else knows what’s good for them and gets out.

                                Lucifer takes a long, warm drink from his pint, mulling over the disaster facing his family. Fledgling or not, Scout fucked up. He curls his lip and repeats himself, voice like lead, “Go ask the Hawk.”

                                Scout’s really squirming now, every twitch an apology. But it’s too late for sorries. “Maggie went to the Hawk and came back babbling about monsters. Seared her own eyes out!” Lucifer snorts, slamming the glass down on the table, frothy red liquid spraying across the tarnished wood. “You should’ve thought of that before you fucked over your pack. Might’ve started a war tonight. I don’t see a way out of it. Neither do you, clearly.”

                                “Please, anything else!”

                                The old vampire licks a fang and shakes his head, mentally washing his hands clean of his dumb-as-shit childe.

                                “The only one who can help you now’s the Wicker… and that’s punishment enough.”

                                When you’re backed into a corner and can’t see a way out. When you’ve made a mistake you wish you could take back… go ask the Hawks.

                                The Wickers glean secrets never whispered, each pair of glowing eyes foreseeing a different permutation of a disastrous future. They seek meaning in the Requiem, but their path to succor is twisted and full of terror — for them and their prey. The desperate seek them out, looking for counsel, support, or just impossible solutions. And they do know things, but other Gangrel say they’re too far gone. Perhaps they’re so secretive because they’re trying to create distance. Owl and Hawk, ancient enemies, tug on the same threads of fate, but there’s only room for one monster in the skies, and the Flock will break into a thousand pieces before they’ll allow themselves to be caught. Where the Striges bring vile omens, the Wickers dare to prevent those dooms.

                                If a Gangrel’s goal is to be at the top of the food chain, a Wicker’s is to be the carrion bird, cleaning up after the fray. They remain on the fringes of society, holding court among the derelicts and destitute. If you only see one, take care; they can be in many places at once. You might not even notice them among the pigeons clogging the city streets, the murmurs of starlings inking the twilit sky. When they coalesce, they cut the kind of figure most folk would cross the street to avoid: ragged, soiled, not quite right. In their efforts to outpace the Strix and defend the All Night Society from its fringes, they’ve slipped to the other side of the mirror. If they catch your eye, you might see horrible things, or feel like you’re falling into the void, or be forced to fight against a sudden, debilitating fear. Consider this a handshake. It’s a courtesy; they don’t have to let you see them at all.

                                When hunting, they take to many wings, shifting into murders and rookeries and parliaments and scattering themselves across the weald and suburb alike, joining with the nightmares they create. You don’t want to be on the end of their talons unless you’d like to lose a few pints. It’s easier to feast from a thousand cuts than it is to drain a single corpse. Drink too much from one and their memories can transfer, swilling around with all the other voices in the Flock. That kind of invitation isn’t given lightly.

                                So, find the crumbled stairway, the forsaken ruins, the thousand glowing eyes. Make your request. When they’re ready, the Hawks will find you.

                                Parent Clan: Gangrel

                                Nicknames: Hawks, the Flock

                                Bloodline Bane (The Curse of the Parliament): Long have the Wickers and Strix warred, and the Owls watch for any signs of their enemy. If a Hawk [REDACTED]. On a failure, a Strix will find her before the end of the story. Each time this roll is a success, the Storyteller applies a cumulative –1 the next time it triggers. Once it goes off, this bane is dormant for the rest of the story, and any accrued penalties reset at the beginning of the next. As a rule of thumb, the Owl’s Shadow Potency should be equal to the vampire’s Blood Potency.

                                Disciplines: Animalism, Auspex, Nightmare, Protean

                                New Devotion: The Murmur
                                (Auspex ••, Nightmare ••)

                                A Hawk can mingle her mind with the fragmented consciousness of her bloodline brethren, granting access to various memories, patterns, and esoteric secrets. This augury takes the form of a horrifying, existential vision, or a riddle of fragmentary images tainted by cajoling whispers. The prophecy requires decoding once connection to the Murmur is severed, but it contains the ability to disrupt fate in a significant way, including preventing an event, a disastrous decision, or even a death.

                                This Devotion costs no Experiences if the vampire meets the prerequisites.

                                Cost: Upon successfully using this Devotion, subtract the number of times the vampire has used the effect in the current story from six. She suffers a breaking point at that level if it equals or is lower than her current Humanity. If the resulting number is negative, she takes a Humanity 1 breaking point with the difference as a penalty to her player’s roll.

                                Action: Reflexive

                                Duration: One night per Blood Potency
