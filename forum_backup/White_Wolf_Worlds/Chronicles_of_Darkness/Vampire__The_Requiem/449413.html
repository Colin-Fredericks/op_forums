    A bunch of questions

    03-19-2015, 12:57 PM
    Hi, all!

    I’m about to start a new Requiem (2nd Edition) campaign set in London as a storyteller, and I have a bunch of questions about the game and the system in general.
    Here they are:

    Predatory Aura Conditions: after resolving one of them, the influenced character cannot be subject of the condition again for a full month. My question is: Why? Why it has to be an entire month? I’m probably gonna play out my campaign night to night. A month would probably pass after like 10 sessions. Probably these conditions will all come into play in just one session or two, and then I have to wait a whole in-game month to see them again! Am I missing something here? Because in my opinion the predatory aura conditions are very powerful - both strategic and thematic - tools and it would be a pity if my players couldn’t use them frequently.

    Invictus Oaths: What if a character could force the Invictus Notary though intrigue and conspiracy to administer an Oath, but then the character, as a liege, wouldn't have enough Experiences to actually buy the merit.

    Feeding (With Dice) while hungry or starving: if my character succeeds the feeding roll, do I still have to roll for frenzy, since I’m actually spilling some blood while hungry (hence running a frenzy scene)? But maybe I’m missing something here: does hunger frenzy is triggered just by reaching 4 or fewer Vitae or by actually having 4 or fewer Vitae AND sensing any spilled blood?

    Thanks!

      I just finished a long term 2nd edition game, and I found that in a night-to-night game, Humanity loss and Predatory Aura contests can really pile up. The immunity lets the character have a bit of breathing room. That said, my game took place over only a month or so, and there was a point in downtime where I pretty much arbitrarily reset the immunities. If you want the ebb and flow of the game to be a bit more high drama, the system isn't hurt at all by making the immunity shorter.

      Edit: And when it comes to NPCs, I'm a lot less stringent on that. Unless the character has been zapping an NPC with Predatory Aura way too much, you can be looser with NPC immunity and not hurt anything.

      If you hunt successfully, I would say you still have to make the roll. I don't think the book says either way (?), but I feel like that's the risk of getting low on Vitae. On the other hand, I tend not to make characters roll for frenzy when accessing Herds, but that's me.

      Regardless, you make the frenzy check if you taste / smell blood while you're Hungry or Starving, not when you reach those levels.

          Predatory Aura: Are you talking the winner or the loser? The winner can keep blasting people whenever they want to earn the bonuses associated with a flexing / victorious beast. Once someone is on the losing end, however, and get the negative condition - that does have both a sunset clause (BP of Aggressor in Nights) and a refractory phase (The one month). Mainly I think this is just there so that you can't get dog-piled over and over with the same condition - or to a very lesser extent to avoid a factory run on resolving the condition over and over again for beats (though I doubt that would happen at a real table). That all being said - there is a clause about letting conditions go that don't matter any more to the story. It makes sense that the reverse would hold true, if it is important to the story / drama / character - reapply the condition. I'd probably fiddle with that mechanic a little bit, making sure that the subsequent Lash Out was of higher stakes in some manner, but that's not necessary either. Also note - I differentiate between resolving the Bestial, Competitive, Wanton conditions and it fading out naturally. If it just fades naturally, I don't give the one-month free time.

          Invictus Oaths: I'd either let the player go into XP debt to make the Merit "permanent" and applicable to the Sanctity of Merits (the next expriences they get must be attributed to this debt before they can buy anything new.) Or I would treat it just like any other Merit gained through Roleplay / Story - mark the dots with X's so that when/if they are lost, the player is not compensated.

          Feeding: It always has to be a legitimate trigger, and starving/hungry just adds a modifier when frenzy is checked for. You need to decide how much of a stickler for triggers you want to be for your game. For large games, I pretty much ignore bloodsmell triggers unless the character has also been wounded or there's some additional extenuating circumstance - low willpower, just suffered a breaking point, etc. For a more intimate game - especially a game focusing on newly created vampires, I'd play with those triggers a little more heavily. But again, always remember that drama is king, so if the trigger is just going to slog down play or get in the way of a dramatic moment, feel free to ditch it.

              I think it was to do with making sure you can't get perpetually stuck with the Predatory Aura Conditions. I tend to remove the once a month cap for similar reasons, and I haven't had too many problems with it. If some vampires are rolling around with those conditions a lot, it goes a long way in explaining why so many are dicks. :P

              Joking aside, I suggest you remove the cap, it should be fun.

              @Oaths, that's a non-issue provided the Oath in question isn't a mutual Oath. The vassal pays for the Oath, not the liege in those Oaths.

              @Frenzy, Frenzy isn't hardcoded to happen, but rather, it happens where dramatically interesting. The modifiers are both guidelines for what kinds of situations it's likely to occur in, and how hard it is to resist in those situations. They're not really ironclad, but serve as a great starting point.

              I wouldn't treat Hungry (-2) as a condition for Frenzy all on its own, but if someone had a trivial open wound (+1) or a massive open wound (-2) and I felt it would enhance the drama of the scene, I might have my player roll for it.

              A situation where they're Starving (-4) would probably increase the likelihood of me asking for a Frenzy roll, because that vampiric life is hard, dammit.

              Other potential feeding scenarios where I might ask for a check against Frenzy: If one has Bestial or Wanton, POSSIBLY, and PARTICULARLY if they're Hungry (-2). If it's going to add to your game to have them frenzy in a situation like that, it's definitely a great way to demonstrate the downsides of being Kindred, that you aren't always in control.
