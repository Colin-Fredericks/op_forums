                        I mean, their biological functions don't... err... function. I certainly wouldn't object to describing their condition as "a kind of life", as they are objectively animate, conscious, and sentient, and not decomposing. But like, their hearts don't beat, which means their blood doesn't flow, which means its coagulated in their veins and their flesh is pale and cold. No two ways about that.
                        For the sake of plausibility and consistency, not to mention minimizing the gross 'zombie' quotient, I rather prefer to assume vampire blood never, ever coagulates and remains entirely fluid as long as it is within their body, even if it doesn't flow if the blush of life is not active. I mean, it is fairly easy to prevent coagulation of static blood even for mundane science and technology, or blood banks wouldn't exist. Surely the Requiem can do much better. Just assume the Kindred naturally have some kind of really, really good anticoagulant, and use their efficient voluntary control of their own body tissues and fluids (short of being able to violate magical conservation of energy) to deal with potential blood loss. Coagulation seems a needless, pointless complication for them. If would mean they'd have to re-fluidify their blood every time they use the blush of life, or spill Vitae for all kinds of uses. It would be much more complex and odd than suppressing coagulation in the first place.
