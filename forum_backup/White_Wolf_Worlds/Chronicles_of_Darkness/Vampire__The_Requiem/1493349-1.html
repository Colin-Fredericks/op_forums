    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    I must say that when I was considering Asphyx in 2e, I felt it may be appropriate to be a form of Devotions for Cachexy, considering that the Morbus and the Trenchers have some similarity and both poison and disease share a lot of thematic space, especially as Cachexy is already a Discipline in 2e. Any special reason why you didn't go with that direction?

    Also, I loved the poisonous Revenant variant. Really has the "living Discipline" feeling I like and a cool and fitting concept overall.
    Unfortunately, the Cachexy in DE is also pretty underwhelming and falls into the trap of making players pay an XP tax for making their character type playable. Besides, I rather like *not* connecting the Trenchers and Morbus more than they need to be. While there is some overlap between plague-carriers and the poisonous dead, I think their thematics are different enough that such a connection would ultimately be a disservice to both. Making Asphyx a set of Cachexy devotion would have required some overlap between the two that just would not have thematically gelled very well IMO.

    On a strictly mechanical level, CofD 2e has a sometimes very clear, sometimes very vague line in how it abstracts diseases vs. poisons (something I had to delve a bit into to write Asphyx) even though one could argue that they could easily use mostly the same mechanics. For Cachexy vs. Asphyx this is another reason to separate the two though.

    The basic idea of "ey, hardcore revenants" came to me as a hard brain wave almost as soon as I got the instructrion to get away from the old Bane in the outline. The main problem was that it required quite a bit more additional word count than a more conventional Bane and thanks to Asphyx, wordcount was already in short supply. Yossarian did a great job on tightening things up more neatly in the end

                Tell you what, I'll cover them on Friday! Today, though, we're doing one you illustrated: The Sacrosanct of "Clan" Hypatian.

                Including new bloodlines in this book was pretty much in the outline from the start. Partly, that was just a general interest in expanding on the lost/minor clans from Spilled Blood, but it was also an attempt to fill in some gaps: I had to cut a sidebar talking about this for space, but we've covered a few "Ancient" bloodlines in previous works, namely, the Apollinaire, En, Mystikoi, and Septemi (though the En ended up in a slightly different form). For both those reasons, I really wanted to get some fresh concepts in this book.

                My overarching direction for these bloodlines was that they should be weird. Lost clans are, by their nature, evolutionary dead ends that never managed to find a true niche in the All Night Society. To my mind, that means their bloodlines are going to be pretty damn strange. That's not to say the typical clans don't have weird offshoots, but for lost clan bloodlines, that's less an exception and more a rule.

                That said, the Sacrosanct are probably the least weird of these five. They get on pretty well with the rest of the All Night Society, as far as Hypatians go, and they do provide products and services! Just don't ask about the price.

                I really liked the author's concept of trading memories and aptitudes for raw advantages. That fit really well with the clan's overall themes, and it gave their procedures a more specific flavor than just being more body-part-based alchemy. The Hypatians were one of my clans in Spilled Blood, and I always regretted that I didn't have room for more Blood Chymistry powers, so this was a good opportunity to get more of those into the world. Notably, other Blights can learn Sacrosanct procedures, though you'd have to convince one to teach you first (and you might not come out of it remembering much else). I also wanted an opportunity to fix a little error I inadvertently left in the original version of Blood Chymistry, so there's a short sidebar errata'ing that.

                In terms of development, this one had a bit of ink spilled over it. There was originally a more elaborate system of humors and sacrificed ephemera, but I ultimately found there were a few too many capitalized nouns being thrown around, which is always a danger in a Vampire book.

                Anyway, here are the Sacrosanct. Tomorrow, I'll be talking about the Sta-Au, the Wicked Dead of the Wild West. In the meantime, if you're liking the book, please consider leaving us a star rating and/or a review! I know I'm a broken record, but it's so much easier to get people who aren't familiar with our work to buy community content when they see that our stuff is highly rated.

                The Sacrosanct: The Ones Who Transcend You

                "Matter over mind."

                Personality is not as cohesive as people think. The whole is greater than the parts, a patchwork of lessons, traumas, and mental flotsam that accumulates over the course of a life. Good or bad, what we are — who we are — is an illusion of consciousness, one that can be easily revealed with a blow to the head or a vampire’s command. Perhaps it would be better not to put so much weight on such things. Maybe it would be better to drop the anchor of self to become whatever “you” is required in a given moment: an Übermensch for all occasions.

                This is the goal of the Sacrosanct, one of the only bloodlines of the Hypatian pseudo-clan. Via the annihilation of “self,” these Blights believe they’re getting close to what Hypatia always hoped for her experiment: perfection.

                The Sacrosanct are eager and willing to become whatever’s needed. Nothing so crude as Protean or as subtle as Coils of the Dragon; a Tweaker changes with a gulp, attaining whatever talent the moment calls for, specialties and affinities shrugged on and off as easily as clothes, perfection only ever a potion away. However, for every reaction, there is an equal and opposite reaction, and so a Tweaker’s apotheosis burns away metaphysical pieces she no longer needs: talents, skills, and even cherished memories. To achieve the magnum opus, one must dissolve those things that anchor the mind, and with Alkahest, the universal solvent of the mind, they just might do it.

                In the heyday of the alchemical occult, many attempted to follow this Sacrosanct path and met Final Death, and perhaps that’s why there are so few Chameleons, even among a “clan” that barely scrapes the triple digits. The 21st century started with nary a whisper about these prodigal Blights, and success only came when the first Tweakers sponged up the rapid advances in genetics, chemistry, and pharmacology with one hand and dusted off treasures lost in medieval medicine, occultism, and humorism with the other.

                Merging these studies, they found a way to advance their Blood Chymistry arts to new and horrible levels, temporarily changing their skills, holding back the ravages of the Beast, and adjusting their clients on demand. But when perfection is fleeting, you have to keep chasing it. You have to find ways to reach higher heights … and those who’ve tasted your potions are going to want more, too. Supply and demand become compelling reasons to keep Tweakers safe from the baying of the mob.

                Tonight, they’re one in a million, using their rarity as a testament to how difficult it is to obtain what they offer. As highly exclusive consultants, they help tailor Princes, assassins, and politicians to rise to specific challenges, and on the black market, their humors fetch prices high enough to fund their research labs and unsanctioned experiments. Behind closed doors, they steal Vitae to bestow themselves with powers of the Blood that should be beyond their reach. They are magicians and hucksters and court advisers, but like all Blights, it’s only a matter of time before they arouse too much ire and bring all they’ve built crashing down. If the brightest flames burn out fastest, then Tweakers self-destruct like, well, a firestorm. But hey, it’s better to burn out than to fade away — even if they have to take half the domain with them.

                Why you want to be us

                Bloodily Embracing victims, suckling like babes from pliant ghouls, chasing a vicious cycle of bestial hunger? Such unevolved approaches to immortality. There’s a more elegant solution, a tidier way to live out eternity. Free yourself from your primitive shackles of self. We can give you the power to face any challenge, to be all things to all people. Move past the petty weakness you call a personality and become the god you were always meant to be.

                Why you should fear us

                Knowledge is more than power; it’s perfection. Our humors let us stand above the weaknesses of vampiric nature, unrestrained by humanity, unhindered by frenzy. With the right humor, neither the Beast nor even our own bodies can hold us back from achieving what we want. We’ll learn your weaknesses and customize ourselves into the perfect antithesis. We’re better. More versatile. We’ll not just outpace you — we’ll replace you.

                Why we should fear ourselves

                We can’t make the magic happen without the right ingredients. Not just the Divine Fire, but the raw materials, and there’s only so much you can hollow out till you’ve got nothing left. How much are you willing to forget in the pursuit of those great heights? How thin can you scrape the façade of perfection before it cracks, and when will your mind crumble from the inside out? Prometheus could last eternity regrowing his liver — but you? The fire is burning through you with every slip. Sooner or later, it consumes you.

                Parent Clan: Hypatian (Night Horrors: Spilled Blood, p. 90)

                Nicknames: Chameleons, Tweakers (derogatory)

                Bloodline Bane (The Corrosive Curse): Slowly but surely, the Sacrosanct dispose of those elements of themselves that hold back perfection. Unfortunately, the alchemical reactions inherent to this process never really stop reacting. Each time a Chameleon fails to resist detachment, roll her new Humanity rating. On a failure, she gains the Amnesia Condition regarding an important part of her Requiem; work with the Storyteller to come up with something substantial but not debilitating. This memory loss lasts until she gains the Humanity level she lost the memory at, though Amnesia can be resolved in the usual way. The narrative effect of this bane is cumulative; each failure removes another piece of the vampire’s memory, but mechanically, the character only ever gains one instance of Amnesia, the resolution of which may be a trauma all its own.

                Disciplines: Animalism, Dominate, Nightmare, Resilience

                In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

                The Ruins of Empire — 1893–1924 CE (DE1, p. 426): Not even a century has passed since Hypatia first jotted down her findings, but the Sacrosanct are stretching their newborn hands to the Divine Fire, yearning for an eternal future. In an age of paraded mummies stolen from tombs and social séances striving to peer through to the other side, the first Tweakers clumsily try to perfect themselves. With the bloodline in its infancy, and their clan’s unconventional origin — made rather than Embraced — still dividing the Kindred community, the Chameleons strain for legitimacy. Thus begins their enduring drive to be deemed good enough, to be useful, to be indispensable. Perhaps something buried deep in the desert might teach them a thing or two about the alchemy of eternal life and the ravages of memory...

                New Merit: Alkahest (• to •••••)

                Prerequisites: Hypatian, Blood Chymistry •

                Effect: Purity comes from dissolution. The Sacrosanct can turn their victims into living athanors, dissolving away the bonds that hold their memories in place to produce a caustic substance called Alkahest, a brassy, shimmering liquid that can act as a “succedaneum,” a substitute for Pyros in both Blood Chymistry and Hypatian Embraces. In return, the victim perfects a particular mundane ability to its pinnacle, all at the cost of a little piece of herself.

                Drawback: This substance is only half as potent as the genuine article, so the ratio of required Pyros in Blood Chymistry recipes is doubled when using Alkahest instead.

                Cost: Variable Vitae

                Requirement: Your character must pump the cost through the body of a conscious victim.

                Dice Pool: Resolve + Science + Alkahest vs. Stamina + Blood Potency (if unwilling)

                Action: Contested; resistance is reflexive

                Roll Results

                Success: Your character produces one point of Alkahest per Vitae spent. Each point lasts a night, dissolving into nothing at the next sunrise.

                Meanwhile, the subject gains the Amnesia Condition (Vampire, p. 301), forgetting an important aspect of who they are; typically, this is random, though some Tweakers pretend they can extract specific memories. The process fills in new gaps in her mind, granting three dots in a Skill of her player’s choice.

                The Amnesia Condition isn’t (quite) literal; the subject’s memory is more askew than absent. Amnesia lasts for nights equal to Alkahest produced, and the subject cannot be used to produce the substance again until it resolves. When Amnesia fades, so do the Skill dots.

                Some Chameleons are rumored to be able to make these changes permanent, but this would be a closely guarded trade secret. If it were true…

                Exceptional Success: Your character produces one point of Alkahest per Vitae spent, plus two for each dot in this Merit. This does not extend the period Amnesia lasts.

                Failure: Your character produces sludge.

                Dramatic Failure: The subject dies or falls to torpor and cannot be used as an athanor again.

                                        Alright, let's look at the Sta-Au. From a distance.

                                        This is one of the very, very few "any/multi-clan" bloodlines from First Edition, which for some reason was a thorny issue in early Requiem. Infamously, the Moroi bloodline write-up includes a sidebar that goes out of its way to declare that they're in fact two bloodlines that are exactly the same. To a lesser extent, then Mortifiers of the Flesh made you jump through several hoops if you wanted to play a non-Daeva. At any rate, that reluctance seems to have tapered off by the time Ancient Bloodlines came out, because there don't seem to be any caveats about who can join. At any rate, this is all to say that I like all-clan bloodlines. I feel like a future project might be to do a book of them, although I have to admit I'm a little bloodline'd out at this juncture.

                                        Anyway, my big directive for the Sta-Au was to make them more playable. Their original weakness was that they could never buy back Humanity which, uh, certainly scales with that trait, but is maaaaaaaybe a little harsh; direct quote from my outline: "Boy their bane goes hard." It's a weakness that's interesting in concept but in practice (and with apologies to the sidebar on p. 35 of Ancient Bloodlines) mostly relegates them to Storyteller character territory, in which case that particular weakness doesn't really matter anymore. I'm sure one could play a Sta-Au with their original bane, but even ignoring how narrow it is, it's a tad boring. As such, I really like their new bane. We have a few more "externality" banes in this book (e.g., the Brothers of Ypres, the Nehebkau), in that they don't directly affect the character, but still influence behaviour. This bane also integrates the Land of the Worms more intimately with the bloodline, making them direct vectors for its spread.

                                        Speaking of the Land of the Worms, I'm really happy with how our version turned out. It's not too heavy mechanically, but it's a bit more modular than the original. I'm a big fan of Mysterious Places, so I'm always happy to fit them in where we can.

                                        I don't have much to say about the development process here, as not too much changed from the final draft apart from a couple of tweaks here and there. This was one of the bloodlines that was really solid right from the start. My only major addition was adding an additional Dark Era (the Lifting of the Veil one), as well as the "Who We Are Tonight" list. My biggest contribution was mostly the art.

                                        Anyway, I believe the author wanted to talk a bit about the writing process here, so I'll end on that note.

                                        So, here are the Sta-Au. As always, if you like the book, consider leaving us a review. Tomorrow, let's finish the week with the chilling Caporetti...

                                        Sta-Au: The Ones Who Slaughter You

                                        "Wolves to them; sheep to us."

                                        Across the Great Plains, Kindred tell tales of the Sta-Au, ashen-skinned boogeymen with jagged teeth like rows of knives, covered in trinkets and trophies of human bone. This bloodline makes its home in the inhospitable badlands of the region, where all trespassers are dealt with accordingly. “Normal” vampires in the area cleave to humans as much as possible, spending their nights in cities and only traveling on heavily trafficked roads if possible; anything to avoid facing the Sta-Au and their hunting parties, or coming across the skull-adorned borders of the Land of the Worms.

                                        The Wicked Ones live up to their moniker. They have no regard for morality, instead celebrating depravity and cruelty as if they were virtues. The Sta-Au act as if they’re the Beast incarnate, seemingly giving in to every dark urge and instinct with abandon. Yet unlike the draugr, they’re not mindless monsters incapable of higher reasoning — which only serves to make them more insidious.

                                        The Sta-Au take their name from the ghosts of Blackfoot belief, the wicked dead who choose to harry the living instead of passing on to the Great Sand Hills. Unlike their incorporeal counterparts, the Wicked Ones believe all vampires have failed to properly die, reprobates eternally stuck in their corpses. To break free from these shackles, the Sta-Au die a second time, bringing themselves ever closer to a true death. After that, it’s just a matter of finding the passage to an afterlife befitting such vile creatures as vampires and, finally, proving worthy of entrance.

                                        The Sta-Au’s afterlife is said to link to the locations collectively known as the Land of the Worms (p. 91). It’s within the Land of the Worms that Sta-Au traditionally dwell, reinforcing its ruinous resonance and preparing themselves for what lies beyond. By deliberately and carefully shedding each remaining scrap of his mortal life, a Wicked One shall become a pure monster, unfettered by any inhibitions yet not reduced to a simple animal. When he’s freed from the limitations of his Humanity, the Twice-Dead sheds his human body and enters his new hunting grounds.

                                        True monsters don’t abide by any rules, but until the Sta-Au can escape their immortal coils, rules serve a crucial purpose. As a group, the Sta-Au would’ve self-destructed long ago were it not for the traditions and customs they developed centuries ago. One seeker’s path to transcendence shall not interfere with another’s, and a threat to one’s road is a threat to all. While violence is the natural way to settle conflicts between Wicked Ones, it’s ritualized, and the victor is expected to respect their opponent’s defeat. Even bitter rivals cease their attacks once the other lies in torpor, and they even band together should another Twice-Dead break one of their few taboos, or fail their quest and fall to the Beast.

                                        However, not all Sta-Au are committed to anarcho-primitivist deaths in the wilderness. As the American frontier drew to a close, towns and cities boomed. During the Great Depression a few decades later, hunting parties discovered the Land of the Worms could manifest in built-up areas, such as derelict industries and abandoned residencies. Since then, more and more Wicked Ones have made their homes in urban environments, though not without adaptation. Rather than existing in bands separate from the All Night Society, they hide by intermingling with the uninitiated — joining mixed coteries and even participating in the covenants.

                                        This is not to say traditional Sta-Au are rigid and unchanging. Elders expect new blood to adapt to their brutal ways, with scarification and the filing of one’s teeth being common ways to show dedication to the goal of transcendence, but individuality is otherwise encouraged. New ideas just mean new possibilities to explore — and ultimately, the goal is for you to become the best monster you can be.

                                        Why you want to be us

                                        The first death cursed you. You’re caught in the world of the living, unable to break free of your invisible bonds and move on. You play make-believe and hide behind petty schemes. It’s pathetic. We discard all these meaningless pursuits, fetters that would keep us from reaching our true potential. We know what we’ve become, and we’ll show you how to become more.

                                        Why you should fear us

                                        Your second death is a promise. The world is violent, and sooner or later, everyone dies for good. Sooner if you give us a reason. But we aren’t beasts who attack on sight. We act deliberately. With reason. Just make sure to always treat us with respect, stay out of our way, and never, ever show weakness. If you can do that, you’ll be fine.

                                        Why we should fear ourselves

                                        Our third death lies ahead of us. We know the path. We only need to reach beyond the Land of the Worms and grasp our reward… but the path is narrow and treacherous. Violence begets violence, and many of us perish before we ever reach our goal. We celebrate our dark desires and denounce our vestigial Humanity, but without restraint, they will surely destroy us, and only darkness will be our reward.

                                        Parent Clan: Any

                                        Nicknames: Twice-Dead, the Wicked Ones

                                        Bloodline Bane (The Ruinous Curse): The Sta-Au are creatures of defilement and destruction, both physical and moral, and wherever they go, they bring this nature with them. Whenever a Wicked One suffers a breaking point or causes another to suffer a breaking point against Humanity or Integrity, roll her Humanity. On a failure, the location around them suffers the Spiritual Infestation Tilt.

                                        Disciplines: Auspex, Obfuscate, Protean, Resilience

                                        Spiritual Infestation (Environmental Tilt)

                                        Description: The curse of the Wicked Ones has infested the area. It eats through the spiritual foundation of the world, perforating its structure and leaving nothing but refuse behind.

                                        Effect: The area takes on the traits of the Land of the Worms. When this Tilt is applied, the area suffers the Spiritual Blight Aspect (p. 93). Additional breaking points suffered within the same area reset the Tilt’s duration, and if a breaking point leads to the loss of Humanity or Integrity, the area gains an additional minor Aspect (Potency 1, if needed).

                                        If the area would gain a fourth Aspect, it’s major. At this point, the area is at the cusp of deteriorating into a true Land of the Worms. Provided the location is not otherwise unfitting (such as being populated or heavily trafficked by humans or wildlife) a single significant push will end the Tilt and create a Land of the Worms at Potency 2.

                                        Causing the Tilt: A Sta-Au suffers or causes a breaking point.

                                        Ending the Tilt: The Tilt ends after a number of nights equal to the Sta-Au’s Blood Potency times the number of Aspects that currently apply to the area.

                                        In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

                                        A Handful of Dust — 1933–1944 CE (DE1, p. 470): Amid economic hardship and ecological disaster, the Sta-Au became yet another scourge afflicting the Midwest. Barren fields, abandoned factories, and storm-struck towns allow the Land of the Worms to grow at an unprecedented rate. The Sta-Au are quick to follow, and they find unlikely rivals. Animated piles of dust and detritus, also known as Dust Devils, have no aversion to the Land, and they stalk the same human victims as the Wicked Ones. They’re also strangely drawn to the dead who dwell in Twilight…

                                        Bloodline Gift: A Path Between Worlds

                                        The Wicked Ones do not act as they do due to blind faith alone. No, they see clearly what lies ahead of them. Every step they take on their journey of transcendence is clear.

                                        The first step is as simple as opening your eyes. As soon as a Sta-Au joins her new siblings, she also starts perceiving the dead who were freed from their physical forms yet still linger in the world of the living. Ghosts in the state of Twilight (see Chronicles of Darkness, p. 124) appear as if physical to her — only their muted colors and lightly distorted voices betray their true insubstantiality. Other entities and phenomena of ghostly ephemera are similarly visible.
