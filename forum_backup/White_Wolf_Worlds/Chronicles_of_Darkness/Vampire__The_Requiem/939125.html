    Player versus Player Vampire

    07-28-2016, 05:09 PM
    I will soon be starting a game of vampire where the idea of the game is that each player will essentially go off and be their own force of nature. Due to this there is a high chance of a lot of PvP taking place. Both socially and combat based.

    Myself and a player were having a discussion as to if the optional rules for social maneuvering should be used on players should be in place.

    My view is that as the game is generally a co-operative game therefore making it competitive requires social maneuvering to be applicable to players, obviously it still doesnt force the other players to do anything against their will as they can accept the condition, but at least its not completely useless this way. After all combat works just as well on other players as it does on NPCs.

    The alternative is that you cant, but if so it just seems like an invitation to make murder hobos so that nothing is wasted.

    Also as mentioned in another thread regarding safe place, Rose went into detail of how to deal with it if its an NPCs location, but for a game that is PvP how would you deal with it?

    And are there any other issues I should be aware of in a PvP focused game?

      If it's PVP, then social maneuvering against other players absolutely should be permissable. Being susceptible to having the social scene turned against you is the price you pay for building a character who chooses to build a character that wields flamethrowers or axes dipped in oil and set on fire. From a pvp perspective, if they are allowed to ignore being charmed or dominated in exchange for a condition, the game is still slanted in the favor of the combat build because the noncombat build isn't leveraging their social contacts or intellectual prowess when they're beaten into torpor or dead.

          This is something I have a lot of experience with, coming from a LARP background, where PvP is kind of the name of the game. This was 1st edition so some of the mechanics were a little different, but I think the same lessons can be applied in 2e.

          First of all, regarding Social Maneuvering. Requiem has so many literal mind control powers that worrying about being able to dictate other players' actions with social rolls is in my honest opinion, kind of silly. That said, players are not always rational beings, and having your character forced to do something when no power was used can be a feel-bad moment. I can say that in my LARP troupe, we did not allow mundane social rolls against PCs, and just as you suspected, no one ever took dots in Social Skills unless they were needed for the dice pool of one of their Disciplines. In 1e, this was something we unfortunately just had to live with, because there wasn't really an acceptable alternative. But in 2e, Conditions are a great way to incentivize players to do things that might not be in their characters' best interests.

          My recommendation would be a very slight house rule to the way Social Maneuvering works against PCs. Instead of giving the player the choice to comply and gain a Beat or offer a beneficial alternative and a Condition when all their Doors are opened, just give the player the Leveraged Condition. Then they can choose to comply and gain a Beat now, or keep the Condition and do some favor for the character that Leveraged them at a later time when it's more convenient, they still get a Beat, and they don't feel like they have to comply or get saddled with a harsh Condition.

          For Safe Place (Haven Security at the time), we required players who took the Merit to write up a description of the specific defenses their Haven had, which had to be approved by an ST (big game, so we had more than one) to make sure it was appropriate to the dot level. I would... Not recommend this approach. It was a lot of extra work on the players and the STs, and it lead to a lot of workarounds to the mechanics like what you described in your earlier thread about using hacking software and drugged meat. This is fine when you're breaking into an NPC's Haven as it encourages a more roleplay-centric approach over a dice-focused approach. But in a heavy PvP environment, circumventing the mechanical advantages that another player invested experience in just leads to problems.

          What I would recommend is handling Safe Place highly abstractly. Work with your players to determine what dice pool(s) are required to break in, what penalties apply to the roll, and what equipment can be used to gain bonuses on the roll. Then leave it up to the dice. Ideally, make Safe Place invasions a downtime-only action. Handle it between sessions so it doesn't bog down the game for everyone else.

          As for other issues in PvP focused games... Use the environment to force the players to work together sometimes. Part of the fun in a PvP game is making and breaking alliances, and having stuff going on that's bigger than the PCs squabbles is a great way to facilitate that. Nothing fans the drama flames in a PvP game like bitter rivals having to temporarily set aside their differences to protect the city from a VII attack, or a Strix outbreak, or whatever. Give them time to play that PvP game, but don't let the PvE game die. It's a welcome break from all the political maneuvering and backstabbing.

          Also, when using PvE to throw a monkey wrench in the PCs' machinations, make sure to give them time to cut the problems off at the pass if they're paying attention. Drop clues about impending plot events and give them opportunities to interact with the coming problems. Most of the time, they'll pass those opportunities up in favor of continuing their PvP schemes, and that's ok. When they ignore the city's problems long enough and it blows up in their faces, they'll band together twice as strong because they'll realize the problem could have been avoided if they hadn't put their personal squabbles first.

                                  Beats make unattractive options seem more attractive. That's basically what being persuasive is all about.

                                  If you want to do something quick n' dirty, make a contested roll whenever an NPC wants to convince a PC into doing something unattractive, or one PC wants to convince another PC into doing something unattractive: Attribute + Skill vs Attribute + Skill.

                                  If the player resisting wins, give them a beat for resisting the NPC's / other PC's persuasive charm. If the player resisting wins with an exceptional success, give them the Steadfast condition.

                                  If the player resisting loses, give them the Leveraged condition. If they lose and the NPC / other player scores an exceptional success, give them two conditions: Leveraged and Inspired. I would give the player trying to persuade the other the Steadfast condition on an exceptional success, too. If this is done in a public space, I would give them a variance of the Connected condition with an exceptional success on top of that.

                                  Basically, Leveraged is a carrot on a stick for any player. If you go along with what is requested, you resolve the condition and score a beat, and Inspired can add to that by rewarding the player with a Will Power point, another beat, and allow an exceptional success on a roll pertaining to whatever they've been persuaded to do with three successes instead of five. Steadfast is a great way at rewarding those with extra beats upon resolving the condition and by giving them an option to turn future failures into successes, and to make impossible situations seem more possible. The Connected condition in the context of trying to persuade and charm somebody publicly - basically makes onlookers marvel at your character's charismatic prowess, and therefore gets a +2 bonus on all rolls relating to this group (simulating he's become more chummy), or resolve it to score a beat and get a one-off +3 instead.

                                  HOWEVER! Your players still have agency, that's important. They can still say 'no'. They aren't under any obligation to say 'yes'. But there's a reward for going along with the persuasive character. And there's outside factors to take into account, too - that's what the Connected condition represents - if these negotiations are happening publicly, and a person still says 'no' after being trounced, they're going to potentially look foolish. And the persuasive aggressor certainly has the chance to turn the public against them with a second roll (now incurring a bonus) to make that so. However, that person might want to use that Connected roll to allow the other person to save face, trying to get in their good books instead.

                                  edit: also, don't forget modifiers. If you're wearing a crown, or the other person is in a position where they aren't going to be very inclined to say 'no', then feel free to give modifiers to the roll (-5 to +5). The way I figure it is requesting somebody to do things that personally costs them incurs negative modifiers, and requesting somebody to do things that they're being incentivized to do in-character incurs positive modifiers. The size of the modifier depends on the extremity of what's on offer or what the cost is. Modifiers could be "it goes against your morals", "you could be punished severely if you do it", "it would be personally embarrassing", "bribery", or "a loved one has been taken hostage".

                                  On top of that, it doesn't need to just be the Inspired condition that you give players. You might want to replace the Inspired condition on an exceptional success with something more contextually appropriate: perhaps the Swooning condition (seduction as a means of persuasion), Guilty condition (making the person feel bad until they do what you want), the Notoriety condition (you make them look awful in front of everyone), the Distracted condition (you've managed to fast-talk your way into distracting and confusing them, in fact it turns out your initial persuasiveness was merely a red herring for something more sinister).

                                  ---

                                  It makes what mundane persuasive should be: attractive, but you don't lose all sense / reason.

                                  "Cut your hand off!"

                                  *does do, scores a beat*

                                  or

                                  "Nope, that's insane." *no beat for you*


                                  Good leaders can lead their followers to greatness (specifically with beats / Inspired). You're far more likely to be rewarded with experience and a greater likelihood of producing exceptional successes following their directives.

                                  Mind you, social monsters should focus their power bases on NPCs and the play-environment. If the character has high status / big connections, reflect that - just because a physical monster can beat the crud out of them doesn't mean they should. It should have repercussions (if they're the Keeper of Elysium / hugely popular and well-liked, and you beat them up, nobody is going to say 'oh, you strong fellow, might makes right', you're more likely to get a target on the back of your head). If they have a Mentor 5, and it's represented as their sire - if they're put into torpor, daddy dearest probably won't be happy. If they're higher on the social totem poll, attending nice parties, they're likely to have buddies who are willing to stick up for their fellow. If they have money, they can afford lawyers.

                                  Another aspect: boons. Social characters are more likely to have boons, and being the most worthwhile currency among the undead, this can be pretty scary - because anybody could be indebted to the social monster - even vampires that hate the social monster. What if it turns out YOUR mentor is indebted to the social monster? What if it turns out some of your covenant leaders are indebted to them? And then they start calling in those debts to make your unlife a bit of a pain...
