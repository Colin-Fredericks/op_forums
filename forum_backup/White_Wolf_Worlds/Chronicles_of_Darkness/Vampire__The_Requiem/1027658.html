                                          I don't see a problem with the sheriff being the muscle. I mean, ok, we're not going to talk about a super detective, but I think that role can be very different given the type of management the city has, the political landscape, the size, and other stuff.


                                          Here's how I see things. The Sheriff can be a skilled detective, BUT it some (I'd even say most) cases, the sheriff works for the Prince, they're law enforcement, the Prince's law. A sheriff's role could be an investigator's, but the Prince could also have spies to do that, and would have an easier time questioning other vampires who don't know they're investigating. Then, sure the sheriff is basically like a glorified hound, but there's nothing wrong with that. It also has advantages.

                                          The problem with the hard boiled type is that typically, they get no respect. People know they're smart, but they hate them for that, they just want them to stick their nose elsewhere, and I feel respect is an invaluable tool for the sheriff. Thing is, kicking ass is a god way to garner respect. The sheriff is authorized to use violence if it's for the prince. Even if they're not the best investigators, people will hesitate doing things if they know it can get them in trouble with a powerful gangrel. The probability of getting caught is less than with your typical mekhet spy, but dealing with the consequences can be a lot more troublesome. And anyway, not putting mental first doesn't mean your gangrel has to be an idiot. They can be a solid investigator. Breaking heads is also a method of investigation, especially when you're not tied down by things like warrants and stuff. A vampire the prince liked got killed. Everybody knows that nosferatu and his ghoul gang hated him, so the sheriff goes to kick down their door. What if they got framed ? With a little luck, they won't want any trouble and play really nice, hoping the sheriff decides not to break their knees, giving other leads worth investigating. With less luck, the sheriff beats them up and then starts asking questions for the same result. Is it a safe or good investigation method ? No, but it builds a reputation, and if it causes some other vampires to plot a revolution, then it's more story.

                                          One example I love that's not quite this but kinda is Max Payne 2. The game is one large investigation, and it's basically the hero going from place to place on pretty thin evidence, stumbling upon stuff he's not supposed to see and getting by simply by shooting everyone. In the end, he gets to the bottom of the investigation all the same. He missed all the subtle clues (which are in the game and paint a story the hero completely fails to see until the end, which is awesome), but he's enough of a tough guy that he gets the bad guy anyway.


                                          As for respect, it depends how you play it. You could be the asshole who doesn't hesitate to walk over anyone who's in the bad place at the bad time to complete their mission. You'll be feared, but probably hated, but fear is already something. Or you could be the sheriff people KNOW could kick their ass easily, but doesn't do it unless it's absolutely necessary. Then, your strength will be feared, but also respected for being fair, kinda like a paladin (in a very different way), and the advantage of that is that even without that much social skill, people might figure it's safe to tell you stuff, and that's also a good investigation method.


                                          Also, depends on how you're doing things, but getting minions might be good. The sheriff/hound I used in my first vampire game had three or four big ghouled dogs for example. First, it's always easier to be menacing when you have backup. Second, if you're going for a kind of thug, gang member enforcer aesthetic, it makes sense you'd have buddies helping you, as long as it's clear to everyone you're the boss. If they're human, it obviously bring the problem of the masquerade, but that's another matter. You could have an infant following you around, or another vampire you took under your wing. If you want to be respected, it can also show other vampires you take care of your own and you're not just a violent brute.
