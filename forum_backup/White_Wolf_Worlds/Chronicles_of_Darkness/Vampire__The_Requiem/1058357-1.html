            So, question time.

            I've been messing around with some Vampire settings of late, thinking of trying some new directions with the game than my usual things. Back with Blood and Smoke first came out, I remember talk about how each of the covenants had reasons to have rivals stick around. Each covenant kind of fitting into its own niche, having its own specialties.

            The Invictus specialize in keeping the Masquerade quiet through organization manipulations and raising the best Retainers (even if the PC is that retainer!). Notories who bond their cotories and ghouls can grant them some pretty powerful abilities.
            The Carthians have an entire "shrug off outside influence" vibe going on with them with their Law, and make everyone like them with lawyer speak. They are awesome at upholding the law, no matter what it is.
            The Lancea et Sanctum has the whole Lore Keeper vibe going on with their Libraries as well as a lot of witch hunter abilities and "sanctuary." Even Theban lends itself to the latter two. Very handy to have. Theban actually lends itself to making awesome sheriffs, in my not-actually-humble opinion.
            The Circle of the Crone has the best "witches" - especially for diviners and other mystical paths, like summoning, dealing with ghosts, etc.

            Each of these kind of fit well into a society, and gives reasons why you'd want to keep each of them around in your domain. Need the future foretold, go to an Acolyte. Have someoen that needs to be tracked down and judged? Lance, you're up. Carthians, for all their trouble causing, make excellent aids and will follow the Law, because that's their strength. Invictus make everyone around them better.

            Now, I'm sure that some people will agree or disagree with the above. Maybe you'll have different takes, maybe you'll have different wants. Its the general direction I'm taking my city in. But, here's the cornerstone of where I'm drawing a giant "???" over my head while looking at the covenants.

            What good is the Ordo Dracul? Seriously. Ignore Zirnitra / Ziva for now - they're both rare enough and varied enough that I don't want them to influence the covenant as a whole in my city. So, what do these Dragons do for the city at large? What social role do they fill? What niche do they claim beyond "be the best vampire you can be?" Asendant could be good for "daylight vampires," but that's literally what most other covenants use ghouls for. I'm at a loss on how the Ordo Dracul would really contribute to vampire society as a whole here, as I'm looking at for my game.


            Now, I know that the Ordo Dracul are technically supposed to be secret society. Never seen them run that way, but there you are. So not having a role in society might be a thing. But they don't really lend themselves to being a hidden society within Vampire ranks either. They just seem to be... there. Maybe just really good Elysium Keepers, given all the Wyrm Nest merits? I was kind of hoping for something pulled from the core book, but they're just short on ideas imho.


            So, does anyone have any other ideas?
            Firstly, you should ask yourself what rite does OD use in your Chronicle/City and in which year do you play, because for the most of it's history in most of the places Ordo Dracul in a secret society. What role does the Freemasons have? They could be the city's most wise occultists or rather be ordinary Kindred to keep the secret. Most trusted Prince's seneschal could be the hidden Kogaion, who has the wish to overthrow the Prince. Or maybe OD is more peaceful and just want to continue their studies without inquisitor's knowledge- it's up to you.

            Ordo Dracul could help the city in many things, but the questions is, would they do it, since for many Academies keeping hidden is one of the most important factors.

                        The original question seems like a fair one to me so I will try to answer it in case anyone else has the same question. If I mapped the covenants, I would put the Invictus and Carthians on one side opposite each other and the LS and CC on the other side opposite each other with the OD in the middle. The OD is traditional and innovative. It is also occult but subtle. The other covenants have a narrow focus and a niche regarding what they do well. The OD fills the gaps. I think that can make it difficult to define them and pigeonhole them.

                        The OD could be described as "scientist vampires" for lack of a better description and that means they would play the same role as scientists/inventors in our society. The OD, generally, fits roles that aren't clearly favored by another covenant or that fit multiple covenants well. There is one logical disconnect though. If the OD is an open secret society (not judging since it is clearly an option) then their knowledge should be no more secret than any other covenant. All covenants have secrets. A vampire can walk up to a LS church to ask for info so if a vampire knows where the local OD chapter house is then they should be able to get info as long as they pay a price based on the information's value.

                        As one of the occult groups, the OD fits a special niche in between the other two occultists. For example, a vampire might own a haunted building. Destroying the ghost is the realm of the LS. Magically summoning and controlling the ghost fits the CC. Now what if the vampire wants to make a long term arrangement with the ghost to guard the building. The OD is the best place to find all the info about the ghost's anchors, banes, and bans as well as means to create conditions to manifest the ghost. Other covenants might be more likely to have part of the information, but they won't have all of it. They also won't approve of using the ghost as a guard dog. It is dangerous and probably unwise which fits the OD because dangerous and unwise is their bag. This differs from the CC which necessitates including crazy to dangerous and clearly unwise.

                        A vampire could find the information on the ghost from the OD while drawing less suspicion. The OD would approve of using a ghost to guard the building from outsiders, requiring that they be considered insiders I'm sure. Removing material that could be used for bans and banes or protecting anchors is the OD way of dealing with the ghost. Also, if a vampire is caught trying to monopolize the site the OD is less likely to have a negative view as that is what the average OD member would be expected to do.

                        There is a subtle difference in the manner a jack-of-all-trades handles a situation compared to a specialist doing something similar in effect. Sometimes it is better, other times not. This becomes a matter of personal taste as to when the OD can excel. The OD is a little bit of all the other covenants at once and not at all like any one other covenant. That makes sense only because of how the other covenants conflict. I think that is the long and short of it.

                                    Saying "step back and look at vampires as individuals" may not be the most satisfying answer, but thematically it is important to retain the individual awareness even while examining how kindred society functions and why you might have a city with all five core covenants represented.

                                    OD's main claim to fame is it makes the kindred of the city "better vampires". This is a good thing when applied to allies, and it gives a set of tools that may or may not be missing in other covenants at the time. Covenants are now somewhat intentionally interchangeable--if that is not desired then you'll need to do some careful editing in your setting.

                                    The "best" OD member from an outside perspective would also be a member of another covenant and directly lend their strength, wisdom and experiences to supporting that covenant's goals. There are merits supporting this direction. Other benefits for having them around include as discussed a non-judgmental, secular approach to solving supernatural problems, as well several more mundane ones.

                                    Both Lancea and CC are fanatical groups. Leaning too heavily on them for help in running your city, or solving its problems, runs a risk of tipping things too far in their direction--and then you lose influence/control in favor of the cult. Wise princes will avoid that by balancing both of them and having a third, wildcard option helps keep them guessing.

                                    VtR is not really meant to be clean-cut.

                                            Requiem has an interesting breakdown of covenants. Someone else said this a long time ago, but there's a kind of political "quadrant" you can slot them into. On the y-axis you have traditional at the top and progressive at the bottom; on the x-axis you have secular on the left, and religious on the right. The Invictus is traditional-secular; the Carthians are progressive-secular; the Lancea et Sanctum is traditional-religious; the Circle of the Crone are progressive-religious.

                                            The Ordo Dracul laughs at this: they're spiritual, but they defy god; they're progressive, even meritocratic, but they're a cult of personality around a nigh mythic Wallachian warlord.

                                            If the Dragons fit anywhere, it's on an occulted z-axis. In Nietzschean terms, they've said no to both master and slave morality, and they're looking to become the uber-Kindred. That doesn't necessarily mean that a typical Defiant is (always) a heartless, strawman libertarian, but her goals are ultimately internal, not external.

                                            Which is all to say, I concur about the Dragons being the covenant of individualism. A Dragon doesn't care if she's useful. She can provide services to the city at large, but it's almost always transactional -- it's for the advancement of self, regardless of the consequences to others (this is the covenant that murders humans just to see how the dominoes fall). It's not for nothing that they're the smallest sect. They're not as obviously useful as the others, and their ideology is ultimately quite isolating.

                                                            I definitely don't think the Ordo Dracul is the smallest Covenant, so much as it rules the least amount of Domains.
