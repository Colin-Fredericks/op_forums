                        So, I've finished reading the book, and here's my thoughts.

                        I. Cover and Opening Fiction.
                        While not -wow- over the cover, its a good picture. The opening fiction is nice. Flows well. I kind of wondered why the elder daeva embraced that childer in the first place when its clear he's got a lack of common sense. Still, I like the story of how the poker game flowed, using it as a testing of each other. The ending was amusing, and admittedly a twist that I wasn't quite expecting. An entire conversation through the game. I even like the buck-tooth Nosferatu picture.

                        II. Chapter 1 - Opening Moments
                        Well, first let me say this - I find the xp distribution to be rather shallow. Disappointing mechanics is a common refrain I have throughout this book. The "example elders" were kind of boring to me - just a few small skill and attribute bumps, and merits. This feels more like how to make an experienced -mortal- rather than a vampire, no real considerations for vampire's nature or abilities in the write ups.

                        That aside? Lets get into the real meat of the discussion. Character creation. We're being walked through, step by step, how to make an elder game, what considerations to make to the thought process behind each age. Which is admittedly pretty cool. I like the emphasis on early days defining a vampire throughout their Requiem. It reminds me of how your first three years as a child basically shape your entire life. Yay formative years! Far too often, we do get lost in the age and supposed power of an elder, but bringing back the focus to, yes, that first human scent is good. I love the idea of a faded touchstone, highlighting this entire thing perfectly.

                        Portrayal of clans. Now this is pretty interesting. I especially like the lost clans - that was a welcome surprise I did NOT expect to see. I also am in love with the idea of elder curses (which are one of the mechanics I do adore). I'm not quite sure on the Nosferatu one (lets drop humanity fast!), but that's because it seems to make them, well, haunts. Very lair-oriented. The problem being that it makes them unlikely to leave said lair. The Ventrue one is particuarly inspired, I think, though, and I will probably swap this one with the regular one. The base five clan writeups themselves were good, and, while nothing ground breaking, they're good directions to read up on and gives good considerations to how you adapt while keeping anachronisms.

                        Side note - I'm still not understanding what the picture of the guy holding his bleeding arm, and the woman on the ground holding her head screaming, gun next to her. Page 28. Is that supposed to be overloaded senses? The Curse of Five coming into play?

                        And we get to the first part of the book's fluff I'm not liking. I feel like the covenant section is basically summed up as "you get cults of personality, flavored by covenant philosophy." Or "go off on your own." I don't feel there's much here to explore beyond "trusted elders = leadership roles." And I also feel like we're dealing more with the 1e covenants rather than the expanded 2e. I loved the different flavors of the Circle we saw in Secrets, but here? Back to the pseud-wicca Mother-Maiden-Crone bits. No references to the Invictus as the Masquerade enforces, just as Establishment. Dynasty is listed under Invictus again, despite the core pushing it towards clan-based (Also - The reference to dynasty oath could use a page reference, or something, as I was getting it confused with the core book merit). Dynasties have nothing to do with covenant! If anything, it should have been under Clan! And that sidebar! Dynasties are already cross covenant by nature. Its utterly meaningless.

                        Cerberus pact feels like a variation on VII. Which might have been the intent - playable VII that doesn't tell you what the VII are. Its got its appeal, but I'm still not sure why we're going with elder-only, however. I don't feel anything elder-ish about the Pact.

                        III. The Long Solo
                        We start with a small thing about why vampires prize memories and "transcend history." Feel like there's references to how Ancient books talked about vampire history. Cool. Vampire elders want to make a mark on the world and not be forgotten. Okay. Ah, Flashback scenes. Now this is tasty. Not a fan of all the Masquerade-era "vampires fear change" leaking in, but ideas for going back in history is nice. Nothing immediately useful outside of an elder game, but solid ideas on what to focus on. I like structuring it on idfferent themes like love or intrigue or the Embrace. Nothing really stands out to me, but that's because I dont have an immediate need for it. I can see the definite value during future games, however.

                        IV. What we Learned Along the Way
                        Aww... first chapter title not related to music. Sadness. So, we have a section on 6+ attributes. Okay. I don't understand why its unique to elders, when its basically something that all vampires experince through disciplines. Wits made me feel like it was talking about an Auspex user. Strength and Stamina above 5 are common with disciplines, and they can't be turned off. Bits of things about "superhuman attributes are noticable Masquerade breaches." Honestly, its a good bit of discussion for any game that lets you get over 5+ stuff. I'm probably going to use it for mages and why stats 5+ cause issues with Dissonance too. Its a good discussion to have, and kind of important. "Here's how you role play a high stat character." Something anyone routinely pushing themselves past 5 in a stat should read, and I'm glad its here, for every game out there.

                        The same with skills. You see this all the time with people in different fields, let alone supernaturally advanced mastery. I'm currently playing a high occult character in changeling, with supernatural means of boosting my actual score to around the equivalent of 8 dice. Just from occult alone. I'm finding this pretty handy for how my character already. Again, another good read for anyone with supernaturally enhanced stats. Not groundbreaking, but excellent reference.

                        I found the whole devotion section a bit confusing. So, learning devotions is difficult for vampires, because they need teachers or something? They make it sound like learning a devotion is a giant ordeal akin to learning rare blood sorceries, which is kind of at odds with how the core book describes the devotions. I get it, we want storyhooks, but I have to fall back to the core book here. As for the devotions themselves? Kind of neat, some of them. I like the Auspex one. For the most part, while expensive in terms of vitae, these are actually abilities everyone could learn or take, not just elders. I'd expect elder devotions to have a Blood Potency restriction, like they did in 1e. In terms of direct power? Honestly, I think the semi-official devotions that were supposed to be in Secrets via dropbox are stronger.

                        Also? Vampires now have a pseudo-fire ability (Spontaneous Ignition) as well as telekinesis (Unbridled Force). I'm not sure if Unbridled Force can be used to fly or not, but I'd allow it. These devotions, while not powerful and often clunky, are at least iconic abilties that are nice to see. The only thing missing now is an icy aura, blood manipulation and venom fangs.

                        Elder sorcery is apparently restricted in terms of age, but that's more of a social construct than ability. Much like the devotions above, there's a prohibitive cost associated with them, doubling the necessary willpower or vitae for some reason. Not really seeing the point, since most of the regular costs have built in offsets. But, as above, not a fan of this books mechanics for the most part. As for the rituals themselves? Detached limbs that can run around are always fun. I do like. 10 vitae and 10 successes to animate trees for the night? Less useful given the time invested. Maybe if we absolutely know there's going to be an attack on one's Mandrake garden on a certain night. Or set up an ambush. That said, it also seems to be attacking the ritualist? Scapegoat... just not a fan of the magical implications of being able to avoid derangements. You take derangements if you learn about something you did under frenzy later. Yet "because magic" you get to go crazy and do whatever without problem. Theban's Curse of Isolation has issues - putting aside things like forests or tombs as touchstones, but it doesn't touch on things like Impression, or if we're dealing with transfered feelings or anything. Very clunky. What happens when someone uses this against a True Friend touchstone? Or a faded touchstone? Orison of Voices is cool - you get to hear prayers directed to you. Not really worth a five dot ritual, especially when we have merits for regnants appearing in trouble. Cool, but should have been a merit with a Theban prerequisite. Sins of the Ancestors feels like it should be an Auspex power, and is of dubious use. Iconic, I suppose, but not something I see come up in play often, either for PCs or NPCs.

                        Coil of the Quintessence. There's some implications here. 1) we can extend one of the three core coils by attaching a new Coil to a Mystery. 2) A coil built around territory control means we can have coils that do more than affect the vampire itself. Still a bit weak in terms of actual mechanics - Smother (and Cold of the Grave by extension) and Forever Mine are good. Everything else is questionable, imho.

                        Merits - While I get the intent of most of these merits, I just can't see them being used. They sound good in theory, but in execution, I just don't see the benefit for the cost. Like... Major Domo. Its benefit is roughly equal to a two dot Status or Striking Looks merit but costs 3, has a drawback, and affects less social rolls. Married by Blood - a one dot merit that has a drawback, but lets you share Haven and Retainer dots, which you pretty much could do already? Well, maybe not Retainer, but Haven definitely already could. Plus, we have a marriage Oath already, which is far more powerful for the same cost. Whomever wrote these mechanics love the 9-again rule.

                        Now, there are a few good options - using Receptive Mind for multiple Auspex 4 use is nice. Undeniable Aura is a thing; it has its downsides, but its pretty nifty overall. Special Treat is a trap tactic for getting someone else in trouble, or whoring out the blood as a drug for favors (mental note, never feed from a ghoul, even if offered). But most I don't care for as unnecessary.

                        Carthian Law - Stare Decisis. Well, makes for a good carthian lawyer ability. But at this point in one's undead life, willpower shouldn't be hard to come by. The main use is in making other people waste resources fighting you socially. Has its uses in some games.

                        Dynasty Merits - So, Dynastic Progenitor can be cool. Flat dice bonus to discipline use, but sure. Oath of Dynasty... Dynasties are not made, nor confirmed, by Notories. You already share blood sympathy with everyone, because you're all part of the same clan to begin with! Talking with torpid members is already done via Speaker for the Silent. I'm sorry, but I feel like whomever wrote this section on Dynasties was stuck with the old Invictus book instead of how the actual merits work in 2e. I feel like this Oath of Dynasty was meant to be the book's Invictus Oath. But, it doesn't even lean towards that.

                        V. What We Learned Along The Way
                        Still sad we're done with music references in title. Moving on. So, we get to Spinning the Web. There's been references to this all over the place. So, now we finally see it. In brief, its a long list on how to roleplay your elder, and to help flesh out his history (revised by others), and all the little quirks. All in all, a very handy tool to have in terms of writing storyhooks. Always good to have as a reference. I particularly liked The Queen is Dead, Long Live the Queen.

                        Touchstones get a bit of look at, but in another way - faux Touchstones that work differently. Its definitely an interesting take to reflect things, especially when combined with phantom touchstones. I'm pleased with tthe variety of touchstones presented in the book. The Halfway Port: Ghouls at least firmly establishes ghouls as potential touchstones, and not as human company. Discusses ghouls in an elder's life, though not in too much detail. Probably could have skipped this in favor of the half-damned book. The Death Barge: Revenants... I'm not sure what's going on here. "There but for the Grace of God..." Huh? Isn't that a draugr? Outcasts? They can be uplifted fairly easily, and wanted to by most cities, I imagine. Revenants are used to track sloppy feeders. Maybe serving as a touchstone? Wha? Given the next section on vampires, you can have other vampires a kind of faux touchstone... huh. I admit to skimming this part, as there's just too much to really focus on.

                        VI Wolves at the Gate.
                        A picture of someone pulling an Emperor Palpatine on another. I presume this is a mage doing the lightning bit, given the sigils on her palms. The victim isn't a vampire (no fangs). Go figure why its in a vampire book. Its well done, but... how is this Vamipre? I guess a reference to the crossover section, but still.

                        So, monsters that hunt the Kindred.
                        Inamorata are a cruel twist - the dead coming back to haunt the undead. Lots of potential to be almost anything. Is it the dead lover? Is it a kind of shapeshifter that wants to destroy vampires? Angels? Other vampires? Ghosts (x-over with GtSE!)? Can be fun to play out, much like a Hollow Mehket's shadow can be.

                        Draugr - powerful monsters, but more of a mirror than any kind of threat. You can run a kind of search-and-destroy game, but those tend to be over pretty fast, in my experience. No Prince wants a walking Masquerade breach hunting the streets of their city. And then, you have responces by Task Force Valkerie and other hunters, let alone other supernaturals. I can't imagine that they're a threat for an elder game. I'd also suggest reading up on them in the Gangrel book more.

                        Empusa and Lamia - alternate vampires. Night Horros Dead Wicked style non-Kindred vampires. Basically, though, they're just glorified vampires who hunt vampires. I have to recommend using the Night Horrors books instead of these girls.

                        Methuselahs are just old elders that don't lose BP in torpor. Not much to talk about here. Other than Ezekiel. Founder of the Akhud clan owning a PI firm is amusing, but not scary. And why does a ghoul have Blood Potency? I must have missed that being mentioned. Or its a typo. Same with the Lost Clans section - sample elder threats. 'Nough said.

                        Body of a Nation - tier 3 global threats for elders in the form of Danse Macabre Conspiracy?

                        Immortals:
                        Deborah - basically, an immortal hunter, with a Compact/Conspiriacy based on her family, using psychic abilities.
                        King Kitamba - another immortal hunter that may as well be a rogue ghoul with a slightly better time frame between feedings.
                        Sons of Phobos - reprint from Blood Sorcery book, basically.

                        Crossover section - notes on immortal mages (liches), long lived 'lings, and more. The only thing I'm going to mention is that no, Auras aren't how Auspex work anymore. Not really a fan of crossover, so skipping over.

                        All in all, I can't say I'm impressed with this chapter. Feels like its just taking existing things and putting more stats on them. Same things as the neonate, just hits harder.

                        VII In Conclusion

                        So. There you go. Basically, we have some very good ideas that go into the thought process of being an elder, how to build up history, some really good story hooks, new Touchstone ideas, and the occasional devotion worth having.

                        If youw ant to do an elder game and need help getting into the mindset, good book to get. If you want it for the mechanics or antagonists? I suggest checking elsewhere.

Not really worth a five dot ritual, especially when we have merits for regnants appearing in trouble. Cool, but should have been a merit with a Theban prerequisite. Sins of the Ancestors feels like it should be an Auspex power, and is of dubious use. Iconic, I suppose, but not something I see come up in play often, either for PCs or NPCs.

                                                        The problem with this is the very beginning; there's no such thing as derangements any more. Scapegoat is the Circle of the Crone's philosophy in miniature. Humanity is necessary, but more of an obstacle to transcendence; by storing it away, they can do the things that the Acolytes do if they've been pushed and know they don't need to worry about losing their minds to Wassail (as for the Scapegoat, screw mortals-who knows, their flagging Integrity may allow them to build something better than before). You misread Orison of Voices (it hears every conversation in the world where your name was mentioned, and gleans the context-it's probably the most OP Theban Sorcery power for anyone with any interest in politics), you can't do anything to a True Friend without player permission so that's a non-issue for any PC, and the questions Sins allow you to ask is any simple question-you get to interrogate a target's entire direct family, for any reason for a Vitae drop.

                                                        Coil of the Quintessence. There's some implications here. 1) we can extend one of the three core coils by attaching a new Coil to a Mystery. 2) A coil built around territory control means we can have coils that do more than affect the vampire itself. Still a bit weak in terms of actual mechanics - Smother (and Cold of the Grave by extension) and Forever Mine are good. Everything else is questionable, imho.

                                                        It is incredibly useful for a Prince-you know freaking everything that happens, at least in general, and the very first dot allows you to be effectively omnipresent. You don't do anything in the Dragon's city and expect him to not notice.

                                                        Dynasty Merits - So, Dynastic Progenitor can be cool. Flat dice bonus to discipline use, but sure. Oath of Dynasty... Dynasties are not made, nor confirmed, by Notories. You already share blood sympathy with everyone, because you're all part of the same clan to begin with! Talking with torpid members is already done via Speaker for the Silent. I'm sorry, but I feel like whomever wrote this section on Dynasties was stuck with the old Invictus book instead of how the actual merits work in 2e. I feel like this Oath of Dynasty was meant to be the book's Invictus Oath. But, it doesn't even lean towards that.

                                                        It never was; they explicitly said there's more than one Oath of Dynasty than Invictus ones, and even suggested them in a sidebar. It's just called that, because it's still an oath between elders.

                                                        Touchstones get a bit of look at, but in another way - faux Touchstones that work differently. Its definitely an interesting take to reflect things, especially when combined with phantom touchstones. I'm pleased with tthe variety of touchstones presented in the book. The Halfway Port: Ghouls at least firmly establishes ghouls as potential touchstones, and not as human company. Discusses ghouls in an elder's life, though not in too much detail. Probably could have skipped this in favor of the half-damned book. The Death Barge: Revenants... I'm not sure what's going on here. "There but for the Grace of God..." Huh? Isn't that a draugr? Outcasts? They can be uplifted fairly easily, and wanted to by most cities, I imagine. Revenants are used to track sloppy feeders. Maybe serving as a touchstone? Wha? Given the next section on vampires, you can have other vampires a kind of faux touchstone... huh. I admit to skimming this part, as there's just too much to really focus on.

                                                        Revenants are the mirror that you can talk to; they're all Curse, no consolation, and as mentioned in the core, they have a huge prejudice against them that puts the lie to them being wanted. It's not rational; it still happens, and it's a huge problem that leads to many stories.

                                                        [stuff about dragur I accidentally deleted]

                                                        Not socially. But the fluff is your friend-dragur are not lacking in cunning, they lurk in places where they have the advantage. The short-lived dragur are the one who can't find lairs; once they do, they tend to be quite happy to not threaten the Masquerade if only to help their hunts.

                                                        Body of a Nation - tier 3 global threats for elders in the form of Danse Macabre Conspiracy?

                                                        That, and a quiet remark on shadow politics-the Body is a group that controls the American Government...in the name of the American Government. They formed up because they were all friends of Ben Franklin, and it's a tragedy they've been hijacked by something.

                                                        Immortals:
                                                        Deborah - basically, an immortal hunter, with a Compact/Conspiriacy based on her family, using psychic abilities.
                                                        King Kitamba - another immortal hunter that may as well be a rogue ghoul with a slightly better time frame between feedings.
                                                        Sons of Phobos - reprint from Blood Sorcery book, basically.

                                                        Except for the part where the Sons are completely different; they aren't Roman cultists blessed by the Strix, they're Greek sorcerers who have their unique type of blood magic altogether not unlike Cruac, but its own thing. They've also been buffed with more rituals and the ability to use Vampire Disciplines.

                                                        Crossover section - notes on immortal mages (liches), long lived 'lings, and more. The only thing I'm going to mention is that no, Auras aren't how Auspex work anymore. Not really a fan of crossover, so skipping over.

                                                        Which is a fucking shame, as that is probably the best written section. Changeling blood causes the vampire to hallucinate being alive again, meaning a changeling blood doll is likely the powerful one in this relationship, as the changeling is likely quite capable of cutting the flow whenever he feels like it. And it gets more interesting from there, very respectful and conscious of all game lines.


                                                        All in all, I can't say I'm impressed with this chapter. Feels like its just taking existing things and putting more stats on them. Same things as the neonate, just hits harder.

                                                        VII In Conclusion

                                                        So. There you go. Basically, we have some very good ideas that go into the thought process of being an elder, how to build up history, some really good story hooks, new Touchstone ideas, and the occasional devotion worth having.

                                                        If youw ant to do an elder game and need help getting into the mindset, good book to get. If you want it for the mechanics or antagonists? I suggest checking elsewhere.

                                                        Actually, it's also good if you want elder antagonists, it's a vital insight to how they think.
