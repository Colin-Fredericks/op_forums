                    Originally posted by MCN View Post
                    Here's my question - why are we assuming mid-Death Rage feeding?
                    Honestly, because I had a scene in my head and I wanted to see if it was mechanically supported. It wasn't really anything more than that.
                    Originally posted by nofather View Post
                    You know vampires and werewolves aren't enemies, right?
                    I know I've already replied to this once, but I've been thinking about it and I'm not sure it's true in 2nd ed. Even if they're not explicitly enemies, I think they're going to be in conflict more often than not.

                    My understanding of their relationship in 1st ed is that they've got very little cross-over. Vampires want to feed, and they want power. Werewolves want to hunt spirits and maintain the balance. They have no reason to attack each other, and can basically co-exist without ever noticing each other (Outside of a particularly dumb vampire accidentally trying to eat a werewolf, or a vampire working with spirits).

                    With the greater variety within the pack, 2nd ed Werewolf drastically increases the amount of non-werewolves that your average werewolf cares for. You've got the wolfblooded, you've got the human members of the pack, and you've got all of their friends and family. Each of those is a viable target for your average vampire, but if they get snacked on, the pack is probably going to react. That's about as visceral a breach of a werewolf's territory as you're going to get - One of their people has been targeted by a vampire.

                    From the vampires point of view, there's no real easy way to differentiate these from their regular prey. And I think few vampires would be willing to constrain their feeding to avoid someone who might be related to a werewolf pack. Placing further restrictions within their feeding grounds is a big deal.

                    With sufficient numbers of each, conflict seems pretty inevitable. And avoiding it seems like a lot of sacrifice that neither side will be willing to make. If they're balanced in power, I think you'll see a lot of conflict. If the werewolves are dominant, I'd expect to see more vampires within packs and the freelancers being very careful about who they feed on. If the vampires are dominant, they're going to feed on whoever they want. I don't know if the werewolves have a good reaction to that short of escalating to outright war.

                                And werewolves have a lot of ways to easily locate vampires. Especially within their territory. If there was going to be a conflict, the werewolves could feel the vampire out before it could become an actual problem. Since most vampires have, basically, steady feeding sources, it becomes a matter of telling the pack not to go to that bar, or the vampire not to feed off the Jefferson family.

                                Either way, if a werewolf did decide to kill a vampire, or vampires managed to kill an entire pack, it's not likely the kind of thing that would lead to a war, mainly because vampires aren't exactly a unified front and unless the pack is part of a protectorate there's no guarantee anyone else would care. Even if there was a protectorate.

                                But that's just possibilities. I'm not trying to be the verisimilitude police and there's plenty of ways to set it up in your game so that things work the way you want. But, books as given, don't really have a werewolf versus vampire antagonism built in to it.

                                I believe the developer said it well.

                                An MI6 agent observes a CIA man in 1974 Moscow. She knows what he is — she can tell by the newspapers he reads, by the routes he drives and what brands of cigarettes he buys on what days. She doesn't need to ask him, and he doesn't need to ask her.

                                Every month or so, they meet in a park; mostly, they exchange pleasantries and empty platitudes. Sometimes they share information — or disinformation — but broadly, they both have the same goal. She may set a bomb under a junior minister's car; he may spend a night with a senior security guard, turning him into a double agent. Their methods are different, but they both want the same thing in the end. They come from different places, but you can recognize them by their similarities as much as their differences.
                                But, again, the game has the tools to put the setting you want for your game together. Seeing 'Garou' made me think people were thinking Forsaken was more like Apocalypse, with the wyrm-taint hate.
