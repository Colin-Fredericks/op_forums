                                                    The Storyteller First, let me real quick apologize if my comment of sim vs. nar felt like a blow-off. You definitely belong here. Even more than a Narrative game, CofD is a sandbox game, so take what you like, ditch the rest, etc. As Charlaquin mentioned, these discussions are hard to have without the tone of "...but you're doing it wrong," bleeding in. But, fact is, you're the best judge of your table, and the all anyone can do here is try to armchair some advice for you.

                                                    As for immersion, this is heavy YMMV territory. I actually don't see much conflict between that and story-game elements. Rolling dice, marking stats, and looking up rules already breaks people 'out of character' in that sense (best immersive game is straight up diceless LARP, imo). While it does take a little adjusting to add these elements and think about as a player, I've not found it significantly more difficult for people to do so. It's certainly much easier for an experienced player to consider Anchors/Beats and stay in character than I would say it is for a new player to try and process all the base rules while doing the same.

                                                    A the end of the day, I suspect we're splitting hairs and aren't too far off baseline from how we make our decisions. Random scenario: The PCs need the permission of the Nos Priscus to set up a hideout in the Undercity, cause they're worried about heat from the Sheriff. I suspect you'd look at all the current goals of the Priscus, their willingness to work with the PCs, their relationship with the Sheriff, how they feel about possible non-Haunts in the Undercity - and then figure out what Boon/Favor the Priscus would be most likely to ask for at that time. I do all those same steps, only at the final decision point, rather than "What are they most likely to ask for?" I think about, "Out of the options they could reasonably / realistically ask for, which request gives me the most interesting drama, conflict or experience?"

                                                    The analogy I use is pool. Narrative elements don't mean just hitting any random ball into the pocket (drama for drama's sake alone). It means you have to hit one of your stripes first (Choices still have to make sense), but you're thinking where you want to position the cue-ball after that initial hit to make your next shot even better.

                                                        I don't think we're talking about the same meaning of Immersion, because what you're calling immersion is as close to the opposite of what I am calling it as you can get. I think you're talking about something like "Story immersion." You are investing in what happens and getting excited by the rise and fall of drama and action. You are on the edge of your seat waiting for the next cool thing. Like, as if you were watching a great movie or something that you got "immersed" in.

                                                        But my group, we immerse in the characters. We become them while playing. The characters can't control drama around them, they can only control themselves. When you, as a player, alter the story around their characters, you are taking an action outside of what your character is capable of doing, and affecting the game world in a way that would require the character to shatter the 4th wall to accomplish themselves.

                                                        Originally posted by Griautis View Post
                                                        I just like to also back a bit to the Aspirations. Why are you guys ignoring these? Like, do the player characters have no objectives at all? What are they doing in their games? Even if it's completely simulation of a "normal vampire day", they still have something to do? In their duties, or in their nightlife? Aspirations are basically objectives set out by the players. So, what's the problem with announcing your objective before session?
                                                        The problem is 1) Remembering to announce them 2) figuring out what you want to do in rewardable objective statements 3) chasing rewards, rather than acting naturally if, say, your motivations change due to the organic flow of circumstance during the game.

                                                        Ok, let's take two examples, one with aspirations, and one without.

                                                        With: The session starts and you know your character wants to find a Haven. So, your aspiration is "Acquire a Haven." You start working on that goal, then, half way through the session, an old friend calls you up. He needs help with something! Hmm, now you have to weigh helping your friend (for no reward other than helping a friend) or getting your Haven, which you still want to do, and getting a beat. In fact, if you can get the Haven and the beat quickly, you might be able to add a "help your friend" aspiration and get another beat out of it. Hmmm, tough decision. Your friend needs help, but, damn, a beat is awesome. It can feel to some, in fact, that you are being punished by helping your friend, rather than following through on the thing you wrote down at the beginning of the session. However you choose, though, you're thrown from immersion because you are now weighing a meta resource your character has no way to know about (a beat) against actual in character relationships.

                                                        Without: The session starts and you know your character wants to find a Haven. You start working on that, then, halway through the session, an old friend calls you up. He needs help with something! So, you decide how much you care about this friend, and decide, yeah, you want to help. Done. Full character immersion maintained.

                                                        Not using Aspirations doesn't mean characters don't have goals and motivations, it means we don't mechanize those things and reward/punish doing/not doing them with meta-rewards.

                                                        If it isn't appropriate for the character then it shouldn't have been chosen by the player, but it doesn't need to be Always appropriate. Your not constantly trying to regain willpower.

                                                        Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                                        Why would you think that? Just because something gives you willpower doesn't mean you'll do it in every scene, it just means you get incentives to do the thing related to it often. Often =! Constantly. The fact it doesn't always fit is a good thing.
                                                        No, it's not. Because you still receive the reward when it doesn't fit. You are mechanically incentivized to take the action in ALL circumstances. There is no stipulation that requires your action be appropriate to how your character would actually act. If you're going to add such a thing, how is that different from just removing the reward? Both change the rules, but mine requires no judgment calls.

                                                        Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                                        Often I find that people in certain situations alter their behaviour and don't act as they normally do, and when they do this altering it is generally at least minorly stressful and makes it harder for them to deal with their problems because they aren't themselves at the time... Not getting willpower, since you aren't fulfilling your anchors seems like a good representation of that to me.
                                                        And I find that people are way more complicated than that. Summing someone up as a single word archetype does not do them justice, and so even if someone is a Jester, they do not crack jokes constantly, nor do they necessarily feel uncomfortable when they can't. Real human beings change fluidly from day to day, moment to moment, and while they tend to follow behavior patterns, those are not absolute. Most people, even comedians, don't make jokes at funerals--they don't even feel tempted to do it. It's not going to cause them discomfort and pain beyond what the funeral is already causing.

                                                        Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                                        Because that way the game changes how you think and act. This isn't a "why not trust them to do it thing" in my opinion, since even without it, there is the assumption that your going to play your character properly. These mechanics make it so that when do self-affirming actions you have an actual representation of the effect doing that sort of thing has. When my character commits his vice, he regains willpower, not because "Lol metagame", but because he just did something that gives him respite.
                                                        This argument is much more nuanced, and I appreciate it. But I would just say that, when immersed in your character, you feel self affirmed when taking a self-affirming action. There is no meta-reward needed to feel affirmed. You just feel it because that is how your character would feel.
