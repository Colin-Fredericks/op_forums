    Time for a preview!

    Here's our last ghoul family: The Chaménos. Another new one to the setting, the Unwanted straddle a strange line between social and genetic ghoul family (see Half-Damned). They appear spontaneously in Nosferatu subcultures around the world, all with the same weakness and strength...

    Chaménos: The Ones Who Were Thrown Away

    "Save your pity. I have purpose; you have nothing."

    Where we came from

    In every culture across the globe, children are precious gifts, shining lights representing the future of their communities… except the ones who aren’t. Sparta cast out “imperfect” infants to die, and the Incans sacrificed their most beautiful children to ensure the favor of the gods. In the filthy streets of Industrial London, the desperate tossed wailing babes into the Thames rather than suffer more mouths to feed. Even today, unwanted newborns can be found on church steps, at hospital backdoors, or cast aside in dumpsters.

    However, what’s thrown away can be found again. It’s unclear when Nosferatu began collecting discarded children, or even where the practice first originated. Was it in the holy caves of the Andes Mountains, waiting for the sun-priests to leave so they could steal away their tributes? Or perhaps it started with watching churchyards in medieval France, snatching bassinets before the priests knew they had a new arrival.
    Regardless of methods, the why of it remains constant. What better way to secure retainers than from those humans other mortals cast out? No one goes looking for a divine sacrifice; no one files a missing person report for a baby a trash can.

    The Nosferatu rear these Unwanted as blood-slaves to their rescuers, indoctrinated into rejecting human society in favor of the All Night Society. At certain times and places in history, discarded children have been so common that the Haunts have created whole secret Foundling communities, living out of sight of the rest of civilization and with one generation training the next. As the world grew smaller, however, Haunts realized what they thought of as a local practice is in fact mirrored all around the globe. Now they’ve started to talk to each other.

    Who we are tonight

    While dozens of regional terms for the Unwanted exist, “Chaménos” has the widest recognition, and some even adopt it as a surname. This name was coined by a family of ghouls in Greece who claim descent from Sparta’s rejected infants, and they’ve made the greatest strides in connecting the various tribes of Foundlings in the modern world. Not so much a single family as an eclectic network of communities, Chaménos around the world are remarkably similar, no matter their cultural origin. Rather than everyday people forced into bondage, as many ghouls are, the Unwanted are raised with the idea that serving a Nosferatu master is the highest possible calling, and look forward to the day they become half-damned. The rest of the All Night Society might view that as brainwashing, but the Chaménos see it as duty. After all, without the Haunts, their ancestors would have died fresh from the womb — something their masters are quick to remind them of.

    This abnormal dedication is what the Foundlings are best known for, and they serve their patrons with a remarkable zeal, even for ghouls. Their extreme devotion can be a mixed blessing, however. All ghouls become obsessed with their domitors eventually, but a Chaménos’ life revolves around his master in a way even the rankest Vitae addict would find a tad overwrought.

    Rarely able to hold down normal jobs, groups of Foundlings often turn to crime to meet their masters’ needs, as well as their own. Drug cartels and black markets are common, with the Unwanted using the profits on behalf of their family, and a few of their would-be clients as dinner for their patrons.

    Stereotypes:
    • Daeva: Their “devotion” is a fleeting imitation of the real thing.
    • Gangrel: They act like being alone is good.
    • Mekhet: Always sneaking in where they aren’t wanted.
    • Nosferatu: Our saviors and our charges.
    • Ventrue: They make the best faces when you tell them to fuck themselves.

                Well, I'm never one to fly in the face of popular opinion.

                The Noctuku as a concept go all the way back to Vampire: The Masquerade. In VTM, they were the Nictuku, proto-Nosferatu who hunted their clanmates on behalf of their antediluvian master. In Requiem, they became a bloodline of Nosferatu cannibals, forced to exist on the outskirts of the All Night Society.

                Our take on the Noctuku had a lot of ink spilled over them. I noted above that we were originally just going to continue with the bloodline interpretation, albeit a corrupted one with some special mechanics. However, that interpretation just didn't work for me once the first draft came in. It felt arbitrary to separate them into the Horrors chapter if they were still just a bloodline with a fourth Discipline and a bane, and much too player-facing for something that (mostly) is going to be used as an antagonist. However, the author — right at the last minute, where all great ideas form — included an optional sidebar about them being a viral infection. That was much more interesting to me, and it brought the whole idea together. Obviously there are some parallels with Malkavia here (though you'll have to wait until [REDACTED]: Ventrue to find out how we deal with that), but unlike the M, the Noctuku Strain is something you can actually put under a microscope. It's a true virus, but it just happens to only affect Nosferatu.

                In terms of systems, a suite of inherent abilities made more sense than dots on a character sheet. As for customizations, Phagia began as Protean Devotions, but again, it felt too player-facing. To unpack that term a bit, I mean things that you can spend XPs on for your character, and that are built with an eye toward player utility. Instead, I wanted something that didn't cost a single XP, and was brutally unfair. So, we repurposed the systems from those Devotions, and turned Phagia into a series of Dread Powers. They're not really the same as the ones in the Blue Book, since many of those are redundant for Disciplines, but the format worked well. I considered just removing their access to Disciplines altogether, but that felt too far off the baseline. Though, if you were using them for a mortals game, that might work as a stand in for having to track full-on vampire powers..

                Anyway, I've gabbed on enough. Meet the Noctuku:

                The Noctuku Strain: The Ravenous Dead

                “Go? I’ll let you go. But run hard. I want to work up an appetite.”

                The Nosferatu are stronger, sneakier, and more terrifying than other vampires. They are nightmares of the dead, and draw strength in their own horror. Yet predators rarely exist in a vacuum. There’s always a bigger fish, and even monsters have boogeymen.

                The Noctuku are what keep the Nosferatu awake in the day. The only thing more terrifying than their grotesque powers is their hunger for the flesh of other Kindred. That, and that any Nosferatu could be one — or become one.

                Background

                The Noctuku Strain has plagued the Kindred for thousands of years. Defiant and Sanctified researchers have found the earliest records of the contagion in records dating back to pre-contact North America, ancient Greece, and even the steppes of Mongolia. These geographically distinct locations offer eerily similar accounts: A vampire, easily identified as a Nosferatu, arrives in the community, and soon after all the other Nosferatu start to behave strangely.

                Where the accounts differ is the cause. Ojibwe Kindred believed Noctuku had been punished for devouring their own kin, while the Mongolians thought the Nosferatu’s innate filth made them hosts for unclean spirits. The Greeks saw the Noctuku as a cult of the Titan Cronus, who devoured his own children to maintain his throne. Whatever the fine details, one thing is consistent — when Noctuku arrive, Kindred vanish.

                The Strain is particularly virulent in periods of famine and war among mortals. Time and time again, such events have primed Kindred for small explosions of new Noctuku. When food becomes scarce and violence reigns, vampires have little choice but to turn on each other for sustenance, but by then it’s too late to ever go back. One bite is all it takes.

                A Plague of Hunger

                The Strain is a malignant virus transmitted through the blood and flesh of infected Nosferatu. Presently, only the Haunts are vulnerable to this virus, but some Kindred scientists fear the day the Strain mutates. Once it fully gestates, the infection is incurable. All it takes is a drop of Vitae or a tainted bite to become infected, and then it’s only a matter of time. The strong-willed can stave it off, but those who give in to the virus’s new desires change rapidly. The Haunt’s body begins an agonizing metamorphosis that makes them stronger, faster, and deadlier. The virus twists and reshapes their mouths to be better at ripping and tearing, and instills a deep and consuming hunger for dripping raw meat, still warm off the bone.

                Even more horrific are the changes to the Noctuku’s mind. The Beast never rests for most vampires, but a Noctuku’s Beast becomes an equal partner, constantly urging wanton violence and bloodlust. The Noctuku are always hungry, always hunting, and the only way to truly satisfy their need is the flesh of their former Kindred.

                The Cannibal Kings

                Despite their ravenous appetites, the Noctuku aren’t feral. Indeed, once the infection completes its transformation, they appear no stranger than most other Nosferatu, and that’s how they hide. The Noctuku blend in like any vampire among the kine, albeit with a more restrictive diet. To keep up the charade, Cannibals must remain ever mindful of the local Kindred ecology, and rely on separation and discretion when hunting.

                Their specialized feeding requirements mean the most successful Noctuku tend to fall into two categories: nomads, or apex predators in metropolises where Kindred often come and go. The Cannibals are highly territorial for this reason — which is one of their few positive traits, as it means they almost never come in groups. Almost.

                However, one is often more than enough. The Noctuku are stronger, faster, and deadlier than other Kindred. Their ferocity and scarcity are such that they’re relegated to myth outside the Necropoli, who pass whispered stories of solitary Cannibals devouring entire broods. If the Nosferatu are talking about the Noctuku, it’s already too late.

                Noctuku Strain (Persistent)

                You are infected with the Noctuku Strain but are not yet one of the Cannibals. As the disease takes hold, you lose the 10-again quality on all actions any night you resist the urge to consume Kindred Vitae or flesh. This hunger is so great that the mere presence of other, uninfected Kindred provokes hunger frenzy, though it only applies once a scene. If presented with Kindred Vitae, you suffer a –4 modifier to resist frenzy as if you were starving. However, you can spend a point of Willpower to ignore this hunger for one scene. Ending this Condition requires you not to feed on anything stronger than human blood for one night per dot of the infecting Noctuku’s Blood Potency.

                Should you consume Vitae, you regain a spent Willpower in addition to the usual effects of drinking vampire blood. If you indulge in flesh, you regain all spent Willpower. Each time you give in, the time it takes for this Condition to run its course resets. If you indulge in Kindred Vitae or flesh a number of times equal to (11 – the infecting Noctuku’s Blood Potency), the infection enters its active stage: Resolve this Condition and gain the traits of a Noctuku.

                Possible Sources: A Noctuku’s blood or bite, rampant diablerie.

                Resolution: Accept your place among the Cannibals.

                Beat: Infect another vampire.

                                        I'm just wondering, what was the main theme yall went for when designing the bloodlines and ghoul families? I'm sorry if that question is one yall would rather not answer (which i completely understand)
                                        Totally happy to answer questions!

                                        Theme-wise, my major note to the bloodline writers was to emphasize the horror. Some of the early drafts (and this included my own) were a tad too generic; they were good bloodlines, but not necessarily great Nosferatu bloodlines. The Gethsemeni, for instance, had a lot more emphasis on breaching the Masquerade in their first draft, whereas the final version focuses on the holy horror of being a prophet touched by the indefinable hand of God. All the bloodlines evince a different kind of scary: the unknown, knowing too much, being a cog in a machine, addiction and decadence, and so on.

                                        The ghoul families are more about the ways Nosferatu blood (for lack of a better term) fucks you up. Haunts have the most overt curse of the Damned, and that twisting nature can extend to their servants. Nosferatu ghouls are just weirder than those of other clans, and all of our ghoul families are a little off-kilter. For example, while we never come right out and say it, the Chamenos are really a subtype of ghoul rather than a family. We used the ghoul family mechanics for convenience, but they're more akin to things like mnemonic ghouls and Heisenbergs (both from Half-Damned). I've always liked the idea that Nosferatu ghouls mutate a bit, which is something you can find in Masquerade a bit more than Requiem, so that was the guiding light there.

                                        From a broader perspective, the overarching theme of the book was "Lonely Together", and everything in Better Feared ties back to that. Nosferatu and their ghouls are insular rejects, so they had better get used to each other.

                                            A fine Friday to you all!

                                            The following is one of our five sample Necropoli. While about half the Warrens chapter is taken up with background and systems, the rest is dedicated to these example domains. Second Chances did 4/5 of them, and he did a fantastic job as usual. The following is from Lethbridge, Alberta, in a continuing effort by he and I to Canadianize the Chronicles of Darkness as much as possible, eh?

                                            The Coal Shafts: Sik-ooh-kotok (Lethbridge), Canada

                                            Deep in the Albertan south, Sik-ooh-kotok has always been a bastion for the Nosferatu. The Coal Shafts started as a hiding place from the hated sun, but now they drip with power as the greatest Kindred come home to roost. Intrigue and danger are afoot, though, as the other clans test their chains, and covenants threaten to tear the domain apart from within.

                                            Where we came from

                                            The first of us arrived in the 1870s and 80s when the settlers found coal along the Old Man River, but most of us didn’t arrive until the first railways came in ’85. We snuck into the mines, picking off our victims one by one. Who would question a miner feeling exhausted and sick after a long shift? And if someone disappeared in the darkness, well, it’s a dangerous job…

                                            The mines started closing in the early 20th century with the oil boom in Western Canada. Coal wasn’t profitable anymore, not worth descending down the shafts where sharp teeth waited to prick the skin. It was a mixed blessing for us. The Warren was now truly ours, a safe place where Haunts can rest away from mortal eyes, but our food no longer wandered close to our homes.

                                            As Lethbridge adapted to a new global economy, we ventured out of our mines and seized control of the town that was rightfully ours.

                                            Who we are tonight

                                            The first Kindred in Lethbridge were Haunts, and it remains Haunt territory tonight. That much didn’t change, but our purpose evolved. As the University became more and more central to the city’s lifeblood, we fused our faith with scientific innovation. The Lancea et Sanctum and Ordo Dracul joined forces to understand the Kindred condition and build the best monster possible. The Warren’s dark caverns, far from where any mortal can hear the screams, echo with their gurgling experiments, creating monsters that are both holy and blasphemous.

                                            Their alliance also keeps the other covenants in check, although such vulgar politics aren’t supposed to matter in the Necropolis. The Sanctified have the sheer numbers to sway the domain, but the Dragons are hot on their heels, and aren’t above bribing the Judges with new monstrosities to get their way. It’s a dangerous night when a priest returns to the surface with a grin on his face, covered in soot and blood. And that’s ignoring the Establishment’s debauched parties and the Firebrands’ hidden dynamite caches, secreted away amongst the twisting tunnels. The mines aren’t just a place to hide from the sun — they let us hide from each other, plotting and conniving our way to rise to the All Night Society’s pinnacle.

                                            These politics are the biggest threat to our society. All are equal within the Coal Shafts, but the covenants still jockey for power and influence, even if they go out of their way to make it look like they aren’t. Make no mistake though — the other clans would love to take the foundation we’ve built and reel all the benefits for themselves, without having to do the hard labor. It’s all a twisted rosebush labyrinth: beautiful to look at, but twisted and dangerous to touch. Rest assured, your nights in Lethbridge won’t be quiet, but they sure as hell won’t be boring.

                                            Secrets and Lies (Excerpt)

                                            Sik-ohh-kotok’s Nosferatu still age, albeit slowly. Punch an elder hard enough and you’ll find he doesn’t have bone under his dead muscles anymore, but coal. Given enough time, this anthracite petrification oozes its way into the vampire’s flesh, stiffening and immobilizing the ancient creature. Before that happens, the elders roost deep within a secret chamber in the Necropolis, becoming twisted, bloody stalagmites in their clan’s lair. Drinking the blood dripping from these humanoid coal formations allows younger Haunts to tap into the elder’s knowledge and power until the next sunrise.

                                            Systems
                                            • Heart: The elevator shafts of the abandoned coal mines.
                                            • Focus: Uncover secrets about the Kindred condition (Information, formerly Protection). Residents of the Necropolis gain a Beat whenever they contribute to the study of vampires, or invent a unique power of the blood, like a Devotion.
                                            • Benefit: When spending Willpower on Academics, Occult, or Politics rolls about vampires, residents gain the 8-again quality.
                                            • Burden: Let no member of another clan enter the mines.
                                            • Clot: Guardian Swarm (undead rattlesnakes)
                                            • Residents of the Coal Shafts have access to the Bleak Annals and Dark Hub Merits.

                                            -----

                                                    Let's have another Night Horror preview!

                                                    The Nowhere Men are entirely new to Vampire, not being based on any first edition material. I'll let the write up below explain their niche, but first I wanted to talk a little about why I like them so much. The Nowhere Men don't have mechanics. They don't have traits or Dread Powers; they just work when you (the Storyteller) need them to. Okay, they have one mechanic, which is the Condition they place to track their victims, but even that's mostly a narrative tool. The Nowhere Men aren't meant to be something STs use to vex their players: they're a tool you can use to weird up your games, and get players to think creatively about how they use their powers, and how they solve their problems when they can't just hide in the shadows.

                                                    Nowhere Men: Stalkers of Shades

                                                    “How can you not see them?! They’re right there! They’re right there! They’re right—”

                                                    Every culture has urban legends. These stories reinforce morality through fear, instilling cultural taboos and promoting group conformity. The hook-handed killer on Lover’s Lane may or may not have existed, but his legacy puts a damper on teenage sexcapades. Bloody Mary won’t really get you if you say her name three times in a mirror, but it’s a bonding ritual. None of these things are real. They can’t hurt you.

                                                    Until they do.

                                                    Even the All Night Society has urban legends, and the Nowhere Men are a favorite of the Nosferatu and Mekhet. These men — or what are assumed to be men — are invisible stalkers, wraiths who hunt vampires that spend too much time hiding away from mortals. Use your Obfuscate too often, they say, and the Nowhere Men will take you, piece by piece. It’s a warning against becoming too reliant on one’s Disciplines. They’re just a fairy tale. They can’t hurt you.

                                                    Until they do.

                                                    The Nowhere Men aren’t just a story. They exist in a place beyond human perception, or even supernatural perception. Impossibly, they can only be seen by vampires using the powers of Obfuscate, who are also the only ones who can be their victims.

                                                    Background

                                                    Nowhere Men appear as humanoid voids in the fabric of reality. Their features force the eye to slide to the margins of their forms, and it’s difficult to focus on them for long. Any vampire who uses Obfuscate gains their notice.

                                                    If a Nowhere Man can reach a victim and touch — just touch — a piece of her, he’ll take it. A hand vanishes. An eye disappears. A leg slips off into nothingness. In each case, nothing remains except a raw, red wound, one that looks as if it’s pressed against clear glass. The Nowhere Men are never sated for long, however, and they’ll keep hounding their prey until, piece by piece, they take every part. Pity the powerful, for if the Nowhere Men catch one of them demonstrating full mastery of the shadows, they may not bother taking parts.

                                                    Kindred snatched up by the Nowhere Men cease to be. They cannot be traced with any known technology, Discipline, or blood magic. They vanish from the world, leaving behind little more than a memory as they’re stolen away to somewhere — nowhere — else.

                                                    Close Encounters

                                                    Modern vampires teach their childer that Obfuscate tricks the mind, but maybe the truth is more subtle. Some ancients believed it drew upon a darkness at the edge of awareness, or even on the veil between worlds. Whether or not they were right, one thing is certain: Obfuscate attracts the Nowhere Men, and it’s the only way the creatures can interact with physical beings. They may be denizens of some world in-between, or perhaps they just hunt it, and live in another shadow reality.

                                                    Why they hunt is a mystery, and those who’ve survived encounters offer a myriad of theories: They are broken ghosts or spirits, prohibited from interacting with the rest of their kind; they are the souls of the damned, trapped between worlds; they are elder vampires, so heavy with blood their very existence clouds the minds of their prey.

                                                    While any vampire can be a victim of the Nowhere Men, it’s the Haunts and Shadows who’ve encountered them most. However, adding yet more grist to the rumor mill, it seems they have a preference for Nosferatu victims, when given a choice. One Dragon scholar speculates they were once a Haunt bloodline, one that drifted too far into the dark, and now seeks to return to the flesh world by juryrigging new physical forms. An Acolyte mythologist says he has evidence the Brothers Worm sealed these creatures in an annex in the depths of the Underworld, and the Nowhere Men avenge themselves on their childer.

                                                    Or maybe? They just like the taste of Haunt.

                                                    Rumors (Excerpt)

                                                    “The urban legend about the Nowhere Men is exactly that. Want to know what’s powerful enough to see through invisibility and overpower vampires? What else? Vampires. Duh.”

                                                    Just before World War I, Kindred archeologists recovered an centuries-old scroll from a crypt beneath Kyoto. Among other things, it contained a short poem called “The Man Shaped Like Nothing,” which described a creature who transformed first into a bloodthirsty oni, and then into a being of pure nothingness. Recently, scholars from the Lancea et Sanctum have begun to re-examine poems like this, as well as similar stories in ghost and demon mythology. They’ve found a disturbing prevalence for words like “void” or “absence,” frequently mistranslated as synonyms for shadow, darkness, and night. If this correlates to a real phenomenon, not only is it possible the Nowhere Men were once Kindred, but it may be there are many more of them, and yet more to come.

                                                        Good Monday to you all! I took a break from excerpts yesterday, but I've got a fresh one for you now.

                                                        The Fear Eaters are the kind of antagonist I love to find in a Night Horrors book. They're a simple design and a strong concept, and you can use them easily as either villains or allies. They'd also work well in other games, like Hunter, or even Changeling. They have lots of potential story hooks, too, and I think the author hit all the right notes to make them creepy, scary, and a little sympathetic.

                                                        Incidentally, the structure of the Fear Eating Merits take a bit of inspiration from Shunned by the Moon, as well as the "minor templates" from Hurt Locker.

                                                        Fear Eaters: Taking Your Life Back

                                                        “Breathe in. Deep. Let the fear gather up inside you and then… swallow it. All of it. See? Better already.”

                                                        Background

                                                        The Fear Eaters began, as many things do, with trauma. In 1989, brothers Ken and Jason Arden were dared by a group of older boys to break into a neighborhood haunted house in Overland Park, Kansas. It was a sweltering, sticky summer night when the boys went in, and a soupy Midwestern morning when Ken alone escaped, sheet white and nonverbal. He would spend most of his teens in psychiatric wards, suffering the indignities of shock therapy and quack hypnotherapy. Nothing worked. Not until the book arrived.

                                                        If asked, Kenneth — as he prefers to be called now — says a distant relative sent him a simple, brown paper–wrapped package on his 16th birthday. In truth, it had no return address. Reading had become Kenneth’s only outlet, so it wasn’t unusual for him to receive books from family and friends. But this one was special. The book was old and charred, with a smiling lithograph on its blackened cover. Neatly printed in gilded letters was the title: On the Consumption of Terror. No author was listed.

                                                        Although half the book was either damaged or missing, he devoted himself to the text. He read it hundreds of times, over and over. Then, one day he asked a shocked orderly if he could call his parents. He checked out of the psychiatric hospital that week and used his family’s guilt to support him as he dedicated the next fifteen years to the study of religion, psychology, and esoterica, all in service to his goal of rebirthing the text of the book. When Fear Eating: How to Take Your Life Back was finally released in 2010, it was an underground hit on the self-help circuit, but the book was only the beginning.

                                                        It’s been thirty-one years since the haunted house, and Kenneth has never moved past his terror and grief. It’s helped him realize the book was incomplete for a reason. It needed his fear. It needed his memories of the long-tooth man with the bloodless skin whose smile was torture and whose shrieking laugh had cracked his life open.

                                                        Arden knows others like him are out there, those who’ve also experienced these creatures firsthand. Now he travels the globe searching for them under the guise of an endless book tour. He makes a point to offer one free seminar per city, with a reasonable entry fee for all non-private events. If he takes notice of someone “special” at one of these events, they’ll receive an invitation to an exclusive weekend worship. That’s where the chosen learn the true power of Fear Eating.

                                                        The Art of Eating Fear

                                                        You can’t learn to eat fear if you haven’t experienced it. Arden and his acolytes can sense those who are open to their teachings, people with the potential to consume fear through exposure. Like, for instance, victims of the Nosferatu. Not all mortals who’ve encountered the Haunts are capable of Fear Eating — even those who’ve lost blood to them. To become a Fear Eater, one must have lost something in the exchange, more fundamental than just a few pints. Still, the Fear Eaters haven’t been successful just because of vampires. Their seminars can be… intense.

                                                        Once you’re chosen, the real work begins. Initiates are first taught simple techniques, like meditation, breathing exercises, and mantras to ground them. Then, they’re given an unsafe dose of LSD and walked back through their buried pain, over and over and over until these memories can be summoned as a reflex.

                                                        And it works. Somehow. Calling forth the echo of a past trauma suppresses the fear reflex in the here and now. Fear Eating changes you. It doesn’t make you invincible, but it does make you confident, bold, and decisive. You won’t sweat the small things, like hesitating to ask for a phone number, or failing to speak your mind. The sudden injection of confidence into a life of anxious trepidation leads to sudden and profound changes that, if nothing else, feel like a good thing.

                                                        Once the program takes hold, the book becomes your holy text. You can see the truth Arden hid behind the prose to keep out the unworthy. It’s a simple one: Fear is a lie, and the only way to kill a lie is to make sure everyone knows the truth. You realize the more Fear Eaters you can bring to the cause, the weaker the lie will become, so you start to guide others to seminars, workshops, and events. You give out the book as a birthday present, or the office Secret Santa, or leave strategically tabbed and dogeared copies on public transit.

                                                        It’s Not a Cult

                                                        Of course not. It’s a mindset — a way of life. There’s no grand network of Fear Eaters holding each other accountable, or love bombing each other into complacency. Perish the thought. Sure, you meet a few times a year and chat on forums, but who doesn’t want to be near likeminded people? And yes, maybe they do spend a lot of time talking about the book, re-reading ragged copies, or practicing breathing exercises until they choke. It’s not their fault they’re passionate about something. Isn’t it the same with CrossFit and veganism? You can stop at any time… but with results like these, why would anyone want to?

                                                        New Merits (Excerpt)

                                                        Through careful training, mind-bending drugs, and regression therapy, adherents of Fear Eating can call on memories of past terror to bolster their spirit. This sudden flash of fear suppresses natural responses to anxiety, be it a stressful situation or the use of a supernatural power. With careful study and cultivation, an individual can radiate fear back at those who would wield it against her.

                                                        To learn these Merits, a character needs to fulfill one of two prerequisites. Either she must have failed a breaking point due to a supernatural power of fear (e.g., Nightmare), or during one of the Fear Eaters’ “seminars”, where they break down their recruits so the healing might begin in earnest.

                                                        Resist Fear (•)

                                                        Prerequisites: Mortal, Empathy ••

                                                        Effect: Fear is fleeting. Your character has learned to wrestle her emotions for control of her mind. She adds her Empathy dots as a bonus to any action to contest or resist social coercion using negative emotion, from mundane intimidation to Nightmare.

                                                        In Social maneuvering, the Fear Eater can spend a Willpower to ignore hard leverage involving intimidation or threats.
