                    Originally posted by Ever Professional View Post
                    I was curious about the Maiyarap, I saw that their non-favored Treasures were limited by their bloodline status, so is bloodline status a thing similar to clan status so you'd need like five dots of maiyarap status or was that a more informal "if people in the bloodline like them enough to teach them" kind of status.
                    You'd need dots in a "Bloodline Status" Merit, yeah. That's not a form of Kindred Status explicitly defined in the core, but we've used it to key into a few things for bloodlines in other books, and since you can pretty much have Status in anything, we didn't feel the idea that bloodlines have their own form of Kindred Status was too much of a stretch. Basically, it works the same way as Clan/Covenant/City Status, albeit more niche. Because the Treasures are kind of a BIG gift for a single bloodline, we wanted the barrier of entry to be a little bit higher than usual, so we used the same Status limitation the Ordo uses, but without a cost break.

                            Originally posted by Teatime View Post
                            There seems to be a paralell between elder Bak-Ra and their alleged patron, in the sense that they're all senile and out of touch, as depicted through myth. Was that intentional?
                            More a lucky coincidence (though it may have inspired the original Ancient Bloodlines version, I cannot say). The idea of elder Bak-Ra coming of as deranged (by elder vampire standards) to the rest of the All Night Society was a very obvious setup to build from the initial hook of "vampires who believe they belong into the sun." After this initial setup was clear, the idea of making it a bit of a simile to senility or dementia in humans was an obvious followup, which then also introduced the concept of the generational conflict, which I found out a neat contrast to how generations in a bloodline are usually portrayed. Personally, I find the idea of playing a young Bak-Ra who navigates their elder-sire's weird whims and claims and their regular modern Requiem rather intruiging.
                            On a more meta level, I also found it a good way to get some of those old Fog of Ages plot hook back into 2e while leaving the Fog as a universal thing (rightfully) in the coffin.

                            There are some more Ra-specific aspects in some of the unspoilered content though.

                            Trying to balance the idea of the Sun-Stroked being obviously confused on one hand and "maybe there is something to their claims after all" on the other was the biggest challenge. I find that ambiguity serves ancient mysteries well.

                                        10-07-2022, 03:24 PM
                                        Happy Friday, everyone! Today, we're taking a look at the Nehebkau bloodline of the Twice-Cursed...clan? But first, a programming note. This weekend is Canadian Thanksgiving, and due to travel, I probably won't have an update for Saturday. I should be back Monday, though I'll tease which bloodline we'll be looking at next (regardless of date) below.

                                        To talk about the Nehebkau, we first have to talk about the creation of the Twice-Cursed. When I wrote them for Spilled Blood, I honestly thought people wouldn't grok them. They're one of the stranger concepts, and while they don't really rock the boat mechanically, they're probably some of the most screwed vampires in the game: two clan curses (Daeva and Nosferatu, the two that play worst together), have to pay five Experiences for Majesty and Nightmare, can only Embrace Touchstones, etc. Don't get me wrong; I was happy with what I came up with, and they fill a vampire archetype that exists in a lot of folklore and media, but I fretted that people would find them a bit half-baked, or unplayable. They were one of the last things I came up with during first drafts, and I kind of thought my reach had exceeded my grasp.

                                        Fortunately, as I often am, people seem to really dig the Twice-Cursed, and they're probably the thing I wrote from that book that I see most discussed on forums and whatnot. Not to say that just any player can pick up a Twice-Cursed character; definitely more of an advanced class protagonist choice, but there's always someone who likes the play on hard mode.

                                        This is all a rambling way of saying that I really wanted to make sure the Stalkers got a bloodline. In SB, you can optionally allow them to join both Nosferatu and Daeva bloodlines with some finessing of Discipline spreads, but I really wanted to see what kind of bizarre mutations this already bizarre clan* could produce.

                                        In that, I think the author filled the brief. The Nehebkau can slough off one of their clan curses onto a doppelganger, letting the creature go out and have its way with the world while the Twice-Cursed pretends to be a Daeva for a while (or a Nosferatu; no judgments!) I'll let the write-up below go into greater depth on that concept, but one thing I want to touch on a bit is the historical aspect of this bloodline. Something the author and I talked about during development was where to place this bloodline in the Dark Eras. The vanilla Twice-Cursed are "modern", in that the paper trail starts in the US during the New England vampire panic. Of course, there are some alternate theories about that in the write-up, but nothing too concrete.

                                        So, you know how we were talking about clans/bloodlines appearing in different places and points in history? This is one of the few bloodlines where we pretty much state that that happens explicitly. The Nehebkau contradict the Twice-Cursed origin story; whether you believe their founder and her claims about lost divinity is another matter... This isn't really a contradiction, of course; it's something I was obviously hinting about in Spilled Blood, and that I wanted to elaborate on here. It's a contradiction in the world of Vampire, which is just a fancy way of saying it's a plot hook.

                                        Alright, that's it for me. Here are the Nehebkau! Next time, let's look at the Amara Havana!

                                        Nehebkau: The Ones Who Disgust You

                                        “Oh, my dear, you don’t want to see us. You aren’t ready to see us.”

                                        It was too good to be true.

                                        I’ll admit, I was desperate. It’s not like I had any good options in front of me, so being once-cursed instead of Twice-Cursed seemed like a great deal.

                                        They just left out the part where that thing won’t shut the fuck up.

                                        I can feel it trying to move now, trying to wrench out of my back…

                                        Once in a while, it has something useful to say. The only reason I’m not a smear of ash on the pavement right now is that it figured out that the guy I was hitting on at the bar was a hunter. But right now? It’s hungry. Hungry enough that it’s gone past the point of words to just growling and screeching.

                                        “Fucking fine.” I stand up, making sure to bump it against the back of the chair, hard, to get it to stop making noise for a second. I hold my breath, grit my teeth, stretch my arm behind me to grab its skin, and pull.

                                        No matter how many times I do this, the feeling of flesh and bone tearing away from me always feels awful. It staggers out, blinking in the moonlight, looking just like me. Like always. It sniffs the air… then runs off without a second glance.

                                        It’s going to leave a mess.

                                        We all have a dark side. The acidic part of the mind that craves violence and revenge for a slight; the self-serving voice that knows “what’s mine is mine” is the only sensible philosophy; the voracious thing that wants to sever all the bonds holding back the id. Vampires call it the Beast, but not even a saint lacks for vices. Nor can everyone tame that primal urge, and many turn to all sorts of remedies to keep the monster in check, whether spiritual or chemical or both. But we can never really excise our demons.
                                        We shouldn’t, in point of fact.

                                        Who knows what they would get up to on their own.

                                        The Twice-Cursed are problem enough for the Kindred, but a recent development has provided even more cause for concern. The so-called Stalkers have grown populous enough to foster the creation of a bloodline: the Nehebkau. These Twice-Cursed are perhaps the most literal manifestation of that name, with each Two-Face having, as the name suggests, a second face located somewhere on their body, something horrible and rotting, with pieces of bone and sinew exposed. It whispers secrets to its host, warns them of threats and, in return, only demands a little… extra for its assistance. For the lucky ones, inasmuch as any Twice-Cursed can be lucky, it’s somewhere easy to hide, usually on their back.

                                        But even for those who aren’t so fortunate, there’s a little moment of reprieve. That Second Self can be split away into a clone of the vampire, leaving only the beautiful (or, at least, presentable) half behind while a shambling horror wanders about the night, following the worst impulses of its host. Just don’t think too hard about what it’s doing or why you feel fuller when you have to become one again. Ignorance is bliss, and what’s dead probably won’t return. Just keep an eye out for the ones who do.

                                        But where does this ability come from? It’s awfully specific, and to those few Kindred who know of this bloodline (and don’t attempt to exterminate it), it far more closely resembles black magic and strange curses than a lineage. If you buy into the stories presented by its probable founder, the self-titled Enheduanna, they aren’t Twice-Cursed at all. The story she tells the revenants and childer she’s gathered over the years is that she’s an ancient deity of life and death, the two-faced divinity of the ancients. Hathor–Sekhmet, Mitra–Varuna, Hel; whatever you wish to call her. Her legacy is older than any name. Enheduanna claims — believes — she once walked among the pillars of the First City, convening with immortal beings far greater than the Kindred and witnessing the faces of gods even the hoariest elders would not recognize.

                                        This clashes with what most Kindred know of the Stalkers. The Twice-Cursed are supposed to be a contemporary problem for the All Night Society, a relatively modern twist of the blood that no one’s yet managed to untwist. It’s easy to dismiss the claims of Enheduanna and her cultists as comforting lies to lure in wayward Stalkers, but the problem is, the more one looks through history’s tapestry, the more one finds threads about “vampires” disconcertingly similar to these Amphisbaenas. This calls the entire narrative surrounding the Twice-Cursed into question. The Nehebkau vehemently insist they’ve been around for centuries — and, by proxy, so have the Twice-Cursed.

                                        Does this mean all Kindred were once like the Stalkers, dead wretches caught between the duality of lust and repugnance? If so, perhaps there’s a voice waiting to wrench out of the flesh of every Lick…

                                        Why you want to be us

                                        So, you’ve had enough of being a “Stalker.” Of course you have. Well, you don’t have to be Twice-Cursed anymore: We’ll elevate you to the status of your peers in the All Night Society. Which ain’t much, no, but it’s the closest you’ll ever get to normalcy, and the cost isn’t even as bad as it sounds!

                                        Why you should fear us

                                        You don’t need to be scared of us. You can trust us. We’ve fixed ourselves. We’re just like you now. Don’t mind that shadow — it’s just your imagination. Don’t look at it.

                                        Why we should fear ourselves

                                        You can divide yourself in half, but when you get right down to it, that’s still you out there. It’s the worst part of you, and that’s… pretty fucking bad, honestly. You can’t keep it away forever.

                                        Parent Clan: Twice-Cursed (Night Horrors: Spilled Blood, p. 102)

                                        Nicknames: Two-Faces, Amphisbaenas

                                        Bloodline Bane (The Curse of Duality): Unlike the Twice-Cursed, the Nehebkau can technically create childer from mortals who aren’t a vampire’s Touchstone. The result, however, isn’t pretty. If a Two-Face’s Second Self (p. 113) drains a mortal to the point of death, the vampire’s player must roll Humanity as though a spontaneous Embrace were occurring (Vampire, p. 94). On a failure, [REDACTED]

                                        Disciplines: Animalism, Celerity, Protean, Vigor

                                        In the Dark Eras

                                        Arthur’s Britannia — 400–500 CE (DE2, p. 86): It’s a time of promise for the Nehebkau, or so they believe. The Camarilla has collapsed, and they’re able to run free without the threat of their nature exposing them to Kindred repression. Unfortunately, some would tame this glorious anarchy, from mortals who hunt the supernatural to would-be kings to those who bow before unworthy gods like the Parliament of Owls — or, for that matter, the capital-G God. Amphisbaenas form their own little domains far away from larger cities, positioning themselves as the two-faced deities of prehistoric civilizations once again. The more paranoid among them fear this will soon come to an end, but most are too content with this rare moment of acceptance that they refuse to worry about any encroaching forces.

                                        Doubting Souls — 1690–1695 CE (DE1, p. 358): A hundred years before Frederick Ransom and Mercy Brown and the horrors that rise from their sad tale, a strange creature stalks the colonists of New England. The nature of the beast is unknown to even the Scarlet Watch, the local experts on vampires, and they speculate that the thing may not be one of the dead at all, but a demon that feasts on the living and takes blood as tribute to an infernal master. In truth, it is the Second Self of a torpid Nehebkau, a refugee from Europe who made the journey to the New World many centuries ago. Somehow, the symbiote is able to leave its host without their consent. The creature finds the mortals in disarray over witches and devils, and it uses the brewing social chaos to mask its feedings as the work of Satan’s servants.

                                        The Reign of Terror — 1793–1794 CE (DE2, p. 316): While there are no written records of the Nehebkau before the modern era (at least not under that name), Enheduanna claims she bore witness to the Terror when she first woke from torpor, and that she and a handful of childer posing as Daeva helped guide the Tenth Choir covenant, teaching them to refine their bizarre blood sorcery with secrets from Lost Irem. The Chorists made a good fit. There’s nothing more natural for a Nehebkau than walking the line between the divine and infernal. To those who buy into the idea of being personally divine themselves, it’s only natural that lesser servants of the divine should either bow before them or be destroyed. And destroying a rival God is an activity they’re more than happy to go along with. The vainest of the Amphisbaenas believe overthrowing the old order will bring back the world Enheduanna was born into, one where they will be revered either out of terror or love. They’re not particularly concerned with which of those it might be…

                                        Bloodline Gift: The Second Self

                                        To cope with her nature, a Nehebkau can literally split off one-half of her double curse.

                                        In addition to the following ability, all Two-Faces gain the Dark Passenger Merit upon joining the bloodline.

                                        Cost: 1 Willpower

                                        Dice Pool: None

                                        Action: Instant

                                        Duration: Nights equal to Blood Potency

                                        The vampire literally splits in half, with its doppelganger gaining either the Lonely Curse or the Wanton Curse, while the Nehebkau loses it for the duration. Should the Nehebkau have any dots in Protean, they may offload further banes onto the Second Self, one per Discipline dot. This does not affect her detachment dice pools, however.
