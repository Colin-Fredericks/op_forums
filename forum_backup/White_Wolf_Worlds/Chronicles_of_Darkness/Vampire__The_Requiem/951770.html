    Other supernaturals as NPC's.

    08-16-2016, 12:20 PM
    Hi everyone.

    I'm running a primarily vampire game with some Hunter mixed into it and I've been wanting to throw in a couple werewolves and mages. Now note, these won't be big characters. Some will be antagonists and others will simply be NPC's that the players can talk to (in short, there is a TF:V-style organization that houses like-minded individuals, whether it be mortal, mage, vampire or werewolf). Now my only problem is, I have never done anything with mage and werewolf so I don't know how they actually play out. Guess what I'm asking is, what should mage/werewolf antagonist be able to do? Also, do you have any nifty characters that make for more interesting antagonists? I'm cool with werewolves acting like some sort of gang, but how would a mage act? I feel like they would play more mind games/mess with their opponents than anything.

    Thanks

      Unsure which edition you're using. First edition Hunter had a 'monster builder,' which second edition expands for the whole game with its 'Horror builder,' that you could use to make either. You could use either of those, or use the Werewolf or Mage rules if you had the werewolf or mage books.

      The Night Horrors books offer interesting antagonists fully built with backgrounds and motivations.

      The big thing you would want to keep in mind for a werewolf is changing into a monster that can slaughter people and healing very fast. They tend to be territorial but gathering in a gang is the least of ways for them to do things. Still, you don't seem to want to focus on the actual Werewolf so just going by cliches is fine. Mages cast spells, you might want to focus on an are of expertise but they tend to be about enforcing the Masquerade and investigating magical occurrences.. If you have the Hunter, Witch Finders book offers a substitute for mages with their witches.

                      Mages: Curious about EVERYTHING. Mages gain power from learning things so the opportunity to learn about a vampires immortality first hand will really interest them. Depending on the mage (black cloaked necromancer) he may suggest a partnership of sorts. Mages know stuff but they don't usually like combat. Get them in their lair though and they may have all kinds of nasty magic traps waiting for hapless intruders. Mages will never face a group of dangerous monsters directly they'll mail you a cell phone (peaceful contact) or direct a cult to take out assets (not so peaceful contact). Oh yeah and it's really easy for a mage to know things that no one is supposed to know. Your off shore accounts? He has their numbers and passwords. Your mortal relatives? Friended them on Facebook yesterday. The locations of your havens? Marked on the map. Even an apprentice mage with a little time can find out a frightening amount of information.

                              Originally posted by Krzyzstof View Post
                              What about werewolves? I know they are generally portayed as savage beasts, but I feel like there is a lot more to them. Also, what reason would they have to cause conflict other than territory?
                              tl;dr The Wolf Must Hunt. For glory, for honor, for territory, for hate, for personal reasons. It's a need from their very soul and not something they take lightly.

                              The last coherent scions of the god of a world that no longer exists, werewolves are part spirit of the hunt and part flesh-in-flux. Without their world, they have no place, no real home, and most have found themselves following in the footsteps of one of their progenitors, the fallen Father Wolf, with the guidance of their other procreator, Mother Moon, as a way to make a place for themselves in the world. Some rage against Mother Moon's edicts, seeing her as a betrayer, and other Uratha as her willing slaves. Others try to find their own way, ending up bonding with spirits strange and alien and occasionally evil. Across the board only one thing is true, the Wolf Must Hunt.

                              Territory isn't something all werewolves will deal with. Some are nomadic, some stay hidden, most are willing to extend their hunts beyond the range of their territory. But I'll try and stick close to the basics.

                              All werewolves have a foot in two worlds, our world and the Shadow, a world of predatory animism, where everything, from concepts to objects, has a spiritual reflection. The only exception is humans, who lack a spirit. The state of the 'environment' of the world reflects what's going on in the physical, so if a city is full of angry riots and fear, in the Shadow that anger and fear palpably manifest in the form of spirits, who can be pretty powerful. These spirits can then influence the real world, including possessing people, to further create whatever idea they represent.

                              The Forsaken, generally, try to make sure either side of the world is in chaos. They do this by calling a sacred hunt, for the pack or group of packs, and targeting whatever is upsetting the balance. However, there's other reasons to call a hunt, such a particularly notorious monster who just might make for a glorious hunt, a personal rivalry such as someone who has attacked one of the werewolf's human or wolf-blooded packmates or family. Bans or mandates from lodges can direct the werewolf towards always hunting down certain beings, the Eaters of the Dead lodge have a hate-on for vampires who they see as a taint upon the world.

                              The Shadow sometimes develops places of power, called loci, which spirits and werewolves both covet as a source of energy and magical items. These can be considered part of territory, but means that sharing a hot spot isn't just a matter of acreage, as loci can be the equivalent of 'the Rack' for vampires, a place where they can refill. Without one, werewolves have other ways to get Essence, most notably eating people, but they tend to have more work associated with them.

                              Packs often form like social organizations, and aren't limited to werewolves. Humans, wolf-blooded and even vampires can be members of packs. The 'easy' way to do it is to have them as a gang, but they can be built around corporations or city governments, the Pack book has examples of them as a band, a fire department station, and even the staff and administration at a hospital.
