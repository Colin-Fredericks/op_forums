                                                    Well, you may want to read up on cunning men and Simon Forman for more ideas. Dee's library was fairly famous and is a day's journey from the city. There's an interesting book called "Under the Molehill" (a companion to Giordano Bruno and the Embassy Affair) both by John Bossy. Kendall's "Christopher Marlowe and Richard Baines" is very very interesting, as is The Reckoning by Charles Nicholl. I'd look into the mysterious death of Lord Strange (possibly an early patron of Shakespeare) at around the same time...

                                                    To, Elizabethan London is a great Mage setting as well, given the "School of Night" and the alchemical symbolism scattered everywhere in poetry and theatre at the time (the same argument could be made for an awesome Promethean game, actually).

                                                    The use of alchemy by women (as by the Countess of Cumberland and her daughter), and the queer-ness of so much spy craft at the time, along with theatre, is also thematically apposite to any number of interesting games. There was a great deal of skullduggery because of the precarious situation of Elizabeth viz a viz Hapsburg-dominated Europe.

                                                    Then one could take a Spenserian rote re the Changelings and make the Queen the fetch of Gloriana.
