    Requesting assistance with a story idea

    01-16-2018, 11:33 PM
    BACKSTORY (feel free to skip down to the main point)
    Hello, I'm Sean. I'm new here. A little back story on myself: I was first introduced to Vampire: The Masquerade in 2005 and I've been hooked on it ever since. I bought a lot of the books (not sure how many exactly), and I attempted to play the game with some friends of mine, but none of them were interested. It wasn't until 2016 that I found people to play it with, but they didn't really take it seriously. We were the Malkavian Detective Agency, a group of Malkavians that solved silly crimes. Our first case was a hit and run, and it turned into an elaborate murder plot. My character, John Wayne Gacy (no relation) solved the crime by interrogating a squirrel. As you can see, we weren't very serious about it. But last year, thanks to the Gentleman Gamer, I discovered Vampire: The Requiem. I purchased all the books in the first edition, and then the second edition and a couple of the newer books (Secrets of the Covenants and A Thousand Years of Night). And by purchased the books, I mean I bought all of the ones they had at my local book store and then bought the rest as PDFs on DriveThruRPG. Anyway, needless to say, I'm a fan.

    This brings me to my story idea. Since I couldn't find anyone to play with, I decided to create my own story and play it by myself, rolling the dice to see what my characters would do, but essentially leaving all the decisions up to myself. It's gone through several changes, from taking place in present day, to taking place in a fantasy world I created with vampires, mages, and werewolves as the main population, to what it is today.

    In the story, all the supernatural creatures from the Chronicles of Darkness have existed for the last 14,000 years. The vampires attempted to maintain the Masquerade, but around 2000 BCE, the Masquerade failed and the existence of vampires became public knowledge. This led to the other denizens of the CoD to reveal themselves, too. Eventually, the vampires, werewolves, mages, changelings, all of them began to take over human cities and governments, enslaving the humans and ruling the world. Now, I've not really read any of the other books, so I don't exactly know if this fits with what the werewolves, mages, changelings, and others would do, but that's not really important. All of these supernatural governments eventually went to war, a war which lasted 400 years. The war decimated the various populations, and seeing no end in sight, several of the most ancient and powerful of the different factions met together and decided the only way the war would end was if they were all destroyed, or separated from the rest of the population. This group, consisting of two members from each of the factions, created a spell. In an event that's now known as the Deluge, they pulled a Noah and took the most respected and powerful of each of their factions and relocated them to different parts of the world. For the vampires, they took the Princes of clans Daeva, Gangrel, Mekhet, Nosferatu, and Ventrue and brought them to what we know to be Russia, as well as each Prince's courts and several hundred humans. Then, the two Antediluvians (as they are known tonight) cast their spell, erecting impassable Gates around what would become known as the Darklands (essentially, Russia). These aren't, like, invisible gates or anything like that. There's no invisible game wall that you can't pass through because the developers didn't design the rest of the world. There are five Gates: the southwest Gate is a desert, southeast is a marsh, west is a mountain range, north is a snowy wasteland, and east is the sea. If one attempts to cross these Gates, no matter their power or disciplines or magic or anything, they will continue walking only to find themselves right back where they started. So if a Daeva attempted to go through the marshes, used all the means at their disposal to ensure they would only go south, they would still find themselves right back where they started. That was the first part of the spell cast by the Antediluvians. The second part of the spell destroyed anything that was not human or vampire within the gates (not including wildlife). That was 2018 years ago.

    Tonight, the Darklands are separated into five Domains, each ruled by a Prince. There's Prince Mina of Clan Daeva, who rules the southeast Domain called the Rose Quarter. It's a beautiful lush forest with roses galore. Of all the Princes, she is the youngest, although she is still ancient. She is 1,754 years old, and as of the start of the story she is in torpor. Prince Enaï of Clan Gangrel rules the southwest Domain, the Eternal Desert. He was one of the respected elders the Antediluvians brought to the new world, and is one of the few vampires to actually remember the old world. He is 2,397 years of age. The Eternal Desert, as I'm sure you can guess, is a vast desert region. Next there's Prince Yassbi of Clan Nosferatu, who rules the Domain called the Skulls. These are the mountains to the west. She was the original Nosferatu Prince brought to the new world by the Antediluvians, 3,092 years old. According to legend, the mountains are the skeletal remains of dragons, hence their name. Also, The Sword of Shannara is one of my favorite books of all time, and the Warlock Lord lived in a mountain that looked like a skull, so references. Then there's Prince Eyyeshyk of Clan Mekhet, ruling the northern snowy Domain called Ycodu. She was one of the humans who was brought into the new world, Embraced 2006 years ago. She has been in torpor for the past six years. And finally we have Prince Adu of Clan Ventrue, ruler of the Silver Coast. He is the oldest vampire around, having been ancient even before the Deluge, being 3,518 years old. Like Prince Yassbi, he was the original Ventrue Prince brought to the new world by the Antediluvians.

    As for the covenants, all five from Requiem are around, they just serve different purposes. The Invictus rule the domains, with the Lancea et Sanctum serving as the religious center for the Darklands. The Archbishops are second only to the Princes, and respected as much if not more than the Princes themselves. The Ordo Dracul are seen as historians and scientists, attempting to 'solve' the Kindred condition. The Circle of the Crone is a group of zealots that are outlawed, and the Carthians want to establish a democracy over the monarchical rule of the Invictus and are also outlawed.

    The story is one of fantasy. Technology is what you would expect in a Lord of the Rings or Song of Ice and Fire story. Humans are, for the most part, considered slaves and have next to no rights whatsoever. A few of the human families are more respected than others, and are bred for Embrace. The plot involves an ancient Nosferatu vampire using his powers to spread fear around the Darklands, sending vampires into frenzy and killing many before they come down. In the larger cities, a couple dozen vampires and a couple hundred humans are killed, but in the smaller towns, everyone dies but for the final vampire standing. Each of the Princes meet in the Dark Court, an old ruin in the center of the Darklands that only the Princes or their Speakers are allowed to enter. Prince Enaï, Prince Yassbi, Prince Adu, Speaker Qybe (a child vampire who is Speaker for the Silent for Prince Mina), and Speaker Odxekt (Speaker for Prince Eyyeshyk) discuss the threat, and each comes to a different conclusion. The Nosferatu don't know anything about what's going on and deny any involvement. The Gangrel don't believe this, and take the attacks as a sign of war. The Ventrue do not care if the Nosferatu are to blame or not, but they will not take an attack on their lands lying down. The Daeva do not believe the Nosferatu are to blame, and simply want to be left to their own devices. And the Mekhet do not know what to think, and deign to investigate further. Each Prince/Speaker returns to their capital, and this is where the story starts.

    Prince Yassbi brings together a coterie to find out what is really going on. She knows she did not order these attacks, and thinks there will be war soon. She knows the Gangrel and Ventrue are building their armies to march on the Skulls, and wants to coterie to stop this rogue vampire before that happens. She sends five newly created vampires, as well as her Hound Helbec, on the adventure. A few words about Helbec before I continue. He's a Mekhet vampire, and after I made him, I was like, "Wow. This guy's Batman." It was completely accidental, but he's basically Batman. Which kind of fits, as the bad guy is basically Scarecrow. Unintentional, but that's just how it happened.

    MAIN POINT
    This brings me to the assistance I would like from you guys. I've asked my friends for help, but as they don't know anything about Vampire, I thought it best to ask you guys. I have a few ideas as to the motivations for the Nosferatu bad guy, but I can't seem to decide. Any opinions or insight anyone can offer would be appreciated, as well as any notes, ideas, or tweaks to the story.

    So the Nosferatu (I'll just call him Scarecrow because I haven't really made him yet) could be one of five, maybe six things. One: He's just a regular, old Nosferatu vampire who gets his kicks off killing people and sending them into frenzy. Two: He's a draugr. Essentially the same as option one, but different only in that he has no humanity and is motivated by his primal need to feed and spread fear. Three: He's insane, with the Malkav disease thing. I'm a little hesitant about this option because I don't know much about the disease, or even if it IS a disease. I would have to read more about this, and if anyone could tell me which book in which it is discusses, I would be grateful. But with either of these first three options, I was thinking Scarecrow was sending people into frenzy, having them kill each other, and whoever is left standing he would diablerize. It would be how he chooses his victims, only taking the strongest. Now for the other three options. Four: He's one of the VII. He's causing the vampires to kill each other so as to rid the Darklands of vampires. Five: He's in Belial's Brood. He's spreading fear because he worships the demon of fear, and if he causes enough fear-based destruction he'd be able to summon the demon. Side note: Demons in this are the good old fashioned ones from Hell, not the God-Machine rejects. I have nothing against those demons, but I just don't think they'd really work in my fantasy setting. And finally, Six: He's possessed by a Strix. Heh, Strix for Six. That was also unintentional. Anyway, he's possessed by a Strix, and this is just something this particular Strix is into.

    I know this was a lot to read, and I hope you guys made it through. Thanks in advance for anyone taking the time to read all of this, and for any assistance, opinions, notes, critiques, or thoughts anyone offers.

      Alright, I'll admit I skimmed a bit on the backstory. Also, there are probably better Requiem experts out there. Having stated that disclaimer...

      Of the motivation options, I'd go with Malkavia Infection (it is in the Ventrue Clan book, 1E, I think). By having a mind bending illness, you can contrast the Before and After. You could also/instead have their actions be efforts towards seeking a cure for their condition - they induce a kind of Malkavia in subjects, then try to consume the strongest/most resistant, hoping to acquire their resistance and thus break free themselves.

      For the affiliation options, why not all 3 (and none)? They seek VII out for rumors of a cure they possess (using copy-cat crimes to draw their attention). The same time, they are bargaining with the Inferno via Belial's Brood. And a Strix has taken up the role as their "advisor" for its own purposes. The Scarecrow isn't actually part of any of them, and is just hoping to use them (and they use the Scarecrow in turn).

                              Alrighty, so story villains and their motives.

                              First off, we have Scarecrow. In the comics, he works on his fear toxin to spread fear in others because he himself cannot feel fear (except for when he faces Batman). In the story, he's spreading fear in others, causing them to frenzy, so he can find the strongest vampire and commit diablerie on them, as he thinks only with the strongest Vitae will he be able to cure himself of Malkav, which was given to him by the Joker character, who will serve as the background antagonist for the first three stories, and the main antagonist for the fourth. Vampire-Scarecrow will be a Nosferatu vampire with extraordinary Nightmare powers and devotions, and his face will be rotting and crawling with maggots.

                              Second, we have Poison Ivy. In the story, she's seeking to destroy the vampire cities to restore the country to its former natural beauty. She will have been in what is now the Darklands since before the Deluge, and will worship a nature goddess or see herself as one, I don't know yet. She will be Daeva, and... I don't know enough about the Circle of the Crone or whatever mandragora is, but if someone could point me in the right direction, I'll study it (I assume it has something to do with the Circle of the Crone, but that book doesn't have an index, and I don't recall reading the word when I looked through the book). Because as of now, I don't know what sort of powers she'd have that could make her control plants.

                              Third, we have Mister Freeze. Rather than using ice powers and trying to find diamonds to bring his wife back, he uses blood magic (I'm hoping there's more than one kind of blood magic, because I don't want both him and Poison Ivy to use Crúac) and is collecting Vitae for a ritual to bring a demon into the Darklands. That was the closest parallel I could come up with. ("Never end sentences with prepositions, Shawn!" "Not now, Gus!") I don't know what clan he'd be, but I'm feeling his personality would best fit with the Ventrue. Plus, he could 'freeze' people by dominating their minds.

                              Another character I'd use in the third story would be Two-Face. I like the Nosferatu using Obfuscate to appear as two different personas, but I'm thinking he just has one half of his body look disfigured, and he has Dissociative Identity Disorder to an astounding degree, switching from one persona to the other with... the flip of a coin? Hehe, puns. Two-Face might be a bad guy (as vampires go), but I'm thinking he'd help the characters out in some way. They happened to get the good guy persona. But that won't stop him from being a villain at some point in the future.

                              And finally, the Joker. A Malkavian Ventrue possessed by a Strix, skilled in Dementation. He employed Scarecrow to see if Nightmare + Dementation could be used to create some truly horrific (and, for him, fun) devotions, he set Poison Ivy loose on the Darklands by bonding her to his blood, all to cause a little chaos (this also makes her Malkavian), and he sent Mister Freeze to collect Vitae for him, but Freeze went rogue and sends Two-Face to come to the characters to deal with Freeze. His motives are simple: cause havoc in the Darklands, because that's what he's all about. And in the fourth story, the characters finally put it all together, that he was behind everything, and they set out to stop him. Maybe I'll add a Harley Quinn-esque character, I don't know.

That was the closest parallel I could come up with. ("Never end sentences with prepositions, Shawn!" "Not now, Gus!") I don't know what clan he'd be, but I'm feeling his personality would best fit with the Ventrue. Plus, he could 'freeze' people by dominating their minds.
                                              Something you could possibly go with is an old 1e bloodline called Architects of the Monolith. Firstly, they're Ventrue which fits nicely with the whole "excessive use of Dominate to 'freeze' people" angle. Secondly, and probably more importantly, they have a version of blood sorcery called Gilded Cage, so you wouldn't have to rely on Crúac. It's focused on manipulating the mystical geometries of cities so it could lend itself nicely to the theme of gates, summonings, and maybe just some good old-fashioned Lovecraftian impossible architecture if the need arises.

                                              There's probably a conversion in the homebrew hub, worth a look.
