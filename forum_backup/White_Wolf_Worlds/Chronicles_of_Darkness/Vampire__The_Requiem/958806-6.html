                                            Every table is unique. No game is monolothic and completely pure around a single theme. I don't see anyone arguing against that concept, MCN .

                                            Or describe a romantic comedy as a horror movie because there was a jump scare bit in it. Etc.

                                            Genres exist. They aren't binary, you're absolutely right. They get peppered in with other concepts (low fantasy, high fantasy), or merged (dark comedy, dramedy). Or specifically messed with to try and make something both sci-fi and fantasy (Spelljammer). Or actively a-genre and just a neutral system (GURPS core).

                                            Vampire is not a-genre. Vampire is a game of personal horror. Any table can sprinkle anything they want into any game they want. There's no Onyx Path police that are going to come and take away your books for 'playing wrong.'

                                            The frustration for me, and I suspect others, is that it can be difficult when forming a game - either in pick-up groups, or under inexperienced troupes, or less socially savvy groups - when people come in with wildly different expectations of what the core genre is. Not every table is lucky or experienced enough to be able to settle these differences with a social contract. And so when it seems a person is completely ignoring the personal horror aspect of Vampire, it can cause problems.

                                            It's totally okay to tell someone who shows up with Deadpool as a character concept to your Requiem game that their character may be better suited for Champions. And you can do it without making them feel like crap about it or that their preferences don't matter.

                                            I'll even agree that some people pull out the superhero comparison too quickly or on unrelated issues. That doesn't make the overall point wrong: Vampire has a particular problem with genre-shift away from what a lot of people consider personal horror toward what a lot of people consider superheroics.

                                            "I want to play a Blood and Smoke game without any personal horror," - is a valid feeling for anyone or any table, but I think it's pretty clearly veering off into different territories than the book.
