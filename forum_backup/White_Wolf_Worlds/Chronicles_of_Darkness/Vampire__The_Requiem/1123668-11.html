                Originally posted by impernious View Post

                I think you've hit a nail on a head here. This book is focused almost entirely on the experience of the half-damned characters. So their PoV colors how we talk about their relationships and how they feel about vampires.

                Our aim was to present these as playable characters, and so these are the character mindsets that we wrote in. I can understand the feeling that this departs from the norm, but part of the vampire's horror is how she ruins other people's lives by being too extra. So those people are bound to feel messy and complicated feelings about the vampire, and resentment is high up there.
                Yes, and that's a major part of the tonal complaints.

                So far I've withheld feedback on the Ghouls section, but it feels more like 1e Ghouls because 1e Ghouls was written from their perspective too, and portraying power dynamics from the bottom up tends to lead our modern selves to view that as inherently abusive. I'd have preferred to see more nuanced takes that would help a player portray relationships that aid a healthy gaming table, for example: how do ghouls rationalize their relationship to a "wrong" creature? How do they rationalize the devil's bargain they took and can't get out of? How do they consider themselves and maintain their self-esteem and integrity (even, humanity). How do they husband and manage their little rebellions of attitude that tell them they're a person? Because--trust me on this one--even in a gaslit relationship of mingled genuine love and abuse, there's pure awareness of what's happening.

                Honestly, a person suffering from a "[sense] of self-worth ... totally obliterated" (p.94) and suffering from PTSD (which would be handy in context to detail the symptoms of, btw) as well as other mental issues--well that person is a particularly poor servant and really only useful as a display piece for the low cunning it takes to break a human. (This bit is from the Invictus "bad guys" section, but it stand out as a strong stroke of color both because it brings out the thread in other sections and conforms to "expected norms" of bad behavior by vamps.)

                Vampires invest their most precious resources into ghouls, and while some monsters would do so just to have a convenient dog to kick, you can get that without spending a willpower point to make an actual ghoul, and you can get someone slavishly devoted by chaining them up and spending three Vitae over three nights. Refresh yearly. A ghoul: made and maintained at least monthly with precious, precious vitae, is--and should be--reserved for something more, or it comes back to haunt the vamp in how their society views them. We rightfully judge our neighbors based on how they treat their animals, vamps would do the same.

                How do vampires interact with ghouls in such a way as to tend to their dog and avoid making it into a savage? Honestly, imagining abuse is pretty easy after VtM and VtR 1e books--focusing on something else is handy for players, storytellers and gaming tables. Focusing on, say, the value that ghouls represent, and how that helps drive the All-Night Society?

                Now, the Ghouls section delivers some of this. I'm re-skimming it while typing to minimize bias, and there are useful and thoughtful sections. The Covenant takes on ghouls is especially good (Invictus aside).

                Other than the disappointing tone, however, the ghoul section in particular falls flat for me once we reach the families part: some text is contradictory with preceding sections, and there is a broad lack of mechanics. Honestly? Ghoul families and unique ghoul twists are what the book has to offer in terms of new playable ideas. There is precious little to hang a character on, to instruct a Storyteller on what is expected. Family ghouls live longer (p. 104, sidebar), they get more "touchstones" (p. 105 sidebar, irrelevant as ghouls use Integrity), and a family benefit and drawback (p.105 same sidebar, "Merits, bonuses to Skill Specialties, or access to a certain Discipline" versus "derangements or physical flaws"). That's it. The example shows how "Emma" sets up the background for her ghoul family, and then there is nothing about on the selection of their characteristics--the most contentious part of a Storyteller's arbitration.

                Then we have 2.5 pages on the Crosses, and a sidebar giving an example of their merits and drawbacks. A lot of text, but the mechanics example is useful. Another 2.5 pages for the de Portocarreros; there is no mechanics sidebar at all. About 3 pages of text on the Golds; again, there is no mechanics block--wait, they do not suffer from addiction to Vitae? Or is it to the Vinculum? These are two different things! It sure would be nice to know how this works mechanically and they seem to have no drawback. Side note: 8-again on Occult for obscure sub-sects of a major religion != immunity to full Vinculum effects (I thought there was a merit that reduced Vinculum to a max of Stage 2, but cannot immediately find it). One of these things is not like the other--how do we benchmark a family mechanically then?

                So... we get maybe 1.5 examples of ghoul family mechanics. What about the rumor that in Japan are ghoul families who retain access to physical disciplines without Vitae? What about other mystical variations? Is there anything standard about a family, or just ST homebrew it? Oh what's this in Antagonists: "Many Alley Men are familial ghouls, and therefore do not suffer the pull of hunger other ghouls possess" (p. 121)--sounds awesome, are these Alley Men all Golds, then? Is this their benefit? Nothing is mentioned under systems.... is there something consistent that wasn't shared before?

                Ghoulish aberrations is better organized, with a clear "systems" tag and short text for each, and the mandragora creatures are bonus, but in several cases the flavor text is not satisfied by the systems provided. There is no provided example of "ghouls who don't need regents" as one example, and many more within the Gorged text. Conversely, the systems text for Phagians gives a useful mechanic for feeding from ghouls and when you get blood versus Vitae out of them--this SHOULD be lifted into earlier text as it's... kinda important in their interactions with vamps.

                ...

                This is not intended to be a full review of the section, but to illustrate the two main problems: tone and lack of mechanics. If you're not super-interested in using the backstory of the ghoul families, into mandragora, or like the couple special variations of ghouls, there's not much here you won't find in the main book or, especially, the 1e Ghouls book at much greater length. 2e uses its own mechanics and has its own themes--having that shine through would have helped.

                --Khanwulf
