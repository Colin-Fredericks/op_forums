    I don't know about interruption being used to make people miss so easily. It's up to the GM I guess, but I wouldn't say using your interrupt action to move out of the way would automatically make the opponent miss. The opponent can move too. If you take the power as a miss if you blink interruption, then yes, it would work, but I don't think it's a very interesting or creative. I would allow moving out of the way if the character uses another vitae point to boost their speed so they move too far for the opponent to reach them and attack them for their action.

    When the book says that if the character does something that makes the opponent's action impossible, then the opponent loses their turn, I'm more thinking along the lines of grappling (preventing movement, or at least quick movement), disarming, grabbing an object before the opponent, that kind of thing. The hit and run tactic as permitted with that interpretation of celerity doesn't make for a very interesting or dramatic fight. Ok, actually, I would allow it, but only as long as the opponent doesn't understand what's going on.


    I'm having another thought, now though, trying to picture the fight mechanically. So there's A and B. B has celerity. Maybe it's not that easy to do unless you're facing a wild boar.

    Round 1 :
    A and B are in melee range and are using brawl/weaponry.
    A attacks B
    B interrupts, hits and moves away.

    Round 2 :
    A and B are not in melee range.

    What now ?
    A can move towards B and attack.
    B will interrupt to replicate round 1. What happens then ? For it to work, that means B has to interrupt A between the move action and the attack action. Thematically, there's no problem with that, but mechanically, that means B isn't interrupting A's turn before it happens but in the middle of it happening. If B can only interrupt B's entire turn, then they have to get close before attacking, which will put them in A's range. Unless you consider that B moves basically behind A, attacks, and then B runs past A and attacks the spot B occupied at the start of round 2, putting them again out of melee range.

    Or else :
    A wises up and doesn't pursue, and instead waits for B to move towards them and attack so they can strike back. B can't get in, attack AND and get out in the same action. If B wants to attack, they must get in A's range and stay there. The interruption doesn't really help much there.

                        You activate the Interrupt after someone announces their action. After you complete your Interrupt, they have to continue with the action they announced. If their action is no longer valid, it fails. So yes, if you Interrupt to move out of melee range after they declare a melee attack, they cannot change their mind and move before their attack. They already declared the attack. The attack fails because you are now out of range.

                        I'm not sure if I'd allow that to block Ranged attacks unless you move out of line of sight. Or you could move behind cover to give them a penalty. But as long as you're still within range, I'd still let them roll it after being interrupted if it's ranged.
