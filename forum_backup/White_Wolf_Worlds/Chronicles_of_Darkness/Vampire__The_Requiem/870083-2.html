                                            NOTE: I wrote this for another similar thread, I figured it would make sense to post it here, too. If you see some references to what others have said, it might be from the other thread.

                                            Hi, everybody,

                                            I am Muslim and I bumped into this thread as I was planning to narrate a chronicle set in Cordoba in the year 1000 CE, so culturally-Muslim vampires will feature heavily.

                                            Note: I only became aware of the Banu Shaytan and Iblic Creed stuff reading your posts, and I am going to check them out immediately. Thank you.

                                            First, to be clear: Islam's abrogations of previous revelations does not mean they stop being true and of divine origin. It simply means that the norms they set are no more binding and that, if they say something explicitly denied by the Quran (such as Jesus - peace be upon him - being the Son of God), that is to be considered a human addition. Beyond this, Muslims throughout history, even pious ones reputed to be saints/walis, have had all sorts of attitudes to previous religions. Also, there are some Muslims who do not believe in abrogation and see previous religions as still valid, though they are few and criticized for this. They exist, nonetheless, so Muslim-cultured Kindred might well claim similar ideas.

                                            My personal take on the Lancea et Sanctum as seen by vampires in Muslim lands is this: when Islam appeared, the Byzantine Empire was mostly Sanctified (i.e.: LeS), Arabia was Circle of the Crone and Persia (Zoroastrianism) was CotC or something else (still need to figure it out, since Persian stuff does not feature in my Cordoba chronicle).
                                            Early human converts to Islam, once embraced, would not find Muslim guides in their vampirism. The closest thing they would find were Sanctified teachers. Longinism would make more sense to them than adapting Islam to vampirism because:
                                            1) Islam's religious precepts are evidently not appliable to vampires;
                                            2) only God can issue religious norms, so vampires can't just take the five pillars, dietary restrictions and other rules and adapt them to vampires (like five prayers at night, don't drink blood of drunkards or pig-eaters, starve to torpor/frenzy in Ramadan, etc), as one would need divine authority for that. Regarding the prayers, I prefer not making them perform any of the five daily prayers, not even the night ones, on the basis that, drinking human blood and being more or less walking corpses, they would be considered ritually impure in various schools of Islamic law, thus being forbidden from performing the five obligatory prayers (although they can perform other forms of prayer that do not require ritual purity, such as dhikr - the remembrance of God - or dua - supplication and personal conversation with God);
                                            3) Muslims acknowledge previous prophets, including Jesus (peace be upon him), so the tale of Longinus receiving guidance can work. Theologically, he may be classed as a wali (saint) from the community of Jesus, as opposed to a prophet in the strict Islamic theological sense, and this would make things easier;
                                            4) surah al-Jinn (72nd surah of the Quran) says some Jinn were listening to the Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) once while he was reciting the Quran and recognised its divine origin. In general, Jinn are believed to be of different kinds, to descend from Iblis/Lucifer/the Devil/Shaytan/Satan, to be responsible for part of polytheism (as jinns made humans worship them as gods), to be able to mate with humans and have offspring from them at times, to have free will, to follow different religions - the same humans follow, since, as far as I learnt, they do not get their own prophets but are guided by human prophets and saints, hence the idea of Longinus being a wali/saint instead of a vampire prophet. Muslim-cultured vampires may see themselves as a sub-species of Jinn (the Strix may well be another sub-species), especially if this is how Arabian pre-Islamic vampires saw themselves. Whichever vampire claimed to have interacted with the Prophet (peace be upon him) or his Companions and received instructions from them may have simply said that the main order was to carry on with Longinism, just knowing that Jesus is not God or His Son, etc., but without prescribed rules that differ from Longinism;
                                            5) the Quran and the Hadith do not mention (to my knowledge) what rules Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him) set for the Jinn, although it's generally understood that they did receive rules from him and previous prophets (peace be upon them all), so whatever rules Longinism imposes on vampires, that can be claimed as the rules set for them;
                                            6) Longinism does not see Longinus as God, but only as a kind of prophet. This does not overtly contrast the Islamic creed, although it would make it slightly in theological debates easier to see Longinus as a wali/saint or sort of mystical disciple of Jesus. As a wali, he could still receive visions and inspirations, though there are some slight theological differences between walis and prophets.
                                            7) there are various interpretations of the verses of the Quran about the crucifixion. It's enough to say that Longinian "Muslim" vampires stick to the view that Jesus (peace be upon him) was put on the cross, pierced by Longinus, and then taken up to heaven (before dying).

                                            In my setting, a Lancea et Sanctum Council was called in Damascus in 800 CE and established that the Church of Longinus was independent of the developments of human religions and its members were free to believe that Muhammad (peace be upon him) was a prophet or not, settling the issue for most adherents (obviously there can always be minor conflicts and divisions here and there, but they don't entail the forming of a wholly different Covenant). Moreover, vampires were not meant to push humans to one or the other Abrahamic religion, as this could effectively harm the appeal of that religion, but only remind humans of God, making them fear Him, tempt them to test them, and fight the Circle of the Crone and any vampire who promoted polytheism or atheism.

                                            I assume that there are vampires who carry on their mortal religion (whether Islam or Christianity or Judaism or other), but these usually act alone and have not formed proper Covenants that can rival with the LeS. It is quite common for new vampires, too, to hang onto one's mortal religion for a while, but this is usually temporary. One exception that I plan to include in the Chronicle is a group of vampire vampire-hunters called "at-Tawwabun" (the Repentant), who believe the only way to be forgiven by God is by killing other vampires and feeding only from animals forbidden to Muslims (ex.: pigs and predatory animals). This would be an antagonist covenant, however, not open to players. I have more interesting ideas about them, but I'll share them in time, God willing.

                                            In my Cordoba, the Bishop/Great Imam of the Lancea et Sanctum/ar-Rumh wal-Quds (I use double names to reflect the double culture of the vampires of Muslim Spain) has imposed on the city's vampire to feed only from sinful humans and not to kill the kine, unless they were condemned to death by the human authorities. In addition to this, Longinians are encouraged to shepherd the flock they belonged to in life (vampires who were Muslim prey on Muslims and vampires who were Christian prey on Christians), as they are most likely to understand how to remind their victims of God, but this is only an encouragement.

                                            I hope all the above makes sense. I am going to read the material about the Banu Shaytan and see how that is, God willing.

                                                    I ask this that in the Second Editions and the printed material that followed the Covenants are purposely left vague except the basic tenets.
                                                    I tend to think in terms of the first edition, given that the second one is still developing (in terms of more elaborated background and details for clans, covenants and bloodlines. That being said, it's up to the storyteller whether to use the published Testament of Longinus or something else of his own device but with the same name. I read parts of the published Testament of Longinus after inventing this version of the Covenant and realised that some passages of the Testament of Longinus would be problematic (supporting clearly Catholic doctrines in contrast with Islam), but one way I dealt with it is that "Muslim" LeS tend to see those as the point of view of either Longinus or the Monachus, conditioned by the ideas of the time, but not actually part of the revelation. Generally speaking, they regard the Testament of Longinus as revealed in its general message, but not in its specific wording, and Longinus, not being fully a prophet, was not immune from error. Also, the Testament of Longinus, like the Torah and the Gospel, is considered corruptible, unlike the text of the Quran. So, basically, anything in the Testament of Longinus that doesn't fit with "Islam" (as a LeS "Muslim" vampire would take it) is considered a human addition or error, even though the text in itself keeps being followed and revered.
