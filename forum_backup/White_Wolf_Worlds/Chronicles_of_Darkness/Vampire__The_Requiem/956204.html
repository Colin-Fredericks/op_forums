    Help for a new Storyteller

    08-23-2016, 04:36 AM
    Hi everyone! I'm a new storyteller, and I am preparing for my first chronicle in VtR. I have decided to use the Criminal Intent Storytelling Adventure System module to start. What I'd really like is advice and tips on creating an immersive and memorable gaming experience.

    Here's what I've got so far

    World of Darkness (hardcopy + pdf)
    Vampire: The Requiem (hardcopy + pdf)
    Criminal Intent (printed and bound)
    Ready-made Characters (pdf)
    Character Sheets
    Condition cards (printed)
    Storyteller's screen (printed and laminated)
    Gothic Pendants (to signify membership in the coterie)
    A selection of gothic ball masks (for the discerning vampiress)
    A custom-made Ankh wax seal (letters from the Prince)
    and, of course, a bunch of plastic fangs.

    So far, my players have received a letter from the Prince (really just an in-theme invitation to my game).


    Can anyone recommend some good music that I can loop in the background for the various scenes in Criminal Intent? Or for other WoD scenes, more generally?

    Are there any other props or materials that would help the story and characters?

    What tips and tricks would you give to an aspiring Storyteller?

    Many thanks

          EDIT
          For CofD specifically, when my friend wanted to start GMing, I gave her an assignment - every day, write down two conditions based on something that caught your eye or thoughts during the day. This helped her IMMENSELLY grok the Condition System, and between one game and another, she went from "how do I do this help" to "I can conjure out an interesting Condition in an instant". If you have access to Mage 2nd Edition corebook, there is a really good section there for writing up custom conditions; if you don't, well, I don't know how "OK" is posting it here, but probably someone can help you.

              My advice? "No plan survives contact with the enemy." Well, you shouldn't consider the players to be enemies, but you should expect them to completely derail any plot you have planned. So, instead of having lines planned out, focus on understanding each character, their motives and drives. Be flexible and respond to what the players want to do with the game, don't railroad. Vampire is all about the individual drives and needs of each vampire, and how they merge and clash in the Danse Macbre.

              Other sound advice - don't let players try to talk you into getting stuff for free. If they bloodbond a chief of police, they still need to buy up Retainer, Allies, or whatever to get long term use out of him. They dont' get a xp-free pet.

              If you're using 2e rules, keep an eye on player Aspirations. Those are huge hints on what kind of game they want to play.

                                      Originally posted by MCN View Post
                                      If you're using 2e rules, keep an eye on player Aspirations. Those are huge hints on what kind of game they want to play.
                                      This is the best advice.

                                      I'm running my very first campaign as a DM/ST, with a group of complete RPG newbies and to start with I let them run wild with their characters (within some limits). So one player created a 400yr old wrestler, and even though that didn't work with my existing campaign we worked it in. because it's what the player wanted. If the player's don't have a stake in your world, the game won't work.

                                      So set some boundaries and let them go wild inside that. Clans & bloodlines are great for giving player's jumping off points. I got all the clans & many bloodlines printed onto little cards that the player's could browse and that really helped.

                                      I've also taken to introducing concepts piece by piece. So I've had a 'setting the horror theme' session where I put the players in a car crash and saw how they reacted. Then a 'meet the city npcs' session for world building, and only now we are into the 'further your goals, combat, & social manoeuvring' sessions.

                                      One mistake I made was to leave out the Mask/Dirge stuff at the start as I was worried about overwhelming the players. But thinking back Masks, Dirges, and Humanity are really important and my players are now very deep into their Requiems and ignoring their mortal links. Something to bear in mind. But I still recommend the little-by-little approach for new players.
