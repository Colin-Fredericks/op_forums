    World Convenants?

    08-18-2015, 09:57 AM
    Has anyone ever considered the possibility of covenants originated from around the world rather than the usual five?

    For example, covenants originated from East and Southern Asia that are Taoist, Shinto, Hindu, Buddhist, Jainist, Sikh, based?

    Also what about Convenants originated from south western asia that are based on Judahism, Islam, Mesopotamian religions, etc?

    Also what about Convenants originated from Africa such as based on Egyptian Religion in East Africa and such?

    Well lastly what about Pre-Columbian Kindred who have covenants based on Mesoamerican and Pre-Columbian North American religions as well?

    Or hell what about Covenants originated from Northern Western Asia (Europe) that are Ancient Greek, Norse, Slavic, Celtic, Basque, Germanic, Etruscan, etc based?

                      Its not only a good idea, its assumed by the setting that such things exist.

                      But I would refrain from simply making cultural/religious Covenants. That's both a limiting view on the concept of Covenant and on the very cultures you will base them.

                      Think about what people in a given region will find relevant to work for. A person in Egypt won't look for a Covenant based on being Egyptian. Its kind of expected that almost everyone will be Egyptian in Egypt, anyway, that isn't relevant. But there you have some sort of conflict between Egyptian Revivalists and Orthodox Muslims. So a Covenant based on cultural revivalism without the religious side would be found there, and another similar to the Lancea, but following Islam.

                      Regional Covenants should focus on regional politics, not in being just regional. That's, though, IS a thing among IMMIGRANTS. On Texas you could have a Covenant based on Mexico and being Mexican, or generally being Latin-american, because it is a thing there. On New York City you could have a bunch of minor Covenants based on diverse cultures, like Irish and Chinese, and as time passed some of those coalesced into two or three somewhat conflicting Covenants, or just one Covenant based on being an Immigrant.

                      Lots of possibilities are given. In the end, the Covenants present in a given city should work the themes and conflicts of the city. And sure, some of them can be very distinct of the most known Covenants, and even be based on specific cultures. If for some reason half a dozen of the Egyptian Orthodox Muslim Vampires migrate to, lets say, Vancouver, so you would have their Covenant in Vancouver. And though they define themselves by being Muslims, the Covenant would be strongly modern-Egyptian "themed" simply because it was founded and is mainly composed by Egyptian vampires.

                              Here's what I've picked/speculated from the second edition:

                              Eastern Asia: Blood and Smoke mentions some in Beijing and Tokyo. Requiem for Regina surprisingly put Invictus in the Far East, while Blood and Smoke referred to Ventrue empires with little more detail.

                              Pre-Columbian America: The Mission in San Francisco is Native American in all but name and trappings. That's not to mention Shadows of Mexico, although that's described as one hoary side of the Circle of the Crone there, albeit one which had as much in common with Invictus in its heyday.

                              Europe (but mostly the British Isles): There are two ancient cults in Athens that correspond to the Lancea et Sanctum and the Circle. The former is recognises Apollo as well as the Christian god by the looks of it, but they don't accept the Lancea et Sanctum proper. The latter might not have actually contacted the Circle of the Crone as a movement before. As well as the presumably extinct Gallows Post and children's crusade, Requiem for Regina briefly describes:
                              *the Weihan Cynn - in spite of their Anglo-Saxon name (which was used in London, but not Edinburgh where it remained stronger), they were in Britain when the Roman came. They make deals with various other supernaturals.
                              *the Qalandariyyah - an originally Muslim sect from Andalusia that operates within the above.
                              *the Fuil-Beannaithe - An Irish cult that reveres the Virgin Mary and supports Catholicism within Britain, but also incorporates beliefs from the New World.

                              Those may be extinct or drawn into the Circle of Crone now, but they were around as late as the early 18th century. I'm guessing that the Lancea et Sanctum managed to almost wipe them out in the late 18th/early 19th century before a massive counter attack. I would imagine that the Circle of the Crone has soaked up a lot of Western pagan traditions for the sake of solidarity (even ones that don't recognise any crone figure or value tribulation or Cruac), but is near unheard of in places where the Lancea et Sanctum was never strong enough to persecute other cults.

                                                          In theory, the book says there are other covenants out there, across the world, that are different from the core seven (including Brood/VII). They're each unique. Even in the US, we have several that are different.

                                                          In practice? I rarely see a new covenant that's not just tweaking the history and a few of the practices of the core seven. Call the Buddhist organization that learns Treasures a different covenant all you want, but in execution, the mechanics and themes are still very close to the Ordo Dracul. Vodoun religion in Ancient Bloodlines has much in common with the other religious covenant. Same with the new covenants in Danse Macabre - each could easily fit as a variant of the established groups. The four Zaibatsu in Tokyo might as well just be labeled as localized Carthians, Invictus, Circle, and VII with slightly different mechanics.

                                                          Politically, culturally and historically, these various covenants are not the same. Thematically and mechanically, we're so close we might as well be the same for others. Unlike Mage, Vampire is a tier 2 game, which means that we don't have global organizations, but the covenant archetypes remain pretty much the same. So, if we look at a game in southern Africa, I'd expect to see the Lancea reformed into an extreme Muslim-based group with similar mechanics, a CotC equivalent that is based on Yoruba and other local religions, likely an Invictus epoxy that's left behind as a result of colonialism, etc.
