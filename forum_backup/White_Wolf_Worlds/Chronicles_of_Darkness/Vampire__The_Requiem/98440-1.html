                        Griautis is correct - you can have two different Daeva bloodlines who pick up Dominate, for example. How they relate to that Discipline should be different in some fashion, presumably connected to their culture/background.

                        I quibble with his 'guidelines where none are needed' bit. While yes, the standard game for B&S is supposed to be a small table of adult players who trust each other, that's not necessarily the reality for everyone. Putting aside dysfunctional tables, when you have larger scale convention, LARP or online games, some balancing mechanism is useful to encourage diversity. Drama aside, some bloodlines are objectively worse than other ones.

                        I think as a substitute for "just work out balance / theme concerns with your table," your idea has merit as a balancing system for new lines. Unfortunately, I think it would be a lot of work to try and go back and shoehorn older and the more recently blog'd bloodlines into that paradigm. Since Requiem is meant as a toolbox, we're getting cool and interesting tools that people are free to use or not, but some tools are going to be better than others in both a general way, and are going to be better in different styles of specific campaigns.

                        I've seen more success in an overlay system of balancing Bloodlines. Where there is just a value judgment assigned by the Storyteller on each special snowflake item (Bloodlines, alternate Sorceries, Crazy Devotions, Hollow Mekhet, etc). And each player gets a limited number of those. Whether they come across as Merit Dots or Rarity Slots, you balance it with the overlay rather than trying to tune each thing individually. Ends up meaning if you want some crazy Bhumisparsa defense bonus that's going to be very powerful in your campaign, it will eat up most of your points. If you just want a Mekhet bloodline with Resilience In-Clan, that's a lot cheaper.

                                    Originally posted by zenvious View Post
                                    Its only my opinion (thus open to conflict, and the designers will always get the final word) but in my opinion Bloodlines should come in three levels depending on what the 4th discipline gained is, since when it boils down to it you are gaining an extra Gift at the cost of gaining another Weakness. From that theme and culture should be built and expanded as this would do two things:
                                    1) Create some degree of mechanical balance between all bloodlines (greater power comes with greater cost - making every bloodline worth considering)
                                    2) Theme is present and reflective of the gifts, instead of theme being created a gift jammed in just for the sake of having it

                                    From there the three tiers should then be as follows:

                                    - Tier 1 gives one of the five common disciplines (Animalisim, Obfuscate, Vigor, Celerity, Resilience). Since any Kindred can learn these without a teacher, they are a minor boon at best but can encourage some unique devotions from the combinations. As such the weakness from these Tier's would be on the equivliant of a minor Bane - meaning having this Bloodline would result in the Kindred technically having a max of 4 Banes (3 as per normal max, plus the 4th from their Bloodline). Due to this design there will only be a total of 15 of these Bloodlines since each Clan already has x2 of these common disciplines.

                                    - Tier 2 gives one of the five clan disciplines (Auspex, Dominate, Majesty, Nightmare, Protean) - a discipline and Kindred can learn provided they drink the blood of that clan member and be taught how to use the skill (coming with the penalties that follow in doing so). Having this discipline means you can learn it without risk of vinaculum, leading to some very powerful devotions. As such, these Bloodlines should usually gain a weakness akin to almost a Clan weakness - indeed a simple version of creating these Bloodlines would be to have the Clan weakness associated with the 4th discipline (e.g.: a Bloodline with Auspex + Dominate would have the Clan Weakness's of a Ventrue and Mekhet)

                                    - Tier 3 offers a completely new and unique discipline - one that none outside the Bloodline can learn, and likely producing effects that rival the five clan disciplines (and sometimes even surpassing it). For this great power should come great cost - the weakness's of these Bloodlines should often be to a Clan weakness, what a Clan weakness is to a minor Bane. Such is the cost for power. Naturally this may discourage Kindred from using it, but then the temptation of power can be hard to resist.

                                    Thats just my take on it anyway. I'm sure I'm probably the minor in thinking this design is functional.

                                    I love this as a design guide!
