    Just as a reminder, I did end my post with "Just my take on it." I'm not expecting people to agree with any part of what I said - I know that the vast majority won't. Nor did I say that what I was saying was canon.

    I also need to apologise, because it seems I failed to make clear that I was trying to show how - in my perspective - the Strix would have made sense. I also seemed to have failed to make clear that there is no certain claim (in my scenario) that the Ventrue and the Julii are the same clan. But then... where did the Ventrue come from? And they clearly came after the Roman period, because there is no record of them in the year 543 AC/CE, because they are not in the Byzantium appendix in Fall of the Camarilla.

    More importantly, this is no longer on the topic that this thread was started under... is there another thread all of this could be discussed in? I don't want to be the instigator of a thread derailment... I am putting up this reply, and am leaving it at that, intending to stick to the Khaibit and Obtenebration after this point. Any further discussion - in the right thread - I would be happy to continue with. Or by PM.

    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Their relationship with vampires is a bit more complicated than that. They don't "want to be" vampires, they believe that vampires are just like them, but with physical bodies of their own. And yet, the vampires deny this fact. Instead of indulging in all the debaucheries afforded by physical form, they just act as much like regular humans as possible. That's why the Strix hate them, for being a waste of potential. They're The Joker, they want to prove that deep down everyone (or every vampire, anyway) is just one bad day away from being just as monstrous as they are.
    Right... if vampires are just like them, then it lends very strongly to the notion that all clans originate in some form from the Strix. They can merge with a body, or a vampire, and take control. They also in the process become a vampire themselves, and one of the five clans. If they were becoming Clanless, or part of some Strix-orientated clan, I could understand... but it seems rather confused, to me. But if everyone else sees sense in it, that's fine. I'm not trying to dictate what is - or is not - canon here. In any case, I ended up waiting for this to come out, because it was a month or so behind schedule. So I ended up creating my own version of the Strix, and they are nothing like what Onyx Path have created. So this is all an academic exercise for me, because the setting I'm still building up is very different to what was originally presented by OPP. Again, if everyone else loves the Strix, think they are great, I'm happy for them. I don't like them, and have something that works, which was an evolution on the original snippets and tidbits I could get hold of.

    Have you read the Clan entries in 2e? They pretty much straight up confirm that not only do the clans have different origins, but there are most likely multiple origins within each individual clan.
    Just re-read them. It's been a while. Don't get me started on Akhenaten... that one is a load of rubbish. Interesting how they keep on using fairy tales to explain the origins, at least possible interpretations and possibilities. Point of interest, one of the Ventrue origins state they are the last scions of the Camarilla... page twenty seven.

    Why the Ventrue? The Ventrue are not the Julii, the Strix have no special connection to them.
    Can you prove this? Can you hold this as absolute fact? That the Strix have no special connection to the Ventrue? As already mentioned, the Ventrue are the youngest Clan, and a source of mystery on that front. Who is to say that the Strix didn't try their experiment again?

    In any case, I said:

    Are they really the creators, or just saying it for their own goals? And if they are lying... what are their reasons for doing so? If they are telling the truth, why emerge now? And what are they wanting from the Ventrue... and can they afford to refuse any demands from them?
    I never said that the Strix - in my scenario - were connected with the creation. I said that the Strix claimed that they were. Then went into possibilities to be being true, and false. My scenarion is not canon, and I claim not that it is.

    And what do we have with the Strix and the Julii? An origin myth that suggests that the Strix turned a Revenant into a full vampire. Again, look at the Ventrue, and then the Julii. If they are not the same, then why are they so startlingly similar? The Discipline spread, clan weakness... It has always been hinted and teased at that the two might well be intertwined, if not an evolution of the clan... And we are again at the question: Where did the Ventrue come from? But, again, this is going beyond the scope of the thread, which is meant to be about the Khaibit and Obtenebration. I have no issue with discussing it further, but... I again ask, is there another thread this can be transferred to?

    Apart from the Ventrue-specific part, this is exactly how the Strix are presented in 2e. Some of them claim to be the origin of all vampires. Some only claim to be the origin (and end) of the Julii. Some make no such claims whatsoever. Why the lies? Why the contradictions? What do they want? Why are they disturbingly vampire-like in some ways and completely different in others?
    Um... because Rose Bailey and the development team wanted it that way? Seriously, you seem to not understand that the intention is to make us come up with answers, and to then have the adventures we want to have. The thing is, we can make the Strix into whatever we want. Do they need to just harass vampires? Why not werewolves? What if they suddenly turn up, and claim they are the source of their creation, not Father Wolf? Not saying it should happen, but someone out there will likely have come up with that idea, and run it - and the group enjoyed the plot twists it presented.

    I did buy and read this back when it was Blood and Smoke, for the record. And I went through the Strix chapter carefully, because I had no choice. I had been playtesting a version of the nWoD, and was seeing if what I had put together worked. In any case, people had seen my development notes for playable characters (they had asked to understand the characters better), and had seen a bit about the Strix in one of them. About how they were using a girl to hunt vampires, making her do it. She was gaining power and protection whilst doing it, if I recall correctly. I got questions about all of that, and I had to admit it had been an idea I had put in there, but had forgotten about. I had said that I wasn't going that direction any more, and I got asked why, and in the end I had to have them. They proved popular, and something of a hit. I had turned that original adventure into a novel by that time, so I added them in, to explore them. I still am, building up the game universe by writing stories. And they appear, in the background... and I still have no definitive answer to them. But what I ended up with is cool and brilliant, and something I know will be a hit when I get to start running the setting.

    And I think that is what it is all about, to be fair. We can split hairs about the little details, but we all need to follow the directions we feel compelled to, and if we have fun in the process, then we are doing things right.

    And I still think the Khaibit are cool.

                    Assumes a monolithic vision of what the Strix are that doesn't scan. "Strix" is like "Kindred" — it's not a term for only one kind of being, and the Strix are as mutable as Blood-based vampires (if not moreso) besides.

                    The Jiang Shi of the Triangle have been getting more like Kindred vampires across the generations — what does that say about the strain of vampirism they represent and its relation to humanity?

                    It could be that early Nosferatu rose from improper burials because the Strix were attracted by the inauspiciousness of a soul not put to rest, but it could also be that some squirming thing from within the earth seized an opportunity instead. It could be that the Daeva were raised from the ground by the passions of the dead for the living, or maybe some Owls puked blood down their gullets.

                    The thing about vampires — of every stripe — is that they lie all the time.

                                    OH, okay Barbe, I think I get what you're coming from.

                                    On Clan origins, it's important to look at the origins of the Clans as basically being irrelevant. Requiem isn't overly concerned with progenitors and a genealogy of the Kindred.

                                    Pretty much all the history that Requiem 2e seems concerned with can be found in a few key places, namely Requiem for Rome and Dark Eras: Requiem for Regina. Those give you the history that's most definitely "still canon."

                                    If you want more history that may or may not still be canon then you should look at Ancient Mysteries, which has several time periods and different locations.

                                    For the Khaibit, you want to look at Bloodlines: The Hidden. The trick of reading their write up is to keep in mind that the blog post supercedes that writeup. If you read it though, you can really clearly see where they cleaned it up and focused it.

                                    For most Second Edition stuff, they don't reference things from 1e without sort of re-explaining them in the context of the new edition. I think Dark Eras is the only exception sort of, because it references some material from the first edition Lancea Sanctum book?
