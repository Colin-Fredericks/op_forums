                    The Camarilla and Invictus are pretty much pallete-swaps of each other, both meritocracies were the merit is age, though that does work slightly less well in Requiem, what with the way Blood Potency works. Still, the Invictus and Camarilla are both stultifying pyramid-scheme power structures with little room for advancement for the neonate.

                    The Sabbat from Masquerade was intended by a pure villain faction, until people decided they liked them better than the Camarilla, they underwent serious villain decay, and became a playable faction. This really damaged some of the core concepts of the Sabbat, notably not giving a crap about the Masquerade became a lip-service philosophy, with them still hiding from mortals most of the time, and in the end pretty much made the Sabbat just the Camarilla with a different set of titles and a slightly less draconian policy on diablerie. The Sabbat have kind of been split in three, depending on how you want to look at it: Part of them is the Lancea Sanctum, with the religious overtones (also toned down a lot as the Sabbat underwent villain decay), part of them is the Carthian Movment, and the implacable, unknowable villain faction is VII.

                    The Anarchs become the other half of the Carthian Movement, the ones who want change, violently if necessary (sometimes if not).

                    The Inconnu are gone, and I rather say good riddance. They were pretty much just mysterious for mysterious sake. Joining the Inconnu was pretty much just another nonstandard game over, as your character retreats to whatever mystic temple they're holing themselves up in and spends the rest of eternity contemplating their navel.

                    As for the Requiem Covenants, while a number of them borrow themes from some of the earlier Sects, they're still very much their own animals. The Invictus are the closest to a direct import, being more or less Requiem's Camarilla (though the Camarilla did exist in Requiem, in Ancient Rome, and it didn't fare well). The Carthians are kind of the Anarchs with some Sabbat thrown in, the Lancea Sanctum have a Sabbat veneer but with a wholly different motivation, and the Circle and Ordo are pretty much completely new.

                    The main advantage to the new Covenants is that they're frameworks on which to build. In Masquerade, the Camarilla was the Camarilla no matter where you went, it was a world-wide organization. In any Camarilla city, the same basic laws were in play, with only minor variations based on the whim of that particular prince. The Sabbat was also pretty much the same wherever you went. In Requiem, while the Invictus is an old, tradition-bound Covenant, exactly what that means to the Covenant in any given city can be wildly different. One city could operate exactly like a Game of Thrones-esque medieval court, another might be a pre-revolutionary aristocracy, yet another might be parliament council with members chosen from a "nobility" of some kind. Heck, the Invictus in one my cities in my on-hold game is a Mafia family. The Carthians may be true anarchs, set only on disrupting whatever status quo currently exists, they may be true "power to the people" democrats, absolute Marxist communists, or supporting "the Glorious Democratic People's Republic Of South Gothtropolis And Its Beloved President For Unlife." The Circle can be harmless hippy-dippy spiritualists, hardcore human-sacrificing evil witches, scary powerful neutral witches who's bad sides you don't want to get on, or mysterious wise women. The Sanctum can be a full-tilt religion of evil, a misguided and corrupt church, or an institution desperately trying to provide some meaning and guidance to the unlives of the Kindred. The Ordo Dracul can be crazy philosophers, vile mad scientists, despicable Nietzsche wannabes, or erudite scholars who understand the ins and outs of the vampiric condition. It's all about how you want to play any or all of them in your game. Requiem is a sandbox game, and the tools are there for individual storytellers and players to make use of. No one vision of any of the Covenants is any more correct than the others, and that's what makes them better than the Sects of Masquerade.

                                        Yeah. The Path of Metamorphosis is about change, but they go about it in drastically different ways. The Path are solitary scientists, who refuse to share their information. They believe there is no one way to master change and that everyone has to try changing themselves as much as possible. Their goals are different, as well, with the Path seeking out mostly physical and spiritual might, and not the sort of overcoming of weaknesses and embracing of powers that the Ordo is into. As their name suggests, the Path promotes change simply for changes sake, anything that might get you an edge, while the Ordo believe change without purpose is pointless. Followers of the Path believe humans are completely unworthy of notice, while the Ordo believes them to be premier examples of change, as they are not 'static' like most of the dead. They're both about change, but they go about it in different ways, their aims are different, and Ordo experimentation results in Coils and Scales.

                                        And the Path of Metamorphosis is just as laden with occult bells and whistles.

                                            Re the Camarilla and Invictus:

                                            I actually view them as inversions, rather than copies, of each other.

                                            Elders in the Camarilla want to accrue power, and they use the Masquerade to do it. They pretend to care about the Masquerade, so they "have to" create rules and a hierarchy and keep the neonates/ancillae in line, but really they just want to sit at the top of the food chain and grow fat.

                                            The Invictus, however, wants to protect the secret (ie keep the Masquerade) and uses power to do that. After all, it's a lot easier to maintain the Masquerade if you carry a big stick to keep your fellow vampires in line, can discretely get mortal journalists reassigned as needed, etc. This also accounts for their newfound tech savvy (compared to 1E): they're on an all-night alert that lets them know when they need to step in.

                                            So they look the same on the outside, but dig deeper and they're really not.

                                            Edit to clarify: I'm going off VtR 2E

                                                        I think it's not really all that helpful to look at the ideology of different groups. That only results at endless discussions that lead nowhere. It's better to look at their goals. What do they want to achieve? I think the Invictus is mostly about achieving power and money, the Carthians is for vampires who want to shake things up, the Order has members who want to become an übervampire (all benefits, no drawbacks), the LS mostly has vampires who want to be conservative or to stay traditional and the Circle wants to get back to a less structured time and remove all restraint.
