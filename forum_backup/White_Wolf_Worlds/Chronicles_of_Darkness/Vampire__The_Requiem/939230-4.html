                    You know, at this point, I’m glad the book isn't prescriptive. It's really evident many of us have completely different views on what the Beast is/means. And that’s totally fine. We all have the right to play our games our way.

                    What the Beast means to me:
                    The Beast doesn’t negotiate. It doesn't speak. It isn’t sophisticated. It is (at the very least) a savage animal. It kills; it feeds; it flees.

                    I’m allowing all physical Disciplines, without a second thought. I can see the Beast using Protean, to draw its claws, etc.
                    Outside of that though, I don't think I want to allow it to use any others. I’d think about Obfuscate to flee.
                    As for Predatory Aura, the book OK’s it, and so do I. It's different to Dominate et al, because it is a primal, wordless, animalistic threat/challenge. It’s terrifying the gazelle to run before you chase it.

                    I’ve talked to my players about “how can we make this seem like we’re playing vampires and not Supers-with-Fangs”, something we all felt with Masquerade.

                    And I’m endeavouring to Direct their approach (as, about to be embraced, vampires):
                    Think of yourself not as a vampire.
                    Think of yourself as a human brain in a vampire body.
                    Don’t think of the Beast as your dark subconscious. Or as a part of you.
                    Think of the Beast as an alien entity in your brain that waits to overpower your mind and take over your body. An alien that only has wants and hungers.

                    I’d just watched “Under the Skin” and “Rabid” which informed my viewpoint.

                    I want this part of the game, certainly in their early days, to be about the Personal Horror of what they have become. And the Beast is my Poster Boy for the whole concept.

                        FutureBondVillain: if you really want to focus on the personal horror aspect, and you want the Beast to be the iconic expression of that, I would really recommend not running the Beast as a separate, alien consciousness. If it's alien, it's by definition not personal. It's something from outside, something other, and while it might be sharing your body and fighting for control of your mind, it's distinctly not you.

                        Now, the idea of having your body, your thoughts, and your control invaded by this atavistic entity is horrifying, to be sure, but it's not personal horror. It's still John Carpenter's first type of story, the one where evil comes from "out there". Even if it's physically in the same space with you, it doesn't come from within you. It comes from the curse, or from the Strix that first created it, or from what ever hellish pit it first slithered out of. It's not "what have I done?", it's "the Devil made me do it."

                        Now, there's nothing wrong with that kind of horror, and it'll certainly get you away from "superheroes with fangs". But if what you really want from the Beast is personal horror, then the Beast has to be just as much you as the Man. That's John Carpenter's second type of story, where evil comes from "in here." Vampires might blame their actions in Frenzy on something the call "The Beast", but it's a lie they tell themselves to shift the blame. They tell stories about the Strix making a deal with their progenitors and creating the Beast, but it's a way to comfort themselves, to give an external origin to something they don't want to admit they're fully capable of all on their own.

                        If you really want the Beast to embody personal horror, then you want it to be the vampire's own darkest instincts and desires, free of inhibition or limitations placed on it by the self-judgmental Man. Pure Id, with no interference from Ego or Super-Ego. You want Frenzy to be what happens not when something else takes over, but when the vampire herself lets go.

                        That's what makes the difference between the two types of horror. Are you afraid of the monsters (even the monsters inside your head)? Or are you afraid of yourself? Both are valid choices, but I personally find the latter far more compelling.

                        That's part of why I prefer to err on the permissive side with Frenzy. It's not the primary reason (that would be ease of use), but it is a contributing factor. Because I don't like to run the Frenzy as what happens when an animalistic conscious that isn't capable of the same higher order thought as you takes over. I like to run Frenzy it as exactly what you would do, if you stopped giving a shit about the consequences and just took whatever you wanted by the most immediate means possible.

                            That's also part of why I like Riding the
                            Wave to be possible at any time. You don't need provocation, you just need to let yourself go. The only question is, once you've let go, are you going to be able to reel yourself back in? And that's why success on the Riding the Wave goal lets you stop after achieving one goal and failure causes you to ride the Frenzy from one goal to the next until you hit a Breaking Point. You get a taste of that freedom that comes from releasing your Ego and just taking what you want, and you don't want to give it up. You keep going, you see something else you want and you take that too. And so on, until you finally do something that snaps your Super-Ego back into action. When you reach that point where you look at yourself and say "Dear God, what have I done?" then and only then does a botched Ride end.

                                    Hi Charly. I appreciate the (time and) thoughts. I think I understand where you're coming from. We definitely seem on opposite ends of the spectrum when it comes to our approaches to the Beast, and what we want it to mean. Though our goals seem very similar. This discourse is certainly helpful to me, in clarifying my own take. So, thanks for that.

                                    I've ran the Beast the more traditional way in Masquerade. And honestly, it always felt like more of an annoyance to the players. Never something horrible. Maybe part of that is my groups approach (me included).

                                    Personal Horror is a funny word. It can mean different things to different people, I think. Ultimately I think this approach is still personal precisely because it is not outside of you. It is within you.

                                    It’s internal, and personal horror, because whether the Beast is you or not, you are responsible for it. You are allowing it to exist. To perpetrate horrible crimes. Because you can stop it at any moment by walking out into the sun. You chose not to. Therefore you are responsible.

                                    And hell, maybe this whole “it’s not me, I didn't do it” IS just a comforting lie you tell yourself.

                                    So, to be clear, I'm not (necessarily) saying my Beast is an alien entity *literally*. But I am strongly suggesting that thought as an approach to roleplaying it (a Director to my Actors, as it were). It doesn't have to be literally true. My wording above is deliberate and fine, “*think of it* as an alien entity”. It’s a way to free the player of the responsibility in the moment. Because the Beast has no conscience. None.

                                    The problem, when we think of it as an “us”, is it encourages thoughts of “I wouldn't do that”. And that thought is a significant step towards a controlled beast that can limit its actions. I don’t want (any) of their conscience (as characters or players) kicking in. I don’t want them to feel in control. It’s hard to feel horror when you have control.

                                    As a Storyteller I feel no need to decide if it even is, or is not, alien. Because you don’t need to understand the Beast. No one does. I’d say no vampire has ever known where it comes from, or what it really is. Because what it really is is a metaphor for the horrible things we people can do. And the more I can empower that the happier I am likely to be with the results.
