      Generally speaking the difficulty serves as a penalty to the roll, and not a target number necessary for success. So trying to make a really difficult jump would impose a -4, for example. A player with a 3 strength and 2 athletics with a specialty in Jumping would roll 2 dice in that instance. Not likely to succeed, but certainly possible.

      The exception to that is in contested rolls, where a stealth roll would have to beat an opponent's perception roll, for example - and extended rolls where the character rolls to reach a target number (the number of chances to roll limited by his attribute + skill).

      Cracking a midgrade safe for example. Let's say has a difficult of 10. Dex (2) + Larceny (3) + lockpicks (1) means your lockpicker would roll 6 dice up to 5 times (total of his skill+attribute). Each roll would be 5 minutes of work.

      Hope that helps. I'm writing this on my phone so sorry about the lack of clarity.

              Feeding roll requires 1 hour. You either succeed or you dont. There are modifiers in the book, negatives to your dice pools. Yes you use Attribute + skill + merit modifiers +/- Anything else.
              for example, if you dont fill up and you need to hunt again you get a -2 to the next roll.

              To determine how much you get, you have to ask your vampire what they are willing to do. If they are willing to kill their victim give them anywhere between 6 and 10. If they want to leave their victim without conditions and relatively healthy, give them 1-5. The victim would get the drained condition if the vampire takes more than the victim's stamina.

              Certain extended actions have a difficulty they need to hit. Unless I am wrong, you can roll until you failed or you hit your cap on rolls. So your first roll you got 2 so you can roll again. You have 5 dice, so you could roll 5 times. each roll takes X amount of time depending on what you're doing and merits.

              Contested rolls like a perception vs stealth would mean you failed even though you got 2 successes, they got 5 so you didnt beat them.

                          As said, the feeding roll is for finding someone to feed from. How much the vampire takes is up to the player.                               Originally posted by Xizzicks View Post
                              How do you determine how much vitae a vampire receives from feeding?
                              There are two ways to run feeding scenes: one with dice and one without dice. The diceless method is very narratively focused and involves tracking how many times the character's Mask and Dirge come up in the scene, and ends with the character finding a victim to feed on and having to make a Breaking Point roll at a certain Humanity level based on what they did during the scene. If you want to read more about that, it starts near the bottom of page 96 and goes through the end of page 97. The dice based method just involves one Attribute + Skill roll, and you can spend 1 Vitae to add your dots in a Discipline to the roll as well. On a success, you find a victim to feed on, and on a failure you don't.

                              Which ever method you use, if you find a victim you can take as much Vitae from them as you like. Just remember that every Vitae you take causes 1 point of lethal damage to the victim, and if they die you'll probably have some complications to deal with, not the least of which is a body to dispose of. Better hope they didn't have any friends or family that cared about them. Not to mention a Breaking Point at Humanity 4 for accidental killing. Then again, if you don't kill them, you'd better have a way to modify their memory, or be willing to bring them into your herd. Otherwise, it's a Masquerade risk.

                              Originally posted by Xizzicks View Post
                              Also is this the correct formula for dice rolls? Skill+attribute+merit bonuses+any other bonuses= number of dice rolled? Then determine difficulty of roll? Say I roll 5 dice at 5 difficulty get 2 successes, does that mean I succeeded at the roll or because it has more failures is that a failure?
                              The basic formula for any dice roll in the system is Attribute + Skill (or sometimes another Attribute) + Equipment bonus + any miscellaneous bonuses (including bonuses from Merits) - any miscellaneous penalties = number of dice rolled. Difficulty is expressed through dice penalties, and if any successes are rolled, the action is a success. If 5 or more successes are rolled, the action is an exceptional success.

                              There are a few variants on this basic formula: Contested actions and Extended actions.

                              Contested actions are used to pit two characters' rolls against each other. Both characters roll using the formula above, and whoever rolls more successes wins. If the winning character rolled 5 or more successes, it's an exceptional success.

                              Extended actions are used when a character is trying to complete a complicated task in a limited amount of time. The character rolls using the formula above, but is trying to accumulate a target number of total successes in a limited number of rolls (normally a number of rolls equal to their unmodified Attribute + Skill dice pool). So, for example, if you're trying to fix a flat tire, you might roll Intelligence + Crafts, and would probably only take 5 successes, since it's a pretty easy task. Let's say this character is fairly average and has a 2 in Intelligence and a 2 in crafts, they've got a decent roadside emergency kit which will give him a +2 equipment bonus, but it's raining heavily, so we'll give him a -3 penalty. That's a total of 3 dice, and he can roll that dice pool up to 4 times. If he doesn't accumulate 5 successes in 4 rolls, he can't fix the tire.

                                      Outside of rare circumstances, an 8, 9, or 10 are always successes. A 10 allows you to reroll and possibly get more successes on top of the success of the 10. On a normal roll, you roll your dicepool, if you have any 8s, 9s, or 10s, you succeed. If you get 5 successes, you get an Exceptional Success, which often improves your results. Sometimes you may have an extended roll, which requires you to get a certain amount of successes in a certain number of rolls.

                                      The most common of the rare circumstances are with a Chance Roll, where you have an effective dicepool of 0 and roll a single Chance Die. In that case, only a 10 is a success, though you can reroll it for potentially more successes. Also some rare abilities can change someone's Target Number up or down, most if not all of these are in Mummy.

                                      Old World of Darkness has adjustable target numbers for rolls.
