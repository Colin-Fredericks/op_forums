    Nightmare 4 – Waking Nightmare – How do you handle the power?

    07-21-2026, 12:33 PM
    Hello all,

    While I love nightmares and the roleplaying possibility of its 4th dot of nightmares, as an ST, I always struggle a bit adjudicating the limit of such a broadly written power.

    On one end, I want to encourage player imagination and respect the fact that it’s a fourth dot of a discipline : It should be strong.

    Multiple other discipline powers can end conflict in a single test and essentially make their victim at the character’s mercy ; Mesmerize, Confidant, Face of the Beast, The Grand Delusion, and multiple other devotions.

    On the other end, even strong powers, such as Loyalty have clear limits to what they can achieve.

    Here are some examples of the powerful effects I have allowed to go through as an ST :
    1st example – massive earthquake

    Scene : A street brawl between the coterie and some dangerous revenants.

    Player Intention : Remove an opponent from battle.

    Illusion : A massive earthquake that makes city buildings crumble, causing dangerous falling debris everywhere.

    Result : The target panicked, succeeded in a few athletic tests to dodge falling debris to avoid falling prone, and fled the scene to somewhere safer, looking extremely confused and somewhat relieved when they realized they had been on a massive psychedelic trip this whole time.
    2nd example – Replicating Clones

    Scene : The gangrel player character had a dramatic failure on a frenzy test after being insulted by their snarky nosferatu teammate, who didn’t realize the gangrel was this close to blowing up.

    Player Intention : Distract the raging beast to avoid being ripped to shreds by their coterie mate.

    Illusion : Replicating taunting clones of the user to distract the gangrel long enough from them to flee the scene.

    Result : A very messy shared haven, but a safe nosferatu and a calmed down gangrel.

    Frenzy Justification : I allowed it because mental discipline can redirect the beast, not its want. Since all senses were affected, I judged that illusions could redirect the beast’s anger to distract the gangrel.
    Questions

    When does its victim start to realize that they might be trapped in an illusion and what can they do about it?

    Is there anyway to break off the effect?
    • I would personally think that Auspex might trigger Clash of Will. I’m tempted to say that increased sense from Protean might as well.

    How do you handle the power yourself?

      Precedent from Obfuscate indicates that Discipline-based illusions are durable enough that even if you suspect your perceptions are being meddled with there's not much you can do to fix your senses by default. A Devotion akin to Shatter the Shroud might be used to proactively break through Waking Nightmare, but the standard kit doesn't really have anything for this.

      Waking Nightmare affects all the senses, and while a case could be made for allowing Auspex to discern the illusion like it can with Obfuscate, I don't think sensory boosts from Protean help any more than glasses or hearing aids would — physically altering your body, even through magical means, isn't going to do anything to help with an attack on your psyche.

          I'm with Satchel.

          This kind of illusion don't rely on being believed: it hijacks the senses directly.

          You can have a character logically conclude the effect is not right, but that won't stop their every sense from screaming it's real.

          From your examples: the Revenant could know this is most likely an illusion from the known illusionist in the coterie, but the ground shaking and buildings crumbling are no less real to the senses and seeking cover isn't just avoiding debris they can't say for sure to not be real, it's also avoiding real attacks they'll be incapable of distinguishing from the illusion.

          And if somehow the frenzied Gangrel concluded it was an illusion, they'd be still incapable of telling which one is real.

          So most of the time the question is actually moot: not believing the illusion won't change much, and any decision taken at this point is risky.

              The way I personally handle Walking Nightmare is that, if the power's roll succed, depending on the effect described by the user (which can be either a player or a NPC) the power affects the victim even though he might be aware it's just an hallucination, then he will get penalties to any roll equal to the successes rolled on the activation roll, until the nightmare is up.
              Another option could be to give malus equal to Nightmare level (either 4 or 5) both options are viable for me.

              Then, if the user activates Walking Dead to prevent the victim to go through a certain direction, e.g.: by making the doorway appearing as a kraken's Jaws, or on fire, or the alley seem severed by a crater, with hellish hands reaching out from its depths, then the victim must take a roll on Resolve+Composure- with the penalities discussed above to be able to just going through the hallucination.

              The difference between these two cases is that, in the first case, the victim is passively distracted and terrified by the environment. In the second case he has to take an effort to go through a specific hallucination.

              Another application I faced during some of my game is using Walking Nightmare to make an ally appearing as a monster (or the enemy they're facing) hoping for the victim to attack the ally in a isntictive reaction. In this case cannot force the player to do a specific action but in order to understand that it may be an hallucination he has to succes on the same reolve+composure roll-nightmare (or - successes rolled on the activation roll)

                      I really like Marcus' split between passive dread and having to actively push through a specific hallucination, since it gives you a clean way to scale the Resolve + Composure roll to the stakes. And I'm with Satchel and monteparnas that realizing it's fake shouldn't just switch it off. My rule of thumb: knowing it's an illusion lets a character act despite the fear, but it never removes the sensory input, so they still can't tell the real threat from the fake one. That way the power stays a fourth-dot heavy hitter without being an auto-win, and clever players are rewarded with agency rather than immunity.

                                  Sorry for the double post, I want to use some examples of waking nightmare and ask you how you would handle each of them or if you would allow them at all.

                                  I ask because I want to see where I stand compared to others, especially in relation to its limitations; one detail, no significant pain and its thematic limit.
                                  1. Abyssal Rift

                                  Scene : A Nosferatu is ambushed by hunters in an alley street and uses waking nightmare in hope to reverse the role.

                                  Illusion : The ground breaks under everyone’s feet, opening a wide bottomless rift black as night.

                                  Question : Which interpretation feels better? A, B, C, or none. If so, how would you handle it?

                                  Interpretation A : The Hunter feels the ground break hunter their feet. Each of them falls to the ground and the world around them slowly feels more distant as they ''fall'' further down the chasm. From the Nosferatu perspective, the hunters are on their backs clawing the air and panicking, leaving the Nosferatu ample opportunity to send them to early retirement.

                                  Interpretation B : Since the illusion is already affecting the ground, it can’t affect other’s ‘’location’’ relative to the place, nor can it obfuscate the Nosferatu. The hunters can see each other standing on a black rift; the hunters feel their whole sense of balance thrown off*.
                                  Some may fall, some more dextrous than others barely manage to stay on their two feet. They can see the Nosferatu walking above the rift unhindered and they realize they have been messing with the wrong kindred. The battle isn’t completely won for the Nosferatu, but the hunter shouldn’t pose much problem.
                                  *Having done something similar for fun in a VR, walking on the edge of a building into ‘’nothing’’ it is truly dissorienting. It was fun to experience what an actual, albeit weak, illusion could be like in real life.

                                  Interpretation C : A mix of both A and B. Hunters are affected by the illusions, they, since they all act in accordance with the illusions, they all see themselves fall. However they can clearly see the nosferatu move unhindered in the pit. After a few seconds, some might realize that the walls of the nearby building aren't getting smaller as they fall. The effect is simillar to B.

                                  Opinion : I’m a bit torn. I honestly don’t know which one I’d prefer. I would generally lean toward A if a player used the power on hunters, or on B if a player was the victim of the power to allow some tests to perceive some clues and allow an ‘’out’’.
                                  2. Solipsism

                                  Illusion : The victim appears to be alone ; their senses can’t pick up anyone in the vicinity, they feel the crushing weight of solitude.

                                  Question : Is this a valid use of the power?

                                  Valid : The power affects a single feature in the location. Having people in a location is a feature of the location, thus removing everyone is a feature in itself.

                                  Invalid : This affects too many features, the power can’t change more than a single person, every person in the room is too many.

                                  Opinion : I would allow it, it’s a powerful effect, it kind of overlaps with obfuscate, but both are illusory powers, one just happens to focus on fear more.
                                  3. Sun Rise

                                  Scene : A Nosferatu is defending its territory from rival kindreds they caught feeding in an alleyway of their territory. They use waking nightmare to conjure the image of a rising sun to urge them to flee.

                                  Illusion : The Sun rises in the distance, its glaring rays begin to reach the kindred in the alleyway.

                                  Question 1: Can Nightmare conjure illusions of the sun? Or fire?

                                  Opinion : On one end, the Sun and fire are definitely among the scariest things for vampires. On the other, it feels a bit weird thematically for a vampire. I’m torn.
                                  Question 2 : If yes, which interpretation sounds like the correct one?

                                  Interpretation A : The kindred affected by the illusion feel the burning gaze of the sun. They test against fear frenzy at -1 (if humanity 5 or above) or at -3 (if humanity 4 or lower). Those that fail run away, seeking the nearest available cover from the sun. Those that succeed realize that while the air feels hotter and the sun is uncomfortable, they don’t actually feel significant pain so they ignore its effect. The most informed kindred realize that they are facing a Nosferatu that can conjure illusions; the less informed ones wonder why they are suddenly resistant to the sun. Let’s hope, for their sakes, that they don't think they can resist the true sun.

                                  Interpretation B : The kindred affected by the illusion feel the burning gaze of the sun. They test against fear frenzy at -1 (if humanity 5 or above) or at -3 (if humanity 4 or lower). Those that fail run away, seeking the nearest available cover from the sun. Those that succeed might realize that there is an illusion, but the sun still feels real, and while it doesn’t hurt significantly it’s still hot and uncomfortable and they can feel their beast panicking as they urge them to flee for cover. They might stave off the frenzy for a bit longer, but they should not linger. They need to test for frenzy at each interval of fake ‘’sunlight damage’’ they would take.

                                  Opinion : I would go toward B. This is where i would probably end up using Marcus house rule for rolling composure + resolve – Nightmare to ignore the effect.

                                  Do you have any other interesting examples that come to mind that you allowed or your storyteller allowed you, or a fellow coterie mate, to use in a chronicle?

                                          In keeping with my position that Waking Nightmare's big mechanical function is manipulating victims of Face of the Beast and The Grand Delusion, and in keeping with the limitations of those powers and Mortal Terror, my reads are as follows:

                                          1. You can affect how the rift reacts while it remains in your presence. You cannot directly control how the hunters react to the rift, and though it manifests to all senses that doesn't make them think they're falling (and in fact I would argue making multiple victims perceive each other to be falling runs afoul of the stipulation that the hallucination only change or add one thing).

                                          They can see that they ground is falling below them and may react accordingly, and you can try to steer their reaction by controlling how the ground appears to crumble, but you aren't going to automatically inflict the Knocked Down Tilt on them just because you showed them something that would make them lose their footing if it was real.

                                          2. I'm with Marcus on this one and will add that the semantic argument that "having people in a location is a feature of the location" is very blatantly not the intended use case between the Nosferatu having a different clan Discipline for hiding people, singular people being another upper limit of the power, and the power's application specifically being additive or transformative.

                                          (This feeds back into the first example: you can make someone see something as looking like it's unstable or rotting or about to explode, but while cases could be made for a hole in the ground as a change to the ground you're not going to use Nightmare's ability to pull fears out of people's heads to convince them something is no longer there.)

                                          3. Following the aesthetic restrictions of both Waking Nightmare and Dread Presence, as well as the stipulations of other powers in Nightmare and Obfuscate, it cannot create a pleasant image nor control the victims' response, which I would argue means, in a hypothetical situation where there's a mortal mixed in with the vampire targets, the sensory experience of the sun is going to be tainted with the touch of nightmares in some way that tips off anybody who doesn't immediately panic and go into frenzy as they would for, say, a theatrically altered UV lamp. (The hallucination can't inflict significant pain, most vampires who have been vampires for long enough to risk frenzy from sunlight damage will not need much exposure at all to recognize it's not actually harming them, and as with the rift, you can't make the hallucination trick the targets into thinking they've been altered in some way without other powers. You could use Waking Nightmare in conjunction with Face of the Beast or The Grand Delusion as a setup for hurting them with Mortal Terror, but simply making a light source or altering the ambient or directed light in a location is unlikely to simulate injury at a level the vampire's Beast will specifically care about beyond the scope of surprise.)

                                          Fire gets a little less trouble on the aesthetic front but still hits on the "you can't make something that actually seems to affect the target on its own" issue with the rift. The function of Nightmare powers before Mortal Terror is to add levers to help steer targets through the use and manipulation of fear, and the Discipline's use of illusions is based on this magical sleight of hand rather than the ability to selectively warp reality in a truer sense than making somebody have a really bad day.

                                              1. You can affect how the rift reacts while it remains in your presence.                                               They can see that they ground is falling below them and may react accordingly, and you can try to steer their reaction by controlling how the ground appears to crumble, but you aren't going to automatically inflict the Knocked Down Tilt on them just because you showed them something that would make them lose their footing if it was real.
                                              To clarify, by option B I essentially was in line with this interpretation of yours.

                                              The victims perceive the fall through all their senses, including their sense of balance, which could manifest as vertigo or dizziness. However, they do not literally see themselves falling into the void. Instead, they see the solid ground opening beneath them. They feel the emptiness below, experience the sensation of falling, and suffer the resulting disorientation, without actually seeing themselves or the others physically plunge downward.

                                              As a result, one person might collapse to the ground screaming, while someone more composed might manage to maintain their balance and continue acting. I still think the VR analogy fits well: even when you know the experience is artificial, some people are more capable than others of overcoming the instinctive reaction and walking across the apparent void.

                                              Mechanically, I would represent this through a roll. Nightmare is a four-dot power, so I think it deserves a concrete and reliable effect rather than one that is excessively dependent on interpretation or randomness.

                                              Once the activation roll succeeds, the victim is affected regardless of whether they understand that it is an illusion. I would require both PCs and NPCs to roll Resolve + Composure, with a penalty equal either to the Nightmare rating or to the number of successes scored on the activation roll, in order to act normally.

                                              Even if they succeed, they would still suffer a penalty equal to the Nightmare rating on their actions due to the distraction (hallucination is still there), unless they achieve an exceptional success on the Resolve + Composure roll.

                                              However, this is only my homebrew ruling, intended to bring the power in line with other four-dot abilities. It is not part of the core rules.

                                              Generally we are aligned the way we read the power.
