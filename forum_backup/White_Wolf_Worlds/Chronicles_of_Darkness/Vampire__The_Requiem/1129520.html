    Heroic Vampires and Other Setting Hacks? (Setting Hack/Homebrew General Discussion)

    08-15-2017, 09:05 PM
    Okay, I have a question to ask and an idea to share.

    The big question I want to ask in this thread is "What are your setting hacks?" and this can apply to both 1e setting hacks and 2e setting hacks.

    Here is my biggest setting hack, which I apply to 1e Requiem (I don't play 2e, the mechanical and setting changes aren't my cup of tea)

    I like the idea of the possibility of heroic Vampires in the New World of Darkness (and the Classic WoD as well). I mainly play 1e, sans Predator's Taint and the Strix (and the emotional deadness fluff as well) and I like applying setting hacks where Kindred are not emotionally dead nor are they automatically evil. Instead, they have free will to be good or evil, and there are both heroic vampires and villainous vampires, with the majority of Kindred just being ordinary citizen types who just want to live their unlives as peaceably as possible.

    As a general rule, my games have three heroic covenants (Carthians, Ordo Dracul, Circle of the Crone) and three villainous covenants (Invictus, Lancea Sanctum, Belial's Brood) with VII and the Strix excised from the setting completely. But I'm experimenting with other setting hacks as well that will turn 1e Requiem into an anime-influenced Action-Adventure setting instead of the assumed Gothic Personal Horror setting (and there is nothing wrong with either interpretation of WoD/CofD).

    The great thing about nWoD 1e (and CofD 2e), is that the whole franchise was designed to be a toolbox where you could eat the chicken and throw away the bone. There was no metaplot and little if any objective truths to the early nWoD setting. The issue was that some people mistook the default setting assumption of personal horror as the only setting assumption, instead of one out of many equally valid options. The release of Mirrors in 2010 only seems to have confirmed my toolbox theory.

    I love the toolkit approach of New World of Darkness and so I ask the question again.

    What are your setting hacks for Vampire: The Requiem?

    The main purpose of this thread is to discuss our own setting hacks and homebrew material for Requiem, can be both 1e and 2e.

                          Originally posted by Camilla View Post
                          Okay, I have a question to ask and an idea to share. Actually, there's two questions I have to ask.

                          First off is the elephant in the living room. Why the hostility to the concept of heroic vampires? I've gotten tired of people throwing around phrases like "Vampions" or "Supers with Fangs" as insults, and I also don't like the idea that personal horror is the only way to play Requiem and that if you're playing anything other than personal horror, you're "playing the game wrong" and should play "Some Other Game". This attitude was a major problem on the old White Wolf forums, RPG.net, and is still a problem on other discussion sites as well. Onyx Path Forums isn't as bad for it as other places, but the attitude still crops up every once in a while. But that's not the major reason why I posted this thread, it's merely something I need to get out of the way.
                          So, first off: there's nothing wrong with playing the game the way you like it. There's nothing wrong with ditching the gothic horror aspects of the game in the name of adventure and action.

                          I think the thing that you (and I mean specifically you, but others too) run up against is a problem of context. When many people think of Vampions and Super Heroes with fangs, they think of bad player experiences they've had. "Oh look, here comes Big McLargehuge of Clan Kickass who's also an orphan and answers to no man; he has no backstory, and he has no motivation beyond doing sweet moves and looking awesome. Won't that be a fun addition to my subtle chronicle of intrigue and personal horror." The problem in that scenario isn't that there's anything inherently wrong with playing a campy Blade expie. It's that many people who've dealt with that character archetype associate it with a negative player stereotype. To many people, that character type indicates the player doesn't want to engage with the story, and would rather play out a power fantasy that the rest of the table doesn't want any part of.

                          But it's more complicated than that:

                          Originally posted by The Danse Macabre, p. 178
                          There’s lots of reasons a player might chose to create the isolated and unconnected badass, and unfortunately many of them relate to bad play experiences in which a Storyteller or other players abused his sense of fun by using those connections to make him feel inadequate about his character, vulnerable, or ineffective."
                          The core of the problem is miscommunication (and also that people are often jerks, but for the sake of argument let's just assume everyone we're interested in playing games with is playing in good faith). Bob wants to run a game about dark fantasy angst, and Alice wants to play out Underworld. It's less that the game is wrong, and more that the group wants different things. But people are people, and it's hard to admit to friends that, hey, maybe we shouldn't be doing this together. People bottle that anger or resentment up, and they make the symbol (Trenchcoat McNinja and personal horror, respectively) the object of their anger rather than the person they're actually mad it. This is a bit reductive, but I think you get what I'm saying.

                          (Some people will tell you that it is inherently wrong to play campy Blade expies, and go out of your way to inform you of that, but those people are joyless fucks who aren't worth your time.)

                          There's also -- and this is not a judgment on you -- a stereotype that any form of power fantasy is a bit juvenile, and that therefore it's a bad thing. Adults aren't allowed to indulge in their ids, and therefore anyone who does is doing it wrong or is childish.

                          And you know what? Screw the haters. Play the character with five dots of Blowshitupus. Wear sunglasses at night and wield a katanagun. Be rad.

                          Vampire is written with the assumption that participants want to engage in a particular subgenre of gothic fiction. That doesn't mean you have to do it, and as you say, the toolbox is very accommodating. There's lots of room for action and adventure instead of wangst. The thing is -- and again I don't want you to feel judged -- is that it isn't necessary for you to announce your preference for this style of gaming in every post you make. It would be a bit like if I went into every Masquerade thread and started off with "Well I prefer Requiem! But to each their own!" in every post I made. It would be a bit tiresome.

                          Real talk: We know, dude. You don't need to apologize for what you like, but you don't need to make the conversation about it every time you talk about Vampire.

                                              Anyway, onto actual setting hacks. I like to alter the Bloodline creation rules when it comes to blood potency.

                                              At level 2 potency if the Kindred is hit with a particulary nasty curse, and over the course of the game they start turning the curse to their advantage, then I let the Kindred found a bloodline; no special disciplines or devotions are created. They get four in-clan disciplines, a bloodline gift (possibly a merit of some sort), and the nasty curse as the bloodline bane.

                                              At Blood Potency four they can form a bloodline without any extra nasty bane; however their bloodline advantage will be learnable by Kindred outside the Bloodline, so there is added risk.

                                              At blood potency six, when the Kindred has become a true terror of the night, things become interesting. They can create blood sorceries, new disciplines, powerful devotions, and the like; their advantages will not be learnable outside the Bloodline.

                                              Anyway, that's my Bloodline alteration. I'm currently working on some homebrew blood sorceries from some Covenants I was designing, so I might post those sometime.
