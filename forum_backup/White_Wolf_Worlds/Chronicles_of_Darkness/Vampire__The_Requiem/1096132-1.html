        OP - have you sat down with your GM and explained the themes and motifs you want your character to have, and ask how they can best be incorporated? As a constant GM, I try to avoid saying "no" but I have had players come up to with me ideas that will only throw the setting (and the other PCs) into chaos. For example (true story, tbw):

        Me: "I'm proposing a Changeling: the Lost campaign with the Fear-Maker's Promise as the opening. This requires your character to be active enough within the Freehold to know about the ritual and to be present".
        Player: "My character is [long backstory, essentially blending Batman with Green Arrow, a son of a multi-millionaire, killed his fetch and replaced him with zero problems]".
        Me: "OK. Your wealth could work as one of the themes of the campaign is lack of resources so..."
        Player: "Oh, no. [Character name] doesnt engage with the Freehold. Instead he patrols the street with a bow and arrow, protecting abused children."
        Me: "Right. Ok. Maybe the Freehold is aware of your character and extends an invitation to him so he can a) engage with the community and b) ultimately gain access to his wealth?"
        Player: "He would reject their invitation as he is a lone wolf."
        Me: "So how would he be present at the ritual in order for your character to be able to participate in the campaign?"

        This went on for a few days. Eventually - and despite me proposing a variation of the PC's bow-and-arrow stuff which actually gave him a major role - I said the character couldn't be used. The alternative he came to the first game with was an American police officer who had escaped from the Hedge, joined the police and now ran around the UK (where the campaign was set) with a desert eagle - with Strength 2. The group indulged the character choice for one or two sessions before it was clear the campaign was being seriously unbalanced. If the player had approached me at the start and stated what the themes they wanted the character to have, we probably could have made it work. As it was, he came up with this very-rigid concept and refused to budge.
