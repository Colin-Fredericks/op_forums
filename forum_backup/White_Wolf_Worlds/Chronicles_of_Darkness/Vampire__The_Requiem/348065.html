          Here is a link to the free PDF of the changes to the blue book rules. The basics are the same, but a lot of subsystems have had some overhauling, most of all the experience system. First edition material is still very much usable in second edition games, but some of it will need a little conversion work if it interfaces with any of the affected subsystems.

          Vampire is the only line that the second edition core book has come out for so far. All of the Disciplines have been rewritten, including both Blood Sorcery Disciplines and the Coils. They all have a lot more oomph now. Clan weaknesses have been redone as well, and now get worse as you lose Humanity.

          Humanity has been slightly reworked so that it actually measures how human (or inhuman) a character is instead of how well they keep to a moral code. You suffer Breaking Points for doing inhuman things like surviving something that would kill a mortal, or going months without human contact, as well as for heinous acts like murder. However, you can protect your Humanity somewhat by maintaining a relationship with one or more Touchstones - someone (or more rarely, something) that reminds you of what it means to be human. You can also take Banes - additional weaknesses mined from vampire folklore, to make a particular act not count as a Breaking Point any more, but you can only do it a limited number of times, and it makes other Breaking Points hit a bit harder.

          Sunlight damage is variable now, with your Humanity determining how much damage you take and your Blood Potency determining how often you take it. Now high Humanity neonates can survive brief periods in the sun as long as they take the proper precautions while jaded elders go *poof* almost instantly.

          Blood Potency limits on Vitae Per Turn advance much faster now, though being restricted to Kindred blood only happens at BP6 now instead of 7. However, you can get around feeding restrictions somewhat by spending enough Willpower. All vampires have a certain degree of heightened senses now as part of the base template, basically they can see in the dark and smell blood, hear heartbeats, etc. from a long distance, which increases with Blood Potency. You also add your Blood Potency to all physical dice pools while you're Frenzying.

          Frenzy is no longer split into anger/hunger/fear, instead every time you Frenzy, you determine with the Storyteller what the goal of that Frenzy is, which can be "kill", "feed", or "get away" but can also be something else if appropriate. Predator's Taint has been replaced with Predatory Aura, which is conceptually similar, but instead of forcing a contested roll every time two vampires meet for the first time, you just sort of passively recognize other vampires for what they are, Unseen Sense style, and you can actively challenge another vampire with your Predatory Aura if you chose to.

          I'm probably missing a few things, but that's the gist of the mechanical changes. The fluff has been reworked a bit as well, to give Requiem a bit more of its own identity, independent of Masquerade. Nothing drastic, but for example there's no talk of Golconda in the new core book, and it's made much more clear that Vampires probably don't all share a single common ancestor and even the five Clans, which share more in common than most other types of vampires, probably come from different origins.

          You can expect the other lines to have similar changes, and can read more about them on the open development blogs here on the Onyx Path website.

              As for using first edition material with second edition books, there will be some on-the-fly translation needed, but significantly less than you'd think. Since the structure of rolling the dice, counting successes, failure/success/exceptional success, most traits, etc. are intact, a lot of mechanics can be used as is, with a lot more having fairly straightforward tweaks (for instance, the rule of thumb for converting 1e weapon bonuses is to reduce the bonus by one and then apply it the 2e style of adding damage instead of dice; the rough conversion for experience costs is to divide raw numbers by five or take the cost of the third dot in a progressive dot rating and divide that by five). There are a handful of mechanical corners, like powers that interact with systems that were removed or significantly altered (Morality, specific Virtues or Vices) or with game events that did not previously have a comprehensive system (Social Maneuvering). These are not too common, though.

              First edition setting material is easily compatible. Setting hasn't changed much, and where it has, it's mostly changed to try to better reflect the tone that was already developing across first edition.

                  I believe people may be underestimating just quite how different things are in between the editions.
                  They aren't wrong to say that things are mostly the same mechanic wise and that only a few subsytems are different.

                  However, the differences, to the subsystems and the change to the lore things interact in such a way as to pain a very different picture from what was painted in 1st edition.

                  For exemple torpor is handled differently. While it is still based on a combinaition of blood potency and humanity it does tend to be quite shorter on the lower end of the scale.
                  Also the fog of ages is now the exception rather than the rule. And there are many many ways for a vampire with sufficient aount of power (often political) to get around feeding restriction.

                  All these factors contribute to fundamentaly change the nature of the setting. Clueless elder that can be manipulated after spending centuries if not millenia in topro aren't really a thing anymore. More often than not an elder will be able to avoid torpro more easily, stay in it for shorter length of time and still know exactly what was what when he wakes.

                  This in turn affect kindred view of history. History is no longer this unknowable thing that can't be relied upon. Now it's a thing that should be doubted a little but since not everyone suffer from the fog of ages it can be argued that at least in the broad strokes history as the vampires knows it may be correct. There is a seed of doubt, but that's just that a seed.

                  The removal of predator taint and replacement with the predatory aura also alter kindred society. As a result it is much less xenophobic and closed up than it was. No longer are new kindred a dangerous source of potential frenzy for other, nor are kindred easily able to tell who is older and stronger between them. Now vampire can meet new vampires easily and whitout such risks and any use of the aura is seen as a blatant agression and not easily forgiven for it is a voluntary thing.

                  A few disciplines have changed but the one that comes to mind as having the most potential to have changed kindred society is auspex. Auspex used to be super senses wih a dose of postcognition, telepathy and astral travel (in the common and not mage sense of the term) thrown in. Now the low level of auspex are far more focused on ferreting out direct answer to questions.
                  Before mekhet where an annoyance because you could never be quite sure of what they knew, now a mekhet can potentialy learn anything about anyone no matter what precautions are put in place.
                  This has staggering implication for other vampire attitudes toward Mekhets, implications that have been quietly ignored and perhaps not even realised. But in my sense if you lived in a society based heavily on secrets, backstabbing and paranoia and someone showed up with the ability to tell all your secrets at a glance, not just read your mood but outright finding out factual information about you, something that was limited to whether or not you were a diablerist before but now apply to everything, You would not suffer their presence gladly. The implication is, at least to me, that mekhet should be treated like pariahs even worse than nosferatus at best and outright killed on sight at worst.

                  Clan weaknesses have changed, making the way everyone belonging to each clan act differently from what was happening in 1st edition, somtime drasticly so.
                  Gangrel used to be...well...somewhat dumb. Now they just have a harder time resisting frenzy. They shouldn't be seen as dumb but potentialy very dangerous, not entirely out of control, they can guide their frenzy just as well as anyone else but they will frenzy more often.

                  Daeva were big vice indulgers, turning hedonistic out of necessity, now they they get a pseudo blood bond if they feed more than once from the same person, that makes them either supremely promscuous or supremely careful.

                  Ventrue were prone to insanity, now they just have slightly more trouble with touchstones (it seems to be the conscencus that their clan weakness is a joke)

                  Nosferatu however have if far easier now. Sure people are still unsettled but their curse only applies to relating to people, a nos can just as easily seduce someone else as anyone, just don't ask for them to make a good shoulder to cry on if you are human.

                  Mekhet were suffering from worse banes than other, they still do, but they are also less able to get a thick skin, so to speak, when it comes to what prompt vampire to realise they aren't human.

                  There are many more things that have changed and so have different implications compared to first editions, those are simply the ones that came to mind immediatly.

                  In short: seemingly small changes have staggering implications that it may be opportune to consider.

                                  Sure the ST can ignore anything it wants, nothing forces anyone to play anything in any way. But better awareness of potential issues does lead to better resolution.

                                  But none of this is entirely relevant to the question at hand.
                                  Backward compatibility.

                                  What's backward compatible ? Mechanicly wise a lot is unless it depends on subsystem that have been changed. And some old rules are essentialy obsolete and it's the little thing that catch you by surprise.
                                  Mythos wise I'd say compatibility is broad stroke only. A lot of little things have changed and make 1 for 1 port difficult at best.
                                  And sometimes, in re-examining things you find stuff that make you go "well that's interesting, what if ?" and now you have a wicked story hook to explore, or fun brain teaser.

                                  One exemple that happened to me not long ago.

                                  My player was a member of the ordo dracul and for unrelated reasons I was looking at the strix. So as I was brushing up on my ordo knowledge and seeing what fluff wise need to be updated my brain did a funny thing.

                                  It said this: "Wait a minute, a lot of Dracula story as told in the old books can be seen through the lens of Dracula having been a strix !"
                                  My gut reaction was of course to go "no way!" and ignore it. But then the potential dawned on me. The order does track the number of sireless embrace, of course most will just be about a clueless abandonned neonate or accident, but surely a number of these are the result of strix using synthesis on a corpse or living being. What if dracula was one of them ?
                                  But dracula said he talked to god, well, with the discovery that the whole out of body experience/tunnel of light thing can be replicated by using certain drugs, drugs that are produced by a dying brain, that certainly would rattle the position of traditionalist in the order.
                                  So what if some in the order realised the "truth" (whether it is true or not is up to the ST, but a faction may certainly believe so) an heresy whithin that seek to develop coils that turn vampires into strix.
                                  What if dracula was a strix and sook to develop method to corrupt vampires or make the beast inside burgeon into a full blownstrix ? It certainly make sense given the nature of the coil looking quite like what happen when a strix is possessing a vampire. At least superficialy.
                                  And in a domain where strix are present and everyone is paranoid about them, maybe the order doesn't advertise dracula story too much because then someone might get the wrong idea and start a witch hunt.

                                  The point is, there is a lot of potential to be found when updating things and there is fun to be had in not simply ignoring implications and do the same thing you've been doing since 1st.

                                  You can take a wild stab at exploring those new implications, maybe it work, maybe it doesn't. Or you can ignore those implications and maybe it works, maybe it blow up in your face when a player point at something and goes "that doesn't make sense"

                                  I think it's worth exploring even if in the end you decide to ignore the implications if you don't like them, at least you'll do so with the full knowledge of what could be.
                                  In short, don't knock it till you try it.
