    How do people use the Strix?

    11-05-2016, 07:47 PM
    I had this vague plan for my Seattle game of looking up hauntings and urban legends and basing a strix or two on that, but Seattle doesn't seem to have a lot of urban legends that'd work.

    So I think I'll create an urban legend and it'll be about a strix. Hopefully some of the Seattle hauntings might be of use (and there are so many).

    But mostly I'm wondering how people use strix? How many might be in a given area? I don't want to make them a central thing, but more a somewhat spooky thing that eventually comes to a head, but isn't the central conflict (what that is will depend on the players).

      I think you're on the right track. I think the Strix work best as boogeymen, so I hint at them often but actually bring them in quite sparingly. As for how many might be in an area, I would say that depends on what's happening (or about to happen) in the area. Strix can sense impending disasters and are drawn to them, so you can use them like black dogs (or what ever doom omen you like). A glimpse of an avian shape out of the corner of the eye can herald a coming battle. A small handful in a domain can mean the whole city is at risk. Larger numbers could foreshadow major story climaxes.

                          I use a highly houseruled setting and I do not use Strix as they are described in the official setting. However, I use one version of them, and I think I can relate to your question a bit. I don't use Strix as separate entities because I would rather have Vampires as main antagonists, and older, weirder vampires as boogeymen. I prefer to have vampires be the pinacle of horror and I don't really need something else to top it.

                          In my homebrew setting, when a vampire is destroyed, her dark essence, the Curse itself that makes her a vampire, is free to leave the body. This dark essence is the "supernatural DNA" of the vampire, as well as her consciousness. Basically, it's everything except the body. The higher their Blood POtency at the moment of their death, the less time their dark essence takes to reform and become strong enough to possess a body, allowing the vampire to be reborn. This dark essence freed from the body IS the Strix. They can possess humans or vampires (at higher risk but greater reward) to be reborn and often seek the ones responsible for their death in order to possess them.

                          This is the reason why destroying vampires is not well regarded. They can come back and there is virtually no way to prevent a possession although it is resolved woth a clash of will. As a result, I use Strix,or at least, my equivalent of them, as a potential threat to vampires who destroy one of their kind. It also gives vampires a reason to use stakes and keep their torpid ennemies hidden instead of destroying them. The only way to cause final death is through Diablerie, the Strix or Dark essence being completely consumed.

                          When a Shadow is sighted, vampires know one of their kind is about to rise again, and that any of them could be potentially used as a vessel. Thus, despite the difference of setting, I tend to use them a lot like some of the people who answered you, that is, as a potential threat hanging over the kindred's heads and influencing their society as a whole rather than as direct antagonists.

                                      I ran Blood and Smoke before it was retitled, so I wanted to make the Strix prominent, but I still kept them as a mysterious threat striking from the shadows, probing for weaknesses and turning the city's Kindred on each other. The players' characters tried to find a way to repel them, and their best bet was reviving an elder in torpor who none of the local vampires wanted to come back - saving the city at a cost of setting off a Kindred civil war. Even defeating the Strix was cause for a violent upheaval.
