                                What counters this character and isn't hyper-specialized or is at least believable? Numbers, grappling, tilts, long-ranged gunfights, Obfuscate, Celerity, mind control, anything that auto-counters stealth (like Auspex arguably). Some longer thoughts below.

                                The character can indeed oneshot things. Make sure his backstory is consistent with this.

                                The problem with hyper-specialized characters are challenging them, keeping the other characters involved, keeping the other characters relevant, and the rocket tag effect.

                                You're going to want to avoid challenging the player with high-defense opponents because then the other characters will feel useless. Also because the system is offense-oriented, especially when you go supernatural. It's much easier to get offense than defense, there's no way you can compete with the insane amount of dice Thrust gives Munchkin McNinja from Light Weapons style.

                                Honestly, you can't really avoid the rocket tag effect. Combat *should* be lethal in a setting and system like this. The PCs should try to only take fights that they think they can win. This naturally lends itself to making a campaign non-combat oriented so that when combat does occur, it's a big moment. By taking this approach, you're already naturally dealing with the combat monster, because he's reduced to his secondary roles of Larceny and Stealth to support the coterie. It also makes McNinja feel special, because the rare thing is his domain (unless he's a D&D player who expects combat all the time)

                                One way you can occasionally let the other characters feel powerful is to give them access too allies via their social maneuvering. If they talk the allies into helping them for a deadly encounter, give them near-full control of the character in question. (Basically access to temporary retainers/mentors/allies)

                                Unless the coterie does something insanely ambitious in the beginning, you should def have McNinja or anyone else who has a specialized character kick ass in their domains in the first few sessions of the game. That way it emerges who is great at what. Once they have their moment in the spotlight and character roles are solidified...

                                Now let's talk about challenging McNinja. You seem to discount disciplines. This is a supernatural game, at some point fighting actual vampires and not their human pawns is going to happen.

                                You seem to also discount numbers. If the character doesn't want to 1v3 or the party doesn't want to say 5v8 or 5v10 or whatever, it's your job to incentivize this or have their goals fail as a result of not wanting to take on the potentially deadly encounter. In superhero games for example, a scenario is often not about beating the bad guy, but trying to both beat him *and* save innocents in the process. Come up with appropriate scenarios why the party would want to at least consider taking on superior numbers in Vampire, such as getting a guarded McGuffin for the Prince surrounded by lots of people.

                                Low Threat
                                Gangs of Mooks - Raw numbers and sheer # of attacks are a great way to lower Defense and a big incentive to not All-Out Attack. A low-level example if a bunch of gangers sitting around eating shit, protecting a McGuffin. Medium level example would be a security force all getting together around a McGuffin when they notice everyone is getting oneshotted and not reporting in because McNinja is killing everything.

                                Badass Normal - A very perceptive mortal can probably spot McNinja, allowing him to radio a larger group of mortals, ruining any chance of further stealth. A legendary wrestler type thrown in the mix can probably grapple Munchkin McNinja, at least until he gets Double Jointed. Maybe give these guys Quick Reflexes so they have a chance of going first.

                                1 or 2 snipers - Maybe have a sniper or two guard a location. It'd be a nice, non-lethal surprise for the party if they didn't do their legwork or properly scout.

                                He Got Away! - A surviving mortal or whatver can get away easily if he gets to a car in time or maybe just the party doesn't have access to Celerity, chasing power, or Survival for tracking. Appropriate consequences ensue.

                                Social Faux Pax - The Prince or an important frou-frou douchey NPC invites the whole coterie to have tea with him, L5R style. McNinja fumbles the social rolls and a potential ally becomes distant, or even hostile.

                                Role Reversal - The party is out of commission or separated for some reason and time is a constraint. McNinja is forced to go be The Face (or whatever other role), likely screwing things up in the process

                                Medium Threat
                                Hidden Badass - The coterie takes on a threat they think they can take on. They kill the Big Guy or the Obvious Leader of the encounter. Surprisingly, after the oneshot occur, it turns out there's a badass in the mix that happens to hard counter McNinja, like a vampire grappler. I don't recommend doing this too often. If you do do it, fluff it as his muscles have been wirey and hidden beneath his clothes, anime-style, Bruce Lee style. Allow decent Perception checks beforehand to spot this.

                                Ninja vs Ninja - McNinja is pretty good at sneaking around. Have another ninja come after him and grapple him when he's alone. To make this dramatic, you can have it be while the party is a few turns away, so they get there in time to save him.

                                Alucard vs Coterie/McNinja - A high Celerity ranged character can kite the party and put on some serious damage. McNinja can't do his Light Weapons Thrust shenanigans to someone he can't reach.

                                He Got Away... Again! - With access to Celerity and Obfuscate, it can be easy for bad guys to get away unless the party does amazing legwork and has good teamwork (or insane alphastrike potential)

                                Kindred Duel - Design a rival, maybe more powerful, basically just for him and railroad him (if he's down for it) into a non-lethal duel. Use say, wooden knives. Game it as basically a normal combat but with no actual damage being done, first one to put the other guy in fake-torpor wins... Or just have a judge, if that doesn't make sense. Maybe even have him use a very similar build to the player's character, and namedrop him when Important Vampires are first impressed by the coterie ("McNinja's cold edge reminds me of McRival... did McRival sire you, McNinja?" and such gossip like that) Later in the campaign, have the rival hunt McNinja. Since he knows how rocket-taggy his own character is, there'll be heavy paranoia for a while.

                                High Threat
                                Any of the specialized builds mentioned in this thread. Also, any of the general advice mentioned (for example, maybe his reputation begins to precede him, Hunters are put on to him, I dunno, anything.) I mention grappling a lot because biting is a big part of Vampire (so it's common) and frenzied biters can auto-grapple and grappling is a weakness of the build (at least, until Double Jointed)

                                -----------------

                                That said, if you truly don't want to say "no" to the character but still wanna nerf him, you can resort to house rules. If I were to go this route, I'd swap Thrust (Level 2 Light Weapons) with the level 4 or 5 Light Weapons merit, forcing the character to invest heavily to have access to obnoxious dice. I'd also increase the cost of Cheap Shot and Defensive Combat by 1. You could also maybe nerf Kindred dueling somehow. All of these things would delay his build heavily.

                                    I'd just say none of the three fighting styles in question stack in any way. Even if there was no potentially OP fighting style involved. Seems silly to me to try to employ three maneuvers on the same turn in any given situation, except perhaps when said maneuvers are largely passive.

                                    I don't consider a shiv a weapon that relies on finesse, especially if/when you're using Brawl to use the weapon (which you'd have to use to use any Street Fighting technique). Second, I'd say carving requires a sword.

                                    Punch daggers are illegal in most of the US, so he'd have to expend some effort to get those. Actual shivs I'd argue aren't appropriate for any fighting styles as written. Daggers though, sure. That's all on you and how you run your games.

                                    Second, your guy's max pool is 26 or something to that effect if everything goes right, which averages out at 11 damage. Any character with 5 dots in Resilience blocks more than half of that. Then they get a chance to get this guy in a grapple, or a frienzied attack. Or just a similar attack with a bunch of vigor. Throw 6 xp on any starting character (if in-clan) and they can have 5 dots of Resilience. Neither implausible nor uncommon for a character who wants to fight. Cheapshot isn't likely to work again, and on the chance it does, said opponent would likely still survive

                                    The 5th dot of the Close Combat fighting style disarms an opponent more or less automatically, and again, completely stymies this attack.

                                    As does Celerity. Which has been mentioned, but when/if the character gets Celerity himself, winning that Clash of Wills isn't any less likely than him winning his Cheapshot roll. That's two rolls he has to succeed at before he can even get his OP fighting maneuver off the ground. If his opponent has more Celerity or a higher BP, they'll likely win that Clash of Wills, which leaves our guy very vulnerable at this point having Thrusted and sacrificed his defense (assuming in this scenario that he's sacrificed all of it). At which point he can be grappled or just flat hit with a similarly devastating assault.

                                    If he makes enough enemies, an Assassin with Obfuscate and some Carving and/or Vigor can do what he does with more reliability.

                                    Then we throw in other characters using Obfuscate and Protean, with more wily ways to win battles and it becomes even more complicated. Swarm Form, Mist Form. A frenzied Gangrel with Claws of the Unholy, Horrid Talons or Barbed Hands will wreck him in a Grapple, which they could apply on attack. The BP Bonus from Frenzying and a higher focus on brawl would leave him very disadvantaged, as he's unlikely to win the grapple.

                                    Or a Ventrue with Dominate and a horde of manservants, bodyguards, ghouls, etc. willing to die for him. 10 dots of merits translated into 10 dots of retainer/protection is just as effective as a grab bag of fighting styles, if not moreso. God forbid he has more. Or a Nosferatu playing with his head, can easily just sending him fleeing from a battle. Lashing out can subdue him.

                                    And if you want to put him in his place, just throw him against a vampire with 10 more xp than he has. Maybe a couple extra BP.

                                    As far as winning the cheapshot roll, as has already been mentioned, acute senses, danger sense, auspex, etc. There are a dozen ways to beat a 7 die roll that degrades with each attempt.

                                    So his combination is powerful, but not nearly as strong as you seem to think. And honestly, if you're so worried about it, just throw in two characters with the exact same spread and throw them at him. Or one with a dot more Vigor. You're the ST, it should be laughably easy to challenge your PCs. You can do anything.

                                        Lots of good stuff, though I have a few issues with this paragraph:

                                        1) High Defense characters are actually something the non-combat helpers can be useful for: each attack (fail or not) reduces Defense (baring Merits/Powers). It's one of the reasons that while 2e has upped Defense significantly from 1e, numbers remain the most brutal factor. A high Defense oriented enemy has to choose to maintain their Defense against major enemies (how do they know who at first?) and let low combat characters get free hits on them, or let their Defense drop as minor attacks they can easily fend off leave them open to a big hitter.

                                        2) You can actually compete with Thrust. Armed Defense great for stomping it down - remembering that Dodge uses a base dice pool of double base Defense and then adds any additional bonuses. A character with no powers, and not maxing out Dex and Wits, can still easily get up to ~18 Dodge dice, and then apply all sorts of counter attacks with the style.
