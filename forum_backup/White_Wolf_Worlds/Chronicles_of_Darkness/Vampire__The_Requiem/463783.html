    03-31-2015, 04:20 PM

    I did not see any mention of this in VtR 2e. Have there been any official crossover rules promulgated for 2e, and has this issue ever been addressed. Thanks.

                      Nitpick - werewolves claim to not be human. They're not on the same level of not-human as the Created and Unchained are. The latter two are more akin to self-aware robots, while the former was born of humans and spent much of its life as a human before the Change.

Over time, the whole "you are what you eat" rule came into play, and became more and more like each other, until we got the modern day vampires.

                      So, Tremere, to me, aren't just vampire-like. They're beings who've started on the path to evolving into actual vampires.

                                          Originally posted by branford View Post
                                          I believe that the Werewolf books explicitly, both in terms of setting and mechanics, do not consider werewolves to be human, or at least no longer human. They are half-spirit beings.
                                          See, here's the thing. With the exception of hunters, none of the game lines are "human" by that standard. Like Saibot said, the writers have called the Awakened monsters as well. Mages may appear the most human, but they are, in fact, not. For a given value of "human," that is. Remember, as well, that in-character voices (like the one nofather quoted) need to be taken with a grain of salt - they are what characters in the game tell themselves, not what is an objective fact from a game design stand point.

                                          However, all the PC splats (with two notable exceptions) start off as humans in one way or another. They had a human life before their transformation, they had a human soul that reacted like a human soul to soul damaging/stealing effects, they suffered as humans do to the Kiss, Lunacy, the Lie, and so on.

                                          This is completely different than Prometheans and Demons, both of whom have never been human. They don't have human souls, they have never had human souls, and human experiences are foreign to them. Trying to put werewolves on par with these alien minded beings is unfair, especially when a large part of the game's themes involves the balance between human flesh and wolf spirit. Trying to equate the three does a huge disservice to all three gamelines. My point was never "werewolves are humans," it was that "werewolves are just as much human as changelings are." Considering that changelings effectively transform into fae lords, that 'human' aspects are very much up to debate.


                                          Similarly, mages are generally treated in all respects as human, although no longer subject to the Lie. They are like any other human with supernatural merits, although their abilities are significantly more versatile and powerful than virtually all other mortals. Conceptually, I've always compared Mage to the Dreden Files. Supernal Mages are akin to the very human and powerful White Council Wizards, feared and envied by the relatively numerous number other humans with lesser magical abilities.
                                          Mages are very much not humans with some powers. They are a different race of beings altogether. While physically, there's not a lot of change, spiritually, they are as different from a human as a vampire or werewolf.
