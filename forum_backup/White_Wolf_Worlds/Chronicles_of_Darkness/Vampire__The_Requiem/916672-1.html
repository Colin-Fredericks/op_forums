                    The upcoming Hurt Locker addresses the fighting merits question. This would be a draft but indicates the official line of thought. So my question would be explain how using light weapons and street fighting works at the same time (to the player)? It won't work if it can't get passed the first rule.
                    Stacking Fighting Merits

                    Can you use more than one Fighting Style maneuver or other Merit in a single attack? Sometimes. The following rules apply.
                    1. If nobody can explain how the maneuvers would stack in the story, it doesn’t work. The player needs to describe an action that brings them together. Your two pistol backflip headlock’s probably going to sound dumb—unless you can somehow make it sound amazing.
                    2. The required equipment or other circumstances for all maneuvers used must be present.
                    3. You can’t stack Fighting Merits that penalize one of your character’s traits more than once, or impose the same disadvantage more than once. For example, you cannot stack two Merits that lower Defence.
                    4. You can’t stack maneuvers that use different dice pools, unless changing the dice pool is a benefit the Merit specifically confers. For example, you can use Fighting Finesse with maneuvers that require Strength + Weaponry, and they’ll use Dexterity + Weaponry instead, but you can’t combine a Merit that uses Weaponry with one that uses Brawl.
                    5. When stacked maneuvers provide the same benefit, the higher of the two apply. All disadvantages apply (though remember that you can’t stack maneuvers that impose the same disadvantage twice). Note that when two maneuvers provide the same result by different means, they do stack, so the Rote benefit (re-roll failed dice) does stack with bonus dice.

                                                            Hey, the zweihander Sherriff isn't "an ancient" build. It requires just 4 merit dots to work :P.

                                                            In general, there are two parts to this dude's build that make it strong. First one is Thrust manuever of Light Weapons; it offers great flexibility and potential alpha strike capabilities, because it allows you to spend any number of your Defense to get attack dice. Again, this character doesn't have to bring himself to 0 Defense; he may as well just bump his attack by one or two, to get into sweet range of 5 dice, or use it as a super all out attack when it's tactically plausible. This makes a great ambusher and great duelist tactic as you noticed.
                                                            However, this doesn't work as well in normal circumstances; many supernatural creatures in CofD can survive an overwhelming alpha strike. Vampires are pretty good at it; they can reflexively heal up pretty much any tilt you throw at them, due to being able to heal a lot of bashing damage in a single go. A Blood Potency 2 character can instantly get rid of any tilts attached to 4 boxes of bashing damage. This means that a tilt will last 2 rounds at best.

                                                            Again, this whole thing goes south because of Kindred Dueling style. Ability to deal lethal damage in a ecosystem balanced for bashing damage will make things work not as intended. Standard Kindred fight might involve broken bones, snapped jaws, dislocated spines...and a vampire going "fuck, this hurt, you asshole, im out of here", spending some blood and exiting the scene without any damage, but with a dent in their blood economy.

                                                            Here is a thing - casual violence in Vampire is a thing. Vampires tend to flip out. Game knows that Vampires WILL throw cars at themselves when they got pissed off to settle a dispute. So it had a very difficult dillema to solve - how do you allow this, pretty iconic, behavior to happen regularly without introducing a serious risk of death? The answer they arrived was, I believe, this:
                                                            You keep damage brutal and visceral, and embrace the idea of damaging tilts to evoke the gory imagery; however, you downgrade all damage to bashing; this, coupled with massive health bar *and* reflexive way to heal massive amounts of damage ensures that "a lucky crit" issue doesn't exist; there is literally NO WAY to kill someone accidentally. You can't punch another vampire and roll a crit that will kill him. In order to kill someone, you need to have clear killer intent. However, obviously, if it was just this, combat would loose it teeth; without consequences, combat doesn't feel right, and we just removed "characters might die" consequence from combat. So, in order to keep combat not-trivial, LETHAL damage costs more to heal; the point where a Vampire get's punched into Lethal damage is a good warning sign, a signal to stop fighting, because these wounds stop being something you can heal off with just a thought. Then, going into aggravated offers you another warning sign; these wounds won't heal anytime soon; one aggravated damage is enough to stop pretty much anyone from pursuing further conflict, unless it's really dramatic and important.
                                                            Of course, these are consequences of Lethal and Aggravated brawls. What are consequences of the casual, vibrant and ecstatic Bashing Brawl? It's easy to heal off, and you won't even remember it 5 minutes later, yes? Isn't it meaningless?
                                                            Well, no. Keeping yourself away from Lethal Damage will have a very real consequence - it will put a dent into your blood economy. Casual brawls force you to go and eat, they give you a moment of weakness during your night, they might make you stop and think twice before doing something you planned on doing. It's not a permanent consequence, but a very real, gameplay shaping consequence that allows brutal fighting have a civilized place in bestial nights of the dead. This is why you can really have two vampire friends who regularly flip out and tear each other's arms off and then are all like "uh, dude, im totally sorry about that last week, ok? it was not cool", and it allows your violent vampire archetype to be viable and fun to play. There aren't many games where you can engage in a joyful, terryfing muscle mash with your favorite NPCs, turning them into bloody meat, and not break the setting or make your character out of place.

                                                            Why I'm talking about this? If your player enjoys violence and combat in games, you might try to persuade him to drop the killer intent; killer intent breaks this whole social contract of "tonight we tear each other's arms off, but tommorow, tommorow we drink together"; it's a funny thing, but while optimizing to kill sounds like a natural thing to do for a combat character, it isn't really the best solution for a character that want's to participate in a lot of combat in Vampire, because the moment where you reach for flamethrower, for Kindred Dueling, and for Claws of the Unholy, you remove yourself from the accepted culture of nightly fight clubbing, and step into "murderer" territory. And when you go into murder territory, game starts to become much more binary; you live or die. In the world of muscle mash? You fight, you win, you lose, you break a bone, you tear off an arm, you go and unlive with it. There are many more possible outcomes in the world of self restrained bashing than in cutthroat lethality.
