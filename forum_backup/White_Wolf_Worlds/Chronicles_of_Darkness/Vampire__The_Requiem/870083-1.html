            Eh, I still disagree. Having studied all three Abrahamic religions in detail, the Lance still strikes me as uniquely Christian, in history, in world-view, in teachings. I can imagine being a Christian and being told "When Christ died, the soldier who killed him was cursed, and we follow the teachings of the soldier who killed him because he got revelations from God." The answer to that is "Ok, sure." And then the Christian becomes a Longinian. If I'm Jewish, it might involve a "Ok, not sure why Christ is so special, but ok." And then the Jew becomes a Longinian. If I'm Muslim, it goes "Ok, sure, but why is his revelation still valid?" To accept that it is valid, I have to accept that Muhammad is no longer the seal of the prophets, have to recant the Shahadda, and thus have to STOP being a Muslim before I become a Longinian. This specific point here is where things go wonky. The dogma FOLLOWING this point may be uniquely Longinian, but the basic premise of it, the premise that the revelations of someone who is not Muhammad are still valid, excludes Muslims.

            Do you see where I'm going with this? The Longinian faith is solidly an Abrahamic faith (belief in the Abrahamic God), one whose origin story (and subsequent development) is inextricably tied with Christianity. It may be a unique Abrahamic faith that is meant solely for the undead, but that doesn't erase it's ties and pre-requisite beliefs.
            The Banu Shaitan have a similar issue. They have to claim that a revelation AFTER Muhammad is valid (outside of the Mahdi, a special case), and thus renounce that Muhammad is the last prophet, and that's a sticking point.

            Now, specific points of dogma may contradict with Christianity or Judaism, such as the wiping of sin that you pointed out above. But that's a specific point of dogma, one that is, to my knowledge, debated in Christianity anyhow. The basic premise that the dogma is built upon, that there is a reason to follow Longinus at all, does not contradict Christianity or Judaism. You can be a Longinian, and still believe that Christ was the Son of God, and that his coming was to replace the Old Testament with the New, or you can be a Longinian and believe that Christ was merely a new prophet, but you cannot be a Longinian and still believe that Muhammad's revelation is the final word of God.

            Edit: plus looking over my previous posts on the subject, I forgot the point that you would have to deny that the Qur'an is the literal word of God, as the Qur'an literally states that Jesus never died but was uplifted from the cross alive into heaven.

                                        Even if you call it a different covenant with a different history, they're likely going to have the same overall archetype as the Lance, as well as the same overall teachings. And, honeslty, the vast majority of players I'm familiar with don't really care that much about a grand history that you write up for a group. At best, its a footnote. They care about the night-to-night operation, how it informs their characters, and the personalities involved. Of course, they also care about the benefits.

                                        So, from a practical, game design standpoint, what exactly is different here beyond the history and the religious overtones? Because that's something that should be different for every city you devise, and for every covenant. Are the factions notably different? Any benefits that should be different? The reasons to join? How they act when in power or out of power? What the goals are? How the priests and laiety act?

                                        At least, when Ancient Mysteries / Bloodlines covered vampire-voodoun, the highlighted stark differences from the Circle beyond the superficial. We have different practices, different belief systems, different benefits, different relationships, different reasons to join, different drives, different goals.

                                        Its easy to say "Oh, they should be their own covenant!" Its harder to put in practice. So, Ancient Mysteries covered a look at the Muslim version with their own book (modified Qaran) and didn't worship Longinus. They have a unique history. What exactly should be different about the Benu Shaitan to make them even further different than what's mentioned? Because history and trappings seem to be already there. What else?

                                        As far as I'm concerned, this is where the conversation fails. People want a different covenant for Islam, but never explains a difference beyond a reflavored Qaran as the basis instead of the Testament of Longinus, and a different history. We have both with the Benu Shaitan, but that's apparently not enough. So, lets start. In fact, I would absolutely love to hear from an actual Muslim or Jew on their take of a vampire version of their faiths. But, while I've had Hindu gaming friends, I've never actually met a Muslim or Jewish gamer to talk with. I've never seen any on this board who want to speak up about the matter, possibly because they're afraid of backlash. All I've ever seen are armchair theorists who cliam some background in study, but never anyone who actually practices.

                                        So, when all is said and done, I want to hear it. What exactly would be different here in a way that stands out in a game?

                                        Also, I feel that there's a theme of anti-semitism here, giving that people are happy to shove the Jewish into the Longinian church, while we have to treat Islam special. Simply treating the Jewish as pre-Christians is insulting to their cultur and beliefs. You either have to consider all three Abrahamics as separate, or none. Anything else is just marginalizing and insulting.
