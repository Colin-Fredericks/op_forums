                  I don't see why it wouldn't work any worse than such arrangements (factional rule) do in real life. The key is to ensure that you, as the organizing leader:

                  1. Know the interests of the parties involved, and that
                  2. They agree that working together is better than outright warfare.

                  Generally any congressional or parliamentarian structure ends up with a series of power blocks in play representing people with generally aligned viewpoints and interests, who then horse-trade to some degree of parity and/or irrelevance. VtR's Covenants are not, as a rule, set against each other's throats in a permanent fashion, though certain Domains may have long histories of bad blood to work through.

                  That's... kinda the fun of the vampire political game--untangling such (or, tangling...).

                      I've been in a mixed PbP/TT game for 6 years and running that has had a (Carthian Experimental) Council as the ruling Praxis.

                      Now, a lot of its foundation is because of the history. We're playing in Philly (original American capital), so tossed in some historical characters from the past. Matthew Quay was a big RL political boss, so we made him an Invictus who ran a Praxis with a (corrupt) board of directors since the civil war. Eventually, Carthians came down from New York in the eighties and agitated while Quay was trying and failing to deal with globalization and the loss of his union power. Then ensued a ten-year Praxis battle which saw the Carthians actually win and and gain the Prince/Praxis spot. That Carthian Prince ruled for another 10 years in a pretty traditional Praxis (Sheriff, Scourge, Harpies) etc. The first chapter (first game really) had the Prince voluntarily step down to form the experimental Council with the city all reacting to this new reality.

                      The Carthian's allies went with it. And even their enemies went with it, since they actually ended up getting more rights/power in the Council system then they had under the Carthian Praxis. Plus, many thought the experiment might fail rather quickly, or they'd be able to sabotage it more easily than the previous full Carthian Praxis.

                      The Prince stepped down to act as "Steward" - he became a non-voting member on the Council, but breaks any ties and has the power to veto laws and proposals.

                      The Council itself is formed out of the Primogen and Prisci of the city.

                      Each Covenant Primogen was a full voting member of the Council. While each Priscus became an advising member - meaning while they didn't normally vote on issues, they could still propose laws and speak on issues.

                      It takes a simple majority to pass a law unless it is modifying the Bill of rights, in which case 2/3rds of the full Council (Primogen and Prisci) are needed to pass.

                      If the Steward vetoes a law, 2/3rds of the Full Council (Primogen and Prisci) are needed to overcome the veto.

                      The Kindred Bill of Rights:
                      • The Right to Sustenance: ​All Citizens have the right to hunt in the Racks of the City without payment to any Regent.
                      • The Right to Safe Haven: All Citizens may declare a Haven with the Factor. A Citizen acquires and supplants the rights of the local Regent over their registered Haven at no cost.
                      • The Right to Elysium: All Citizens have an expectation of clarity and acceptance at the dedicated Elysia of the City. No citizen may threaten, supernaturally influence, or assault others while at Elysium. Recognized Elysia are: City Hall and the Independence Malls, as appointed by the Keeper.
                      • The Right to Free Association: All Citizens may freely join or leave any recognized Covenant without repercussion. Covenants are forbidden from any retribution, punishment or vengeance against those changing or revoking affiliation.
                      • The Right to Representation: All recognized non-Probationary Covenants may appoint a Primogen (Voting) member to the ruling Council. All recognized clans may appoint a Priscus (Non-Voting) member to the ruling Council. Each Primogen and Prisci may appoint a deputy Whip. All appointments are done through processes chosen by the respective factions. Should a stalemate occur, such ties may be broken by the Steward in normal fashion.
                      • The Right to Consolation: All Citizens are allowed to register one Confidant who may pierce the Masquerade. Such Confidants may not be the target of threats, supernatural influence or assault by other Citizens. Citizens may be held responsible for the crimes of the Confidant.
                      • The Right to Appeal: Any punishment or civil forfeiture issued by an officer of the Court may be appealed to the ruling Council. The Ruling Council may refuse the appeal or schedule an appeal hearing for the Council.
                      The Council appoints the City officers, and with a simple majority can direct the officer to take specific actions, or can overrule actions the officer has taken on their own in the upkeep of their duties.

                      Kindred officers:
                      • The Harpies: There are two Harpies. One appointed by a simple majority of the Primogen, the second by a simple majority of the Prisci. They deal with Status, inter-domain diplomacy, and boon adjudication. In essence, the civil court. They may appoint a lesser Honored Second to assist as a subordinate officer.
                      • The Factor: Responsible for maintaining the Kindred Census (there is an enforced population limit of Kindred), for territory (havens, regencies and feeding grounds), and prosecuting any Citizen-on-Citizen crimes, which are then referred to the Sheriff for punishment. They may appoint a lesser Censor to act as a subordinate officer.
                      • The Sheriff: Responsible for matters of domain Defense, Intelligence, and Punishment of criminals found guilty by the Factor, Harpies or Council. A penalty of Final Death may only be passed down with a majority vote by all citizens. The Sheriff may appoint lesser Deputies to assist as subordinate officers.
                      • The Keeper: This position is a subordinate to and appointed by the Steward. They are responsible for the registration of property/sites (street level, not full regencies), the organization of official gatherings, and the appointment and enforcement of Elysia.
                      ---

                      The experiment itself has mostly worked. As stated before, even the Invictus sees this as a partial step to power. They had been backed into a corner before, and now they are at least at the table involved in the decision making. Plus, they are very good as legal bureaucrats, and give the Carthians a run for their money on using the Council format to advance their agendas. The Ordo Dracul like it because they can act very mercenary and profit on almost any given issue. They've sold votes and appointments quite publicly, and are currrently in the lead in terms of prime territory that they control. The Lancea and Circle are the least populated in the city. The Lance generally follows the lead of the first Estate, and the Circle generally acts against the Lance - so they usually kind of nullify each other.

                      Additionally, under the experimental direction of the Carthians, the Council has passed a lot of street level initiatives to increase the 'fertility' of the city itself. In game fluff this means Philadelphia is able to support more Kindred per-capita than most domains. Covenants are expected to contribute regular public works and investments to make sure that happens. So there's some pressure there - concern that if the Council and those measures are abandoned, less fertile hunting means more pressure on the domain itself in terms of hungry vamps and the Masquerade.

                      Another factor is that the Carthians are seen as the most physically strong of the Covenants right now, so any direct revolution doesn't have Vegas-odds of overcoming the Carthians in a stand up fight, and the Census-caps mitigate one Covenant's ability to swell their ranks in a meaningful fashion to change that dynamic.

                      However, the system is also not working in some key areas. Decisions are always slow, so the ability to quickly react to threats isn't there. The selling votes issue is problematic. An outbreak of Revenants is causing issues, and this chapter's antagonists are a pair or Judges who have dropped a Judgment Fast on the city, which earned them a Blood Hunt, but they are still at large. Some of the revenants have banded together and are attempting to be recognized as a new covenant, but they don't fit in the census-caps. And a Daeva is currently acting as Gangrel Priscus because he's the most physically strong in the city, and challenged the old Gangrel Priscus to a fight for the spot. No one really wants him on the Council, but there wasn't a process to boot someone in the original Bill of Rights / System, so they are kind of stuck with him until they can get 2/3rds for a rewrite (of which he is obviously a vote against).
