                            So, I've been thinking about a couple of different mechanics for the "Wave of Apathy" devotion in my California Xiao re-design. In both cases the power would affect a single target, as in the original Kingjan discipline, and would require a contested roll between the Xiao and her victim:

                            1. The target loses a number of willpower points equal to the Xiao's successes, up the a maximum of her nightmare dots. At the end of the scene, the victim regains any willpower lost to this devotion (there is something of a precedent for "refunding" willpower points like this, in the form of the 4-dot ability of the Riding the Wave style merit.

                            or

                            2. All of the target's dice pools are capped by their Resolve+Composure for the remainder of the scene. This is farther from the original effect of the discipline, but seems thematically appropriate.

                            In either case this would be a proprietary Xiao devotion requiring Nightmare 2.

                                                    After several years of running I've essentially dropped both Clans and Covenants entirely from Requiem. I replaced Clans with Bloodlines (converting the original 5 into Bloodlines themselves), which IMO are more intimate and better suited for a Tier 1 - 2 setting. Covenants were loosened, no longer assuming that all characters are in one of five distinct global structures. The end result is a Kindred society that is disparate, no longer bound together by common clans or organizations, with different regions featuring very different societal structures. It also allows a storyteller to get creative with single Bloodline Covenants, cults, weird crossover groups, and all sorts of other strangeness outside the 'core' presentation of the setting.

                                                    Highly recommended if you're looking for a more personalized take on the setting.

                                                        Clans prescribing the character's personality, dress, social standing, etc. is a very Masquerade thing. In Requiem they do no such thing. They're also largely a misnomer, as evidence suggests not only multiple origins for vampires, but often multiple origins for individual Clans. From an in-game perspective, it is likely there is actually no such thing as Clan as the Kindred understand it, they are just incorrectly assuming that because all of these vampires have similar powers they must share a common ancestor. As such, Clanless games are actually rather appropriate, but by default Clan exists as the package that delivers affinity Disciplines and inherent weaknesses.
