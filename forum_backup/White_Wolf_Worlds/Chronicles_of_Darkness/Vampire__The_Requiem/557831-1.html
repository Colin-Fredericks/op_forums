                                    Originally posted by Griautis View Post


                                    I'd say if a rock star get's embraced, he does whatever the hell he used to do. But due to his discipline spread he starts doing it in more clan-like way.
                                    That is true. But that's also an external way of looking at things. Twins, one armed with a roll of duct tape and a screwdriver, the other with a hammer and pliers, will solve a mechanical problem in different ways based on their tool set. That's external. Nothing wrong with that at all, of course.

                                    Harry Dresden, the Dark Knight, and Sherlock Holmes will handle a mystery differently, not just because they have different tools at their disposal, but because they have very different worldviews, and fundementally different psyches. Jethro Gibbs and Rorschach can both sneak around and beat up people effectively, but their internal dialogues are worlds apart.

                                    I have to figure that the Beast, the Other, or whatever the vampire calls it, is a more powerful force in a person's psyche than an addiction or mania that a mortal might have. It's bigger than a mere dietary restriction or UV allergy. It's a palpable presence that influences everything the vampire does. Without that it just becomes a game about supercriminals IMO.

                                    I guess what I like to see are descriptions that highlight, not what a character can do, but what they are inside that makes them do what they do. That sort of detail adds a great deal to my interest in a character.

It's no different from Buffy in that regard: just some other being possessing the dead person's corpse. Once again, it's a part of my worldview when it comes to identity and self, but an external force (the Beast) changing who someone is fundamentally is akin to that person being dead and something else walking in their place.

                                                            Tough less worried about the "soul death" thing, I go with Maina. V:tM used precisely this kind of reasoning, and was a great setting.

                                                            Until you notice that a majority of players will do the same characters. They change names, gender and dots, but a Toreador is like every other Toreador. You can go outside the stereotype, sure. But most players won't. I even saw a guy leaving the game table feeling insulted with the oppening speech of the ST, just because he said a Tremere Harpy would throw a party. The player stood up and walked away steaming and replying that "Tremeres don't party. I will play with someone who knows the game."

                                                            Ridiculous, but the more you give a stereotype to the clan, the more most players will stick to it as if it was sacred. And that because Masquerade never created any mechanical boon for "roleplaying right". As good as V:tM was, the stereotype got old, even with thirteen clans, plus clan-like bloodlines, plus Antitribu. In a game with five it will kill the game in no time.

                                                            The difference between "external" and "internal" is a perceived one. Everything you put a rule on is ultimately external until the player bring it into the character. Aspirations, Humanity and Touchstones, even Masks and Dirges aren't exceptions, by the way. The player, not the rules, will choose to bring this in-character or just use like a tool and them put aside. Even Disciplines can be brought in-character. Give someone the power of frightening the hell out of anyone else. It will be a matter of time before this power and its use start to inform that person about who she is.

                                                            All that aside, I do find the Clan Banes less than satisfying and I'm not done with this thread. The idea of using Humanity is good, because it highlights the strugle Human vs Beast. But I wouldn't give any bonus, just more generic penalties. I also liked the idea of linking the Banes to other stuff in the character sheet, exactly because it highlights who your character is BEFORE the Beast, and THEM lets the Beast twist things.

                                                            Daeva could be related to the Dirge, instead of Aspirations, or even to retaining the human Vice. Ventrue could be easily linked to the Mask, since it is a regal thing, I can see a Beast with dominating ambition parasiting the way the Mask works.

                                                            Or another idea that occurred to me, they all could relate to the Predatory Aura. That is the way a vampire makes his Beast manifest, so why not? The Banes could affect the Predatory Aura in a failed Humanity roll to keep the Beast onthe line, or even trigger the Aura without the vampire decision on certain situations. Mix the two, and you could have a Ventrue that exibits the Aura when gaining willpower from his Mask, and a Daeva doing so with the Dirge. The Beast don't change the person directly, but when the person does certain things it cames from the bottom of the soul to the fore, snarling.
