            Originally posted by Ventrue Life View Post
            I guess that leads me to a new question:

            What would you guys recommend if I want to guard my minions against a Ventrue's Dominate? How can I make sure a Ventrue isn't just going to walk up to my minion and use my own minion against me with Dominate? Countering his Dominate with my own Entombed Command could potentially work, as we discussed above. But what other options would I have? What if I don't have access to Dominate myself? How would a Mekhet protect his minions against Dominate meddling? How would a Nosferatu? A Gangrel?

            Or is Dominate literally so overpowered that there is no way I can protect my minions against it, other than telling all my minions to avoid eye contact? Which in no way is a solid protection, the Ventrue can just brute-force the eye contact with a grapple and hold move, or if the Ventrue also has 1 dot in Majesty he can just use Awe.

            And even if somehow a high-Resolve human with the Indomitable Merit manages to resist a Ventrue's attempt at Mesmerize, what's stopping the Ventrue from simply trying again? As long as the failure is not a dramatic failure, the human does not necessarily realize the Ventrue tried to mesmerize him. So the Ventrue can then just try again, and again, and again, and again, until the ghoul finally realizes something weird is going on. There's no Vitae cost to Mesmerize, so other than Storyteller intervention, there's nothing stopping a Ventrue from just trying over and over again until he succeeds.

            Of course, this only counts against humans. I'd argue a vampire is much more likely to pick up what's going on after 1 failed Mesmerize attempt, and respond accordingly.


            Or, to make this question an even broader question: How would an ST set up a murder mystery or some other kind of L.A. Noire style detective mystery when the Ventrue player can just Mesmerize every single suspect or human who might know something, and always guaranteed get all the information each individual has, including confessionals if the human is dominated to confess his crimes? The only hurdle I can personally think of is having another Ventrue pull the strings behind the scenes with Dominate (to counter the player's Dominate) or other kind of supernatural meddling with the memory of the humans. Surely there are other ways to prevent Dominate from being the end-all be-all tool in a detective mystery?
            You assume your minions can be subverted and plan accordingly. Therefore you don't entrust them with information that you want to keep confidential. If you need them to remember something at a critical juncture, you erase their memories and set them to be restored via trigger phrase or action. If you want to get byzantine, you install pawns with contradictory and false information.

            A cumulative -1 penalty to successive attempts after failing prevents trying over and over.

            Mechanically I would just port over Conditioning from 1st edition. It assigns a penalty to Dominate use on anyone who isn't the conditioner.

            You can engineer a Resilience devotion to make your minions resistant to all Dominate effects.

            One game I played in my character's sire had a devotion called widdershins, that turned the next successful activation of a mind or emotion activating power against the user to the would be victim. The user became Majestied, Dominated, Nightmared, Animalism frenzied etc. at the bidding of the victim. (It only took one instance of a younger Ventrue fucking with his ghoul and having it reversed on her at a semi formal elysium gathering to inform everyone that his minions were not to be screwed with.)

            As for your murder mystery. Create extraneous characters that don't know anything and have zero involvement in the story and add some red herrings. Better yet, have a few people confess to it in order to protect someone else. (Bonus points if one of those confessions is based on false memories implanted by a Ventrue or scrambled by a Mekhet with a fetish for triggering traumatic memories in someone.) After wasting several hours and gallons of vitae chasing through dead end clues, maybe your Ventrue player will realize that perhaps his time will be better spent looking for more evidence before mind raping everyone. But honestly, you should be more worried about the Mekhet simply using Auspex to find out everything.

                        Originally posted by Ventrue Life View Post
                        What would you guys recommend if I want to guard my minions against a Ventrue's Dominate?
                        Blind/deafen them.
                        Use animal minions.
                        Ghoul them and teach them Obs.
                        Keep your minions away from them.

                        Other than that, not much. Once the Ventrue is in a room with your folks and free of time constraints, they are eventually gonna break through. As mentioned above, 1E Conditioning gave some resistance buffs, but that got stripped with 2E. I think there may have been some Carthian Book devotions that did a similar thing, but I'd need to go research that for sure.

                        Originally posted by Ventrue Life View Post
                        Or, to make this question an even broader question: How would an ST set up a murder mystery ... Surely there are other ways to prevent Dominate from being the end-all be-all tool in a detective mystery?
                        This speaks to me, as one of my players went heavy on Dominate and I've needed to get creative to keep mysteries and mortal roadblocks a challenge. At the end of the day, though, yeah - you need to accept that Dominate is a game changer. But so is Auspex, and at least Dominate can be resisted each step of the way. Most Auspex abilities can't be resisted at all. You will not be able to run basic mysteries when a clever players brings Dominate into it. However, on a meta level, it isn't much different than the Celerity character who isn't going to be challenged by basic combat anymore either.

                        First step is talking with the player/ST to figure out limits. This kind of thing will escalate pretty quickly if it isn't handled in the social contract, and nipping the 'Well, I'll just buy this other ability to short-circuit through your plots!' is better handled there than with mechanics.

                        As for in-story efforts:

                        Go abstract with the investigation. Dominate just becomes another avenue for collecting Clues rather getting perfect insight into crimes/motivations.

                        You can have witnesses be inaccessible. My player had an antagonism with a more powerful Kindred, decided to mess with his stuff, walked right up to his security guard, Dominated them to gain access to the site, did a bunch of political/physical damage to the location. The next time he showed up to do something similar, he was met with video screens and intercoms rather than real human security at the gates. Can't Dominate a TV. You can have folks in jail or abroad so the mystery has to be followed up by phone / email.

                        Remember the Masquerade. If the character is brute forcing things instead of taking a lot of time to be creative / subtle, that can bite them. Lost visage will mostly protect them personally from cameras, but not audio recordings or the like.

                        Remember Humanity. You are absolutely within your rights to impose a penalty on breaking point checks for removing free will. There are diminishing returns on this since a bane or a point or two drop will put them outside the danger zone. But also keep in mind putting the player on the hook for any crimes / damage to the person is also fair game. I had my player make some checks after they caused a shootout between some cops and criminals, even though he didn't technically pull any triggers.

                        Mortals can also be protected. If you are messing with people's assets, for example if you need to Dominate the DA, that person might be a minion or at least under the purview / domain of an established Kindred. It won't stop the Dominate in the moment, but the other Kindred could have a case before the Prince, Harpies, etc and political consequences could easily come down.

                                                    5. Nothing. If there are no other forms of perception at work (a moment on this point), then you have a perception check and/or attack penalty as if Blind.

                                                    Personally if the Obfuscated vampire is known to be/have been at that spot I'd keep the penalties down to Blind. If the Obfuscee is actively relocating themselves then that's an Active Defense and will make them virtually impossible to hit.

                                                    Keep in mind that there are ways to detect the presence of an Obfuscated vamp that do not rely on sight: you may note their changing footprints in the dust (or muck) on the ground. You may find their passage in swirling fog or smoke. You may detect squeaks and such as they cross the floor. Etc.

                                                    Obfuscate only seems impenetrable--like Dominate or Auspex or any of the Disciplines--if you interpret it broadly. Keep things narrow and literal so you, as GM, have mental wiggle room to plan around the powers. That said, they are, indeed, powerful: the intent has been to make it easy for vampires to be inhuman and abusive, at a cost to their Humanity of course.

                                                            Ventrue Life consider for the moment how you would go about telling if that shadowy owl-form was counting rice or not? I mean, realistically? Is it picking them up with its ?beak? and sorting them?

                                                            It seems like a straightforward interpretation would hold that the shell inherits uncommon banes and only protects against against those inherent to the condition of being a Strix.
