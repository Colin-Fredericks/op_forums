    Diablerie and Auspex

    09-17-2016, 12:44 PM
    Diablerie is probably the most unaceptable crime among kindred society. I'm wondering about how long the tracks of diablerie are detectable through Auspex? Forever or just for the duration of the tainted condition? If the amaranth is always detectable hohard the life for the vampire should be? can you give me some advice about it?

      Since the question is "Is this person a Diablerist?" and not some variant of "Do they have the Tainted condition?" I'd rule that you can always detect it.

      Some ways to make this less miserable for a Diablerist that I have heard in the past are as follows:

      1. Many Domains make Diablerie legal in certain circumstances, such as the target of a Blood Hunt. I dislike this personally, since Diablerie is more than just murder. It's devouring a soul. I can't fathom vampires being okay with it ever, not even in special circumstances.

      2. Without additional evidence, it's your word versus the accuser's. Nobody but another vampire with Auspex can corroborate the story. People are probably loathe to trust vampires with Auspex at their word without evidence because: you can't really be sure they even have Auspex without questioning them repeatedly on things they couldn't otherwise know, they could be lying even if they have Auspex, and supporting their accusations now could make them more credible if they come around and accuse you later. I prefer this explanation. Even if a Mekhet knows you are a Diablerist, that doesn't mean they can do anything about it or that anyone will believe them.

                      Originally posted by Marcus View Post
                      Diablerie is probably the most unaceptable crime among kindred society. I'm wondering about how long the tracks of diablerie are detectable through Auspex?
                      I personally say for the duration of the Tainted Condition; alternatively, if they're still addicted to Diablerie. The power is Aura Reading, not "Past Crime Reading." If you're not Tainted or Addicted, then your aura has been cleaned out. No reason I can see to pick up somthing that's no longer there in one's aura. Granted, getting rid of the addiction is nearly impossible - there's a reason we say that someone is a recovering addict, not "no longer an addict." But I can see excessive uses of torpor and some Disciplines that could work to that effect.

                      But, really, the best answer to this question is "What works best for your game?" Maybe your Prince has Auspex, and given that she's the final authority anyways, can easily make a judgement on the matter. Or perhaps someone uses Dominate or Theban Sorcery to force the issue. So, in order for a certain plots with diablery to work, you need to have a certain way that all this works. Making broad, sweeping generalizations about this issue isn't really a good idea.

                      Or, better yet? Deliberately leave the question unanswered and have the characters at a loss. They suspect this person committed diablerie. Auspex 2 says "no." But, under Dominate, she says "yes." Does Tainted have an effect? Was her memory twisted? Plausable Deniablity or one of the sorceries in play? Strix? Something else? Mysteries and doubt are a good thing, and leaving the answer open gives you more flexibility as an ST, while having no answer leads to better plot hooks as a player. There's doubt in the air. What do you do?

                          Dramatic failure. Auspex is unreliable so I doubt it is used to officially cast guilt on someone. If the Prince says, "By my power of Auspex, I declare that vampire a diablerist. Get 'em" then everyone would probably start looking for a new Prince unless "that vampire" just happened to have lots of enemies. I think it should always be detectable by Auspex because diablerie addiction is both automatic and permanent, as are the benefits received from diablerie. It is the counterbalance.

                          The life difficulties will be personal rather than societal. Some will fear or hate based on the rumors. Others will blow it off because even if it were true there are no circumstances to go with the charge. A person can be forgiven terrible deeds if their reason is good enough. Man A tortures then murders man B so man A must be evil. Except, man B raped, tortured and murdered man A's 5 year old child. Man A is not so bad anymore. Knowing a vampire is a diablerist is evidence that they are dangerous, but the vampire part should have done that already. I doubt most vampires go around fearing diablerie more than final death.

                          Then there are the strix. Since a diablerist reads the same as a strix, I imagine they need to get out of town if a strix is known to be around. Nobody wants a third nipple when a witch hunt starts.

                                      I've never liked the combination of being able to detect diablerie by Auspex and it being some unpardonable act in vampire society. Either one alone would be fine, but put the two together and it's tremendously limiting in scenarios, campaigns and characters. Auspex 2 is easy to get and whilst people make brave attempts to rationalize why it isn't useful (how do you know you can trust the person with the Auspex), such efforts go pop far too easily (the Prince has Auspex, someone the Prince trusts more than you has Auspex, your coterie mate has Auspex, some random other vampire has Auspex and even though it's not proof, tells people that you're a diablerist and that's enough to make people not trust you, the prince finds another vampire to give a second opinion just like a doctor can check another doctor's work... and so on).

                                      It feels to me like something Mark Rein•Hagen put in just to stop power-gaming players immediately setting about devouring all their elders. (Which to be fair, it works as). But imo, the number of plotlines and character choices that get shut down immediately by it, I prefer to make it so that it's not easily detectible. I think a lot of GMs tie themselves in knots trying to find reasons why a vampire with Auspex detecting it doesn't lead to a bloodhunt. Or else they end up with a setting where Diablerie becomes just a thing everyone accepts but nobody acknowledges. Like having watched a Michael Bay film.

                                      I prefer to just bite the bullet so to speak and say "can't detect", or at least make it require some exceptional rolling so people are never sure if they might have missed it. Now I'm no longer faced with my campaign becoming an endless attempt to justify not killing a PC whose aura screams devilry; or trying to figure out how my diablerist elder can keep their secret from everyone. If thine auspex eye offends thee, pluck it out.

                                      Fnord!
