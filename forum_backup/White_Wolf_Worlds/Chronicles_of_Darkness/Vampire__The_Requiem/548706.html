                              Thanks
                              Well, like tsuasi said, the default assumption is that you're going to engage in politics. That's kind of a necessary buy-in with Vampire. You're a social predator, swimming in a bloody tank filled with cut-throat sharks. Vampire is primarily a social game, that's its draw. So, when you're making characters, mostly you're going to be thinking about what kind of character you want to play, what your goals are, etc. So, if your goal is to amass power, then you're drawn to the Invictus, etc.

                              As for the draw? Every covenant comes with mystical bonuses. Even in 1st edition, this was true. There's huge benefits for no matter what way you go. Each covenant also represents a different kind of vampire. Want the classical pseudo-victorian aristocracy? Invictus. Want to play a new age vampire in touch with humanity and its evolution? Carthians. Golconda seeker? Ordo dracul. Children of Lilith or another monstrous being? Etc.

                              Each of the five covenants are more than just their mystical benefits; it wouldn't be the first time someone played a covenant member without buying any sorcery or merits. The covenants are iconic vampire personalities. They're the kind of vampires you get from the wide variety of stories out there. And that's the real point - you play a member of the Invictus because you want to be the Undead Masters of the Night. You want to play Carthians because you want to be the rebels. You play the Brood because you want to be a hellrazer. And so on.

                                  Players in my group tend to be drawn to the Covenants that will work with their character's goals. My players who loves magical stuff and pagan elements gravities towards the Circle of the Crone while my player who enjoys political interplay and business dealings leans towards the Invictus. Personally I really like the shadowy spy games of the Ordo Dracul as well as their focus on transcending the vampiric condition.

                                  Membership in a Covenant helps a player emphasis their character view of the world, if one plays to the stereotypes. It can also be a fun way to play against type, such as having a character who was forced into a particular group but favors another. That sort of thing works get for intrigue and espionage style games; playing on loyalties and personal convictions.

                                  For mechanical insentives to go with the Carthains or the Invictus: In 1E Carthians and the Invictus got nice discounts on certain merits and gained semi-mystical abilities in their respective Covenant books. In 2E both Covenants have a merit set that their members based on said semi-mystical abilities from the previous eddition. They also have a suite of non-mystical merits exclusive to their members (which other Covenants now have as well).

                                      You mentioned that you can get what Invictus gives you with just Allies and other social merits. This isn't really true. Or, it may be true, but it's also not entirely accurate.

                                      Allies gives you the ability to ask a few favors.
                                      Contacts lets you ask for information.

                                      Status, which is typically how membership is mechanically represented, gives you both of those. It also gives you access to the Covenant's resources and facilities. This is where the non-mystical Covenants shine.

                                      What does the Crone have for resources and facilities? A temple? A run-down shack or hole in the ground to sleep in?

                                      Ordo gives you a place to conduct experiments, maybe, sure.

                                      Invictus? Penthouses. Corporate ties. Blood-bound government officials and ghouled police officers. If you want power over the Kine, you go Invictus, because they have spent centuries setting up the strings to puppet the old kine power structures.

                                      Carthians are the same, I presume, but for the middle and lower classes. They may not have the raw political and financial power, but they have the ear of the masses.

                                      The power to call down favors from Bill Gates or your local Senator or whatever is not really any less than the power to summon a weapon made of your own blood. It's just more subtle. And where Covenant membership differs from social merits is flexibility. You can buy hundreds of Allies to mimic what the Invictus has access to in their power pool, or you can just ask your Covenant to help you get what you need.

                                      The "Eldritch Trifecta" simply does not have the pool of social contacts and material resources that the social Covenants do.

                                      It may not be a concrete "here is your magic spells" benefit, though they do get unique Merits, but it's still a solid benefit.

                                                          You can; it's just difficult. The Covenants guard their secrets pretty tightly. Generally the anger you insight by stealing from a Covenant is greater than the gratitude that you gain for selling those secrets so traitors are most common at the lowest levels. Weirdly, this is also the most useless level to be a traitor. Say you show up at the local Circle of the Crone, having freshly betrayed the Lanca et Sanctum and you present your basic knowledge of dark miracles. Their response? Eh, seen it. Somebody probably already stole that a long time ago and they didn't really find it that useful because it's onle the most basic power.

                                                          Usually to learn the bigger secrets (more than say one dot in blood sorcery or a single coil) you have to get invested in the Covenant. You get in what you put out, to borrow the Invictus saying. Once you are that deep into one of the Covenants it's unlikely that you really unlikely that you'd benefit too greatly from leaving it unless you ended up on an especially poor position. It's these usual situations, where something large might be betrayed, that can drive Covenant focused stories.

                                                          For example, there's an example NPC in the Ordo book. Now, rank in the Ordo is largely measured by your Coils and by having Sworn an Oath to the Ordo Dracul, which is basically joinning a subfaction within the order. So, there's this guy. He's old, powerful, knows tons of Coils but he hasn't Sworn an Oath for the Covenant, which is a big deal for the Ordo. Thing is, no one will let him become Sworn now because it'll jump his rank up to basically the top of the Covenant since Sworn outrank similarly Coiled members. So his attempt to get an Oath are being blocked and he can't get much higher in Coils alone. So he's stuck and getting frustrated. It's this kind of situation where you might get a traitor if one of the other Covenants make him a better offer. Problem is, by the time you get to that point, what you actually want to only available within the Covenant you've already committed to. This guy might be able to go to the Invictus and sell out the secrets of the Order for a ton of money, status, herd, the finer things in life. But what he actually wants is the respect and fear of his academic peers and to study transcending the vampiric condition and the Invictus, for all it's money, can't every really give him that.

                                                          As for the second, getting to an effective position within a Covenant takes alot of time, years for most individuals (PCs often being something of an exception). Sending somebody into the ranks of another Covenant to infiltrate it often ends one of two ways: the vampire never makes it very deep in and reports surface level intelligence or they get deep in through years of hard work and effort and eventually just decide to stay in their 'new' Covenant.

                                                          The only group that seems to regularly make efforts in get inside of the great Covenants is Belial's Brood and they have a specific reason to hide who and what they are and have a goal of tearing down the entire system of Covenants.
