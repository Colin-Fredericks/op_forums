    Asking for Advice on "rebooting" a Setting for a New Chronicle

    04-19-2016, 01:58 AM
    So it has finally happened for me. I have a hardcover copy of Vampire the Requiem: 2nd edition. I have it because I have two potential new players and will possibly be running a Chronicle again with more than one player for the first time in about 6 years.

    Now some backstory. I have always made my setting New York City, even before the Vampire the Masquerade source book: New York by Night was even a thing. When that source book came out I adjusted by my setting to fit in with what was going on in the book, making changes where it needed to be in order to make that book work for me. I then changed RP groups due to a move and then readjusted again to a new group and pushed the timeline back a bit to accommodate the new characters and allow them to fit into the large power struggles of the setting.

    When Vampire the Requiem and the NWOD was created I had lost my group again, and restructured NYC wholesale to fit the new setting (Sabbat characters became Lancea Sanctum, Anarch's became Carthians, Tremere became the Ordo Dracul, etc.) Damnation City made me make some more changes as well. But...

    Now I'm about to start with a new group. One out of the four players has been playing under me since VtR first came out and while she has "rebooted" her characters several times over the years, she is pushing me to make the NYC setting forward. She thinks that reverting it to a 2000 CE battle for control for who will be in charge is "playing it safe," while I think it allows for the new players to be involved in the biggest change in power of the setting as I have it written.

    And... I'm not sure what to do. The story I want to tell, doesn't require the power struggle of "How Will be Prince" but at the same time, I hate the idea of a setting in Vampire with no way to time jump forward. I'd rather have the setting in 1999 or even 1980 to allow the PC's to have time jumps that play to the ideas of immortality and history. But I get her point of getting out of the "comfort zone" of a story I've told 3 times. Once with Masquerade and twice now with Requiem.

    I'm already making changes to my conversions (for example Sabbat =/= Lancea et Sanctum) but I'm trying to decide where to set my new Chronicle. In the past, built on the stories of past player characters or in the past to allow the new players to have a deeper role.

    What do you all think? Should the Rotten Apple "reboot" or should it just continue on?

                  Well, what is stopping you from doing a new story that's still about vampire politics in a more current time? It seems to me that, if you're only doing this one plot to show flexibility, then its kind of creating an impression that this is the only point when you can have an impact, which would undermine your goal. Actually, can I ask a quesiton? Why are you opposed to a game set in 2010+?

                  Anyways, to be honest, I think that maybe your fiance has a good point. While politics was the major Vampire defining feature in Masquerade, and a lot of it carried over into Requiem 1e, its becoming increasingly less of a mainstay as we move into 2e. Vampire is still a social game, but the way that the social conflicts manifest aren't necessarily tied to politics. The central conflict of the game is "You're now an undead immortal. What do you do with your life? How do you stave off boredom? Make up for your sins?" Politics is something a couple of vampires do to answer that question. They insult and deal with each other because its something fun to pass the time with. Its a thrill. From the sounds of it, you might be stuck in a rut worn by Masquerade when there are other plots in Requiem 2e could be more enjoyable to experience.

                  So, I'm going to stick with my original answer. What story is the most fun for you? What kind of story do you want to explore? That's the most important thing to answer first and foremost.

That actually applies to all vampires, though less with drugs and sex like the Daeva, and more in other areas. Delving into progressively crazier occult conspiries if a Shadow, for instance.

                                  Requiem 2e has some rules for a generational chronicle in the ST part of the book. That might be something cool to play up as a plot. Or, if you have Sothis Ascends from Mummy, they've got some ideas on how to run a game using flashbacks as well, which would be cool to tie in.

                                  Well, there is a focus on politics with the Invictus and Carthians, much like the old Camarilla versus Anarchs. So, you can still have the politics around through that venue if the players want to go that route. That said, the Lancea Sanctum, the CotC, and the Ordo don't really care about politics. The former only cares about the ability to spread their religion, and the latter two just want to practice their faith/experiements in peace. And then you get strix who show up and want to just make a mess of things and have a good time.

                                  Each of the five covenants have built in reasons why others would want to keep them around. For instance, the Invictus Prince would want to keep a Carthian sheriff around because, to be frank, Carthian Law protects the Sheriff from magic. She's all but incorruptable. Having the Sheriff in the Prince's cotorie and feeding fromt the same territory means that poachers are instantly known. Meanwhile, the Invictus Prince can use Oaths to ensure that no one will voluntarily betray, and will empower their Sheriff. They synergize. The Lancea rituals are awesome at protecting and following other vampires, while the Circle has the best divination abilities to track down problems. They need each other to function within the city. Not quite sure what the Ordo offers, to be frank, but at least four of five need each other to function well.

                                  I lost where I was going with this. Umm... Politics can be a thing, but its only part of the game. There should be hooks that keep the others immediately interested and involved as well. Mysteries, dangers from the strix, arrival of the Jiang Shi (rules in 2e Requiem) and dhampir (rules coming in the ghoul book eventually). Requiem is a far stranger place than Masquerade, with other forms of undead running around their cities beyond just ghosts and regular vamps. One of my favorite little plots to make is a "revenant plague." The zombie apocalypse is a real threat in Vampire, with how the Curse spreads. You could end up with a whole city of hungry dead corpses running around if you're not careful.

                                  Thanks to VII, you have all this strangeness herded directly into NYC. You have more coming in from overseas. If I might make a suggestion, I would showcase this stuff to help highlight how much the CofD is different from the WoD. Vampire poltiics are a thing, but they are far more complex than in 1e/Masquerade. The world is far stranger at night. Consider how all these things will throw a monkey wrench into your plans.

                                  One of the reasons the Strix exist is specifically because they're designed to upset apple carts. If your city is stable, then the strix hear about it, start gathering, and cause problems. They literally cause misfortune by their mere presence, and not have to do anything themselves. In fact, these guys can help show how politics are dynamic, because the strix literally force change to happen with the Kindred. They're the ultimate bored-elder, who deliberately cause problems with gleeful sadism, but can be handled by the players.

                                  Another idea is to check out Reap the Whirlwind. That's actually a pretty "political" storyline to follow, but a twist. Instead of the "cast out the tyrannt prince" story, it starts off with the Prince already dead, and the fall out. Personally, I'm not a fan of the story, but others like it, and could help with inspriation. And it was given out with free rpg day, so you should be able to get it free off drivethru, if memory serves.

                                              The following is my experience with the Owls.

                                              The strix from Requiem for Rome do have some surface similarities to the ones in 2e, but they really act like a completely different beast (pun intended). In RfR, the strix were out for vengence. In 2e, they're more like the Belial's Brood and sixth clan. Yeah, they're all shadowy, but its rare for them to actually stay that way - strix are body-hoppers. 9 times out of 10, the players will encoutner a strix already possessing a corpse. The shadow part escapes if they don't die via fire, and jumps into a new body, in many ways acting like an elder with Dominate 5 possessing people to face the other vampires. When vampires do encounter one of the Strix outside of a corpse, the Owls serve as more of a demon-tempter than antagonist at that point. Offering forbidden knowledge, revenge at a price, etc. I honestly recommend against thinking of them as anything related to Rome other than as an interesting historical footnote. Too many story aspects and mechanics are different.

                                              As for VII and strix... that's just a plot hook for investigating VII. There's plenty of examples of VII doing things without the strix, and the Owls as a whole couldn't care less about VII.


                                              In either case, the strix are a handy tool to have in stories and are one of the major things that differniate 2e Requiem from the traces of Masquerade, but they're not omnipresent everywhere. So, while they can make a great plot hook, they don't have to be a major part of your city design.
