    Two questions from a Vampire newbie

    09-03-2015, 05:39 AM
    Hello!

    I have Vampire: the Requiem book at home, but I have yet to play or GM it. Nevertheless, I tried considered both - and there are two questions that I struggle with:

    1. When coming up with possible characters, I started to wonder: why would anyone actually decide to become a vampire? I mean... being vampire obviously sucks, because you have to literally prey on human beings. Wouldn't that turn almost anyone off the idea of allowing themselves to be Embraced? It would seem to me that only complete psychopaths and very desperate people would be willing to become vampires... How do you have your characters rationalize it, then?

    2. When I was considering GM this game, I realized that I... have no idea what Vampire games could actually be about. It's a very rich setting, but what stories can you actually tell in it? Could you share any ideas?
    Tags: None

      1. Immortality (for young people - being young forever, for older people - not dying), power - being strong, fast, virtually indestructible etc., amazing tricks - dominate, majesty, obfuscate - who doesn't want to be able to hypnotize people at will And those are just three possible things. I assume that for each person it would be something different, but if given the choice I guess that most people would at least seriously consider it.
      2. You can make it about whatever you want - politics is usually the favourite, but there are some alternatives:
      love separated by the embrace
      loneliness (you can't really hang around your old friends anymore)
      madness (you become too strong for your own good)
      or just good old detective game, where your prince asks you to figure out a mystery and you have to use your powers to find the guilty person or a missing object.
      Again, obviously these are just some of the things you could use, and perhaps you'll have the most fun when you use them all in one game

              In addition to the stated answers to #1, there's the obvious: a lot of them don't. If a vampire has decided to Embrace you, there's little you can really do to stop it. Posthumous Embraces and second edition's revenants reinforce that further. Once you have already been Embraced, it becomes a very different question, because now it's about personal survival and the fear of eternal death. Even if you don't want to spend eternity as a parasite, it takes a great deal of courage to intentionally leave yourself out for the sun. And the taste of sating the hunger is seductive. It becomes easy to rationalize to yourself over the years. Maybe you can live a good enough unlife that you can make up for what damage you do. Maybe you can feed relatively safely, on willing vessels or on vermin, and get by that way. From there, the temptations start by inches.

              Most of those old, callous elder vampires started out as relatively reasonable, empathetic people. But they got put into a situation designed to turn men into monsters.

                  The Embrace isn't usually performed with the consent of the character that is about to be made a vampire.                           I'd do it. Hands down. For the sake of answering your question, here's why (plus a few extras):
                          You don't actually have to hurt anyone. You can feed from animals. Even if you don't, there are plenty of people that would willingly get bit once they know what it feels like. It's not harm if its consent. Now, if you feed from more than animals, there's a chance you'll kill someone. The chance gets bigger as you get older. But you can minimize that chance, and you also have a chance to kill someone just driving your car. A lot of people on this forum seem to disagree with me on the "you can feed without harming" thing, but the point is I believe it. It's pretty clear that there are others in the World of Darkness who could believe the same. Given that this is a matter of reasons and rationals, I'd like to avoid yet another debate on the matter.

                          in addition, there's the promise of a world that few people have ever seen. The World of Darkness so to speak. There are people driven by curiosity, especially towards the unlives of something so iconic as the vampire. Imagine the interviewer in Interview with a Vampire. Anne Rice vampires seem to have it far worse for far less benefit in the movie than VtR vampires, and he still jumped at the chance. There are also people desperately in need of the idea that there's more to this world than the daily toil of a 9-5 until you retire and then die. Vampires prove that there's more to this world. Being one provides an escape from that drudgery.

                          There's always wealth and power (physical and mystical) and immortality, too. Fear of death is potent.

                          As other people have mentioned, there's nothing that says you have to consent.

                          Most importantly, perhaps: Nobody ever said that your Sire had to be completely honest before he or she sires you. That'd be breaching Masquerade a bit too far for someone who isn't a vampire. There are so many myths about being a vampire that the average human doesn't really know what it entails until they're told or they experience it themselves. Nobody says they have to be told before they're sired.

                          As for campaign ideas?
                          For VtR I usually have Neonates doing the bidding of their elders. Investigating Masquerade breaches are one. Investigating in general, really. One player is Ordo Dracul. He gets sent to a Wyrm's Nest by his boss. The other players are sent along to make sure that the Ordo isn't overstepping their bounds by keeping forbidden Lancea knowledge or whatever. A Revenant is raised and attacking unaware Kine. The players have to find him and deliver him to Final Death or maybe the Prince if the Prince needs him alive for some reason.

                          That's if you need a more standard "quest"-based campaign. A lot of people have suggested to me that you should make a roster of NPCs, give them all aspirations, then set the PCs loose. Between the NPC aspirations giving them reasons to ask the PCs for help and the PCs aspirations giving them reasons to ask the NPCs for help, you should have what you need. The benefit to this method is you'll probably have more time for dealing with Touchstones and whatever personal projects the PCs want. The downside is you can probably give up on the idea of keeping the party together all the time.

                                              I was thinking about some character concepts and energy conservation and it doesn't say you have to wake up. So if you were a party person you could just wake up on Friday and Saturday nights. That way you wouldn't waste time and Vitae.

                                              I'd probably do it myself. Immortality is a big draw, magic powers help. But as stated above most people don't really have a choice. It's the vampire that decides for them. I was reading the Beast Within anthology and the first story was a tale about a girl with no place left to go begging a vampire to turn her because of it, while he gnashes and snarls over the decision because he wants to but it's against the Prince's rules. It seemed silly the vampire didn't think of the third option, ghouling, which was likely allowed, would have brought the girl into the All Night's Society but not cursed her. It was a Masquerade book so the curse angle was played up more.

                                              Now, appreciating that a vampire didn't necessarily choose to become a vampire, as for what vampire games are about, at the basics you have, 'What do you do when you're dead?' How do you spend your Requiem. Ideally for me, a storyteller weaves a tale to tempt and challenge your vampire's mindset on that. Is your vampire going to decide to only eat animals? Are they willing to do the bidding of the Collective, who control local pounds and slaughterhouses, to do so? Especially when, after that last frenzy, human blood tastes so good, and it's all around you in easy-to-drink containers practically begging to be popped open. Look at Mia, she's so commanding and confident, she doesn't scrounge in alleys sucking on rats, maybe she's got the right way about it. And repercussions and consequences are highlighted, what happens when the Collective decides one job isn't enough? Or it turns out the victim of your last frenzy is part of a werewolf pack and now you're hunted and might need to increase your vitae supply just to survive. And of course I like to throw in an overarching story or two that doesn't focus on the characters in particular but serves to highlight themes of vampire or the location they're in or other things, like the inter-covenant warring leading to a head and an all-out war, an usurper to the title of Prince, a drastic change of policy that may change the vampire's city as they know it completely, for better or worse.

                                              But that's just how I do it, there's really a lot of ways to go about that sort of thing, a story can place an emphasis on the vampire's attempts to cling to their human life despite their changes, a 'quest' of sorts to find their sire and stare them down as you ask them, 'Why?' or any number of stories with any number of ways to tell. You don't even have to limit it to vampires and can throw some other supernatural templates into the mix.

                                                  Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                                  1. When coming up with possible characters, I started to wonder: why would anyone actually decide to become a vampire? I mean... being vampire obviously sucks, because you have to literally prey on human beings. Wouldn't that turn almost anyone off the idea of allowing themselves to be Embraced? It would seem to me that only complete psychopaths and very desperate people would be willing to become vampires... How do you have your characters rationalize it, then?
                                                  One, you don't need to be a psychopath to be willing to kill someone to survive yourself. Serial killers come from all walks of life, and for many reasons, not just psychosis. And the pay out is rather nice - immortality. So, no. The thought of preying on another human won't turn off people from being a vampire. Its a cost, yes, but not one that people can't live with. If you want to really get into it, there's a significant number of people that leech off others for their own wealth and prosperity - just this week, we have a Detroit city priest arrested for embezzling money from a church that was earmarked for charity work. Drug dealers kill people with poison for money. We can make some comparisons to the really wealthy if we wanted to.

                                                  Two, when you're young and newly vampized, you can live off animal blood. Or avoid killing people if you're strong enough of will. Sure, it will eventually happen at some point. But the feeling is that, if you're careful, you can avoid it. Eventually, over time, you come to peace with drinking from humans as your mind changes over time.

                                                  Three, people glamorize the upsides and downplay the drawbacks. Immortality and disciplines are very nice. As is immunity to disease - if you're dying and don't believe in an afterlife, undeath seems like a pretty awesome alternative to not living at all. There are lots of positives to being a vampire, and its hard to really understand the drawbacks without experiencing them.

                                                  Four, you can become a vampire in any number of ways. There's the revenants/larvae that rise from overfeeding, there are post mortem embraces, there are embraces where your sire is punishing you. Maybe you were a ghoul for a long time and its become too much of a strain to maintain that state, and time to try something new instead of dying.

                                                  2. When I was considering GM this game, I realized that I... have no idea what Vampire games could actually be about. It's a very rich setting, but what stories can you actually tell in it? Could you share any ideas?
                                                  There's plenty of possibilities. Vampires live in the dark side of humanity, the seething underbelly of crime, taboo desires, drugs, abuse, and rampant sexuality. The slow slide away from humanity into predator. There's the politics, trying to find feeding grounds, the struggle of opposing philosophy. A new way to explore the new World you've been shown. A struggle of dark forces against pure evil (Brood, VII). Explore how necessary evils are needed for society to thrive. Use vampires as metaphors for modern racial concerns. I've done games where vampires came out of the coffin and were revealed to the public at large. That was fun.
