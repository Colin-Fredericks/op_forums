            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
            I probably wouldn't allow Striking Looks to apply, regardless of what form the ghoul's or touchstone's striking appearance takes. Everyone looks like lunch to a Frenzying vampire.
            Agree to Disagree here. I think Striking Looks and/or physical appearance in general could easily be a discerning factor in determining beast's desires. Trying to decide which prey is the most appealing, which threat is the most dangerous, which rival presents the greatest challenge - there can be a lot of physical cues from how someone looks in any of those choices. Saying a frenzying Kindred fundamentally ignores all physical cues - that all prey looks the same - I don't think is supported.

            There are other factors too - who's closer, kindred vs. human vs. animal, blood potency - but I wouldn't flatly discount the purely visual ones.

            That being said, I would play it both ways. Striking Looks might make you more a target of frenzy in some cases, too.

                Originally posted by lnodiv View Post
                Why on earth would being pretty help you talk down a vampire about to frenzy?
                " Effect: Your character is stunning, alarming, commanding,
                repulsing, threatening, charming, or otherwise worthy of
                attention                . Determine how your character looks and how people
                react to that. For one dot, your character gets a +1 die bonus on
                any Social rolls that would be influenced by his looks. For two
                dots, the benefit increases to +2. Depending on the particulars,
                this might influence Expression, Intimidation, Persuasion,
                Subterfuge, or other rolls. "

                Whether or not Striking Looks allows you to influence people by giving a bonus to social rolls.... It's right there as RAW.

                "“I would sooner destroy a stained glass window as an artist like yourself. However, since I can't have you following me either.”"

                I assume that it could mean ( in this context ) that you believe your touchstone to be a beautiful thing and you would not want to destroy something so beautiful. Or perhaps the beauty distracts you from your rage even if it is momentarily. Or like Holy said it isnt just "pretty" it is a lot of things. But again as RAW it's right there. it gives you +1 or 2 on social rolls.. Talking down a vampire is a social roll. That pretty much should end the debate right there.

                Also just because your looks are striking doesnt mean you are "whoring yourself" I think that is a pretty big leap there. It could be the thing of beauty or hope in all that blood soaked mess.
                Getting your frenzied vampire to notice you with awe is almost the same thing as getting a vampire to notice you by using your looks... They are both bolstering Presence rolls.

                EDIT: For better or worse: It is a proven fact that people are more likely to get what they want based on their looks. Thousands of studies have been done on this...

                                                            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                            This is, I think, a point where people are going to differ on what Striking Looks can or can't do. To me, "Innocent" is too subjective to be a Striking Looks quality. You can have Striking Looks: youthful face, and some people will be more inclined to assume your innocence, but others might not. You can have Striking Looks: nasty scar, and some people might be intimidated by that while others might be turned on.                                                             Effect: Your character is stunning, alarming, commanding, repulsing, threatening, charming, or otherwise worthy of attention.
                                                            All of these are subjective and are the direct book examples under the merit description. I think 2E moved toward a more subjective mechanic than the old 1E mechanic. It no longer specifically calls out a directly physical component - I would say much like the Nosferatu Flaw switched from oWoD to nWod from physical appearance to a general social penalty which CAN include physical appearance.

                                                            But you are correct that it's situational:

                                                            Depending on the particulars, this might influence Expression, Intimidation, Persuasion, Subterfuge, or other rolls.
                                                            If you are using any of the above skills during a talking down (or any other) roll, then Striking Looks might apply.

                                                            Edit: But totally, if your game decides that Striking Looks is a purely physical attribute like a Scar or Youthful Face, AND you play that the beast doesn't notice those kinds of nuances - then you are totally justified in saying a Striking Looks bonus doesn't apply to the talking-down roll.
