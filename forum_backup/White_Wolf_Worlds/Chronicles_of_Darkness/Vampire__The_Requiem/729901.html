          I wouldn't expect most vampiric legal systems to be complex. If a prince doesn't want his subjects doing something he must tell them a) what it is b) how they'll work out who did it and c) what will happen to who did it. It may be subjective, but the prince probably doesn't care how future authorities would interpret his laws because he has no intention of losing his position. That's assuming they get round to writing one up before or after seizing power, but it's something to aim for.

                  Constitutions and feudalism don't mix. Feudalism requires that the people (subjects) are property of their ruler. Constitutions were created to completely throw that out. That isn't to say that people have no rights under feudalism. Their rights are determined by the ruler, however. A feudal society is a dictatorship, benevolent or not. I'm not sure that vampires are actually using feudalism. It is probably some quasi-feudalism. A constitutional monarchy has all the noble titles as well. It would be up to you to decide where you want to fall between those two ideas.

                  I think a legal system starts with defining the rights of the people subject to the laws. What are people entitled to? What are people allowed to do? What limitations can be placed upon people? What is the procedure to do so? There may be different strata in a society with groups of people having different answers to those questions. I would avoid the minutia in codifying every law and just go with those basics I mentioned. You can add the small details on the fly. As long as they are within the broad guidelines previously established, there shouldn't be a major issue.

                                              I'm calling it the Dictus Bellum

                                              By this signing, the primogen and prince of the city of Dallas do establish laws to better preserve the society of the Kindred.
                                              1. The domain of the Prince is by right of rank the whole of the city and its districts. The divisions of territory is to be determined by right of control, though the prince retains the right to give and revoke territories as his or her discretion.

                                                  Originally posted by Chattnos View Post
                                                  Magna Carta?
                                                  That was an example of a king giving rights to subjects. The US Constitution was new because it legally changed the people of a nation from subjects to citizens. A person born in Britain or one of her colonies was British and therefore a subject of the British crown. Such a person could not declare independence, a person was either a loyal subject or a traitor. US citizens can (and always could) give up citizenship to become citizens of another nation without penalty. Generally subjects can't move to another territory without permission. The life of a serf is the life of a slave with rights. A beautiful prison remains a prison.

                                                      Seems like a pretty straightforward outline of a feudal(-ish) power structure. One thing which I think may be missing, however, is a statement of the prince's duties or responsibilities. Generally speaking, leaders have to offer something to their subjects in exchange for their obedience, particularly when those subjects form significant power blocks of their own. Enforcing the rule of law is a good start, since even most vampires probably don't want to live in a genuinely lawless society. Protection is another thing a prince might offer, both from outsider vampires who might try to make a move on local territories and from the various mortal institutions that might otherwise make unlife hard for the local kindred.

                                                      Indeed, one thing I like about the second edition portrayal of the covenants is that they emphasize the Invictus' role as a "conspiracy of silence" who influence the mortal world in order to maintain the masquerade. It helps justify their position on top of the kindred totem pole, since they're ostensibly using the power and influence they accrue in order to preserve kindred society as a whole, rather than just wallowing in champagne swimming pools or whatever. Not that they can't do that too, but it's not the only thing they do.
