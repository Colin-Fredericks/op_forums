                                                "I met Darwin, and he most certainly did not sound like that. No, no, I don't mean 'I'm old enough as a vampire to have known him in my youth.' I mean that I saw him last year. Met him walking along the beach. I'd heard the rumors, that he was in the area. It wasn't the first time I'd heard them, but those stories are as much a fantasy to our kind as our kind are to the kine. And yet I am wooing some bronzed young thing on the beach under the midnight sun, all set to satisfy multiple hungers, and then he is there. She spooks, of course, but I don't blame her. I spooked. One moment, it's just us, and in the next instant he's there. I didn't see how he approached. Sea birds, maybe a crab, hell, I don't know. Maybe he was just in the water.

                                                "Because, you see, with Darwin, that's just it. He tells me of his long journey amid the ocean's life, resting during the day down where the sun cannot reach, taking the form of fanged predators to consume prey. How he can still subsist on animals I don't know. No one does. He's wandered for a couple hundred years now. You know, and I never asked, but he starts telling me of this grand journey. 'From breath to blood to brethren, I will have known them all,' he says, in the most grandiose voice you can imagine. I'd have laughed in his stiff old face if he wasn't so intense about it. You believe him. Trust me. He says that he's taken the forms of more animals than the number people I've met in my lifetime — unlifetime included. All part of the greatest experiment. To experience his own evolution through taking part in that whole 'red in tooth and claw' thing. I mean, really taking part. Taking them apart.

                                                "And you know, it wasn't that he was hostile. Far from it. He seemed completely indifferent to my anger. I could feel his power. He's old, yes, but he's seen things none of us can imagine. Who knows what horrific shape he might take on, all parts of the million different things he's eaten? [shivers] I don't even want to try it. And if you ever meet him, or hear the rumors that he's around, I'd advise you to believe them. Be careful. You don't know if that fly you're going to swat, or that cat you're going to ghoul, or that dog that is watching you really closely, is just an animal. Best to keep your wits about you. Stay observant. Watch the natural world for the supernatural. You'll feel a lot safer for it.

                                                "The thing is, I think that's what Darwin wants. You get to live a little in his world when you pay attention to those things. Maybe it makes you a better vampire. Maybe it just makes you paranoid. Either way, it's going to change you, and change, my friend, is evolution of a sort. I've never been much of a scientist myself, not much of a reader, but I can tell you this: I eagerly await the follow-up volume to On the Origin of Species once he completes whatever journey he's on!"
