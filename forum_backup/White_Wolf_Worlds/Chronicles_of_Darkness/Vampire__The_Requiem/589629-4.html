        I also remember this, somewhere in GMC. So yeah, an NPC should have AT MOST half his WP available at any given scene.

        But yeah, just to say again what other already said, it isn't about equal levels of hability. It is about offense vs defense.

        You want 6 of base attack pool? Strength 3, Brawl 3. That's pretty much a professional brawler, what means this guy also probably have some fighting styles merits. But lets ignore them for now.

        A defender with 6 of Defense will have Wits 3 PLUS Dexterity 3, what's much more hard to come by than 3 at just one stat. And it is even less likely that he will have also Strength 3 to hit as hard. Athletics 3 is maybe as likely as Brawl 3, but what it says about the guy? How much people out there really have Athletics as much as Brawl at 3?

        The probability of a high defense enemy is low, and of a balanced enemy is even lower. Only some specific athletes could reasonably be as trained in one as in the other, and athletes, themost likely to have high Defense, are also unlikely to want a fight in the first place. You'll have, even as a player, a WAY easier time being a hard hitter than a defensive guy. Specialy when you remember that high Defense means not agains firearms and weakens against numerous opponents.

        Remember them that you ARE talking about a White Room scenario, and I'm not talking about using your surroundings or using WP. I'm talking about weapons and buddies. Defense & Attack are almost equals when you talk about two guys, one-on-one, using their hands. That's a bar fight, or a martial arts competition. A situation where people does very little real harm to each other. If you want to HURT someone, even IRL, you'll want an edge. Maybe a literal edge.

        Just using a melee weapon you already have a considerable advantage. Did you saw the rule for Beaten Down? If you don't use it, so your fights aren't that reallistical to begin with, because when someone wants to live, and most people do, they do leave a fight as early as they start to get hurt. With a weapon you add direct damage, plus your successes, and it is Lethal damage. Well, to a mortal you give the Beaten Down Tilt with ANY Lethal, so we already have an edge here. Even if it wasn't so simple, the tilt would trigger with damage equal to Stamina, and most people have 2 Stamina. Or do you REALLY think that common someone with Dexterity 3, Wits 3, Stamina 3 and Athletics 3? Don't forget other tilts and the fact that isn't so hard to find a heavier weapon.

        Ganging up on someone is another way, since you reduce the target's Defense at each turn. So you can pretty much turn anyone into a bloody pulp just ganging up on him with your buddies. If you are alone and without a weapon, you shouldn't enter a serious fight to begin with. And the WP comes in handy precisely because you are fighting for your life.

            Originally posted by Unahim View Post
            Using willpower when you want to succeed is good. Needing to use willpower to have more than an abysmally low chance at hitting someone with equal skill is bad. Especially for NPCs, because giving them a lot of willpower to spend usually feels unfair to the players, who have a greater need of conserving it. There are ways around it for vampires, but a system needs to be built on a solid foundation. And this is obviously a little clink in the system that does not quite work as intended. Really, nobody is saying the system is bad. People keep dragging everything and the kitchen sink into it, but in the end the situation, reduced only to its core principles, clearly has issues.

            Is this a game breaking problem? Again, no, since you almost never reduce a situation to the bare essentials in actual play. But the fact that the problem exists at any level of abstraction points to a weakness in the system and suggests it could have been done better. That is all.
            See, I'm inclined to disagree. In a struggle between equals I want it to be the decisions, not the dice that decide the outcome. If, in this mirror fighter scenario we have going, either party leaves the fight as anything but exhausted mentally and physically then I'd be hard pressed to call it a fight between equals.

            Lets remove dice from the scenario and just look at it naratively (as this is the Storytelling system, and therefore it should strive simulate stories). When two characters of 'equivalent' skill go head to head in most media the fight is generally long and drawn out, occurring at a climactic moment, with minutes spent as the characters trade blows and vie for position. In the end even the victorious party is quite spent. I think I can safely say that the victorious character could be rightly declared low on Health and Willpower, having exhausted both during the fight (indeed, it is common in stories for this to be the moment where a much weaker character, who would not normally have stood a chance, can step in an seize victory over his less-than-prime combatant).

            Stepping back to the system, I'm of the opinion that a fight between equals should be a bit of a slog. With the caveat that the storyteller should be careful to restrict such encounters to climactic moments where the low dice pools and diminishing resources generate tension, not boredom. As a practical example: I ran a game, the setting of which is largely irrelevant, wherein two players happened to be playing swordsmen of different veins (one a very quick duelist and the other heavily armed and armored). More importantly both were on opposite sides of the conflict and, as the story progressed, the other players began to expect and look forward to the encounter. Thus, when the two met in single combat at the climax of the final session, the untouchable defense of one and impenetrable armor of the other did not render the fight boring. Quite the contrary, each character was stretched to the limits of their options and forced to fight with considerably more care and cunning than they had up till that point.

            TL;DR - It's not that fights between equals are broken. They have a place, and it's the storyteller's job to build enough tension that low dice pools and dwindling resources is climactic, not boring.

                        If we were to model the aforementioned Paul-Edson fight in vampire, we obviously couldn't count all of the significant strikes that connected as successful attacks, since successful attacks always do at least a point of damage and nobody has 64+ health boxes. We might say that a single successful attack represents more than one significant strike and/or decide that some (presumably arbitrary) number of blows which connect will nevertheless be counted as failed attacks. The former method seems preferable; if we use the latter, we could fudge the accuracy to be within whatever range we want (as long as it's lower than the actual observed accuracy) by turning hits into misses. That said, it's possible that it would be accurate to say that some strikes that meet the criteria to be considered "significant" do not, in fact, significantly contribute to the damage inflicted on the opposing fighter.

                            As CatDoom said, one roll does not equate to one punch (or shot, or sword swing, etc). nWoD's combat system is a narrative abstraction, even the idea that one round is equal to three seconds is merely a ballpark suggestion. One can easily run turns as ten to fifteen seconds (or more) of trading blows, or drop rounds down to fractions of seconds as two high-powered combatants go head to head at impossible speed. All things bend to the story's needs.

                                                        As someone said above... dice rolls are an abstraction and time can practically be whatever fits the narrative. Turns don't even need to have the same length of time in the same fighting scene.Whatever fits the narrative and the intent of the characters in it determines how long a violent scene last.

                                                        If a person is assaulted in an apartment by two thugs while a friend witness it from the streets through the window it can look like this...

                                                        Turn 1... the intent of the assaulted person in the apartment is to avoid harm until help arrives. The friend on the street start running up the stairs to the apartment. The friend roll a Dex+Athletics roll for running up the stairs. The GM decide to give a bonus or penalty to the assaulted persons dodge roll, depending on the friends roll and the first part of the combat are pretty much done as one turn. Any damage dealt become part of the narrative and become the condition of the fight as the friend arrive.
                                                        Turn 2+ After this point new intents are declared and several turns which have a shorter interval in terms of time are concluded.

                                                        This would fit well with the narrative and keep the game pace flowing fast and the drama flowing.
