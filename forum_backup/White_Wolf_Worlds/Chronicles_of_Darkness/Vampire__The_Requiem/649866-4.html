        Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
        Hello!

        I have Vampire: the Requiem book at home, but I have yet to play or GM it. Nevertheless, I tried considered both - and there are two questions that I struggle with:

        1. When coming up with possible characters, I started to wonder: why would anyone actually decide to become a vampire? I mean... being vampire obviously sucks, because you have to literally prey on human beings. Wouldn't that turn almost anyone off the idea of allowing themselves to be Embraced? It would seem to me that only complete psychopaths and very desperate people would be willing to become vampires... How do you have your characters rationalize it, then?

        2. When I was considering GM this game, I realized that I... have no idea what Vampire games could actually be about. It's a very rich setting, but what stories can you actually tell in it? Could you share any ideas?
        Regarding 1: Sometimes you don't decide it. Sometimes some immortal predator becomes obsessed with you for some reason. Check Dracula From Bran Stoker for example.

        Regarding 2: Loss of innocence, damnation, damned if you do - damned if you don't, ennui, the depression of realizing everything you've ever knew will, a thousand years from now, be dust - that moment broken by the searing thirst of a sensual moment more pleasurable than the best sex you've ever had.

        The list goes on and on.

            One word: denial.
            Given how much Hollywood has romanticized vampires do you honestly believe your average person wouldn't jump at the chance to live out their Twilight dreams? It is also possible to deny that you will do harm, after all killing isn't a necessary part of feeding.
            There will also be those for whom the idea of living at the expense of others isn't that hard to accept and may already be a part of their lives. Hardened criminals, the hate filled, people who really don't empathize with strangers or the faceless masses. The CWOD example of Montrovant, the Dark One, is a crusader who was turned after he became disillusioned by fighting in the holy land. This was also the idea behind Nicholas Knight in the TV show Forever Knight. Is it that hard to imagine someone who grew up in a violent neighborhood might choose to join a gang that drinks blood?​

                        Varsovian in terms of Embrace stories that are mine and from friends.

                        Dragon Gangrel: Circa 70s turkey, 10 yr old girl was taken from an orphanage and raised in the mansion of a Wyrm Prince (Dragon who is a Prince) and grew up as mansion staff/herd/lab assistant/goffer. At age 19 she was fatally drained because someone attempting a new coil failed and their Beast seized control. Before she died she was found by one of the Dragons on the premise and Embraced. The rational was she had been considered an adequate servant in life, was well versed with the Ordo and because with Final Death of the failed Dragon there was an opening and they decided, why not her?

                        Unaligned Daeva: 1870s, wild west; a Daeva ran across a very beautiful woman who ran a saloon and also happened to be really good with a pistol. The daeve was smitten with her because she had a powerful personality, was beautiful, was dangerous (by kine standards) and was smart. To the Sire the woman was a perfect candidate for Embrace (and she was totally crushing on her) and embraced her. As I recall she blew her Sire's head off with a coach gun several months after Embrace while the Sire was mid-coitus with a man they had picked up together. Had "Payback" engraved in butt of the gun afterward.

                        Unaligned Ventrue: 13th Century Mongol Empire, the son of a minor mongol chief who had been seriously wounded in battle during an early raid into Persia. Laying on field at night waiting to die, two Vampires appeared above him and offered him a deal. He could accept the Embrace, become a vampire and continue to rape and pillage with them in the name of the Mongols (they were mongols too) or they'd drink what little blood he had left and leave his corpse for the crows. He choose the Embrace. He joined their cotiere whose gig was to go into cities and recruit Kindred to the Mongol cause and if they resisted, sabotage defenses/troops/food stores to make it easier for the Mongols to take cities. (it was a pretty fun game of spycraft as it turned out).

                        Carthian Revenant: Victorian England, was attacked by what he believed to a Nosferatu, drained and dropped in a bog. Sank into the muck before day-light and then rose the next time. Bit of a pyromaniac socialist that one turned out to be.

                        Pre-cursor Crone Mekhet: was a priest to in Set Cult in late Bronze Age Egypt. The cult was a front to a large coterie/cult of Mekhet. He was the ghoul/personal assistant to one of the Mekhet. When ghouls appeared in the middle of the day to kill the sleeping coterie, the priest drove them off with Cruac which he had been secretly learning to impress his Master. His master was shocked and quickly Embraced him as a reward for defending his masters and for learning blood magic. Turns out vamps don't like the idea of their human pets wielding blood magic. Went on to be a pretty scary blood sorcerer, helped drive off the Sea People invading Egypt.

                        Invictus Nosferatu: He had been a soldier turned in WWI. He had been caught in one of the earl poison gas attacks before they had masks. Two Nos feeding on the fatally wounded came across him gagging and gasping in no-mans-land and made a bet. There was a rumor going around at the time chemical weapons first appeared in the war that you couldn't embrace victims of them. One bet the other 5 Franks that he couldn't embrace the soldier. He was subsequently embraced and his Sire won the 5 Franks. He was almost killed near the end of the war because he went feeding in a trench and got caught in an arillary bombardment. Two of his coterie-mates got taken out in one round of combat/shelling.

                        I play with a lot of history buffs in case you notice.

                                        1 - Not exactly, it's more like vampires don't adapt well to changes in society as the ages pass by. Nobody likes the idea that the way they were brought up is considered outdated and the times are changing. It's like grandparents having trouble using smart phones and computers. Elder vampires who grew up in a time where you sent messages on hand written parchment and delivered by carrier pigeon still feel uncomfortable using electronic mail or phone calls. Such Elders often use Ghouls, or embrace younger childer in order to act as proxies for keeping in touch with the modern times.



                                        2 - Actually, I would like to know that too. Never thought to ask for confirmation.



                                        3 - The tradition applies to revealing yourself to mortals and then letting them go free. It's okay for a vampire to slip up once in a while as long as they cover up their mistakes. This typically means one of four things; Give the witness a memory wipe, enslave them, embrace them, or kill them.


                                        4 - The actual terms of the second tradition mean you can't embrace somebody without permission and the actual purpose of this tradition is so that the leaders of the domain have knowledge on all the vampires operating within their territory, even newly created ones. This rule seems to have been thrown out in 2nd edition where you're free to embrace as you please, but you take full responsibility for their actions as long as they remain under your tutelage. Besides, if vampires were forbidden to create more of their own, they would have died out centuries ago during the time of the crusades.


                                        5 - You have a point that, yes, a childe is under no obligation to learn from their sire, but you should keep in mind that once they are released, they are wholly responsible for their own actions. Nobody is going to hold their hand and walk them through the night like their Sire tried to do. If they make a mistake, they are the ones who get punished for it, not the one who made them, so even if you hate your sire, it is at least a good idea to learn what you can from them before striking out on your own.

                                            Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                            Okay! I was re-reading the core Requiem book and I have some more questions:

                                            1. The book is says that the vampires don't mature personality-wise after the Embrace. Sooo.. does it mean that a vampire that was 20 when Embraced is going to behave like an impetous, naive 20 year old forever? Even when he's 500 years old?
                                            To an extent. Naivity goes away with experience and knowledge. But he's likely to remain immature, even as he develops as an elder. Or course, that's entirely in 1e. 2nd edition has completely dropped the whole static-vampire thing. So its not really supported beyond the vampire core. I wouldn't put too much worry about it.

                                            2. You guys mentioned that vampires often Embrace ghouls. But the ghouls are all, by their nature, subject to Vinculum. Does it mean that, after their Embrace, such new vampires are blood-bound, too?
                                            Its up to the ST, but generally yes. Many vampires blood bond their childer as a matter of course anyways.

                                            3. By the way, the First Tradition says that a vampire shouldn't reveal himself to mortals and that vampires that do so should be put to death. So, why is the practice of creating ghouls allowed? It is a breach of Masquerade, too...

                                            4. Speaking of Traditions: the Second Tradition forbids the creation of new vampires. Still, obviously, it keeps happening. Sooo... it's a Tradition that nobody pays attention to, right?
                                            Vampires are naturally hypocrites. You're expected to break the law, but just not get caught doing it. Vampires pretty much break the Masquerade any time they feed, but they still feed. Ghouls are excused, because once they're a ghoul, they're part of the Blood. There's lots of self-justifications running around, and that's kind of the point. Childer aren't allowed, but it happens. Even in the Lancea, its a sin, but it happens, and there's public whipping, and then everyone goes home happy. Diablarie is forbidden... except when its allowed. Its the ultimate thrill, the ultimate drug for vampires, and they all secretly crave a taste.

                                            Its all about whatever excuses you can come up with for breaking the law, not just avoiding it.
                                            5. And speaking of Embracing: it is said the sires are responsible for the tutelage of their childe. But... why would any childe listen to his sire? Let's say you are Embraced and turned into a monster against your will - would you really be interested in listening to the monster that did that to you?
                                            One, who else you going to listen to? Just how do you plan on surviving without them? Or do you have convient access to blood and sunless rooms when you didn't plan on becoming a vampire? Sheer necessity here. Two, after a bit, Stockholm Syndrome sets in. Or there's Majesty, Dominate, the blood bond. Maybe you stick around for revenge. Three, not all Embraces are against the will; many go willingly to the fang. To be honest, for all the talk about how much being a vampire sucks, no pun intended? It certain as hell beats growing old and dying, your body betraying you, your memory failing.

                                                Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                                1. The book is says that the vampires don't mature personality-wise after the Embrace. Sooo.. does it mean that a vampire that was 20 when Embraced is going to behave like an impetous, naive 20 year old forever? Even when he's 500 years old?
                                                This is one of those things that's kind of changed in the 10 years since the core book came out and the setting has grown. As Nyrufa said, it's not that they don't mature emotionally, it's just that they have a lot of difficulty adjusting to the rapidly changing times. And even that's been de-emphasized as of 2e.

                                                Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                                2. You guys mentioned that vampires often Embrace ghouls. But the ghouls are all, by their nature, subject to Vinculum. Does it mean that, after their Embrace, such new vampires are blood-bound, too?
                                                Yep. Also, the embrace itself requires the subject to consume a point of the sire's Vitae, which would also prompt a roll to check for Vinculum. It's incredibly common for freshly-embraced childer to start out at least one stage of blood bond to their Sire, if not more.

                                                Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                                3. By the way, the First Tradition says that a vampire shouldn't reveal himself to mortals and that vampires that do so should be put to death. So, why is the practice of creating ghouls allowed? It is a breach of Masquerade, too...
                                                Different Domains interpret the Traditions in different ways, so in some cities, creating a Ghoul might very well be considered a breach of the First Tradition. More often though, ghouls don't really count, since by definition they are blood bound to their regnant and are therefore unlikely to reveal her identity to any mortals. Similarly, in some Domains it's considered perfectly fine for a vampire's Herds to know about her undead nature, as long as she compels them to keep it secret through some combination of blood bonds, Vitae addiction, and/or disciplines like Dominate and Majesty. And if some random mortal happens to catch you feeding or something? No problem, just erase his memory, or ghoul him, or kill him, or what ever you've got to do to plug the leak. As long as you patch up your own Masquerade breaches, no harm no foul. Others will not be so forgiving, just depends on the local political climate.

                                                Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                                4. Speaking of Traditions: the Second Tradition forbids the creation of new vampires. Still, obviously, it keeps happening. Sooo... it's a Tradition that nobody pays attention to, right?
                                                Again, different Domains will have different rules about when Embrace is or isn't allowed. Only the most starkly traditional Prince will completely forbid embrace under any circumstances within his city. Most often, Embracing is allowed, but only with permission from the Prince himself (or maybe he delegates that duty to someone else), and some cities will be more or less discerning with granting permission. Some very liberal Princes might prefer that Sires ask forgiveness rather than permission.

                                                Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                                5. And speaking of Embracing: it is said the sires are responsible for the tutelage of their childe. But... why would any childe listen to his sire? Let's say you are Embraced and turned into a monster against your will - would you really be interested in listening to the monster that did that to you?
                                                Remember the part about most Childer starting out at least one stage blood bound to their Sire? This is why. It's extremely common for Sires to keep their Childer bound to their will through blood bonds and/or Discipline use for the first few years or decades of the Childe's unlife, until they've proven they can take care of themselves. A neonate has to earn her right to free will.

                                                            Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                                            BTW. An observation: Requiem is a game that seems to require a lot of planning. I mean, a Mage: the Awakening game can be a simple adventure with mages investigating some strange happenings etc. But in Requiem, you really need to plan things before you begin to GM - the politics seem central to this game, so you need to come up with a whole power structure for a city before you start anything...
                                                            Really, the politics can be thrown out if you want. There are nomadic games and as mentioned, cities without covenants or real organization, and even in a game with a lush political setting the game is not always going to be about becoming Prince. But at the core, the game just like vampires themselves is a social thing. One of the best pieces of advice I ever got for vampire was to just make a list of characters. You don't have to have a fully written-up character sheet for each, just a name, clan and covenant and a sentence or two about them. That way you always have someone the players can engage with. I've since added some things to it, essentially noting 'Things They Want' and 'Things They Can Do.'
