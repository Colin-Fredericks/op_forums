      Unbound do actually have a mechanical advantage. They aren't under any obligation to kowtow to covenant or city officials based on affiliations. Mechanically this is reflected in the fact that other PCs and NPCs cannot use status against them. (Which often leads to stilted roleplaying where they are expected to know someone's standing despite not actually playing in the setting before.) If you're using 2nd edition rules, this renders them immune to the Carthian Law powers which entail stripping away a victim's status from them or allowing the Carthian to bludgeon their opponents with the Carthian's own status, and saps the effectiveness of disciplines that augment social rolls (notably Majesty and Nightmare).

              Covenants are basically glorified cults of monsters. Joining one is a Humanity breaking point. The stuff your covenant superiors encourage you to do (and, by encourage, I mean order) tends to hurt your Humanity more. So, avoiding all those breaking poitns is one advantage.

That same Invictus Kindred also has reasons to align with the Carthian Kindred and the Crone Kindred, because their resources aren't available to my fellow Invictus. There's tension there because it's not totally clear if we can trust each other, but also valuable things and some sense that we've all bought into this idea of Kindred society and stuff like that. The Unbound don't even do that, and they don't necessarily have anything unique to offer, so it makes sense that they're treated as these nomadic, vagrant types that the Kindred of the city can't trust.                                                           I'm not sure I follow. You're Unbound, so you're gonna get treated like an unbound person. This will undoubtedly lead to Conditions, beats and drama. All good stuff. If you're selecting to play one of the Unbound, you're obviously interested in exploring what it's like to not have affiliation in a society which is highly factionalized. It's interesting and fun.

                                                          The game makes no attempt to suggest you're doing it wrong by not joining a Covenant. In fact, Requiem doesn't take a moral stance on the objective necessity of Covenants at all. It doesn't justify them morally. They exist, whether that's good or bad is up to you.

                                                          One could actually argue that the game implies that Covenants are a bit unhealthy for you Humanity-wise, since joining one is actually a Breaking Point against Humanity 5. I don't think it's suggesting that they're bad by doing that though, just making it clear that joining an organization made for Kindred, by Kindred, with Kindred aims which wholly do not align with human goals, you're running the risk of losing Humanity.

                                                          The reasons people join the Covenants are numerous, but undoubtedly the most common of ALL is that you just don't want to be alone in the All-Night Society. Not every member of the Invictus is a suit-wearing libertarian shark, just as not every member of the Sanctified is going to be a book burning zealot. If you specifically chose to avoid associating with any of the Covenants in a given domain, that is actually a very political statement, and it's no wonder that vampires in those Covenants might seek to disenfranchise you.

                                                          But really, it comes back to the drama of being Unbound. The game is not telling you it's a bad thing. It's a dramatic thing, arguably a very interesting thing, provided people are interested in exploring the reasons and consequences for not associating with the Covenants.
