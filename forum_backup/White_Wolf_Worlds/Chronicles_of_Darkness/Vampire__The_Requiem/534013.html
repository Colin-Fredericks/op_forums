                      The Nameless faction of Belial's Brood is found all over the globe, but is noted to be particularly strong in Calais, Edinburgh and Vienna. The Geheim bloodline has a history of rightful claiming of domains in Vienna and Prague, though with the waning of traditional aristocracy and their flubs with the Nazis they don't hold nearly as much sway as they did before. There is a belief among the Lancea Sanctum that the blade of their namesake lance is in Vienna.

                      The Sangiovanni have been welcome to Paris since the close of the 19th century and the Duchagne bloodline was founded and remains centralized from there. The Architects of the Monolith operate out of Paris, led by Ermenjart la Charpentière, who seems quite insane, though they suffered drawbacks after a move against the Lancea Sanctum. The ancient Invictus House Machten, a nosferatu line, is based in the Parisian sewers, backing up the Invictus Prince of Paris with 'Gestapo-like tactics.'

                              Lets see. They held salons devoted to esoterica, and it's rumored that they didn't restrict their invitations to Kindred, so there may have been other supernatural beings there. They declined in power after the Thirty Years' War, though still rich. They infiltrated a number of secret societies around that time, including the Thule Society, which they thought might help enhance their mystical strength. Members from it formed the Nazi party and while the Geheim may have had nothing to do with it the word was out that they were involved and it didn't help their already dwindling prestige.

                              It looks like Paris is pretty firmly Invictus, in the book setting, at least. They likely have strong Lancea Sanctum supporters, but Paris also has a growing Carthian movement since socialist vampires joined up in the early 1900s.

                              Since you mentioned Werewolf, there's also the Eigner Lodge and Clinic, which has been around in Vienna since the early 1900s as well. They take a Jungian approach to the dreams of werewolves, specifically the prophetic ones, and help a lot with dream interpretation. In the second edition book it suggests they help werewolves deal with the trauma of their First Change, as well.

                              As a rambling and completely unrelated aside, considering how many mentions there are of Paris and Vienna and other major cities throughout cities in the vampire books, and how few are mentioned in the werewolf line, it's sort of disappointing. There's a stereotype that werewolves are 'stick to the country' savages and barbarians. Like during the Dark Eras Kickstarter, people were urging others not to vote for Viking Werewolf because it's 'too obvious.' And while it's certainly a valid stereotype in terms of most werewolf media, it doesn't really hold true with Werewolf the Forsaken, where werewolves are established as existing wherever people do, but there's still little mention of them in the cities. Even their dark era in New York seems to represent them at a time when the city itself is at its a particularly barbaric low. Fortunately it seems like second edition isn't sticking with the old stereotype.
