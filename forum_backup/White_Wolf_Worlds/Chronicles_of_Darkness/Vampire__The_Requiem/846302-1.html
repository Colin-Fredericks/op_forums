                            And I have no intention of going into it. But its unavoidable to talk about how the Blood Sorcery book rules do have an influence on the core book stuff.

                            I don't like using the word "broken" when refering to game mechanis very often. In common gamer vernacular, its nothing more than an exaggeration for something that someone thinks is slightly more powerful than average. To me, I take the term more literally, or as close to literal as I can take it. If something is broken, it means that the something is not working properly. If I call a game mechanic broken, I mean that the game literally cannot work with the mechanics as written, and needs to be fixed in some way. Now, I made a point to say all of this so that people will take the next statement as I intend it.

                            I fully believe that the core book Blood Sorcery rules are broken. The rituals do not work by RAW, and require either adapting the rules from Blood Sorcery (which need some tweaks from their 1e rules), or house rules. In either case, you need to make some hacks to get blood magic to work. Which is a shame.

                            So, whenever something like this conversation comes up, I think that making up rules so the game works the way you want is something that is literally required to play.

                                                    Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                                    As someone who doesn't know 1e blood magic, hasn't had a chance to see the 2e blood magic rules in action, and have a player who wants to do Cruac, what issues are present in the system?
                                                    Basically, the effects of 1e rituals were created with the fact that you could enact them in seconds in mind. Yes, it might take 2 or 3 rounds, but 1 was doable and regardless, it was expected you to be able to perform the ritual within the confines of a combat, or, out of combat, within a moment or two.

                                                    Therefore, most of the rituals are things that are useful in that context. Direct damage in combat, maybe, or quickly setting up the ability to see through someone's eyes as the walk by down the block.

                                                    2e, instead, requires 30 minutes instead of one turn per roll. But, they used all the 1e rituals, which, unfortunately, are things that are most useful when you only have a few seconds, rather than hours. The ability to penalize someone's next physical action is nice when it takes one turn. It's kind of pointless and disappointing when it takes an hour.

                                                    So, basically, you will want to create new rituals or use some kind if homebrew from someone else or the open development stuff on this site if you don't want your pc to be mostly underwhelmed with his options.

                                                        Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                                        As someone who doesn't know 1e blood magic, hasn't had a chance to see the 2e blood magic rules in action, and have a player who wants to do Cruac, what issues are present in the system?
                                                        Basically, in 1e, blood magic rituals were performed with an extended action, with each roll representing one turn of casting, and a target number equal to the level of the ritual. In 2e, blood magic rituals are still performed with an extended action, but each roll represents 30 minutes of casting, and the target number is determined by factors that are found in the Blood Sorcery book, but not reproduced in the 2e core book. Aside from the different roll intervals, the changes to how target number of successes are determined, and the 2e changes to the extended action rules, the system is basically the same between editions, which is why I maintain that the system itself is not fundamentally broken. The problem is, both edition's core books have the same sample rituals. Those rituals were originally designed to work in a system where you'd be casting them in a matter of turns. Since casting generally takes hours in 2e, most of the sample rituals are practically useless, and since the rules for determining target number of successes aren't in the 2e core book, it's difficult to create custom rituals to replace the sample ones if you don't have the book they are in. Then, of course, there are many people who don't like the casting system from the Blood Sorcery book.
