        On the topic of the Sta-Au's original bane (not being able to raise Humanity): I absolutely agreed that it had to go, but not because it's harsh, imo.             Anyhow, here's today's excerpt and commentary: The Caporetti.

            The original Shivers are kind of a missed opportunity, in my opinion. They’ve got a great aesthetic — frost-bitten corpse Nosferatu — but they don’t quite go beyond that. Their powers are also a bit redundant with Protean, and their weakness involves a design choice I’m not very fond of: making it harder to use a clan Discipline (namely, Obfuscate). I’m fine with making the inherent Kindred abilities a little wonky in service to a bane, bit I don’t really care for flaws that devalue your hard-earned Experiences.

            That being said, I didn’t really have a specific direction for this bloodline. I suggested they could perhaps have Protean instead of Celerity (which we didn’t end up doing), but I more or less gave the author carte blanche to rebuild them, and I think he really built on their original concept without losing the creepy mood. Whereas the version in Ancient Bloodlines are mostly just cold-weather survivalists, our update expands that by giving them an inherent ability to store dead blood and bottle it in frozen corpses, giving them mercantile angle that opens up a lot more character concepts.

            There’s an interesting parallel between the Caporetti and their fellow WWI vets, the Brothers of Ypres, one that was entirely unintentional on the authors’ part. Both produce a kind of horror: Whereas the Brothers of Ypres create Phosgene Angels, Caporetti who feed too much of cold, dead blood become the monster. This is a theme we’ve explored previously with the electricity-eating Keravnos in False Gods: Ventrue. I’m generally of the opinion that Kindred who try to find alternate sources of food are doomed to become something much worse than vampires. The Curse can only be denied so much before something breaks. Anyway, I like the thematic complementariness of this: The Brothers of Ypres are about what you leave behind; the Caporetti are about what you bring back with you.

            There wasn’t too much dev to do on this one, apart from adding the Into the Cold Dark Era, which was too perfect an implied pun to pass up (and, like, the themes an’ shit).

            The only other thing I have to say about the Caporetti is that I wanted the colour palette of their character illustration to be that of a cheap pool toy. Judge for yourselves if I succeeded.

            Without further adieu, take a sleighride with the Caporetti:

            Caporetti: The Ones Who Leave You Cold

            “Let it snow.”

            Blood is everything to the Kindred. The rush of fresh, hot Vitae over the tongue is better than any living pleasure, and they spend most of their endless nights in pursuit of more.

            However, one group of Haunts prefers their drink of choice on ice. Fresh Vitae burns the throats of the Caporetti; only the thick, syrupy blood pulled from the cold flesh of the dead is safe for them to consume. Fortunately, the Shivers are uniquely adapted to their strange palette, able to take far more sustenance from stored blood than their Kindred. Even more remarkable, they can preserve corpses with frigid arts that allow blood to remain viable for consumption long past the point it should congeal into sludge. Normally, a vampire can guzzle a cooler’s worth and barely gain any sustenance, but a sip taken from a corpse preserved by a Caporetto is as nourishing as one taken from pumping arteries — they just can’t do anything about the taste.

            Originating from a single mountainous region in Italy, the Caporetti have spread far and wide in modern nights, though they do still favor frigid climates. Called “Shivers” (or “Snowmen” behind their backs), they were once a bloodline of hermits and urban legends leading isolated Requiems, their frozen pantries hidden by the driven snow and deep caves. As modern refrigeration technology became more ubiquitous, however, many Caporetti came down from the mountains to find the same abilities that allow them to survive all alone are now in high demand by the rest of the All Night Society.

            Over the last century, many Shivers have transformed from isolated spooks to procurement specialists, Vitae loan sharks, and even emergency medics. Not all Kindred can manage to hunt down a fresh meal every night or two, and for those willing to pay, Caporetti blood larders can provide a time-saving (if foul-tasting) alternative. Sure, the Snowmen are creepy, like all Haunts, and they’re often difficult to reach, but they’re also a way for a Lick to get a lot of blood in a hurry that doesn’t involve murder. At least, not directly — those cold-storage corpses have to come from somewhere. Just make sure you pay for what you drink: The Caporetti may not be a formal crime family, but they’re as brutal as any mafia about collecting what they’re owed.

            Even in modern nights, some Caporetti still prefer to lead a more traditional existence, haunting ski runs, hiking trails, campgrounds, and other isolated spots where a few disappearances aren’t going to be questioned. These Shivers enjoy the independence their powers provide them, able to sustain themselves on just a few victims for months, or even years. However, it’s whispered among the bloodline that those who spend too long existing like this start to lose what makes them Kindred. The cold creeps deeper into their bones and warps them into something more elemental, but just as hungry. While it’s rare enough that most Caporetti have only heard the tale second- or thirdhand, the term ghiacciati is muttered in industrial meat lockers and icicle-lined caverns across domains where the bloodline makes their frozen homes.

            Why you want to be us

            Cold is the natural Kindred condition, so why not make it work for you? It’s better to be needed than wanted, and the satisfaction when some pretty Serpent or stuck-up Lord crawls into our freezers with hat (and cash) in hand is almost as sweet as the blood itself. Or maybe you’re just fucking tired of the rotting song and dance of our so-called “society.” Well, we can give you a way to leave it all behind… for a while, at least.

            Why you should fear us

            Most Licks don’t remember what it’s like to be really, truly cold. To have your hands shake so bad you can’t hold a gun, teeth chattering so hard your fangs chip, to feel the blood turn to ice in your veins. Point being: Payment upfront when you want a snack, and the next time you see a KEEP OUT sign on a lonely, snow-filled trail? Obey it.

            Why we should fear ourselves

            The cold sets us apart, and if you give into it, it’ll twist what little of you isn’t a monster into something freezing and ravenous. Just because you can exist with nothing but snow and stolen life doesn’t mean you should… but if warmth and life keep rejecting us, what else is there?

            Parent Clan: Nosferatu

            Nicknames: Shivers, Snowmen

            Bloodline Bane (The Frozen Curse): Shivers crave blood like all Kindred, but fresh Vitae can scald their frigid veins with its warmth. When a Caporetto feeds from a living creature (including ghouls), her player must roll Humanity. Success means the blood is merely uncomfortably hot as it goes down, but on a failure, the Shiver suffers bashing damage equal to half the amount consumed, rounded up. This damage cannot be reduced with Resilience or other supernatural powers. The Frozen Curse does not apply to feeding from Kindred, corpses, or deathly beings such as Sin-Eaters. In addition, Caporetti blood struggles to heat itself in counterfeiting mortality. When a Shiver uses the blush of life, she must spend two Vitae.

            Disciplines: Celerity, Nightmare, Obfuscate, Vigor

            In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

            Into the Cold — 1961 CE (DE1, p. 530): The Cold War keeps the temperature just right for Berlin’s Shivers, who find they can remain safely apolitical as long as their supply chains are unmolested. Doesn’t matter if you’re a capitalist pig or a commie rat, everyone needs to eat eventually. Of course, there are Kindred in both East and West Berlin who distrust the Caporetti for “failing to choose a side,” and some believe that, if they could take control of the Snowmen’s business, appropriating their sizable blood stores, they could strike a blow against their ideological foes on the other side of the Wall.

            Horror: Ghiacciati

            Something lurks in the Caporetti bloodline that hungers for more than just blood. A Shiver who spends too long subsisting on nothing but cold, dead Vitae runs the risk of turning into a flesh-eating creature of killing frost and ravening appetite. Referred to as ghiacciati (roughly, “frozen ones”) among the bloodline, these beasts are akin to draugr, but their hunting patterns are much more destructive.

            A ghiacciato craves not just blood but also the meat of its victims, consuming them down to their very bones. What’s more, the creatures are always hungry no matter how many victims they eat, perpetually searching for more flesh to fill their aching bellies. Inevitably, their hunger drives them out of their isolated environs and into more populated areas, where they’ll proceed to kill and eat anything living they can get their fangs on. Left alone, a ghiacciato can slaughter an entire community and is a dire threat to the Masquerade. For these reasons, the Caporetti make sure the phenomenon stays secret as much as they possibly can, and keep tabs on the more isolated members of their line. If a ghiacciato is discovered, the bloodline handles it in-house, putting it down before it can do too much damage.

                Yes! Caporetti time!

                It isn't exactly a secret that the Caporetti are my favorite bloodline. The idea of abominable snowmen vampires that are perfectly adapted to hunting in mountains just tickles my monkey brain, and their write up in Ancient Bloodlines is so evocative. I really like the fiction at the start of Ancient Mysteries that sorta suggests they might be older than the Great War. It gives them a subterranean, primeval quality that meshes nicely with the blood and snow aesthetic. It all just goes so well with what makes mountains scary! As I said earlier, I've been really looking forward to seeing NMD's take on them.

                And it does not disappoint! While the focus is no longer on bloodthirsty snow monsters, the new identity is slick and suits really contemporary and politically active character concepts. Even better, nothing about it actually keeps someone [me] from still playing them like mountaintop boogeymen. In fact, I think it makes it easier, since they can preserve the blood in corpses for later consumption, which is a neat trick in such a harsh environment. It is a smart way to take their larders, and not only mechanize them, but tie them back into the bloodline's identity through more than reputation. They are still the extremophile mountain vampires I love, but in a way that lets them come down and play with others more.

                I also like the way their burrowing Devotion was updated. I am firmly of the opinion that the Caporetti should not have Protean just because a couple of their old Devotions are now baked into the Discipline, and Beneath the Surface is a good compromise. It basically lets them do what they used to [minus wall crawling], but not in a way that steps on Protean's toes or requires them to lose Celerity [which I think is key to their horror]. It is also has great imagery, really upping the wintery cryptid vibes. I like it a lot. It is also basically a merit, even though it is a Devotion, and those are my favorite bloodline goodies.

                I quite like the new bane in general. I was a fan of the aura of oppressive cold from 1e, but mostly for visual reasons, so I'm not too sad to see it go. I can almost see a resources death spiral if a character gets really unlucky on the damage rolls while feeding at low Vitae, but it would require really contrived circumstances. It would probably also be a fun story to talk about later, if it did happen. It is also interesting that the bane doesn't apply to blood from the Bound. Not really going anywhere with that, I just think it is fun.

                This one really surpassed my expectations [not that I really kept any aside from a nebulous, "it will probably be good"]. I will probably use it over my own conversion in basically its entirety. Well, maybe not the Ghiacciati. Not because I don't like them; they are fun and chilling alternate draugr [and I think it is neat that the Trenchers get a revenant variant and the Shivers get a draugr variant], but because they bring a lot of extra stuff that I will not always want to deal with. Sometimes, being a vampire is enough of a threat to hold over a character's head.

                Also, gotta say, I love your art for this one. It is really goofy, but it works. He may not resemble the blue, skull-faced Caporetti from 1e, but he has just as much personality, imo.

                Posting gives me anxiety, but I'm still happy to be here

                            You know what, let's do one more for the week, and I'll take a day off on Sunday. Let's talk about the Maiyarap.

                            This is a bloodline that went through a lot of changes from its original incarnation (pun unintended). Readers of Ancient Bloodlines will pretty quickly notice that we combined the Maiyarap with the Sakadagami covenant. This was for a couple of reasons: As per the sidebar I included on p. 71, we wanted to make the Treasures of Mae Ji more accessible to non-Thai characters. We were originally going to update the Sakadagami as a proper covenant, but I couldn't really find a hook for them that didn't leave them entirely regional; I'm sure there is a hook for them in the broader All Night Society, but I had trouble finding it. However, the writer assigned to the Maiyarap pretty early on asked if he could steal them away from me and combine the two, and that solved the problem pretty cleanly (work smart, not hard, that's what I always say.)

                            The other reason that we discussed was that, all told, there wasn't much to the Maiyarap in the original write-up, as they were so strongly associated with the covenant already. So, it made sense to us to save some space and merge the two concepts. YMMV on that, but I think they ended up stronger together. And you can pretty easily use our updated systems with the old lore if that's your fancy.

                            Apart from the big lore adjustment, the other change was their Discipline. Bhumisparsa is ultimately kind of a mashup of Celerity and Resilience, and we didn't feel a need to update it. Normally we try to add a little shoutout to the original Discipline if we're getting rid of it, but we felt the Treasures were a much more interesting and thematic advantage. Since the Maiyarap already had Resilience in Ancient Bloodlines, we considered just replacing Bhumisparsa with Celerity, but Auspex was a bit more thematic. Not quite a literal translation, but I think it's a better fit for the bloodline on the whole. And who doesn't like a religious fanatic Nosferatu who can read your sins with a glance?

                            This one did go through a few changes in dev. The initial version was a tad too "vampire Buddhists", so I asked the author to really amp up the body horror, which is a bit more Nosferatu. I also added the i to their name; it's a slightly more accurate transliteration of the demon they're named after; there were a few linguistic choices in the book I wanted to fix up (we'll talk a lot more about that when we get the Geihemen).

                            Alright, that's it for me for the weekend (though feel free to throw me questions). I'll be back on Monday with more Nosferatu: The Order of Sir Martin.

                            Maiyarap: The Ones Who Teach You

                            “All desire must be released to reach enlightenment. Your desire to end this suffering is no different.”

                            He’s back again tonight: the thin man with the shaved head, sitting crossed-legged by the side of the bus station. He’s been there every night the past week, never seeming to mind the constant cold drizzle, even though he’s only wearing a rough black robe. He speaks in gentle tones to those who pass, but you can never hear what he says from across the street. Most walk by without a glance, and you’ve never seen anyone actually stop to talk.

                            You feel sorry for him, so tonight, you decide to cross the street, a cup of hot coffee in hand. He looks up as your approach, and his eyes are dark and still. He smiles. “May I teach you about dharma in exchange for a few moments of your life?” The words are accented, but carefully rehearsed, so you understand well enough.

                            Not sure how to respond to this unexpected gravitas, you smile back and nod, just hoping that whatever he has to say won’t take much time. The man begins speaking, and you realize he must be some kind of monk, as he speaks of enlightenment and “making merit” — whatever that is. You mostly nod along, mind elsewhere. When he finishes, you thank him and hold out the coffee.

                            He smiles again. “You’re kind.” The monk reaches out, but not for the cup, and his too-cold hand closes on your wrist in an unbreakable grip. “Thank you for sharing your life with me…”

                            That’s when you realize that you didn’t understand at all.

                            Kindred suffer. They suffer from a craving for blood, from the rages of the Beast, from their very existence as dead things clinging to a semblance of Humanity.

                            Kindred cause suffering. They steal life from the living and fight each other in a never-ending struggle for dominance. They ruin and kill in the name of both survival and politics. This is how it has always been, but the Maiyarap say it doesn’t have to be. The Beggar Monks claim to have squelched their thirst, silenced their Beast, and are free of the need to advance at the expense of their peers. They say the way is long and difficult, but along it lies a Requiem where one no longer experiences — or is the cause of — the suffering endemic to Kindred existence.

                            “Bullshit,” is the near-universal response when the Maiyarap travel abroad with their claims, but the proof is in the message. Or the messenger, in this case. Other vampires witness Beggar Monks shrug off the most violent provocations to frenzy, ignore the most desperate blood dolls, and smile serenely through the disrespect inevitably slung their way. A few of those Kindred are intrigued enough to listen to what the Maiyarap have to say, though even fewer keep listening after the Beggar Monks describe burning limbs off with sacred pyres, or slowly removing their own internal organs by hand, and other harsh practices required to master their teachings. Still, the Maiyarap persist, extending a hand no matter how many times it’s slapped away — they’re serving something greater than themselves.

                            In their native Thailand, the Maiyarap are an institution, and few question their teachings. The black-robed monks have operated out of their central temple of Wat Ning for centuries, and they’ve expanded to many other temples and shrines throughout the country. The respect the bloodline enjoys originates with their founder, the notorious Naga Monk, a formerly tyrannical monster who spread suffering in Thailand for centuries as the Naga King before undergoing a radical conversion to Buddhism. The Monk exists in a state of barely contained fury, his near-constant meditation and total commitment to mindfulness the only things holding his monstrous nature at bay.

                            While the Naga Monk accepted his tenuous existence, his childer seek a better Requiem for their sire and believe they can balance his dharma by making merit in his name, using the methods he taught them. “Making merit” is an integral concept in Buddhism, a beneficial force that accumulates as a result of good deeds, acts, or thoughts. Essentially, the Maiyarap believe that if they do enough good in the name of their founder, he can be fully redeemed and move on to a higher state of being. This belief is behind the bloodline’s recent efforts to spread its teachings beyond Thailand and into the wider world. It’s also bred a brutally utilitarian view of ethics; a Coiled Snake won’t hesitate to snuff out innocent life or cause immense suffering if he believes his actions will prevent greater evil in the future, and thus create more merit for the Naga Monk.

                            It’s in pursuit of this holy mission that the Beggar Monks depart from where they’re respected to spread their teachings in places where they’re mocked and ignored — the places most in need of enlightenment, in their reckoning. Behind their gentle smiles, however, lurks unyielding zealotry. Not every convert to the Maiyarap’s teachings is a willing one, and more than one Lick claims to have a Haunt coterie-mate who went missing only to turn up years later with a black robe and many, many scars…

                            Why you want to be us

                            Do you really? Consider carefully, as the road to enlightenment is long and painful — most fall to ashes before they complete it. However, the agony of the journey can free you from craving, and thus from the suffering you feel every night. Torment is a small price to pay for serenity.

                            Why you should fear us

                            We would rather not be feared, though we appreciate respect. Of course, a reluctance to do harm does not equate to an inability to do so. We no longer share the same weaknesses, but we have strengths you can’t conceive. Humbling you will require penance, but we have a very long time to balance the scales.

                            Why we should fear ourselves

                            We have a responsibility to the Naga Monk to make merit in his name, but do our methods actually make us better Kindred, or are we simply more polite monsters? Is it possible for creatures whose existences rely on suffering to ever create less of it? The best of us consider this question nightly, but thus far, we have no answer.

                            Parent Clan: Nosferatu

                            Nicknames: Coiled Snakes, Sakadagami (“once-returners”), Beggar Monks (abroad)

                            Bloodline Bane (The Curse of Detachment): The Maiyarap teach that letting go of all cravings will pave the way to enlightenment. However, some of the things Kindred crave, such as acceptance and community, are what help them cling to their Humanity, and denying them makes it harder to hold on. A Beggar Monk [REDACTED]. Due to this, Maiyarap hold themselves to exceptionally high standards of behavior and self-control, knowing they cannot count on attachments to others to stave off the Beast.

                            Disciplines: Auspex, Nightmare, Obfuscate, Resilience

                            Treasures of the Mae Ji

                            The Naga Monk became the Mae Ji’s student, and he was able to halt his slide into total degeneracy from what he learned. He passed these teachings onto his childer, who’ve spread this philosophy across Thailand and beyond in an attempt to redeem their repentant founder.

                            The practical results of these teachings are the Treasures of the Mae Ji, states of being that permanently alter a vampire’s mind and body. On a surface level, the Treasures resemble the Coils of the Dragon, but their methods differ greatly. The Coiled Snakes aren’t scientists; they’re philosophers. They use meditation, asceticism, and the recitation of sutras to hone their forms, with a focus on enduring intense physical suffering to strengthen the mind.

                            To an outsider, the Maiyarap’s methods may seem crude, or even needlessly cruel. Initiates are often made to remove their own limbs and leave them for the sun to better understand the impermanence of all things, and many supplicants fall into torpor or meet Final Death while training. All of this is an acceptable price to the Maiyarap, for they understand that to suffer in the name of making merit is a merit-making act in and of itself, and thus benefits the dharma of both student and teacher.

                            There are three Treasures recognized by the Maiyarap: Anicca (Impermanence), Dukkha (Suffering), and Tahna (Thirst). Anicca is identical to the Coil of the Ascendant and Dukkha is the same as the Coil of the Wyrm, though the Maiyarap focus on harshly taming the Beast rather than growing closer to it. The Mystery of the Voivode is unknown to the Coiled Snakes.

                            All Maiyarap may purchase Treasures for the same cost as Coils of the Dragon. They must choose a favored Treasure, but this confers no cost break, and Status in their bloodline limits the number of dots a character can learn in other Treasures. Treasures and Coils that alter the same aspects of the Kindred condition are incompatible with each other, and so no character can ever purchase both Coil of the Ascendant and the Treasure of Anicca, Coil of the Wyrm and the Treasure of Dukkha, or Coil of the Voivode and the Treasure of Tahna. Theoretically, mixing the studies in other ways is possible; for example, a character could learn the Coil of the Wyrm from the Ordo Dracul and the Treasure of Anicca from the Maiyarap.

                            Treasured Terminology

                            Like the Dragons, the Sakadagami have unique titles based on their mastery of different Treasures. A beginning student who has learned up to the first two tiers of a Treasure refers to herself as having “a mind like an open sore.” A more advanced learner identifies as having “a mind like a flash of lightning.” Finally, a master of all five tiers of a Treasure can claim to have “a mind like a diamond.”

                            Tahna: The Thirst

                            This Treasure focuses on releasing Kindred from their most powerful craving. Students of this Treasure routinely starve themselves of Vitae, and are forbidden from feeding on blood that is not offered freely in order to learn both patience and humility. Diamond minds of this Treasure can subsist on minuscule quantities of blood, and it is through Tahna that the Naga King denies the weight of true torpor despite his immense age.

                            Denial •

                            The first step in forgoing cravings is to cease forming new ones, for yourself or others. By spending a point of Willpower, the vampire’s Vitae does not cause blood addiction for the rest of the evening. Furthermore, she enjoys a bonus equal to her dots in Tahna to any rolls made to resist Vitae addiction.
