Contrast with someone who isn't around dead bodies all the time, and even seeing a dead person might be a breaking point.

                                                            This is why answering the Integrity questions are pretty important for determining a player character's threshold, because if you answer that your soldier's idea of what is the worst thing he could imagine himself doing? is "betraying his allies" then the ST might rule that, because it's dramatic and appropriate, that the soldier being put in a situation where he has intent to hurt his comrades would constitute a breaking point.

                                                            For NPCs the ST has to eyeball these things, but with a general eyes towards what people are capable of and normative society, I'd certainly say that pointing a gun at your spouse with intent to kill them is a breaking point for most people, because that behavior certainly qualifies as possibly violating a personal moral code, is definitely considered unacceptable in society, and the situation of being made to kill them unwillingly is certainly a traumatic experience as well.

                                                            Your Storyteller has to make that call. I'd advise it for normal people, maybe not the mobster who kills people for a living? that the act is a breaking point in addition to the result. But yes, as we are both saying, it comes down to individual people, how that character's integrity is conceptualized and whether it is dramatically appropriate or not for the ST to check for Integrity.
