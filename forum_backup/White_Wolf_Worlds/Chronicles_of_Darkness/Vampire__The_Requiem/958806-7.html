        Originally posted by Holy View Post
        I don't see a need to separate the concept of conflicting play-styles from conflicting genre-styles. I see them as part of the same overall package.
        And that's the heart of my problem. They're not. They're completely different things. Conflicting playstyles refers to players not able to game together because they want different things out of the game and play characters in ways that clash, and not have fun together. Conflicting genre styles (pretending for a moment that such thing exists, I don't think it does) happens at the meta-game level and chronicle planning by the ST, no player involvment necessary. For the record, a large part of my point is that superheroes and personal horror are not naturally conflicting story types. In fact, I will argue that personal horror is rather central to most superhero stories.

        In short, my point is that its not the style of the game that's the problem, its the players not meshing together. Blaming it on a genre is misleading. Using a genre as a way to shout down people that enjoy that style game (and, yes, there are successful Vampire games run in that style) is bad taste.

        And here I think you are knee-jerking.
        Actually, I'm trying to point out something indictive of human nature. Humans tend towards extremes, not balance. You reject one extreme, you will naturally gravitate to the other instead of reaching a balance point. Its part of the reason nature always appears in cycles, rising and falling, instead of stasis at a single point.

        If a game is out of balance, and you believe there are too many superheroics and not enough personal horror, what is your advice on fixing that balance?
        My advice is "what is most fun for the players and ST involved?" As long as the mix is fun for those involved, its not an issue. Acknowledging that others tables prefer different ratio, hells, different ingredients than your table is also good. You don't want superheroes at your table? Fine. That's your perogative. But its not fair to say they don't belong in the game at all.

        I am really, really sure, yes. If I had to keep one aspect in a vampire game, it would be personal horror. It wouldn't be dependent romance, it would't be steam punk, it wouldn't be wizards or orcs or zombies. I think it would be far easier to drop those latter concepts than the former. That's what I'm arguing.
        I'm not asking you to drop anything. Rather, I'm pointing out that Vampire is all these things by default. Which is my argument from the beginning. Dropping everything out but "personal horror" is reductive and an inacurate depictation of the game.

                            Originally posted by nofather View Post

                            Rose has said lack of room. I'm inclined to believe her.

                            The Circle and the Brood still have different goals, because the Circle is about more than 'not being like the Lancea et Sanctum,' and the Brood was never about that. The big difference in their belief system is that the Circle doesn't believe in losing all their Humanity, while the Brood does. The Brood believe, though this is putting it very simply, their vampirism is a shard of the Adversary that they're being blocked from embracing due to their humanity. Getting rid of all their humanity, embracing not just the Beast but what they believe to be its source, is their goal. Whereas the Circle believe their Embrace is a gift, and the Beast is a part of it. Abandoning oneself fully to it is as damning to the self as much as losing yourself completely to addiction would be as a living person. Their every step down the ladder of Humanity is meant to be fully appreciated, but one simply cannot do that at Humanity 0.

                            It's true they have connections, as they are both a persecuted party, they follow pagan beliefs, though the Brood's are more in line with gnosticism, and stepping down the ladder is one aspect of their philosophy, but on their journey the Brood look eagerly ahead to becoming a native and putting their human existence behind them, while the Circle knows they can't appreciate their Gift without remaining a little like what the once were.

                            If anything, the Brood is more like the strix, or more likely to follow them. But there's an added social component the strix lack and the strix encourage the abandoning of humanity the way the Beast does, not out of any embrace of something greater like the Brood, but because it's easy and feels good.

                            Regarding the rite, I do think it's telling that there's a Theban sorcery ritual that offers service to Belial, considering Theban sorcery are granted to the Lance by Amoniel, who claims to be an angel of the Lord. But given what we know of Secrets of the Covenants, that it's based on the clanbook approach with fiction and mechanics that link up to it, I'd expect there to be at least one section if not focusing than involving Belial's Brood, perhaps reports from a Lancea et Sanctum Inquisitor seeking out a heretic who absconded with knowledge of the ritual.


                            Another line I've used a time or two, to summarize the Circles Views on their Humanity

                            "Our humanity is Infinitely precious, that is what makes it fitting as a sacrifice to the gods"

                            I could see them in that context being offended by the broods deliberate treating humanity as something unwanted and unneeded the Circle SACRIFICES humanity

                            The brood treats it as a prison.
