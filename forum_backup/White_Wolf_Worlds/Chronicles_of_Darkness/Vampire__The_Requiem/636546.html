    [2ED] Blood Bonds - Totally Overpowered

    08-22-2015, 10:43 PM
    Run today a crossover game of Beast & Vampire 2ED - and I found out that Vinculum, Blood Bonds mechanics are totally overpowered and illogically imbalanced.

    Basically, they are "instant Win Button" solution for any Kindred and there no restrictions on just freshly Embraced character to make total slave from younger Prince, mages Hierarch or any non-demigod status supernatural. JUST. FROM. EMBRACE.

    I will go here on use corebook quote to ease discussion and show mine point.

    Originally posted by Requiem 2ED
    Any time a character imbibes a point or more of Vitae, it creates or reinforces a blood bond. The bond comes in three stages, usually at the first, second, and third drinks.

    Kindred characters can attempt to resist the bond by spending Willpower. Make a Blood Potency roll, minus the number of Vitae ingested. The Willpower point does not add to this roll. If successful, that drink does not add to the bond. However, the character suffers Vitae addiction normally. Any further attempts to resist the bond from the same vampire suffer a cumulative –1 die penalty; even ancient Kindred cannot resist the bond forever. Mortals have no such defense.
    So on crossover play I assumed that Blood Potency can be exchanged on to it's Supernatural Advantage equivalents - Primal Urge, Gnosis, Lair, etc. But it's not important here.

    Thing is: Any non-super powerful monster character can almost surely lose the test, even after drink a tea/coffee/whatever with small dosage of freshly Embraced vampire. To imbibes a point of Vitea coffee or tea is enough ( Vitea is about distilled life force, not the whole liters of blood ) and this one "dosage" means -1 penalty. On victim with Blood Potency 1 it means Chance Roll even if she resists by the Willpower point. On Blood Potency 4 ( level of young Prince, or mages Ruling Council and many Masters ) you should be able to resist with Dice Pool 3, but you still are not sure of it. Maybe at Blood Potency 6 you are rather safe, with Dice Pool 5 to get success to resist. Maybe. And each other attempt after first success is much easier, as victim is more and more willing to do the same, from Vitea Addiction and artificial fond fillings to the character. And this mechanics works from yesterday Embraced vampires giving you blood!

    What I mean "non-super powerful monster characters" are beings with Supernatural Advantage on levels 1-5 ( no superhuman stats ) - so, like 90-95% of local supernatural society. Very large majority of vampires, mages, werewolves, or any other monsters just going to lose on first roll and become living puppets of vampires.

    There is no reason as to why starting neonates had no Blood Bonds on Protectorate Alpha werewolves or mages Ruling Councils. When vampires maybe open about their secret to not drink one another, other monsters does not have this instant knowledge, and can fall through by this. Or does suddenly or supernatural knows all weaknesses of their splats? Even then, beings like Prometheans or Beasts, with no real society, would not know, most of the time, to not drink vampires bloods.


    Basically, any kind of play between vampires and non-vampires makes the non-vampires PCs slaves to Kindred. Or that dedicated Kindred players can have his freshly Embraced PC rule the mages, werewolves or any other supernatural society from shadows, having slave Hierarch or Alpha Wolf. It's clearly overpowered...

    Possible solution: Resistance to Blood Bond should work as any other Mind Control resistant rolls and use Resolve + Blood Potency ( or equivalents ) as Dice Pool. Using second Trait in resistant makes that vampire character just cannot take ones free will on first drink ( even starting characters will have like 3-4 in resist Dice Pool, so the should pass ) and superhuman monsters elders would be mostly unaffected. Also, high Resolve characters would be rather safe to multiple Blood interactions from the same Kindred, and this mean that our political driven monster characters will much more willing to interact with vampires, falsely confident they can resist call of Blood much easier. What a beautiful self-deception. Win-Win scenario, I think.

                                                          In my game, I would not accept a blood bond through a cup of tea of coffee. Let's see why:

                                                          1st, I absolutly now that vitae points and lethal points are abstract and I always thought that giving them numerical values as canon is wrong. But still, it can help us to evaluate proportions in relation to one another, while recognizing that the result should not be a strong golden rule.

                                                          2st, let's try to evaluate in real values what it means. 1 vitae = 1 lethal damage. You can kill a human by drinking him dry. An average human = 5 damage. An average human has also, from biological standarts, 5 to 6 liters of blood. We can approximately say that 1 vitae = 1 liter of blood. To avoid making this a canon, let's say it can depend from the narrative context (defined by the ST or a dialogic construction between ST and player), and that 1 vitae = +_ from 1/3 to 3 liters of blood. Generally, specially during the game, it's not useful to give real life numbers to these conceptual indicators, but let's do it here for the purpose. As a blood bond equals 1 vitae. It means that your cup of tea or coffee should contain at least, in our largest case, 1/3 liters. It means you don't have coffee in your cup, if you fill it with blood.

                                                          3st, vitae is not blood. It is a magical transformation of blood through the vampire's being. It has more potency. It has a magical taste. I would say that the blood does not liquefy easily in a liquid. One cannot dring half a cup of tea without knowing what he's drinking. The magical potency of the vitae is stronger that the blackness of your ethiopian coffee. Of course, there's the taste, the magical appealing to the taste of vitae. You feel you're drinking something very special, but you bloody know what it is : not blood, but something that you can directly categorizye to "something f*kin weird that is link to human blood".

                                                          These are to me 2 good reasons to make the blood bond a plot device (it is difficult to do it, it does not only require a good roll or a wise scene, it requires a whole machination if you don't physically or magically force your target). Btw, I would use the Social Maneuvering System before letting anything that has such a consequence on the game to go.

                                                          With an exception of course : the blood bound the PC is making is really good for the story.
