                The Underworld contains ghosts, not souls. It is not an afterlife; it is simply a plane of existence which resonates with the echoes humanity makes upon death.

                As for souls, a vampire can indeed steal or even devour something which people call a soul, but if that soul is in any way connected to your conscious existence or any sort of afterlife, the setting is mum on it. After all, if a vampire steals and destroys your soul, you might not even actually notice at first. You'll eventually wind down into what amounts to exceptionally severe depression (there's insight, here, into the authors' views on mental illness, I am sure), but you still remain you.

                Again, none of these things give you any more reason to believe in the afterlife than anything else. The only reason they make you draw any sort of connection is because the word "soul" has been associated with the afterlife and the Underworld explicitly models itself off of humanity's ideas relating to death. If the soul was called the elan vital, you'd never associate it with any sort of afterlife, any more than you do Vitae. Death spirits model themselves off of humanity's ideas relating to death as well but no-one assumes they're actually psychopomps because we know it's just a spirit.

                Ghosts, in the Chronicles of Darkness, are not the person they resemble. They're just echoes. Ectoplasmic imprints a dying person makes on the fabric of the universe.

                        The 'soul' is part of a living being, or undead being in the case of vampires. It's in Twilight with you but otherwise doesn't do much except get hurt or strengthened by your actions. It's loss is represented by the Soulless Condition, but if ones soul is lost, it can be replaced by any soul, apparently.

                        Sometimes ghosts are called 'souls' like in 'lost souls,' but they are not representative of the actual soul of a person. This has been discussed repeatedly across the board in various threads but it's easy to miss too.

                        I think part of the problem is that specifics of the soul are unknown, in-world, to most gamelines and it's been depicted in different forms, with different importance, in different games. In Mage and arguably vampire it's a source of power, but in mage it's also a bridge to the Astral, in Promethean it's part of an end-goal as they don't have souls but their condition is far worse than Soulless, in Demon it's an anchor for soul pacts and is, apparently, in Beast it's replaced by a Horror. None of these necessarily exclude the others, but each game usually describes them or their impact on things a bit differently.
