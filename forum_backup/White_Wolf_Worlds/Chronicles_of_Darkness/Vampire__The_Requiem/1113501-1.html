                        Ok, Ok. I'll admit to unnecessary flippancy and apologize. Actually the direction of conversation is interesting enough I may well have a struggle not to *try* the approach myself. There are some significant implications on vampire society and play that should be batted around.... but first, a contribution.

                        Daywalker (Condition)
                        You have succeeded in thwarting your beast and remained conscious under the eye of the sun while it is thrust into slumber. With it the curse sleeps, but that can be a drawback as well as a benefit. You may only spend vitae for Blush of Life and do not gain vitae from feeding; similarly your vitae is powerless to embrace childer or ghoul mortals. Persistent Discipline effects are reduced by 1 for each point of Blood Potency, and Discipline and Devotion activation rolls are penalized by 1 for each point of Blood Potency.

                        Sunlight burns you more like an unfortunate sufferer of porphyria, and less like a supernatural scouring--reduce all damage a severity step (lethal to bashing, aggravated to lethal) and increase all times (minutes to hours, turns to minutes). Mundane sunlight protections are much more effective as well: full covering (only eyes revealed) reduces your effective Blood Potency for damage calculation by three levels, and a heavily overcast day by a further two levels; lesser covering may provide smaller reductions to effective Blood Potency. If your effective Blood Potency is reduced to zero for sunlight damage, no damage is suffered even under extended exposure.

                        Your sleeping Beast sullenly bothers you less: take a bonus of half your Humanity (rounded down) to resist frenzy. However it also aids you less in resisting supernatural effects or projecting your own nature: halve your Blood Potency (rounded down) for purposes of Lashing Out or resisting in a Clash of Wills.

                        When Daywalker resolves, impose the Tempted condition, as your Beast remains none too happy with your shenanigans.

                        Possible Sources: Spending a Willpower point and succeeding in a Willpower contest with successes equal to your Blood Potency, to remain awake at dawn.

                        Resolution: Nightfall.

                        Beat: Take a beat if your weakened condition puts you in danger of destruction or breach of the Masquerade, requiring more elaborate measures than your supernatural powers would usually provide for.


                        Edit and amend!

                        --Khanwulf

                                    Originally posted by Resplendent Fire View Post
                                    That is an excellent starting point, and has me thinking about possibilities of keeping some degree of sun burnination. Thanks.

                                    I mean I want staying awake to be easier at the very least, but this is a nice compromise.

                                    Yeah, emphasis on starting point. For example, I forgot to include a bonus to resist frenzy--which could be up to Humanity/2? Also, what effect on BP for Clash of Wills purposes? (Important when resisting other supernaturals/ephemerals).

                                    Add:
                                    Your sleeping Beast sullenly bothers you less: take a bonus of half your Humanity (rounded down) to resist frenzy. However it also aids you less in resisting supernatural effects or projecting your own nature: halve your Blood Potency (rounded down) for purposes of Lashing Out or resisting in a Clash of Wills.

                                    I think the thread deserves some practical thinking on the in-game repercussions of vampires being able to choose to set aside most of their supernatural ability and walk in daylight (at least when at low BP--and Dracula had recently arisen from torpor, no?). Starting with the above:

                                    1. You cannot heal during the day.
                                    2. You cannot feed, which means daytime is mostly useless for hunting unless you're into kidnapping for a larder.
                                    3. Your physical disciplines *may* still provide a benefit, if they are higher than your BP.
                                    4. Your non-physical disciplines are mostly useless, the exceptions being Animalism 1, Auspex 1, 2 (first uses) and 3; Dominate 1, 3 and sometimes 2; Majesty 1 and 2; Nightmare 1; Obfuscate 1; Protean 1 (soil only); and a few Devotions that do not require vitae (and that I don't care to research). Even those that you can attempt, you are at a significant penalty to, depending on BP.
                                    5. You start the day down at least one Willpower point--possibly more if your BP is edging up there and you had to spend for more dice on the roll. --You are tired, in other words!
                                    6. Your defenses are severely degraded: any mortal is a threat and can at least put you into torpor. Since the condition wears off at nightfall regardless of your state, being left in a field means you'll start smoking tomorrow, anyway.
                                    7. Frenzy is going to be a potential issue in the evening. This worsens for repeated Daywalking, if meaningful interaction with Touchstones is not feasible. (There may be other conditions that should be applied, more than just Tempted.)

                                    Keep in mind that you are thereby actually *Weaker* than Ghouls under this condition, as written!

                                    I'm not sure what the Lancea would think of their flock setting aside the curse to walk in day, but if it's just a thing some vampires *can* do, probably they'd look at it as an opportunity to further the Kindred mandate for harassing mortals. Or something. Someone can do Sanctum better than I.

                                    Benefits?

                                    1. You can see the sun without your eyeballs running like wax. That's a thing.
                                    2. Significantly easier interaction with Touchstones.
                                    3. Ghouls no longer absolutely necessary to stand in line for you at the local license branch. (For example.) Avoiding this codependency plot is a significant twist from base VtR, and makes ghouls more of a voluntary matter or something you slide into needing as BP increases.
                                    4. As you rise in BP, your alien-ness and estrangement from the day increases. This slow degradation serves as a second note to the sudden loss of the day at embrace.
                                    5. Instead of being the All Night Society you have vampires embedded like ticks in mortal society much more deeply. That weird guy at the library all day? Your office colleague who always has a half-full coffee cup you never see him drink? The woman in the power heels at the municipal department building who no one knows her job? The guy in a beater hoodie hanging with the homeless all the time at the park even when it's 90 out? Heck--that shaggy, wolf-like dog in the neighborhood who lives in the culvert? Your high-school classmate who vanished over the summer two years ago and now makes straight-As despite all-night band sessions? Absolutely anyone and everyone 9-5 could be a vampire waiting to shed their mortal skin--figuratively, of course. Note: being a modern vampire is rather easier than during ages before electric lights, when people just went to bed as the sun went down.
                                    6. Much more personal time as a vampire--more similar to mortals, in fact, who can choose to push themselves over a week or so to get more done (reading, study, other activity) but have to crash eventually. As a vampire your willpower WILL go out if you pursue frequent daywalking, leaving you more vulnerable at night when the real horrors are out. Keep in mind that vampires do not, RAW, receive a Willpower point per day, and if they're up all day then they wouldn't have a "waking" moment at nightfall anyway (though they should spend a vitae point per normal as the sun sets).
                                    7. Hanging out with other cross-splat supernaturals now a LOT easier to justify and arrange: werewolves? Sure, be a pack member! Mages? A valuable research assistant and backup muscle. All the others? You're now relevant as opposed to being a literal boat-anchor half of every 24-hour cycle. This in fact makes a time-skipping cross-splat chronicle more viable as well, as the vampire can serve as a narrative anchor as others age and die around her. (I'm reminded of the first, Promethean, fiction in Tales of the Dark Eras.)

                                    Another note to this essay: you won't have BP 4 or 5 vampires pursuing any kind of Daywalking lifestyle for long. Either they burn out their Willpower pool in a couple of nights, or they end up failing a roll and missing planned activities. That means either Torpor becomes a thing, or you see a LOT more XP dumped into upping Willpower. Keep in mind Willpower has no bound, so a truly dedicated Daywalker could accumulate 20 or 30 dice to throw at the problem of consistency in the face of rising BP. Most won't do that however, and slide inevitably into the slow horror of the night.

                                    --Khanwulf

                                            I think I am going to allow spending of one vitae/turn for the sake of healing, melding with the earth, or using blush of life. Also allow feeding.

                                            I was thinking of limiting all disciplines to zero or one dot. Admittedly, one dot allows the use of earth meld and doesn't need to cost vitae.

                                            I think I'd like it to be easier for higher BP vampires to walk in the daylight and put the real barrier at BP 6 where elders gain access to superhuman traits, a dependency upon supernatural blood, and much larger vitae pools.

                                            I like the need to spend one WP to stay awake. Given what I said above I'd probably be more inclined to make the roll requirement start at BP 6. So 1-5 can just spend a wp and stay up. Or maybe 1-3 can spend a willpower, and 4-5 also have to roll...and 6-7 roll even higher, 8-9 even higher and 10 nigh impossible. And spending a WP to stay awake or make the roll to stay awake. As opposed to gaining WP for resting. It's a nice cost/benefit thing.

                                            I'm going to keep damage the same as it is at night - lethal becomes bashing, etc. Baseline vampiric advantages can stay. Characters are going to be more vulnerable anyway because of limits. The only Kindred who won't really feel the pinch are the BP 1 neonates who only have one dot in all of their Disciplines.

                                                Glad to be of service, but please, credit to all previous posters who put down idea seeds. Now there's a framework to adjust from--and easier to loosen it as you're inclined to do, than tighten it up.

                                                After speaking with my player we're inclined to give it a go as well. I think there are significant setting benefits to making the whole "vamps only at night" thing MUCH more uncertain. I do need to consider if a roll is really needed below higher BP: it will run characters out of Willpower anyway, limiting their activity to bursts. Imposing a dice check as well cuts that burst in half (roughly), except for very low BP, but the original intent was the idea that as Willpower drops the dawn roll becomes more and more difficult, eventually forcing more Willpower on just the roll and an inevitable failure....

                                                Inevitable failure SOUNDS great as a theme, but is less so when you've planned a climactic daytime scene that's now derailed. Something to ponder.

                                                Note that the condition as written above does not affect usual damage--only sun damage. It does impair Resilience benefits however, so if yon Kindred is used to being a tank he's in for a rude shock during the day!

                                                My inclination on limits is to avoid removing access to skills and abilities learned (for example, the condition does not remove skills and abilities greater than 5), in a nod toward Sanctity of Merits. Instead, the character faces a knowable penalty they can manage through Torpor or mystical means that suppress BP temporarily. I do like the core idea that Vitae during the day is effectively useless--except for Blush of Life, which was thrown in as a gimmee that could just as easily be a Merit or Coil/Scale effect.

                                                Last comment: keep in mind that if Daywalking is a normal kind of thing your VtR setting ought to have vamps with Merits, Devotions, Rituals and Coils dedicated to manipulating the costs and drawbacks thereof--much as the Coil of the Ascendent does (would do?). So consider if you want to just charge extra XP for making the vamp's life easier, or need to raise the whole floor a bit.
