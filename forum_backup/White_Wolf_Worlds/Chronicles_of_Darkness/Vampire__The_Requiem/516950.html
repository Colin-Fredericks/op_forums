          Never experimented with either of those in charge, but I'd say that, typically, both Ordo and Circle are content to let someone else handle the boring leadership stuff, as long as they can pursue their interests (relatively) unhindered. That having been said, if they've been under repressive regimes in the past, they make take charge specifically so that they can ensure their works can continue without interference.

          The Ordo would likely use whatever power structure they felt was most efficient for their city, probably aping whatever had existed before. Strict age meritocracy if the city had previously been Invictus, representative democracy if it had been Carthian (granted, gross oversimplified generalizations, but you get the point.) They might even just oust whoever had been in charge previously, then let whoever was interested step into the leadership vacuum while they go back to their research.

          The Circle would probably put whatever internal power structure they have in place in practice on a larger scale, with varying degrees of inclusiveness for non-Circle vampires depending on how pissed they are with everyone else. In a city with an "everyone vs. Circle" mentality, you'd probably find a government more oppressive than anything the Invictus could come up with, likely involving incredibly ironic "witch hunts" against anyone opposed to the Circle, or anyone the Circle thinks is opposed to them, or anyone the Circle just happens to be cross with at the time. In a more open and accepting city, the Circle might be as "benevolent" as the highest Carthian ideal, giving everyone a say and working for the best interests of all Kindred. Of course, the average would be somewhere in the middle.

          But really, I don't think that much changes based on which Covenant is in charge. Everyone will come up with similar sets of rules (based on the Traditions), and there will be harsh punishments for breaking them, with Final Death dispensed for a large number of infractions. Everyone will try and get most Kindred to sign up with their Covenant, bolstering their numbers, and have internal measures to weed out those they don't think are worthy. Older and more powerful vampires will still be the ones in charge more often than not, either because of tradition (Invictus), merit (Carthians), priestly tenure (Sanctum), knowledge (Ordo), or mystic power (Circle.) Young vampires will still be at the mercy of those with more power in the system, and those who buck the system will be dealt with harshly.

                  When the Ordo Dracul rule a city, I imagine that its done through the Coil of the Voivoid, with a dash of "We know your dirty secrets, so do what we say or we air your dirty laundry." I see them as almost a kind of para-military bureaucracy. Everyone is part of a linear structure, five neonates under an ancillae, five ancillae under an elder, the elders making up the Primogen who answer to the Dragon Prince. Each branch has a different job - that elder holds the Sheriff position, and every ancillae and neonate under him are in charge of security and feeding grounds. That elder is the Senschel with attendant harpies (official position). Third is the Minister of Sorcery, or something. And so on. Everyone has well defined positions and jobs, and you are rewarded with access to such and such feeding grounds when you complete your jobs. In your off time, you are allowed to pursue anything you like, so long as it doesn't interfere with anyone else's free time or studies.

                  The problem with the Ordo Dracul is that they're basically the mages of the vampire world, in that they're obsessed with arcane knowledge. They don't do a lot of socializing. The main reason the Ordo rise to power in a city, I imagine, is the same thing that drives mages to a city and establish a Consilium - there's a Mystery there that is attracting their attention and helping them with their arcane research. So, in that respect, anything they do is likely going to revolve around protecting and maintaining their previous Wyrm Nest / Mystery.

                  With the Ordo, its always about gathering knowledge, so having the whole spy network and blackmail with truths. After that, a bit of brainwashing through Coil and mystery-cult like action. I'm seeing a model that mimics the whole cult of personality around Dracula, with the three Sworn paths being enforced on the whole city. And the Prince not caring about other covenants, or if they're happy, so long as they don't get in the way of his interests.


                  -----------------------------------------------


                  With the Crone, however, its always about embracing the Beast. Their domains are probably easiest to understand, because its all but no rule. They're a very laize faire style. They run off respect and power - the Dark Mother is the one in charge because she's the biggest and baddest Kindred around, and if you piss her off, then you're in deep shit. Otherwise, have fun. Just shy of pure anarchy.

                  Darwinism is the name of the game. If you do stupid things that piss off the bigger and badder predator, or even just cross his path on the wrong night, you get what's coming to you. "Do what thou wilt is the whole of the law." This is probably the most brutal form of tyranny, because its the rough kind that arises from pure anarchy. Rule by brute strength of force, and the laws can change on whim, night by night.

                      There's a city Alice visits in the Gangrel clan book that is ruled by Circle of the Crone, and possibly solely populated by them. They are viciously territorial, suggesting a blood hunt at the thought of some Sanctified who think they need to spread the word inside this heathen territory. But essentially everyone gathers for an occult ritual and shares some blood on an altar and they don't really get into the whole ruling thing, which is presumably pretty sparse.

                      Damnation City goes a bit farther into this, and I think the covenant books do as well but I'm not sure.

                      Apparently the Ordo Dracul 'rule' the fewest amount of cities with the Circle right behind them. Damnation City has examples of Princes from each covenant. With the Ordo Dracul it suggests they are mostly hands off rulers, with a single Master setting some markers for Ordo Dracul hotspots and ley-lines down and letting everyone know they're off limits to everyone who isn't Ordo Dracul. They also have the 'Gifts for Master' custom, where once a year vampires are supposed to get a gift for the Master and place it at some predetermined site. The Master's primogen council is a tight-knit group of other Dragons, hard to break into for outsiders. It suggests using themes from the movies The Prestige or The Illusionist, to help portray a strange, occult science mystique. But above all, they don't really care about other vampires, or even the power it affords them, just what it gets them in the long run.

                      The Crone Prince is probably an anomaly, as she sort of channels her various gods or spirits while having a right hand do a lot of the ruling at her order. But the Circle of the Crone ruling is pretty easy to guess, it's a theocracy, but not an organized religion-based one, instead one with pagan gods and base, primeval laws. With the example Prince, it's set up so that essentially while you don't have to be a member of the Circle, everyone has to come to the rituals because she demands to be heard, and the Crone respected. Those who don't do not fare well. It suggests there's a lot of strange laws passed and rescinded seemingly at a whim depending on what the gods tell her.

                                          The Ordo Dracul have an origin in old world nobility, and while it doesn't dominate them it informs them. So you might expect an archaic looking Princedom, the kind of thing more stereotypically associated with the Invictus (who are in fact eminently receptive to new levers of power). On the other hand, Dragons reserve their harshest discipline for their own subordinates, so the Ordo probably wouldn't lord it over covenants to the extent that the Invictus might. On the downside, it also does experiments - and if it's in charge, it's going to feel confident about doing big experiments. So expect a lot of weird but temporary directives, which may well be enforced by stricter punishments than the lasting laws. And those sentenced to death will be more like to disappear into a lab never to be seen again than executed in what passes among the Kindred for public.

                                          The Circle of the Crone in a sense doesn't rule at all. There are particular interests it has - things centring on its worship (however the local cult worships), and the needs of its high-ranking members - and the law is essentially "don't fuck with those." If you're lucky, you'll know what "those" are. Otherwise, it will mostly leave you alone - though it will also mostly leave alone anyone who wants to come after you.

                                              For the Chronicle I'm currently running, the current Prince is the Ordo Kogaion, who runs the city pretty much entirely hands off.
                                              There's a yearly gathering to present new childer so they aren't captured by the Sheriff under suspicion of being with the Undercity (Vampire refuge built when the Lancea were running the show, populated by vamps that refuse to acknowledge him as prince) and all parties can discuss/debate/bitch at each other about things, but that's pretty much it as city management goes.

                                              Really, the whole thing is set up so as to provide ample opportunities to try and overthrow the current Prince, who plays out sort of like the cipher example in Damnation City.
                                              Because he acts that way though, the Carthians, Invictus, the Primogen and the Unaligned are all confused as hell and don't really feel confident they know what's going on.
                                              They're also not too keen on burning him out on their own, since he and his right-hand man wiped out the last of the Circle and Lancea when they decided on having a meeting up in one night of fighting to end their war, there's rumours that he actually can walk in sunlight with no issue, and he always seems to know what their doing most nights (I mean, he wouldn't have mentioned the hospital if he didn't know something about how there's a new ghoul on staff, right?).

                                              So... I have this city's Ordo chapter run the city like it's a big game, waiting to see which Kindred crack first and, far more interestingly, how.

                                                      I always end up basing my city power structures off the Invictus for the Dragons. The Prince is the eldest, most powerful Dragon, and beneath them are their inner circle. Each member of the inner circle is responsible for the training and supervision of the other Kindred within their domain. There's less social events, and other meaningless frippery, but a lot more resistance to other non-secular parties moving in.

                                                      The Crone I see being the most hands off, and second only to the Carthians in terms of most easily divided. I would use covens, in about 4-5 Kindred each, practicing their own form of Cruac, with their own pantheons, and trying to play nice with the neighboring covens. I might introduce a council of 3-5, Maid, Matron, Crone, Beast, and Man; essentially the true believers. They would be able to act safely, because their identities are hidden by the masks they wear. I don't think the nature of their religious practices lend to much unification and cohesion, except in the face of religious incursion.

                                                          An Idea:

                                                          For the past 100 years, the City had been ruled by "The Union", a Carthian form of government modelled heavily off the labor movement of the early 1900s and influenced heavily by the philosophy of Eugene V. Debs. There was just one problem, the Union only allowed some members (who happened to be white and men) to vote, even though everyone owed "dues" (generally these were paid to the Board of Managers who ran the Union in the form of favors). Sick of this system, a mysterious Caribbean woman named "the Crow" swept into the City with a group of four other loyal Kindred. They brought with them substantial skills in Cruac, and within the first week, whatever sleight the Board of Managers had caused to the Crow were erased as all the members of the Board met gruesome ends.

                                                          Nine years years later, the Crones, mostly concerned with revenge, have set up a shadow government over the largely Carthian population. The Crow appointed an "Emergency Board of Supervisors" from a loyal members of the Carthians, and has forbid any elections. On the surface the Emergency Board handles everything, but it's no secret they are in complete fear of the Cruac that the Crow and her small Cult of Legba wield. The Cult is content to let the mundane aspects of rule be left to their Carthian pawns, while the real power is maintained by them through a system of fear where they are largely invisible. Being visited by members of the Cult is never considered a good thing, and the only time they'll visit you is when you're alone. They've visited every Kindred in the City like this and it has tended to drive most of the Carthians closer together. Unfortunately, the Cult does not take too kindly too sedition, and mentioning the name of the Cult (even casually) can lead to secret visits or mysterious cruac rituals affecting one.

                                                          The only real public role of the Cult comes through the Crow leading monthly dance parties instead of a formal Elysium.
