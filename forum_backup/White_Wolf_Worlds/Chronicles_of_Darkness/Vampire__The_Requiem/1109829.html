                  I am gonna be the dissenting voice here, this year i have systematically been reading all the Requiem books so this is my recommendation.

                  After the Core rulebook i recommend the Covenants ones, NOT Secret of the covenant. The covenants books all expand on the covenants and make them make sense and stand alone as entities. All of the them are full of useful info about how the covenant works. Skip the last 2 chapters they all are an NPC chapter and 1 with the covenant special power which are mostly "obsolete" as 2nd edition has the rules you need for them. Weaker of them all is Circle of the Crone.

                  Damnation city: It explore on the neofeudal city but i will personally say read chapter 1, 2, 5 and 6. The others are chapters are not that good.

                  Blood Sorcery is good but only the first chapter. Threnodines are, complicated and even maybe not worth it now in second edition. The idea of the magic of the edgy poetry is cool enough but it actual power in 2nd edition is lukewarm.

                  Coteries is only useful for its first chapter which has info on multicovenant coterie but the rest is better covered on the covenant books.

                  The blood: Marginally useful. First chapter Chargen advice that you dont need if you have been playing for a while, Second chapter is optional rules for 1st edition. And 3rd chapter has fluff on the psychology of vampires which i found very basic. Ravenloft has better advice to be honest.

                  Danse Macabre: First chapter more ST advice on tier of game (Local, regional, global) pretty standard, Second chapter is alternative covenants which is mix bag so its gonna depend if one of them click with you and you wanna change the setting to incorporate them. 3rd chapter optional rules for 1st (Humanity, social combat etc). There is the nice rules for Gargoyles which there isnt a 2nd update yet.

                  Ghouls: Rules for animal ghouls and plant ghouls. The rest is pretty similar to Masquerade material.

                  Belials Brood: Good book but nothing to write home about. It gives you plenty of factions for Belials but nothing too shinny.

                  VII: Its not a good book honestly but VII is not very good by itself. What it gives are possible origins, wildly different for VII. All of them are horrible IMO but maybe you like one of them.


                  And this is where i get to the dissenting part. I dont recommend the Clanbooks. They are of limited use at best and glorified fiction at worst. Clans in Requiem are meaningless unless you, as a DM, want to make them a thing. Covenant is where you politic standing is gonna dwell and the Clanbooks are just fluffy stories about NPCs doing stuff in a context with little to no exploring.

                  Is my same grip with secret of the Covenant. Is a useless book. Its all feeling and no data on how the covenant manage things or run the day to day business. Reading about character doing stuff in a city which is controlled by a Carthian who forbids the Circle and Lancea is not really useful if i dont know how that city works and how and why it runs the way it does or why thats rare/common. Same with the Clan books, Clan dont have a political structure inherent to them so all i am reading is fiction, that really doesn't help me to run a game because the conditions that put the characters where they are, are unique to them. If you read masquerade books picture a book made entirely of the usual first fiction on the beginning of the book that most people skip.

                  For a better example: Reading the Secret of the covenants Lancea Sactum section vs The Lancea Sanctum covenant book. One dweel on a member who had a relationship with a Acolyte of the Circle, they broke up and both have to go to a meeting but the main plot is that the "prince" is acting erratically. The other explains to you the dogma, indoctrination and ranks of the Lancea. The latter is infinitely more useful to run a game.

                                              Originally posted by Crowley View Post


                                              i got enough budget. but mainly im looking to understand more about the Canon of the game, the big events, the evolution of vampires. to become a....Jack of all trades when it comes to its lore mainly
                                              The canon is essentially what you make of it. There are some definite arcs that get detailed in bits and pieces across the fluff of the various books, but you are free to ignore it. And even then, you can ignore established fluff: Secrets of the Covenants establishes that New York is about as close to a functional Carthian domain as you can get, but nothing's stopping you from saying, "In my Chronicle, the Invictus have ruled New York for centuries, and the Carthians only emerged after rebelling against the First Estate in the 60s/70s." Did the Carthians get started centuries before the books say they did by Italian vampires tired of the Invictus and Sanctified ruling with an iron fist back in the Renaissance? Is London the unofficial Mecca of the Circle of the Crone? It's all up to you to decide. And nothing's going to stop you from making that choice.

                                              But if you want the Cliffnotes version of how the books present it:

                                              Nobody knows exactly how the Kindred started in Requiem, but some of the more prominent rumors hold that the Daeva started in Mesopotamia with Inanna and Lilith, the Gangrel evolved out of immortal beast men on the borders of the Roman Empire, the Mekhet were priests of Set who tore out their souls and became undead to fight a Lovecraftian monster summoned by Akhenaten, the Nosferatu were creatures who ate a Lovecraftian creature deep in the earth, and the Ventrue were once the ancestor spirits of Troy. There's also the Julii, who are/were relatives of the Ventrue from Rome, but they got (mostly) wiped out after they went back on a deal with owl-like spirits called the Striges, who destroyed the Julii and Requiem's equivalent of the Camarilla around the time that the Western Roman Empire fell.

                                              The Lancea et Sanctum was founded by vampires who followed the teachings of Longinus, who claimed he had been Embraced by the blood of Jesus at the Crucifixion and told by God to be a prophet to vampires, teaching them to test the faithful and punish sinners. It suffered a major split in its membership over matters of doctrine and ritual in Elizabethan England.

                                              The Invictus is the sort-of successor of the Camarilla, but new fluff from SotC suggests that they were originally vampiric members of the Praetorian Guard who allied with the Striges and turned on the Julii, the Camarilla, and Rome itself after the Praetorian Guard was disbanded- or made a new pact with the Striges when it became clear that the Camarilla was failing and the Julii were losing their (tenuous) grip on reality to try and preserve what could be saved.

                                              The Ordo Dracul was founded by Dracula himself as a product of his attempts to understand the vampiric condition and overcome its limitations.

                                              The Carthian Movement was supposedly founded in France during the time of the Revolution as a rebellion against the tyranny of elders over neonates- though SotC suggests that the Movement may have started in New York.

                                              The Circle of the Crone is a loose federation of differing vampiric faiths who banded together to rally against oppression by the Lancea et Sanctum and to preserve their way of life.

                                              VII is a mysterious assortment of vampires who hunt other vampires, leaving only a symbol resembling the Roman numeral VII at the scene of the murders. They may have been founded by a "lost" clan called the Akhud, who originated in Sodom and Gomorrah.

                                              Haberdasher's Requiem Conversions and Homebrew

                                                      Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post

                                                      The what now?
                                                      The books Ancient Mysteries and Ancient Bloodlines.
                                                      Mysteries talks about playing elder characters (only not as in depth and, imo, not as well as Thousand Years of Night) as well as describing ten different eras around the world (with one example character per era).
                                                      Bloodlines expand somewhat on the same eras by introducing two bloodlines per era plus something extra per era.

                                                      They're good for getting some Kindred history. I personally love Black Streets of Babylon and Ancient Egypt (the only two eras that I consider actually ancient despite the names of the two books). Only problem is that the history probably won't show up much in modern nights, and the eras I've read aren't written in ways that allow you to use them as settings. Thousand Years of Night does the whole elder thing a lot better, and the few vampire specific entries in Dark Eras (+Companion) is much better at describing playable eras.
