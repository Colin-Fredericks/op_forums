                            Yes - these tit for tat firebacks - "but the mage could have had this/vampire this" do assume OOC knowledge the character (and player) may not have. I do not want to get into a weird "I quote you this, now you counter my logic" argument about what could go down theoretically, but will give a bit more insight into the Obfuscate 3 vamp taking down two mages.

                            The mages were trying to protect one of their children who had come home from a weekend with their father bloodbound. So the matter mage turned the house into a safe haven (improvised matter 4 spell); also rose a golem; both mages put on enhanced senses with life, the death mage went shadow form and also a death sheilding spell on both (won the clash of wills vs obfuscate). As paradox ramps quickly in combat - in our experience/playstyle - they didn't load on more protection, to leave some room for damage spells.

                            Two ghouls attacked the house to "rescue" the kid. Yes, the mages quashed them - fairly quickly, if I recall - but garnered some pdox. But then - in the lull afterwards (and as they both failed to penetrate the boosted stealth rolls of the mekhet, even though one of them was rolling 11 dice) they started the clean-up. Matter mage goes up to sooth the kid - only just awoke; death mage reforms to dispose of the bodies/deal with the cops after gunfire outside the house. Then the Mekhet cannot withold frenzy any more from the shadows, pops out and flattens the death mage, then goes up to do the same to the matter mage.

                            I twisted the rules a bit. The death mage should have been dead, but I allowed them both to wake up afterwards, bloodbound in a cellar on a vitae drip, being served water by the child.

                            Yes, I am sure this is playing badly by some standards - but it was a great fun and dramatic session. Sorry if we are playing it wrong. But the mages were prepped - for vampires. And although on the page we could argue "but I can counter this with that" - the way it worked out in play was very different. And mad craic.

                            If the players had all the knowledge - when and who and how many and from what direction and at what time etc - they could beat anything. But even as I start to incorporate time magic into my chronicle - with divination - it is limited by potency, gameplay and knowledge of the targets (as the acanthus can only foresee the future of things he can target; so himself is valid, but "my enemies" is partial at best).

                            Anyhow - work beckons. But I think a lot of the arguing on behalf of mages assumes a god's eye view. Which is perhaps the strength of mages, and armchair theorising, but is not always absolute in gameplay, even with time magic.
