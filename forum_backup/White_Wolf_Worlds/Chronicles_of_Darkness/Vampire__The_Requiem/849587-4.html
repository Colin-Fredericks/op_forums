        I feel like you are too focused on "types" of Frenzy. There is no such thing as a "fear frenzy" or "anger frenzy" in 2e. There is just frenzy. The goal is more important. Ask yourself instead "What does the Beast want right now?"

        So when a vampire is cornered and provoked to Frenzy somehow (hyperfocusing on this example seems also not good; as has been said, this scenario wouldn't likely trigger it to begin with), you don't go "what type of frenzy is this? anger frenzy" you go "what does the Beast want right now? It's cornered, and it wants to not be. It wants to stop being cornered." That answer defines how the vampire acts while frenzying. The vampire will do whatever it takes to stop being cornered. This could be anything from Lashing Out + Intimidate (the book explicitly says Predatory Aura is a valid means to the Frenzy's ends) to, yes, murdering the person in the way. That's up to PC interpretation.

            Cornered Beast

            You feel trapped and must escape at all costs. The closer your Beast lies to the surface, the worse this feeling becomes, as the devil inside seethes with resentment at your confinement. You must take direct action, either to escape the situation or to lash out at your perceived captors. All other non-reflexive actions require a willpower point to attempt and are capped by your Humanity. Rolls to resist frenzy suffer a penalty equal to the penalty you suffer on social rolls relating to humans due to low Humanity.

            Possible Sources: The Gangrel clan bane
            Resolution: Frenzy or escape the confinement that triggered this condition
            Beat: n/a

            In any case, apologies for the derail. I just find concept discussions like this enjoyably fascinating, and I appreciate your responses. In regards to your actual question re: the condition. Obviously it's going to have to be a judgment call on when you should apply the condition. I'd start off easy, erring on the side of it not applying in the edge cases until you have a chance to work it in some.

            For the mechanics, I think the Spend WP and Capped by Humanity combo is too harsh. Did they keep any "Capped by Humanity" mechanics in 2E? If not, I'd ditch it entirely.

            For the Spending Willpower, I can see the point that was made for it being just kind of a mini-frenzy. "When <triggered> you must act in a specific, direct way. If you want to do anything else, you must spend 1 WP to take the contrary action." Statement describes both Frenzy and Cornered Beast.

            Other limiting options in lieu of humanity caps:
            • May not spend Willpower on extra effort for actions not related to getting away.
            • Others who attempt to influence or manipulate your character into taking an action that would end the confinement score an exceptional success on (3) instead of (5) successes.
            For example, a Gangrel has been brought in chains before the prince for a crime he didn't commit. One of the PCs is the 'prosecutor' for the case. The PC offers the Gangrel to get everything over with right now if they just admit their guilt, and accept a punishment of losing their fangs for one month. Let the PC make the social / mental / maneuver roll per your style, and only require the three successes for a big win. Or, you're confined in an alleyway, but your terrible foe is also there. Cornered Beast wants you to get out, but you really want to kill this guy. He Dominates you to run away when he sees the murder in your eyes - and it's easier for him to do so.

            I think the must spend willpower bit would worries me most because it sounds like your game doesn't treat WP as fluidly as 2E, and I'd worry about Gangrel getting hit too often, or placed in confinement such that they run out of WP and can't do anything useful. Could the Gangrel in jail start seducing one of the guards in hopes they might let them out? Or would that require a Willpower vs. Just pounding on the reinforced steel door, even if they already tried that and failed?

                                                        Okay. . . lots of things to say, having skimmed the replies since my last one.

                                                        First, on the nature of predators. Fights to the death among animals are vanishingly rare, because it's just not survival-effective. "Nature, red in tooth and claw" is complete bunk. The paradigm for all animals is "minimal effort for maximal reward." An animal that feels threatened will use a threat display to get the other animal to back down, because an actual fight is in no one's interest. Predators pretty much only kill so they can eat, because otherwise they're burning calories for no good purpose. As this relates to vampires (especially "animalistic, primal" Gangrel) is that, if simply Lashing Out with the Beast to establish dominance over someone would satisfy the Beast's desire, that's what they'll do. If their goal is to leave an area, and someone stands in their way, grabbing and biting and drinking all their blood is contrary to that goal; it's keeping them in the same place longer. They're far more likely to just shove the obstructing person aside and keep moving than to stop and engage in combat (which biting and drinking under time constraint pretty much inherently is.) Basically, unless their goal is explicitly or implicitly to kill someone, odds are vanishingly small that they'll actually do that. By which I mean, unless the goal of the frenzy is explicitly "kill this person who humiliated/threatened/tried to kill me" or implicitly fatal like "drink until I'm full," vampires aren't going to use deliberately lethal force as a first resort. Now, Potence/Celerity/et al enhancements can easily make actions become fatal. If your goal is to escape the club, and the bouncer doesn't want to let you leave, and you bowl him aside with a Potence/Celerity 4 bull rush, tossing him 20 feet through the air to land on a table, that bouncer may well suffer fatal injuries. A vampire in frenzy may not expressly want to kill anyone who gets in his way, but he won't give a shit if he does, either.

                                                        Next, on RP and rolls. If a player at my table wants to do something, but doesn't quite know how to go about it, I'll just let them roll normally. I encourage them to RP as much as possible, but if a player just can't come up with an impassioned plea or rousing speech off the top of their head, but their character has excellent ratings in the things that allow them to do that, I'm not going to punish them for it. They get to roll, and if the roll is good, they deliver, say, Mal's St. Crispin's Day speech from Serenity. If they roll poorly, they instead deliver ALL of Sheridan's MANY St. Crispin's Day speeches from Babylon 5, and the audience just tunes out after the first one. If the player does come up with a good speech, then I give them a bonus to the roll. A great example of this is the player in my game who played a Geist cop. She was a fantastic RPer, able to switch between "good cop" and "bad cop" at the drop of a hat, and managed to wring all kinds of information out of lowlife thugs really easily. I didn't always make her roll (especially if the information she was after was the only way of advancing the plot), but when I did, she did so at some hefty bonuses because her role-playing was so good.

                                                        As for failures, people don't always know what they're made of until they're tested. In the example of a person who just watched their buddy get shot, then gets told they're next unless they talk, this random person could very well turn into a gibbering mess, or they could find a strength and courage they never even knew they had, look the shooter dead in the eye, and tell them to go fuck themselves. And either one, interestingly, could be the result of a failed Intimidation roll. A person could find the strength to face death with dignity, and withhold information from this very scary and dangerous person out of honor or spite, or they could be so terrified they can't remember their own name, nevermind whatever information the person is looking for.

The idea of the fancy parties is still a sticking point for me. It's fine if you say that in your games vampires don't do that, but that's not going to hold true for all (or, I daresay, even most) games. Which means that your Bane would inherently only work for your group, or other groups very much like it. Which, if what you're looking for is help for a house rule for your table and no farther, is fine. But in that case, you need to explain more thoroughly what your game is like and what themes you're working with, so we don't start putting our own experiences on it that will not work for you. Also, the idea that "rules the Gangrel themselves accepted" was not made clear (at least, not to me.) Even so, it still feels like a Bane that can be abused by a jerkish ST or players by springing surprise clauses to rules the Gangrel in question had already accepted. Especially within Covenants like the Invictus or Ordo, where part of their, for lack of a better word, "charm" is the fact that as you rise higher in the organization, you learn more about how it works and have more obligations and responsibilities thrust upon you. Take the Ordo: "Hey, join up with us, and we'll teach how to overcome all the weaknesses of being a vampire!" Then the Gangrel actually signs up, and "Okay, before we teach you anything, you have to apprentice for X amount of time as a Slave. Go do this for me." Once that's done, "Okay, what do you want to learn? Oh, I can't actually teach you that. Go apprentice to this guy, he'll help you figure that out." And finally, "Oh, no, we didn't mean ALL the vampire weakness, just some of them. Kind of. Well, not really." Granted, rules cannot accomodate all instances of the ST being an asshole, but they should at least try and minimize the opportunities asshole STs have to screw over their players. The idea that Gangrel can find themselves in a more disdvantageous position than other vampires because someone decided to screw them over with fine print doesn't sit well with me, I think because it flies in the face of what I consider the Gangrel's greatest strength: adaptability. Their signature Discipline is basically about literally evolving to meet the needs of their new lives, the idea of Gangrel being rendered impotent by clever wordplay just bothers me. Basically, I don't feel it encourages Gangrel to act "wild and free," because it doesn't prevent them from being trapped by the rules of Kindred society. The best they can do is tell the others "fuck your rules" and keep doing whatever they want, but they can still be "trapped" by them, forced to either obey the rule and suffer the effects of their Bane, or break the rule and suffer the consequences for that. It would also encourage players to make such social interactions as brief as possible, reinforcing the "uncouth and direct" stereotype of Gangrel. They don't go to a party, beat around the bush, chat up lots of people, and then finally get around to making small talk with the person they really need to see before, in a roundabout way, attempting to accomplish what they came to do. Instead, they'd walk right up to that person, say what they want, and then proceed to getting it.
