    Our chronicle: "Bloodless Lands" (game report)

    11-07-2016, 11:20 AM
    Hello again,

    My friends and I are having a blast with the chronicle we started two months ago and I wanted to share it with other people. So far, we played a two part prologue and just finished the first chapter. I will try and synthesize the story because there is so much stuff going on that I would spend too much time writing if I shared all the nice details. Also, we are using my homebrew setting (although very recognizable), and maybe you should take a look here: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...-houserule-set


    The Chronicle: Bloodless Lands

    The story is happening in the North-East of France, in a region called "Les Ardennes", and specifically in two towns close to each other: Sedan (20 000 people) and Charleville-Mézières (60 000 people). The year is 1984. The chronicle is called "Bloodless Lands" because the once prosperous industry of the region is decaying and the whole economy suffering as a result.

    The characters (all start as humans):

    Alain Vanderbeck: he is 42 years old and works at the castle of Sedan (the biggest medieval castle in Europe) as a restorer of medieval artifacts and art in general. A couple of years ago, his young sister (now 28) was hit by a car driven by the son of a local politician, and is now handicaped as a result. She can't walk and her failing nervous system causes multiple speech and motor problems. Her life is hell. Alain takes care of her and pays for her treatments and the services of a nurse. He is not wealthy though, and sometimes, he is forced to work for an organization of art and antiquity traffickers; he basically falsifies authentification certificates or recreate copies so the criminal organization can sell them. He gets a small commission. This criminal body is simply called "The Organization". Alain's life is sad, and if he did not have to care for his sister, he would have tied a noose around his neck a long time ago.

    Elena Brandt: 27 years old, this beautiful young woman was born in a rich and prominent family on her mother's side, the "Hautdecoeur". The Hautdecoeur built a local, if not regional empire in the textile industry, actively participating in the economy of the region. The business was taken over by Elena's father who married Célèste Haudecoeur, Elena's mother who died after giving birth to her. Today, the Brandt's empire has been reduced to just one factory. Ernest, Elena's older brother, was named by their father, Philippe Brandt, to take the head of the company after he decided to retire in Switzerland, where he was originally from. But Ernest plays poker a lot, drinks a lot, and he never wanted to lead a company. The factory is now brankrupt. Elena just got back from Oxford where she studied, and her father named her to succeed to her brother in the hope she can repair Ernest's failure. She just arrived in Charleville-Mézières and is determined to prove herself, save the company, and make it profitable again. Her dream is to build the company back to its former glory so she can positively influence the region's dying economy. She calls herself an idealist and a humanist.

    Luc Constant: 35 years old, Luc is a treasure hunter. Ok, he is a robber, and a would be treasure hunter. He is a lone wolf and the only person he trusted, his brother Michel, betrayed him after they both found some art the nazis had hidden during the Second World War. He sold the whole thing to the Organization (yes, same one Alain is working for) which earned him a good position within, leaving Luc hanging up to dry. Michel feels terrible about it though, and ever since, he has tipped his brother off on jobs he could do for the Organization or himself. Their relations are not good, but they serve Luc and help him survive. That's his only ambition in life, survive. He has no friends, lives in a shitty apartment and his only passion is the mysterious train that treasure hunters search for because it supposedly contains the biggest treasure the nazis ever had. If he finds it, maybe his life would change. Just maybe...

    Next, I will post the Prologue: part one.

      Prologue, part one

      Luc received a call from a man named Ernest Brandt who said he needed his help to retrieve an old book of value from an old antiquarian in Sedan. He had gotten Luc's contact information through Michel, his brother, whom he had met playing poker at illegal tournaments organized by the very lawnshark he owed money to. Luc thought his brother Michel wanted to make amends by sending an opportunity his way and accepted to meet Ernest. When they met, Ernest explained he heard of the book by his creditor who had well placed ears and told him of that opportunity so he could get his money back. Ernest needed an expert burglar to help him retrieve the book from Leon Amiel, the antiquarian. The book apparently dated from medieval times and was called "The True Story of the Four Sons of Aymon", translated from Old French. The Legend of the Four Sons of Aymon is a very famous one, especially in the Ardennes and the book supposedly had the potential to make the legend historical fact, hence its tremendous value.

      Interested, Luc decided to first verify the authenticity of this book, or at least its potential existence. While working as a contractor for the Organization, he had met a guy the Organization called upon to help them forge fasle authentification certificates, a guy who knew a lot about History. He went to his place to meet him.

      Alain was surprised and very intimidated to see Michel's brother on his doorstep. He lied to his sister and said Luc was some guy he worked with on a project. Luc promised him payment if he could find some information about the book and Alain accepted. He needed the money.

      Luc left Alain's place and decided to go check out the antiquarian's shop. After taking a quick look around and checking the different points of entry, he decided, on impulse, to break in. He successfully managed to get into the shop but not without waking the neighbour. He found the storage room trashed and two bodies. One belonged to the old antiquarian, still holding on to his sword cane; the other belonged to an intruder whom the valiant old man had killed. Luc searched the room, not really hoping to find the book. Instead, he found a very nicely crafted metal box that had been opened. There was an engraving on the box representing four knights fighting the Devil, with a castle in the background. Luc promptly left...with the box.

      The day after, Alain Vanderbeck started doing what he did best: historical research. First, he went to the castle and digged into all the antiquities that were stored there in order to find something. An engraving caught his attention. It represented four valiant knights fighting the Devil, a castle on a hill in the background. This piece of art was signed by Father Jean-Baptiste Lamônier, a catholic priest having lived in the 9th century around the time of Charlemagne. Charlemagne being an important actor in the legend of the Four Sons of Aymon, Alain thought it could give him some insight. He first discarded the engraving, though, because the four sons' famous magical horse was not present and it might not have been a representation of them after all. However, he later had a flash, remembering something, and needed to make sure it wasn't his imagination playing tricks on him. He scrutinized the engraving with a magnifying glass. There it was ! On the shield of one of the knights, the symbol of a horse carrying four knights. The engraving DID have something to do with the legend.

      Alain decided to do more research on the priest at the public library as well as his own, at home. By cross-referencing different facts with the famous legend, he managed to discover some interesting things. Father Lamônier fell in disgrace after showing his support to the four sons of Aymon after they allegedly betrayed Charlemagne and stole his treasure. Excited by those discoveries which could potentially become historical facts, Alain decided it was worth meeting with Luc again.

      As for Luc, he quickly called Ernest to inform him of what had happened. Ernest did not seem surprised and told Luc he might know who got to the antiquarian first, or at least he knew who could tell them. Luc went to see Alain at his place again and informed him as well. Alain also shared his own discoveries. They decided to meet at Ernest's. Ernest knew his lawnshark had probably heard of the book by the very people who got to the antiquarian. He also knew he would need more money to convince him to talk. Thus, he decided to call his sister, Elena Brandt, because she had the money and would do anything for him.

      And this is how Ernest, Luc, Elena and Alain had their first meeting at Ernest's place...

          Prologue, part one (continuation)

          When they all first met, Alain had an opportunity to examine the metal box that might have contained the old book. He estimated the box to date back to the 10th or 11th century; so one or two centuries after the actual book it might have contained. However, the link between the box and the book became certain when Alain saw the same engraving on the box that he had seen at the castle. Also, there was an inscription in the box, written in Old French, that Alain knew how to write and speak: "The true story of the four sons of Aymon, by Father Jean-Baptiste Lamonier". Finally, Alain once more made good use of his magnifying glass and found another, smaller inscription that said: "Fraternitas Bayari", or Brotherhood of Bayard, Bayard being the name of the sons of Aymon's magical horse. More and more convinced the whole thing could be true, Alain talked about the fact the book could potentially contain the location of the treasure the sons of Aymon stole from Charlemagne. The old book's value might be higher than they thought, which could explain why some people were prepared to kill for it, hence the death of the antiquarian.

          Ernest confessed he knew about three other people who might have been searching for the book as well. He explained the lawnshark he owed money to, a man name Frédéric Bullommeux, or "Bulleux", had told him about three men who were bragging about stealing a book who could make them rich. Ernest thus suggested they go meet "Bulleux" so he could tell them who those guys were, specifically because they now probably had the book. However, the lawnshark would not give the information for free, and that's why Ernest had called upon his sister who had some money and strong negociation skills. Still, they all had a big talk to figure out if they really wanted to get involved in a business that had gotten two people killed. But Alain needed money for his sister, Luc needed money for himself, and Elena needed money for her company. They all decided to at least go meet the lawnshark.

          The group of four went to "The Emerald", a nightclub in Sedan where Bulleux usually hanged out to manage his affairs. "Bulleux" was fat, and spoke like a viper; he was polite though. Clearly, he did not think much of Ernest and spoke to him like he would to a child. He, however, really liked Elena and the way she handled herself and she was the one who led the discussion. A deal was made with Bulleux: he would tell them what he knew but would get Ernest's share of the sale of the book to make up for his debts, as well as his own share for the help he was providing. As a deposit, he kept the metal box. Bulleux also did not trust Ernest and imposed the condition that he did not want him involved. Thus, Ernest would have to sit his ass down at his place and wait. Then, Bulleux shared what he knew. Basically, three guys had a drink at the Emerald and he heard them talk about a book that could lead them to a treasure. Bulleux was well known at the Emerald and he offered them drinks to get them talking. They bragged about getting a job from a occult librarian in Charleville who asked them, or at least their boss, to steal the book for him because it could lead him to a treasure. They could have a share of it as well. Bulleux did not get the names of the men, but he got the name of the librarian's shop: The Fantastic Den. He also told them that the three thugs probably had no intention of getting the book back to the librarian because they worked for a criminal organization which only served itself.

          The group split up after deciding to meet the day after and go talk to the librarian in Charleville. Luc called his brother Michel and asked him if he knew who the three guys were. Michel told him he did not but that he knew for sure they were part of the Organization. When he had heard about the job initially, he wanted the Organization to give it to Luc but the boss preferred to give it to three guys he knew. However, Michel told Luc he would try and ask around to see if he could dig up the information.

          The day after, Elena, Luc and Alain met up and drove straight to Charleville. They found the "Fantastic Den", an occult library hidden in a small street and full of dusty books and other oddities. There, they met the libarian who introduced himself as Etienne Rode. Very well spoken, he had an odd air about him. Luc tried to intimidate him quickly in a way to get the information they were looking for. Etienne did not seem to respond to this attempt. In fact, he did not flinch at all but remained polite and open. He never tried to hide anything and insisted that they could simply ask their questions in a civilized manner. Once they finally understood threats were not necessary, Etienne explained he went to the antiquity shop in Sedan and tried to convince the antiquarian to sell him the book. The old man refused so Etienne asked the services of a criminal organization to help him retrieve it. He did not hear back from them, and heard about the old man's death in the newspaper. Although he did not seem bothered at all by it, he was aware the criminal organization probably had no intention of getting back to him. Thus, he told Elena, Luc and Alain that he had no issue with helping them if they promised to give him the book after they found the treasure. He assured them he only cared about the lore contained in the book. Alain offered to translate the book for him if he let them have it. Etienne accepted. He did not know who committed the burglary though but offered them a suggestion. He said that in order to find the treasure, the burglars would need to have the book translated and that they probably intended to find a translator. Given the probable low number of Old French experts, it should not be too hard to find the one the burglars might have contacted. Finally, Luc asked, out of curiosity, how Etienne had heard about the book initially. He responded it was a very long story that he might share it with them later, once their business was done.

          Right before they left, Etienne left them one last piece of advice that sent chills down their spine: "Once you find the treasure, take it and go. Don't touch anything else. There are things that are better left in peace."

                          Prologue, part one (ending)

                          Alain knew there weren't a lot of people who mastered Old French like he did. However, he knew of a professeur at Lycée Turenne, in Sedan, who could understand it. He wasn't an expert, but he was a well known History professor so he seemed like a good lead. The group waited for him to leave the school and followed him home. At night, Luc decided to disguise himself and knock at Payot's door. He pretended to be working with the burglars. Immediately, Payot's expression changed and Luc knew the man was, indeed, a lead. Through a smart subterfuge, Luc managed to learn that the two surviving burglars had come to Payot so he could go over the book they had stolen and tell them where the treasure was located. Payot therefore only took a few notes and then put some markings on a map for the men who were threatening him. The professor had, however, also told them the book seemed to be a work of fiction and that they probably would never find any treasure. He wrote some more instructions on a map for Luc, showing the treasure to be located in a cave or catacomb under one of the hills of Bogny-Sur-Meuse, a small town North of Charleville-Mézières. According to the book, the four sons of Aymon had hidden Charlemagne's treasure in Montessor Castle and then took it into the forest before the king's assault on the fortress. As it turned out, the ruins of Castle Regnault, in Bogny-Sur-Meuse were among the handful of ruins in the Ardennes which might have been those of Montessor, the four sons of Aymon's famous castle.

                          With this new information, Luc went back to Alain and Elena and shared everything he knew. They all went to Luc's apartment and packed some bags with food and equipment such as flashlights, shovels and pickaxes. Then, they drove to Bogny-Sur-Meuse. There, they parked the car and ventured into the forest with Luc serving as guide because his treasure hunts had made him able to find his way in such places. After hours of walking, they came across two backpacks and a handful of tools. Luc estimated the tracks around the equipment to date back a couple of days. It was weird the burglars had not come back to get their stuff. They pushed forward anyway, and finally arrived at the location, at the base of the hill. They found more tools like the ones they had brought, abandoned near an entrance that seemed to have been resealed quite recently with heavy stones. Using teamwork, they managed to free the entrance, unraveling a tunnel. They ventured forward and ended up in an underground gallery with seemingly nothing in it except some sort of rectangular and rough stone block that looked like a tomb. They approached and looked inside but it was empty. Elena almost slipped on some object that rolled on the floor. She picked it up; it was an old wood stake.

                          Luc explored the gallery further and found a very small entrance through one of the walls. He crawled inside and stumbled upon the body of a man whose throat had been grossly torn. Controlling his growing fear, he searched the body and found nothing but a chain bracelet around the man's wrist with the following inscription: "Polo". However, there was no trace of a treasure. Alain, Luc and Elena made sure they had explored everything and promptly left the premises, a dread feeling growing inside them.

                          They split up and Luc called back his brother Michel to ask him if the nickname "Polo" meant something to him. It immediately did, and Michel told him Polo was in fact, Paul Omanec, a petty criminal who worked with the Organization from time to time. He lived at his grandmother's in Sedan and used her house as a sort of a headquarter to plan his robberies. Michel also told Luc "Polo" always worked with two buddies of his, one of them being David Allais and likely the third robber, the only one of them who was still unaccounted for.

                          The day after, Luc called Alain and Elena and they all decided to use Elena's car to go to Paul Omanec's grandmother's house. If the three robbers had the book, they probably had left it at their headquarters, Omanec's place. Before they knocked, Luc decided to check behind the house. He managed to look through a window that offered him a view on the kitchen. There was a man eating a plate of beans at the table. Luc hid himself and observed for a moment. The man then voluntarily cut his hand with the knife and sucked the blood that was dripping from the wound like a hungry animal. Disgusted, Luc went back to his colleagues and shared what he saw. They decided Alain and Elena would knock as much as necessary to attract the attention of the strange man who might, or might not have been David Allais, while Luc would go around and break into the house. Their plan worked, and although nobody went to open the main door, the strange man left the kitchen, allowing Luc to use his tools and break in. Stealthily, Luc left the kitchen and glanced inside. He saw the man running toward another door and disappear inside. Luc took the opportunity to go open the main door and let his colleagues in. Then, he led them towards where he last saw the man. Elena made a quick detour by the kitchen to grab a knife. Alain, for some reason, started to feel like his life was about to be forever changed.

                          They opened the door on a flight of stairs, probably leading to a cave. Then, they heard steps and found themselves face to face with the strange man. He spoke some nonsense and started singing his own twisted version of a nursery rhyme mentioning an old book. Then, he ran back downstairs. Elena, Alain and Luc promptly followed him and ended up in the cave that served as headquarters for the robber. There were three things which attracted their attention. The crazy man who had taken refuge behind a table at the center of the room, a plastic bag left in a corner which likely contained a body (the grandmother ?), and the old and precious grimoire set on a clean mattress spread on the floor. Elena asked Alain to distract the crazy man while she would walk towards the book to grab it. Alain started talking to the deranged individual and engaged in a disturbing conversation. Luc positionned himself so he could react quickly if Elena was attacked. The young woman reached the mattress and grabbed the book. Alain told the crazy man that the book was cursed and that he would better give it away or the beast would come to get it back. Then, the deranged man responded:

                          "Maybe. But I didn't touch the book. Paul did. And the beast...she already has the book."

                          Then something quickly passed through Elena's vision. She freezed. Then the lights were shut and a door slammed. The deranged man started singing again. Then, Elena fell. Followed by Luc. Alain tried to flee back to the stairs but he was grabbed and thrown against the wall. His last thought was for his sister.


                          End of Prologue, part one
