        I was really happy with the lineages this time around. I think they link up thematically the best out of the three books' bloodline chapters, and they all have different scales of play, which I think is important in big splat books like False Gods: The Gottlings are generally pretty street-level, even though they're quite ancient, whereas the Warumono are relatively young (officially), but are a bit more global in scope if you want them to be. And then the Rotgrafen are pretty much whatever they want to be.

        Anyway, that brings us to today's excerpt: The Adrestoi. As noted above, they had a bit of a facelift since they last appeared in Lords Over the Damned. I'll start with my note in the outline about them:

        Originally posted by Me
        The only vampires with a weakness that was actually a benefit. This was the lone bloodline in Lords, and apart from the aforementioned glitch in their bane, they’re a bit forgettable, so I’m interested to see what we can do to glow them up. I doubt anyone feels strongly about them and their one-page write-up, so it’s an opportunity to take that seed and make it your own. The overall impression reads more like a character concept than a splat, so a lot of this is a matter of giving them structure and goals. Maybe swap out one of their Disciplines for Vigor, so they’re not the same spread as the Rotgrafen. You might want to give them a Devotion or a Merit.
        As you can see, I wasn't feeling particularly precious about the Adrestoi and their original presentation. I think one of my few complaints about the original clan books is that the bloodlines are often more miss than hit, and the Adrestoi were the first thing that came to mind when I thought of that quality. The accidentally-beneficial bane aside, most of their write-up is about their founder, who is as bog-standard a Lord as you can get. That was the overall problem I had with the original: they're just Ventrue, but more so. This is a common problem in some old bloodlines. That's not to rag on the original; it's not a bad little bloodline, but without some changes, it didn't really suit our needs.

        To make a long story short, the author felt that the sadomasochist themes implicit in the original ought to be the focus, and I agreed. However, it's the twist she put on that concept that really brings the idea together. Rather than mere S&M Ventrue, the Adrestoi are a gestalt of pack animals, submitting to a collective will rather than an individual. They use a Coil-like Discipline to master their Beasts so they can experience extremes of pleasure and violence, and employ special ceremonies to grant some of that gold to their followers. While that's a pretty radical change, the first edition version definitely influences the new, and we tried to include the old backstory where we could. That said, I really like the hints of lore about Golden Matthias, and what he had to do with all this...

        I could go on about the Adrestoi; apart from the Malocusians (oh boy will we get to them), the Unchained* definitely got the biggest upgrade in the book.

        Adrestoi: The ones you submit to

        “The pack’s heart beats in my breast with the fury of a thousand lashes.”

        Mistress walks through the pulsing lights of the nightclub. Waves of human flesh press against her, euphoria overflowing from the pleasure burning bright within. Her invisible threads unspool into the night, connecting to her pack. Each member thrives on their shared desire: the stripper in the club, the life-drawing model at the university, the porn director adjusting the light on his latest stars. She follows each thread, glimpsing the smörgåsbord of potential prey fawning for her pack’s attention. Which worshipper shall they hunt tonight?

        She tugs a thread: An art student lingers after class, bashful eyes stealing glances at the dead model.

        Yes, she whispers. That one. The pack’s blood sizzles. She smiles, sending the kine in the nightclub into raptures. She will unchain the Beast, allowing the pack’s combined fury to erupt into the streets. They will converge at the university, slipping through the shadowed streets to corner the student, to feast, and take satisfaction from the shared banquet of beauty and death.

        Sated, Mistress will collar them once more, and the tethers will stretch out again between them. Separate but ever-linked, her pack will savor emotions far more precious than blood.

        The Adrestoi are Lords who use domination and submission to forge deeper bonds than just blood ties. They know how thin the line between pain and pleasure is, and, in death, that both can be so sweet. Let other Ventrue aim for political mastery; the Children of Midas have found something far more precious: mastery of the Beast. Some would call them hedonists. Others would say they’re reckless, playing with forces impossible to control. Forces that ought to be feared. But the Unchained have learned to harness the monster within, breaking it to their collective will and unleashing it in a maelstrom of unified purpose.

        The 80s club scene epitomized the heedless risks Kindred and kine alike take in pursuit of satisfaction. In bars and backrooms, illicit deals were harder to trace, and it’s no wonder this is where the Adrestoi found their beginnings. Here a Ventrue hotshot named Paul Gordon kept his hooks in every friend and foe, spinning his web across the Motor City. He offered his clients that elusive gold — not bourgeois material wealth, but the gold of Vitae’s pure euphoria. All it cost was submission. Total, complete surrender, and the world would open like a treasure chest. Everyone he anointed found the heights of success, as if they’d made a deal with the Devil himself. For this, he called himself Adrestos, the Inescapable, self-named for the son of mighty Midas who turned all he touched to gold.

        Except that Midas had no son, only a daughter named Zoë. Was this oversight or misdirection? Such subterfuge was the mark of Adrestos’ business dealings — submission sounds so easy in the abstract — but, in the end, it’s the results that matter.

        Adrestos was eager to share his gift, but only with those he deemed worthy. To prove their loyalty, he tested his Children of Midas with the lick of the lash, and as they explored the limits of their flesh and the depths of their desire, they were rewarded. Once they submitted to his will, the inducted were linked to a whole greater than the sum of its parts. Emotion bloomed like a white-hot flame, burning between these initiates and uniting their Beasts into one monstrous, undeniable hunger. Exalted and Unchained, they became stronger together. Within the Blood Tether, they became transcendent.

        The Adrestoi haven’t had long since those first fateful nights, but they quickly established a hold in the underbellies of several major cities. Now they hunt together, more like prowling packs of urban wolves than those other, calcified Lords with their withered passions. The Unchained keep watch from alleys and nightclubs and chatrooms, waiting for their master’s command to strike. When she gives the word, the Beast’s rage rips through the entire pack. Together they feast, relishing in the compounded power of so many hearts beating as one.

        Whatever form it takes, Adrestoi venerate the hunt above all, and their packs prey on the ugly desires lurking behind every impulse. As blackmailers, conmen, and even dominatrices, they target those disconnected from their truths, bending repressed and lost kine to their will and teaching them submission must be earned. These “clients” don’t always know what they’ve seen — the twisting flesh and the erotic visions and the limits of ecstasy and pain — but if these supplicants are worthy, they might be inducted into the pack. In exchange for their individual wills, the Children of Midas tether them to the euphoria of unity, seducing ever more moths to the golden flame.

        Why you want to be us

        We escape the isolation of our Ventrue cousins by keeping our emotions burning bright and hot. All together. You will never know belonging like the sharp embrace of the Adrestoi. We will strip away your masks and self-doubt, break you down until you attain true self-mastery. Only then can you rise up, beloved of your pack, more alive than you ever were in living flesh.

        Why you should fear us

        Other Kindred think their fates hang in the balance between Man and Beast, but we know true power comes only when the two are bound into one. Domination does not require voice; it merely requires surrender. Our Beasts are collared under our collective command, ready to rage when we need them, only to snap back on their leads when our purpose is served. All the wrath of our darkest bloodlusts flows between us, connected, aware, and watching. Run afoul of one and become prey to all.

        Why we should fear ourselves

        Did emotions burn like this when we still lived? Was passion always just agony? We look for extremes to feel alive again, and sometimes that pulls us past our limits. The union with our packs is a high, filled with understanding and unrestrained power — but after? When the Beast is back in its cage, we shake in the dark like fucking junkies. It’s a trade, this vitality. A gamble. And some night, we won’t be able to pay off the debt.

        Bloodline Origins (Excerpt)

        Matthias the Golden, all smiling and shining on the late-night TV screen, promised life everlasting, an unending state of bliss on your money back. Some say he was the first mask of Paul Gordan, while others whisper he was Paul’s sire, or even the miracle childe of both Adrestos and Mistress Avarice. Whoever he was, he offered to wash away the sins of his Children of Gold — and perhaps he even did. Each initiate disappeared little by little, donating all their earthly possessions, then cutting off their friends and families, then branding Matthias’s seal in their flesh. They only walked among the sinful to bring in more initiates before disappearing again behind his compound’s walls. When the news captured the whole complex aflame, reports of hundreds dead in positions of surrender… well. Who could say it wasn’t all part of the plan?

                Originally posted by Minty View Post
                I'm still going through the book but I just wanted to say that your take on Malkavia is super cool and I want to use it in my game.
                I hope you get a chance to! We put a lot of work into it, and I'm really proud of the result.

                So, for today's excerpt, I want to talk about the Melissidae. The Queens are one of the most memorable and beloved bloodlines from the first edition of Vampire. They're often the first thing people think of when you say "Ventrue bloodline."

                Unlike the Adrestoi, we didn't feel a need to fundamentally change the Melissidae. They're still tyrannical bee-people, and we mostly rely on the same backstory. However, we also wanted to see how things had changed. The Melissidae were said to be "destroyed" in the 19th century, and we wondered what it might look like if the survivors had reinvented themselves. How do you put a PR spin on a hivemind? In the original, the modern Queens were a shadowy cult, but we thought it added some playability if they had decided to put on a charm offensive. Instead of denying their practices, they're out and proud, albeit with a good dose of spin-doctoring.

                However, as opposed to the backstory, which we really only expanded, we changed the bloodline's mechanics quite a bit. Part of that aforementioned PR campaign was switching out Auspex for Majesty. I'll admit, if you had asked me before what a Melissid got as her fourth Discipline, I would've told you it's Majesty. And indeed, that's what I thought when I did my outline. Imagine my surprise when I found out it was Auspex. Auspex isn't a bad choice for the Melissidae; Telepathy (now Lay Open the Mind) fits pretty well with them. But the other levels of Auspex were more of a stretch; the Melissidae definitely have a bit of that "I know all your weaknesses" quality to them, but like...Majesty can (almost) make people kill themselves for your love. It also didn't help that only one of their numerous Devotions in Bloodlines: The Legendary included Auspex as a prerequisite. So, Majesty made a lot more sense thematically and mechanically.

                The second big change was their bane. The original weakness is that they had to be surrounded by their hive at all times. As with Auspex, that fits fine, but I'm not a huge fan of weaknesses where you need to have an NPC hanging around you. Such things can be played well, but, more often than not, the Storyteller is just going to be saddled with a warm body they're going to forget about half the time. It also felt like we'd be missing a bet if we didn't tie in the Ventrue clan bane, which is all about your connections to others. So, to give the Queens something a bit more Second Edition, we decided to just amp up the Aloof Curse: Melissidae can't have inanimate objects or places as Touchstones, they have to have their Touchstones be members of their hives, and having no attached Touchstones is as bad as having none at all. This means Melissid characters pretty much have to be swimming in Touchstones. I'm a fan of this bane because it encourages a character to behave in a certain way. Something I try to emphasize to writers is that banes aren't little punishments your character acquires over time that she's meant to fight against: they're roleplay prompts (or at least the best ones are).

                The third change was more of an expansion, i.e., giving their hivemind powers a specific edge. To that end, we borrowed the Cultists Merit format from Demon, and plugged in some Melissidae specific effects.

                All that said, if you prefer something closer to the 1E Bees, we've got that covered too.

                Edit: Oh, and the fourth big change was actually a backstory thing. Other than as a rumor, we tossed out the explicit idea that they're mostly women. If there's a trope I dislike in Vampire (and there are many) it's gender-exclusive bloodlines and clans. It just gets into a thematic space I don't think has or ever will be done well. We also explored a variation of that idea with the Qedeshah, and the author didn't really want to repeat that concept.

                Anyway, I've gone on for too long, so here's the excerpt. Tomorrow I'll be talking about the Gottlings. Normally I'd continue on with the 1E conversions, but I've already posted excerpts of the Malocusians and Rotgrafen on the forums. We'll get to them eventually with some developer notes, but for the next few entries in this...series?...I'll be focusing on the new bloodlines.

                Anyway, I hope everyone's enjoying the book. Please consider dropping a review! It's super helpful.

                Melissidae: The ones who make you theirs

                “I’ll never let them take you away — you’re mine.”

                She tells you that she loves you.

                Of course you believe her. What else could this feeling be but love? Her smile is so dazzling you hardly notice the wasp crawling from her ear, flitting up to join the others circling her head like a buzzing halo. The wasps don’t matter. All that matters is how she makes you feel: perfect. Just like her.

                Ask for a story about the Melissidae and you’re likely to get a sensationalist answer. They could control thousands of insects at once and transformed into giant bugs! They were all women, and could turn any man who fell in their clutches into a mindless puppet! Hyperbole is all you’ll get, though, because the Queens are dead. They were wiped out over a hundred years ago in a carefully orchestrated extermination.

                What crime the Melissidae committed to deserve this destruction, and who carried it out, are the source of much speculation. Did their human drones become too great a threat to the Masquerade? Was the richness of their blood too irresistible, a delicacy for predators lusting after a taste of honey? If anyone remembers, they aren’t talking. For over a century, the bloodline was considered extinct, reduced to no more than a strange footnote in the long history of Clan Ventrue.

                However, any exterminator worth her salt will tell you that, when dealing with insects, you really have to make sure to kill every last one. Miss even a single larva and the hive will return, smarter and more dangerous than before.

                Not many Kindred walking these nights have ever met a Melissid. They were always isolationists, and while they were numerous in the United Kingdom before the purge, that time is long past. This rarity allows the Queens to take control of their narrative as they squirm back into the All Night Society. They present themselves now as family units, and downplay their mastery over insects in favor of their ability to manage people better than anyone else, a skill that’s always in demand among the Damned. These are the modern Melissidae — charming and humble. Nurturing, even. They don’t talk about what happened before. Water under the bridge. They wouldn’t dream of forcing anyone to stay with them. Perish the thought! Their followers (“drone” is an ugly word) stay with them in their home (never “hive” — how gauche) because they’re happy there. They need them too — they could hardly get a thing done without them! Everyone is quite pleased with the arrangement. No need to worry. If you’re still curious, you’re always welcome to drop by. Just mind the flowers on the way in. You wouldn’t want to get stung.

                It’s all a lie, of course. The need to form a hive buzzes in the Melissidae minds like a thousand gossamer wings. They can no more deny it than any vampire can deny her thirst. They’ve learned from their mistakes though. Oh, yes. They understand now that their isolated ways made them too vulnerable to attack. While they still maintain their independent hives, Melissidae now communicate frequently to offer each other advice, support, and resources. The result is that anyone trying to attack a Collector is likely to make an instant enemy of every hive for miles. And their bond with the swarming, stinging, buzzing things of the world is just as strong as it was before; they just don’t flaunt it.

                But don’t be deceived — a Melissid’s haven is literally crawling with insects, be they swarms of wasps in the eaves, armies of ants marching between the walls, or perhaps a beehive nurtured within the dead flesh of the Queen herself.

                Even putting aside the insects, Melissidae are never alone. A Collector surrounds herself with adoring humans, doting on them as much as they fawn over her. This eerie co-dependence isn’t just supernatural bondage, though. It’s a rapport. It starts with a simple gesture, sitting next to the despondent man at the corner of the bar, or whispering to the lonely girl in the back of the classroom. The Queen makes them smile, makes them feel seen — feel wanted. They don’t ask anything of them; just the opposite. That gorgeous dress she can’t afford, or the student loans she’s about to default on? Gifts! Think nothing of it. That interview he was worried didn’t go so well? A honeyed word to the manager and it’s his. Anything for the Queen’s new friend.

                Once a mortal is enamored, the next step is simple: A drink of the vampire’s sweet blood. All Kindred blood is addictive, of course, but Melissidae blood is to regular Vitae as heroin is to caffeine. The taste is sweeter, the high is higher, and the crash is non-existent, so long as one keeps getting their fix. Once the drone-to-be has their first taste, their will to leave all but vanishes, and they’re ready for the hivemind.

                Why you want to be us

                We are love. With us, you will know what it’s like to be accepted without question, to be needed by those around you. To be one of us is to be surrounded by countless friends crawling beneath the ground or buzzing through the air. Be one of us, and never, ever feel lonely again.

                Why you should fear us

                Do not disturb the nest lest you rouse the swarm. Our wrath is a thousand piercing stings, a million burning bites. No matter how many allies you think you have, we always have more. Or perhaps we’ll take your little friends, or make your loved ones our own. We think they’d be much happier with us.

                Why we should fear ourselves

                We have evolved, but our need is the same. We can’t be alone, can’t stop adding to our family, can’t abide any control other than our own. When does love end and addiction begin? We don’t know, and the answer is building up venom.

                Hivemind (•• to •••••)

                Prerequisite: Melissidae

                Effect: A hive is part social network, part labor force, part support group, able to take on a wide variety of roles by working together under the direction of its Queen.

                At two dots, Hivemind members eagerly share resources and information with your character, and are an easy source of blood. Once per story/month, your drones provide support equal to Allies 3, Herd 3, or Resources 3. They will happily help with any mundane task that poses no significant risk, but will not endanger themselves or risk breaking points.

                At three dots, drones can share surface thoughts and emotions, allowing for silent communication and coordination as long as they are within about ten meters of one another. In addition to the benefits of a two-dot Hivemind, all members (including your character) gain a bonus to Teamwork actions equal to your character’s Merit dots as long as all actors involved are part of the hive. Your character also does not have to speak to communicate simple ideas and requests to members of her hive. At this level, drones will do virtually anything for your character that doesn’t actively involve harming themselves or others.

                                            Alright, I promised the Gottlings, and since I'm compelled through Dominate a keeper of promises, that's today's excerpt.

                                            The Gottlings have a special appeal for me as a player because my writing background is actually in theatre, not fiction (you can tell by the way I use the pretentious spelling; not just Canadian). Anyway, when the author pitched them to me, I was pretty much onboard right away. Requiem and Masquerade have done a fair number of "performer" bloodlines, but I don't know that there's ever been explicit backstage vampires. These are the Kindred who smoke cigarettes and write screenplays instead of daysleep. They were also an opportunity to do something a bit more small scale, even if their niche was pretty high concept. Some of the bloodlines in the book have pretty grand plans, or are more overtly organized, but the Gottlings don't have much overarching structure. They have a tradition and a profession, but there's no grand union of Dramaturges. They have no greater objective than putting on the next big show, and I think that was a good contrast to some of the the more megalomaniacal Ventrue in the book (the Gottlings are more egomaniacal). And despite their write-up's sassier/lighter tone, the Gottlings might be the most unapologetically evil bloodline in the book, which I'm always a fan of. Sometimes, vampires are bastards.

                                            Not a huge amount changed in the course of development. Honestly, the only tricky detail was creating art for the sample character. I quite liked that she's the non-obvious interpretation of "theatrical", but it was difficult to come up with a representational image that conveyed the bloodline's themes because of that. The artist and I had a lot of back-and-forth figuring out a good look for her, but eventually that theatre background of mine came to the rescue: I remembered stage hands in Japanese puppet theatre tend to dress like stereotypical ninjas (and there's a possibly apocryphal story that says that's where the ninja look comes from), so, we went stagehand meets shinobi. The garotte also works well as a subtle nod to the bloodline's puppeteering ways.

                                            Anyway, that's it for now. Tomorrow, I think I'll do the electricity-eating Keravnos, who have the only special Discipline in the book. If anyone has any questions or comments in the meantime, I'm keeping an eye on the thread!

                                            Gottlings: The ones who pull your strings

                                            “All the world’s a stage, and all the men and women are my playthings.”

                                            Some artists, self-proclaimed, believe restraint is an ideal. These people are cowards. No, moderation is an excuse to make mediocrities feel better about their blandness. Writing, music, and theater are all perfectly adequate mediums for such middle talents. Like cheap wine — good enough. For true artists, only one canvas will satisfy: life. Real life, where the actors aren’t aware of the script, and the stage directions are compulsions screaming into their souls. Of course, creating in such a medium means someone inevitably, invariably, gets hurt. A necessary evil. If you want to produce art of value, you must be fearless. Transgressive. Perhaps that doesn’t make sense to you, but art doesn’t need to make sense, and the Gottlings are true artistes.

                                            Most Lords see manipulation as a means to an end, a way to keep their subjects in line within their petty fiefdoms. For the Directors, manipulation is the end. They crave control the way most other Kindred crave Vitae, yet paradoxically, Gottlings tend to have very little use for keeping their own accounts in the black. “Order” is just a word for “squandered dramatic potential.” Nobody cares when everything goes according to plan. A baker goes to work and makes bread every day; who gives a shit? What if, instead, the baker just happens to slip a pinch of arsenic in the morning dough? He can’t say why he did it. Maybe he doesn’t even remember. That part doesn’t matter. What does matter is that people will be talking about the Sourdough Massacre for months. Years! Just think of all the emotion such a small thing can generate. The rage, the despair, the hatred… all of it fertile ground for the Gottlings to do their work.

                                            The Dramaturges get a thrill from creating that sort of cause-and-effect reaction. All it ever takes is a gentle touch here or a whispered command there. It’s not a matter of making order from chaos, but rather of choosing the shape chaos takes. The stories they produce can be action-packed thrillers full of murder and destruction, tragedies rife with loss and heartbreak, or just twisted and bloody slapstick comedies. Often, they’re all these things; just depends where you’re sitting. Those safely in the audience have brought much profit to the bloodline. They really are the best show on earth, if you don’t mind a little moral compromise. All the Directors do is provide a service. For the elder who’s seen the Bard a thousand times, what could match the thrill of watching the Scottish Play unravel in real time? Or maybe you’re just a jaded Gen-X neonate who misses the time when “punk show” was a byword for “riot.” The Directors are already queuing up the music.

                                            The Gottlings have honed their craft over thousands of years. They did it in the amphitheaters of Athens, and they do it tonight with starlets on Broadway and silver screens in Hollywood. They were the original phantoms of the opera: He is an immaculate corpse using stage makeup to cover his lack of complexion as he sweeps some wealthy dolt off his feet, only to use make the fallout of their affair into a paying voyeur’s thrill; she is a black-clad stagehand waiting in the wings to hand an unwitting Juliet the knife that’s about to make her famous. When the Gottlings look at a crowded room, they don’t see people, just countless plot threads, and it’s up to these Dramaturges to weave them into the most intriguing stories the All Night Society has ever seen.

                                            Nothing is ever really enough for the Gottlings. They have a pursuit, but no destination. They build on the work that preceded them or struggle to surpass dramatic events that unfolded by chance. Boundaries exist to be pushed. Limitations are only necessary as a challenge. The Directors are far more interested in what happens when you cut a thing open and turn it inside out, and nothing will stop them from sating that curiosity.

                                            Why you want to be us

                                            They say history is written by the victors. A lie. History is written by us. We choose who wins and who loses, who’s cast as the hero and who’s remembered as the villain. We decide who gets their happy ending and how long it lasts. Any rube can kill, but it takes talent to turn an innocent into a predator. You want to be us because life could be so much more interesting if you could plot and pace it.

                                            Why you should fear us

                                            Are you sure those words are your own? And those odd impulses roiling in your belly — are those yours too? We feed your lines and provide the motivation for whatever part we need you to play. We craft the narrative and play the song you dance to, and if you’re lucky, maybe you’ll even get to thank us for it.

                                            Why we should fear ourselves

                                            We’re the architects of our own demise. When we scare away or break all of our many playthings, when we’ve dissected and rearranged everything we have, when we’re left alone with nothing but our drive to create to keep us warm — well, then we need to find a new subject. Art is suffering, and our own is often our final masterpiece.

                                            Rumors

                                            “Couple thousand years ago, some Lord made a deal with a devil to be the greatest artist of all time. In return, he sold his mind, and every Lick he sired inherited both the gift and the cost. Makes you wonder when it might come due.”

                                            While Gottlings love to see this rumor make the rounds (Demons? So dramatic!), there’s only a slice of truth to it. Demons and Kindred have little in common at face value. Different goals, different non-lives. A vampire would need to be off her gourd to risk dealing with the kind of heat the Unchained pull down. So, it works out that most Gottlings are pretty off their gourds. Now, they don’t typically work together directly; that would defeat the point. But demons have a constant need for new cover identities, and there are few creatures on this ball of dirt better at stealing or creating personae than the Gottlings. It also isn’t hard to see the value in having something as powerful as a demon in your debt. What a show that would make.

                                                Good morning! Time for one last excerpt before the weekend.

                                                The Keravnos are one of the stranger bloodlines in the book, but they fill a solid Ventrue niche: the industrialist. However, what makes the Shockers special is taking that idea and making it literal. They're into technology in the same way a cannibal is a humanitarian. They control electrical sources to not only build up their coffers, but to keep their Vitae topped up without any need for human blood. Sort of. Drinking too much juice can ground out their humanity, and if they grow addicted to they act, it can burn out even the Beast (insert your vampire capitalism metaphors here).

                                                The Shockers didn't change too much in development. Initially they were more oriented toward digital technology, but we broadened that concept to be more about technology in general, which fit better with some of the implicit details about their origins. And unlike the Connected in Strange Shades, the Keravnos use machines as prey, not minions. Their bane also went through a few versions before we settled on the one in the book. Normally, I'm not a fan of connecting banes to special powers, but I felt being able to avoid feeding on blood needed a limitation beyond just a Vitae limit per scene. This version of the bane also doesn't punish you for choosing to use your abilities (bought with hard-earned EXP), which is usually my main concern; rather, it just makes being a real asshole with them all the harder.

                                                I suspect the Keravnos will be used more as antagonists, and to some degree, that's intentional. However, I'd be really interested to see what kind of player characters people make with them.

                                                Anyway, here's the Keravnos. I'm taking a break from excerpts till Monday, when I'll be back with the Warumono, the tattooed gangsters and walking typhoons. After that I'll probably do a double header with my Malocusian and Rotgrafen notes; I have lots to say on the former and not much on the latter, so why not kill two birds. After that, would people be more interested in horrors, covenants, or some more Malkavia stuff?

                                                Keravnos: The ones who shock you

                                                "Power runs through these veins. Literally."

                                                He prepares for his transatlantic flight, chair reclined and a blanket draped over his knees even though he doesn’t feel the chill. He’s made it clear the windows in the first-class compartment are to remain shuttered for the duration of the journey. The handsome tip he plies the stewardess with ensures his will shall be done.

                                                He settles his hands upon the armrests, opening his awareness to the wiring hidden beneath the plush leather. The pulse thrums against his wrists, and, in a moment of pique, he imagines it to be his own. He presses his thumb against the headphone jack, the coppery tang of the port so like the flavor of fresh blood. He breathes out, then sharply inhales.

                                                A jolt leaps up his arm, filling him with warmth, setting his nerves atremble and bursting stars behind his closed eyelids. His desiccated heart turns over once inside his chest, like an old car sputtering to life.

                                                He dares not draw more, not when he’s drinking from engines keeping him 31,000 feet up. But the shot of juice will keep him going until he lands in Cairo and can have a proper meal. The stewardess comes around with her cart, offering highballs. He flashes an electric smile, the faint acrid smell from the jack already recycling into the filtration system.

                                                “Thanks, but I’ve got everything I need.”

                                                The Keravnos are the bleeding edge of the All Night Society. First emerging as automation exploded across the globe, it’s a common misconception among Kindred that these developments correlate, but it’s more accurate to say the Shockers thrived because of the boom in electronics. As washing machines, then TVs, then PCs, then Wi-Fi moved into every home, their food sources multiplied at a geometric rate — as well as their footholds on power. The Keravnos are unique in their ability to feed from electrical sources, an edge that (as they see it) puts them far ahead of their rival Lords, stuck as they are with their fragile, traditional herds.

                                                Industry is a Jovian’s hunting grounds, not the streets or the clubs or the squalor. Whether she’s the hands-on CEO of a hydroelectric concern or just the “lifelong” foreman of the plant, her influence extends as far and wide as the current coursing from her lair. Some Shockers are union leaders working to liberate the means of production for their own hungry ends, while others are tech bros developing the next killer app to wipe out dead and living competitors. A few are Silicon Valley oligarchs using cryptocurrencies to power their bank accounts and corpses. Whenever technology is exalted and human flesh is cheap, you’ll find a Keravnos.

                                                Among the Kindred, Shockers have members in every coven*ant, as their adaptability makes them both desirable masters and versatile retainers. However, most Keravnos retain a link to others of their blood, even continents away. This worldwide web calls itself the Technopoly, a union of industrialists, engineers, and dead nerds who keep a constant tab on the power shifts rippling through the All Night Society. Within their circle, they cut deals, exchange information, and share access to new technology… for a price. Theirs is no nostalgic fraternity, but rather a meeting of like-minds, using one another to mutually advance their own ends. From code-crackers to bank robbers, vote fixers to corporate spies, the Technopoly’s goals are as varied as the mortals they once were. The subtlest among them pass unnoticed among the lower ranks of their covenants, sabotaging equipment with their sizzling touches, or using the spark of their blood to gain access to sensitive records on behalf of the highest bidder. The more overt stride into combat with lightning lancing from their outstretched hands, destroying anything machine or meat that gets in their way.

                                                Like all Ventrue, every Keravnos has an angle, and to achieve their goals they’ll bend the technological world into any shape that serves their power lust. The more they achieve, the more brazen they become. The more they allow the Surge to sweep away Humanity, the more their curse haunts them. Power, as they say, has its price. Those Shockers who lose control of the are swallowed by the dark, becoming ravenous monsters that threaten to destroy all they’ve worked for. Their outer façades burst, their flesh rent and raw, stinking of burnt fat and fried hair, lips peeled back from permanently blackened teeth. Nothing of Humanity remains in them, not even the desire for blood. Instead, they siphon electricity with abandon, fearless of being spotted, and stripping it out of their former Keravnos brethren if given even half the chance.

                                                Why you want to be us

                                                Gone are the nights of hiding behind castle walls. If you want to survive in an increasingly sleepless, interconnected world, you need to use modern systems. That includes your food, and as one of the Jovians, you’ll get your fill without ever spilling a drop. Our lifeblood is in the very air, from the smartphone in your pocket to the satellites in orbit to the socket on your bedroom wall. With us, you’ll never go hungry again.

                                                Why you should fear us

                                                Zeus with his thunderbolts was most high of the Olympians. Thor’s thunder made him fiercest of the Norse pantheon. Marduk’s storms brought glory to all of Babylon. We are their inheritors. A new age dawns, one defined by steel and the crack of lightning, and it belongs to us. Give yourself to the new gods and pray they’re merciful.

                                                Why we should fear ourselves

                                                The more we feast on the wires, the more we sink into the belly of the machine, the further we slip from Humanity’s embrace. We’ve tamed a new monster to help us survive the modern world and keep the Beast at bay, but it’s birthed a blistering fire within us. Drunk on our own capacity, we tempt electrocution.

                                                Surge

                                                That prickle that lifts the hair from your arms when the pressure changes? Multiply it by a thousand and you’ll only begin to sense the Surge. Electricity is everywhere, but the stronger its concentration, the more it pulls at a Jovian’s dead heartstrings. Surge enlivens the Beast, staving off its natural hungers and unlocking access to a world of electric power.
                                                Cost: None or 1 Vitae per active effect

                                                Dice Pool: None

                                                Action: None (for persistent effects) or reflexive (for active effects)

                                                Duration: Permanent (for persistent effects) or one turn (for active effects)

                                                Like physical Disciplines, Surge has persistent and active effects. Persistent effects are always on and have no cost. Active effects are reflexive and cost one Vitae per effect.

                                                Persistent: The vampire can sense the presence of electrical sources in the world around her. This constant hum is an invisible but tangible friction against her skin at all times, and she can use the system for finding blood with Kindred Senses to track current. If she touches a source or endpoint of electricity, she can draw it out, taking a Vitae per turn of contact. She can safely take (Surge) Vitae per scene, but past that capacity she suffers a bashing damage for each Vitae gained, with wounds resembling Lichtenberg figures around her extremities. If a unit isn’t meant to pump out raw electricity, exceeding its damage potential in Vitae shorts it out until it can be repaired.

                                                Active: By spending Vitae, the vampire can expel and control electricity. For each point of Vitae spent choose one effect from the following list. A vampire may spend additional Vitae to invoke multiple effects simultaneously, but no effect of Surge may be used more than once per turn.
