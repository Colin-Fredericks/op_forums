    I know that Vampires play fast and loose with their bodies because ... well blood, violation, and horror themes... but in real life, would such trades not suffer the inhibiting effects one might associate with using ones body in sexual ways?

    I take a weird pleasure in reminding guys who declare that if they were women, they'd find it easy to elevate their social status because they could just 'bare all' on camera and such to obtain views, fame, what have you, that if they were indeed women, they'd be as conservative with their bodies as they're now. (Apologies for the proliferation of commas in the previous sentence.) Gay-for-pay is a thing, to my knowledge, but I noticed upon been informed of such, the guy rarely sets up a camera and starts making bank. Actual hurdles in plying that trade aside, it seems they believe they would until they can, and then they don't.

    All this nonsense just to say, perhaps PC's do it because the players are playing. The characters of the world may not like the idea of surrendering themselves for clear gains for the same reason the lads above shy away. Some clearly do do it, but we aren't all engaged in the sex industry; likewise for experimenting which may not be as common as some think and more common than others think.

    On paper, even IC, it might be a purely utilitarian trade but I'd play it that it is rare (especially among the neonate and ancilla), because when push comes to shove, sharing YOUR blood is intimate and you've got this requiem thing sorted, so you don't NEED that power. Maybe you need to think about this, because what if you don't REALLY want it? What if it is a trap or a fad? Are we sure there are no side-effects? The others want it but are you so easily led? No, you'll take your time and consider this. Besides, you're immortal, you can try it later, no need to rush. You'll have unlimited power, but tonight, might just get a bite to eat, ya know?

                            I've never seen it done in the fiction, or can't recall it happening even among close friends (and you don't really blood bond close friends unless you want to ruin the relationship).

                            Usually with the Beast, with the nature of the All Night's Society it gets drilled into vampires early that their powers are going to be the only thing keeping them going and keeping them from being taken advantage of in the worst of ways. And their own nature isn't exactly encouraging generosity and trust. Look at things like the assorted blood sorceries and the Coils and Scales. Everyone seems to be able to learn them, but unless you're really tight with that covenant they're going to tear you to pieces. The only thing that likely keeps clans from doing similarly is that they're not as well organized.

                            But PCs are by their nature special. Even if you as ST run a setting where characters are all 'on the same level' and the PCs aren't meant to stand out, they'd do so by the very fact that they are played by people other than you.

                            I'd probably let the PCs know that their instincts cringe away from the idea but leave it up to them.

                            Realistically, the players in my group would be more likely to seek it out from NPCs, either stealing it from them or perhaps from a superior or lover.

Using the Kiss as a sex metaphor though, it might be more common if vampires grouped together to survive ( i.e. neonates) have a tendency to 'eat' each other. All this modifies likelihood but so far, I think trade proposals would be met with a raised eyebrow by good vampire society.
                                • The utilitarian perspective. Again, against all the doubt and danger, how often will vamps act according to their rational side instead of bowing out? I agree, insofar that it doesn't feel wrong, it will most likely come down to pure logic. The beast reacts differently to its own and I think it would manifest feelings of dread/disgust at allowing others to 'eat' it. In sum, even if it wasn't sexual, it might still disturb the closest thing a vampire has to instincts.

                                                So I haven't read the entire thread. I just wanna give my two cents on the original question, even if maybe someone has hit the same points.

                                                So look at the question from the perspective of a vampire. Vampires are creatures that can, potentially, live forever. There are, essentially, two ways a vampire dies.

                                                One is they get careless and fall to one of their weaknesses, mainly sunlight. Blood is, all things considered, easy enough for a vampire to get, so dying of, for lack of a better term, starvation, is not, really, a concern. Sunlight is their main natural concern, and even that isn't *that* hard to deal with.

                                                Most vampires who die their final death do so due to their second problem: unnatural final death. As in something kills them.

                                                Hunters are a problem, of course. But vampires who manage to survive their first few years are likely clever enough to not fall prey to such a trap. And remember that for most vampires, years are nothing.

                                                Another potential problem for vamps are other supernaturals. The amount of interaction between supernaturals, admittedly, is gonna depend on how your game is run, but I've usually read the books with an understanding that the different supernatural groups having any interaction between them is rare. So yeah, a vamp may die if some werewolf decides he needs to go. But I don't see that as being a common thing.

                                                As far as I have ever read the books, it seems to me that the biggest threat to a vampire's continued existence is other vampires. They are naturally selfish, manipulative, and dangerous creatures. A vampire who doesn't immediately interpret other vamps as potential enemies, if not enemies outright, is one who's not gonna survive many nights.

                                                So consider all of this information. No discipline is necessary, really, for any vamp to survive. They can all hide from the sun and get blood easily enough with their natural abilities, without even tapping into their own disciplines, let alone other clans. And their whole existence, really, is survival. That's the gamble of being a vamp. I can live forever, if only I can avoid my weaknesses and avoid being killed.

                                                So with all that being considered, consider the risk and reward of trading disciplines. If there's a discipline I, specifically, need to possess for my agenda, then maybe, MAYBE, it is worth it. But that's a big if. What's stopping me from trading a favor for a favor with a vamp who has that discipline? Just because I don't have dominate doesn't mean there isn't a ventrue who needs someone to do his dirty work. And that ventrue would probably be more than willing to dominate this or that person, mortal or vamp, in exchange for me using, say, my auspex to find some information out for him.

                                                But as far as trading disciplines, remember the number one cause, as far as my interpretation, of vampire death is other vampires. Maybe not by fist to fist violence. But manipulation and deceit and all that good vampire stuff. And vampire alliances are fragile things. So just because this vamp is my ally at this exact moment doesn't mean he will be in 100, 50, or even 10 years. And giving him access to my clan discipline right now is giving him a tool that might be potentially turned against me later.

                                                I'm not saying vamps trading disciplines doesn't happen or make no sense. I could especially see it being common between younger vampires who still think in human terms and don't have this kind of "everyone is either a tool or a potential enemy" mentality. But older vamps are likely to be more cautious and more likely to deal in temporary alliances than to give potential rivals tools that can be used against them a long ways down the line. The longer a vamp lives, the less likely he or she is to think in terms of wanting to acquire power. That's a human concern. Vampires are concerned with survival. It's why the beast is so scary. Because it is what all vampires tend towards: pure survival instinct. Power, really is only a means to that end. A vampire straight out of character creation is powerful to a degree that we really can't conceive of as humans. Hell, a ventrue who puts all his starting dots into dominate can literally end the world by dominating his way into the white house and having the president launch all the nukes. Power is something tempting to us humans, but vamps are parasites. They don't need power. And the longer they exist, the more they realize that. They just need to survive. Everything they do is geared towards that one hope of immortality.
