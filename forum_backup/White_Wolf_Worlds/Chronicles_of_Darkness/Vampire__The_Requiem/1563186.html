    Casual Discussion - What is your general experience with the game?

    08-17-2026, 03:31 PM
    Hello everyone,

    Even though we all play the same game, interpretation and local gaming culture is probably going to have the most impact on how we express the game at our respective tables. While I always knew it was the case, the few discussions I took part in here made me curious about my fellow poster gaming styles.

    As a fun exercise, no badwrongfun here. I wanted to ask : What does your typical VTR game look like? What is your general experience with the game?

    I’m also interested in both the player and storyteller perspective.

    Here are some other questions to guide your answers. Feel free to ignore, or add any :
    • What does your game tend to focus on?
    • How to you handle time in the game?
    • What do you enjoy about the game?
    • Do you homebrew a lot, or play mostly as written?
    • How did you get introduced to the game?
    • Are you still currently playing or have you drifted toward another game? (Would you come back, if not what made you quit?)
    • What is your game setting like?
    • Any favourite clan?
    • Any favourite character? (PC or NPC)
    • Favourite scene?
    I’ll answer all the questions above, but the post is pretty long, so feel free to engage with whatever you feel interesting or want to share! 😊
    My typical game structure.
    In my circle most CoD or WoD chronicles tend to run as plot-kit based cityscape sandboxes with an overarching background plot generally structured in one or multiple arcs.

    Players will typically have solo scenes and coterie scenes, depending on what they are currently doing and how inclined they are to work together, although we usually try to include others because it is more fun. Unlike other RPGs, such as DND, we found that VTR really lends itself to ‘’splitting the party’’.

    Plot kits are basically the equivalents of ‘’side quests’’ that fit within the macro-narrative of the chronicles. Sometimes they are just fun ideas, other times they serve to present crumbs for the larger hidden mystery behind. They can also be the result of previous player character’s actions or be based on themes or simply things the player wants to explore with their character (basically aspirations). Ignored mysteries either shelved for later, develop into something else or get resolved off screen in some ways. Even if the game naturally centres around the PCs, our goal is generally to make the city feel alive. For example, it can happen that NPCs gets into conflict with one another and if players don’t push in any direction, it will eventually resolve one way or another.

    One aspect that I anticipate will differ from most is that I sometimes use miniatures for combat, larger ‘’missions’’ or even some social gatherings where I can physically place NPCs around for players to see. Since I own a 3d printer, I can print each player’s own character's miniatures and paint them. I generally find battle maps online and print them on paper sheets, there are a few very good artists for modern battle maps. This is definitely an aspect I picked from DnD, for sure, but I feel it helps with immersion.

    Game Focus
    Our game typically focuses on a mix of political and personal drama, depending on the actual character's focus. It can even sometimes look like a slice of life. (Or slice of undeath?)
    Vampiric life will be part of the game, but after a while it will tend to make more place for the sake of the story. It’s never fully ignored, but in our experience we’ve found that feeding scenes, touchstone scenes and scenes that involve the personal horror of being an undead monster are fun once in a while, but can get repetitive. Personal horror generally creeps into other scenes, sometimes in unexpected ways.
    We tend to roleplay in the first person unless it’s to describe what our characters look like, or doing, but in our group we take a very acting approach. For as much as we can.
    While action is part of the game, we tend not to focus too much on combat. Which mind sounds weird, considering I use miniatures, but it does happen from time to time. However, when we want more mechanically interesting combat, my group and I look for other games that have combat as its focus. (I'm perfectly fine with that aspect of CoD. I quite enjoy that combat is quick and gritty.)
    How do I handle time?

    I previously explained my method in another post, but I ran my game on a calendar system I made up and made to work with my hunger dice rule (also available in another post).
    Basically, the game runs on a monthly basis, with four weeks plus one weekend representing Elysium night. Each week, players get:
    • 1 feeding action to reset their Hunger dice, representing how well they hunt during the week. (I homebrewed Hunger dice into the system.)
    • 1 personal action they can devote to... anything, really. It can be a Touchstone scene to regain Willpower, they can work on a plot kit, find rumors, etc. They must make a Rouse check before this action to see if they get hungrier.
    • 1 coterie action they must use in conjunction with their coterie to advance whatever the coterie wants to work on. They must also make a Rouse check before their action to see if they get hungrier.
    So far it works pretty well, makes times advance at a steady pace and represents the lack of time vampire suffers from by losing at least 4 hours of active time; having to think of hunting all the time and devoting time to menial activies.
    What do I enjoy about the game.
    Apart from appealing to my nostalgia to me, vampire the Requiem manages to strike a perfect balance of thematic structure and freedom of expression. Lore ideas are interesting but aren’t too restricting like I’ve felt Masquerade to be (even though I quite like Masquerade).

    I like how the five clans are each unique yet general enough that you can make any concept work.

    I love the covenants. I’m not quite sure why I love them so much, but like clans, they are faction archetypes that are generic enough to allow different character concepts, or even covenant expression, while still having their own solid lore.

    As a storyteller I enjoy strixes as some form of evil trickster spirit. I think they have to be used in moderation, but since they don’t have human goals, it allows me to stress a bit less about the why and put more emphasis on the how.

    Homebrew?
    Yeah, I homebrew a lot. I love the game a lot, but I tend not to shy away from homebrewing rules to push incorporate certain feels, or deal with some merits I find disruptive in my experience. Not an exhaustive list, but I’ve homebrewed :
    • Hunger Die. (Similar to hunger dice, but limited to a single dice, the rule is accessible on another post.)
    • Reworked humanity into categories of sins. (Took some inspiration from an old post on this forum.)
    • Reworked some merits.
    • Added Brawl & Weaponry to base defense calculation instead of gating it behind a merit.
    How did I get introduced to the game?
    I originally got introduced to Masquerade when I was but a young teenager, around like 11 or 12? We played a pretty loose version of LARP VTM with rules we could find on different wikis, guided by older teens who ran local LARP games in my youth center.

    Requiem was technically released pretty much around when I started playing VTM. Since I was playing with a bunch of grognard, it wasn’t until much later, around 2006 or 2008, that I learned that a local Requiem LARP was being run in my hometown, as part of the Camarilla International Organisation ( or something like that I don’t quite remember its name). But since I wasn’t 18 at the time, I couldn’t join. ☹
    Due to my impossibility of joining the local game and with the numerous horror stories I heard from a friend who didn’t enjoy their LARP much, my interest waned out.

    It wasn’t until 2018-2019, where I finally decided to pick up VTR 2e again and give it a try. I read the base book from front to back and really liked what I had read. Fell in love with the setting again and its overall structure and finally decided to run a chronicle. Thankfully, even though my friend preferred Masquerade, they ended up getting hooked on VTR as well.
    Currently Playing?
    Yes! I run my own chronicle and I participate in a chronicle a friend recently started. We’ve reached 24, 5 to 7-hour-long sessions for my chronicle, which I’ve been running for about a year now. I still have material for quite some time and have ideas for some spin-off or time skips into cyberpunk VTR eventually.

    My friend’s chronicles started 3 months ago and has reached 8 sessions. I'm really enjoying his campaign so far.
    My Current Setting
    Currently, the chronicle I run takes place in Tokyo from 2025-2026. The game started in April 2025 and we have reached Febuary 2026.

    I previously ran a chronicle in the same setting 6-7 years ago, which took place in 2012, roughly 3 to 5 years before the Hotogisu appearance, which ended up never happening thanks to some player’s actions.

    I won’t go into too much detail, as I know some of my players are aware of my online persona; but the basic overarching plot is that some forces are trying to destabilize the city to wrestle it from the 3 Zaibatsu’s control, whose leader acts as city council in the absence of an official prince.

    Right now the game focuses on the power struggle between the various city factions. There’s also a huge emphasis on a revenant crisis and Belial Brood infiltrators using the revenant crisis to destabilize the city.

    I have 3 players; their characters are :
    Tanimura Hisako : An Ex-idol Nosferatu, starved for attention and parental approval. She recently got a high position in the Maeda, thanks to her mentor; her Grand Sire the Master of Elysium.

    Inoue Shinobu : A grieving mother Mekhet, who lost her child 15 years ago, thanks to a previous PC’S action from the 1st chronicle. She’s handles the occult stuff for the coterie thanks to her mastery of Kigan and her membership in the Ume house. As the childe of the harpy, she’s also very well connected in the All night society. She was recently infected with Malkavia and is trying her damned best not to spread it nor get caught, as it would most likely result in her execution. (Malkavia was introduced as a plot kit that can lead to a bloodline).

    Kuronuma Rei (Ex-PC) : An Idealist Gangrel with a weird twice-cursed bloodline. As the childe of Ventrue possessing a Gangrel, he was an idealist fledgling who got himself entangled with the wrong people. The player ended up dropping the character due to increasing in-coterie conflicts, which they did not enjoy.

    Shiki Ryona : New PC of the previous gangrel player, she’s going to be a muscle-lady gyaru Daeva of the Ducharge Bloodline. She will act as a fighter/social character for the coterie. I’m looking forward to seeing the character; she sounds like a lot of fun.


    The Maeda Tower : Which acts as enforcers. In my setting they are pretty close to what the Invictus would represent. I even ended up giving them the Invictus contract instead of their basic merits because it felt more interesting. Thanks to some player action, they become a real power house instead of falling into obscurity, as they were fated to be at the beginning of the first chronicle.
    • The Ume House : House of Shinto-adjacent priests have access to their own version of cruàc, called Kigan. They are the real power behind the throne.
    • At their head a duo of very active elders ;
      • Ume Yukie, the reclusive Daeva Witch cultivating her cult far away in the mountainous area of Tokyo, handles the spiritual hegemony of the Ume.
      • Saito Shiga, the politically savvy Nosferatu, handles the distribution of territory by the city, mandated by the council of the 3 zaibatsu. She is incredibly influent and acts as a prince without technically being one.
    • The Takahashi : Until very recently, I ran the Takahashi as they are described within the book; a pyramidal company focused on money and power. They used to be the most populous covenant and most influential until they were infiltrated by a Belial brood who leaked their haven to a faction of desperate revenants. The council board members, who mostly got left intact, decided to reorganize into smaller, more tightly knit units. They also kicked the Nosferatu out after a few scandals.
    Other Factions :
    • The Nosferatus : The Nosferatu are spread among all factions, but they have access to very powerful elders and they care about their clan status within the city. For this reason, Nosferatu holds a lot of power and other clans tend to be weary of them. They recently defended one of their own after he committed some diablerie (after being forced to by a Belial brood to activate some other political scheme), so other kindred are slowly waking up to the extent of Nosferatu nepotism within the city. For this reason, the mostly Mekhet/Daeva council members of the Takahashi essentially expelled them from their organization.
    • The Lancea et Sanctum : Led by Saito Shiga’s eldest Childe, the bishop is nonetheless very critical of the zaibatsu and uses his status as one of the most powerful kindred childe to shield the Lancea et Sanctum from persecution. The Lancea has always been a stark critic of the Takahashi structure and they have been vindicated lately by the whole Takahashi fiasco. Their ranks are steadily increasing with each of their political opponents’s mistakes.
    • Oda Clan : Until recently, they used to be Ventrue only Ordo Dracul faction, but they finally opened their door to more applicants, as a plot by the leader to eventually claim the praxis over the city. Led by a direct descendant of Oda Nobunaga, the clan leader was involved in some very dark stuff involved in unit 731. (Trigger warning, unit 731 was involved in actual crimes against humanity. Very, very dark stuff. Read at your discretion.)
    • Nora Inu : Literally means errant dogs in Japanese; they are a faction mostly composed of gangrels that hang around Ueno Parks. Mercenaries, anyone would call in their favour for more dangerous endeavours. Lately, they tried to push to get a place on the council using their banked boons, but their push failed. This pissed them off, and now they will definitely be up to no good.
    • Conglomerate : The conglomerate is a new phenomenon, led by a coterie of young ex-revenant kindred. They took advantage of the more quiet moments of COVID to establish some foothold in Shibuya-kyu. As a young faction, they very much look like unfocused anarch, with two simple goals : make the city take responsibility for the large population of revenants and wrestle control from the zaibatsu’s hand, which they do not trust at all. They have a few kindred, but sport an impressive number of revenants, which they shelter from the more oppressive forces of the city, which tend to exclude them or even execute them with little due process. Unfortunately for them, their open-minded policies and their lack of experience have made them ripe for Belial brood agitators. I gave them access to some Carthian merits.
    • Belial Broods : Not really a playable faction, although it could technically be made, the Belial broods are few, but at their head they have a mystical figure who always seems to be one step ahead. They recently recruited a lot of frustrated revenants and thought them about the wondrous benefits of diablerie. Thanks to their infiltration of the Takahashi, they managed to bolster their ranks with new diablerists on their way to either wassail or join their ranks permanently. They carved a hunting ground for themselves in Nakano-kyu after having diablerised the local independents. They often raid other kindred havens and have given the new Maeda security chief some real headaches.
    Favourite clan
    I love most of them, but Nosferatu are so cool in Requiem, i lament their inexistence whenever I play Masquerade.

    I love how varied their curse can be expressed using auras and scary supernatural traits, in contrast with Masquerade which focus purely on deformities or count Orlok clones. I personally love to play social nosferatu and mechanically; their curse makes every human interaction anxiety inducing, which is pretty fun.

    Their disciplines are also very cool; I love Nightmare. It’s certainly a bit more niche than other mental discipline, but you can get creative with it, and it brings a certain level of safety to have a too that allows you to scare off most potential dangers.
    Favourite Character & Favorite scene

    NPCs : My favourite NPCs have to be ‘’Kuchisake-Sama’’ my setting’s Master of Elysium. She’s a nosferatu based on the legend of the Kuchisake-Onna. She’s a master of Nightmare & Majesty and handles Elysium security by making her retribution swift and painful.
