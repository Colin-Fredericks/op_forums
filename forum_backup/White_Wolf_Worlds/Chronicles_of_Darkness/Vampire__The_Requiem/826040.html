    Heretical Coils of the Ordo

    02-08-2016, 12:38 PM
    So with Secrets of the Covenant just around the corner we've seen the upcoming release of the Coils of Ziva, which is considered heretical amongst the dragons due to its goal of turning back to humanity. A similar Mystery existed in first edition with the Anoushka's Ladder.

    Now my question is quite simple: what does it take for a Mystery to be considered Heretical? Is it simply the idea that humanity s something to thrive for (one way or another) or would a Mystery centered around Amaranth be considered heretical too? Due to it encouraging breaking the Third Tradition, I could understand why it would be heretical, but if you could control the dangers of Amaranth to your own soul or hide its taint on you, then it would allow you to become one of the greatest predators ever.

    Any other ideas of Heretical mysteries which could be interesting to have?

      On the opposite side of the coin of Anoushka's Ladder there was the sworn of the Locust, who believed that there was nothing inherently special about the soul other than the pleasure and power one gets in devouring it. They were able to commit diablerie on humans and gain abilities as long as they had the soul in them. I don't recall if they were considered a heretical sect.

      There was also the Dragolescu, whose Discipline of Essentiaphagia allowed them to, sort of, commit diablerie on ghosts. The problem with the Discipline was that it was possible that a ghost could usurp control of a practitioner's body, which was considered far too dangerous to balance out the benefits. On one hand you could learn the secrets of the dead, on the other your entire branch of the Ordo could be possessed by a particularly cunning ghost.

              It has to be something that goes against the teachings of Dracula, as written in the Rites of the Dragon. Becoming human isn't a goal of the Ordo* as a whole - that's going backwards when the Ordo wants to move forwards. Which is ironic, considering that one of his Brides, Anoushka, agreed to help develop the Coils specifically to help reclaim her humanity.

              Anyways, some of the teachings of the Rites include the following - 1) No student shall have no loyalty above his studies, and seeking perfection of the self. 2) All dragons must recognize that all things are mutable and can change. 3) Must take responcibility for own actions, and come to understand and use failings, not deny them. 4) The rankings of the Oathsworn and Titles should be obeyed, save in conflict with any others. 5) Do not submit to other supernatural beings, be they ghosts, angels, devils, or anything else. Make deals as equals or superiors is allowed, but not as a follower. 6) No "Prostitution of the Coils" to the unworthy. 7) Sworn of the Mysteries cannot drink the vitae of others - blood bonding is a no-no, diablarie is a no-no. 8) Hungarian marriage (two vampires feeding off each other) is forbidden. 9) The goal of the Ordo Dracul is to transcend the vampire condition and become something greater, not to become human, degrade to a mindless beast or consume the world in vampires.

              There are probably others, but those are the ones I can think of off the top of my head. Heresies would be factions that go against these established doctrines. So, using Coils to unleash a plague of vampires on the world is as much of a forbidden path as becoming human. Serving the God-Machine. Dealing with the strix and letting them ride your body. A faction that's devoted to evolving all vampires as a whole, in a kind of enlightenment-for-all theories. Not quite a heresy faction, but a teacher that enslaves his pupils is considered a heretic.
