    Vampires and the World

    09-17-2017, 09:07 PM
    So as I'm working on the latest HtV fanbook, I just got hit by a thought that I want to run by the vampire forum.

    I noticed that compared to figures like witches or werewolves, vampires seem...small scale. By that I mean that the mages are fighting over how to shape reality itself, as werewolves are fighting over whether or not the Spirit World will become part of the real world.

    What threats do vampires have that can match up to this level? Am I thinking about this the wrong way?

                  Check out Danse Macabre, it gets into tiers of play. As you get into higher tiers, things like elders and methuselahs become conspiracies and covenants of their own. Or the Mother's army, who seek to convert every vampire to their cause through the Mother's own Vitae, which they can summon via ritual.

                              I mentioned some stuff back in March that might be broadly useful here for framing's sake:

                              Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                              Werewolf and Mage both deal with magic that operates on much more global forms of contagion, whereas Vampire occupies the awkward split between "'feed responsibly' covers most of the problems a personlike vector for the Curse can cause without deliberately fucking about" and "most of the non-personlike vectors for the Curse are either ignorant of the concept of limiting exposure or actively derisive of it" — there's no consistent secret world of vampirism that threatens to infect the mortal population besides the Kindred and a whole lot of weird bullshit that can't or won't keep the lid on on its own because it's a cursed letter opener or a tainted plot of land or a Strix, so the bulk of the hush-up operations are dealt with on the Kindred end of things through cultural taboos like the Traditions or dedicated conspiracies like the Invictus. Things still leak through the gaps between the boards, because that's the nature of vampirism and the supernatural at large, but needing to secure enough blood to reanimate your corpse until dawn for seven nights a week is generally harm enough and reason enough to try and keep a lid on your unfortunate diet and allergies as one of the Kindred.

                                      To be fair, Werewolves aren't exactly big scale either. They do have precise goals, contrary to vampires, which are "exterminate or assimilate the Forsaken and maybe bring back Pangea" for the Pure and "Keep the world as it is by being a glorified border Patrol" for the Forsaken. The players playing Forsaken most of the time, that means players' most basic goal in a Werewolf game is "maintain status quo". Now, 2e also introduced the hunt thingie so now, games can also revolve around PCs hunting stuff because it's in their blood. Aside from that, the Pure aren't exactly unified anymore, and the Forsaken fight each other a lot too, so there's no real global threat on their end, and the Spirits are little more than a bunch of unpredictable mess. The only things that can really disturb that dynamic are unusually powerful spirits, and the Idigam, which are a big fucking deal, although they're more of an outsider to the typical Werewolf ecosystem and kind of freak occurences.

                                      Now, vampires, really are about the day to day life as a monster, if you follow the book very closely. The good thing about that, to me at least, is that it means you can run almost anything as a vampire campaign and it won't feel like a distraction. Werewolf have a mission to prevent spirits from messing up the material world, and most of their preoccupations gravitate around managing a territory, hunting spirits and fending off the Pure and anyone that threatens their ability to do those things. Vampires have nothing to do but survive and try not to become monsters, really, so while that can be the core of the game (that's what it's more or like supposed to be), they can keep doing that while doing a lot of other stuff.

                                      Also, another idea. Most vampires probably don't see it this way, but, with the way the Strix act, could you imagine what they would do if vampires weren't there for them to torment ? The Strix are weird and centered on the vampires enough that the latters are kind of the only ones who realize they even exist, and how dangerous they can be. They're really the frontline against them, wether they realize or not. If the rest of the world of darkness managed to eliminate vampires, all they might end up doing is opening the way for a much less human theat, one that isn't even interested with coexisting, one nobody is prepared to face.

                                              A more meta reason for Requiem not covering world-wide and time-spanning plots could be that we already know what it would look like, in the form of Masquerade. When expanding Requiem games' scale, most of the time it's about tracking the nature and source of the Curse or worldwide conspiracies, both of which have been dealt with in Masquerade.

                                              Either games could focus on both personal/regional stories and historic/global ones, and they in fact do, but they decided to specialize.
