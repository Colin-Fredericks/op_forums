            Originally posted by The Storyteller View Post
            I just want to be clear about my objection to these systems. It's not because I want them hard, it's because I want to encourage actual immersion in the characters. I actually think these sorts of things break immersion. All of those storytelling reward mechanics, like FATE points or getting willpower back, they create reward cycles that encourage people to take specific actions. In storytelling games, those specific actions tend to be those that either highlight aspects of your characters or those that create more drama. When that's what you want, it's perfect. When you want something more, something like real immersion in a character so that they act, you know, like a real person and not like a character in a drama, those reward cycles are an obstacle. It will create an internal conflict for players between immersing and getting rewarded. I'd rather just trust they'll do it right without a carrot and stick to force them.
            Okay, point taken. One way I want to react is by echoing Milo V3 in saying that rewarding players for 'thinking as their character would' is awesome simulation. I don't think it is dramatic but correct modelling of vampire psychology. So I very much resist the notion that the player feeling compelled to crack a joke at a funeral is bad design, if the person is a jester. It actually matches player incentive (more resource) with character expression (that is just the type of person they are) so that behaviour emerges organically and isn't forgotten.

            HOWEVER, to be fair, that is just my bugbear. If I carry on arguing why you are wrong for not following my one twue way, then I might get a willpower point back but I won't be too helpful or friendly. So my inner player is going to bypass the free willpower and engage with what is at stake: Immersion.

            Immersion is person specific and if what works for your players is to remove or amend the soft systems, then so be it. My advice has to presume traits about the group based one what you have supplied, so when I am really off, just ignore me. Oh and discuss all these changes first with the players.

            First, I would go all out and embrace player freedom by identifying player thought with character thought. That is to say, if dramatic systems are breaking immersion by creating distance between play-as-immersive-experience and play-as-optimal-solution, then outright scrap them where they interfere but retain them lorewise. For example, get rid of humanity as a stat. The beast is still doing beasty things and vampire society keeps it at bay but it sees no mechanical expression. Vampires with low humanity are simply those that don't resist the beast as often. (You do lose the corpse effect but minor loss). People sense the predator the same way we recognise a creep at a bar; body language and not-at-all-hidden intent. Getting rid of the mechanic means touchstones can go. Not needed. The numerous subsystems that use humanity can be hacked when you encounter them; fire and sun deal a set level of damage per turn, for example, and torpor requires resisting the urge to wake and feed, with being harder to resist with thin blood.

            Second, give your players a flat refresh whenever they get a day off. The day off is always off-screen and can be given a quick description. However, it competes with doing downtime stuff, and maybe feeding, as well as being inaccessible during long campaigns 'in the field' or if kidnapped. That way there are no competing concerns during play but willpower is still a resource you must tend to or lose the privilege of having. (sidenote: score points with your Marxist buddies by making job downtown willpower neutral or draining, thus highlighting the capitalist nature of leisure time. The poor characters will be way less productive than their rich peer. ) The only problem I have with this is that 2E was wrote with the assumption WP is spent and regained quickly, and so you MIGHT run into trouble. Best practice is to test and calibrate.

            Third, change the curses to be major affects that require manoeuvring around. Plus, with Humanity not being a thing, they may need adjustment anyway. Just remember, this will affect all the clan in aggregate and thus may produce a certain stereotype. Suggestions:

            Deava can remain the same. They gain the stereotype of indulgence because they are either with everyone or all about one.

            Gangrel can't be stopped and doesn't want to stop. Whenever they must stop, because of rules or authority, they gains the Tempted condition. It doesn't make them frenzy but a long night of pussyfooting around Elysium takes its toll. They all gain the stereotype of being feral because they seem to have no time for social mores and rules.

            Mekhet can remain the same. They no longer burn faster but their clan as a whole is still weird.

            Nosferatu somewhat remain the same. Their creep factor is now level with blood potency. When they can feed on animals, they suffer a -1 penalty to social rolls, when they can only feed on people, that is -3, and when it is just vampires, they scare the batshit out of everyone [everyone gets frightened condition]. Whether this affects other splats is up to you and I recommend letting all people spend WP to suppress the effects for a scene, allowing those close to the Nos to treat them well but at a cost, so nobody wants to interact with them but can out of devotion or friendship. The stereotype? They're unnerving/terrifying.

            Venture don't have a weakness: the Deucalions are right. However, they do enjoy winning and their desire for control is so strong it would be seen as weakness by lesser individuals. All Venture have the Obsession (Authority) condition. There is some ambiguity around the concept of authority but 'personal' authority is not authority: the Venture want their excellence recognised. Furthermore, actions that risk 'victory' require a will power expenditure (one per scene). Lastly, since order begets control, disorder irritates Ven more than others. A lack of clear hierarchy* result in the Distracted condition and pressure to engage in an extended action before order is affirm provokes frenzy. (hierarchies have a least two levels and somebody must be present in both. The easiest hierarchy is 'I'm the boss; do as I say!')

            I think these options open up immersion. Ventures are better when focused and structured, Gangrels in places where regulation is light, Nos where they are not in the spotlight unless among friends, Davea when they can control their feeding, and Mekhets when they avoid their bane. The advantages and disadvantages are well within player agency to affect.

            What else needs amending?

                                            A common transition re: Humanity in Sim games is to actually make it a known quantity by the characters. The physical changes, the limits to frenzy, the social modifiers - all of these things can be perceived in the game world, and thus perceived by the characters and commented on or manipulated by the characters. The man-beast struggle, Golconda / Draugr - these are game-world events intrinsically linked to humanity.
