        I don't think hard and fast ratios suit the ChroD in general. Every city is unique and disconnected from most other cities. That said, as a general rule of thumb, vampire population scales to mortal population, and competition scales to population density. You can manageably cap your vampire population by assuming that they aren't squabbling simply over feeding rights but also over local power and influence.

        When building my city, I adopted a method of breaking the environment down into neighborhoods, identifying the defining force of that neighborhood, and using that as a barometer to determine the general population. So I have an Invictus Prince Pro Tempore who's a real estate maven and who's seat of power is the financial/tourism corridor. This is a region where domain right are likely to be fairly strict, so I expanded out what sort of bureaucratic positions would be necessary for this region, sprinkled in some powerful kindred who don't have a direct role in the government but have the influence to maintain penthouses, and added a few vassals and others who are flying under the radar. As such, you have these hubs of control that stretch a few blocks and are the sole domain of a single kindred or a kindred family and then these gaps between the spaces where aspiring or struggling vampires can slip between the cracks. Apart from the core of the tourist hub and the airport district which are tightly overseen, lords and ladies are largely required to monitor their territory themselves. It's a medium density neighborhood.

        Then I looked at Emory University. It's a college neighborhood that houses both prominent hospitals and the CDC headquarters. This means that it's both ideal hunting grounds for predators and a place of concern for the establishment. Supernatural violations will be treated with extreme prejudice by mortal authorities. As such, it's tightly controlled by a small group of Ordo Dracul and closely monitored by the sheriff. Unaccepted incursions into the area are largely treated as capital offenses. As a result, it's a very low density and homogenized neighborhood.

        On the far end of the spectrum, you have Southwest Atlanta, an expansive spread of the city that's traditionally black and low income and also disproportionately high in terms of its presence of churches and other religious institutions. There's a high population and a low lack of non-herd resources, which makes it a high density neighborhood for vampires. It also has a disproportionate amount of Sanctum influence, and the LS in my city tends to maintain large flocks of kindred believers. As such, characters who venture into this stretch of the city are more likely to encounter baronies that shift every couple of blocks and see communal broods overseeing territory.

                            I imagine Kindred survival rates are sort of like ancient mortal survival rates; survive your early years and you're pretty likely to make it far. If you've made it to 200 or so, it's by not making the kind of mistakes most likely to get you destroyed in the next thousand years too. So there's a likely bubble of young Kindred who've been Embraced by also relatively young Kindred, and then a gap, and then a decent population of subtle elders who spend some time torpid or relatively divorced from mortal affairs.
