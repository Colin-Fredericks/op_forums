                You are confusing the player with the character. The character is making the jarring realization that he is a monster.                 As for using superpowers to fight. If this game was not intended to facilitate vampires using badass powers to fight each other on occasion, then they should not have made a game with tons of cool superpowers to fight with. My Storyteller tends to like to put cool fights in his game, although they only make up about 20% of the play time. If I want to make a character that is good at fighting, I am actually at a disadvantage for most of the game, which will be spend in social conflict and investigation.


                Ah, it sounded like you were planning to keep him as a good guy from the first explanation of him.


                As for the game having so many super powers, that's because World of Darkness does not focus on one particular Vampire mythos. It includes all vampire mythos rolled into one. That was the whole point of having multiple clans and bloodlines, because different cultures have different versions of what a vampire is supposed to be like. Some legends claim they can shapeshift, while others say they have superhuman prowess, or the ability to hypnotize you with a glare, or they're irresistibly charismatic. In some cultures, vampires aren't even reanimated corpses, but rather they're disembodied spirits.


                The original world of darkness with Masquerade did focus more on the whole supernatural battle scenario. As it was described to me, you'd have vampires engaging in Kill Bill style katana fights on top of sky scrapers. The New World of Darkness broke away from that in order to focus more on the subtle, underlying themes of horror. You were raised as a human, now you're something that preys on them. How you learn to cope with that is up for you and the Story Teller to explore.



                Each of the 5 clans in Requiem is an archetype representation of the popular vampire story.


                Venture - Powerful and aristocratic. The kind of vampire demonstrated in the tales of Dracula, or Anne Rice.

                Gangrel - Savage and predatory. The kind of vampires that Fright Night demonstrates.

                Nosferatu - Hideous and terrifying. The kind of vampires represented in old country folk lore.

                Daeva - Seductive and graceful. The kind of vampires represented in just about any modern media.

                Mekhet - Mysterious and cunning. The kind of vampire that conspiracy theorists go on about.


                Those are just the basic archetypes. The Bloodlines are a means of applying cultural history to them in order to provide more depth to their functionality.



                EDIT: Also, while it is technically possible for a Vampire to learn every Discipline, the chances of them actually doing so are extremely slim. Vampires don't just spontaneously acquire new powers, they need to be trained. Usually, this training comes from their sire. And most Vampires cap out after learning 5 Disciplines because of the stress of keeping up with all that training. That means that, at most, the average vampire would only exhibit 5 power sets.

                    I can understand that, although it seems like one of the best ways to do that is to take out all physical diciplines.                     It's less the power mechanics, but more how they're utilized. As you point out, a character focused in combat abilities might get left behind in the political/social scene. If players are okay with that and fully aware there will be times they are taking a back seat - that actually helps a lot from my perspective. Sometimes, though, players say that - and then get bored or restless and so want to transition those physical powers into the social sphere so they can get back into the spotlight.

                    The thing is - that can work too, but it takes a little more nuance/skill to pull it off. The Nosferatu Sheriff shaking your hand with an iron vice-grip as you agree to support his efforts in covering the Masquerade reminds you exactly what he can do if you fail. The Gangrel harpy whispering to you off in the corner who trails the tip of her claw along your earlobe reminds you that more than her words have edges.

                    Instead, when it goes wrong, you have physical players punching their way to social 'success' and it can be very disruptive. The social characters feel like they're taking a backseat in their own realm, or they need to now get their own physical capabilities to compete. Basically it can be an infectious playstyle. It's really hard to teach a class or appreciate the fine details of sculpting, for example, when there's a bull in the studio wrecking things. Everyone kinda has to address the bull. Softer political scenes can be like that too.

                    But I'll admit, purely physical disciplines will usually rank pretty low on my wishlists for the PCs I make.

                    Thanks for the discussion, and good luck with your game.

                            Originally posted by K9ine View Post

                            Hmm, I never really thought of disciplines as something you would actually need training for. I thought that they were powers, either natural to the vampire, or given by blood, and that the dot represents getting stronger as you get more Vitae.

                            Come to think of it, Mekhet aren't really a vampire from folk lore or pop culture.


                            Well, it's in their name "Disciplines." As in, it's an acquired skill.

                            Any vampire is capable of learning any Discipline, but it requires a conscious effort in order to do so. As I said, this training usually comes from the vampire's own sire, which is why at least 2 of your starting Disciplines must be clan related. Think of it as the parental vampire passing their skills down to their descendants.

                            Disciplines that aren't part of the clan's natural affinity require the vampire to either find a teacher someplace else, or develop it through trial and error. That's why it requires more experience to master them.


                            The signature Disciplines of each clan / bloodline are exactly that, their signature ability. They're the ones who invented it, they perfected it, and they keep its power a closely guarded secret. They can be convinced to teach others what they know, but this knowledge does not come freely. Any vampire who wants to learn it is going to have to earn that privilege, which your character can not possibly have done if he was only embraced yesterday.


                            Protean was developed through the Gangrel's deep connection with the natural environment. They've weaponized their Beast, turning it into a tool for satisfying their needs. Most vampires are too busy trying to suppress their Beast to think about harmonizing with it. So learning how to shapeshift for a non-Gangrel is not a subject to be treated lightly.



                            EDIT: I should have said that the Mekhet represent the occult mysticism that surrounds the undead. If the Ventrue are the public face of leadership, the Mekhet are the shadowy puppet masters who watch from afar.
