    Vitae effects on mortals

    11-29-2017, 01:18 PM
    So, a player wants to blood-bond an elderly couple without ghouling them, and wanted to know what effect it would have beyond addiction. Good question; a very quick perusal of the book didn't yield an answer. I wouldn't necessarily give it stat bonuses, but it should have some describable effect. A mild-to-moderate euphoriant, sure. Maybe a slight stasis effect, in terms of longevity and physical degeneration, but not outside the range of human norms. Odd dreams, and if the user is under the influence of any other mind-altering substance, the Curse skews the hallucinations. Of course, healing, I should think, but no disciplines. What would you do?

                      If you're not ghouling them, then they would not gain the benefits of healing (which takes the expenditure of an internal vitae point... which you got from your regent as a ghoul). They would also not have enough vitae in their system to retard aging (another ghoul thing) or manifest Vigor, etc.

                      They would suffer the effects of the blood bond, which is likely to be negative to their mutual relationship as their thoughts and attention shift to the donor. That means living together will become more difficult, minor things blow up more, and decades of transgressions become "un-forgiven". Not pretty to see such a rapid emotional breakdown.

                      Whether there is vitae addiction that sets in would be partially determined by the addiction rolls on drinking. Since we're talking about small quantities (or at least, quantities not charged with the Willpower expenditure needed for ghouling), then the addiction roll could be at a bonus.

                      What is certain is that they are now forever "tainted" by vitae, and when they die (of old age or complications), there is a chance that they will (individually or together) wake back up. Revenants.

                      --Khanwuf

                          "Vitae is life. Life tastes very, very good. In fact, it tastes so good, it’s highly addictive to both mortal and Kindred alike." Pg 90
                          "Vitae addiction feels very similar to addiction to a strong drug." -- Now you might not interpret it the way I do, but if it looks like a duck, tastes like a duck, and quacks like a duck... it's probably a duck. In my games I have equated vitae to the drugs that it implies it is. Ecstasy, Heroine, Cocaine... except way way better tasting and feeling. I would say an extreme "Euphoriant"

                          One can not say that it has zero effects other than "blood bonding" because if it was like drinking water... it wouldn't be addicting. it tastes good. It feels good. It gives you a fix and a feeling of temporary euphoria that you want to have again and again and again. That's how addictions work. Like Khan said they will grow irritable and easily annoyed with small things.


                          However mechanically 1 vitae is still 1 vitae with or without the willpower spent to make it a ghoul. The rules in the book are very clear on how to proceed with the roll. Since the book doesn't deal with .25 vitae or .5 vitae, then there would be no roll for addiction if the amount was less than 1.

                          RAW: No healing potions as vitae doesn't last long out of a vampire. It becomes inert. No anti aging, unless you are ghouling. No stat bonuses even if you did ghoul. No funky dreams, though with some drugs, you can dream strange things if you fall asleep high... so maybe on that one. Unless you ghouled this addict, there are no disciplines either.

                          As Dante said, you might be confusing addiction with ghouling.


                          When a mortal drinks vitae, one two or three things can happen and they are all very different things.
                          Blood Bond comes in 3 stages -- automatically happens and a mortal can not stop it. 1 stage per night ( or scene if you'd like, the jury is out on that. )
                          Vitae Addiction -- he must roll Resolve + Composure, with a –1 die penalty for each point consumed. - might not happen
                          Ghoul -- this is a purposeful willful act and it can not happen on accident.

                              Not confusing anything, just experimenting with potential options that the books don't explore.

                              I'm looking at the body of evidence. From a strictly mechanics standpoint, Vitae exposure at the time of death can cause a monumental supernatural change to the flesh and (depending on your cosmology) spirit. Prior Vitae exposure can occasionally cause undeath as well. Vitae provides a mystical, chemically undetectable change in the body that psychically ties a mortal to the undead provider of the Vitae, and with a effort of will that effect can be focused to give the mortal supernatural abilities powered by the Vitae.

                              So, my take, based on both mechanics and available descriptions, and the fact that this is ostensibly a horror game, is that Vitae is a potent, insidious substance capable of significant alterations to living physiology is such a way that one might see it as a living entity itself.
