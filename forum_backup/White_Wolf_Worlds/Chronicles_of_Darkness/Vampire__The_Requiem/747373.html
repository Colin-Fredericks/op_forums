    [Actual Play] Darkness through the Golden and Heroic Ages

    11-01-2015, 04:36 PM
    After getting more players interested in my earlier chronicle through the ages (starting in Athens during the Persian Wars), I decided to rework some some stuff and start from the beginning.

    Although we're still in the process of character creation, I will be posting background material throughout the week, starting with the clans and followed by a version of Temporis (modeled around the 2nd edition Obtenebration power) that is appropriate for both the setting and Requiem. As always, criticism and suggestions are welcome.

    Clans of Classical Greece

    Nosophoroi

    From the depths of Hades, the Worms arose to claim pre-classical Hellas and turn it into a playground of horrors. Hunting out in the open and spreading disease at first, they were driven underground once more by shadows darker and monsters more nightmarish than themselves -such as the Strix, Lupines, and others. By the time of the Vrihi's arrival, the Worms gladly remained in their underground catacombs and tunnels, letting the Philosopher Kings and Queens reshape mortal and Kindred society above. While the Nosophoroi like to think they were the least affected by the changes brought about by entering the Classical Age, they too adapted to new roles such as spies, thieves, and information brokers among the Kataramenoi.

    A hideous clan in a city of beauty, the Nosophoroi of Athens are not the terrors walking in plain sight they once were. They operate mostly through a vastly expanded Necropolis that connects all of the city's graveyards, the gates on all of the city's main walls, the hills -including the Acropolis, the Agora, and several of the major temples of Athens.

    Every bit as hideous and terryifing, the Nosophoroi metics known as Euthanatoi lead their own cults of cthnoic, Thracian deities of death and the underworld. Renowned practitioners of Nekiya, the gift of necromantic sorcery, their presence in any Hellenic city is tolerated in so far as they keep to their own business and do not disrupt the business of the Polis.

    Disciplines: Nightmare, Obfuscate, Vigor

    Maniae

    In the Polis Erebus, insanity denotes insight and genius. Wasn't it Pythagoras, and so many others among the great ones, that heard voices or witnessed maddening visions? Nowhere are -or will- the Afflicted enjoy as much respect as they do in Classical Athens. Whereas the Vrihi lead as lawmakers and political rulers, the Maniae serve as their advisers and prophets. Most Philosopher Kings and Queens are wise enough to listen to their rumblings…

    The Maniae are not a clan proper, rules-wise. Instead, they are united by their Affliction and the gifts and curses it carries with it. Namely, the discipline of Dementation and the Deranged Curse.

    Disciplines: Dementation

    Clan Bane: The Deranged Curse – All Maniae suffer from a Persistent Condition that expresses itself as some form of mental illness.

    Empusae

    Priestesses of Hecate and practitioners of blood sorcery, the Empusae are credited with creating the Eleusinian Mysteries -or at least introducing Vampires to its secret pleasures. In their temples, worship of the divine is fueled by the use of drugs, sex, blood, and magic. Their location is known to a select few mortals who willingly come to them, offering their blood in exchange for a touch of the Goddess. Kindred of Persia and the Near East recognize in the Empusae's worship of Hecate, the similar rituals and imagery shared by their native Daeva cults of Lilith and Inanna.

    Disciplines: Celerity, Vigor, Majesty

    Siskioi

    The Mekhet, as they are called in their native tongue, do not mind that local Kindred consider them a more primitive kind of Vampire -due to their similarities with the Homeric shades of the Underworld. They know better, and so do the Vrihi, who value them for their scholarly pursuits, patronage over occult knowledge and artifacts, and mastery of Obtenebration. These worshipers of Set remember what lied beyond the Darkness that once surrounded cities and settlements, and are probably among the few lines best prepared to push them back there in case they ever re-emerge.

    Disciplines: Celerity, Obfuscate, Vigor, Obtenebration

    Vrihi

    If the stories the Vrihi tell of their origins are true, the Kindred and kine of Greece owe them a great debt for birthing what essentially became the Classical civilization. Their progenitor arrived here from a far away land that was almost destroyed by the Strix, and pushed democracy and higher forms of organization among mortals, so that the Birds of Dis would find it near-impossible to do in Greece what they did in Vrihi's native land.

    Upon arrival, the legend goes, Vrihi embraced two of the most gifted mortals the land had to offer him. In the forests and mountains of Macedonia, he found Doris, an extremely insightful barbarian warlord that had within him the capacity to inspire and bring together even the most unruly warrior. From matriarchal Crete, he also embraced Atana, famous among the educators in Knossos Palace for her intelligence and eloquence. One gave rise to Athens, and tasked her childer with her vision of her Sire's missions. The other went to Sparta, and organized a balanced, militaristic form of government. Before their release from their Sire's care, the two scions were lovers and had exchanged blood. Shortly before their separation they promised each other, the metropolis of one would never threaten the metropolis of the other. This all change with the migration -others might call it invasion- of the Lamia...

    These Medusae, as they are known due to their powers, originated in North Africa and was unable to establish a foothold anywhere in Greece. In xenophobic Sparta, Doris made the mistake of agreeing to meet the Queen of their caravan, only to ask her to leave his lands or suffer Final Death. By the time their meeting had ended, Doris had handed Sparta to the Lamia and was nothing but a servant to the new Queen. Out of pity, upon hearing of what transpired in Sparta, Atana sent her assassins to liberate Doris of his undeserving fate. The rivalry between Athens and Sparta, that would climax in the Peloponnesian Wars, had just begun...

    Disciplines: Auspex, Celerity, Majesty, Obfuscate, Temporis

    Clan Bane: The Civilizing Curse – The Vrihi prosper only in the presence of civilization, losing peace of mind amidst the uncertainty of the wilderness and chaos. Unless they have established a safe, permanent residence through slumbering in the same area for a number of nights equal to their rating in Blood Potency, they lose the 10-again quality in all mental rolls.

    Erinyes

    When the Earth was still young, our Mother Ekhidna trespassed on Dionysus' sacred vineyards and our Father Typhon usurped his Oracle in Delphi. Dionysus' brother Apollo cursed us to fear the Sun, but Zeus withheld his wrath, until we aided our Dark Parents in assaulting Olympus. Only then did he send the Erinyes after us, to punish our hubris. The Erinyes and their allies tell a different story though. It was Ekhidna who turned them against us, for abandoning the monstrosity she had instilled in us, becoming less of the monsters that had aided her in the assault on the abode of Gods and Goddesses. Giving us the choice to act as humans, they add, that was Zeus' real curse...

    Lamia

    Progeny of Queen Lamia who tamed Libya and planted the seeds of what would become the Carthaginian Empire, the Medusae quickly established themselves as the force behind the nascent empire's ruling elites partly thanks to their power of Dominate. Led by Dido, Lamia's favored childe, the Lamia contained their thirst for power and managed their thirst for the blood of infants in a more restrained and civilized manner -through the religious sacrifice of children. It was only after Dido's suicide, following her abandonment by Aeneas and his theft of the secrets of Dominate, that the Lamia became what they are tonight. Wherever they go, from Carthage to Sparta, they outright demand mass child sacrifices and commit mass embraces to the point of overpopulation.

    It is their greed that led some of them to migrate to Greece, and Sparta in particular. Like parasites, they infect their own cities until they can sustain them no more, at which point half of them flee to new ports and begin the process anew. When they arrived in Doris' court, they usurped the city's rule from the Vrihi and ultimately led to its undead ruler's death. Their power over Sparta uncontested, they established infanticide to serve their dietary requirements, and strengthened monarchic and oligarchic institutions over more democratic ones. Their presence is barely tolerated in Athens, where they are considered enemies to the Polis and are allowed to remain only for as long as diplomatic etiquette prescribes.

    Disciplines: Dominate, Vigor, Auspex

    Clan Bane: The Innocence Curse – All Lamia vampires receive the Distinguished Palate Merit, with its drawback limited to infants (VtR 2nd edition, p.111).

      Temporis
      Three powerful women visited Vrihi on the night before the Birds of Dis first attacked. They opened up the eyes of his Beast to the truths of Time, past, present, and future. His hand now felt the heights and depths on the Bas-Relief of History. He was ready to survive, sliding out of time and watching helplessly as his brothers and sisters were devoured by the owl demons. He had to survive, the three women told him, so that he could travel to a land in the West that needed the service of him and his childer…

      Attunement, the most basic power of Temporis, allows the vampire and its Beast to begin to grasp the possible applications of prerequisite disciplines outside the limitations of Time. Vampires progressing their mastery in this gift are often shocked to discover that the more they understand Time, the less they can hold on to their sense of continuity -and indeed reality. After extended use of Temporis, many vampires begin questioning the present and whether what they see is truly taking place.

      All Vrihi gain the first Devotion, Attunement, soon after their embrace. Additional Devotions have prerequisites as normal, and cost 2 Experiences each.

      Attunement
      The Beast becomes attuned to the exact passage and flow of time, as well as any supernatural distortions to it.

      Consult the Moirai
      Through this devotion, the effects of the Beast's Hackles are extended to a slightly wider time frame. The Beast, now better attuned to Time, can perceive things that happened a few seconds ago, or will happen by the end of the scene.

      Prerequisite: Auspex 1

      Cheat Atropos
      Retroactively erase any memory of your presence from a scene that happened as earlier as one week per dot of Blood Potency. Your actions are not canceled, and indeed they may be recalled vividly by onlookers. But any characteristics or signs that could be used to identify you, fade away from the memories of those who witnessed them.

      Prerequisite: Obfuscate 1

      Wrath of Ananke
      The Beast skips forward a turn in time, witnessing the outcome of an action you are about to perform, and reveals it to you in the form of a vision. Effectively, the vampire can re-take an action just performed, re-rolling the dice pool, or simply abstain from taking any action for the turn. The maximum number of actions that can be re-taken in a scene are capped at the vampire's Celerity rating.

      Prerequisite: Celerity 1

      Touch the Thread
      With a simple touch, the vampire severs the target's perception of time, making them disoriented, feeling removed from its continuity. Some parts of their surroundings appear to slow down, while others to have sped up uncontrollably.

      Prerequisite: Obfuscate 3

      Clotho's Touch
      The vampire is now capable of cheating Time to the point of appearing to travel through it, for a number of scenes equal to her Celerity rating.

      Traveling forward in time, the character basically fades away from Time itself, re-emerging in her future destination. She does not skip to the future point however. She lies hidden safely outside the perceptions of others -indeed outside of Time. Her perception and undead body (if necessary) are altered to ignore the passage of time entirely, until she has reached her destination. As far as the vampire is concerned, the leap occurred instantaneously.

      Things get stranger when the vampire travels back. Essentially, what took place in the present becomes a vivid vision that is transmitted to her past self, who can now alter her actions and the course of events that would have otherwise followed.

      Prerequisite: Celerity 3, Obfuscate 1 (for traveling to the future), Auspex 1 (for traveling to the past)

      Summon the Disposed Thread
      The vampire summons forth a solid image, a materialized illusion of an object or person from another time to the present. These entities were not, however, removed from their own time. They possesses and are capable of demonstrating what would be expected of them, in terms of knowledge, skills, memories, or function (in the case of objects). Eventually, summoned people or items simply fade out of existence.

      Prerequisite: Majesty 4

      Veil of Time
      Distort the perception of time for all those present inside an entire large area.

      Prerequisite: Obfuscate 5

      Summon History
      Summon to the area a historic event that manifests itself as a materialized illusion, similar to Summon the Disposed Thread.

      Prerequisites: Obfuscate 5, Majesty 4

                  I wrote something about Temporis a few years back. (I want to say I posted it last forum, but it might have been the one prior.) My take was somewhat similar, but with significant differences. I sort of re-imagined Auspex, Dominate, and Obfuscate through the lens of time... specifically the Fog of Eternity. Alas, my copy didn't survive my rapid chain of hard-drive failures a couple years back. (really. It sucked.)
                  Basically, they got alternate versions of the mentioned abilities that were time-themed, most of which were only cosmetically altered, and a few of which go different bonuses and penalties, but otherwise really similar to the normal stuff.

                  What I recall were things like the ability to draw the mists of time across a person's mind to cloud a memory and force the mind to forget the details of an event. (similar to your Cheat Atropos) Another variant on that, of course, could shroud the details of events in the fog of the distant past. (Forgetful Mind) Conjure illusions or visions of things past.
                  Part the Veil was a re-skinned Chimistry/Spirit's Touch that parted the veil of time. It had a quirky advantage in that it could do so even if something scrubbed the mystical/psychic signature from a thing, but gained little benefit from intensity of the event. Having a thing linked to the same time helped, though. I forget the details.

                  Some esoteric stuff, like the predicting the future or gaining Dream clues, were on the list. Having the ability to fog a person's memory or get them to blank on a face is useful. Calling up shadows from the past or hastening and prolonging torpor can be cruel.


                  It just occurred to me, though, that one of the things I utterly missed was a multi-attack power set:

                  Temporal manipulator schmoe attacks, activating his fast attack. Target's Defense reduces as if the attacker was attacked once per level of Temporal Shenanigans for the schmoe's attack and all successive actions. If the target has an irreducible bonus to Defense due to preternatural speed, such as from Celerity, that also reduces by any remainder left over from reducing Defense, but only for the schmoe's own attack.
                  Another mechanical toy to apply is: Reroll a failed die once per level of Temporal Shenanigans. For purposes of this power, do not count a die as failed until after any rerolls from the rote quality, should that apply.
                  Yet Anada: Activate Over and Over Again for some blood and willpower or whatever, and the target doesn't refresh Defense for a number of rounds equal to Shenanigans...Nope, better: Target loses the ability to recover Defense for the rest of the scene unless he takes a Full Dodge Action. (Insert some sort of activation and resistance rules here. I'm out of practice with vampire stuff.)

                  That's really funky wording. I'm thinking that the ability to refresh Defense requires focusing on it with a Full Dodge action, not that the power is cancelled when they dodge. That would be pretty lame.

                  That fits the devs' current fetish for multi-attacks in fluff only. For bonus points, make this a supernatural mental and temporal effect.
