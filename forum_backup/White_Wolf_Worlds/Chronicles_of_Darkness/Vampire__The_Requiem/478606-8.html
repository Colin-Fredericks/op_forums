        In the original Covenant Book the Dammitc and Iblic creeds were presented as minor parts of the Lancea Sanctum but a different Faction there in as an attempt to make a blanket Covenant for the various Abrahamic Faiths. The problem with this is that each religion has mutually exclusive ideas, specifically pertaining to the Crucifixion and the details surrounding it. To be true to the mortal Faith each is based on they would have to either downplay the importance of the situation or change the details of it to fit their world view, both which would be at odds with the fundamental philosophy and Longinian Dogma that the Lancea et Sanctum is based upon.

        In the Dammatic Case Jesus would have had to be a Jewish teacher with no more importance than that. He wasn't the Messiah or a Prophet which reduces the role of Christ in the Crucifixion and the role that Longinus would have played in his own damnation. The entry even says that the creed dropped large portions of the Testament to make it a better fit for Jewish interpretation.

        For the Iblic Creed Jesus was a prophet, but not THE Prophet and the details of the Crucifixion's events would have had to change. He couldn't die on the Cross and was of lesser importance than Mohammed. He wasn't the sacrifice for the sins of man. Which, again, downplays the role of Longinus.

        The only one of the Three Abrahamic Faiths in which the Crucifixion was a World shattering event was Christianity, which is also the most Important event in the Lancea et Sanctum's religion.

        If you want to make different Covenants based off those religions, fine, if you want to lend them some credibility or mystical powers give them Theban Sorcery with uniquely flavored rituals or something else entirely. The issue is that they are odd fits for the Lancea et Sanctum. Particularly with the game's larger (Though not exclusive) focus on the Western Civilization and the books Focus on each Covenant.

        And as for the Exotheists, that was a blanket term for any Lancea et Sanctum Kindred that believed that the Vampiric religion shouldn't copy mortal faith and should be their own thing. Exotheists could believe that Christianity was the True Religion for mortals but that the Lancea et Sanctum shouldn't be modeled off of it.

        And an important idea to note is that the Brood and Livian Heresy would but just that, Heresies and enemies of the wider Covenant so their belief system wouldn't have a bearing on the Covenant philosophy as a whole.

        So I don't think a Catholic-flavored spell is an odd fit for a Covenant that is largely Christian and Catholic based.

                Very little jumped out at me as a problem in this document. By and large I really like what you've presented here, and I'm looking forward to using it in play.

                Crusade is fun, flavorful, and cool. The covenant fighting styles having effects that are borderline magic is just fine with me, you see similar effects from Temple Guardian and the Impaled. Some tightened guidelines on what sorts of powers are hampered by it would be good (what other supernatural powers count as "sorcery" in your conception?) Scaling its penalty to Merit dots rather than Weaponry is a good suggestion, and I add my support to that.

                Flock is great, as is Temple of Damnation. One of my favorite things in reading these playtest docs is going "Oh man, that would have been perfect for this part of that game I ran..." Temple of Damnation in particular in inspirational in that regard and is already spawning ideas.

                Laity. The effects are solid, but I'm not sold on the name. I was under the impression that "laity" referred to members of the L+S that aren't anointed priests. Not a huge quibble, but I'm a fan of clarity in terms and this seems a bit muddy.

                Sanctuary. Solomon Birch and his "Solomon Swastikas," anyone? Sold.

                Stigmata. Love it. As for the question of what benefit it provides to a mortal, I didn't see anyone note this in the discussion so apologies if I missed it, but a mortal with Stigmata heals lethal damage at double the normal rate. That's a pretty handy benefit, and makes that mortal a serious asset to the covenant, and specifically to any vampire who feeds on them normally.

                Apple of Eden. It can grant attributes above 5, can the affected character spend Experiences to KEEP an attribute above 5?

                Marian Apparition. The menstrual blood in the sacrament might be a reference to the "black Madonnas" of Italy, which are highly venerated for being miraculous, and there are some lines of thought linking those miraculous icons to power inherent in menstruation.

                Revelatory Shroud. Very cool, but it's a one-dot ritual treading on what it otherwise takes Auspex 3 to accomplish. Granted it's slightly more narrow in focus, but only slightly.

                The rest of the rituals are solid. I particularly love Pledge to the Worthless One. Great Prophecy is extremely cool, and the outline seems to keep it tight in scope while leaving it a versatile tool for whatever a given game requires, not to mention the sacrament required. That should keep it from being flung around willy-nilly, which could become a problem. Really well done.

                                                    The thematic idea behind Bounty of the Storm is the acolyte belief that suffering brings growth. Whereas Theban Sorcery could transmute dross into gold, the Circle doesn't believe in such neat, easy, clean solutions to anything. So for them to gain wealth through sorcery there must be trial, and suffering. The ritual causes, potentially, quite a bit of death. 2/3 of the bystanders around the target of the ritual when the storm strikes end up dead. The caster risks humanity loss, the target is physically punished. In return for all that, some form of wealth comes to the target. Could be settlements, insurance claims, any of several different sources for the actual money (or the equivalent).

                                                    Suffering for gain is very Circle. You need a lot of money in a relative hurry? Okay. But it'll cost you, and everyone around you.

                                                    EDIT: And lest we forget, the ritual destroys an object of personal significance to the victim. How many "deeply personal objects" that aren't touchstones do vampires keep around? Some, sure. Probably not terribly many in most cases. Nothing given, nothing gained.
