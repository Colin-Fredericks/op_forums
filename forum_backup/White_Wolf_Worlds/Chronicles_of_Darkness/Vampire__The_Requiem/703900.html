      Green Eyes basically allows the Kindred using it to change the emotional states of anyone affected by Confidant or Loyalty. So if you have a room full of people you have charmed or enthralled, you can make them happy, sad, whatever you wish. It also allows you to basically "shift" how conditions affect victims. A charmed mortal under the effect of Green Eyes basically behaves like someone who was enthralled. An enthralled mortal under the effect of Green Eyes has one limit: they won't kill themselves. Otherwise no action they take, including taking damage or what would normally be a breaking point, will cause the enthralled condition to resolve. It basically guarantees that the enthralled condition will last the maximum amount of time, and then the mortal suffers all of the consequences.

      ​Loyalty just reinforces the charmed condition inflicted by Confidant and makes the mortal suffer the enthralled condition.

                                          This is how I play Majesty (figure I will go full-on descriptive as a fun mental exercise, give room people to disagree / agree / add)...

                                          Awe: the biggest boon Awe gives a person is a sense that they're almost untouchable, unapproachable. They're awesome, attention-grabbing, and can dominate a conversation without any effort - people under its sway become dumb, nodding sheep. They have trouble looking away. They're just so engrossing and attractive (perhaps not physically). Maybe it's just the way they say things and put things into perspective, y'know? It's not automatically recognisable as supernatural to anybody under its sway, either; it's like meeting a person with a bucket-load of likeability, charisma, and raw presence. If you've ever met someone and thought 'shit, that person can be whatever they want, they're so funny, cool, what-have-you, they're amazing, they can say anything and people still love them no matter what,' and you hope nothing but the best for them yourself, that's the sort of vibe Awe gives people. It leaves a major, positive impression, too - and it doesn't fuck with a person's memory. After that vamp's left, becoming a memory, people will still remember that amazing person and the incredible impression they made on them and their friends. Imagine a person that shows up and conversation centres on them. They leave. People may still talk about how amazing that person was. Imagine especially weak-willed people just staring like idiots. Imagine someone who's incredibly crude and can get away with it due to being likeable and popular. They might be shocking! But for anybody listening, it's just funny and unexpected. Wow! That guy waved a gun around! Only that guy could possibly get away with something so shocking! Who cares if he's poorly dressed for this function? He's so interesting! It's just a quirk and nobody minds! He can pull it off!

                                          If you use Awe alone, going up to a stranger, whispering "hey, baby, mind if we go to a hotel room and fuck," instead of freaking the poor girl out as per normal (being hauled away by beefy security guards or male friends like a creepy pest), she'd probably be flabbergasted and amazed by such a daring proposition - even if it came from an ugly, creepy weirdo with Presence 1 statistically. People listening in would be slack-jawed by your brazen confidence and envious of the perfect delivery. But, importantly, it all depends on the character's personality and disposition beforehand - perhaps they're unattached, liberal about sex, not overly fussed by looks, naturally submissive, weak-willed, so they just end up nodding dumbly, and following the vampire like a silly little lamb. Maybe they're strong-willed, attached, deeply in love with that person, and deeply loyal to that person - maybe they're simply not into ugly guys - you're not going to convince that person with Awe alone, although they will excuse and forgive any trespass all too easily. ("Sorry, um," she blushes, absolutely shocked by the brazen, crude offer, "but I can't. Sorry. I have a boyfriend." She looks away guiltily - but only for a moment. She seems uncomfortable. Yet she's glued to the spot. -- "I am flattered, but you're not my type. Sorry.") Mind, the vampire also has an advantage when it comes to persuading people with raw animal magnetism, and people are frequently talked into doing things they later regret.

                                          The important stats to consider when taking Awe into account, IMO, are the vampire's Majesty, compared to the person's Wits + Composure. Another effect Awe has is grabbing people's attention and making them the centre of any onlooker's universe. If a person wants to notice anything other than the vampire (or ghoul for that matter) using Awe, they need to succeed at a Wits + Composure roll - the vamp's Majesty dots. In the narrative, I'd play this out as those with high Wits + Composure able to act with a semblance of being normal and composed. For those with especially low Wits + Composure, however - say a vamp's Majesty dots penalty forces a person to roll a chance die if they want to notice anything else - those people would basically be staring at the vampire like an awe-struck, slack-jawed zombie; the type of people who would turn into jabbering morons if they met a celebrity or model. It doesn't usurp a character's free-will, though. They're aware of what's happening. Their memories still work. They can get flustered, embarrassed, and overwhelmed as easily as if in their right minds.

                                          Confidant: Confidant causes the Charmed condition. It makes the vamp very trustworthy to the "victim". It's like meeting someone and clicking with them like it's refreshingly natural. They're less likely to be able to see through a vampire's trickery or deception; you're ridiculously likeable, charming, and most of all you have that person's full trust. However, it has some weaknesses: people aren't going to watch the vampire harm them or anybody they love with a dopey, stupid smile on their face. Harming them or anybody close to them automatically 'breaks the trust'. The other weakness is that the condition is not permanent; there's a risk of making the victim feel used and that their trust has been abused - which can be the case. I imagine most people have met someone who always asks 'can I borrow twenty dollars?' or something similar. I have a longtime friend who literally gave thousands of dollars to some random guy he met, considering him a good friend, only for it to dawn on him one day the dude was full of crap - and that he was spending that money on god knows what. Obviously, that person was cut out of my friend's life once realisation dawned. You can be very foolish and trusting when it comes to helping people out when you consider them a friend (just a fact of life, nothing supernatural about it); however, the penny can always drop at any moment. People re-evaluate relationships they have all the time. I personally have some girl I know who texts me from time to time like clockwork with 'hey, how are you' 'good, how are you?' 'i am good' 'what are you up to?' 'nothing much. hey i was wondering if I could borrow 20 dollars? i promise to pay you back on my next pay' 'sorry, i have to pay some bills and don't have the money' 'oh that's okay' and she proceeds to send texts to other people I know asking for money until someone does. She owes me $40.00 from a year ago. One of my other friends is owed $200.00. It's insane. But nobody's going to accuse her of being a vampire or using magic powers. We're all just gullible morons who got sucked in by someone (who is still a friend, honestly - just wouldn't lend her any more money) we trusted asking for money.

                                          ANYWAY! Importantly, for it to feel natural, I wouldn't have the NPC feel cheated right after the Charmed condition is resolved (assuming the character has any reason to feel cheated to begin with). For example, if a vamp uses Confidant to talk a person into giving them $1,000, then the next night or a week later they pay that person back, they're just as likely to think without being Charmed - 'hey, what a trustworthy friend, they paid me back'. They're only going to feel cheated if they're lending all this money to some random guy who gives them a reason to feel used and cheated (like, continuing to blow them off, always 'borrowing money', and never paying them back).

                                          In your example, the girl could feel cheated by having sex with the vampire. She could feel used. She could feel guilty. She could totally be fine with it and treat it like a one-night stand. Maybe she's disappointed in the sex. It depends on some context: is she the type to expect a relationship with someone she has sex with, is she the type who isn't interested in a relationship currently, what is the vamp telling her to get her into bed, does she have a significant other that she's cheating on... it can get very complicated very quickly. The fact the Charmed condition has resolved isn't going to dilute the memory that she felt absolutely captivated by the vamp, and she isn't going to automatically decide that feeling was false, and may very well seek the vamp out again to recapture that fun night. Maybe meeting the vamp again without any supernatural help will disappoint her and she'll be left feeling like it was a mistake and a fleeting thing. Speaking from personal experience, sometimes you meet someone and your first impression is so impressive that you think you could fall in love with that person, and then the next time you meet you just don't connect in the same way you did that first time -- so it fizzles out pretty quickly and you're sort of left scratching your head in confusion about what went wrong. Have you ever got a call from a girl who was clearly falling in love with you suddenly freaking out after one 'meh' experience together? That's how I imagine it. They might call the vampire, or want to talk (assuming they see what they assumed as a romance fizzling out) wanting some reassurances and wondering why things suddenly feel different or why the vampire is 'being distant'.

                                          A vampire isn't going to ignite these sorts of strong feelings in people without some repercussions. People's memories are intact. They will want to know where the crap they stand with the object of their affection. If they suddenly fall out of love, people don't just go 'oh, hey, i don't love that person anymore... oh well, time to continue on with life like nothing ever happened...' In fact, they'll feel hurt and confused, usually. They're going to try to recapture that old feeling, want to talk things over with the vamp, talk about their concerns (drifting apart, not connecting). They're as likely to be rational and try to 'work things out' with the vampire as they are to be irrational and blame the vampire for making them feel like crap.

                                          Green Eyes: The first thing to realise about Green Eyes is that it's potentially more potent than a full Blood Bond. That's how emotionally potent Green Eyes can get. If Green Eyes is used on somebody with the Charmed condition, it's not quite as strong as a full Blood Bond (they won't betray their domitor, that would be a Breaking Point, which causes the Charmed condition to resolve automatically). However, using Green Eyes on someone with the Enthralled condition (even a fully bonded ghoul) can cause that ghoul to betray their domitor with an obsessive need - because they'll do anything for the vampire with the only exception being suicide. Green Eyes is insane, obsessive, and all-encompassing. IMO, it's a noticeably unhealthy desire (Renfield style) to please the vampire, especially when it comes to the Enthralled version - potent enough to make the victim disregard their morals almost completely, turning them into something more base, making the only important things be their own life and their vampire's life. That's it. The flavour of this obsession is up to the vampire; fear, depression, happiness, anger, jealousy, love. It's also addictive in a flavour-sense (the feeling, the vampire's presence). Even when the effect resolves itself, the memory should make a lasting impression on the victim - and depending on what they did, they could feel absolutely disgusted in themselves, betrayed, or suddenly lonely, desperately wanting to reclaim that lost feeling once again.

                                          In your example, I could definitely see that girl seeking out more from that vampire - even if she's totally confused - just out of the addictive nature of Green Eyes, desiring to reach that emotional high once again - never has she felt so in love, so sexually aroused, certainly she'd want to feel that way again, and rely on the person who made her feel that way originally to make her feel that way again.

                                          I am not a huge fan of the Doors system, but I would say that it definitely affects her impression of the character in a narrative sense (having a baring on future meetings) and that should reflect via the system. Was the experience negative? Then her Impression should drop. Was the experience positive and does she want a repeat? Then her Impression should increase.

                                          Loyalty: It gives the Enthralled condition. It makes it morally reprehensible to go against the vampire (literally, you have to spend a WP to even consider making a move against or in opposition of the vampire, then you must succeed at a single die roll or you 'chicken out'). It's more potent than a full Blood Bond IMO, and a victim is basically turned into a Renfield-type.

                                          Idol: I remember reading a few posts in the past about Idol being useless against Elders. Nonetheless, it's potent against crowds. Also, Ghouls, Neonates, ordinary mortals - they're almost powerless against anyone using Idol (usually being stuffed with a chance die to make any one action against the vampire, assuming they weren't Enthralled beforehand - otherwise they're just putty in the vampire's hands due to being unable to spend WP against the vampire now, effectively making any thoughts of betrayal an impossibility - really, if you're using it on an Elder with a high BP, your aim is to actually force them into a state of being Enthralled and then hit them with Idol, not rely on Idol's natural effect). Most importantly, however, there's a sense of self-preservation with every other power in Majesty's repertoire - except this one. Everybody has a 'damage limit' in every other Majesty power, but with Idol there is no limit - you can literally tell a person to sit there and beat them to death - and they'll do it with a smile. At least, that's my reading of Idol and narratively in my view why it's so frightening.

                                                      I take it the pick-up line is also his Lash Out attempt using the Seductive Beast. He's most likely to win the Contest, since he's got a decent dice pool here, so she'll probably gain the Wanton Condition, and potentially a +2 on mundane Persuasion attempts if she chooses Fight rather than Flight. And even if he loses, there's no real risk for him.

                                                      Next, Confidant is going to give her the Charmed Condition (assuming he wins the Contested roll, which is a pretty reasonable assumption given his stats). That doesn't change the Impression, but it does make her trust him like a close friend, and she'll be willing to do small favors for him without him needing to use Social Maneuvering.

                                                      Now, I assume based on your explanation that the seduction roll is a mundane Social action, so that is going to use the Social Maneuvering system. I also wouldn't count what he's asking as the kind of small favor Confidant would make her willing to do without a roll. I also assume he's Forcing Doors, since he's clearly looking for immediate results, which is just not gonna happen at a Neutral Impression. With 5 successes (not unreasonable given his high base stats and Awe adding his Majesty dots to mundane Presence rolls), he's probably able to open all of her Doors.
