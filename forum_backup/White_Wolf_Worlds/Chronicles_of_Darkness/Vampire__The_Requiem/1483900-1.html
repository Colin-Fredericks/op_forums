    I think the other thing to look at is not comparative, but in absolute terms: just as starting-character Mages /feel/ wizardly, do starting-character vampires /feel/ like playing a scary cool vampire?

    My test for this is, can a starting character Vampire feel like it's the monster in a horror movie. And I think they definitely can. Even before we get to Disciplines, a starting vampire is tougher, faster, and stronger (Physical Intensity), can see you in the dark (Kindred Senses), can heal from all but the most grievous wounds (Healing), and can make you do things that you wouldn't otherwise (Predatory Aura).

    Once you get to Disciplines, the horror-monster vibes escalate. They know about the holy water in your flask before you even reach for it - maybe the rats in your walls whispered your secrets to them. They disappear into a crowd, or sink into the earth. Your mind is theirs. Your /want/ to be theirs. Now, it can sometimes take some clever GMing to allow these weaker powers to have their moments to shine, but give a player the opportunity to use a one-dot power to great effect and vampires don't feel disappointingly weak at all.

                    While I think comparing the power levels of the various games in CofD is an exercise in futility, I do think a lot of people confuse simplicity for lack of power when it comes to vampires. As previously mentioned, every Discipline does exactly what it is supposed to straight from the first dot, and not in any half-measures. Beyond the first dot, Disciplines are all straightforward, uncomplicated, and focused, even if the focus of, say, Animalism is not actually what is expected. And because each Discipline is relatively cheap and very good at what it does, players are constantly using them to solve problems, at least ime. Something about a hammer and everything looking like nails.

                    And, actually, I want to elaborate more on their powers being cheap, because it comes in two flavors. First, the first dot of basically every Discipline is free, or conditionally free. Vampires can just do that whenever they like, at no cost. Some Disciplines even do not have a Vitae cost until the third or fourth dot! Second, blood is cheap, or at least, it can be, and only the fifth dot of any given Discipline is going to add a Willpower cost [and when they do, the benefits are usually worth it]. This gives vampires lots of leeway to just use their powers which only mages, beasts, demons, and some deviants can match. Except, by having such a focused and powerful list of abilities, the vampire is paradoxically less likely to have a power that will not solve or bypass the story's current problem entirely.

                    Basically, the vampire toolkit is built on exerting control, and every Discipline is among the best powers in the various games at exacting its flavor of control, imo. And if your players feel like they are missing out on the potential versatility of other games' powers, well, that is what Devotions are for [or the blood bond/ghouling if they like their vampires bastardly].

                        Originally posted by Newb95 View Post
                        Another thing that penalizes vampires is their extremely slow progression, unlike the other splats, their triggers for beats are extremely self destructive in nature (frenzies and degenerations roll) so most players will tend to avoid them unlike werewolves for example that get a ton of beats from all the various positive conditions they can get.
                        This is another thing about points of comparison.

                        For example, most splats don't get free Power Stat increases without XP over time, and they can't eat their own kind for more XP either.

                        Sure, most people sitting down and playing VtR, or playing a mixed CofD chronicle, aren't going to cover a long enough period of time for that to matter for PCs, but in the setting at large vampires can get power just by surviving long enough. VtR is also far less "kill all Diablerists on sight," than VtM is, and makes detecting them in the first place harder (no more needing to buy special things to hide it from Auspex 2, and Auspex is now a Clan signature Discipline instead of a common one anyway).

                        So yeah, since the PCs are unlikely to get the chance to spend 100 years being Dialberie addicts before "seeing the light" and switching to the LeS or OD to cure them of their addiction (sincerely or not it doesn't really matter) to easily net ~75 XP (including costs to keep Humanity up) more than most splats would get without having to go through a Beat loop (plus if they get the Addicted condition for Diablerie they also get a Beat loop to exploit out of it as well), it can feel "weak." But the setting itself has plenty of vampires that took a short cut to impressive supernatural power (at least by the standards of immortal creatures).

                        Originally posted by Gwyn chan 'r Gwyll View Post
                        I think the other thing to look at is not comparative, but in absolute terms: just as starting-character Mages /feel/ wizardly, do starting-character vampires /feel/ like playing a scary cool vampire?
                        I think this test is really hard because... who's idea of what that means? A 0 XP neonate vampire isn't going to be David from the Lost Boys, they're going to be Michael. Or at least one of the lackey vampires that's gotten a bit more skill under their belts without being as powerful as David. You could do the same with Near Dark, etc.

                        Going back farther Lord Ruthven, Carmilla, and Varney are easily done as 0 XP vampires, while Count Dracula is way above what you're doing at 0 XP.

                        One of the on-going problems with both Vampire lines, is that what a "base-line" vampire should be capable of is highly variable; but vampires are frequently shown as taking time to come into their full power. If people come into things expecting their "cool scary vampire" to only have been a vampire for a few years, it's a much lower bar than a few decades (part of the WoD's problem in 1e through V20 is that very assumption that starting level vampires have had a fairly significant time as fledglings, where VtR and V5 orient things such that 0XP starting characters are expecting to be much younger vampires so their power level is more in-line with expectations).

                            Since that vampire can use 10 vitae per turn, that implies a Blood Potency of 10. Given that the constrain posted was maxed stats and assuming we are trying to maximize damage, I think the resulting calculation would be:

                            10 (Strength) + 10 (Skill) + 5 (passive bonus from Vigor) + 9 (maximum amount of Physical Intensity - 1 BP already used to activate Vigor as a damage bonus) + 3 Willpower = 37

                            Which means 37 hit dice, where each hit does [weapon damage] + 5 (Vigor's active bonus). Which means that if the maxed vampire is wiling to use 13% of their max blood pool in a single attack (so theoretically they can do this at most 7 times before requiring some downtime to refill it), they can bring forth one an incredible amount of force on the fly. But this is provided that they do not need to divert resources in things like chase after their target, interrupt enemy actions via Celerity or resist a deadly barrage via Protean. But still, its very impressive amount of consistent power, especially considering that things like targeted attacks, fighting styles and Devotions are yet to be accounted for.
