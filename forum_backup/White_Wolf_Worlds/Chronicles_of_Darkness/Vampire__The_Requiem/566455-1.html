            There are revenants, in first edition ghosts with a numina to possess their own bodies, in second edition it would probably be a manifestation effect.

            Possessing someone else's corpse is possible with intruder, again a numina in first edition but probably a manifestation in second edition.

            There are also bhuta, ghosts who possess other people killing them, and only able to replenish their essence through vampirism and cannibalism.

            And to answer your question about the first change, minor templates may or may not be trumped. So it's up to the storyteller if say a wolf-blood possessed by a demon or given vampire blood would stay a wolf-blood or become one of the possessed or a ghoul. Major templates always trump minor templates. So a ghoul who visits a watchtower or gets chosen by Luna to become a werewolf will be a former ghoul.

            In second edition, they're allowing some templates to leave marks though. A wolf-blooded turned into another supernatural will retain their tell but lose any wolf-blooded merits they might have had for example.

                                        Originally posted by Krzyzstof View Post

                                        The Sightless Mother from Night Horrors: Immortal Sinners is a canonical example of a vampire "evolving" into another kind of powerful being (if you can call it "evolving").
