    Libertié • Égalitié • Vitae - Actual Play

    03-23-2015, 07:39 AM
    A city on the edge of chaos, Paris is a capital of squalor and suffering as the 18th Century draws to a close. While for hundreds of years the regular procession of plagues kept the peasant population in check there has been no great sickness in generations, and the numbers of the underclass swells in the city. Now Paris is home to almost 650,000 people, and only a bare ten thousand are of the first and second estates, the clergy and the nobility. The rest call the third their home and make up the peasantry. As the population has swollen the balance of production and consumption has become ever more unstable and dangerous, the poor roads and tracks making it impossible to import grain to the city at anything other than extortionate prices, and so the populace relies totally on the agriculture of the city surrounds themselves.

    Only the regulation of bread prices keeps the city from starving and even then, when a bad harvest occurs, the peasantry riots nearly tearing the city apart, lynching bakers, stealing bread and burning down buildings that have stood for half a millennium. The clergy and nobility pay no taxes and take all that they want, and are impeachable in the courts of the land. The young king and queen, Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette, luxuriate in the palace of Versailles far from the heavy masses, totally isolated from the pain and suffering of the lower classes. King Louis, though most incompetent, refuses to appoint important ministerial figures claiming his right to rule the country directly and believing that it is his duty to do so.

    His father, and his father's father, wasted the riches of France on a series of petty wars and disputes, the lands and monies of France slowly eaten away. The rich have gotten richer, and the poor poorer as they are squeezed more and more to pay for the excesses of the first and second estates. The people call for a three house parliament to represent the common man, but such pleas are ignored. France teeters on bankruptcy, a matter which is not helped by Marie Antoinette's lavish spending, and the vast sums Louis sends to aid the American Wars of Independence.

    It is June 1787, and the hungry gap is beginning, when the harvests are yet to be brought in but the grain stores are running low. Bread is scarce and the city boils and fumes in the heat and the poverty, a powder keg that somehow never explodes.
    As above, so below.

    The three estates of the dead struggle against each other just as the kine do. The nobility of the night and the followers of Longinus claim the first and second estates, with the rest relegated to the third. Those kindred who are not embraced into noble lines, or who cannot worm their way into the church, are left to rot, forced to pay the blood taxes of the nobles and given nothing in return. A bare handful of bloodlines hold all the power and the rest scramble to just survive.

    As the kine population bloats as do the leeches that feed from them, hundreds of the dead hanging like parasites from the city. With so many deaths through crime, starvation and a thousand other evils, the peasantry of the city are a never-ending buffet for the kindred whose predations are always overlooked. Yet fury and resentment ferments amongst the downtrodden masses of the dead, just as they do amongst the living. The kindred might have blood aplenty but they lack safety in a city which burns on a yearly basis, and which is ripped apart by riots almost monthly. They look at the sturdy chambers and manors of their betters and then plot amongst themselves.

    Just as the pamphleteers of the mortal world fuel the ever-encroaching revolution, so do the radicals of the dead urge their fellows to action. The name Emmanuel Baptiste Carth is upon the lips of the many, and soon they will be bound together into a great movement whose actions can only result in blood and fire.

    I’ve been running World of Darkness for almost a decade, and of all the roleplaying games I’ve played and run (there have been many) it’s the one I return to again and again. When Blood and Smoke (now Requiem 2nd Edition) came out I desperately wanted to run something using it. I came up with the concept for a game charting the early days of the Carthians from their radical roots in Paris during the last part of the 18th Century through the bloody revolution that gripped that city and, perhaps, concealed a similarly bloody revolution amongst the dead. I ran the game and it was a great success but unfortunately it was cut short – I had to move cities for my work and my gaming group was left behind.

    Well, now I have a new group and I think it’s time to celebrate a rebooting of this setting with an Actual Play. I'll be posting up some more introductory information shortly, and then the first three sessions of play after that. Once that's done new sessions of play will go up on Wednesdays most weeks.

      Themes

      I’m going to take a leaf out of DaveB’s Actual Play threads here (which are just balls-out amazing) and be upfront about the themes I’m teasing out of Requiem for the purposes of this game.

      Everyone is Alone Together

      I sometimes joke that Requiem should really be called High-Functioning Psychopaths the RPG and though that’s a little overegged as a concept, it’s not that far off the mark. Every kindred has a beast inside them, this monster that wants to just play with the world. It used to be out and out bestial in first edition, but since Blood and Smoke came along it’s found a little more nuance. The bestial triad makes me sit up and pay attention because it’s a great framework for what kindred are like, really like, at the core of themselves. They’re equal parts pack animal, solitary predator and fucked up human being. I don’t want to forget that.

      The thing is that vampires define themselves by their connections with others, but those connections are always hollow – they might find scraps of loyalty and brotherhood amongst themselves but in the end fraternity is a lie amongst the dead. You might fool yourself into thinking you’re still a human being for a little longer than the others but you’re just not, and that’s… well, not OK, but that’s a thing. You can’t fight your way out of that. Vampires just don’t have the ability to trust anyone else anymore, especially other vampires, and so it’s all about finding the ways to interact socially without just ripped each other’s faces off. That’s why kindred form such complex organisations and structure. Because you’re right not to trust each other.

      Predator or Prey

      Vampires are predators. That’s just a fact that bleeds through every part of the book when you read Requiem, and which plays off nearly half the mechanics in it. Worse, when your food supply is something that everyone has, you can’t even switch off that instinct with anyone. Whenever you meet another vampire you’re categorising them into something I want to eat or something that might eat me (or both). It’s not as blunt as in first edition (where predatory aura was a like a sledgehammer to social situations), but it’s omnipresent. Masques and Dirges are about working out your place in the pecking order, your predatory niche. Status is about who gets to eat who. Blood Potency is about who’s got the bigger teeth.
      Which is funny, in a sort of awful way, when you start thinking about the Carthians. Because the radicals in the city of Paris genuinely believe in Égalitié, in making society equal and fairer, to stop there being people at the top and people at the bottom, monsters at the top and monsters at the bottom. The problem is that you can strip away all the pomp and circumstance, all the centuries of rotting power, but you won’t ever get that equality because every single part of your being makes you divide people into above me and below me. That’s what lashing out is about proving – that you’re the one doing the eating.

      Die Free

      Kindred need those structures, those complex organisations and associations because otherwise their basic social natures will break out and those natures ain’t pretty. But that doesn’t mean any of the dead actually like those structures, and those kindred who do badly out of them are going to like them least of all. That’s what the radicals really want of course, to stop being on the shitty end of the current system. The problem is, instead of trying to beat the system and get on top they think they can just get rid of the system. For reasons I’ve already covered, that’s a bad idea all round, and almost certainly won’t work.

      The kindred of Paris who are scrabbling to form this new movement want Liberté, they want freedom from the structures and systems in place. The problem is none of them can agree on what should replace it and all they can agree on is tearing what’s there at the moment down. That’s a recipe for disaster and though the players might realise it the characters are too trapped and desperate to think ahead to what the domain will look like when (or if) they get what they want. They want to be free, but free in vampires is just the worst idea ever. So they’ll forge their own chains as they go along, just like real people trying to have a revolution do. You ever thought about what the reign of terror would look like with a dead man at the helm?

      The kindred at the bottom of the pile really are trapped though, in a way few vampires ever are. The system of tenancy, barony and corvée means that their requiems are very hard indeed, and that they are constantly at the whim of others. Breaking out of those traps would be freedom of a sort, regardless of the consequences.

      Blood Bonds and Blood Oaths

      Because when it comes down to it, really when you strip it all away, it’s all about blood. Thick, rich dark ruddy blood, pooling in your mouth and your dead veins. You’re bound by your blood, not just through the vinculum but also through family, your kinship with your lineage and your clan pulling you in lots of different directions.at once. That’s before you pledge your allegiance to something or someone else, giving your blood oath of service (or at least loyalty). We know that loyalty is hollow so all that’s left standing is the blood.

      But there are too many ties for that to be easy. You have allegiance to your clan, your family, your estate, your faction, your coterie, your liege… to everyone and everything with a stake in your requiem. You can’t make all of those people, all of those organisation, your priority, and you sure as hell can’t please them all. So you have to make choice about which way you go, who you stay loyal to and who you betray. You also need to decide if those decision are based on your own survival, that blood that drives you, or if you try to cleave to something greater, some noble cause.

          The Player Characters

          Just a brief blurb for everyone, as I want to reveal their character through play rather than just giving you the details up front. Sorry about that. Characters are built on base dots + 10xp, I will append proper sheets when I remember.

          Agathe Saint-Simon Moreau

          An illegitimate daughter of the youngest son of a minor noble, Agathe was barely noble in the first place and her fall from grace has done her position few favours. Though given some education as a child she soon rebelled even on those minor restrictions; she adopted masculine dress even though she doesn’t pretend to be male, rebelling even against the strictures of her gender. When she was embraced she hunted down and killed her mortal father and his legitimate family as an expression of fury against him, and the resultant chaos forced her underground, where she has stayed since. Recently she has begun working as a tough in a brothel, dealing with customers who get too rough with the girls (who double as her herd). Nosferatu of the sprawling Moreau brood, she favours direct action over cunning plots when circumstances allow. She currently makes her haven in Montparnasse and her regent is Georgine Rome.

          André LaPointe

          Embraced by the since executed Daeva noble Christophe LaPointe, Andre was inducted into the Second Estate. He made that transition relatively smoothly, despite being of the tender age of eleven at embrace, since he was of mortal noble stock and took to command well. When Christophe was condemned to final death for treason against Le Requin (the Prince), however, he was exiled to the rougher areas of the city and forced to accept Comtesse Giselle Rome as his regent, being granted the right to remain in Fauborg Saint-Germain. He’s a member of the Children’s Crusade and a deadly kindred duellist, but he still retains many of his noble connections giving him a big social advantage. He’s also inherited his sire’s bloodline which gives him the ability to create vinculums in others not to himself, but to ideals or duties.

          Benoit Moreau

          Found dead in an alley by a ghoul of the Moreau brood, Benoit was taken to the bloodline’s holdings before being subjected to the post-mortem embrace by Phillipe Moreau. Why the patriarch decided to do this, is unclear, and Benoit is a little fuzzy on the details from just before and just after his death. Quick-witted and extremely nimble, he ran with the gangs of street children before he became kindred, and keeps in contact with them now. A thief beyond anything else he’s got a good mix of skills and is something of a soft touch in a coterie of heavy hitters. He lives in Montparnasse with his extended family, and though his liege lord is technically Georgine Rome, but he takes most of his instruction from his sire. He is also a token member of the Children’s Crusade, which is how he first met André, but he’s never put must investment in the covenant.

          Gabriel LeClaire

          Ex-musketeer (and still bitter about their disbandment), Gabriel LeClaire is a rough and ready kindred mercenary from the Le Garde du Sang bloodline (a group of ex-Invictus kindred who feel they were betrayed by their former covenant). He’s honourable and trustworthy, but more because he stakes his reputation on it than anything else, and he’s a little naïve about the way in which the world works. More wearied soldier than just a common thug, he’s physically capable but with a solid physical background including horse riding and survival skills. He’s also socially quite adept, but sometimes a little tactless. Daeva and a member of the Third Estate only; he doesn’t officially have a liege lord, but instead is employed on a temporary basis by Comte Courtmanche making his haven in the Quartier Latin.

          Remy Fergris

          Something of an outsider, Remy is of French extraction but spent most of his mortal life in Algiers in one of the recently established French colonies. A military engineer by trade, he’s a good all-rounder with a broad range of physical and mental skills, and even a dabbling of social capability too. He’s a member of the Gangrel Fergris bloodline which means his vitae hardens into iron when spilt, a property that gives him a long of problem solving flexibility and nasty tricks in a violent situation. He’s a member of L’Academie du Serpent and so will potentially get access to coils (though he’s low down the ladder right now and hasn’t picked up expertise in any of the Mysteries). He also doesn’t have a formal liege but is theoretically under the dominion of Comte Courtmanche, making his haven in the Quartier Latin.

              I’m following DaveB’s format to the letter, so expect these sections to be out of character, behind the curtain asides. Unfortunately the player of Agathe was sick and couldn’t make the first two sessions, but she’ll appear later on.

              1.1 “City of Lights”

              June, 1787

              André Lapointe is waiting in the parlour of Comtesse Giselle Rome, his liege lord and baron, who has requested his presence that evening on a matter of urgency. With little choice but to present himself he’s waiting nervously. Giselle opens with some flattery and small talk, praising him for his loyalty and restrain, before launching into the meat of the matter: a man has been murdered in Les Invalides and it looks like it might be a masquerade breach. She makes a few carefully-judged insult/compliments about his (previous) noble heritage that has now gone to wrack and ruin, and sends him on his way with the promise that this task would pay off his Corvée for the forthcoming year.

              In the domain of Paris all common kindred are forced to pay a yearly “rent” to their baron as payment for living on their territory. It was written in pretty much as a blatant way of hooking PCs into situations they don’t really want to be a part of.

              Meanwhile in Montparnasse, Benoit Moreau is being entertained by his sire Phillipe Moreau who is the Patriarch of his brood, and who is trying to put the thief at his ease with his usual rough charm. He appeals to familial loyalty and duty and his young protégé is more than happy to acquiesce to his request. Put simply, in order to curry favour with the nobility, Phillipe would like Benoit to head over to the tannery in Les Invalides and see if he can help investigate this murder. He’s told to work with the kindred who arrive there.

              The setup is a little blunt at this point, but I didn’t want to waste any time. We’d already established some connections between the characters beforehand so the coterie can naturally come together – Benoit and Remy have run into each other before through Remy’s touchstone (a young orphan street girl) who Benoit knows through his connections with the group of street kids that live in the slums.

              Gabriel and Remy are currently in conversation with Comte Courtmanche, who tells them that to keep the peace and ensure they are not disturbed in the Quartier Latin it would be good if Gabriel and Remy could help look into a potential masquerade breach for him. “After all,” he tells them, “it’s best if we can stay on everyone’s good side, and a threat to the masquerade is a threat to us all”. Remy and the Comte have a short conversation about who will take over his research in L’Academie du Serpent whilst he’s investigating the murder, and he passes off some of his work to a young kindred called Jennifer.

              André arrives first, running through the crowded city (still bustling with people just after dusk) with wild abandon that fits his apparent 11 years of age. He stops a few dozen yards down the street from the tannery which he can see piercing the moonlit sky in the distance, judging the situation on the street. There are a lot of mortals milling around here – people selling flowers, someone peddling black ribbons in a hopeful if slightly indelicate way, and a large group of people huddling together just outside the tannery whispering amongst themselves. He judges the crowd and guesses that the mortal authorities, the marshals who keep order in the city, have not yet arrived at the scene. It seems the call to action was swift indeed.

              Remy arrives next as he’s taken the shortcut of climbing up to the roofs and running across the district well above the ground. He reaches the edge of the roof and looks down at the street, noticing André standing there below. There’s a momentary pause and then he clocks the little noble boy, dressed in a slightly shabby blue suit, as kindred; something in the stance, in the way he holds himself and the lack of movement in his chest and face gives André away as not being amongst the living. André looks up and notices Remy and the same thing happens in reverse – as ex-soldier makes his way down back to street level André moves towards him so they can meet up and talk.

              It’s not long before Gabriel and Benoit arrive, not having taken quite such a hectic pace to reach their goal. They know the other two and soon the coterie-to-be is in conversation about their next move, having shared their instructions from their various authority figures.

              This was a great little scene of introductory roleplay that I can’t do justice to – the dynamic of the group really clicked almost immediately. André takes a position of authority, even though the two adult-appearing kindred might take their age difference as defining the mechanic. The two military men stick together, following André’s lead, whilst Benoit soon takes his own direction.

              Benoit disappears around the back of the buildings looking for alternative access to the tannery, trusting to his slight frame and practiced stealth to hide him from view. The other three make their way through the crowd (avoiding getting too close to the brazier that a small group are warming their hands on) and head to the door of the tannery itself, André taking the lead.

              A woman leans in the doorway, clearly barring entry to the tannery itself. She’s about thirty and sort of pretty in a worn way, clearly the recipient of a hard life but still trying to make an effort: sloppily applied makeup marks her face, but has been smeared by tear streaks on her face. As the group approach she wearily holds up a hand: “Please young master, madame and monsieur cannot be intruded upon until the marshals arrive.”

              André barely misses a bit and tells her with perfect confidence that they are in fact from the marshals office, and have been hired as thief-takers in order to investigate the murder. She looks surprised but ushers them inside, glad things are finally happening. André begins to talk to her whilst Remy and Gabriel climb the stairs to find the body itself, passing a rather less attractive, older woman on the landing as they go, and heading into the back bedroom. André begins to talk to the woman about what has happened and has found that the tanner is Henry Capet, whilst she is his mistress Lucille. Apparently Henri’s death was a double blow to Henri’s wife Claire, as she discovered not only that he had died, but had been unfaithful. “I fear I may still be in shock,” Lucille confesses to André, “for I saw the whole thing and I cannot tell you what horror it was.”

              A social challenge began at this point as André begins to pump Lucille for information – the fruits of his labour will be revealed later on.

              Meanwhile, Benoit has made his way to the rear of the tannery, scaled the fence, made his way across the tanner’s yard and up the rear of the tanner’s house, finding the bedroom where the body has been placed on the bed with ease. He crouches over the bed and examine the dead made: corpulently fat, pale almost waxen flesh where he has been exsanguinated, and with evidence of being badly beaten as well. In one place white bone pushes through his arm exposing it to the air. It is in this pose that Remy and Gabriel find him as they make their way through in a more conventional fashion.

              Remy steps over to the corpse and begins to examine him, relying on his experience with field surgery from when he was in the army, and notes that the corpse almost certainly died from trauma, rather than blood loss. He notes the strikes and breaks on the body don’t match a blunt implement, but more just unarmed blow of incredible force. Giving the dead man a little more of a general look over, Gabriel notes what looks like dried blood around his mouth, and the two of them arrive at a rough time of death – about three hours previously.

              A knock at the door stops them in their tracks, and Benoit dives under the bed to hide as André, followed by Lucille, enter the room. Lucille is in tears and André leads her mercilessly to the bed, and shows her the brutal wounds on the body – “Can you see why, when faced with horror like this, the mind might rebel?” She cries a little but shakes her head saying “Young master, I promise, she was no figment of the imagination, but as real as you or eye. She was faster than a person should be, young master, and she beat my beloved to death with her bare hands without breaking a sweat.”

              Guessing what André is up to, Remy chimes in, and begins to explain in somewhat gruesome detail the extend of Henri’s injuries. Lucille begins to break down in tears but shakes her head, “I know it is terrible, but I know what I saw.”

              André has started a new social challenge here, convincing Lucille that she has had a breakdown of sanity in the face of Henri’s murder, in an attempt to explain away the overtly supernatural nature of the killer. The challenge started well but faltered badly, and bringing her up here was a last ditch attempt to get her to cave.

              Gabriel steps in and informs Lucille rather a little too baldly that she is clearly hysterical and should be ashamed of herself for denying it, and she slaps him in her hysteria. For a moment the room crystallises and the kindred freeze, waiting to see what will happen… but Gabriel controls his urge to frenzy and apologies to her. Sensing that this is getting away from them, André make a bold decision.

              At this point the players were getting desperate to contain the potential breach of the masquerade: they had no disciplines or other powers that might have convinced her otherwise, and the social challenge was floundering. They began to discuss how they would deal with it if they had to kill her, but they were, understandably, reluctant.

              André puts a hand on Lucille’s arm and then flickers, a strange sort of juddering and in a moment is over by the door. The woman screeches and André, keeping his cool, merely looks at her and say: “What is the matter?” That’s the last straw and in tears she runs past the young vampire sobbing that her mind must be broken and she cannot be trusted.

              The discovery that Celerity didn’t just make you move faster but made that movement unpleasant and overtly supernatural made André’s player cackle with pleasure, and this scheme was hatched without any input by the rest of the group – it was a risky gamble, but I liked it enough to reward the attempt. André did take a detachment check for this vicious little escapade, however, dropping down to Humanity 5.

              Remy and Gabriel begin to scold André for endangering the masquerade further, but relent when he points out that the situation was already getting away from them. He goes on to tell them that Lucille had witnessed a woman – tall, thin and dressed in dirty white rags – burst through the door about half an hour before sunset, screaming incoherently. She had moved to Henri, clearly consumed with hatred, and began to attack him – Lucille had run thinking that Henri would be able to take care of himself and fearing for her own safety, only to be knocked aside as the woman rushed past her. When she checked on Henri he was dead. The woman had screamed only one intelligible phrase: “He will suffer!”

              At this point Benoit pipes up, peering at the corpse again. “Isn’t it a potentially bad sign if he has blood around his mouth, but it’s not come from a wound of his that I can see?” The group quickly reform to look at the body again, and begin to discuss the possible implications – Gabriel tastes a little of the blood on the dead man’s lips and announces it to be vitae, probably from a Ventrue. The implications are unsettling and, fearing him rising as a revenant, begin to plan on how they’re going to sneak the body out of the house.

              We ran out of time at this point… to be continued.

                  1.2 “Beneath Your Feet”

                  June, 1787

                  Without saying a word to the rest of the group, Benoit walks over to a chair, snaps of a leg and, with his knife, carves a very serviceable stake. He passes it to Gabriel in silence.

                  Still under the guide of representatives of the Marshalry, Remy elects to go and speak to the widow and explain that they’ll be taking the body away to let some learned men at L’Universitie examine it, and promises to have her back as soon as possible – a week at the outside. When he later explains this promise to the rest of the group he receives a nod from Benoit and blank looks from André and Gabriel.

                  It is decided that this total lack of feeling on the part of Gabriel is worth a detachment check, which Gabriel passes. André is far too embittered for this to pose a risk to his Humanity.

                  Whilst Remy is speaking to the wife, Benoit slips back out the window and goes to “acquire” a cart they can use to transport the body. He finds a trundle cart in an alley nearby where a sausage-seller has set up his tray and, unnoticed due to his use of Obfuscate, he quickly removes it and brings it round in a loop to outside the tannery, just as if he’d brought it from elsewhere.

                  André uses the opportunity to go downstairs and examine the scene of the crime, to see if he can find any leads. The back room where the murder occurred is in disarray – furniture turned over, part of the plaster on one way torn out in hunks, and smear of blood and hair across the floor in one place. The back door, which leads to the tanner’s yard, is torn asunder, splinters of wood littering the room. André heads outside into the darkness of the yard, and stops for a moment to consider his next step.

                  This was the first time that vampiric senses and the difficulties of operating in darkness arose. André’s player decided the penalties were worse than the inconvenience of finding some light source.

                  Crossing the yard swiftly he heads into the tannery proper and finds just the thing: a mesh-covered lamp which will provide light without risking either ignition of the tanner’s fumes or too much exposure to flame. He takes the lamp and heads out into the street, pushing his blood to call out to those around him, and luring a woman towards him.

                  That was André activating Majesty 1.

                  He bats his eyelashes at the woman and does his best to look like an adorable lost child. “Are you lost, little sir?” she asks him, and he shakes his head. “No Madame, but I sorely need your help. Could you light this lamp for me, I fear I don’t quite know how.” Swayed by the call of his vitae she smiles and takes the lamp from him, lighting it easily (and André takes a sharp step backwards as she does).

                  “Let me help you. Where are you trying to go?” she asks him, and though he tries to discourage her subtly, she follows him into the tanner’s yard carrying the lamp for him. Losing patience, he tells her: “I am on a private errand, I really cannot say. Thank you for your help, but it would be best if you went.”

                  Dismissed her face falls and her body slumps, but she nods dutifully and turns, leaving the yard. “She better not have mucked up the prints” mutters André to himself, the childlike persona falling away in an instant. He begins to use the light from his lamp to search for tracks in the muddy yard to try and determine which direction the assailant came from.

                  Upstairs, Gabriel stands over the corpse, tossing the stake from hand to hand.

                  Having concluded his discussion with the widow, Remy heads into the tanner’s yard to help André investigate the scene and, with his enhanced sense, he quickly spots some faint prints leading over to the back of the tannery where heavy basement doors lie open, a dark cellar opening up at the bottom of a short flight of stairs. For a moment André and Remy stare at each other, willing the other to be the first to go down and investigate. “I should wait here with the lamp,” says André, “so I don’t spoil your night vision.”

                  Instead Remy checks around him for mortal witnesses and, seeing none, pushes his blood to shift and change, his body twisting until he possesses a sense he did not before – the echolocation of a bat. With a short sharp noise he sounds out the cellar below, finding it empty except for shelves and crates and, in the corner hidden behind a large crate, a rough-hewn entrance way to a passage. He concludes instantly that it is an entrance to the necropolis, and almost certainly where the kindred who killed the tanner came from.

                  The pair head back inside and Gabriel and Remy collect the body (by this point wrapped in a sheet) and take it outside to the waiting handcart that Benoit has collected. They discuss in hushed tones where they’re going to take it and decide on L’Academie du Serpent, as there are people there they might be able to trust with watching over it. They make their way through the streets to Le Quartier Latin, and Benoit and André wait whilst the other two deposit the body into the care of Jennifer, the Serpent who took over Remy’s research whilst he was busy.

                  Arriving back into the cool night air and are planning to head back to the Tannery entrance of the Necropolis when, his blood still up from earlier, André suggests a little contest to see who can reach their fastest. Gabriel immediately agrees, his blood up, and the others are goaded into participating and they clamber up to a rooftop to begin.

                  Gabriel has the Competitive condition on him at the moment, so this was perfectly in keeping with his current mental state.

                  André immediately lashes out a foot to try and trip Remy, who nimbly hops over it and begins running at a steady loping base, his experience with parkour giving him an advantage over the rooftops. André, set a little behind after his attempt to cheat goes awry, is also highly skilled in this kind of movement, and sets a fast pace. Gabriel, however, is the real monster here, using his vast physical prowess and disciplines to pull ahead of the other two as he runs at full pelt across the city. Benoit merely waits for the other three to move away before sliding back down to ground level, finding the nearest necropolis entrance and popping inside.

                  “Who are you then?” the burly watchmen on the entrance asks him. Benoit nods his head and says “Benoit, of the Moreau.” The heavyset man sniffs at Benoit then nods. “Fair enough. Off you go.” And steps aside to let the little Nosferatu through.

                  As they leave the Latin Quarter the three kindred on the rooftops begin to seriously gain speed. Les Invalides has broader buildings and now pure speed plays more part, and Gabriel, whose strength far outstrips his opponents, finds himself drawing further and further ahead. Benoit, meanwhile, runs as fast as he can along his much shorter, easy route, and approaches his destination. As a Nosferatu he knows all of the shortcuts.

                  The trio above reach the tanner’s yard and are presented with a choice – to jump down from the roof or to climb down and spend a little more time doing so. Their blood pumping, all of them choose the former, and André and Remy launch themselves to the ground, hitting and rolling with only moderate injury (André snaps his ankle, but simply shakes it out and keeps running). Gabriel, his beast screaming at him to win, pushes his vitae into his muscles and throws himself up and out from the building. He flies through the air and crashes down and through the wooden doors covering the basement, his shin bone slamming up through his skin, dark vitae spraying across his face and chest, and onto the walls. He groans and pulls himself up, vitae knitting the wounds back together as he wrenches the bones into place gain, and he staggers into the basement to claim his victory.

                  As he had just won a contest in which someone reached a breaking point (him, for suffering injury that would hospitalise a mortal), he resolves the Competitive condition. Which is relief for everyone involved.

                  Benoit arrives from inside the necropolis only second later, and André and Remy catch up only a few seconds after that, André fuming at losing.
                  Benoit notes that there was no kindred guarding this entrance to the necropolis – it must be one of those which is simply concealed from view, rather than one which is actively watched. They take stock for a moment and then, armed with André’s gas lamp for illumination, head into the caves.

                  Gabriel takes over tracking the kindred who entered through here, and quickly identifies two sets of tracks laid over each other – one coming out of the necropolis, and one going in. They follow the tracks deep into the network of tunnels, using Benoit’s limited knowledge where Gabriel’s tracking breaks down, picking up the path the best they can as they go. Eventually they reach a place where the tracks diverge- they can either head in the direction she came from, or where she went to. The decision is a matter of debate for a few minutes, but in the end cowardice prevails and they follow the tracks of where she came from – “to learn more about her before we try to apprehend her.”

                  The tracks leads every-deeper into the necropolis, the twists and turns confusing the group as they desperately try to keep on target. They clamber down one sheer slope, and then a few minutes later wade through thigh-deep water (chest-deep for André and Benoit). André notes that the water is tidal, and could rise further yet – almost certainly drawn from the Seine.

                  After exiting the water, Gabriel is working more by guesswork than real evidence of their target’s passage, and brings the group to a halt with the admission that he honestly has no idea where they are headed. They notice directions cut into the wall, but they are all in kindred cant, and none of them are part of the cacophony. Benoit takes the lead and, working on gut instinct and experience of travelling underground, begins to take them into more well-travelled tunnels, rising back towards the surface and away from the confusing labyrinth of the depths.

                  Gabriel and André hear it first – a faint whistling in the distance, the lilt of a tune being performed by someone with no need to use their lungs to breath. Even from this distance they know the tiny clues that speak of one of the dead. André draws his rapier and Gabriel shrugs lose his buckler and sabre, though fighting with them in these corridors would be dangerous, for they are tight and trapping. The other two alerted Remy places his hand on the hilt of his sword, but does not draw, and Benoit simply steps back out of notice of the others, his presence disappearing as he exerts his blood to activate Obfuscate.

                  In the distance in the gloom, they group watch as a man turns the corner into the tunnel where they are stood and, whistling, approaches the party. He seems perfectly at ease. As he approaches they can see his face is oddly shaped on one side, almost like it was wax that had melted a little, and waves of foul stench break over them, filling their senses. He is almost dripping in what seems to be sewerage, his hair slicked down with effluence. He pauses perhaps five yards from the group and raises his hand in greeting.

                  “What’s your purpose down here strangers? You’re intruding on territory not your own.” The group share glances and André says “We’re looking for someone.”

                  “Looking for someone… looking for someone. None of yous smells like kin, so what right you got to be looking for someone?”

                  Gabriel raises his eyebrows and says “We’re travelling with a member…” and turns to find Benoit nowhere in sight. “Where has the little shit gone to now?” Remy mutters to himself.

                  “Oh ho,” the newcomer guffaws, “it seems your guide is not as confident as you seem to be of his company. And who are yous then, who are yous if you ain’t got a Nosferatu to vouch for you?”

                  The group introduce themselves. The newcomer (who can only be a Favager, one of a Nosferatu brood in the city) laughs at the name Lapointe, and shakes his head and tuts as Remy and Gabriel introduce themselves.

                  “Oh dear oh dear. Two foreigners and a stuck up nob, ay? Not healthy for you to be wandering down here in the dark.”

                  Benoit decides enough is enough and steps out of the shadows. “And one of the Moreau.”

                  The Favager doesn’t even blink, but instead nods casually at Benoit. “Well, I guess you got rights to be here then. Now why don’t you tell me who it is you’re looking for?”

                  André describes the woman who the mistress described – long hair, ragged clothes once fine, mad and furious – and the kindred nods impatiently. “The Hag then? You seek a myth not a monster, lad. I can tells you where she makes her den right enough, for the right price.”

                  Furious negotiations follow. In the end they exchange directions for a Laisse Mineur from André to the Favager, and two Laisse Banal from Benoit to him. Happy, he relates the directions in rapid fire and the group attempt to commit them to memory, before muscling past them (leaving greasy trails on their clothes as he does), and wandering off again whistling into the darkness.

                  Armed with directions to the Hag’s “den”, they begin to navigate through the necropolis, heading down once again as they do, the winding tunnels becoming lower and lower, rougher and rougher in the craft, until finally they reach a break where instead of worked stone the tunnels become natural limestone caves. They head into the caves, but more cautiously, all of them feeling a sense of foreboding as they do so.

                  The caves twist and turn and soon they notice little niches cut into the walls, each one housing a tiny little figure or carving. Some of them seem to be animals, other human in shape, but all of them twisted and strange. The stop to examine one: a woman carved delicately but with her lower jaw dropped to her navel, lines of tiny sharp teeth lining her mouth. Remy declares it be carved from bone, probably human. They consider this and, after hours of use, the last of the oil in the lamp is consumed, and the light gutters and dies, plunging them into darkness.

                  For a moment all is still and black, but then, as their eyes adjust, they realise the little fetish is glowing softly in its hollow, a dull reddish light emanating from its surface. Something inside each of them pulls at the little statue, their beasts rising and seeming to obey some obscene call, a desperate yearning that is a drain on all of them… and Gabriel is still worn from his earlier excess, and is tempted by the call of his beast more than would be expected. He suddenly lunges forwards in an attempt to grab the idol and, seeing what is happening, Remy flickers and twists in the air, grabbing at Gabriel’s arm in an attempt to stop him. His fingers close around his ally’s wrist, but Gabriel is far too strong and tears away from Remy. André lashes out trying to trip him again, but his foot cracks back off Gabriel’s shin with no effect. The ex-musketeer reaches out and grabs the statue.

                  The tiny teeth bite through the leather of his glove and his cries out softly as vitae begins to drip from a dozen pinprick wounds, and the fetish seems to suck at him. He feels a little fragment of his power drawn out through his blood and into the statue which pulses, almost as if with a heartbeat until he drops it, clattering to the floor. He stares down at it and all of the gathered kindred feel hungry, looking down as if the fetish contains food, rather than being dry bone with a few flakes of rust brown blood upon it.

                  The spell seems to break all at once, and Remy and Gabriel begin arguing, Remy half-snapping at Gabriel’s foolishness, but at the same time consumed by curiosity over the little figure. Gabriel begins to talk about half-remembered rituals and rites he’d heard of and then a voice, thin and crackling, cuts through their conversation from over his shoulder:

                  “You should not talk about things you know little about. Such wonders are beyond your understanding.”

                  The four of them wheel around, their eyes fixing on a woman who can only be the Hag, who they could have sworn was not there a moment ago but is now in their midst. She is dressed in filthy rags and her features are twisted and ugly, warts erupting from the skin of her face in clusters. Her long greasy hair is white speckled with grey, and she long talon-like nails protrude from her fingers and curl underneath her bare toes.
                  Keeping his composure, Gabriel bows low to her, and introduces himself and the group. The Hag seems less impressed with his courtesy and more interested in their intrusion on her territory, and he apologies profusely for any insult.

                  “At least you’re polite, little one” she hacks, and cackles a little to herself, before extending one of her ugly nails at them and beckoning them to her. “Come on, and I will show you a little hospitality.”

                  The group follow her down the rough tunnels until they exit into a larger chamber, a roundish cave overgrown with moss and lichens, strange fungi bursting in fronds from the walls. At the centre of the room is a low crackling fire and behind it, a sort of altar, just a plain slab of stone with rust-coloured stains laid down in layers upon it. The kindred avoid the fire, but the Hag doesn’t seem to care, wandering past it and beginning to clatter around with piles of old rubbish, before pulling out two clay goblets. “Do you wish for something to drink, younglings?” she croaks at them.

                  Only Remy nods ascent, and the rest look at him strangely, but their attention is soon grabbed by the Hag again, who spits a hunk of thick wet vitae into one of the cups before spitting a few strange vicious words at it. They can see steam rise from it in a plume, and when she clatters back across the floor and thrusts it into Remy’s hands, it is full of rich dark blood. He drinks.

                  Remy at this point, as you might guess, suffers a blood bond to the Hag. His player fully expected this, but felt that Remy would be so fascinated by the magick being demonstrated that he couldn’t resist trying it.

                  “I don’t see many visitors down in these tunnels, young ones.” The Hag spits at them. “I hear the worms scurry around at the edges of my domain, my blood sings to me when they approach, but none are foolish enough to intrude like you have.” She sips at her own goblet, and then looks directly at them for the first time: “I wonder what would fill you with such need as to visit me?”

                  Remy attempts to explain their goal in coming to see her, explaining that a mortal was killed and the authorities in the city above wanted them to investigate. She continually interrupts, telling him to speak plainer again and again, and eventually he is cut off altogether by a hacking laugh. “Pathetic little fools scurrying around, thinking they have power. They are not worms, I like the worms, but insects. Little creeping insects.” She shudders in their vision and is suddenly at a cave wall before skewering a large brown centipede with one of her nails, popping it in her mouth, and crunching it down in a few chews before swallowing. She returns to the fire, without even breaking the flow of her words: “I hate insects. They think their hives are so important. And their creeping stupidity makes the worms come deeper and deeper, until they bother me. I resent that. You should do something about those insects, little ones.”

                  The group looks awkwardly between then, and Gabriel tries to express how as much as they might like that, there are certain realities, and they don’t have the power necessary…

                  “Then get more power!” She hisses, spraying a fine mist of blood over the floor. “Do I have to tell you everything? I have been down here before they were up there, before there was a city up there. They are little insects, and all insects can be eradicated. I would do it myself if I could, but my duty binds me to the cave.”

                  “What duty?” asks Benoit. The Hag coughs another laugh at this, and simple beckons them again.

                  “I will show you. It will do you good to see it. Puts fire in your bones.”

                  She scampers away from the fire, heading to a part of the cave which is drenched in shadow and the group follows. Down a tight spiral of stairs she goes, the kindred following her best they can, descending down into the depths of the earth.

                  They walk for long minutes, the feeling of great weight above them building, the darkness feeling every colder and deeper. The stairs are shallow and slick, but they keep their footing. The Hag mutters and spits to herself as they walk, seemingly engrossed in her own thoughts.

                  Finally they emerge into a dark chamber and their companion spits blood once more on the floor and mutters and stabs at the air with a long nail, drawing strange shapes that seem to hang life afterimages in the darkness. A deep red glow erupts from beneath them, filling the chamber with a dull light and they can see they are stood on some kind of platform or rough balcony, a long drop over the edge to one side. As one they cautiously edge across and look down.

                  Beneath them, forty or fifth yards below, but burning with a terrible crimson light, a roiling pit bubbles. An unimaginable amount of dark rich blood seethes and ripples below them, and they stare, captivated by the sight. The Hag joins them, her voice breaking the silence: “This is my duty. I guard the way.”

                  “What is it?” asks Remy, his voice trembling.

                  “A door to a place elsewhere, a place of blood and flesh and madness. You would feast forever there, but your mind would be ripped from you the moment you crossed over. I wait here and watch, to stop those above seeking what is below, and to stop what is below escaping above. My duty binds me here. You are the first visitors I have had for a hundred and… no a hundred and thirty years.”

                  André licks his lips and begins to open his mouth, planning to try and use his normal charm to get her attention, but then reconsiders. He speaks plainly instead: “Can you teach me this duty?”

                  The Hag laughs long and loud at that. “Why would I teach you, little one? What have you done to make yourself worthy of that duty?” Without hesitation the boy responds:

                  “What can I do to be worthy?” She smiles at him, exposing sharp little yellow teeth in her maw, and she heads to the stairs, leaving the group to follow her, scurrying upstairs as the light fades below, Remy throwing glances over his shoulder as they leave, longing to stay and look at the pool for longer yet.

                  “I too,” says Remy, once they are seated again, “would like to learn some of this duty. The trick you did with the goblet of blood was extremely impressive.”

                  For the first time the Hag seems angry. “Trick? What I do is not trick child. You have no conception of what it is that I do. I don’t do tricks, these are no mean or petty magicks; I make the beast sit up and beg, I call out the blood in the world and I force my will upon it. Tricks? If you want tricks you should go back above.”

                  André leans forwards and asks again: “But what can I do to be worthy?” The Hag sits back and thinks for a moment before replying.

                  “Here is a deal – the insects above infuriate me and they force the worms down to interrupt me. It will not be long before more like you come calling to me, and I will not be overrun with frightened children looking to gawp or stare. You smash those insects into the pulp and blood, you destroy them, and I will teach you. A little at least.”

                  André finally asks the question that they came to ask: “But, why kill the fat man?”

                  The Hag stares at him blankly. “What fat man?” she asks, and the group exchanges a look across the fire, knowing that if the Hag is telling the truth she could not be the murderer.

                  Gabriel speaks up, telling the Hag “I fear someone is trying to frame you.”

                  “Frame me? For what?”

                  “For killing the mortal we spoke of earlier.” She snorts in derision.

                  “Why bother, why should I care?”

                  “If someone has vendetta against you,” Gabriel continues, “then they will send more people like us down here, there will be more interruptions.”

                  She drums her long filthy fingernails on the ground beside her and nods slowly. “Yes, yes. That cannot stand. An insult to me, and a distraction. Why do you say framed?”

                  “He was killed by a woman matching your appearance, and there was blood left on his lips.”

                  She looks up at that, staring him straight in the eye. “Blood? What blood?” Gabriel shifts uncomfortably in his seat.

                  “Ventrue blood” he tells her. Her lips twists, curling upwards in disgust and anger, and she spits again, a meaty lump of red vitae splatting on the floor.

                  “Ventrue? The little scrabbling cousins from the city long ago? The pathetic lords of the north and eastern stretches? And they look to frame me with their blood? Rome had greater blood by far than theirs, and it is an insult to make it look as if I share it.”

                  Eagle-eyed readers will note that this almost certainly makes her Julii which, combined with the big old rift/gate/blood thing in the basement is a serious warning sign… if the characters had any idea what to look for. The players spent a couple of minutes cackling at this, and Remy’s player was mocked as “Stix-bait” for drinking her blood, but they kept their awareness strictly OOC.

                  She shakes her head in disgust, and continues: “Well, you stop this slander, and I will reward you. I will teach you some,“ she fixes Remy with a harsh look, “little tricks.”

                  The group, seeing this as a dismissal begin to get up to leave, very keen at this point to get out of her den with their skins intact. As they are leaving, Gabriel pauses, and asks her: “What is your name? I fear we were never properly introduced.”

                  She shakes her head and says “With age you get less attached to names I find. I’ve had many. But if you want to call me something, call me what the rumours and the whispers do. I can be Baba Yaga, if that makes it easier for you.”

                  Remy mutters to Gabriel as they leave “Perhaps we could grab that statue on the way back, and take it for further study” and in an instant she is in front of them, her teeth bared, her hair flailing around her, the shadows deepening.

                  “They are my property. All of this is mine. This is my land, my territory, my possession. If you steal from me I will know, and I will not abide it. Am I clear?”

                  Swallowing Remy nods, and in an instant she is gone again, and they group nearly runs to get out of the caves.

                  As they leave the natural caves behind, they find themselves in the necropolis once more, but Benoit, confused, leads them by a much shorter root to the Tannery entrance. “I don’t understand it, but we were much closer to the surface when we came out than when we went in.”

                  The dark night hangs above them as they leave the basement, the stars shining down in their multitudes, a low moon shedding light over the city. Four hours until dawn. With little conversation the group splits, each member heading to take care of their own business, to digest what they have just seen in their own way. Some time apart is needed for all of them.

He doesn’t know off the top of his head, but suggests that they should consult the family’s records. They all head through the necropolis to where another Moreau has the books, and they discover that no Ventrue recorded Laisse the previous night. That means their intruder was using the tunnels without permission, but doesn’t give them any more leads.

                      Meeting back up again with Gabriel and André they discuss their next move. Gabriel suggests that some of their contacts in the Radicals might know of a young Ventrue causing trouble in the district and so decide to head over to a parlour in Champs-Élysées where a number of neonates are meeting tonight to discuss radical philosophy. All of them other than Agathe head that way, whilst she detours via the tannery, hoping to spot some signs of the cacophony which might give them some clues as to the identity of the mystery assailant.
