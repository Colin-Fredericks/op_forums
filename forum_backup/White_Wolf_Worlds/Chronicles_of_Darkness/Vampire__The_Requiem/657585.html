    09-09-2015, 06:35 AM

                  Then don't use Carthian Law. There's a nice alternative in the Tokyo section for their Carthian-equivalent. Plus, there's all the social-benefit merits. As for why you include them? Politics. Vampire is heavily invested in political games, and there's that much less drama and plot hooks if you ban entire covenants from the game (not necessarily the same thing as being banned from the city - invasion plots are quite interesting plot hooks).

                  As for why they work like they do? Well, I describe each of the covenant sorceries in terms of the other supernaturals and their magics. You have ceremonial magics, necromancy, techno-miracles, shamanism, and pledge magic. Oaths work like pledge magic, Theban are angelic techno-miracles, Cruac is shamanistic, the Ordo's alchemy is ceremonial, and the Carthian Law is necromantic. It uses an Underworld channel that relies on personal belief that necromantically alters how things work. So, that's my explanation - its works like GtSE magic.

                  I have a standard plot hook involved, tied into the multiple-origin theory of clans based on this, and stealing and adapting others into the Curse. Its a bit crossover-y, but that's how I construct my world.

                              Don't be bullied into using mechanics and themes you think are inappropriate or detract from the themes you want to highlight in the name of accommodating all the desires of your players or just because the book makes them a default presentation. Too many choices stifles your creativity. Instead of working with what you have or trying to get what you need/want, you spend your time going back and forth trying to decide the optimal choice for what you want to accomplish or whether or not you should dial back a power or go full bore. This is compounded when you make those same options available to PCs, who may well find a use for a power you overlooked because you didn't care much for it in the first place. You stated you dislike the mechanics and don't care either way for the themes the Carthians endorse so they aren't really a viable choice. Throw them away.

                              If you don't like them and don't feel like incorporating their merits (I consider Carthian Law to be cheesier than the Guile's handcuffs exploit myself.) then don't use them. If you want Club Subversive as an element whose purpose is to disrupt the status quo then make a group of dissatisfied members of (insert apex covenant here) in league with a few unaligned as agitators. Decide a few signature tactics they'll use to get what they want or disrupt rivals and engineer a few merits or devotions from that. That makes them actually relevant to your setting with abilities that work in a way that's explained in a fashion more to your liking rather than relying on some oblique pseudo-mystical concept of statism-always-supercedes-everything that is Carthian Law.
