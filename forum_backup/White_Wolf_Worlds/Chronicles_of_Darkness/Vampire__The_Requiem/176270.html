    A Thought on Vampiric population

    06-21-2014, 03:28 PM
    I was reading through the Ordo Dracul Covenant book and thought once again how rather thin my city's covenant is. For that matter, all the existing covenants are relatively insipid, since there are so few members of each. And I started thinking about why we have the 50K or 100K rules, yet most settings seem to have (or be written for) hundreds of undead. So, I looked back at the city population (metro-wide, the ratio is something like 1 vampire: 50,000 mortals). I figure that what makes the population limits so important is not food source but the ability to remain undetected. It follows that in larger cities, between increased isolation (how many folks you know in a given area) inter-city travel (folks from all over town showing up at night spots) and increased tourism, etc., the acceptable Kindred limit should rise faster than the mortal population. I came up with a quick, conservative formula to see what the numbers might look like, and my metro area, which theoretically could comfortably support 20-40 vampires, might actually carry well over 100, assuming they behaved moderately well.

    I don't know what other folks think about population levels, but I wouldn't be surprised if it's been talked to death on the various fora.

                                          The last time I saw figures in printed material the number of active vampires in the world came out to be 1 in 50000 mortals. I looked at the world population almost as long ago and roughly half of all people live in an urban area. I assume the number of active vampires excludes any that might live in rural areas because any such vampires would be unknown, like the number of vampires in torpor. That leads me to believe that in a metro area there would be twice as many vampires as the 1:50000 ratio would suggest. It gets tricky when you consider "mega cities" like Los Angeles that are huge metropolitan areas with city after city and no rural area in between.

                                          I try to find the smallest political bound that contains "my city" and has a population density around half of the population density of the city within the area that has the largest population. I figure out how many vampires are in that area based on the world ratio to get the known vampires in the area. Then I take a list of the metro areas within the area in descending order of population. I go down the list figuring out how many vampires would be in each city going by the world ratio times two (2:50000). When the running sum of vampire populations of cities equals the total of the area, I have found all my vampire domains within the area.

                                          All smaller cities either have no real vampire presence or are part of the domain of one of the larger cities. Vampires might be granted territories in these smaller cities. It isn't much different that people living in the suburbs. If there are multiple domains close to each other and I don't want a war of princes, I combine the domains into a large one. The prince of that domain might have regents controlling the other cities.

                                          That is about as realistic as I know how to make up numbers.
