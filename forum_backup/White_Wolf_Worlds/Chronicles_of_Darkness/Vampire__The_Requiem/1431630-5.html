    Final excerpt, and something to enjoy for the weekend! On Monday I'll be back with a few development notes, if I can come up with anything interesting to say about them.

    The Rusted Graveyard: Nouadhibou, Mauritania

    Stereotypical Nosferatu inhabit graveyards, but nothing says it has to be human ones. Nouadhibou hosts the world’s largest ship graveyard, sitting forlornly in the city’s bay, making it the perfect forum for an aquatic Necropolis. These marine Haunts hunt for blood amid the dilapidated hulls, but new horrors rise from the deep and threaten to consume the entire Warren.

    Where we came from

    The Sahara is a bitch to deal with at the best of times, and that’s before the Embrace, so there’ve never been many vampires in Nouadhibou. Still aren’t, but our population boomed back in the 80s when a bunch of fishing companies went belly up and scuttled their ships in the bay. It’s an eyesore for mortals, but for us, it’s refuge from the oppressive, omnipresent, bastard ball of fire in the sky that’s just waiting to smite us. A place to sleep where the mortals won’t bother us is worth the indignity of traipsing through rusty water every time we want to feed.

    Who we are tonight

    Nouadhibou’s got the dubious honor of having the largest ship graveyard in the world. Funny, right? We sleep in both a junkyard and a graveyard. The Necropolis is growing now that we have space to expand into, but it’s pretty much the extent of the entire domain. We sleep in the sand and the mud, safely buried underwater; not like we need to breathe any more. The sun can’t touch us as blood mingles with salt water, and we cultivate bloody corals in aquatic Wyrm’s Nests. We might be waterlogged corpses, but we’re predatory waterlogged corpses, as silent and deadly as the sharks and angler fish the Blood twists us into.

    It’s easier to count the Kindred who aren’t Haunts, so we graciously let them into the Necropolis, as long as they don’t start shit. These nights, we need all the help we can get. A few years back, we noticed some mortals behaving strangely before some damn ocean bugs tore their way out of their mouths. We killed the little shits, but the Dragons among us were still able to identify them — some freaky shit they called Cymothoa sanguinaria.

    Point is, they’re some sort of underwater, vampiric parasite. We didn’t find many at first, but now the seafloor is crawling with the little shits, and some of them aren’t so little any more. Whatever’s going on, these bugs are mutating, growing larger, and hungering for Vitae.

    Secrets and Lies (Excerpt)

    The Dragons don’t know where the C. sanguinaria infestation is coming from, but they desperately want to find out. Rumors of the bizarre parasites draw researchers from across North Africa and Southern Europe to Nouadhibou, and not all of them are willing to announce their presence, or play nice with the Graveyard. In secret bunkers, foreign Dragons implant the parasite into humans and ghouls alike, not caring if they destabilize or destroy the Necropolis in the process.

    The Rusted Graveyard
    • Heart: A large fishing trawler at the center of the Graveyard.
    • Focus: A private place away from the sun and the kine (Protection). Residents gain a Beat when they bring new Haunts into the Necropolis, or keep interlopers away.
    • Benefit: Necropolis members can move underwater unimpeded by tides or currents. They do not suffer penalties to Physical rolls for being underwater.
    • Burden: Patrol the ocean waters at least once per week.
    • Clot: Guardian Swarm (twisted sea life). This doesn’t include C. sanguinaria; they’re much worse than a Clot.
    • Residents of the Rusted Graveyard have access to the Home Turf and Lost & Found Merits.

        Hey all!

        So, I know I said I'd provide some development notes, but my mind kind of blanked when I sat down to write them (Malkavia has been taking up a lot of brain space...). Plus I think I've said pretty much all the really interesting things — at least the ones that interest me. If any of you have questions, though, feel free to toss them up on this thread.

        Anyway, here's a little bonus excerpt, because why not? The only commentary I have on this is that those names for non-Nosferatu "necropoli" where in my original outline, and might have been one of the first things I came up with when I was drafting the shape of the book.

        Necropoli

        The Haunts exist on the margins of the All Night Society, shoved into the deepest shadows and the narrowest cracks. The rest of the Damned don’t think about Nosferatu unless they have to; out of sight is out of mind. While the clan may gripe about that treatment, in truth, this indifference suits them well. If the other clans found out what they were building down in the depths, they might try to take it for their own. Or destroy it.

        Necropoli are places only the Nosferatu rule. Whether underground networks of tunnels and tombs, miniature empires hidden in hollowed out warehouses, or whole towns ripped away from the map, these domains are unique among the clans. One Necropolis is a series of decommissioned and forgotten subway tracks, restored and oiled with blood, allowing swift travel beneath the city streets. Another is an old mine just outside of town, where troupes of men shuffle off to trade their blood for the treasures revenant miners pry from the earth. A third is the twisted wreck of an enormous tanker ship, half-sunk into the silt of the bay, where dead things watch the glimmering surface above for their next meal. Necropoli are fortresses and open forums, record halls and larders. Their forms and functions are as twisted and varied as the Haunts themselves, and every bit as disturbing to outsiders. They’re where the Haunts can truly be themselves.

        Only the Haunts gather together in such communities, and have done so for millennia. The Nosferatu have always been builders — they had to be. Serpents and Lords can force themselves into the halls of power and have mortals dancing to their tune in a night; Shadows slip between the camera feed frames to inhabit forgotten spaces, with other residents never the wiser; and Savages don’t need a roof as long as they have a patch of dirt. But Haunts aren’t gladhanders or sneaks or survivalists. They’re monsters, and monsters live in the muck, under bridges, caves, and sewage drains, away from the terrible truth of light.

        Necessity is a good teacher, and the Nosferatu are excellent students. A few pieces of sheet metal here, a trapdoor there, and what began as a shelter from the sun and society becomes a home. Still, a home isn’t home without neighbors… but who wants to live with a monster? The other monsters, of course, and fuck all the ones who pretend to be something else.

        Other clans aren’t welcome down in the depths. Their authority is worth less than the runoff dripping down the rusted pipes. Rejects rule the Warrens, and any “Kindred” foolish enough to forget aren’t going to survive the visit.

        Ex Urbe Mortis

        The first Necropolis worthy of the name was built beneath Rome, ironically at the behest of another clan. The Dead Julii desired a place of magnificence from which to rule the Camarilla, and they bade the Worms of Rome to burrow deep, and craft a dark reflection of the city above. The Roman Necropolis was a thing of white marble and proud columns, the first and last city created by the dead, for the dead.

        When Rome burned above, the Necropolis remained below, and parts of it still stand tonight as the last shards of the Camarilla, a legacy of its lowest members. The Haunts have never forgotten this, and while other Kindred do their best to rise to greater heights, the Nosferatu dig ever deeper, even as the surface falls to ash.

        In the Covenants

        In order to sustain their numbers, most Necropoli are inter-covenant affairs. However, a given city might have enough Haunts in a single covenant to form their own Warren, and such structures can be powerful forces for their faction’s agenda or philosophy.

        The Carthian Movement: Firebrand Necropoli are communal hives, resembling guerrilla bases and underground resistance cells more than living spaces. With soapboxes on hand for whatever cause they’re championing that night, a Carthian Warren’s unity is a powerful weapon, though they often have trouble with the “secret” aspect, as their righteous anger struggles to contain itself to just one location.

        The Circle of the Crone: Warrens dedicated to the Crone are hellish, viscera-splattered caves with arcane totems marking the borders, promising a slow death to all intruders. Acolyte Necropoli form to consecrate territory to the Dark Mother, or to tear down something or someone that offends her. They’re more primitive than other Necropoli, exploiting natural caverns and sinkholes, with dark altars where cultists can spill entrails to divine their deity’s unholy will.

        The Invictus: Warrens sponsored by the First Estate often center around maintaining the Masquerade. Most are hubs where the Haunts can file away reports and coordinate enforcement, but some dedicate themselves to wetwork if the Invictus is on its back foot in the domain. In cities where the Conspiracy of Silence rules, a Warren might be the center of power, a hidden boardroom where the Inner Circle convenes to make the real decisions. These Necropoli are slick affairs, like oversized panic rooms covered in gleaming steel panels, stocked to the brim with the finer things in death. Hidden doesn’t have to mean shabby.

        The Lancea et Sanctum: Sanctified Necropoli are underground temples where hooded priests read from the Testament by the light of flickering halogen bulbs, and warbling, hissing choruses sing out in supplication. These Warrens safeguard precious records or holy relics, but sometimes they’re just meant to tighten the Judges’ grip on a given area. The cores of these Warrens are always churches, but whether this means soaring arches lined with cracked stained glass, or spartan chambers and unadorned pews, depends on the sect.

        The Ordo Dracul: The Order is the least likely covenant to build Necropoli, as the unity of purpose they require is rare among the Defiant. When they do build them, however, Dragon Warrens are nightmare versions of scientific facilities, with lecture halls carved out of sewer brickwork, and rows of blood-stained labs where the cries of “patients” echo for miles underground. Information gathering is the most obvious goal for these Necropoli, but a concerted effort to “liberate” a Wyrm’s Nest could bring such Haunts together. A Warren built over a Nest develops all sorts of dangerous and esoteric features, any one of which might become the covenant’s next breakthrough.

        Pale Imitations

        Occasionally, one of the other clans tries its hand at an exclusive society. Such experiments never go on for very long — or end well — but they’re horrifying and fascinating case studies while they last. The Haunts prefer to call them teachable moments.

        Daeva Utopias: When the Serpents gather together, the Vitae, alcohol, and sex flow like water, and screams of ecstasy drown out the cries of agony. Glittering monuments to excess and desire, Utopias tend to be cults of personality, with a single Daeva calling on other Serpents to share in his vision. Like fireworks, such places are beautiful, but fleeting. Jealousies flare, tempers rise, and appetites run rampant until the whole thing comes crashing down in glorious fashion.

        Gangrel Freeholds: It’s rare for the independent Savages to try communal living, but it does happen from time to time. A family or coterie will carve out a swath of territory for their own, offering land and blood to any clanmate who wants to join up. It’s a force to be reckoned with while it lasts; nobody wants to mess with a lone wolf, never mind a whole pack. That said, Gangrel aren’t well known for accepting authority, and those who won’t submit find it’s easier just to move on. Freeholds often end with a whimper, not a bang, as members simply drift away.

        Ventrue Kingdoms: Sometimes, the Lords decide they need a place away from the petty squabbles of their inferiors, where the true elite of the All Night Society can gather to shape the fates of lesser beings. Ventrue Kingdoms always begin well, and are organized down to the stationary font — Ventrue love hierarchies, after all. Unfortunately, what they love more is power, and in a room full of CEOs, nobody wants to be the janitor. Kingdoms collapse under the weight of their subjects’ egos, or grind to a halt as the struggle to be king overwhelms whatever purpose brought them together in the first place.

        Mekhet [REDACTED]: The Mekhet don’t bother with such nonsense. Maybe they rent a meeting place, or maintain a bunker for emergencies, but only fools gather all together in one place. Much better to pull the strings from a distance, where no one even knows your name. For what the Shadows get up to when they do organize, see Strange Shades: Mekhet.

                        Originally posted by Yossarian View Post

                        Totally happy to answer questions!

                        Theme-wise, my major note to the bloodline writers was to emphasize the horror. Some of the early drafts (and this included my own) were a tad too generic; they were good bloodlines, but not necessarily great Nosferatu bloodlines. The Gethsemeni, for instance, had a lot more emphasis on breaching the Masquerade in their first draft, whereas the final version focuses on the holy horror of being a prophet touched by the indefinable hand of God. All the bloodlines evince a different kind of scary: the unknown, knowing too much, being a cog in a machine, addiction and decadence, and so on.

                        The ghoul families are more about the ways Nosferatu blood (for lack of a better term) fucks you up. Haunts have the most overt curse of the Damned, and that twisting nature can extend to their servants. Nosferatu ghouls are just weirder than those of other clans, and all of our ghoul families are a little off-kilter. For example, while we never come right out and say it, the Chamenos are really a subtype of ghoul rather than a family. We used the ghoul family mechanics for convenience, but they're more akin to things like mnemonic ghouls and Heisenbergs (both from Half-Damned). I've always liked the idea that Nosferatu ghouls mutate a bit, which is something you can find in Masquerade a bit more than Requiem, so that was the guiding light there.

                        From a broader perspective, the overarching theme of the book was &quot;Lonely Together&quot;, and everything in Better Feared ties back to that. Nosferatu and their ghouls are insular rejects, so they had better get used to each other.
                        I would say the Gangrel mutate their ghouls a bit as well, their vitae is wild, chimerical, and just as weird as the Nosferatu Blood :3
