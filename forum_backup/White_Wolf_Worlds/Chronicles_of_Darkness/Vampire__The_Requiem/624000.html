    Examples of Devotions

    08-12-2015, 05:22 AM
    Hi all,
    I tried to develop new devotions fallowing the tips given in another topic. These are just examples to understand the right mechanics of their creation.

    EXAMPLE 1: (the idea is an effect similar to the oWoD for the Quietus discipline)
    Obfuscate 3 Auspex 3(???)

    It creates a silent area where people inside it can't hear any kind of sound. The area affected is equal to Blood Potency x 2 yards.

    Adding to the requisite Blood Potency could be that the effect area is an entire city (in order to mimic the hig power value of the discipline above 6)???

    EXAMPLE 2:
    Obfuscate 3 Celerity 5

    The vampire teleport itself behind an enemy making a surprise attack with a bonus on the dice pool equal to celerity and then disappear again. The only way the enemy has to hit the vampire is a chance roll. The vampire could use this power in a scene for a number of time equal to his Blood Potency and it can be used on the same target or on more target.
    Question: Could this power lead to multiple attack/use on the same turn (maybe just on different target)

    EXAMPLE 3:
    Vigor 5 Celerity 5

    The attack hit with strenght and speed. The vampire add the sum of vigor and celerity to the attack dice pool and lowers the defense of the target of the celerity value. The single active power of both the discipline can still be activated.

    EXAMPLE 4:
    Auspex 4 Vigor 1-2 (???)
    The vampire can give consistency to his mind power. He acquire telekinetic power with which he can move/trhow object with a dice pool equal to Intelligence(or wits) + Auspex, or even pull or lift enemies after win the contested roll based on: Wits+Auspex vs Resolve + Blood Potency (primal urge). After that he can consider the enemy like the object for the turn.

    EXAMPLE 5:
    Nightmare 5 Vigor (???)

    The vampire make real the wrost fear of the target. Spiders, Shadows monsters of every kind become physical and hunt or attack the target for the scenes. The victim can't act normally against these nightmares becouse his fear. The prefeared action should be to run away. If he is forced to fight his pool are lowered of Blood potency value of the vampire.

    What do you think about??
    Do you have any suggestions? Have I used the mechanichs of devotion properly?

                                  Originally posted by Marcus
                                  I thanks you all for your tips. Like Monteparnas said: these devotions wants to be just a training to understand the mechanics of their creations. For example about the first one: I know is worse then Obfuscate. I just wanted to understand what powers combine to achieve different effects. Is hard for me to decide the right prerequisites in order to achieve a specific effect, and is also hard to give to the devotions the right power effect.
                                  As a rule of thumb, the Devotion have to add something you can't achieve with the standard Disciplines, have a cost in XP that makes it interesting without making it better than them, and the requisites have the purpose of not making the Devotion a shortcut to power. The core idea is being a cheap variation of powers you already have. All of that can mean a lot of things, though.

                                  So a Devotion that creates an area of silence is of limited use, but it IS of some use, so it is a valid Devotion. Specially if, being worse than the main power, it is cheaper to activate. At 1 XP it is reasonable to take even if so limited, but the cost can increase if you later find that it can be abused in unexpected ways. With Obfuscate 3 (Cloak of Night) as prerequisite, you already have access to the standard "invisibility" power, and this Devotion is a partial "invisibility" (inaudibility to be precise), so you can't use the Devotion as a shortcut to pass non-detected before acquiring the main power. If it was something the main power couldn't do, it would be a good idea for it to cost at least 2 XP, or keep the cost in Vitae.

                                  Other good rule of thumb for the requisites is to make them at the lowest level in with a thematic similar effect is achieved. At the same time you use them to avoid shortcuts, you have to keep them at relatively low levels, for a Devotion is MEANT to be used with some ease, unless it is of humongous power, like Juggernaut's Gait, the ever-present example.

                                  Originally posted by Marcus
                                  In this version of the devotion, after the attack I'd keep obfusctae active immediately....so the effect of the -5 penality to be hit remains?
                                  Precisely. The idea is to partially balance the effect with the Vitae cost. The Devotion is roughly equivalent to activate both Celerity and Cloak of Night, and with them you wouldn't have a too hard time achieving a similar effect anyway (depriving the target of Defense by surprise and disappearing for a -5 on his attack), but it cost 1 more Vitae to activate and neatly manages to do it all with way more ease. The cost of 2 XP means it isn't too cheap, but also not unfeasible. I have to add that I'm not sure about it being already balanced, but I believe it isn't too OP for the task.

                                  Originally posted by Marcus
                                  Just in order to make an example (not for a real scenario), what should be the effect of a physical devotion like this one with a level of blood potency really hig? Like 7-8-9???
                                  Hard to say, but the core assumption is that the Devotion should always add something new. Maybe you could have an effect like old Masquerade high-level Potency Flicker, where you deliver a blow at short distance with minimal movement, or maybe a blow able to send the target several yards backwards and prone, or yet a running blow that can target several people at the same time.

                                  Remember, tough, that high level Devotions don't by default need other requisites than some Disciplines at 5. Again, our beloved Juggernaut's Gait: it makes you completely immune to any kind of damage, at a cost of 5 Vitae per turn.                                   Always keep the balance: do not let it be a shortcut to power, but also keep the requisites as low as possible. Internal costs are also part of the balance, and BP is an option, but one for when needed even to make Elder Devotions.
