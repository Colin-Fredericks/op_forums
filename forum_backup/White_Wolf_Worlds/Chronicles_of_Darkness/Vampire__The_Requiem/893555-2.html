        Originally posted by Arcaneumkiller View Post
        How would a domain control by Invictus differ from another domain control with Invictus?
        One Invictus domain may run like a classic noble court, with Prince as master of life and death and a strict hierarchy determining how deep you bow to whom.

        Another may run like a modern corporation, with elders as the board of directors and the Prince as CEO, where etiquette is not that strict and younger Kindred can mostly do whatever they want as long as they produce expected results.

        Another may run like Hellfire Club, where successful Kindred are tempted to join with all kinds of vices, pooling their resources when necessary to rule the city from shadows. In this case, everyone is a frenemy, quid pro quo is the whole of the law, and the oaths are taken in debauched mockery of old rituals, but are no less potent for it.

        That's just off the top of my head, and not taking into account how Invictus (or any other covenant, for that matter) can and will adapt to the influence of other covenants and their local idiosyncrasies. A domain where Invictus is allied with LetS will look different from one where it's in cahoots with the Dragons and opposed by Sanctified hardliners.

        and so If I understand properly covenant are usually only as large a a domain, and don't really stretch across multiple city if they have different prince. The invictus from Chicago don't have to answer to the Invictus of New York of Vice versa ?
        By default, this is the case. It seems less set in stone in 2e, and some of the sample domains in 2e core show hints of how neighboring cities may influence each other, but your Invictus Prince of New York won't have to send reports to the King of America, and he may or may not have established relations with the Prince of Chicago, depending on the needs of your chronicle. Generally, the closer the two cities, and the more their IRL histories are intertwined, the bigger the chance of them having some relations and exchange, but the final decision is up to you.

                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                    One thing I wanna toss out, since it doesn't seem to have come up, is that the contention between Covenants has dimmed down between Editions. As an example, the Carthians still casually point at the Invictus as their main example of why vampire society needs to change, but they also tend to appreciate that the system does neatly direct the Kindred condition into a stable community, and while the Invictus still see the Carthians as trying to fix a system that isn't broken, they do applaud the ways they've found to reinforce the standards of society effectively and quickly. In some cites, the old rivalry stands and they glare at each other from across the dinner table, but in other cities they work tightly together to create an thoroughly efficient, if at times brutal, vampire society, thick as thieves and reaping the benefits.

                    In Masquerade, the factions were much more fixed in their issues with one another, be they Sects or Clans. In Requiem, every city plays out a little differently, and more often than not, the Covenants actually do work together just as much as they backstab each other, using their silk gloves and iron gauntlets in equal measure with one another.
                    So basically its very common for multiple sect to coexist within one city without thing turning into a blood bath - with one sect wiping out the other. Interesting definitely not something that would ever happen in VTM. _ Maybe the Camarilla and the Anarch but yeah.

                                Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                One thing I wanna toss out, since it doesn't seem to have come up, is that the contention between Covenants has dimmed down between Editions.
                                To build on this - the 2e covenants were designed in such a way that each has a reason to keep the others around. The Invictus might not agree with the Carthians, but the Invictus Prince would look at Carthians and their ability to uphold city law as well as ignore treasonous compulsions, and actively recruit Carthians because of that. The Lance's magic is more judgemental and suited for hunting down, while the Crone's magic allows for more general divinations and insights - both are highly useful in their own ways. The Ordo can walk in the sunlight and inflict Vincs with ease, making them highly versatile.

                                The five covenants not only are more cordial, but they also have reasons to actually want to work together and have a stronger, more stable city because of it.
