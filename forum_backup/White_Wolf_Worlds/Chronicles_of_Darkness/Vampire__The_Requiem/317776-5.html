            Originally posted by Rose Bailey View Post

            Vampires only take bashing damage from mundane sources that usually deal lethal damage, like guns or knives. The exception is massive trauma, like getting hit by a truck.

            I've never tried dealing lethal damage on exceptional successes, and that would shift around the balance of combat beyond what we playtested. When the new edition was in development, I considered having certain called shots be exceptions to damage downgrading, but it seemed too fiddly to make the default. I also didn't want to make called shots seem mandatory.

            That said, sometimes I consider head wounds and such massive trauma. If a vampire gets a sword through an eye or something and they're not using Resilience, it'd feel off to me for it not to be lethal. But that's something I do pretty occasionally and depends heavily on the narrative part of combat.

            Thanks for the response! That seems like a fantastic way to handle things. As a follow-up question, does the damage type magic/supernatural attacks get downgraded to bashing depending on the power? I notice that abilities in Second Edition specifically say when they cause lethal to even Kindred (Protean and such), so didn't know if all powers should be treated that way across gamelines or not. Or is it a case-by-case, ST-judgement sort of thing.

Notably, Professional Training has stuff like "Thug" and "Vagrant" and I doubt you'll necessarily be receiving your training from the Department of Thuggery & Vagrancy at the University of Hard Knocks.

                                                    My suggestion for characters that might not be getting formally trained any longer is that they still in same way engage with their profession or people related to it, regardless of circumstances.                                                         Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
                                                        I have been told in other posts or questions that the "flavor text" is not just flavor it is how things are suppose to work. (specifically when I asked about the text in the seductive beast )
                                                        The most important thing to remember is just to apply common sense. That cuts both ways. It means that if a power that says that it seduces a character succeeds, that character ought to act seduced, but it also means that an interpretation that your group agrees is properly in the spirit of a power ought to be acceptable even if a strict reading of the power doesn't cover it.

                                                        Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
                                                        1: I completely understand that people interpret things differently and I have a tenancy to interpret things literally, which i have done for Professional Training. All the flavor text, even the title of the merit and the title of the dots, Imply that you have been trained by someone else. The very definition of Continuing Education is schooling/ workshops. within my group(s) We have come to a disagreement on what this means. Can you be self taught? could you have gone to the library and read up on things or even just practice? Ultimately can you justify taking this Merit if you have received no professional training, no schooling, no mentoring?
                                                        Professional Training exists to incentivize fleshing out your character and providing plot hooks to hang on. Your professional connections are one of those hooks: you're hooked into your profession and benefit from the aid and advice of your fellows, but that means that something interesting happening to one of your contacts is a tool in your ST's toolbox. Rule it as such. For example, a self-taught musician who doesn't join jam sessions or perform gigs, but merely practices a lot in seclusion, might be a virtuoso with a high Expression rating, but he's unlikely to have access to the networking and mentorship that Professional Training provides. Conversely, a lot of computer hackers are going to have learned by doing rather than having attended any special classes or received formal training, but they're going to have learned in part by communicating with other hackers and learning from what's gone before them, so such a hacker is likely fit to benefit from Professional Training.

                                                        Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
                                                        2: Bows are a whole other thing too. Archery is a sport, athletics cover sports. If athletics is thrown weapons, firearms states it's guns and weaponry states it's melee only. Where do you put a bow. In old stuff, it falls under firearms or athletics. But Do you feel that pulling a trigger on a gun, would make you good shooting a bow? Also if you were good with Archery as an athletic sport, would it fall under the same skill as Katniss fighting for her life with the bow?
                                                        It's a situational call and depends on how often you expect the use of bows to show up in your game. Archery involves a lot of compensation and strength that doesn't translate over to the use of firearms, but having Firearms as a separate skill to its own also relates to the World of Darkness's general positioning in the modern genre. If people aren't drawing guns a lot then a single Projectiles skill covering both guns and bows seems fit enough. If there's a lot of gunplay involved, but one character has arisen from the past, trained before the widespread use of firearms, then they might use the Athletics skill instead to distinguish them further. If you're playing a historical game set during the fall of the Camarilla, you might just wholly convert the Firearms skill into an Archery skill. None of these decisions particularly break the game.

                                                        Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
                                                        3: How do you feel about stacking specialties? Encouraged? Forbidden? Specific example: Brawl: Fist and Martial arts fighting style. So pretty much whenever she is fighting, two more dice because that would be her chosen method of fighting. Granted their would be situations where fists possibly might not be used or where the merit would not be included in the attack, but more often than not both would apply. I am sure there are other examples to this as well. (long distance shots and rifle ? )
                                                        Personally, I'd be fine with stacking so long as the specialties are interesting in their own right. "Good at using my fists" is not a very interesting specialization and situations that would rule it out are relatively far between. If you had, say, specialties in "agile fighting" and "aikido," then sure, great, when you're defending yourself with throws and flips and you have a lot of space to move around in, go nuts. As a general rule, you want Venn diagrams that can have significant overlap but aren't just one big circle with a little outline on the edges.

                                                        Again, it's situational. The power works by blending you into the back of memory as not worth noticing or remembering. If you're just passing Joe Human in the park not doing anything, I'd allow it; even without a herd to blend into, that's a situation where it's not uncommon for Joe Human to walk past somebody without noticing anything about them. Separate observers make separate checks to notice the vampire when she interacts with something in a way that might draw attention, so you can be noticed by one person and not by others, though if the one person then points you out I'd say that'd probably break your cover.
