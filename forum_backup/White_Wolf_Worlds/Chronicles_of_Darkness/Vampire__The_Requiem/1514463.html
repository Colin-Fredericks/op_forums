              "Basic knowledge" of the Birthing Ritual is widely known, which is part of a sentence explaining why creating a gargoyle isn't something you need to belong to a specific covenant to learn how to do.

              Creating a gargoyle requires Blood Potency equal to twice the Rank of the gargoyle you're making and the upkeep cost is the gargoyle's Rank in Vitae per month, i.e. more than a ghoul's upkeep for most gargoyles.

              Most gargoyles are not very bright or self-motivated and can only perform basic tasks, whereas a ghoul is a person who can use their own intelligence and initiative. (Of the sample gargoyles, the only one with an Intelligence higher than 1 is a Size 1 messenger with Intelligence 2, built to carry confidential messages quickly.)


              None More Dark Publishing has made a Second Edition version of gargoyles in their 2e clanbook Wild Hunt: Gangrel on the Storyteller's Vault; that version of the Birthing Ritual constitutes a Humanity 3 breaking point if successful.

                                      Following the same pattern as the message above, how you and your fellow players got the knowledge on gargoyle creation and how common was on the domain?

                                      Oh, that is a quite of convoluted story, to say the least - but it all starts with some mysterious event that consumed almost all kindred that were active in the domain in the early 18th century, leaving a city empty of any kindred that were not already in torpor and a number of bizarre beings and phenomena that are mostly gone, according to the words of the one lone kindred who dealt with the aftereffects of this unknown doom, the nosferatu babalorixá, an undead priest of the umbanda tradition, who would in later centuries become the local hierophant.

                                      Most of kindred were completely gone, never to be seen again, though a few would find ways to linger on, some by haunting torpid kindred with whom they had a blood sympathy or blood bond, specially after their awakening. But others through their own "abandoned" ghouls - human, animal and more - and a variety of strange puppet, statue or marionette things of their own creation. These partly possessed and empowered things would cause all kinds of little and not so little headaches for decades until the hierophant managed to subdue, contain them with the help of other kindred through the years, keeping these old, "rogue" gargoyles under his tutelage as watchers and keepsakes in his main sanctuary.

                                      In time he would also come to understand how to make his own, though considerably less independent or unruly, creations, knowledge that he passed to a few choice pupils through the decades and centuries. All of that said, according to his words when discussing the subject with my PC, he was also the first to admit that a few of the original "freed" gargoyles always eluded their grasp, or that some of the captured ones went rogue once again - and who knows which were really destroyed or captured for the personal study of a curious kindred or ghoul hunter instead.

                                      This would lead my character, a Gangrel Dragon with some peculiar gift to manipulate plants or hear and speak with the dead, to make some independent research and experimentation of his own, leading him to come in contact and exchange stories with a number of individual kindred or small groups across the domain, each familiar​ with how to create a certain type of Gargoyle or using some specific materials - a lone Carthian Toreador jeweler that discovered them on his own as a pastime to while away the nights, a synchretic underground "church of Lilith" that would be looked askance by followers of Longinus and Crone both if ever discovered, one or two disturbingly lifelike companions of the Invictus Primogen made in resemblance of his lost childer of yore through their inspiration, a secret cabal of rogue ghouls & gargoyles doing bizarre routines and tasks to preserve their existences or in hope of bringing back their lost domitors and more.

                                              Sorry if this counts as thread necromancy or comes off as too much of a tangent, but reading this thread reminded me of why I fell in love with Requiem 1e way back when I was young.

                                              There was an open-ended weirdness within the whole World of Darkness at the time, especially when all you had were just the two core rulebooks and your own imagination, which was how my friends and I played for years. Gargoyles were a surprisingly common encounter in our games. None of us knew much about the original WoD setting from the 90's aside from the fact it existed at one point and so we didn't have nostalgia goggles on when we played Requiem for the first time (not yet at least, lol). We didn't get the chance to buy Werewolf or Mage back then, so we just made our own rules for lupines and magi because they were mentioned briefly in the core rules. Our group eventually got Hunter and Changeling and a few supplements like Armory and Nomads a couple years later, but that was it.

                                              Requiem 1e's corebook had weird stuff like Gargoyles, Homunculi, and Hellhounds that were never really brought up again until way later if they even got brought up again at all. A lot of people just forgot about them completely and some of that stuff didn't carry over into 2e.

                                              I'm just a trans guy and gaming fan, unabashed hedonist and unusually upbeat nihilist, fairly laid back. He/Him.

                                              If everything is political, then nothing is!
