                            cWoD was dev as a huge crossover of non-interchangeable parts, at the same time separate and glued. It is a mess, even in the 20ths editions.

I'm up with the idea of powerful enough vampires that could act as splat-sponsors. Even if you don't, powerful Elders would still be resourceful enough to perceive and act against an Archmage focused on destroying the Curse, that's where the time makes difference.

                            And you can have a lot of other sponsors out there. Vampirism is a rich source for a lot of Essence, and some pretty awesome spirits can have an invested interest on them. Also other entities interested on the way the vamps do things. None of them would be a sponsor by themselves, but together and with bought time from the very stasis of vampirism, they would accomplish to thwart any attempt to destroy the splat.

                                In the old World, werewolves hated vampires because they were spawn of the Wyrm, and this was canon in Apocalypse, but not in Masquerade. In the new World, werewolves know that vampiresdon't quite fit into their models and have lots of reasons to assume they have something going on in their understanding of themselves, though they occasionally try and figure it out in their paradigm. Likewise, vampires recognize that werewolves are very much their own thing.

                                I think you're mistaking "leaving the answers of mysteries to players" as a hard wall between things. That's not the case. Early oWoD was designed with the understanding that you played Masquerade or Oblivion or Apocalypse by themselves, but not together(Ie, you can't play Masquerade,and Oblivion and Apocalypse together at the same time), which is why each gameline could say they had something that appeared in the other and it adhered to the rules of their cosmology. Later oWoD tried to fix that with mixed results. nWoD, by contrast, was written to facilitate crossover from the word go, and resolved clashes of cosmology(such as the way each splat treats the soul) with it's overarching theme of dark mystery.
