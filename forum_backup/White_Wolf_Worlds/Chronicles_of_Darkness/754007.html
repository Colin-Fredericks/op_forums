    Candidates for the Embrace: What do you look for?

    11-04-2015, 10:54 AM
    In the not too distant future, I will be siring a childe in the game in which I play. I am interested to hear other players experiences with siring childer in game. How did you choose your childe? Did you have certain criteria in mind, or did someone just strike your fancy? If you did have a list of criteria, how did you search them out?

    Once you embraced them, how much parenting did you give them? How much freedom? Did you have in mind a certain role to fill or did you just let them live their unlife as they saw fit?

    What kind of in game consequences did you experience as a result of creating a new vampire?

    Thanks in advance for any replies.

              For most of my characters, the most important desire for any potential embrace is companionship. The sire and childe should have some sort of genuine connection that enamoured them; this should definitely transcend simply wanting to ghoul somebody. This can be romantic in nature, a desire to be that person's parental / teaching figure, or even something as simple as a person you consider a best friend whom you want to join you on your life's journey as a vampire. The best embraces in my mind begin with courting the target: your character insinuates themselves into a person's life, flirts with them, tests them in the same way you would test a person before fully committing to a relationship, and eventually decides whether or not they want to seduce them to the 'dark side'. The embrace is a commitment and I can't imagine a responsible character simply embracing somebody without getting to know them. If they stalk the potential childe in secret, I can't see them remaining completely out of their life either, not meddling in any way. The person should stand out in some way that enslaving them would be unattractive, too (say you were a vampire, you wanted the love of your life to join you in immortality, so turning them into something as pathetic and sycophantic as a ghoul would just not do).

              As for my personal in-game experience with the embrace (playing the irresponsible embrace):

              My Daeva character embraced a random hot girl at a club as a 'fuck you' to his own sire who the ST played as a very controlling mother. Basically, I was playing a neonate seeking independence, being pretty rebellious. I wanted my character to piss off his sire who he was having power struggles with and that seemed like a pretty good way. But things didn't play out exactly as I thought. At first my character's sire didn't seem to care at all, so I was stuck with a vapid, blonde childe for a couple sessions. Eventually, my sire invited my character and his new childe out to go see a play under the pretence of 'mending the relationship' and 'getting to know her new grand-childe'. It started off pleasant enough, well, until we returned back to my character's sire's home. She invited my character and his childe in, and then after interrogating the newly embraced, she basically decided that she didn't approve of my character's taste in childer and decapitated the poor girl. My character was pretty angry with his sire and left in a huff after the fact, fortunately they mended things properly a little later down the track when my character's coterie needed a money-loan.

                          Thanks for the input. In my specific case, my character is a ventrue whose primary discipline is Dominate. So, exit plan is not a problem. Also, my character is a part of a ventrue dynasty that currently numbers 13 vampires. This presents some unique challenges for a new ventrue childe.

                          1. The candidate will need to be able to integrate themself in a family setting.
                          2. Because our dynasty is large, there is a good chance other interests in the city will try to sabotage the new childe in an effort to undermine the increasing power of my family.

                          Currently, I am leaning toward embracing a male in the late prime of his life (late 40s to early 50s.) Perhaps a former cop who lost his family and is itching for a second chance at life. The family needn't have been gunned down like Batman's parents. More likely the demands of his career eventually drove his wife to take the kids and disappear.

                          As for choosing a childe with whom I can share a connection, I will probably choose someone who grew up on a farm with a large family (like my character did) and who is about the same age as I would be had I not been embraced, so as to avoid a 'generation gap.' It's a tall order to be sure, but the other PC has pledged to use her auspex to help me search more quickly.

                          Can anyone spot any unforeseen problems with such an embrace?

                                  "I'd advise you to look first for talent, not mindset. As a Lord, especially of a family who excels at mental Domination - a successful candidate will be one who has innate abilities driving their Requiem, not a lingering tragedy in their past."

                                  "I think your instincts on the skills of a cop are sound. They would have experience in the one-on-one interrogations that mimic many uses of your disciplines, plus the investigative background to understand how witnesses work, to better utilize manipulations of memory."

                                  "However, don't underestimate the more expressive skills. Art plays a role in the Lords' Words, and an early sign of talent there would be proficiency in music or public speaking, or even experience with the press in front of a camera should they be a detective."

                                  "My issue with the tragic past is this - that loss of a family is bound to leave a large mental scar whose results will be difficult to predict. One would hope you'll be able fill that gap or fix that damage, however his fledgling beast will also be fighting to live in that scar - and if it wins, the ramifications to his familial integration could be dire. It's good you recognize a softer loss of family could be to your advantage."

                                  "I think the biggest choice when considering the embrace is this - are you creating a servant, a peer, or a prodigy? A servant adds directly to your personal power. A peer challenges you to become better in your Requiem. A prodigy benefits the dynasty, even when the childe eclipses yourself. Your answer here should flavor the whole process of the embrace."

                                  "I also hold strong opinions that the specific method of embrace will affect their entire requiem. Thus, depending on if you want to cultivate free will, cultivate a sense of ego and accomplishment, want them to think you saved their life, or want to break them in a specific way that you can exploit from then on - each requires a different embrace event. Don't neglect the ritual of the moment itself and how that will change their psyche."

                                  --Madame Morgane LaRoche, Guildmistress and Au Pair of Philadelphia

                                          I don't often play characters who have childer or who embrace in play, but when I do, it's never something the character puts quite that much forethought into. Thanks to the Second Tradition, my characters tend to plan *not* to embrace, but sometime something about a mortal is so striking, they can't resist. With the introduction of Touchstones in 2e, most good embrace candidates for my characters would also make good Touchstones.
