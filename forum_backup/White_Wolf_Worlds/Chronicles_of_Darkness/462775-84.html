        Originally posted by nofather View Post

        You've noticed it says the Uratha gains 'Influence' of an element but it doesn't go into it there.

        Influence is an ability of ephemerals, it is noted later in the book, more specifically on page 187. But it is heavily dependent on story for effect, though it has obvious practical applications as well.

        You can light a candle with no source of fire with Tongue of Flame and four dots of Purity. If you had Catastrophe, you can set fire to everything in a radius of miles equal to twice your Glory Renown.
        But how do you determine how strong the effect is or what you can do. At 4 Purity, lighting a candle seems extremely weak, but also it seems like you could create a floor of fire over an area.

        The language shares a lot with Mage, but the differing levels don't correlate. For instance, Disciple (3 Arcanum) allows Perfecting which can bolster something's durability or structure, but that can be done with Influence 1.

        On the other hand, Influence 4 can create something from nothing, while Arcanum 5 is needed for Making.

        But that doesn't mean Influences are stronger because it takes Influence 2 to manipulate and that's the job of Arcanum 1. It also correlates evenly with Influences 3 controlling things that Arcanum 2 can do.

        Without charts to give examples I'm not sure how far you can reach. 4 Purity creates fire from nothing, but is that just the aforementioned candle or creating a pillar of fire to burn a Vampire?

                                        If you're looking for things like Scale and Potency, it's largely up to ST discretion. Influences are meant to be pretty narrative effects, so beyond the limits placed by the Influence levels, it's kind of just "as the story demands." That said, if you want more hard and fast rules regarding how much fire your Purity 4 Werewolf can create (for example), I would suggest looking to Mage's Spell Factors.

                                        Influences are always activated Instantly, so we can assume Instant casting time. They don't generally require touch, so we can assume Sensory range. Duration is covered by the chart in the Epbemeral Entiries section. That leaves us with Potency and Scale. Scale is kind of covered by Rank but kind of not. For example, Influence 4 is Create, which allows creating a single example of the Inluence temporarily, where Influence 5 is Mass Create, which allows creating multiple examples of the Influence temporarily, or one example permanently. But Strengthen, Manupulate, and Control don't really tell us how many subjects or how large an area they can affect. So, let's assume Influences use the interaction between Create and Mass Create as our model. Use Standard Scale by default, and switching to Advanced Scale increases the Influence dot requirement by 1.

                                        We can use successes as a measure of Potency and/or steps up the Scale chart.

                                                    Originally posted by Gaius94 View Post
                                                    So how deep is the connection between any given spirit and it's physical counterpart? If you have a car and it's spirit in the shadow, what happens to the car if you kill the spirit?
                                                    It becomes 'less' of a car. It's still a car, but it's not as 'car' as another car might be. It's not as noteworthy, interesting, it doesn't have soul. But otherwise there's no mechanical effect other than the car spirit might not be there to give it a push every once and awhile.

                                                    What happens to the spirit if the car gets hit, or even wrecked?
                                                    It might become a wrecked car spirit, opening up its feeding. Depending on the circumstances of the accident it might become a death and car spirit. Depending on the emotions of the driver it might become an alarmed or panicked or fearful car spirit, or an angry one.

                                                    Is there a way to do something to one side for a direct, meaningful effect on it's counterpart, like killing the spirit of a bomb to keep the real deal from going off?
                                                    Well, obviously a spirit of a bomb would have Influence over bombs. Depending on the dots of Influence it could Strengthen that bomb, or Manipulate or Control it. Or make a new one. Or make many new ones.

                                                    I don't believe there's specific mechanics for something's 'lack' of a spirit, but it's definitely something to play with in stories. Throughout the books there are warnings against things like spirit bombs or blocking off the Gauntlet, as without a spirit to access, everything becomes bland and emotionless and spiritually dead. I always pictured it similar to the planet Miranda in the movie Serenity.

                                                    Throughout our games we've used it as a way to more easily deal with certain things, as they no longer have a sort of spiritual backing to them.
