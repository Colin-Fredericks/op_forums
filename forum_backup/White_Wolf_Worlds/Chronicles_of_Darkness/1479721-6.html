                    Hello! Let's look at the clan bane essay! I said I'd do this yesterday, but then I had a whole mess of work to do for my dayjob, and I just wasn't feeling it. But, here we are now!

                    So, in the last three books, we've made a point to include sections on the clan banes as sorta player/ST guides, though this evolved over time. We did have material about the Nosferatu bane, though more in updating their "Deviations of Flesh and Feel" system from BTHTB, and we didn't really go into the Mekhet curse apart from offering some new personal banes and an optional shorthand system for the Hollow curse. This section as a kind of essay started with the Ventrue book; the Lords' bane is really interesting thematically and mechanically, but I think a lot of readers don't grok it upon first viewing, and I wanted to go into why it's such a fun, creepy curse. Subsequently, we did the Daeva bane, which is a lot more self-explanatory compared to the Ventrue's, but it's also one that requires a bit of player buy-in.

                    This brings us to the Gangrel, who probably have the most straightforward bane of the bunch. Now, I quite like the simple brutality of the Feral Curse; it's much more thematic and fun than their original mental fuzziness (a clan weakness I don't think I ever heard a positive opinion about in all my years playing 1E). But, I think that simplicity hides some of its depth. Here's how I put it in my outline:

                    I’d like a brief section on how to work with the Gangrel clan bane: An ST/player’s guide to the Feral Curse. The Gangrel probably have the easiest bane to understand out of the five clans[...]they frenzy more easily. Bam. Done. But what does it mean?

                    You’ve got a limited word count here, but I think you can pack in a lot, especially if you give us some in-character testimonials and scenarios. Like, how does it feel to have that constant rage in the back of your head? What’s it like being the Kindred with the Beast closest to the surface? I want to go a little into the psychology of this bane. In some ways, it’s the most obviously destructive, but I’d like to hear about the more subtle downsides too. All good banes affect playstyle and character behavior, so in what ways does the Feral curse influence a Gangrel?
                    This essay did end up a bit shorter than the ones in the book's sister volumes, partially because we felt we needed a bit more space to talk about Protean, but I'm really happy with the direction the author took it.

                    So, here's our take on the Feral Curse, and with that, I've officially run out of things to excerpt! Like I said before, I'll see if I can dig up any cut content, and maybe I'll talk about some development aspects of the cutting process.

                    Wild at Heart: The Feral Curse

                    "But one mustn’t underestimate the primal appeal — to lose one’s self, lose it utterly. And in losing it be born to the principle of continuous life, outside the prison of mortality and time."

                    — Donna Tartt, The Secret History

                    Cowards call it a bane. Savages call it a birthright.

                    Rage, hunger, instinct — Gangrel don’t view their curse in clinical terms. Nor, really, do they view it as a “curse” at all. This primal force is just the nature of being Savage. Every clan knows what it feels like when the Beast takes the reins, so if the Gangrel know it more intimately than other Kindred, that’s to their benefit… isn’t it?

                    This section discusses the Feral Curse, the bestial urge that burns a hole through Savage souls until only an animal remains.

                    Just Below the Surface

                    Some nights, my mind feels like a thin crust of ice. The Beast is just below the surface, pushing and pushing, shattering everything I am. There’ll be no more sure footing after I break. Even if I claw my way back — fight against that tireless fury — I’ll have destroyed all I love, ruined my last chance, burned every last bridge.

                    “But,” the Beast whispers, “won’t that feel good?”

                    And it does. God help me, it does. It’s more than an addiction: It’s life.

                    Every vampire knows the Beast. Some deny it, others muzzle it. But for the Gangrel, the Beast is riding shotgun, reading the map, and — when you least expect it — taking the wheel. The Beast enables Savages to bring what they do best to new extremes, to be stronger, tougher, and deadlier than all the other Licks. Yet, while that pounding force coursing through every limb can make a vampire into a potent monster, it erodes a little more of the Man each time the tide comes in. One flash flood, and that sense of self might be swept away entirely.

                    Even the youngest Savages don’t need to dig deep to access the Beast. It’s always lurking just behind their eyes, and it doesn’t need an invitation to burst forth. It’s instinctual violence, insatiable hunger, and a defiant sense of survival. It will only ever go down fighting — even if it started the fight in the first place. There’s no logic, or at least there’s nothing more complex than the most primal urges. It cannot be bargained or reasoned with. It cannot be assuaged.

                    After all, it’s inherent to the Blood, and if a vampire doesn’t fight it, she suffers less struggle and strain. Why run from the Beast when you could ride it into battle? This force is an integral part of the Requiem, always present and eager to rise, so the Savages rise with it, borne aloft on a sea of blood. That primal euphoria cuts everything into clear categories: black–white, live–kill, eat-or-be-eaten. Gangrel like clear-cut lines. To be straightforward. Simple. The ever-present rage keeps things very simple indeed, but whether that predilection flows from their slipping Humanity or their swelling Beast is anyone’s guess.

                    Intrusive Thoughts

                    Survival of the fittest, my sire said. I’m young. I need to compete for food and territory or I’m not going to last long in this world. I need to stay sharp. But the Beast snarls at every threat, rages out whenever I’m challenged. It’s off the leash before I can even scramble for the harness.

                    The blood dries. I come back to myself. Or as much as ever comes back these days.

                    The enemy didn’t know a fledgling like me had that much fight inside. I didn’t know either, honestly.

                    “But do they think I’m a threat?”

                    I try to shut out that thought, but I can’t. The predator in me is always loudest at times like this. Always looking for bloody angles.

                    They’ll be back. I know they’ll be back, and if I let them get away, they’ll keep pushing. “Don’t give them that chance,” the voice in my head growls.

                    Just kill them. Kill them all.

                    The Beast slathers like a devil on each Savage’s shoulder, a wicked conscience and an endless font of intrusive urges. Bite him. Take her. Jump! It’s not just a matter of impulse control; it’s a matter of salvation. As reckless as the Beast is — and as sloppy as it makes its vampire — it fights to win. Other clans may see the Savages as quick to anger, but it’s easy to fear what’s not well understood. Gangrel understand intimately.

                    Among packmates, a bit of a tussle, some spilled blood, a little in-fighting? That’s nothing to sweat over. They all feel their tempers flaring. There’s no high ground to stand on when everyone gets the urge to react with abandon. So, while their mistakes and offenses might get them into hot water with the other clans, among their own, a brawl is over when the dust settles. No point hashing it out, trying to dig into the why of it. No point trying to soothe bruised egos when the Beast is all id.

                    Yet, understanding these truths doesn’t negate the danger. The Beast knows no social niceties. It cares nothing for bad timing, and it’s a stranger to restraint. That can get a vampire into trouble he might not survive. Shooting his mouth off at the wrong time. Eviscerating the wrong retainer. Diablerizing the wrong Lick. The Beast can be a damn liability, and while the Gangrel know it, they’re still going to let it loose. They can’t help it! It’s in their nature.

                    Weapon of Last Resort: Playing the Bane

                    This old Savage has weathered wars and feuds and regicides. I’ve been through it all thanks to my Beast. It always gave me warning before things turned bad. It lets me fight back, strike first, get out of Dodge while the getting’s good.

                    But I couldn’t always take others with me. Kin who didn’t have a Beast quite so loud, quite so sharp.

                    I ran, fought, and survived while they were torn to shreds behind me. Lost one after another — but I kept on keeping on.

                    Now I’m old and lonely. But I’m never alone.

                    At the end of the night, the Beast is a Savage’s nuclear option. It’s a weapon they can always use when all else is lost. It might eat through one’s Humanity. It might destroy one’s Kindred. It might even slaughter one’s Touchstones, but the Gangrel will crawl out, still kicking. As the tethers that keep a vampire anchored fray and fall away, the Savage becomes ever wilder. The memories of whatever life looked like before — a life without that incessant pull — become hazy. They cease to hold the Gangrel’s focus. Then, they cease to be important motivators. Then, they simply cease to be.

                    As your character’s clan bane intensifies, think about how this affects her persona. Does she become skittish like an animal at loud noises? Does she growl when displeased? Or does she just forget to not stare at her prey? Many Savages take on these ticks and habits without realizing it, though some rare few adopt them to keep other vampires at bay.

                    Still, the Beast can only tear through its host for so long before it wears through. At that point, the Savage becomes little more than a gibbering husk that haunts both Kindred and kine. Perhaps this is what the other clans fear: That if the Gangrel are allowed to run amok unchecked, there won’t be any stopping their rampages.

                    The secret is, many Savages fear this too. To fully give in to the Beast means that whispering shadow becomes the only one left standing at their side, inseverable as their own Vitae. As such, consider how your own character clings to her human side. All Kindred must attend to this aspect of the Requiem, but Savages are so close to the Beast that ceding ground to it has far more obvious — and violent — consequences. Even the Nosferatu evoke fear because they’re not quite human; the Gangrel are animals. Some Savages use meditation techniques or practice martial arts to keep their predatory sides in check, while others put themselves in spaces where the rage has less of a chance to rear its head; more than a few Gangrel have taken grunt work for their domains because it means they don’t have to deal with people. The Savage who cleans up corpses and crime scenes to preserve the Masquerade doesn’t have a lot of time to hunt, but he still eats well.

                    Of course, isolation is just another way to feed the Beast. Many Gangrel try to separate themselves from their prey because they think they can do less harm that way, but violence is only one aspect of the Feral Curse. A Lick who spends all her time alone neglects the human niceties that gird the Masquerade. She forgets that humans don’t go around with stale blood on their breath, and that clothes aren’t optional in most places. This is the eternal paradox of Gangrel blood, to keep humans at an arms’ length for their own safety, yet needing them as close as possible to remind the Beast to stay in the shadows.

                    Keep this problem in mind as you build your Savage character, and think of ways she might try to deal with it. Even if she’s as humane as a Gangrel gets, this tension should always be at a simmer, threatening to boil over every time she loses control. Some of the best drama comes from embracing contradictions, and the Feral Curse is a great way to make your character’s Requiem thrillingly — and bloodily — incongruous.

                    Common Banes

                        Hello again! I said I'd be back to post some cut content, and here I am, like some sort of promise-keeping guy.

                        The first is Project Shatterglass, the brief plot seed we included with the Mystikoi. This was great, but it made the bloodline seven pages long, and I try to keep them down to six at max. I managed to preserve a chunk of the introductory text, but the bullet points had to go:

                        Story Seed: Project Shatterglass

                        The Ordo Dracul has taken care to maintain good relations with the Mystikoi, sheltering the bloodline from its Sanctified enemies on many occasions throughout history. However, the Defiant rarely do anything without an angle, and they have plans for the Illuminated. Referred to unofficially as Project Shatterglass, some elders of the covenant have been studying the techniques and adaptations of the Mystikoi for centuries, and they’re close to a breakthrough concerning mass application. What does this mean for the domain where the first tests are about to get underway? Here are a few possibilities:
                        • The Mystikoi begin to spread their need for Essence like a disease, but not their adaptations to hunt for it. Soon, the domain is full of addicts desperate for a fix they cannot get on their own. What happens when one of the player characters becomes infected? Or is the coterie a group of Illuminated who’ve unknowingly exacerbated the problem?
                        • Something goes catastrophically wrong, and a massive rift is torn between the worlds of flesh and spirit. Now, dangerous ephemeral entities are emerging on the streets of the domain, and the local werewolves are apocalyptically pissed off. Will the player characters try to get to the bottom of this disaster and put it right, or will they take advantage of the chaos to settle old scores?
                        • Mystikoi have been arriving in the domain for weeks at the behest of the Ordo Dracul. Now, the climax of Project Shatterglass has seen the entire bloodline transformed into ephemeral hunters that can strike from Twilight without warning. No longer concerned with politics or the Masquerade, the Illuminated are running amok, attacking Kindred and kine alike. The player characters encounter Jeremy Wong, the only Mystikos to apparently escape the transformation, and he claims he can reverse the process — but he needs their help to do it.
                        I also found a Devotion I cut from Chapter Three. It was a cool power, but it was very, very similar to a Yarilo bloodline power, and I felt it fit better as a special function of that group rather than a general power (we also had a fair few "the vampire refuses to die" powers in this one anyway). That said, feel free to use this in your own chronicles if it fits!

                        Subsume Being

                        (Dominate •••••, Protean •••••)

                        True immortality is beyond the reach of the Kindred, but this Devotion is the next best thing. The rare vampire who possesses this Devotion can outlive her own Final Death, being reborn into the bodies of others.

                        Rumor has it that this Devotion is based on one stolen from the Asnâm bloodline (Bloodlines: The Devoted, p. 11), but if so, the mechanisms are quite different.

                        This Devotion costs 4 Experiences to learn.

                        Cost: 1 Humanity dot

                        Requirement: The mortal victim must have a third-stage Vinculum to the vampire.

                        Dice Pool: None

                        Action: Instant

                        In a shower of blood and pain, the victim’s mind and body transform into a likeness of the vampire, whose own body crumbles to dust. The victim is Embraced as part of the transformation, and the vampire retains all her Attributes, Skills, and Disciplines, but not her Blood Potency.

That said, feel free to continue asking questions or making comments, as I'm very happy to keep the conversation going, even if it's about one of the other books. I'll keep an eye on the thread (as well as the one on the RPG.net forums).
