                Originally posted by MtC1e Core

                [T]he Faithful's rules reference the amount of edible flesh on a target supernatural (which doesn't have to be "dead" at the time of consumption and in fact is mechanically incentivized to be as intact as possible), the sahu is a simulation of flesh made from memory and congealed necromancy, and Unclean Remains is pretty clearly an insurance quality against "jackals ate your guildmaster's body when nobody was looking" rather than a defensive measure against cannibalism for a splat whose principle antagonists specifically include cannibal mummies.

                    Knights of St Adrian

                    Seemingly one of the most modern and relatively localized conspiracies, the KoSA are basically the mercenaries of God. Composed of skip tracers and bail bondsmen, of truck drivers and bikers, the KoSA are so young that their founder is still around, and their reach and methods seem very, well, American. While they are very, very cool, their culture and aesthetics makes it hard for me to view them operating much with the same methods outside of the USA, as skip tracing as a practice is not something I was even aware of before reading about the KoSA, and I viewed as weird until I actually heard a interview of a bail bondsman on YouTube which explained how it actually works. Now, I don't know how common it is around the world- I only know that where I live it isn't really a thing, and as such it makes the KoSA something very, let's say, "exotic"- nevermind how appropriate they are in the game, especially in the context of Demon.

                    When the information about the conspiracy was just released, some people were a bit confused about what role do truck drivers and bikers have in a espionage game. The answer was, of course, subversion- the KoSA are the manifestation of brute force. They don't just track down demons, they are also able to punch them so hard in the face that they get out of Cover, allowing angels to track them down. Now, while you could assume that people would get all "You Can't Do It Hunter" over it like they sometimes do, thankfully this conspiracy was written together with Demon's team, which gave a free pass for every crazy thing that the KoSA can do that violates some parts of Demon. Overall, I think that this conspiracy was very much well received, between being a thematic opposition to demons, serving as the Hounds of God, and overall being just plain cool as stigmatic Hell's Angels who go around and kick ass.

                    Again, notice that the KoSA keep the theme of monstrous power turned into Endowment- while Stigmatic are generally not being viewed as monsters (having Supernatural Merits instead of Dread Powers), gaining Stigmata could very well count as "power from a monstrous source"- something which the KoSA take upon themselves and utilize in a very distinct way to hunt down demons. Now, something that should be noted is that Stigmata is something which is generally viewed as "accident"- someone stumbles upon a Infrastructure, gets scarred by it and develop strange powers as a result. The more reliable way that hunters of the KoSA gain those abilities makes me wonder if perhaps a more intrinsic system is at play- something which is very God Machine appropriate, as it acts as systems within systems. Something similar to adoption which makes the KoSA "demon blooded" of some kind? Perhaps, but whatever the case may be this conspiracy indeed plays well with the continued theme of "monsters into people through the Vigil" which many conspiracies seem to share.

                    Another thing to note is that while I don't know if it was intended, the KoSA very much reminds me of the movie Frailty, where
                    you have a family chosen by a angel to kill out "demons in human skin", with those people being able to see through the "human guise" by touch (with demons in that context being just horrible human beings). Not the same thing as the KoSA exactly, but still have enough in common for me to assume it served as an inspiration


                    Anyway, overall it seems like there isn't much to chew on in relation to KoSA- we know they were founded by Casey Howards during the 90's, making the conspiracy around 40 years old at best. We know that they have the God Machine as their client, sending them to hunt demons through their contact with their angelic "client". Really, while the conspiracy is very clearly used by the God Machine, it is hard to say that the KoSA are manipulated by it- they know what they are up to and what they are doing. For them, it's a combination of it being a business and how demons take away parts of people lives in exchange for "benefits", while God simply rewards them for their loyalty and use. There is not mystery, nothing is hidden- the contract between God and His Hounds is very clear and well understood. Nothing weird, nothing out of the ordinary. Just common people killing demons, move along, move along.

                    At least, that's what you could think from the writeup alone.

                    Here is the thing- the fact that the KoSA exist makes sense. God needs his soldiers, after all. However, the fact that they are so young is... weird. Sure, with the support of God 30-40 years is nothing in order to make a certain group into a global power- but why did the God Machine waited for so long to create such a organization? I mean, if It can bless humans with demon hunting powers, why it doesn't, like, grant them to any cult in the world? Or at least, any cult which shows loyalty to it? Many, many organizations would have shown great interest in having a regular connection to the God Machine which would feed them info about the location of demons and give them the powers to hunt them down. Why the KoSA and not anyone else? Why to take so long? Why to focus its attention on the US and ignore the rest of the world?

                    Thankfully, those questions get answered, at least partially, in Dark Eras 2.

                    In Empire of Dust and Gold we get a sidebar which details a organization which is uncannily similar to the modern KoSA- the Soldiers of Jabreel. Again, we have a organization of militaristic hunters who serve as "holy hunters" chosen by God to fight demons, including special abilities bestowed upon them which allow those hunters to fight their enemies heads on. Now. while the writeup does not outright connects them to the modern conspiracy, the writer have confirmed that the Soldiers were a different incarnation of the Knights, one which was more appropriate for the spirit of the Era and which fit better the local area.

                    One which may be still fitting in modern times.

                    Here is the thing- technically we are not aware of the SoJ getting disbanded at any point. Like, we can assume it happened (especially after, you know, colonialism), but we don't have an actual date for when did the Soldiers expired and the Knights were recreated. As far as we are aware, the Soldiers could still be there, maintaining a Vigil all across the Middle East or even the Muslim World in general. If that's not the case, however, it could very well be that the KoSA were founded after the SoJ got the final nail in their coffin, with the angel leaving the old conspiracy's remnants behind and starting a new project in the territory of the current global superpower. We also don't know if there were other incarnations between the SoJ and the KoA, which potentially means that we have the same angel moving from one local chapter to another, making a conspiracy and moving along when their time comes to an end as the demons manage to finally destroy the conspiracy. That could make some sense- especially as if it is the same angel making those different incarnations, it could be that this specific angel was granted the ability to gift mortal with divine power as a unique feature, being a part of its function. On the other hand, the idea of an angel operating for a millennia so close to humans and not Falling should be terrifying for an Unchained- as it means that this angel is not simply powerful, but is also very, very loyal. Archangel level of loyalty, perhaps. On the other hand, if the SoJ are not defuncted, it could be that this angels operates a number of different chapters of the same conspiracy all across the world, either having multiple Covers in the same time or, at the very least, maintaining a network of angels which operates those different mechanisms in their quest for hunting demons. If that's the case, why did this angel waited for so long before creating a new chapter in the US? Unknown, but it could be related to certain conditions unrelated to the KoSA- like requiring different Infrastructures being formed and maintained in order to produce the stigmatic abilities of the group. Maybe the God Machine wanted to maintain a strong grasp in the US before establishing a demon hunting group, for example, or it could be something more occult. Who knows? But what we get is that there is a strong possibility that the KoSA are not the only form of the conspiracy, and that either it is just the latest in a successive line of incarnations or it is the current most powerful chapter of the same, worldwide organization.

                    Now that we have both the SoJ and the KoSA, we can make some comparison between those two orgs, and from those we can get some very clear parallels- sure, there is the whole "divine soldiers of God", but that's obvious. What is less obvious is the connection between their "patrons"- Saint Adrian is one of the most popular Military Saints, and Jabreel is the Arabized version of Gabriel- again, an angel named "God's Might". So we have the theme of raw power in here. Now, the interesting thing is that the KoSA are an American organization, yet the USA is known to not be very Catholic- but still, I am not going to claim to understand American Christianity. At any case, we can see that whatever their incarnation, militaristic themes are part of the conspiracy. More than that, we can take into account the fact that the KoSA are called after, well, a Saint- that is a soul of a divine person who connected humanity to the divine. That goes well with Jabreel, as this angel was known for contacting humans in the name of God. That gives us yet another theme to include- the connection between humanity and divinity, with the KoSA serving as the bridge between mortals and angels, between the mundane and the divine.

                    So if we are to take all of those factors together, we can start to get a glimpse of what is actually going on with the KoSA- a certain organization revolving a certain archangel with the power to gift humans with divine gifts through stigmata. That angel seems to have formed numerous conspiracies throughout the years- how many we don't know, but wherever there is religion and there is God there are His Hounds seeking out demons. Those hunters, as it seems, serve as a "bridge" between humanity and God- which could explain why the angel didn't Fell through the years. Instead of humans making angels more humanlike, this angel makes human more angel-like, subverting the more expected consequence (as it may be the deal with VASCU). It could very well be that hunters who serve the conspiracy for long enough time may even "transcend" into an angelic status, being covered with Ink and divine power. And if that's the case, we may start getting a glimpse for what is the actual goal of the conspiracy beyond hunting down demons.

                    That is, turning humanity into angels.

                    Think about it- the goal of the KoSA is, in the end, to stop demons from taking over human lives. God gives without expecting anything in return, humans make sacrifices without expecting anything to return. Their contract is a subversion of the demonic pacts- instead of give and take, it is give and give from both sides. Something weird and strange and based only about faith. Now, if your goal is stopping demons from hiding among humans, the best way to do so it by stopping demons from being able to trade in the souls and life pieces of humans- which could be done only be having another force priority over those aspects. Such as the God Machine. If all humans will become more angelic than human, if their souls and memories and identity are to be sanctioned to God himself through ink and oath, demons would not be able to steal those things for Cover. They'll have no one to trade with, no where to hide, and as such, they would be quickly exposed by the Hounds of God and be brought back to the Almighty's embrace. This is a world where demons can't take anything from you for you are in God's Grace, and as such you are safe and found ion God's care. And when you think about it- this mechanism may very well actually be the goal of the God Machine in relation for humanity in the first place. Stop angels from Falling not by fixing the bug which allows them to fall (that's impossible because entropy) but by making the environment they hide among so hostile that it would become very easy to root them out. A different solution to the same problem. And by doing so, the God Machine will be able to fulfill the ancient promise it made for humanity ages ago, that they, too, could become like God. That they, too, could return to Eden.

                    That what rise must fall- but what's fallen may rise again.

                    Let's just hope that God won't become sick beforehand.

                                                Merrick Institute

                                                Because if we can get a Hunter supplement as a Beast stretch goal why not?

                                                The Merrick Institute is found in an interesting position within the world of Hunter- it is not about being decentralized, of course, and even not about them being mostly teens and young adults. It is that this conspiracy is in fact, not the conspiracy. It is not even a chapter of it, or even its remnants for a future organization. What we have here is a far weirder situation- a cuspule in time, the transition between organizations which involves a bloody civil war, and that while we get something which serves as a playable splat, the actual, true conspiracy is found behind the scenes- the Nameless Organization (as they are going to be referred in here), the dark entity which have formed the children, and which replaces its own form on a regular basis in order to not get caught by either the monsters, other hunters, and of course- their own former slaves.

                                                Let's dwell on it for a bit.

                                                In the context of the wider CofD, it is very easy to see the resemblance between Merrick and Deviants- in both cases we have people who got abused by the larger conspiracy which is found within a complex network of false aliases and facilities. It is clear that whatever the true identity of the Nameless Organization may be, they are, with no doubt, part of the Web of Pain. Now, Merrick share that resemblance with VASCU, something which their writeup even mentions, but where VASCU had Dr. Wintergreen to supervise the experiments which somehow brought less.. painful results, the powers behind the Nameless are far less kind- which should tell you something about them. The fact that the Nameless have turned to experiment on children is horrifying all on its own, especially when you consider that they don't really know the criteria required in order to produce a dreamwalker- they just seek after candidates among teens, get most of them killed and then those who survive ends up enslaved for further researched. Overall, they are true evil incarnated, no doubt about it.

                                                But they are also Vigilante.

                                                Here is the thing- the Vigil exists in order to blur the lines- it doesn't matter how or why you uphold it as long as in the end, you fight the darkness. And that is something that the Nameless actually does- they experiment on children in order to make them into weapons against the Beasts and other nightmarish horrors which lurk in the collective soul of humanity. In the end, even with all of their horrifying acts, the Nameless do fight monsters through their many incarnations and guises- but that doesn't make them the good guys. They are evil to their core, and they are definitely part of the Web of Pain in terms their actions have probably produced a fair share of Deviants, but through that research they have actually managed to produce a stable system which would grant Endowments to the survivors of their experiments. Who knows? Perhaps if the children were to be maintained under control, the Nameless could have found a way to stabilize the process, to gain more information about the criteria, to minimize the damage caused to the subjects. Maybe if they were to keep their research, they would eventually have produced improved method, eventually becoming a conspiracy with dirty secrets in its closet, but one which does not longer requires abuse in order to fight the monsters. VASCU, after all, have been through the same process, and Lady Lucifuge still uses unsavory methods in order to imprint her Will upon her agents. All conspiracies have elements of abuse within them, and many have probably started in a similar fashion for the Nameless- performing horrible experiments on people in order to develop new tools to fight the darkness. All conspiracies were born in the Web of Pain- we are just not aware of their dirty past, and once the conspiracy is too big to fail, it doesn't matter what kinds of horrible acts they have done as long as they maintain the Vigil, in their own way.

                                                An interesting insight indeed- but totally irrelevant. The Nameless were fallen, and now we have the Merrick Institute, right?

                                                Well... it's complicated. We are going to return for it later.

                                                Beforehand, what we want to examine is the exact details of the Merrick process. Now, we know that we don't know much about the process for the simple fact that even the Nameless weren't really aware for the needed requirements- after all, if they did, they would have been much more efficient in their production of "dream warriors". What we do know that it requires the use of teens, and that through chemical and other traumatic methods, they can force those children into projecting into the Astral, allowing them to fight Beasts in their Lairs. We also know that they require some kind of an aptitude for the process to work, or else the process would have been much more successful (aka, less dead children). That makes sense, considering how most things which are connected to the Primordial Dream require a certain aptitude to form a connection- souls which resonate with the darker aspects of the Dream draw the attention of the Dark Mother and ends up Devoured, while those with a sensitive connection to the ripples caused by primordial hunger ends up as Oracles and Heroes, meant to both to warn and fight those horrors. The second type of souls seems to resonate the most with the children of Merrick- but if that's so, if those children have a natural connection to the Dream and have a natural affinity to fight it, why do the Nameless work so hard in order to produce those horrifying effects? Is it simply about their need to control? Or maybe it is because they just don't understand what they are doing? Or maybe it is something else- maybe the process is abusive because there is a reason for that abuse. After all, most children ends up scarred physically and mentally by the horrifying process...

                                                What is what the Nameless do isn't abuse- what if it is a Devouring?

                                                I mean, it would make some sense- the Merrick process is in fact a artificial Devouring, produced by the Nameless in order to awaken the hidden potential of future Heroes (heck, maybe even future Beasts) in order to chain them up and use their talents for the benefit of the Vigil. In the eyes of the Nameless it may even be moral- remember the Code. Monsters don't count. Monsters are the enemy. Sure, you can work with a monster, you can even protect a monster, but doing so is a compromise- and once you prioritize monstrous life over human life, you violate the Code. If hunters have their mentality modified by the Vigil, and the children of Merrick (like most other conspiracy hunters) are indeed monsters-to-be, then the hunters of the Nameless won't even view what they are doing as a abuse. After all, what they are doing is to save people from becoming monsters, and using their abilities to fight the darkness. Sure, innocents may get killed in the process- but those were all suspected or future monsters. It is a sacrifice which has to be made if they wish to protect the world. Don't get me wrong- what the Nameless are doing is disgusting and horrible, but we have other groups who do disgusting and horrible things and get out of it only because they are doing it to monsters. The Abbey, Cheiron, the Faithful, the Promethean Brotherhood- all of those groups and more are made of horrible people who manage to get out of it because their horrible actions are done towards monsters. If, in the eyes of the Nameless, the children of Merrick are potential monsters, and would become monsters unless they'll go through the Merrick process, then aren't they, technically, already monsters? And if they are already monsters, then using their horrifying methods on them doesn't count as harming the innocents, because they are not people. And if that's true, then by forcing their own version of a Devouring, one which maims and controls the destructive urges of those monsters, they not only stop a potential monster from causing harm, they actually stop the monster from being made- and wouldn't that, indeed, fall under the definition of the Vigil?

                                                Maybe- but it still doesn't make the Nameless into the good guys. They suck- but they are still hunters.

                                                And worse- they are still around.

                                                We get it in their writeup, after all- Merrick was dismantled, but the Nameless are still around. True to a part of the Web of Pain, the Nameless still have their own facilities working around the US (and maybe beyond it), doing horrible things while keeping their research going on. Here and there, Merrick find those facilities and fight them, liberating the trapped children- but considering their tendency to replace their own names and faces, it is very clear that the Nameless, in whatever form they may take, are still around, and they probably want to take over their rogue faction. Again, we should take into perspective that in their eyes, the Merrick children are monsters- tamed monsters but still monsters. When they kill, kidnap and brainwash those children, they, in their mind, fight monsters. On the other hand, the hunters of Merrick view they tormentors as monsters- and as such do what they can in order to stop the Nameless and dismantle it once and for all. That cycle of abuse is a product of the Code getting twisted, of hunters targeting each other as monsters and justifying the means through it. That is a dark and unpleasant corner of the Vigil- one which we can finally examine through the eyes of Merrick, and a cautionary tale which could happen for any conspiracy if they are not careful enough.

                                                When we take all of it into account, we can get one thing very, very clearly- that Merrick are not the true conspiracy, they are its sins and past taking form, working to punish a rouge conspiracy for falling too deep into the darkness in their quest to fight it. Merrick could become it, sure, but in their current state they are \far too decentralized to do so. Heck, they are currently during their own civil war (one without bloodshed, however... for now) over whether or not they should become a conspiracy. Because let's be honest, if the Firebrand would win, there will be no Merrick- there will be no further research, no new attempts for recruitment, no steady access for resources meant to maintain a organization. They don't even have a supernatural patron working to unify them like other decentralized conspiracies has. At best scenario, they would defeat the Nameless once and for all and get disbanded afterwards. At the most likely scenario, they would disintegrate into small cells and vanish from the world while the Nameless still lurk in the Web of Pain. At the worst case scenario, the Nameless would take over the small cells one by one, devouring them back into their own organization. There is no "lasting legacy" situation for the Firebrands. The Reformers, on the other hand, are the ones who try to rebuild the conspiracy from the remnants, researching the Merrick process and seek to keep the conspiracy going, yet without the dark methods of the Nameless. If they'll success at the process, then they are very likely to manage to replace the Nameless, caring their candle while leading for a brighter future... but if the process does indeed requires abuse to work, if it is indeed a artificial Devouring, would the Reformers try to minimize the damage but would still perform the experiments? Will they be willing to make the needed sacrifices in order to keep the Vigil going? Will they pay the price- and if so, will they be any different from the Nameless which they have displaced?

                                                Those questions become even more clear when we examine the potential endgames for the Nameless and Merrick- at first, we thought that each of those probably have their own endgame, but after doing the research I am not so sure. It seems that both organizations want, more or less, the same thing- a world without abuse. A world without nightmares. A world where people can sleep soundly at night, that there is no fear from the dark. The thing is that the Nameless have a much more clear vision about their ideal world- a existence where the darkness is being chained, that the nightmares are bound, that the hungry get devoured. That control over the primordial hunger and chaining it is the key to save the world from it. All of that makes me think about that section in After the Fall, where it mentions how nightmares were bound in Constantinople before the invasion and now they got freed- maybe the Nameless are as old as the Byzantine Empire? Who knows. Merrick, on the other hand, are yet to have a coherent vision- end abuse by destroying the Nameless, sure, and fight other nightmarish abusers, no problem, but what they want to do with it? What to do next? Are they going to take the fight against the Web of Pain, attacking its Astral manifestation in order to force people to face their own fears? Or will they take the fight directly against the embodiment of fear, potentially slaying the Dark Mother herself? The fact that the Reformers have opened a lab in Australia in order to have a better understanding of the Primordial Dream from the aboriginals means that, again, this who method may be ancient- and maybe the old Australian version of the conspiracy could offer the solution for a world without abuse while still changing future heroes or Beasts into dreamwalkers- or perhaps offer their own variation of the Endowment. The Nameless, on the other end, could turn into even darker path- that if the children can't be controlled, they need harsher leashes. Like, potentially, binding their sleeping minds into jaded agents which know what they are doing, and will not fight against the conspiracy even if they'll discover the true dirty secrets that the conspiracy hides...

                                                Welcome to Merrick. The Vigil has never been darker.

                                                        Hototogisu

                                                        Finally, it is time for the Cuckoo.

                                                        As being part of Tokyo's setting, the Hototogisu was part of the so called "metaplot experiment"- creating a localized, updating, evolving setting which adds more lore and introduce changes as new core books are getting released. Personally, I was quit a fan of the concept, and the Hototogisu were clearly at the center of it all- a strange organization of mortals which have managed to take control over the local vampire population through extorsion, and then started working its way through the all different supernatural societies of the CofD. Led by a merchant known as Inoue Akio, the Hototogisu have grown to become a powerhouse in Tokyo's shadows, even growing beyond Japan as they research the supernatural to develop new products to trade. While some people may be quick to disregard hunters as a threat for the main protagonists of other gamelines, the Hototogisu stands as an example to show that mortals can not just challenge their rule- but they can actually win. At the latest point in the setting, the Hototogisu have more or less formed contacts with every supernatural society, becoming a power to consider. Heck, the organization's power have grown so much, that Inoue have became Tokyo's apex- the strongest, scariest, most influencing horror among horrors, that leaves its mark on the Primordial Dream. His shadow strikes fear all across Tokyo, making him what other monsters fear- a plain old mortal.

                                                        Or is he?

                                                        Here is the thing- even the text doesn't seem to know what Inoue actually is. He was claimed a mortal in the original writeup. He was then claimed to be a ghoul in Beast. Heck, in the same breath, Beast also mentions Inoue as ancient vampire (like, sure, the book doesn't say that the vampire Inoue and the ghoul Inoue are the same Inoue- but considering the connection of both characters to vampires... well, it's fishy saying the least). And that without considering that while the conspiracy was founded after WW2, there was another Hototogisu which, again, was founded by a mortal merchant named Inoue Akio. I mean, sure, that Inoue may be one of the other tow Inoues, considering that vampires and ghouls do tend to live long but... come on, that's just weird. As it seems, we could very well have at least three Inoue Akio, which are always merchants and always have connection to the darkness, vampires especially. Seriously- what the heck? We can't even tell how many Inoue Akios we have in the setting, not to mention how many Hototogisus we also got. Something is fishy in here, sure- and it grows even more fishy once we consider that Lair Trait that Inoue projects into the Hive- Maze. Inoue forms strange patterns and process which makes those who try to follow the Hive to get lost unless they manage to understand which is the right way, solve the riddle, unlocking the cipher.

                                                        Sounds very demonic, isn't it?

                                                        Now, I am not suggesting that Inoue is a demon- on the surface it would make sense. Resurfaced identity and strange patterns is something which resonates well with Demon, and even the Endowments of the organization, Setto, have some demonic quality to them- elements of trade and taking parts of a monster's identity, denying them from it in the process. However, while it makes sense on the surface, things become weird when you start to examine the pieces- first, Inoue seems very much like the opposite of a Cover. It is the same identity shared by multiple individuals, instead of a degrading identity meant to hide from the angels of the God Machine. That makes me think much more on Yodogiri from Durarara, which strangely, also has some connection to vampires. Really, I wonder if the novels were part of a inspiration for the conspiracy. Still, the other thing we need to take into account is Setto- while the modern conspiracy seems to use certain charms as ways to fight the supernatural, they may very well just be an evolution of the general concept offered by the Edo version of the conspiracy (and from what I heard, in a Tokyo game ran by the original writer the Hototogisu hunters did had Setto so... yeah it is still relevant). At any case, while Setto looks like a demonic pact of some kind, what we need to remember that it is a subversion of it- in Demons, pacts are, more or less, a "fair game". Sure, demons can and do manipulate mortals into giving them those parts they want and keep loopholes in the contracts to keep it in their benefit for a time of need, but it is still, at its essence, a fair trade- the demon gives what they agreed to give in exchange for the mortals giving what they agreed to give. Setto is something different- it is literally means "theft". Through Setto, the hunter actually steals parts of their target's power, giving nothing in return- that violates the very basic premise of a pact, which means that even if it uses the same mechanism as Cover and Soul Pacts, it is much more insidious in nature. Add to this the fact that in Hunter's terms, Immortality is also a Dread Power- and you could easily see that, in theory, Inoue could indeed be the vampire, the ghoul and the mortal all at the same time. The same crafty merchant which stole a vampire's immortality, confusing the heck out of anyone who tries to get what he actually is. Again, returning back to the "Sins of the Vigilant" meta-conspiracy theory, as users of monstrous power the Hototogisu seems to represent Deception, which is not a cardinal sin per see but it does have a 1e Maeljin associated with it, which is enough for me.

                                                        Ok, so lies and deception seems to be the theme for the Cuckoo- but why the Cuckoo?

                                                        Honestly, I'm not sure- in classical Japanese culture, the Hototogisu seems to be related to death (spitting blood and crying) while also being a symbol for beauty thanks for its singing, which doesn't really resonate with the organization. On the other hand, a infernal bird does make you think of a certain winged entity which is known to torment vampires- the Strix, and that would once again enforce a connection to vampires is general- but we also need to remember that Tokyo is known to have actual Strix in the form of the Tengu. Does it change anything? Not necessary, but something to keep in mind. At any case, the name itself seems to be much more related to the western concepts of the Cuckoo- a bird known for deception, lies, mimicry and theft, all things which makes work for the conspiracy... but again, those are Western concepts, and here we are talking about the Edo period which is before a rapid exchange of ideas and influence from the West. Still, I am no expert on Japanese culture so, like, it could be that those concepts about a liar cuckoo were already around during Edo and my great Google research simply didn't found it. As I have no Japanese friends to ask about it and I know the writer lived in Japan, I am just going to accept that it could potentially make sense- the Hototogisu are beautiful liars, related to the blood spitting dead and steal what they want from them through cunning and manipulation. Yes, all makes sense- but before we call it a day, let's add another part to the equation. Werewolf.

                                                        Or more specifically- Sundered World.

                                                        One of the main things we got from Sundered World was the existence of the Pangaeans- great beast gods of tremendous power, serving as pillars of reality. Those entities do not just serve as totems of their species but also as symbols of wider meanings- Wolf was, for example, the God of the Hunt. Bull was Kingship and Civilization. Bird was Cruelty. Snake was Poison and Medicine. Those entities were tremendous powers which have either died or changed after the fall of Pangaea, but they have left their mark upon the world, and their influence was not limited to Pangaea, Shadow and the Flesh- through their sheer resonance alone, those demigods were also connected with the Supernal (they were just that real compared to the rest of the world), and many had connections to other worlds as well. Death Wolf and Snake have relations to the Underworld, Bird have created her own pocket dimension, Fox seems to have some ties to the Hedge, Spring may have found a place in the Astral... heck even the Eater of Names may have some very suspicious connections to Duat. It is little wonder that a lot of people even considered Owl to be a Pangaean from which the Strix were created- but maybe it wasn't the case. Maybe we have another bird which was lurking in the shadows, resonating with the themes of Deception and lies which fit the Kindred so well, parasites that play at being humans, stealing their life while spitting blood. Could it be that the Hototogisu are in fact controlled by a Pangaean?

                                                        Unlikely- but it still may have presence in there.

                                                        I mean, if Cuckoo the Pangaean is connected to the Hototogisu it could explain the universal thematic of lies and deception it carries. Pangaeans don't care about culture, after all- they are symbols, and the Cuckoo is a symbol of lies and falsehoods. As such, it would make sense for Cuckoo to be the source of power behind Setto- heck, it could be that her Ompahlus Stone shards are used to make the charms the Hototogisu uses in modern times. The Pangaean's ability to resonate with other realities and Cuckoo's power over thievery may make her into a "anti Dark Mother" of some sort- connecting the supernatural world through fake Kinship. Heck, she may very well be the Dark Mother, but that has implications which are much greater upon the whole CofD setting. As a false Dark Mother, Cuckoo would try to worm into the nests of other monsters and take from them whatever she wants. Originally, she may very well have been a low god in the divine hierarchy of Pangaea, laying in hideout centuries after the Sundering- and then came Inoue, and everything changed.

                                                        Inoue could be the closest thing that Cuckoo may have for a physical avatar- a person which resonates strangely with her themes and desires, forming a channel to control the whole supernatural community. Either by chance or by design, Inoue may have stumbled upon Cuckoo, and the fateful meeting have changed the world. Maybe Inoue had another name during that time- before he stole the name and immortality of the original Inoue. It could even be that Inoue lives in name only, becoming a parasitic identity which manifests itself every time that an opportunity is brought to his attention. Inoue Akio may be the Mantle of the Cuckoo, the Hototogisu Incarnated- or just a plain old mortal who managed to steal from the Cuckoo, earning her favor and gifts. Setto may in essence be the result of Cuckoo stealing the God Machine's own Infrastructure- or even the ability to produce them, and then subverting them. She stole the mechanisms that the God Machine use to grant boons and demons use for Pacts, she stole the Kinship of the Dark Mother, she stole the relation of the Strix to death and the Kindred... She stole it all, and without Wolf around to stop her and with Inoue's ingenuity to utilize it, she has become unstoppable... with the exception that Inoue have probably stole her as well. Cunning humans with their cunning magic.

                                                        That's the key point- Inoue is the Apex. He controls Tokyo's night. As such, he is probably in control over the ancient theoretical Pangaean as well. Here we are talking about someone who have lived for centuries using stolen immortality and which put the whole vampire society under his thumb. If someone is able to trick a God of Lies, it would be this guy. He is the one to control the pseudo-Infrastructure that gives Cuckoo her ability to manifest and craft charms as well as unifying Tokyo's shadows under his control. The Maze trait? It is not Cuckoo- it is 100% Inoue, 100% his own twisting mind which gives only one right way for success, a product of delicate manipulation and plans which all lead towards a single goal. And if he indeed have subverted the mechanisms of the God Machine through Cuckoo, it could be that you can't actually kill the guy- that he is a living Cover, and that his death will be only a setback as the stolen reality manipulation would allow him to respawn as the same Inoue Akio, same merchant, a plain old mortal with confusing ties to the world. And the interest in vampires? again, yet another expression for his control over the darkness- vampires are the product of disparate Curses bringing the dead back to life, after all, which is held by strange sympathy of the Blood. Inoue stealing that sympathy and name from the vampire may very well be the key for his control over the All Night Society, both socially and metaphysically.

                                                        Now, this is the point where we are to ask ourselves about what is the Hototogisu's true goal- but it is pretty obvious, isn't it? The Hototogisu want to take over the world. They want to steal the powers of the monsters to themselves. Not only that, they want to break the boundary between humans and monsters- the supernatural will be part of the natural, meant to help it progress and evolve. Monsters will be like humans, humans will be like monsters. No one will know the different between truth and lies- an eternal Masquerade where identity is simply the lie you want to be true. And all will be under the strict hierarchy of the same Inoue Akio, the same merchant, the same plain old mortal. Cross the boundary between humanity and monstrosity and true power can be achieved. We are not driving the monsters into the light- we join them in the darkness, snuffing out the light. The monsters by themselves will not know who is a monster and who is a hunter, and when the lines will be blurred it is the time to take over the darkness from within. As they say, if you can't them- be them. Fake it until you make it. And when the time is right, cut down their throat and watch them spitting blood as you take their place in the darkness. Steal from them all you can and throw the leftovers. By the time they'll notice something is wrong, it is too late- the Cuckoo is already here. The Cuckoo is already you.

                                                        We are all Inoue Akio. We just don't know it yet.


                                                        Well! Finally- the last conspiracy in the list. That was quite a ride- the last theory is indeed the wildest of them all, I think. If I'll be in the mood I'll start moving through certain compacts next (not all of them, only those I would view as "conspiracy worthy"), but I make no promises.
