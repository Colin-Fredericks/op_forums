    In-Universe Reasons for why so many Things have Banes?

    05-29-2017, 02:35 AM
    Werewolves are allergic to silver, vampires can be killed with sun and fire, fire also works for Prometheans, spirits suffer from bans, destinies have corresponding dooms, Changelings are allergic to cold iron, True Fae are prohibited from giving their opponents impossible tasks, changeling contracts have catches, slashers have frailties, Proximus dynasties have curses, the Underworld's rivers are both mystically potent and largely poisonous, and there's probably more examples that I'm not remembering.

    It seems to be a rule that things with magical power always have weaknesses, from an out-of-universe perspective this is mostly to allow clever characters to defeat opponents they can't overpower, to prevent players from ignoring the threat posed by hunters, and to force certain NPCs (spirits, slashers etc.) to behave in-character.

    For an occultist living in the Chronicles, how do they explain this phenomena? For Changelings, it seems straightforward to say that the Wyrd thinks it's boring for the strong to always win and arranges scenarios where they can be defeated. Mages probably blame the Abyss somehow But what about say Promethean mystics or Ordo Dracul scholars? What theories could a character who notices this trend come up with?

                                              A credible related theory- most supernatural creatures are immune/resistant to disease. A sign of supernatural power... or a sign that disease is humanity's bane?

                                              To answer the question, some theories:
                                              • The godmachine/exarchs/both did it, ensuring supernaturals have reason not to just rule the world in broad daylight.
                                              • Vampires were, seemingly, created by a curse- so were all the rest, and the banes remind them that they are not meant to be gods.
                                              • It all comes from arcadia and runs on story logic, and what good is a story with an invincible character?,
                                              • The underworld infests everything, ensuring all supernaturals have some rot in their nature. Now everything, even immortals, can die.
                                              • The existence of prometheans and pandorans is a metaphysical abomination, and has spread flux everywhere, corrupting and breaking the supernatural.
                                              • The abyss doesn't just stop the supernal- it tries to stop all magic, and these are its ways of dealing with the rest.
