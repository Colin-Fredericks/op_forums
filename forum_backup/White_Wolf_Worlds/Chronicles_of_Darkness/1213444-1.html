        So I took another stab at the whole "reworking Imbued Items" thing. Dice pools are still abysmal for the non-Awakened unless the Imbued Item has a persistent spell enchanted within it, but given the dangers of Dissonance and Quiessence, it's probably safer that way.
        Modified Attainment- Imbue Item

        The mage can infuse an item with magic, creating an Imbued Item. The mage can imbue an item with any spell she is capable of casting. Imbued items retain their magic indefinitely; a spell cast using an imbued item might run out, but the imbued item itself does not. Attainments may only be included if they modify a spell; for example, mages can imbue items with spells using Sympathetic Range, but not Mage Armor or the ability to create Rotes. The spell imbued into an item remains in the item’s creator’s spell control, even if the item only casts that spell when activated and only takes up one spell control “slot.” Multiple spells may be imbued into one item using the rules for Combined Spells. If the mage retains control over an item’s spell, she may alter its Spell Factors, and even cancel it. By spending Mana equal to the item’s rating, a mage can destroy an imbued item she retains spell control over, no matter where it is.

        Note- Unsafe Relinquishing: An Imbued Item relinquished unsafely does not have unsafe Reach allocated, and as such rolls a chance die. Each success adds a Reach to the Imbued Item’s pool, meaning that such Imbued Items can become dangerously powerful before spontaneously losing all power with an Exceptional Success.

        Cost: Mana equal to the Imbued Item’s dot rating Dice Pool: Gnosis + Prime - dot rating
        Action: Special; as Equipment Building

        Dramatic Failure: The item is imbued, but it is cursed. The object has a different effect, and a different trigger, than the caster was intending.
        Failure: The item’s magic is unstable. The item gains the Fragile or Volatile Condition.
        Success: The Mage imbues the item and it functions as intended. The enchanter decides if the item has a persistent spell on it, or if it needs to be activated.
        Exceptional Success: The item receives an unexpected benefit (such as the 9-again quality to the spellcasting roll, or the benefits of being a Praxis). Alternatively, the enchanting Mage may relinquish the Imbued Item from their spell control without a point or dot of Willpower.

        Imbued Item (• to •••••)
        Effect: An Imbued Item is an object enchanted by an Adept of Prime. The spell costs whatever Mana it normally would, and at least one Mana even if it normally would be costless, calculating Reach as though the spell was cast at the minimum Arcana rating. The item stores Mana equal to its dot rating, which it replenishes either through other spells, or at a rate of one point per day when stored in the presence of a Resonant Hallow. Imbued Items roll their dot rating as the spellcasting dice pool if they are activated and do not have spell control, meaning every spell cast after the first costs Reach. Awakened users add Gnosis and suitable Yantras to the roll. They can spend the item’s Mana instead of their own to mitigate Paradox, but only up to their per turn limit. Paradoxes from an Imbued Item cannot be contained. Paradox dice accrue at a rate of one die per unsafe Reach for non-Awakened users. If an Imbued Item includes an Arcanum Attainment, increase its dot rating by one. If you want to have the spell cast at a higher Arcanum level than its base (allowing for more free Reach to be assigned and better free Spell Factor) add a dot. Imbued Items can include multiple spells using the rules for Combined Spells so long as the item’s total dots spent on Arcana and Attainments doesn’t exceed five. Researching an Imbued Item's triggers requires an Investigation with Clue elements equal to its dot rating.

        I also took a stab at the whole "Artifacts as pseudo-Legacies" thing. One of the things I like about this is that it makes Artifacts much more valuable to the Awakened than just "fancy magical effects, Mana, and a +1 Yantra", especially because of the ways that Attainments differ from standard Supernal magic. Beyond that, this way the most powerful Artifacts can have up to ten different unique and strange abilities, and grant two Arcana up to the level of Mastery to their wielders. It also makes Sleepwalkers with the Relic Attuned Merit spectacularly dangerous pseudo-Awakened.

        Artifact (•• to ••••• •••••)
        Effect: Solidified Resonance and Mana transforms a symbol from the Supernal World into a physical object. Artifacts possess Gnosis equal to their dot rating, and two Ruling Arcana with dot ratings equal to half Gnosis, rounded up. They count as +2 Path Yantras for the Paths with their Arcana as Ruling Arcana or +3 if the Artifact’s Gnosis is greater than the Mage who’s wielding it. Artifacts can store Mana up to their Gnosis-based maximum, and have their own Oblations by which they may gather up to their Arcanum rating per day. Artifacts possess a number of Attainments (that follow the rules for Legacy Attainments, see Mage: The Awakening pg. 198) dependent on their Gnosis and Arcana ratings. If the Artifact contains only one, high-Gnosis, Attainment, reduce its dot rating by one. Mages may use their, or the Artifact’s, Gnosis and Arcana in any combination, and may even wield the Artifact’s traits for use in Creative Thaumaturgy. Each of the Artifact’s Attainments possess unique triggers that can be researched using an Investigation requiring a number of Clue elements equal to twice its dot rating to discover without complication.

            What about this for a hack for Unsafely Relinquishing Imbued Items:

            Their Mana supply becomes fixed, so once it runs out, the Imbuement ends. The Spell Factors and such are fixed, since it was Relinquished, and the periodic decay Unsafely Relinquished spells go through makes the whole thing unstable and gives it a shelf life. Works best for "magic potions/scrolls" and deliberately short-term items.
