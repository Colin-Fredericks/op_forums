                  They're very different. The dampyr labor under a double curse: their nature is a curse upon the dampyr and a curse upon the Kindred.                       I really enjoyed their concept and how the fit into vampire society in Half-Damned.

                      Basically they were the half dead and half alive children of vampires and mortals. This unusual physiology gave them benefits from both worlds. They converted firearms etc. to bashing, had extremely extended lifespans, had a blood pool and their own form of generation so to speak, got dots it disciplines, etc. They could also eat food, walk in the sunlight without issues, heal like a normal human could, etc.

                      They had no fangs and did not have the kiss. They could eat raw meat (or drink blood from storage, etc) to regain blood points, heal, etc.

                      Their nicknames were Jade Walkers.

                      Being one usually made you an outcast in vampire society - but you weren't outright Hunted, etc. You kind of lived on the outskirts - like the elephant in the room or a dirty little secret. Your vampire parent was usually ahamed of you though a lot of the half damned who wanted to be a part of Vampire society usually served their vampire parent in someway to get their foot in the door.

                      They had some specific merits - like Face which acted as status in vampire society. Then they had some they gave them fangs, etc.

                      Just a pretty great interpretation of them in my opinion.

                      I am hoping to give them a treatment in Chronicles.

                                  My vision of dhampir is something a little more high-powered, closer to actual vampire level. They heal fast (on par with werewolves), they can learn Disciplines, they can raise Blood Potency potentially to 10 (though my initial idea is that they can only go up to 5 without diablerie), and they age more slowly. As a rather longtime fangirl of Vampire Hunter D, I wouldn't mind if they could "vamp out" and get a temporary power boost, but I did make them susceptible to frenzy and all it entails. I'd actually create special Merits for them (the short fiction piece I wrote here is an example) as well as Disciplines or Devotions (or both) unique to dhampirs. I had a few other things, but I don't want to get too into it yet.

                                  Dhampirs are the subject of my very first foray into the industry, so they hold a special place in my non-staked heart.

                                          I had it so that their lifespans increase as Blood Potency rises. They also naturally produce Vitae, albeit slowly, and it takes longer the more powerful they are. They can feed, but don't need to, although they gain some serious bonuses.

                                          I wanted to toy with the idea that although they are "half-Damned," they're actually disturbingly idealized predators. Perfected, if you will. They have many of the strengths of humans and vampires, and no Beast. A ferocity in their hearts, sure, but that comes from being a perfect (supernatural) predator. The implications would actually be sort of disturbing to both dhampirs and vampires, that this impossible progeny (the name of the little pitch I wrote up) is almost like it was meant to be. They're the true power of the Blood when married to a vital, living form. It's why I wanted them to be more of a full template, because that's just how I like my half-vampires.

                                                  Well, they do have their own issues. They're prone to bouts of rage like vampire frenzy, they suffer from variants of their parent clan's bane, and they suffer from thirst for blood. They can compete with true vampires (and the old story hook of dhampirs hunting vampires holds true here), or even contend with other creatures of the night. Doing so requires effort, risks danger, and both of those things tax a dhampir. They need Vitae. The more they fight, the more they hunt or strive with vampires, the more they must feed, or else struggle greatly to keep up. So for those who hunt bloodsucking monsters, it's a slippery slope to becoming one in turn.

                                                          Yeah, if you want to try to reduce it down to misleadingly simple terms, sure. Anything could be made to sound not that negative. Werewolves get tremendous amounts of physical power in exchange for getting a little too worked up sometimes. All too easily done.

                                                          Or, you could look at it the other way. Imagine being driven to hunt in a modern world that has left hunter-gatherer society behind. You have a wild ferocity in your heart, an inability to ignore what's in the shadows. An unholy hunger always gnaws at the back of your mind. You fight the urge to feed on people, your people, fellow humans. The rush and power of Vitae is just as alluring to a dhampir as it is a vampire. Because of their inherent volatility, these are passionate creatures; there are no timid dhampirs, no easy life for the half-Damned. I'd probably have them have some uneasy relationship with the day, certainly at least more comfortable at night. Theirs is a lifetime of struggle, wrestling with the power of the Blood and resisting the temptation to feed—or giving in.

                                                          They suffer from other weaknesses as I mentioned above. And they don't have all the vampire's strengths. Sure, they heal fast (not like a Gauru werewolf, though), but they don't shrug off damage like vampires do. They aren't immune to hunger, fatigue, and while they endure toxins and harsh elements better than mortals thanks to their gifts, they're not immune to those. They must sleep, they must breathe, and contend with pain like mortals do. Ultimately, these things may all "balance out," and they're about on part with vampires, but that's not necessarily the goal. I wouldn't mind it, though. They are different, and in ways that make for good stories, which is fine by me. I wrote up everything in more detail in my pitch, but I don't want to share it all here yet.

                                                          Edit: Also, imagine flying into a rage because some guy harasses you at a bar, and you beat him to within an inch of his life. As he lies there, his face battered, his blood covering your knuckles, you feel that pulsing heart, you taste the sweet scent of blood on the air, and it'd be so easy ... even if no one is watching. Maybe you leave before the cops arrive, and then wait for his friends to haul him out into the parking lot. How bad do you want that precious lifeblood? How hot does your anger yet burn? Can you control your savage urges?
