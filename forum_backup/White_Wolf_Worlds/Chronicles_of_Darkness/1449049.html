    Chronicle concept: short waves, toadstools and hodags

    05-19-2021, 05:40 PM
    I've got the beginnings of what I think will be a pretty cool story. I figured I'd cast about on here and see if anyone had any suggestions, specifics or tangential ideas:

    Setting: the Wisconsin Northwoods. I imagine the bulk of the game will take place in Monico (a town of around 300) and Rhinelander (10,000 or so) and the forests and lakes around them.

    Genre: trying to go for a psychological thriller with elements of governmental conspiracy and of survival horror, with little dabs of campfire/ghost stories and faerie tales mixed in.

    Tone: paranoia or isolation. Not really sure which will take precedence yet.

    The core concepts:

    - each character will have been given a key at some point in the past. A grandfather passes it down to them as they come of age. It's left to them in their mother's last will and testament. It appears in a padded envelope in their mailbox out of the blue.
    Some of the characters are from the area, some may be outsiders who received instructions along with their key.

    - there's a short wave radio transmitter, fairly deep in the woods. It's a numbers station, constantly broadcasting since WWI.

    - there is *something* in the woods. People talk about a pack of timber wolves that have become desensitized to human activity, or a deranged survivalist living off the grid with his dogs. Or jus a stray cow.

    - some of the people in town are under some sort of pressure to ignore certain things or maintain the status quo. A sheriff, a pastor.

    - fungi. Falling asleep to wake up in a ring of mushrooms. Toadstools growing in your closet, basement or bathroom overnight. Psychotropic effects and a sense of something trying to communicate with you.

    So basically, I'm thinking of combining unknowable, alien horror with classic faerie tropes and the more mundane horror of a brutal and uncaring wilderness.

    I was thinking the keys were all given out to these disparate people by one person or organization; a sort of changing of the guard, to keep a gate/portal/door closed (the door of the station, presumably).
    I'm tempted to play with time travel a bit with the keys; the person/people who sent them out did so on the future/past/outside of time, but that is a pretty complex bit of storytelling for me, so I'm not sure.

    Obviously, I don't have a very clear idea what's going on just yet. But I figured posting what I have so far might yield even more ideas.

    I'm also always open to unrelated eerie occurances. I was thinking about a late-night local station that occasionally seems to be talking directly to you. And maybe a mothman-style thing, with the winding forest roads and other car's taillights. And something in one of the lakes?

    What do you think?

          Well, it doesn't look like there will be much in the way of feedback. But this will still be a useful sounding-board as the story takes shape.

          Our cast so far:

          - a veteran of the Gulf War who lives as far off-grid as he can manage. Haunted, suspicious and tough.
          At the end of the war, he was approached by a ragged, unstable man who handed him a padded manilla envelope. Inside were some vague, ominous warnings and a rusty old key with a paper tag that reads 1-9-8-2.

          - an ex-cop turned lumber yard security, a guy trying his best and hard-pressed to succeed. He saw some stuff on the force that made him question the justice system. The day before he resigned, he found a padded manilla envelope on his desk. Inside were some vague, ominous warnings and a rusty old key with a paper tag that reads 1-9-8-2.

          - a programmer who, seeking a life of adventure, left his cushy office job to work with the Department of Natural Resources...in programming. A novice outdoorsman at best, his co-workers laughed and brush off his story about a strange occurrence in the woods one night.
          The next day, he received a padded manilla envelope in the mail. Inside were some vague, ominous warnings and a rusty old key with a paper tag that reads 1-9-8-2.

          - the owner of Windsong, an alternative medicine and new age spiritualism shop in the city. Sharp and a bit persnickety, with considerable knowledge of herbs, medicinal and otherwise.
          When they received the keys to the place from the original owner, the ring had a key for the main locks, one for the register and one that was old and rusty, with a paper tag that reads 1-9-8-2.

          - a bored student who, yearning for something more in this dull world, turned to the Ouija, Tarot and other such devices. It's mostly a hobby; they've never experienced anything to make them a believer. But oh, how they want to be.
          A distant uncle, hearing that they had taken an interest in radio waves (really, it was just messing around with EVP), sends them an old short-wave radio. Tapped to the top a rusty old key with a paper tag that reads 1-9-8-2.

          - a Wellness Coach and Insagram Influencer who doesn't actually believe in any of this hokey nonsense.
          They received a letter in the mail one day, a padded manilla envelope. Inside were some vague, ominous warnings and a rusty old key with a paper tag that reads 1-9-8-2.

          - a short order cook at a greasy, run-down bar and grill who wants to get out of this town and make something of themselves.
          One night, a new face blew into the bar. He seemed haggard and agitated. Before he left--without ordering anything--he gave the cook a padded manilla envelope. Inside were some vague, ominous warnings and a rusty old key with a paper tag that reads 1-9-8-2.

              Things are finally solidifying.

              - there is a tunnel/secret underground complex (elements of a bomb shelter and a green house sort of mashed together) near the radio station. Each key opens one of the doors inside.
              Some of the rooms look like abandoned fungi grow projects. Others look like old barracks, but they've been abandoned in the damp dark for long enough they're resembling mushroom gardens as well.

              - investigating into the radio station will quickly reveal that it's still under surveillance by at least one branch of the government. At least, they seem like a branch of the government.

              - the *something* in the woods could be the result of a government project gone wrong. Some kind of carnivorous, parasitic fungus?
              Or maybe that radio broadcast was received by something from another world. And it answered.
              Or maybe the tunnel was too deep and unearthed something that was sleeping in the dark earth.

              - the mushrooms look like they could maybe be part of some MKULtra spinoff. Unlock the true potential of the human mind, Stoned Ape theory, connecting to the collective consciousness of the natural world, etc.
              Or something from another world? Something drawn by the signals or through the tunnel, or something that manipulated humanity into sending/digging said things to allow it to come here?

              - consuming a certain type of mushroom within the tunnel results in an intense psychotropic experience. Lucid dreams and hallucinations and something that certainly feels like time travel.
              The characters travel to different dates in 1982. Each use puts some kind of strain on their psyches (maybe it drains a Willpower each time, then each time after the first you have to roll...Resolve + Stamina or you have a -1 to certain rolls, etc.? I think it would be cool to tell them the penalty only lasts for X amount of time, but to put enough pressure on them that they feel like they can't wait it out.)

              - on March 13, 1982 something went wrong at the station/in the tunnels. An experiment failed, a test went awry. Something broke down or blew up.
              Maybe that's when they lost funding? Maybe it was the lack of maintenance that caused problems later.

              - on March 15, 1982 there was some kind of local tragedy. A fire, a storm, a missing child.
              This should somehow set in motion the events of the current timeline, so that the players can try to go back and prevent it. But I'm also thinking there will be a pattern to recognize and to see that it's happening again.

                  A few more ideas:

                  - the numbers station is the U.S.'s attempt to replicate a code that was used during WWII, but not for communication with Army Intelligence. There are a few bare snippets of information that suggest someone had broadcasted a signal (off-planet?), and that *something* had replied.

                  - there are more effigy mounds in Wisconsin than anywhere else in the U.S. Uncovered from one were a few shards of pottery, possibly containing the missing pieces of the code.

                  - using the mushrooms to travel through time allows the fungus to release spores into the character's brain. The effects are almost unnoticeable at first, but eventually lead to severe mental and physical health issues. And possibly becoming unstuck in time.

                  - on March 13, the station sent out a reply to a complex coded message from an unknown source.
                  On March 15, a child went missing during a bad storm. The search party racked up hundreds of man hours over the next two weeks, but no one ever found anything. Not a body, not a scrap of clothing, not a single hair.

                  - if the players prevent the child from being stolen, they find that history repeats itself. Only this time, more children go missing (2? 4? 10?).
                  If they want to stop whatever is going on, they need to stop (or modify) the response sent on the short waves.

                  - the government will react to them at different levels of resistance, depending on how many times they've used the substances to travel in time or what their goals are ("Cancel that. Stand down. They're just here for the Jacobs kid." or "No, no need for that. Their timelines are still untangled. They haven't gone that far down the hole yet.")

                              Okay, it's been forever but I've finally gotten to a point where I can start this thing.

                              Four of the seven players I invited have incompatible schedules, so it'll just be three players: the veteran, the owner of the Windsong and a wannabe Yoga influencer.

                              I've settled into a more concrete central concept, though I'm not sure how much of it will be understood by the time the game is over:

                              The mycellium network (the root-like system of fungi) beneath the forest floor connects all of the plant and fungal life in a way that is very similar to the way neurons are connected within a brain.
                              The Network is a brain. A massive, utterly alien brain with inscrutable goals and desires. Many--perhaps all--people in the area (or in the world?) are a part of the Network as well. Fungal spores release phramaones and take root in grey matter, and the Network has been slowly, subtly manipulating the human population since they first walked upon the earth and breathed it's air.

                              The Agency (looking for a suitably ominous and vague name for them that condences to a short acronym, a la FBI, CIA, etc.) used Growl PSK to communicate with the Network, using the whole of the forest as a massive receiver.
                              They have made some kind of deal with the Network--every 40 years, they offer it a human child (cue themes of faerie and alien abduction).
                              What the Agency gets out of the deal is further attempts to understand what they at first see as a potential source of power or a biological weapon, but later (when they realize the breadth and scope of it's influence) they are simply trying to appease it, like frightened cultists making sacrifices to an uncaring god.

                              So the game will consist mostly of the players trying to unravel the mystery around the numbers station, the missing children, etc. while avoiding the attention of the Agency and those under the influence of the Network.
                              Been doing a lot of reading on running mystery games and the like. The Angry GM is invaluable, as usual. The Alexandrian isn't normally as helpful for me, but the Three Clue Rule and the concept of Revelations were great points.

                              Now I need to solidify the essential plot points (i.e. the scenes that are necessary for the game to be complete), the revelations (and at least three clues for each) and how they might be connected (looks like largely a branching structure with some open elements and a couple linear ones).

                              I've got a list of people from different parts of town they can meet, places they can dig around for clues.

                              I'm not sure how this will go. Especially the time travel aspect related to the varient fly agaric mushrooms in the underground facility.

                              I've also got the idea that there's *something* in the woods. A distant, thin wailing. A sudden rumbling crash like a freight train in the forest. A thousand people and beasts screaming and growling and dying--but it's just "soft places" in time and space, most of them sort of worm holes leading to the night of the blizzard when the children are taken.

                              Still not 100% on a system for repeated consumption of the mushrooms. I was thinking you lose one Willpower after each trip and that you get a "spore point" or something. After 24 hours it goes away, unless you consume another dose in that time. If you go 24 hours with spore points, it's a breaking point (-1 for each point you have).

                              Thoughts and comments welcome.
