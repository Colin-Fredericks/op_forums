    Originally posted by Caladriu View Post
    I don't even remember where I saw it, I think it was in some long-forgotten Actual Play, but my favorite depiction of the Exarchs is just a gigantic, middle-aged, butt naked human being that's visibly and obviously completely out of place in the Supernal Realms. You'd only get glimpses of their elbow or earlobe or kneecap, they're always obscured by clouds or conveniently placed objects or weird camera angles. They speak in loud, booming voices but stutter and second-guess themselves and double back on their words and generally sound like you're distracting them from a very important phone call. Think Wizard of Oz, man behind the curtain, on a cosmic scale. Nothing about them says "these are the right beings to be in charge of reality." An audience with an Exarch shouldn't be funny exactly, but awkward, uncomfortable, underwhelming, vaguely pathetic.

    It's really, really important to me that the Exarchs are just a bunch of assholes, just like you, who by complete chance happened to be the first ones up the Ladder before they kicked it down. There's nothing majestic or awe-inspiring about them in the flesh.

    That's definitely an interesting way to get that particular theme across, and I love it.

    However, as to the more specific question about Ministers seeing their Exarchs and reporting directly to them, recall that it says they report directly. However, that doesn't necessarily mean direct communion face-to-face. It could be they speak through scrying portals, but never see more than the Exarch's seal. It could be they commune through the aforementioned hole-in-a-soul, and it's a voice they hear. It could be that they get text messages on a Supernal Cellphone in their head. They're still contacting them directly, but don't ever have to see the Exarch.

    Moses once got to see God, at his request as a favor; the Lord allowed this, but only allowed the prophet to see His back, for to see His face would kill any mortal, even a prophet, and thus God blocked the cave Moses was in with His hand as He passed. Moses was speaking directly to God, and saw His manifestations (a cloud, the burning bush, and a voice that spoke to him), but never got to see God head on. At the very least, even if their true presence is not so destructive, it seems to me that the Exarchs would take inspiration from this, and never actually allow even their Ministers on the Fallen World to see their true form. They would hide it behind symbols and masks and speak with magically amplified booming voices; they would appear (if they chose to do so in their communions with the Ministers) as clouds or avatars. They would appear majestic, if they chose to make themselves visible in the mortal plane.

    But it would never be their true form. It would always be another layer of symbols to hide the truth of their existence, and to make them feel divine. The Ministers still commune with them directly, but it is not face-to-face, unadulterated.

        I think my take on the Exarchs is the opposite of where this thread has gone: Of course the Exarchs are majestic, incredible symbols, fractal depictions of misery and tyranny that can only be described even slightly by the intricate iron seals they present. Not because the Exarchs themselves were, in life, necessarily the most majestic and powerful (though, even if they were once human, they were the humans who thought to become the symbols of tyranny, even if they came to power with the Star Ladder). They are that way because it is the nature of the Supernal to imbue things with meaning, and anything which exists within the Supernal is either torn apart on a semiotic level or deified. Ascended mages become a living symbol, and other mages can use their symbolism as yantras, and the Exarchs are that to the umpteenth power. No matter what they once were, the Exarchs are nothing human now. They are also loathsome - every element of their symbolism declares 'you will kneel, worm' and they embody monstrous abuses of power to maintain their hold.
        In short - the Exarchs are entire symbolic languages of degradation and cruel strength, and to downplay them seems to me to miss the point. They did win. They did become the new gods, the rulers of the world. They are, in fact, divine now. And they shouldn't be, at all, because they are petty and vicious and horrible, no matter how majestic their symbolism.

        Edit to clarify: Basically, I think the Exarchs should in fact be terrible and beautiful to behold, because throughout history tyrants and tyranny have had a majesty, poetry written in their honor, the works. And that doesn't mean their tyranny or power is more acceptable, at all. The Exarchs are gods - but they're monstrous gods. Plenty of Bound should be similarly numinous-and-awful, in less human ways.

                    I disagree that always undercutting is a great move. It can serve to undercut the players' enthusiasm as well. Sometimes, a story needs to be the standard structure, performed as well as possible.
                    I wouldn't, personally, ever have the Exarchs appear to mages except in the shapes of their palaces and their actions in the world. They have no need of embodiment.
                    But if the players managed to get to the point of meeting the Exarchs - Ascending, or some massive plot-ending event - I would give them something dramatic and ennobling, because there's nowhere for the story to go after the Exarchs anyways. Why give them a story that peters off into a bad joke, rather than having a conclusion?
                    Boss Smiley, for the record, isn't particularly absurd in context. He's grotesque because he is, in fact, the symbol underlying everything in the story. The story is, after all, already absurd, and it turns out that the God of the story is the monster, the devil who tempted the savior-figure. I'd argue it's more in line with my read on the Exarchs than the bathos version. :P

                                                Each Exarch is a colossus; a monstrous, misshapen effigy of a human form, assembled out of the symbols that define each of the Iron Seals. The General's flesh is blood-soaked earth, scarified with trenches and barbed wire; its fingers are tank barrels, and its eyes atomic detonations. The Father's face is made of a thousand smaller faces: masks and idols and effigies of every god in whose name humankind has shed blood. Its breath is a wind of sickly-sweet incense, barely hiding the scent of burning flesh. The Raptor is made from a million lesser beasts sewn together -- some living, screaming, tearing at themselves and each other; others dead and decaying, with grotesque flora and fungi sprouting from their bodies.

                                                Barely visible at the heart of each titan is a withered, atrophied, impossibly aged human body -- the last remnant of the human mage it once was. So wizened are these husks that it is impossible discern face, race, or even gender. In any case, the Exarchs themselves have long forgotten who they once were, or what passions drove them to usurp godhood. Nothing remains of them but the joy of having power (and they are joyous) and the fear of losing it (and they are fearful.) Most of the time, they don't even remember what they've lost, let alone regret it.
