    Originally posted by The Storyteller View Post
    Reading this thread and some others I found about blood sorceries, holy crap, that's even worse! Someone described this horrifying curse they could levy on someone a mile away that infected their herd for a month with a disease that made them toxic to vampires. That's too much in the other direction--that's horrible and way too overpowered. If we have three versions of Cruac now (1e, 2e, and blood sorceries), I don't know, maybe we need a fourth.
    I'd like to point out, that such a thing requires tremendous experience amounts. As far as I've seen ,most people who say the system is overpowered, have never actually tried it. Really. Even when ypou have those Themes to back it off, more often than not, you'll be constrained by time and by your own dicepool.

    For example, the aftermentioned ritual: He needs Destruction 2, so that's 2 base successes, You need +2 succs for One Mile Range, +4 Successes for a Month, then you need some successes depending on herd size. But let's say that the herd is 15 people, that's +4 more successes (if it's only 8 people it's +3 succs). And let's not forget that the entire Herd must be within one Mile radius of you for quite a long while. What's the chance of that? But let's say your herd is an ancestral family, and they're conveniently located in a single spot, so the One Mile range will be sufficient. Now you need Potency, how strong the poison actually is gonna be... if you leave it as is, which already is a whooping 12 successes to accomplish, it's gonna be one weak curse. Let's say we want the potency at 3 ( a medium strength ritual, I'd say), which bumps us to 14 needed successes.

    So now, how can we achieve 14 successes reliably? We need to roll around 42 dice(Counting 3 dice -> one succ) That might turn out better.. or it might turn out much worse, because if you roll a single fail in an extended action, that pretty much gives -3 to your further rolls, and all but destroys your attempt at the ritual, unless you're much more competent than that.Oh wait, I completely forgot the resistance. So let's say the highest Composure (or Resolve) in your Herd is 3 (it could be higher, or lower, but really, we need the highest one here). So that means that on average we need 3 more dice in each roll we're making, to come out at 14 succs.

    So, let's say we just needed the 42 dice. That puts us at a dice pool of 7, for 3,5 hours. Without the resistance taken in. The actual stats we need is more like 9 or 10 dice pool. Which is what? 4 attribute, 4 skill and 2 at the Discipline? Or, if we put the attributes in more averages (because I found that players more often bump their Disciplines, not attributes), 3 attribute, 3 skill, 4 discipline? We're talking masters here.

    And this is a very superficial attempt at counting here. I'm too lazy to make maths of how good this actually is, but my gut feeling is that it's even worse, due to the Stumbled Condition which wrecks this ritual, should it come up, unless you account for it and add in 3 more dice, which pretty much means you have almost everything maxed out. Good luck, have fun with that.

    And after all of this - we got one fucked up herd (not even murdered or anything), for a month. And a very angry vampire on the other end.

    Someone with 4 of some other discipline (like Dominate, or Protean) can cause as much damage, in a more reliable way. With that many rolls, chance for one of those to come up a fail is substantially increased and that fucks up everything.

    However, I do agree that those rules need some tweaking, and I did get a house rule or two in (minor).
    And as much as I've played
    (My players, don't read further)
    (and even now, got two players with these disciplines (one Theban, one Cruac), they're fine. If anything, both of them are planning to buy up more normal disciplines/merits, as these Sorceries are quite hard to pull off. Cruac user is planning to get much more obfuscate first, and Theban one is buying up Majesty.


    TL;DR; Blood Sorcery book, does have a fine sorcery, it looks OP on a superficial level when people do not take account of what's possible in play. You know, theoretically a single Dominate user could rule the world by dominating everyone.. somehow that's not an issue.

            Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
            As someone who doesn't know 1e blood magic, hasn't had a chance to see the 2e blood magic rules in action, and have a player who wants to do Cruac, what issues are present in the system?
            Extended ritual casting, with a base time of half an hour. Very few core book rituals last longer than the night. That means that, if you want to actually do anything when you wake up, you have a very limited amount of spells you can prepare for the day. If you have one dot of Cruac, and want to prepare a single level 1 ritual, expect to spend at least an hour and half on it. Then having to shower and get dressed. By the time you're ready to go out and do anything, its practically midnight. Having more dots of Cruac and getting Exceptional Successes can cut down on the time, but that tends to exclude casual casters. Which is bad, because magic is the primary benefit of joining two of five covenants. If you want to cast a few spells AND get something done during the night? Good luck to you.

            Still on the issue of extended casting - rituals aren't exactly viable for spontaneous use in the middle of a scene. Lets say you have a Theban ritual that lets you determine who the liar is in a room. If you want to cast it, you need to effectively pause the scene for an hour. If there's any sort of time crunch, or even impatience, or someone unwilling to sit there while you do your magic? You're in trouble. Any interruption during this time, and the ritual fails.

            Some rituals are impractical to cast with the extended casting. Rigor Mortis inflicts a -3 to a physical action. Which isn't a bad effect in itself; but in practice, spending all that time to penalize the next physical action leaves you little wiggle room to choose what physical action - running around the room with a -3 penalty puts half an hour's work to waste. Cheval requires you to touch a person, meaning that your target has to sit still for the entire casting; this reduces the ritual to someone that is either completely restrained for the entire magic ritual, or someone willing. In which case, you could just as easily wire the target.

            Cruac and Theban have a strong tendacy to have magic that curses people. That's thematically approprite for a vampire. However, whenever you cast a spell that targets another vampire, they feel it through blood sympathy connections. And, by default, Cruac and Theban have a range of 1 mile. You can literally walk away from whomever is casting the ritual and make it out of range before they finish. Even if you pick the wrong direction, and have to walk 2 miles away from the caster by walking a full diameter of the range, the average adult can easily walk two miles within the time it takes an average ritual to complete.

            The base duration of the rituals tend to be either 1 scene, or 1 night. Which is a lot of effort for something that doesn't last long. Rituals in the 1e core book could have durations that lasted several nights, if not longer. Vitae Reliquary seems to be the only ritual that lasts more than a night in the core book. And that lasts one month, instead of the 1e indefinite duration.

            Now, none of these are really that bad when taken individually. Its the sheer weight of them all. Extended casting wouldn't be an issue if we had more month long, or even several night, abilities. But, as written, it effectively means we need to cast our rituals immediately upon sunset if we're to get any benefit.


            Originally posted by Griautis View Post
            So, let's say we just needed the 42 dice. That puts us at a dice pool of 7, for 3,5 hours. Without the resistance taken in. The actual stats we need is more like 9 or 10 dice pool. Which is what? 4 attribute, 4 skill and 2 at the Discipline?             There are several very easy ways for you to boost your dice pool. Both the Circle and Lance have merits that directly improve their dice pools (Mandrake Garden, Library, Altar, Undead Menses). Cruac especially gets lots of bonuses. The core book includes options for additional sacrifices for more dice bonuses - merely spending more vitae or meditating (Cruac and Theban respectively) gives up to +3 dice bonus, and using elaborate sacrifices can net even more.

            Getting lots of dice to offset penalties is not difficult. In fact, given that we're talking about a system that's based on Mage, its kind of the assumption here.

            TL;DR; Blood Sorcery book, does have a fine sorcery, it looks OP on a superficial level when people do not take account of what's possible in play. You know, theoretically a single Dominate user could rule the world by dominating everyone.. somehow that's not an issue.
            Generally, the concern isn't trying to curse a wide number of people for a month (which, when we get down to it, is actually a pretty powerful effect for a vampire; generally, that'd be a level 4-5 discipline effect). The concern is a magician spending one day a month to ritually prepare Shielding and personal enhancements that put vampires that focus on Resilience, Celerity, and Vigor to shame, invisibility that makes Obfuscate redundant, and let you be just as charming as someone using Majesty on top of all the other magical effects you have up your sleeve.

            The book flat out says that it is based on Mage rules, and yeah, Themes can do anything that a Mage can. And that's the problem - mages can do pretty much anything that a vampire can do with enough time and preparation. Themes might not be as good at on-the-fly effects as Disciplines, but you can ritually prepare effects that can make you a match for any Discipline. And, unlike Mage, there's no limit beyond time to the number of spells you can use to buff yourself. You can use divinations to know what threats you'll face, as well, so that also a thing. C            ursing people is actually one of the more difficult things in Themes. Self-buffing, protection, and divinations are actually far more effective uses of Themes.

            Now, it is more than possible to play with Themes in a way that doesn't overshine anyone else. But there IS a high potential for abuse in a way. And I've talked to several others who've used Themes in their Vampire game, and have recieved reports that, in those games, it was very overpowered, and was cautioned should only be used in games that include only Theban/Cruac user for PCs. So, for some games and styles of play, Theme magic is OP on more than a superficial level.

                Originally posted by The Storyteller View Post
                The ritual in question was built and explained using Creation 3, Destruction 2, and Divination 2 (not that I know what these things means).
                Themes basically do what they say. If you want a magical ritual that either creates or summons something, then you need the Creation Theme. If you want to do something that involves hurting someone (inflict damage, reduce stats) or destroy physical objects, that requires Destruction Themes. Divination... normally, that uses magic to predict the future, read the past, etc. In this case, however? Its there strictly for "trigger effects." It makes the magic hang around until the vampire performs a certain action.

                From the sounds of it, the ritual uses Creation 3 to "summon" a disease. This makes the vampire a plague carrier, and infects everyone he feeds on. Destruction 2 makes the disease toxic to vampires. And Divination to make sure it only affects the one vampire and not create a massive plague to kill everyone else off. Yeah. The spell is more destructive with less knowledge. And the worst part? This is actually just combining two examples written in the book. Its not abusing the rules, its using them literally as written on the page.

                For the sake of completion, there are two other Themes - Protection and Transmuation. Protection creates protections against any attempts to harm (weapons, environment, whatever) or heal. This includes protection against falling asleep. At protection 5, you can protect against banes. Transmutation pretty much covers everything else - manipulating vitae, buffing people, making people fall asleep, transforming objects into other things, and more.

                Like Graius said, there are practical limits that come into play in terms of preparation and time. However, if you have the time and correct Themes, there is little you can't do.
