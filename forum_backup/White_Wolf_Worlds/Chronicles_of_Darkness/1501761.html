    Seeking Feedback: Making Splats for Fetch

    01-30-2023, 08:18 PM
    So I've mentioned a time or two that I've been running a game centered around a group of Fetches dealing with their counterparts coming back from Arcadia, and I wanted to do some more work to build them as character options (my eventual goal being to release a supplement about playing Fetch on the Storyteller's Vault).

    My initial bunch of splats, covering what the Fetch is made out of, works well, but I wanted to add a secondary splat in the COD tradition of "1 Splat for physical characteristics, 1 Splat for social groupings."

    I was stuck on this for a while, since I don't envision Fetch banding together regularly unless they know what they are, and I wanted something that any Fetch could make use of, regardless of whether they know what they are or not. But since a big theme of this game revolves around identity and choices and The Path Not Taken, I thought that a Splat representing the path The Fetch's life has taken since they were put in their counterpart's place would help reinforce that.

    So I'm working up the concept of Roads (as in "how many road must a man walk down?") as the secondary splat for Fetch.

    I'm taking cues from Demon and Promethean -- I envision each of these roads being modular and subject to change over the course of a game. To do that, I'm taking the idea of Demon Agendas adding a Condition to the character that grants beats under certain conditions and can be cashed in once per chapter for some kind of bonus. Like Promethean Refinements, I don't expect the PCs to be "locked in" to their choice of Road.

    The Roads I've come up with are:

    The Cuckoo -- The Fetch that tries their best to be the person they are "supposed" to be. This is a Fetch that tries their utmost to live up to their Persona (which is like a Changeling's Needle).

    The Wastrel -- The Fetch that failed, and now focuses on indulging their flaws. This is a Fetch that tends to act closer to their Shadow (which is like a Changeling's Thread).

    The Tryhard -- The Fetch that focuses on what they feel they're best at, to an almost obsessive degree. This is a Fetch that I think is tied more to their Skills (I've been considering Skill Tricks from Mirrors)

    The Prodigal -- The Fetch that really has no interest in who they once were, and goes off to do Something Completely Different with their lives. I'm not sure quite what to tie them to.

    The Vandal -- The Monster Fetch, the one that's interested in destroying their former life, metaphorically or literally. I've tentatively linked this with their Integrity (though I've been thinking of using Humanity instead)

    (The names are not supposed to be complementary -- the conceit I'm using it that these are terms that members of the Autumn Court use to describe the behavior of Fetches they've studied, and reflects the typical changeling's low opinion of the Fetch)

    Where I'm stuck right now is on if I've got the right mix of types for character development, and also trying to figure out what each of the Conditions I build for the Roads actually DO, so any suggestions or ideas folks have would be appreciated. Feel free to ask me stuff, as that can help me jog my brain.

                      Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                      Well, let's backtrack. Why do you want splats for fetches, and what do you want them to do for fetches?
                      1. This Chronicle is meant to expand Fetches into a PC option. So it's for the same reason any of the other games in the CoD have splats -- it's a group your character can belong to that defines their powers, suggested skills, and often something about their personality. Splats help lower the analysis paralysis inherent to character creation, and give mental hooks for players to hang characters off of. Heck, the base Changeling game has 3 different splats (Seeming, Kith and Court).

                      2. I already have one set of character splats, based on the ideas presented in Autumn Nightmares about the ingredients of a Fetch having an effect on their personality. However, MOST splats in the CoD have two splats -- one that's more "Nature" (Seeming/Kith, Clan, Auspice, Path, Lineage, Incarnation, Etc.) and one that reflects Nurture (Court, Covenant, Tribe, Order, Refinement, Agenda, Etc.).

                      3. The Bundles (what I call the Splat that is "This Fetch is made primarily of this stuff) are the ones where I've done the most mechanical work -- they provide an extra Echo, a bonus to one Attribute, a skill specialty, and a drawback. However, putting all the mechanical benefits in the Bundle seems kinda bio-essentialist in hindsight, so I want to give more mechanical weight to how the life a Fetch has lived has shaped them.

                      4. I want the Roads to provide a benefit that focuses more on personality and life choices. I settled on the "This splat provides a Condition" model based on the Agendas from Demon because it's a clean way of containing mechanics that might shift during play -- people's lives change drastically when their circumstances and desires change, after all, so I wanted each of the Roads to be easy to switch between Stories. Want to change your Road to represent a story event or a character arc? Just resolve the existing Road's Condition and select a new Road when you'd normally get the Condition reapplied. it provides the same sort of shifting character arcs that Promethean Refinements do, without the inelegant mess of completely changing your character's powers around that I find Refinements can cause unless you Calcify your Alembics.

                      5. The Roads also help reinforce the theme I'm aiming for, which is a meditation on how the choices a Fetch makes affects their existence as compared to how their lives could have gone if the Gentry had never captured their counterpart. Everyone has had events happen in their lives that has shifted their perspective and the trajectory of their lives, even if that change was to rededicate themselves to their current circumstances. This theme is important enough to me to want to represent it mechanically

                      So my main goal here is to identify any gaps I have in the "lifepaths" I've outlined, and then determine what those mechanical effects actually are.

                          I use Echoes, though I add more detail on them than simply taking the ones straight from the book. The character's Bundle (for instance) has an effect on the Echo, just like a changeling's Seeming can alter their Contracts. PC Fetches get a free Echo based on their Bundle, and can buy new Echoes with Experiences (though they're limited).

                          The main Echo I've had an issue with is Mimic Contract, which is leagues better than most of the other Echoes at Wyrd 2 if you're a PC. The main thing I've done is to give this echo to ALL my PCs, but I want to provide an option for a game that is less "Changeling-Lite" and more "Hunter-or-Mortal-But-With-Minor-Powers,"

                          I've expanded the list of Echoes considerably, throwing in some Merits and Bestowments from Promethean at the higher Wyrd scores (which is where I see Fetches getting more and more supernatrual). I've found that Promethean is a wonderful resource to crib ideas from for a Chronicle centered around artificial humanoids.

                                              1. One day I will not have to run into this surface level* argument from homebrewers and I will have died earlier that day. A lot of the rest of your reasoning is actually very sound and I want to get to that, so I hope you can forgive me if I have the gripe as quickly, and hopefully as usefully, as I can.

                                              The concept of "All templates must have multiple splats" is a misconception that ignores that there are many differing layers of breadth and complexity for various character types-First Edition even had two major templates, the Purified and Dark Heroes, who never the less didn't have any splats, and other templates can get quite variable in their number, like Changeling having 6 X-Splats(and at a time, 7) but Demon only having 4 initially. The need for definitive splats is a subject related to the game's scope, just how broad the territory a playable character's forms and iterations could be-there is a reason why Changeling has always flirted on the high end of X-splat numbers, and that's because even getting as archetypal as one can, fairy tales have a very broad number of basic character types that maintain cohesion. One of the reasons why some homebrews flare out is that they pick a concept too narrow to be broken apart into 5 distinct forms, and then struggle to fill them out meaningfully or make stuff that's flat and uninteresting enough to get public support. In general, it pays more to go the other way an develop a single idea and see if any particular growths start to be weighty without feeling like the default. I've run into this problem with a part of the Clade Companion.

                                              Without some of the expanded material that you're playing with and the extended logic you're drawing from, fetches as a playable character would almost be better served with a B-Splat/Dyad style or presentation, or as a Merit Template-in fact, the Merit Template is a pretty solid model for someone whose power stat is dependent on someone else's growth.

                                              All of that said!

                                              2. While the argument presented leans on the surface understanding, it's not too hard to see the soft influence of Promethean on Autumn Nightmare's section on fetch materials with Bundles even if I didn't remember your interest in the subject from The Seven Wonders, and thus fetch's fairy tale shadow of that game in the form of "I will be my own boy" to Promethean's "I'm will be a real boy" is pretty easy to see a weighty and complex enough form to draw and build from and justify the choices.

                                              3. I'm not sure if it's too bio-essentialist for Bundles to drag you one way if Roads are there for you to struggle in another-in fact, that's arguably the core of the struggle, the way that the Fae want you to be versus who you want to be for yourself, that in fact the stuff that you are can be thought of as actively dragging you back into being literal pawn piece-and in fact, if you want to make an argument for an expanded powerset rooted in Roads that contrast the Echoes a fetch projects from their changeling's presence, I'd honestly say more power to you.

                                              4. Which leads to my main advice, which is to beg, steal, and borrow from Refinements, and see how they would come up and manifest when reimagined from the fairy tale genre. There's something of the fairy tale princess in Aurum, a savage beast that can soothed in Stannum, the knight in Ferrum, so on and so forth. Not all of them will match, and certainly not as they are, but a lot of them are and a lot of them will, and there' is something Blue Fairy-ish in the path of Roles, and something in the idea of a Wish that can only be known the more they know who they are, who will have always been beyond the Strangers's whim. Maybe more tasks or quests, with your calcified alembics instead being proofs of your journey. Something kind of Legend of Zelda-y in that.

                                              5. I pretty much have covered this so far, so, uh. "A dream is a wish your heart makes",but "what is a heart, anyways?"

                                              Of your original Road ideas, the only I would argue is non definitive enough to drop is the Prodigal, though a specific "antithesis of the changeling" might fit there. I think having a Road that's not so much a Road as a wanderless state isn't a bad thing, but I feel like we'd probably have to slice down further before getting there. As for powers and things of fulfillments, alongside any other Roads, I'll have a ponder on it. In the meantime, yeah, I would actively steal from Prometheans and then work from there. I'd also consider the 4 Dot Fate Spell from Mage, Atonement, not so much as a definitive answer for anything as much as a mood piece for stepping down from the Pilgrimage.

Creatives learning their art tend to approach the other, and as such, it's not bad to start with a surface level understanding since most everyone starts here

There's a few things I want to model -- the Fetch that just decides to wander off from their previous life, and the Fetch that has something different from their previous life that they want to explore (different interests, different sexuality, different gender identity, different neural diversity -- I can see at least one trying to understand how and why they don't "feel" like they used to and exploring that facet as a possible explanation...I have to wonder how many Fetches wind up as Ace or Enby after some soul searching). Perhaps splitting them up might help focus them.
