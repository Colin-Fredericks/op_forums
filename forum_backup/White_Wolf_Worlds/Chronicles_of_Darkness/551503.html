      Obligatory MY CITY

      Toronto's an interesting choice for a freehold, but I've always maintained that it'd be an interesting one.

      If anything, the Courts of Toronto could be more fractious - there are so many different perspectives that you could view Toronto with. I wish there had been a bit more focus on the suburbs of Toronto, which are a very big part of the city, and where the outlook changes significantly.

      It's also pretty cool knowing that I live next to one of the biggest Goblin Markets in the world.

          Surprised Yonge Street got a paragraph, but Casa Loma, the fairy-tale folly house, didn't. The CN tower description is awesome, and Kensington the Goblin Market is too obvious NOT to use, so that's awesome.
          Other than that, I think it does my hometown justice. The theme of multiculturalism is strong here (Diversity Our Strength and all that), which I like. Other themes in the city that aren't as stressed are the themes of "Toronto the Good", domineeringly moralistic Toronto, and of the 416-905 divide, the great socio-political divide between the Old City of Toronto and the outer regions annexed to Toronto in the Amalgamation. This divide pretty much defines all modern political struggles in Toronto, and the vast majority of major political debates in City Hall can easily map to the liberal Old City versus the conservative suburbs, often meaning that, for example, City Council decides to rebuild a raised highway running through downtown Toronto based against the votes of every councillor whose wards are anywhere near the highway.
          Those themes may be themes better explored in other games, however.

                  The suburb demographics is actually more multicultural than the downtown, even if us downtowners pride ourselves more in multiculturalism, it's just more patchwork than mosaic: large patches of various groups all living in close-knit proximity, rather than the more scattered mosaic of downtown. I mean, thing of Somali Torontonians, they almost all live in one neighbourhood!

                  Edit: either way, I think it does a better job than the Vampire:tR 1.0 attempt at it, can't remember which book it was in, it featured Casa Loma heavily but it felt like you could do the same sort of city anywhere. A good example city should be unique to the city it's in, and I think this pulls that off fairly well.

                  Edit Edit: So which Court helped found the Aga Khan Museum and Ismaili Cultural Centre? Brass or Ivory? Either would work, and given that Toronto doesn't have a HUGE Middle eastern population (not in proportion to it's Indian or Chinese populations), I would bet that the recent opening and ridiculous awesomeness of the Museum was an inspiration for the Brass Court.
