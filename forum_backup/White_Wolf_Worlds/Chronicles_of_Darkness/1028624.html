    Medicine skill confusion

    01-08-2017, 02:43 PM
    I do not understand how many successes are needed with the Medicine skill to stabilize someone who is bleeding out. Probably because the rules (or at least the stated rule & proffered example) contradict themselves:

    World of Darkness Storytelling System Rulebook, p.62, 2nd column, 1st complete paragraph in that column, 3rd sentence:

    "... a patient who's bleeding to death or in a coma is stabilized if one success is achieved on the roll."

    Same page, same column, 5th paragraph (which starts an Example section), 5th sentence:

    "He must accumulate seven successes to stop the flow of blood and save Emir's life."

    So, um - WTH? Do you stabilize someone with 1 success, or do you have to get successes = their total health dots? This would make a HUGE difference in a life-or-death situation (seriously, poor Emir almost bleed out & died waiting for those 7 successes).

    Is there an erratum for this somewhere?

                          I was, literally, just reading this over last night. I had some similar questions, but I think(?) I talked it all out in my head.

                          My understanding is that if you're full of lethal, you're bleeding out. Once you're bleeding out, damage doesn't automatically downgrade, and in fact your lethal upgrades to aggravated at the rate of one per minute, until you get medical attention or you fill up...then you die.

                          "When a character's rightmost Health box has lethal damage in it, he takes another point of damage to each minute (upgrading existing lethal damage to aggravated) until he receives medical attention..." ("Full Health Tracks...," CofD, of. 95).

                          "Normally, a character can heal without medical attention, though the use of the Medicine Skill helps her recover... The only exception is if a character has all her Health boxes full of lethal damage – she's bleeding out. She can't recover from that with an urgent medical attention and emergency surgery." ("Healing," CofD, pg. 95)

                          On page 96, it details what can be done to heal a character from this prediciment:

                          "Medical care is an extended action requiring success is equal to the total number of damage suffered by the patient. In the field or ER, the dice pool Dexterity + Medicine, and the interval is one minute.
                          "Achieving sufficient successes restores one Health point lost to bashing damage..." ("Medical Care," CofD, pg. 96).

                          I get, from this, the following scenario:
                          Jack has been shot and left in the alley to die. He's taken 8 lethal damage - all his Health Pool - and is unconscious from failing his Stamina roll. Two minutes go by and two points of lethal get upgraded to aggravated damage.
                          Fortunately for Jack, an off-duty nurse walks by peers down the alley, and sees Jack bleeding out on the ground. By the time she cautiously approaches, and realizes what's going on, Jack's lost another two points of lethal to aggravated damage. But the nurse starts doing what she can.
                          The first minute of aid she gets four successes - not enough to pull Jack out of danger, as she needs successes equal to Jack's damage (and he loses another point to aggravated, and now has 5 aggravated damage and is slipping away). The next round the nurse - determined not to let Jack die - uses a point of Willpower and pulls in 5 successes! The rightmost box, still with lethal damage, gets upgraded to bashing damage. Jack will stop losing losing Health Points and is out of danger...for the moment.

                          Maybe my interpretation is wrong, but this is how I came to understand it! Hope this helps.

                              That is what I thought at first, but there are a few problems...

                              Firstly, the line you mention, 'Achieving sufficient successes restores one Health point lost to bashing damage." Bleeding out isn't bashing damage, it's all lethal - therefore this does not apply.

                              Secondly, lethal damage can only be treated in a hospital or similar facility (p. 96, "Thus, a lethal wound can be downgraded to bashing, and an aggravated wound can be downgraded to lethal. Such treatment can occur only in a hospital..."). So the nurse could not treat Jack for his lethal wounds in the alley.

                              Finally, p.96 again, "An extended Intelligence + Medicine roll is made. The number of successes required is five for a lethal wound... Each roll takes an hour."

                              Ergo, bleeding out:
                              - cannot be treated in the field,
                              - cannot be treated quickly enough to matter in the hospital, & therefore
                              - a character with a full health bar of lethal wounds is as good as dead already (unless there's someone around with supernatural abilities to save them).

                              Really, I think the Armory Reloaded hack is the only way to salvage this rule until an erratum for it is written.

                                  There are two ways you can read, "Achieving sufficient successes restores one Health point lost to bashing damage." It could mean that one health point, which was lost to bashing damage, is restored. Or, it could mean that one health point, which was lost, is restored to bashing damage. Granted, if the intent is the latter, it's a strange way to phrase it, considering it's atypical to describe health points filled with damage as being "lost", and to describe downgrading lethal damage to bashing as "restoring " but it does explain how stabilizing someone who is bleeding out would be possible, and it keeps the mechanics for emergency care consistent with the later quote, "Note that this treatment does not eliminate wounds. It simply minimizes them."

                                                      Since there were no major changes to the Healing rules from nWoD to CofD, (that I could locate - the GMC doesn't have any changes), I think referring to those rules for a clearer picture is a valid option.

                                                      Both books say a Dexteritty + Medicine check can be attempted in the field, or the ER. Both say that achieving "sufficient successes" that equal to the total Health lost "restores one Health point to bashing damage." My earlier post, and Charlquin's "second interpretation," suggested that this statement meant that one point downgraded from lethal to bashing. I have since changed my mind, because of that wording. So, Snapdragyn, I think your interpretation of my post is correct.

                                                      However, I think the nWod book does a much better job expounding upon skills. I feel that it's a major part of the CofD book that's lacking - the nWod core book does an excellent job breaking down specific tasks relating to each skill. Maybe the developers left that out of CofD because they figured most players were coming from nWoD and had that as a resource and didn't need to reprint it, or perhaps they were pressed for space with the God Machine Campaign they wanted to add. Who knows.

                                                      But, there's a major piece that is missing from the CofD book, dealing with this very subject. Under the Medicine skill, page 61 of the nWoD core book, it addresses this:

                                                      "Achieving sufficient successes (equal to the total, overall Health points lost by the patient) restores one Health point lost to bashing damage, in addition to any healing that the character already does under his own power. A patient may regain no more that one 'extra' Health point by this means per day. Alternatively, a patient who's bleeding to death or in a coma is stablized if one success is achieved on the roll (The loss of Health points to aggrivated damage each minute is stopped; see 'Incapatation,' p. 173)" (nWod core book, p. 61)

                                                      Since this check can be made in the field, I feel it covers the issue.

                                                      I feel this handles it quite nicely. My previous example of Jack and the nurse is still pretty much valid; the only difference is that Jack would remain at 5 aggrivated and 3 lethal and would not lose any more lethal to aggrivated. The nurse can then call 911 and get an ambulance to Jack where he'll receive proper medical attention in the hospital.
