                I think you should read the books in the order in which they were released - Requiem, Forsaken, Awakening, Created, Lost, Vigil, Geist, Mummy, Demon.

                If you JUST want 2e, then I recommend Requiem, Forsaken, Awakening, Created, Demon.

                My Totally 100% Subjective Reasoning -

                Requiem is easily the easiest transition from mortals into the supernatural world. All Kindred were once mortal - it is, in fact, what makes them Kindred. Your mortal PCs could easily become Vampires, and even if you decide to start with them as Vampires, all of them were once mortal. The Five Covenants, or Factions, are all more or less tied to easy-to-understand themes. Power and Establishment, Revolution, Witchcraft and Balancing Humanity and the Beast, God and Faith, and Transhumanism. Super duper easy, and make great plots. Ghosts, Spirits, and Angels of the God-Machine are not tied to the setting at all, and can be used at your leisure or not at all. It's just Vampires, People, and lots and lots of Blood.

                Werewolf is a little weirder - though the players "start" human, Werewolves were never truly human at all and were born to experience their First Change. Werewolf is a really, really fun and immersive introduction to the Shadow/Spirit World, which focuses heavily on the Shadow and will get you understanding how it works and its taboos.

                Mage takes all that stuff up to 11. It has ALL of the complicated stuff, an expansive cosmology and dimensional map, and is effectively limited by your imagination. Your Mary Sue player will love it, if you're willing to put yourself through that. The only thing that makes Mages a LITTLE easy is that they are still technically human, just enlightened humans.

                Created is incredibly complicated - the characters were never human and are made (pretty much literally) from scratch. That can be super fun. There's also a Refinement dedicated to hanging out with all the other splats, so a working knowledge of other splats can help with that.

                I haven't played or read Demon, so I can't recommend or reject it. Everything I've heard about it is good stuff, though.

                                A Numen is a supernatural power an ephermeral entity has, such as being able to blast you with psychic energy or make nearby electronics go haywire. Numina is just the plural form of Numen. Manifestations are similar to Numina, in that they are supernatural abilities unique to ephemeral entities, but Manifestations specifically are what allow ephemeral entities to exist in the material world. Twilight form, for example, is a Manifestation - a state of being in which the entity is present in the material world, but invisible and incorporeal. Image is another Manifestation, in which the entity is incorporeal but visible. Materialize is a Manifestation in which the entity is fully corporeal and visible. Powers that allow an entity to take control of physical beings or objects, such as posession are also considered Manifestations. Essence does act kind of like a mana pool, which ephemeral entities spend to fuel their Numina and Manifestations, though it can also be spent to boost dice pools, much like Willpower. An entity can spend Essence and Willpower to boost the same roll if they choose to.

                                I would never have guessed that English was a second language for you, you use it very well. Anyway, the Gauntlet is a barrier between the material world and the spirit world. It gets its name not from the armored glove, but for a different meaning of the word, which essentially means a dangerous trial or an ordeal, particularly as something which has to be passed through. So, the reason that human activity pushes spirit activity away is because it strengthens the barrier between our world and the spirit world. The meta-narrative reason for this is that it's common in folklore for the untamed wilderness to be presented as being closer to the world of spirits/fairies/magic/etc, that "the veil is thinner" in the natural world than it is in the civilized world. In-setting, there isn't a real reason given for why exactly human activity strengthens the Gauntlet, it's mostly up to the storyteller. There are some possibilities hinted at in the Neolithic Dark Era, Sundered World, but they won't make much sense without background knowledge of werewolf. As for why human activity also strengthens spirit activity at the same time as it pushes the spirit world away, that's because humans are an abundant source of Resonance, which for spirits means an abundant source of Essence. We are strong abstract thinkers, and connect disparate concepts through symbolism very easily. Spirits thrive on symbolism, and are limited in what sort of Essence they can safely consume by conceptual links. Since humans are great at forming conceptual links, it's natural for spirits to want to be around humans.

                                            MORTAL INTERACTION
