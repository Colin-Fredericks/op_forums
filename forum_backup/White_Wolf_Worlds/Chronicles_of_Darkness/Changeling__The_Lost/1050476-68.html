                                                I have some trouble adjudicating what is and isn't an appropriate action for a hedgespinning shift, or indeed don't even know if this is something I should be concerned with to begin with.

                                                Essentially, I'm confused by how hedgespinning is at the same time described as something that happens in addition to the mundane action that generated the successes (albeit giving a penalty and moving some of the successes from the action's normal result to hedgespinning shifts), and in the examples as some specific actions taken for the express purpose of hedgespinning a certain result.

                                                Take the example on p. 204-205 concerning persuading the locals into building a bridge to proceed. I assume in this case you'd roll a persuasion roll (with penalty for hedgespinning) to convince them. But if you succeed, you might still not have enough successes to get the shift to build the bridge. Then what happens? It feels odd to succeed on a skill roll, but then the bridge doesn't get made so it isn't all that successful in the end. I must be missing something, but I need some help figuring out precisely what. (Possible resolution to this that I can think of: The local hobs agree to help, but some obstacle presents itself keeping them from finishing a bridge. You can thus bank the successes you've got already to add them to the next hedgespinning roll, which you'll likely do while helping them clear this obstacle, which might involve getting materials to build the bridge, or getting rid of hostile hobs trying to stop the building, etc... Does that sound right? => Problem: banking successes is only available with a Paradigm Shift, which they likely did not achieve. Bit of a bummer since this really seems like a good answer otherwise!)

                                                On a similar note I find it difficult to adjudicate what skills are and aren't appropriate for a given goal (if indeed that is even a thing). One other possible way of building the bridge reads:
                                                or physically wrestling
                                                with a foe who represents an internal struggle she wishes
                                                to move past
                                                So if my player says "I want to get this bridge built by wrestling with a physical representation of an internal struggle!" is that always automatically an option or can I on occasion go "Err... well, no such opponent seems to be present right now."?

                                                If an action must only be thematically related to the feelings and end result you're trying to achieve, can a Musician kith Changeling heavily specialized in music essentially whip up a thematically appropriate piece of music on the fly (perhaps with improvisation penalties if it's not a piece they've made/rehearsed before) to hedgespin essentially any desired end result on the strength of that one dicepool alone with few variations in approach?
                                                I think it sounds like more fun for all involved if the actions need to be a bit more directly linked to end result (like the examples of smashing rocks together and forcing them to become a hammer, or convincing people around you that a door you draw is real; those are fun and very scene-specific! ) and the dicepools therefore more varied, but I can't find any strong textual support for that; singing all your problems away for the most part seems directly supported. :P (And Hedge-singing sounds cool and thematically appropriate so I want music to be a viable answer sometimes, but not every time. xD)
                                                The specific skill here is only a theoretical example, I'm sure there are other skills to that effect. ^^

                                                Then finally, when your goal isn't to hedgespin necessarily, like when you make an attack and decide to make it a hedgespinning one "just in case", do you need to spend shifts in a way that aligns with your method of attacking/your intentions? How should I see this, exactly? ^^

                                                If anyone just outright can go "Here, this is a PBP campaign I did and this is an outright transcription of a hedge-focused session we did with a bunch of hedgespinning examples," that would be so <3.
                                                Any examples appreciated, essentially.

                                                    Context rules the day. If your player does the narrative legwork to paint a physical obstacle as representative of an internal struggle, then they can definitely use surmounting that obstacle to get over the chasm they're trying to tie a rope across.

                                                            Originally posted by Unahim View Post
                                                            I'm not sure I'm fully understanding your question, but what I mean is: When I'm doing an action specifically to try to hedgespin a bridge, I sort of know what I'm going for, right? But if I make an attack and I hedgespin it, can I then decide to use the shifts to make armour for myself because that's "related" to fighting, or not because I was on the offense, not defense, or where abouts should one draw the line?
                                                            Context and details will make or break particular instances, but if you're spending a shift you got by progressing a conflict to engage further with the conflict I don't think anyone would take much issue with that (hell, it's half the point of Hedge Brawler).

                                                            (It should be noted that direct attacks are generally a less-than-ideal set of actions to Hedgespin from, since you need to roll multiple successes on a penalized roll and Defense makes that even harder, and by a similar token adding a penalty to an action on a whim is a massive gamble for most actions a character could conceivably take.)

                                                            Yeah, my biggest concern is that the idea of "shape the hedge according to my music" sounds a bit repetitive, because you can essentially make music to fit any situation but it doesn't have much narrative "weight", i.e. nothing much is "happening" in the story aside from you playing a lot of music.
                                                            I think the solution to that is to remember that e.g. "shape the Hedge by physically crafting it" is descriptively identical — you press for details in cases like this, because Hedgespinning effects don't just happen without any sort of process playing out to shift them into focus. Attracting wandering hobgoblins to supply you with an organization is one way to get musically-sourced Hedgespinning effects to work, while tugging at the heartstrings of the Hedge itself to make it rain is another, and replicating the howl of a bestial Hedge ghost with your instruments is yet a third.

                                                            There's a reason the section exhorts players to describe shifts in terms of what's happening to the scenery, after all.
