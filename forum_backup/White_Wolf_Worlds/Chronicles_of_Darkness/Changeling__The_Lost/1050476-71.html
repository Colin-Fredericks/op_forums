                                        Originally posted by Wade L View Post
                                        Just gonna keep spouting off questions as I read and have them...

                                        Do Fetches have a Mask and Mein? Or does a Fetch just look like what it looks like?

                                        I can't tell from the book whether a Fetch actually looks like an obviously inhuman construct and is covered over by the magic of a Mask, or if it is constructed to look almost human without needing a magic veil, it just has some tells that might reveal its true nature...

                                        What I'm particularly wondering is if an average Changeling interacting with a Fetch will realize it is a Fetch just by looking at it by seeing through a Mask or the like? Does a Fetch need Normalcy to hide themselves from a Changeling just looking at it, or only to disguise themselves from Contracts and the like?

                                        It's inconsistent. And it's been inconsistent since 1e. A lot of art for Changeling has fetches clearly represented by various types of constructs, but the text tends to imply that fetches don't look different from normal humans (even to those who can see the Mien, which every Fetch can thanks to Attuned to the Wyrd), and the Normalcy Echo says the Fetch with it can't be detected as anything other than human by any Fae detective magic.

                                        So it's up to the Storyteller how they want to portray them.

                                        If the ST wants to say that Fetches have a Mask and Mien like Changelings, and that Fetches can be seen for what they are if the Mask fails (for instance, if they go into the Hedge), then a lot fewer Fetches will know they aren't human -- only those with the Normalcy power would be completely human looking (unless you think that a Fetch is never going to catch a glimpse of themselves in a mirror or see themselves in a picture, which is unlikely). This take plays up the alien nature of the Fetch-as-Gentry-construct, and how they don't belong wherever they've been placed.

                                        If the ST wants to portray them as looking human and only being detectable by magic such as Kenning (and not even then if they have Normalcy), that plays up the "you can't go home again" nature of the Fetch and the potential unreliability of the Changeling's memories, making them question their own reality.

                                        I'm currently running a game where the PCs themselves are Fetches, dealing with their Changelings returning from Arcadia, so I'm going with the second version in my own game, but I could easily run a game where the Fetch is meant to be more of an antagonist and look less consistently human.

                                        When the cat's a Stray, the mice will pray...

                                            Okay, clearing some nonsense up:

                                            Fetches don't have a Mask or a Mein.

                                            Fetches look like the person they replaced at any or all times until they are killed. Sometimes even months after that event.

                                            Art can get conflicting on that, because it's more fun to show a fetch as a stitchwork, but the absolute rule is that a Fetch looks more like You than You do most of the time.

                                                    EDIT: Beyond textual evidence-read Autumn Nightmares, Winter Masques, Rites of Spring, Dancers in the Dusk, and both cores again-an underappreciated element of horror for Changeling is the possibility that they are wrong about their assumption of who they are. Changeling is fundamentally a game of mirrors with plenty of smoke in front of them, and part of the central point of all of that isn't just that all of these shards of people and enemies around you might contain a truth about you you're unwilling to commit to facing, but that they might actually have a better claim to your name than you do. While, honestly, a whole host of people have stakes to that, the fundamental case is the Fetch itself, who, for all the ways they can be adversarially played against you, could honestly be just be some innocent your "Forgot that I was a Mirrorskin the entire time" self-because-that's-a-consequence-of-Arcadia could only accept as the thief of your life because that is the narrative you were sold on.
