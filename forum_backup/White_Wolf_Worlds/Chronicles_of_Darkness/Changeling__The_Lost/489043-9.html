                Yōkai (妖怪?, ghost, phantom, strange apparition) are a class of supernatural monsters in Japanese folklore. The word yōkai is made up of the kanji for "bewitching; attractive; calamity" and "apparition; mystery; suspicious".[1] They can also be called ayakashi (妖?), mononoke (物の怪?), or mamono (魔物?). Yōkai range eclectically from the malevolent to the mischievous, or occasionally bring good fortune to those who encounter them. Often they possess animal features (such as the Kappa, which is similar to a turtle, or the Tengu which has wings), other times they can appear mostly human, some look like inanimate objects and others have no discernible shape. Yōkai usually have a spiritual supernatural power, with shapeshifting being one of the most common. Yōkai that have the ability to shapeshift are called obake.
                Japanese folklorists and historians use yōkai as "supernatural or unaccountable phenomena to their informants". In the Edo period, many artists, such as Toriyama Sekien, created yōkai inspired by folklore or their own ideas, and in the present, several yōkai created by them (e.g. Kameosa and Amikiri, see below) are wrongly considered as being of legendary origin.[2]

                            Here are some interpretations I was able to come up with for Kiths regarding this potential seeming.



                            Fireheart - Changed into paper lanterns known as Burabura, these Yokai appear colorful and delicate to the touch. They have a tendency to wear tassels or strips of kanji hanging off their bodies and their insides glow with candle light for those who can see through their mask.


                            Gravewright - Also known as the Dorotabo, these Yokai are recruited from the ranks of country folk, such as farmers. They dwell among lands with a history of abuse, neglect and violence. Where angry spirits or tormented ghosts are likely to be found. They might have the appearance of a long dead farmhand, their clothes still covered in dirt and in a state of disrepair from years of hard labor in the fields.


                            Brewer - Known as the Kameosa, these Yokai appear to be fashioned out of some manner of clay, plastic or porcelain. In Arcadia, they were used as jars to contain the alcoholic beverages of their Keepers. Their bodily fluids (such as saliva) might give off the smell or even taste of sake to those willing to try it.

If you're a member of that Seeming, you get it free when you buy the Contract. If the Fairest's versions are often more social-focused than most others, that might contribute to the idea of them being potent socialites, relative to the other Seemings.
