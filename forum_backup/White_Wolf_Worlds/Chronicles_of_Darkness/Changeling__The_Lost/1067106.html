          1e had a fairly extensive list that can be summarized as "things in the Hedge are affected by the Arcana that they appear to be (living things with Life, inanimate objects with Matter, etc) unless they're part of the Thorns," "sensory magic has its dicepool capped by your Wisdom rating," and "you cannot exit the Hedge except through a Hedge gate."

          2e shifts some of this — Hedge Gates are subject to the same rules as other Irises, for instance, so a Master of Fate can leave the Hedge as readily as a Master of Death can escape the Underworld — but more detailed information will likely show up in the Crossover Chronicle eventually.

                          Think of it this way: mages may wield more power than most supernaturals, but they are also the most hamstrung by those powers, and everyone else also has a lot more finesse with their abilities. When mages mess up with flexing the universe, the universe flexes back, hard; I believe one example I read was that an archmage attempting to use a spell to heat a cup of tea could accidentally set the neighboring apartment on fire. The other supernaturals don't have nearly that scale of trouble should something go wrong with their powers (save for perhaps Demons, given the whole God-Machine situation).

                          Likewise, remember that both Changelings and Sin-Eaters are capable of opening and even making their respective gates far easier than a Mage can create them. Mages need to be a master of the field to do so, whereas for the other supernaturals, it's all just one piece of their innate kit.

                                              Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                              I still find it weird that Hedge stuff is done by mages with Fate rather than Mind considering the realm is so psychoreactive.
                                              The Hedge is psychoreactive in a way that's a fair bit more granular than Mind typically gets, most of the fully mental stuff it deals with is in isolated pockets that occasionally manifest in ways outside the scope of the Awakened framework for imaginary constructs, and the place's loose interface with the waking world doesn't have any relation to the degrees of resonance and abstraction that other phenomena deal with — a Hedge gate can exist somewhere just because there happened to be a Hedge gate there, with no real environmental bleedthrough or warning signs besides direct artifacts of its usage (and in fact this is an established problem with one of 1e's sample Bridge Burner associations' attempts to shut down emergent trods; they can predict where a new Hedge gate will spontaneously arise with a decent degree of fidelity, but leveraging land development to try and throw off whatever process causes them specifically doesn't work).

                                              Meanwhile, the Hedge has easy roads and hard roads and is occupied by things both really want and really don't want — and the Fate Arcanum deals with intention and desire. This is the border march that lesser djinn inhabit, and negotiating it is a function of what you're prepared to go through and how clever you are about your choices.

                                                          ....I...think I agree with you? However....Mage pg. 133.

                                                          "Open Avernian Gate (Death •••••)
                                                          Practice: Making
                                                          Primary Factor: Duration
                                                          Suggested Rote Skills: Occult, Crafts, Persuasion
                                                          The mage rips open the material world to the Underworld,
                                                          creating an Iris between the material world and the upper layers
                                                          of the Underworld within the area of effect. Opening the gate
                                                          causes the area to gain a Death Resonance and the Gateway
                                                          Condition for the Duration of the spell.
                                                          +1 Reach: The mage can create an Iris leading to anywhere
                                                          she has been before in the Underworld."

                                                          So by my reading, you are correct. Technically. A Master of Fate would have no more luck forming an exit OUT of the Hedge than a Master of Death could form a portal out of the underworld. Since this spell only describe a Mage "ripping open the material world to the Underworld" e.g.- standing in the mundane world and invoking magic to form a gate into the underworld my assumption is that trying to do the same from the opposite side is impossible. You can use the gate you already created, for sure, and you can probably extend the duration of the condition to near indefinite. I'll even speculate that a Mage can probably open any portal to the surface he finds without needing a proper key. But he can't snap his fingers and get a fresh exit. He needs to have one already on hand, including a gate he brought into being with the above spell and a little Reach that placed in at a depth usually not found in the underworld.

                                                          I think we can be a little more generous with the Hedge. It generally wants its victims to stay, but its more forgiving and playful than the realm of the dead about the ones who want to get out. So... 1.) Any mage of fate can form a hedge gate from the material world into the Hedge without trouble. With a little extra Reach you can go anywhere you've ever been inside the Hedge....if the place still exists. Keep in mind, the Hedge is amorphous and ever changing, so you might want to pick a locale that has some staying power like a goblin market, a goblin village, a fae hollow, or one of those other rare places that resist alteration. 2.) A mage can easily form a way OUT of the Hedge near the mortal realm, just like a changeling. Such gates might have their own quirks- like thenceforth being openable from either side by such entities like the fae who are capable of opening a "natural" gate into the hedge. 3.) A Mage can open a pre-existing gateway out of the hedge anywhere within the hedge, including one he created by magic in the mortal world. 4.) If a Mage is lost or deep enough within the Hedge, he cannot create a gateway out of the hedge until he finds his way back to a path that brushes against the mortal world, as per rule number 2.

                                                          While I agree Mages are and rightfully should be considered exceptionally powerful in Second Edition compared to other supernatural beings, I believe the scope of their abilities has been somewhat misinterpreted, especially when it comes to Realms beyond this Earth. I'll also be the first to admit that I haven't read Mage as completely as I have other Chronicles of Darkness, so if there is written material that calls my own interpretation into question please feel free to bring it up. I'd just ask for relevant page numbers and source material for ease of reference.
