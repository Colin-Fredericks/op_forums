            It is definitely not a safe assumption that all characters who fit a 1e Seeming will automatically be members of the same 2e Seeming. This is regardless of how broad or narrow one finds the Fairest Seeming.

            Like, take the Beasts. 1e Beasts are about clawing one's way out of animal instinct to get free, while 2e Beasts are about throwing themselves into animal instinct to get their freedom. Those are completely reversed narratives. 1e Beast is just what you pick if you want an animal-like character, while 2e Beast is what you pick if you want a character who flaunts social taboos. Those two groups will have some overlap, but it is guaranteed not to be absolute. Some 1e Beasts are just going to be better fits for different 2e Seemings. Case in point, I had a lot of fun with Beast characters in 1e, but the type of character 2e Beast represents is just not something I'm interested in playing. When all the 2e Seeming particulars are teased/written, I'll either find 2e Seemings that fit the characters in question or be disappointed and go Seemingless instead.

            This was always going to be true for all the Seemings. It just isn't much of a narrative loss (when it is at all) because 2e Kiths are dissociated from 2e Seemings. 2e Kiths basically fulfill the default narrative role that 1e Kith and 1e Seeming used to fulfill together. Character concepts from 1e aren't less valid, they just might be differently represented.

                                                I haven't read WTF2, so I'm not sure how Harmony works in the new ed, but it definitely seems like Clarity is going to fluctuate a lot more than it did in 1e, given the way these Blessings/Curses work.

                                                Personally, I felt like Clarity was something of a balancing act in 1e. From a strictly mechanical perspective, players wanted to keep Clarity high enough to retain Kenning and not suffer from penalties to perception, but didn't generally want to take it up so high that their ability to use their special powers and advantages was compromised. From a rp perspective, it was about balancing stability and power and, also, balancing excitement and wonder with calm and comfort. If your Clarity got too low, you risked losing touch with the life, the world, that you struggled so hard to get back to (and with your own sanity). If you maintained a very high Clarity, you did so at the cost of mystical tools (Dreamwalking, the Hedge, Tokens) that could not only improve your life, but could save you from being recaptured or killed should your Keeper or a Privateer or Loyalist come for you.

                                                A Changeling's fae side is an important part of who they are, so I'd love to see a Clarity system that focuses a little more on striking a balance. (To clarify, I mean I'd like a system in which striking a balance is represented by a high Clarity score, as opposed to 1e Clarity, where striking a balance might be desirable for other reasons, but was represented by a middling score in Clarity.)

                                                    I was thinking about what a Motley with two different Fairest in it might look like, and it made me think of the Walking Dead (the tv series not the comics). In the current seasons, you've got a handful of leaders that the group looks up to. Rick is like a Fairest who embraces his leadership role (at least in part, because he doesn't feel that anyone else can do a better job). As a result, he pretty regularly loses Clarity when things go sideways- talking to his dead wife on the phone, chewing on people's jugulars, etc. But then he pulls the group out of a tough situation and gets his psychological ducks back in a row - a Clarity gain. It's up and down. He's probably lost more than he's gained, but he's a good leader and he manages to eek out victory often enough that he's relatively stable most of the time. Meanwhile, you've got Daryl. Everybody looks up to him and follows his lead, but he absolutely does not want to be in charge. Sometimes, he ends up having to lead a small group, but mostly he keeps to himself, lets Rick do the leading and either lends him support or stays out of his way. He's a Fairest too, but he actively avoids both his Blessing and Curse. Luckily, he has another Fairest he can defer to when people need a leader, so he avoids making the plans that get people hurt. On the rare occasion when he is forced to lead, he wings it and usually succeeds. As a result he's gained Clarity, albeit slowly, over the course of the series. He's more self-assured and stable than when we met him in season 1.

                                                        Harmony 2e works by making the mid-point (5) the most ideal position to aim for. You can't spend XP on Harmony, and it changes have having breaking points in both directions (Flesh and Spirit). You can (somewhat similar to your Walking Dead example) accept going up and down a lot and try to stay near the middle as much as possible, or try to avoid too much breaking points in either direction to be stable in the middle. The Walking Dead isn't a bad example for WtF 2e in general with the main cast as the pack. They all struggle with needing to be hard survivors in the zombie apocalypse with forces them to make brutal choices (breaking towards Spirit in Harmony, not exactly, but not too far off), while trying to find ways to ground themselves and keep their humanity (breaking towards Flesh). Brutally killing enemy raiders juxtaposed against sitting back and having a bit tin of pudding.

                                                        As for Fairest, I think there's an interesting case of a lot of the main cast of the Walking Dead to be examples of different takes on being a Fairest. A lot of them, even if they're not the overt leader that Rick is, have things that they "do" and everyone generally defers to them on. I think you can generally cluster them into three basic groups: the natural leaders that take the reins (Rick, Shane, Dale, Herschel, Abraham, the Governor even), the ones that rejected other people much to their mental detriment and might be on the path of balance by embracing being looked up to for their reliability (Daryl as noted, but Michonne as well, Sasha maybe, Gabriel though not really doing well with it), and the ones that fell neatly into a balance of not being leaders but still having a vital skill that puts them in charge for specific situations (Glenn, Carol, Eugene, Tyrese).
