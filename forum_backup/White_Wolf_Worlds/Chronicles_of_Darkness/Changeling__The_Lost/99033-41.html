                                                Time to check back in with the crew at the Dream House. There is much drama and excitement this time.

                                                For those more fond of dramatic tension, Gawain offhandedly mentions a major event in discussing things in his journal. You might want to read Lorelei’s first then Gawain’s if that is something that might bug you. If not have at. Also, as a note, Calamity and Insincere Marguerite get a little racy, as they occasionally do. Nothing severe, but wanted to mention.

                                                Session LXXV

                                                Gawain’s Memories

                                                Today was certainly an interesting day. For starters, I got us our fire breathing piranhas! It turned out easier than I was expecting - they stayed put the first time I summoned them up out of Desiree’s dreams. I moved their water bubble into the filled moat around the Dream House and they took off. They’re adorable (they did come from Desiree’s dreams after all) and sometimes, even when you can’t see into the water, you can see where they are from the bubbles and plumes of steam that shoot up occasionally. I really, you know, don’t think it’s fair of Insincere Marguerite to call them violent. Just because they are the unique carnivorous non-sentient animal (that I know of) that prefers their meat cooked doesn’t make them more violent than any other carnivorous animal. She just got all Beaker-y when I pointed that out though, so...

                                                In other news, Calamity’s getting serious about tracking down Anastasia. She pulled me and Becky aside to talk about it, saying things like she didn’t want to make promises and stuff before she’d talked to her old friend. Becky kept looking at me, and one time even asked me if I was OK (she’s very good at taking care of people, you know?) and I didn’t get it - I figured Calamity was talking about the whole being specific about relationships thing that Becky brought up, or maybe was just responding to the whole De la Rocha proposing to Lori thing and I wasn’t really thinking about following suit anytime real soon and I don’t *think* that sort of thing is contagious...

                                                Anyway, I realized a little later that Calamity maybe meant any kind of relationship stuff. All kinds of relationship stuff. I didn’t say anything, right then. I mean, it’s not like I’m gonna say ‘no, you can’t go reconcile with the one person that you never forgot in all your time in Arcadia, the person who the thought of catalyzed your escape, because you might choose her over me’. That just seems like, you know, a super dick move. And it’s not like that’s what she meant for sure anyway. She just wants to know, before... before anything else. Lots of things could happen. Like Calamity said, Anastacia’s probably not gonna be all smiles and welcoming gestures, since Calamity’s Fetch was pretty awful. But lots of things could happen, and I do think talking to Anastacia is the best thing for her, however it turns out.

                                                I’ll always have hope.


                                                Lorelei’s Diary

                                                Omigod omigod omigod!!!

                                                How do you… I mean, I can’t even… I mean, how am I supposed to talk about this rationally? My brain is so scrambled that it’s frankly a miracle that I didn’t fall on my ass and make a fool of myself on stage tonight. I didn’t get a wink of sleep on Thursday night, and it’s looking like tonight (which is almost over as I write this anyway) won’t be much better. Whenever they’re around me, all of my friends keep glancing at me and chuckling, so I know I must be acting like an idiot. But I don’t care!

                                                Okay, Lori, time to try your best to get those little grey cells working and put things in sequential order. Otherwise there’s no way in hell this entry is going to make sense when you come back and read it a few years down the line… and if there was ever an entry worth coming back to, this is going to be it!

                                                Where did I leave off with my last entry? Right, I had just discovered that I could turn myself into a bird, and was completely overcome by the joy of flight. I shared my new talent with my nearest and dearest, and most of them seemed to be fairly impressed. The ability to fly and go unnoticed – well, relatively unnoticed, anyway… there aren’t that many rainbow-colored tropical songbirds in the area – could be a real boon to us in the future.

                                                Then it was time to get ready for the show. As I was getting ready to head backstage, D pulled me aside with a serious look on her face. “What’s up, babe?” I asked, concerned.

                                                “Spidertoes is going to be here tonight,” she said soberly. “Me being in danger earlier… it rattled her. I’m going to sit with her and hold her hand… possibly literally. I just wanted to let you know ahead of time so it didn’t come as a shock.”

                                                I took her hand in both of mine. “D,” I said, “thanks for telling me, but you know I trust you, right? Help out your ex. God knows she’s helped us lately.”

                                                “I won’t sleep with her,” D said dully. “I know you don’t want me to. And because I love you and I trust you, I’m going to be honest here… that’s probably the only reason I won’t sleep with her.”

                                                My chest tightened. “D…” I said softly.

                                                She shook her head. “It’s okay, Lori. It’s not about this rule chafing me or anything like that this time. I won’t sleep with her because I’d never do anything to upset you, okay?”

                                                I averted my eyes. “I’ve been thinking about this ‘rule’ lately, actually,” I said softly, “and it’s not fair. You’ve been so good about this whole open relationship, and then I throw this jealousy and mistrust in your face…”

                                                “It’s okay,” she said again, and leaned in for a kiss. “Don’t say or do anything on the spur of the moment. Give it some thought.”

                                                I solemnly promised that I wouldn’t make any snap decisions, and went backstage to get dressed. I thought about Spidertoes, and D, and me, and tried desperately to focus on my performance (“The Christmas Song” by Nat King Cole, done as sultrily as I could manage). Glancing up at the Girlfriend Table, I saw that Oleander had come by, and she was chatting idly with Julia. Marguerite had left before the show for a date with Felicity (and don’t get me started on what a train wreck that’s sure to be, but I digress), leaving her assistant to manage the lights and music for the evening.

                                                During my first number, I noticed Tamara Chance talking to D. The two of them left Spidertoes’ table and had an animated discussion off to one side of the theater. It didn’t look like either of them was angry or violent, but D was obviously emotionally engaged in whatever was passing between them. Tamara left after a minute or two and headed back to the bar.

                                                I approached D as soon as I had a break and asked what that was about. “I’m going to meet with Twitch,” she said in response. “The two of us are going to talk things out. He’ll have the chance to say what he needs to say to me, and I’ll have the chance to apologize for what I did to him. We’ll meet at the clinic. Neither of us will be allowed to have any weapons. It’ll just be the two of us in the room together, and our conversation will be private… but each of us will be allowed to bring friends who will observe the meeting from behind glass. If either of us gets violent, our friends will be able to stop things from going too far.”

                                                I put a hand on her shoulder. “This is a good thing, right?” I said comfortingly.

                                                She nodded. “Definitely… but it won’t be easy.”

                                                “I’ll be there,” I promised, and she gave me a hug (right there in front of everyone, too). “For now, though, I need to get backstage and wrangle the performers.”

                                                I made my way up to the girlfriend table after my last number, and planted myself between Julia and Oleander. I’m sure they noticed that I was tense; my hands were probably shaking. I was a ball of nerves, and both of them eventually asked me what was wrong. I started spouting off about Spidertoes and D, and their relationship, and how it makes me sick to my stomach sometimes to think that one of my honey’s exes has that much pull over her. I apologized to Oleander for dumping my emotions all over her like that, and she just smiled and squeezed my hand and said “no problem, Angel, I like it when you confide in me.”

                                                And it hit me like a bolt from the blue. I will never get over my anxiety about Spidertoes unless I confront it head-on. If D sleeps with her and comes back to me and nothing changes, then it proves what I claim I’ve known all along: that we were meant to be together and nothing can ever come between us. If she continues to keep her distance because I’m asking her to, then I’ll stay tense whenever the two of them get close to each other (physically or metaphorically).

                                                I got up abruptly and strode toward the bar. I asked Jacob for a double-shot of bourbon. He asked me if I was all right (which was apparently hilarious to Tamara and Halley, who had been propping up the bar all evening), and I replied that everything was hunky-dory, or some such nonsense that he saw through instantly. Whatever, he gave me the drink anyway, and I downed it in one gulp.

                                                Thus fortified, I approached D and Spidertoes. They both greeted me warmly, and I made sure to express my heartfelt gratitude to Spidertoes for the actions she had taken on D’s behalf. She graciously accepted my thanks, and nodded her assent when I asked if I could speak with my love alone for a few minutes.

                                                “What’s this all about, babe?” she asked as I led her to a vacant room.

                                                “I’ve changed my mind,” I said. When she stared at me blankly for a couple of seconds, I decided that it might be helpful to elaborate. “About Spidertoes,” I continued. “If you want to sleep with her tonight, then… that’s okay with me.”

                                                D looked at me like I’d sprouted horns. “What?” she asked.

                                                “I trust you,” I said. “I love you. I know that you love me. And I know that you’ll come back to me, no matter what. So I’m going to let go of my jealousy and insecurity. If you tell me it’s just sex, then I’ll take you at your word. Have fun tonight, if that’s what you want to do.”

                                                D took my hand. “Are you sure about this, babe?” she asked. When I nodded, she narrowed her eyes. “And this isn’t going to make you mad or hurt you, right?” she asked as a follow-up.

                                                I nodded again. “I’ll be okay,” I promised. And yes, I realize that I’m probably editing my memory to make myself appear more collected and mature than I actually was, but I’m still pretty proud of the fact that I at least managed to keep my voice level and stop myself from tearing up.

                                                She leaned in for a kiss. “Thank you,” she said softly as we disengaged.

                                                “I love you, D,” I said as she turned away.

                                                She turned back, winked, and grinned at me. “I love you too, Lori,” she said, and her voice was deadly serious.

                                                So I was kind of a wreck when I got back to the Girlfriend Table. Oleander was quick to comfort me, and Julia – God bless her – was a rock even though I know she was fighting off her own insecurities at the time. “Those two aren’t compatible long-term,” she said to me over and over again.

                                                “Look,” Oleander said with an arm around my waist, “if this is a bad night, I understand. I can head back to my place and plan to see you in a few days…”

                                                “No,” I said a little sniffily. “I need you tonight.” I looked over at Julia. “I need you both tonight… if that’s okay.”

                                                The two of them looked at each other and nodded. In truth, Julia looked a little relieved; I don’t think she wanted to spend last night alone either. The three of us headed upstairs before the crowds thinned out. I didn’t want to see D and Spidertoes leaving together; I was afraid I wouldn’t be able to keep my composure. What followed was a night of slow, comforting love. We made love, sure… but we mostly held each other and gave comfort (which I’m sure was a little awkward for Oleander, but she bore it like a champ). I cried more than a little, and made each of them solemnly swear that they wouldn’t tell D that I had cried. The last thing I wanted was for her to feel guilty for having hurt me when I practically pushed her into the act myself.

                                                So no, I didn’t sleep last night. I don’t think Julia did, either, though I’m pretty sure Oleander drifted off at some point (she doesn’t keep the same late hours as the rest of us regularly). I tried to read a medical journal, but just couldn’t keep my mind on it. I staggered down to breakfast as soon as I thought anyone else might be awake (and still ended up sitting around by myself for the better part of an hour).

                                                D arrived just as Gawain was starting to cook, looking tired but satisfied. I quirked an eyebrow at her as she strutted into the kitchen. “You have fun last night?” I asked, trying to put just the right amount of swagger into the question to draw attention away from how needy I suddenly felt.

                                                “Oh yeah,” she said as she strode up to me. “But it’s good to be home,” she said more quietly as her arms settled around me.

                                                She sat down in one of the chairs, and I planted myself firmly in her lap, snuggling up against her and keeping myself squarely in her embrace. I tried not to be too clingy as we chatted, joked, and ate with the others, but I also communicated that I wanted her arms around me, and she seemed happy enough to oblige.

                                                Marguerite arrived a bit later, and Julia sort of let out the breath that she had been holding since the previous evening. Marguerite was cagey about how things had gone with Felicity, but seemed very pleased with herself. Joy. At least she was also sensitive to Julia’s mood, and became quite solicitous after a few minutes.

                                                “Bon-bon,” Julia said after Marguerite had broken her fast, “you said the two of us could take a walk on the pier together after you got back. Are you still up for it?”

                                                Marguerite nodded. “Oui,” she allowed in lazy French.

                                                Julia turned and looked pointedly at D. “Would anyone else like to take a romantic walk along the pier?” she asked.

                                                D sighed and turned to me with a twinkle in her eye. “You up for a walk, babe?” she asked.

                                                I nodded. “I’d love to spend time with you today, D,” I said, “but I’ve got to try to meet with Augustine this afternoon to make sure he’s okay with the deal we made with Hawk.” I turned back to Julia. “Why don’t you two go ahead; D and I will take our own walk later.” Everyone seemed happy with this arrangement, and the two of them bid the rest of us farewell.

                                                I got Old Billy’s number from D and let him know that I’d be by later to speak with his monarch, and he confirmed that Augustine was free from three to six that afternoon. “You want me to come with?” D asked after I’d hung up.

                                                “Are you kidding?” I asked. “If I had my way you’d never leave. Just promise you’ll behave this time, okay?”

                                                D snorted her derision. “I promise I won’t punch him unless he really deserves it,” she growled after a moment.

                                                “D,” I said, “I’m serious. You’ve got to stop antagonizing the monarchs. It’s going to get you in real trouble one of these days.”

                                                She shrugged it off. “Maybe I wouldn’t have such a problem with them if there was a Latino monarch… if there had ever been a Latino monarch in this Freehold.”

                                                I knew it was a mistake as soon as I said it, but sometimes my mouth bypasses my brain. “What does that have to do with anything?” I asked.

                                                D’s look darkened immediately. “I guess there’s nothing wrong with the setup if you’re white,” she muttered.

                                                I put my arms around her neck. “Sorry, honey,” I whispered, “I wasn’t thinking. My idea was that a person’s race doesn’t necessarily make them a better leader… but of course that kind of thinking doesn’t work if any group is being marginalized. I didn’t mean to upset you.”

                                                Her expression cleared a bit. “I know,” she said, giving me a squeeze. “And hey… at least you’re not Julia-crazy.” She rolled her eyes. “Thank God you’re not Julia-crazy. I like that you’re not that possessive.” She chuckled fondly. “Did you hear her and Mags arguing about names earlier? Mags doesn’t want to take Julia’s name because it’s ‘too English,’ and she doesn’t want to let Julia take her name because she thinks her family are all horrible people.”

                                                I laughed fondly. “Silly argument. It doesn’t matter who has what name as long as they love each other. I’m sure they’ll come to their senses soon enough.”

                                                There was an awkward silence. I hate awkward silences; I always feel the need to fill them. Maybe that explains why I decided to mercilessly tease my love.

                                                “I wouldn’t mind taking your name, you know,” I said with a wink.

                                                The blood rushed into her cheeks so quickly that I was honestly afraid she was going to faint. “Excuse me,” she mumbled as she bolted for the door, “I have to go sit alone in the dark for about fifteen minutes. I’ll be back before we go to see Augustine.”

                                                “D…” I said, but it was too late. I couldn’t help but laugh a little at how easy it is to push her buttons sometimes.

                                                True to her word, she came back into the kitchen about a quarter of an hour later. I turned to face her, and noted with pride that most of her capillaries had closed once more, and she had regained her usual porcelain pallor.

                                                “I’m sorry I teased you, honey,” I said.

                                                She gave me a piercing stare. “Is… is that something you really want, babe?” she asked, and I knew that the time for teasing was over.

                                                I thought about it seriously for a few seconds, then shrugged. “Yeah,” I said simply. “I mean, not like right now or anything, but… when I think about the future, and where I want to be in five, ten, or fifty years, well… you’re always there with me, D. You feel the same way, right?”

                                                She didn’t reply immediately. She looked at me. I mean, she really stared at me. Intently. After almost a full minute had passed (another one of those damned awkward silences), something changed in her eyes. She let her breath out in a rush, but got this funny little grin that only touched the corners of her mouth. “Fuck it,” she said in a voice almost too low to hear.

                                                Then she got down on one knee. I could feel my eyebrows shooting up into my hairline.

                                                She took my left hand in both of hers. My mouth fell open in utter shock.

                                                “Juliette, will you marry me?” she asked.

                                                It’s not a question that anyone should answer lightly. It’s a real commitment, and every aspect of what it means to you as a person should be carefully considered before answering yes or no. When it works, a marriage is a compact one enters into for the rest of one’s life… and for changelings in particular that can be a very long time.

                                                Luckily, I’d already considered all of that long ago. I’ve known for six months or more what my answer would be when and if D ever got around to asking me. I think I managed to gasp out a “yes” almost before she finished the question.

                                                I pulled her to her feet and into my arms and we kissed. When we disengaged, Maria had this sort of goofy grin on her face (I can only imagine that it mirrored my own). Then she gave a mock-scowl. “Julia’s gonna kill me for doing this while she was out,” she muttered to herself.

                                                “Do not worry,” Jacob said unexpectedly from across the room. A second later both of our phones chimed. It seems that our quick-thinking Russian friend had snapped a photograph of D proposing and then texted it to the motley.

                                                D’s phone rang an almost impossibly short time later. “Yeah?” she asked as she answered it. “Well, no, it’s not like I planned it or anything. No, I didn’t have a ring. No, we haven’t picked a date… Christ, Julia, it’s only been like ninety seconds since I asked her!”

                                                After my honey had successfully fended off the interrogation, we started talking seriously again. “I was serious before, you know,” I said as the talk turned to names. “I wouldn’t mind taking your name, if it’s okay with you. I mean, I’d be replacing my own fake name with your real name, right?”

                                                D shrugged. “When you put it that way, it makes sense. We have plenty of time to decide, though.”

                                                “We should head over to the church to talk to Augustine soon,” I said. “You guys want to come with?”

                                                Most of the remaining motley decided to accompany us, but Gawain hemmed and hawed for a minute. “I’d like to go with you, but I really need to hit the grocery stores if we’re going to host Julia’s parents for dinner tonight. I was thinking Greek…”

                                                “I could go shopping for you,” Desiree offered. “I’m not Augustine’s favorite person anyway, so I was going to skip the meeting.”

                                                Gawain made a list and gave Desiree a little money. I made sure to thank her for her kindness. Maybe it’s the giddiness of the engagement speaking, but I’m beginning to think I’ve been a little unfair to her lately. It’s true that what she did isn’t good for Marguerite, but she honestly thought it was the best way to protect the Freehold from the Director. And I’m sure Gawain and Calamity will have her ship-shape in no time.

                                                I confess that I’ve missed having her as a friend. No one else around here is quite as big a theater fan as I am.
