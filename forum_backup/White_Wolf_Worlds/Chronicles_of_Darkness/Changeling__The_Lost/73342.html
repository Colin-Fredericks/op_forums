    What are Goblin Contracts?

    02-16-2014, 04:36 PM
    According to the lexicon in the core book, a Goblin Contract is "an illicit Contract, typically easy to learn but with unpleasant side effects." Each one can be bought separately, unlike the standard Contract Clauses, though they are sometimes grouped together based on thematic powers (such as the Goblin Contracts of the Wyrd). The blessings of a Goblin Contract tend toward trickery and luck, and all exact some sort of price, be it a bit of misfortune on the user's part or a permanent dot of Stamina.

    I guess my question is, with your run-of-the-mill Contracts, there's a noun--an object or phenomenon--that's bargained with. Fire or dreams or Spring. Things you can, in one way or another, point to, anthropomorphize, render into symbols and poetics. What's at the other end of a Goblin Contract though? The opening fiction to the Character Creation chapter, as well as some of the expounding in Rites of Spring, imply a semi-hallucinatory relationship when a changeling activates a Contract. So, what does using Trading Luck For Fate or Blood Binding look like, feel like? It's also implied that most Goblin Contracts are acquired at Goblin Markets, which is fine, but what does it mean in-fiction to purchase one?

      Well, if Contracts are sorcerous deals agreed to by anthropomorphized platonic ideals that manifest bodily when properly called upon ... it would follow that Goblin Contracts are pretty similar, save that they are oaths sworn with goblins. Goblins themselves have a wide variety of innate magical abilities and it seems that they are litigious in the most predatory manner. If making an agreement with a fundamental aspect of reality bestows you with a measure of its power, wouldn't it make sense that signing a contract with a goblin would do the same? Also, we know that anything can be sold, bought or traded at Goblin Markets. I assume that this hold's true for a goblin's personal array of petty sorceries.

      Maybe there is an entire class of goblin lawyers who exist as executors of goblin contracts. If this is the case, when a goblin contract is used, the changeling suddenly becomes aware of the presence of the goblin lawyer. The lawyer takes the clauses' payment, affirms the contract's catch and executes the spell on behalf of the goblin whose magic it utilized. Lower level uses of goblin contracts are accommodated by a host of differing goblin lawyers while more powerful contracts are attended by a specific goblin lawyer of notoriety and prestige. Goblin lawyers themselves possess all of the most despicable characteristics associated with those that practice Law, and if this is so ... changelings who deal with them too much risk running afoul of the worst kinds of magical litigation.

                      I did imagine that the Goblin contracts were whatever the Goblins negotiated with those Platonic powers, but when I look them over and see that then tend to be rather cruel, I want to attribute them to Keeper magics. Who else would give you a poisoned gift?

                      When responding to the second part of the question, I tend to think of these contracts as more ritual based. The goblin gives you something make from a Goblin (a ring made of toenail clippings, for example) and directions about how to activate it. For example, for Trading Luck for Fate, while wearing your bit of Goblin, whisper to a mirror
                      I saw a ship a-sailing,
                      A-sailing on the sea.
                      And, oh, but it was laden
                      With pretty things for thee.

                      There were comfits in the cabin,
                      And apples in the hold;
                      The sails were made of silver
                      And the masts were made of gold.

                      Then break the mirror underfoot to activate.

                          The way I see it Changeling powers all come from the Wyrd and its labyrinthine laws. Some things you are entitled to as a Changeling, like kith blessings, oneiromancy, ability to open hedge gates and so on, while other things you have to barter for. Goblin contracts represent things the Wyrd does not want to give you, being able to wave your hands and drop the enemy stone dead is narratively unsatisfying, so it makes you pay for it. Normal contracts give you a clause that allows you to use them without spending glamour, Goblin Contracts require you to satisfy their clauses in addition to ponying up with glamour to get them to work at all to keep these powers under control.
