                        05-08-2015, 01:48 AM
                        Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
                        I wouldn't say it's necessarily "they look like leaders" or "they are so charismatic people follow them". Rather, it is simply that they are the lords and ladies of Arcadia, and whether you want it or not you cannot deny that they are masters there. Their authority is by virtue of the beings they are, monsters and heroes and demons and angels of legend, evident in their presence that bears down on any who see their true nature.

                        I could buy that. Some glimmer of the Fae's might shines through them. However, their blessing seems to inspire trust and confidence, instead of fear and awe like the presence of Fae. It's less Fearsome Mein of the Fae and more Poise of the Politician. Their "duty and responsibility peek through" not their awe-inspiring power. Since the flavor is so different, if this power is indeed intended to be a shadow of the Fae's, it bears addressing that in the Seeming description.

                        If the Fairest are really meant to be the closest to the True Fae, why is this not alluded to in the draft? And why, then, are their Blessing and Curse focused on others, in contradiction to Fae attitudes?

                        If their power is meant to be a glimmer of the Gentry's might, then how was it acquired? And why does it manifest so differently than it does in the Fae?

                        The 2e Seemings are supposed to be all about how the Changeling escaped and how that changed them forever. So, how does a decision to help others and risk yourself (one of the most un-Gentry things you could do) lead the character to become more like the Strangers?

                        I mean, I really like this concept. Perhaps the Fairest somehow learned to imitate the Keepers' power and used that to help others escape. Or the Fairest stole a portion of their Keeper's power. Maybe a selfless act in their Keeper's realm allowed them to steal and invert some of the Keeper's power because of some ancient oath, contract, or pledge. Or maybe their self-sacrificing act just caused the Wyrd to reshape them as a sort of negative image of the Keeper's with a power that achieves similar results by an opposite means. Any of these things would be cool, but there's no indication of anything like that in the text. As of the first draft, there's nothing to support the Fairest being closest to the Fae, or their power being anything other than that - theirs, not an echo or reflection of the authority that the Keepers wield by might.

                                        Truth. However, the point I had been trying to make with the quote from the book was that an enticing/commanding aura was not a feature of all True Fae. All of the Fae that are enticing have merits, like Striking Looks or Inspiring, and/or very high social stats to make up for their innate otherworldly weirdness. The social penalties they experience around mortals, however, are a feature of all True Fae. So it isn't justifiable to say that an aura that inspires trust, confidence, or deference is a particularly Fae trait anymore than, say, claws or fangs or elemental features are. We've seen at least as many Fae with no such social abilities and traits that are similar to other Seemings instead. So that power, by itself, especially as presented, doesn't justify them being "closest to the Fair Folk". Maybe something else about them does. Or maybe the Fair Folk in 2e are different.

                                        I feel like instead of them being close to the True Fae, it's more of a "darker the negative the brighter the picture" sort of deal.
