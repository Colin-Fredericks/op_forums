    I think a Freehold Law or Pledge against dealing with Huntsmen would do the opposite of actually mitigating or dealing with the conflict of interest. It would exacerbate it. In the moment of truth, it would divide changelings even more between survival and loyalty to their companions. Faced with a deal by a Huntsmen in a position of weakness, they have no options. A Huntsman would not just leave a refusal be, but accepting a Token means breaking a promise on the Wyrd and marking yourself as a traitor.

    So congrats, you've managed to put the entire Freehold consistently on edge about getting caught in the crossfire between Huntsmen and their quarry - which as previous discussion and examples have established is not going to be rare, because they have methods more complex than "beeline for the quarry". From where I stand, the set up will see it fracture under the pressure.

    Changelings want Tokens. None want to be the person who accepts one, but they know that in their place they would do the same. There is no confidence to be gained from a pledge bound law to not negotiate with Huntsmen for tokens. Just the assurance that the freehold is liable to break apart because people are inevitably going to do it driven by never going back.

    That's assuming that a ruler would actually manage to implement it and it wouldn't get shut down by an at best lukewarm response of silent opposition.

                        Okay, so because there's always people who don't get the point(and it's often consistently the same people), we'll try going at this in a less flowery way.

                        Simply put, changelings are both paranoid and opportunistic.

                        It's not an uncommon pairing. Fairies often aren't regular features in the lives of characters we follow in stories, they often keep to themselves. But, when you get in touch with them, they like to take the window and get something out of it. Changelings, broadly speaking, play this too a tee. On the one hand, no one wants to stick their neck out too far. Even Summer preferred to not stick their neck completely out-they might go to war, but they preferred guerilla tactics. On the other hand, when encounters happen, they often try to score something out of it, if for no other reason that it gives them an edge in the future. With this in mind, let's go through and point out some problems with the "We'll just keep the Huntsmen from ever being a problem!" mentality.

                        1) "Just Don't bargain with the Huntsmen!"

                        Yeah, no.

                        For a second, let's ignore the fact that Tokens of Reprieve are commodity that people want, regardless of the consequences, and hit instead on a much larger issue- the condemnation of anyone who fails to beat the Huntsmen.

                        Again, people seem to think that the Huntsmen coming as an invading army, willing to challenge the mass. They aren't. They don't hit the crab's shell, they strike for the joints. They go after their target through cleverness, cunning, and exploitation. If they can't get their target, they go after someone weaker. The Huntsmen are not above grabbing a newly returned Changeling, dragging them back to the gates, and then giving them an ultimatum-take the bittersweet deal or die/go back to Arcadia.

                        And what are you supposed to do in those cases? You're clearly outclassed, and you have I will never go back pounding away at your being. Are you supposed to just give up and sacrifice yourself for the greater good? Sure, maybe, but the Lost are going to lose a lot of people to that. If you take the bargain, you stand a chance of finding a window between your betrayal and their return to make it all right, you might tell yourself if you're noble. But in all honesty, you're probably not. You're scared out of your self, you're falling into the hopelessness of the scenario, and even if the devil is offering a hand up, why would you reject it when the alternative is hell?

                        But nooooooooooooooooooooo, some people out there want everyone to sacrifice for the greater good whether they want to or not. Any changeling in a freehold with a Societal Oath saying "Don't ever make bargains with the Huntsmen" are fucked. Any changeling who gets stuck in that scenario is gonna have to either die or go back to hell, and the Huntsmen will exploit this to shit. They'll send little public messages, hanging from nooses off the Ruler's house, little boxes in the talkative ones' houses with fingers and ears, and little messages that simply state "If only they had another choice". A Huntsman'll ramp that shit up until people are clamoring for the Ruler's heads. They might not make a deal, but if a Huntsman show up and says "I want your King", they might just sit by and let him do it.

                        And in that moment, man oh man, won't a Ruler wish there were some Repreives in circulation then.

                        Which brings me back to my original point. Everyone wants one, no one wants to earn it. A Huntsman coming is a terrible thing, but it's also an opportunity. Arcadians aren't the only ones who scheme and machinate, changelings are just as guilty as that as anyone. By not having said clause, you create an opening for, at best, saving graces for future tragedies and, at worst, you cover your own ass when the Huntsmen come for you. You don't have it for anything if you close the doors.

                        But hey, let's ignore that for a second, and say that you have them coming in somehow, but you still want to control the way people get it. This brings me to....

                        2) "Check In Your Token of Reprieve at the Door!"

                        Fucking hell, the possible logistics of this in a freehold give me a headache. But before we talk about logistics, let's return to a very, very simple fact of changelings.

                        NO CHANGELING WANTS A TARGET ON THEIR BACK!

                        LEt me emphasize a certain part so people get the idea.

                        NO CHANGELING WANTS A TARGET ON THEIR BACK!

                        To illustrate the problem, let's assume a changeling bought a Token of Reprieve at a Goblin Market, about as honest as a way to get is they can. That changeling now has to go and inform at least one person(this ties into the logistical problem) that they have a Token of Reprieve. Which, you know, is a hot commodity people are more than willing to get their hands on. So, now, our changeling her is outed that he has a Get Out of Jail Free card, and they have to live with the attention of that fact. On the immediate side, the changeling can expect to be robbed, blackmailed, threatened, and/or generally have people to trying to spirit said Reprieve away. But, fine, let's assume that people aren't going to do that, because then they need to tell one person how they got it, and that can get them in trouble(Ignoring the thousand and one ways this can be subverted, because logistics is a bitch). You still have people trying to bargain, flatter, seduce, reason,hassle, and generally doing what they can to get you to part with it with no small amount of frequency. And that entire time, you, a paranoid creature trying to hide, are just attracting more and more attention. Guess who's getting targeted. Fuck, even if not all of that, when shit hits the fan, our changeling is still the "Break Glass In Case of Emergency" chick, and if she isn't willing to play ball, a bullet might be deemed excusable.

                        NO CHANGELING WANTS A TARGET ON THEIR BACK!

                        And that's what this basic system, however you incarnate it. Whether you tell it to a private official or the whole of the freehold, it's a target on your back, and for creatures like changelings, that sort of thing is going fuck with their Clarity, at the least. It makes lives miserable, and if life is miserable, why the fuck bother coming back?

                        Oh, speaking of, let's talk logistics, because fuck man, legalese.

                        First off, an acknowledgment-yes, Changelings are masters of legalese. they are all about clause after clause after clause of minutia and details and filling holes.

                        Except when it cuts them off. See, every changeling(let me say that again, EVERY CHANGELING) wants rooms to wiggle out of. Everyone wants an escape, because they understand the dangers of restriction. Changelings are forever in a catch-22 so long as they are paranoid to such a degree as to need massive legal documents to keep themselves safe, because every wall they set up is a wal that potentially imprisons them again. They can either be naked but free or secure but chained. Most freeholds struggle deeply with finding the right balance of this, because changeling will react violently to not feeling safe enough and to feeling too bound up.

                        So, yeah, changelings could just wall this up with harshness upon harshness upon harshness-but they aren't going to, because that makes them fucking uncomfortable as hell.

                        So, logistics. The various incarnations of "Announce to the public" all kind of suck. For starters, let's assume you just have to tell a official of the freehold who has the jurisdiction to hear such things. Well, okay. What happens if we're out in the Hedge, I get one, and I tell the guy and shoot him afterwards to keep my secret, lying to the court about what happened? Nothing, the system is pointless. But, okay, they have provisions against that. That means you still have one person who knows your secret alone, although he might be obliged to tell the other officials about it. That takes times and creates windows for exploitation easily enough, and in the mean time, that one guy has power over you, even if pledges keep him from being horrible about it. Hell, nothing says people have to believe him when you tell him you got it at Market-he might say it with complete sincerity, and the community will still feel paranoid just because it fucking exists. And if he doesn't have to say anything, there's some many ways to take advantage of him before he takes advantage of you. So, to avoid those pitfalls, what, you have to tell a lot of people? What, do you have to get a number of people? What do you do in the meantime? What if you can't get a number together in time? can you just tell your con-conspirators that you have it? Or do you have to tell the whole damn freehold? Does this mean the freehold has to gather every night a Huntsman is in town just to hear "Hey guys, I must solemnly and sincerely tell you I got my Reprieve from the Market?", all the while giving your enemies a nice shot to gather you up?

                        The instant you make a law that says Reprieves must be made public, you open a fucking Pandora's Box of exposure and manipulation, a problem that can't happen as much if people just keep their damn mouths shut about it. It's might feel safer to make it public, but if freeholds already run a risk of being a den of vipers biting at each other, these sorts of rules fucking just accept it as a loss. And in the end, it's not that useful, because....

                        3) "I can trust who I'm talking to!"

                        It's funny, to me, how what you say is what keeps society glued together as changelings, considering that who you look like is never consistent anyways.

                        Like, is this a me thing? Am I the only one who grew up with fairy tales where the constant theme, the major motif, was "Nothing is as it seems?" Did I just grow up with more tricksters than anyone else here? I don't think I am. For the record, this point might be exaggerated a little, but still, it must be made.

                        The point here is that fairies are all about illusion and dream. It's less prevalent in Second Edition, but I must reiterate again- The Contracts of Smoke and Mirror were assumed to be affinity powers for all changelings.

                        Why is no one really in terror of that fact? Why does no one feel scared about it? Because just because it's easy to get doesn't ,mean everyone has, of course. But still!

                        STILL!

                        That's scary as fuck!

                        Because you can walk the fuck into anywhere, say something totally untrue, and people will believe you, because you're bound by pledges to say certain things!

                        AND THAT YOU WAS NEVER YOU!!

                        It gets doubly scary in the scenarios put forth by people who are wrong here, because it encourages a subversive system that invites people to make friends with people who can easily change their shape and lie for them-ie, people outside of the freehold system, who are less trustworthy than the freehold. But that's what happens, this scenario invites changelings to find ways to cheat their defenses for their own freedom, and the Huntsmen and the Loyalists lick their lips and love every fucking second of it, because the system is so rigid that a lie can really break everything down. A Huntsmen just has to walk in as someone else while they aren't there, say "Hey, I fucking sold out someone to the Huntsmen for a token" ,disappear cackling, and that person is going to be in deep shit, because, what they found a way to flaunt the pledge just to piss people off?

                        And that's one of several nightmare scenarios! In a group of creatures who are already skittish and mercurial, trying to stamp down and keep them penned up just pushes the subterfuge card even more! And the more personally restrictive it is, the more people are going to rely on their bug-out kits being held by people in not in the freehold. You encourage the game of faces by being the fucking Panopticon! It's just-changelings are inherently slippery, illusory, deceptive creatures. That's how they live! That's how they survive!

                        It doesn't fucking help, because it immediately pressures any changeling who has a bad day into a much bigger compromise than they would have been on their own. Of course, we can get all third Reich with this and-Hey, not a bad idea.

                        4) "Are you a Loyalist or not?"

                        The following spoiler is only tangentially related. In fact, let's just say it's not, but I enjoy my excuses anyways.


                        HAHAHAHAHHAHAHAHA-huuuuuuuuuh, whew, that was...that was stupid. Well, okay, no the video is funny enough, but it sort of hits on the humor of the "Are you a Loyalist or now?", because like every other binding, blinding, suffocating lightblub surveillance trick here, it's not sane.

                        The simple fact is that while this might root out some loyalists, what this does fundamentally is just create a cloud of paranoia that unsettles the freeholders into a constant state of distrust, which is easily exploitable, and gives the Huntsmen and Loyalists room to just slip on through, because said pledges don't affect them anyways. This inquisition mindset will do much the same as any of the other suggestions and cause changelings to stop relying on the freehold and instead start making clever deals outside of the freehold. I don't want to waste any more time on this. I wanna make one other quick point, and then I'm going to actually answer this entire damn mindset.

                        $@#%!) "The Loyalists are invaders from the outside, not infiltrators!"

                        Okay.

                        Wut.

                        It's abundantly clear, by now, that this idea doesn't truck with me, because the way changelings will work with these Pledge ideas, let alone Loyalists and Huntsmen, leads to a total break down. But I've harped on that enough, it's time I questioned the mentality this comes in.

                        Asides from it just not fucking making sense logically, I have to wonder where people thinks this fits into the scheme of things in Changeling the Lost, or even the larger Chronicles of Darkness. This game rarely favors barbarians at the gate, and often it's just a setup for the larger problems of the infilitrators on the inside. It's never about just the brute and the brawler, it's often far more about the sneaks and the connivers.

                        I mean, stop for a second and think about how most people handle werewolves-hell, even how the World of Darkness handles werewolves in Apocalypse. Big bruisers, death machines, loud, noisy, blunt. They're mauls.

                        But Chronicle's werewolves? They're scapels. They are dangerously sneaky, clever, and exact. They still have some brute possibility, but for the most part, their hunt is a thing of guerilla tactics and precision moments. They are subtle knives.

                        And that's fucking werewolves, one of the most commonly loud and noisy creatures in the supernatural genre. Fairies are gonna be damnably conniving.

                        it's fun to have Loyalists at the gate. It is! I often do use them this way, I've said as much. But the point of having anything at the gate is to give the Loyalists on the inside some room to move and change things. Loyalists( and Huntsmen) are always going to slip their way into the cracks at the joints, they're never going to actually handle the walls. They slip in and they cause chaos and havoc.

                        So, enough of this, it's time for...

                        5)"Okay, So HOW is this supposed to work?"

                        "But Arc," I say to myself in a different voice for entertainment value, " Pledges are supposed to the be glue for changeling society! If all these ideas suck so hard, why would any changeling bother using Pledges at all to make their society secure? I argue that you're fundamentally wrong on this basis!"

                        "Well," I respond to myself in my normal voice, "that would be true if I was telling myself that the basis we're approaching Pledges as was correct. But the biggest problem we have right now is an understanding of just what, metaphorically, Pledges are in a changeling society."

                        And then I drop the act, because I don't have hand puppets to make this fun. But still, there's a fundamental point here that needs to be addressed, the principle on which these ideas fail.

                        PLEDGES ARE NOT WALLS.

                        They aren't walls or curtains or inquisitions that you can use to keep out the enemy. That's not what Pledges, as a basic function of the Wyrd, do, it's not even how they act. The Wyrd is a connective force, it bridges one thing to another thing. It's not a wall. Pledges are string and ribbon, or maybe in their strongest iconography, chains. They bind people together.

                        How does that matter? Well, let's get down to it. Are the ideas presented here viable in any way? And I answer, yes! And you ask, How, Mister Pedantic?

                        Easy, I say.

                        Stop playing at the macro-level and apply it to the micro-level.

                        Scoietal Oaths acting as impositions have to cover too much and just foster resentment and distrust. But at the motley level? With people you trust and care about, or at least really want to? Here, it's a lot more effective, because it's tight knit, personal, fueled by trust rather than paranoia. It's not someone infringing on anyone, it's each person keeping each other safe, it's their idea and their bindings.

                        Freeholds don't build walls. Societal Oaths can't be walls. What Societal Oaths do is provide a framework, a loom, from which Personal Oaths stretch out to wrap around. Freeholds build little clusters of safety, then daisy chain them back and forth within the framework as motleys and function groups tie together inevitably, until there's this strong, but flexible net of security the unites everyone together and builds genuine camaraderie far more easily than the tyrannical methods impositions from on high.

                        These ideas at the Motley level fill in the cracks and is harder to fool because there's a higher level of knowledge of others that backs that trust up-loyalists and Huntsmen aren't demons, and are much more likely to crack under the eyes of their 'mates' than those of the freehold. It means there's less logistics, because motleys govern those details themselves. These ideas, an expression of respecting borders, menas there's less pushing and greed and envy and general backstabbing.

                        Now, I know what I'm thinking you're thinking. "But Arc, you can't make every group try to do that shit, anymore than you can make it work on the large scale!"

                        Not wrong, and here's where the officials of a freehold have to trust their citizens-but the odds are in their favor. The odds of a motley being willing to tightly bind themselves to one another is pretty high, and from there, it's just working to get motleys to build up trust and faith in each other, which is still easier than getting them to trust a gargantuan force like the freehold system or the freehold mob. It's chainmail, not plate.

                        Conclusion

                        Look, I'll be honest and blunt, because apparently that works. These ideas, forced at a freehold level, are stupid. Really, really stupid. Changelings as a whole just won't work with it, because it's too much, it's too Gentry-esque. Sure, on the motley level, it's a lot more likely to happen, and from there you just have to network one motley to another until you have a solid enough wall. That has it's own risks, but it's a lot more likely to happen and stand. But as a freehold wide imposition?

                        No. It's stupid.

                                                It's not badwrongfun. It's not like I haven't had some of these assumptions myself.

                                                BUT it's important that these assumptions are fan assumptions and not subject to what the text has said. A lot of what I've said is true for First Edition-it's all there in the text of the core and expanded on in the supplements. Second Edition has been built on that foundation. If people aren't understanding why something works because of a failure to appreciate what the long-standing design pointed out in the text was doing, someone has to point out what the 'canon' point of departure.

                                                Beyond that, Let me drop the freelance writer title for a second and just simply say "No, this sort of attitude doesn't make sense to me." Despite seeing how things have built up, really, it's just that. If I was a player in these scenarios, I couldn't invest in them. It breaks my suspension of disbelief.

                                                If other people can see how that works, great? I guess? Until they can't see how something is supposed to work. I enjoy the Huntsmen, and as a player I would want to go against one as a player. But people probably wouldn't use them, because their vision doesn't give the Huntsmen the base assumptions they need to work, and so I, as a player, would always be left wanting at that table.

                                                You can't expect me to listen to you all say "The Huntsmen don't work" when it's clear that crucial facts about the game seemingly got missed by you all. This is why I have made such a clear point of going back to the core basics that Changeling works on. You can go back to having fun with the wrong way after that, but I'm going to have my say all the same.

                                                If anything, I'm trying to keep people from severing fun with bad logic.

                                                    I genuinely don't see why people would want to avoid the Huntsmen. Like, I get it if you don't want to use them. But if you are going to use them, it makes no sense to try and "gotcha" the Huntsmen. You can't "gotcha" a creature that is literally made of Arcadia and True Fae. You can *defeat* it, sure, and you can escape its Grasp, but you can't just pre-emptively deny it what it's meant to do. And Changelings know this! They have Fate and Narrative in their blood! They know how the story is supposed to go!

                                                    Trying to deny the Huntsmen the use of their strengths before they even show up is such a... *human* thing to do. It's just not how stories go.

                                                    Now, what can a smart Freehold do against the Huntsmen? Prepare. Keep an ear in the news for murders. Document all Huntsmen tells they know of and have everyone memorise them. Learn and document as much as they can about Huntsmen in general and teach it freely. Cold iron, always keep some in the Freehold somewhere. Magic, use whatever magic you have to add to your state of preparedness. Develop quirks, things that might make the Huntsmen harder to imitate you, something that might also help cement your clarity. Delve into Hedge lore and develop Entitlements to help you and the Freehold.

                                                    You can't weasel your way out of a story. But you can be genre savvy and prepared.
