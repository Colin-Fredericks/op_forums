                A fairy tale that I think would work really well in a Changeling: The Lost setting, is the story of Undine. The story begins with a fisherman's daughter drowning, when she falls into a lake. However, the next morning, they find her wet and asleep in her cot (some versions imply while others state outright that undine was exchanged for a nymphs daughter and is a changeling). The child is very quit and mild. However, when they take her to a church to be baptized and prayers are said, the child begins to shriek and throw and tantrum. As Undine grows up, she become an extremely beautiful woman, but due to not having a soul, she is also very selfish. One day, a knight comes to the fisherman's cottage and upon seeing Undine, falls in love with her. After strong hesitation from Undine's father, the fisherman, the knight eventually convinces him to allow the knight to marry Undine. While the knight and Undine are traveling back to his kingdom, he makes a vow to love her for all of eternity. During the marriage, God gives Undine a soul, which makes her sweet and gentle. In the knight's kingdom, it just so happens that they knight has a lady friend who is also in love with him. Initially, she and Undine get along well, but once she learns that Undine is a water spirit, she sets all her power of persuasion to convince the knight that Undine is a malevolent spirit.

                Tragically, the girl's poisoned words begin to take hold and Undine is pained to see the knight's love for her leave his eyes. One day, Undine, the knight, and the girl are horse riding, when the girl loses her necklace. The knight and his girlfriend accuse Undine of dropping the necklace in the river. When Undine reaches her hand in and brings up a more beautiful necklace, the knight cries, "you are no Maiden! you are an evil witch! Away! return to the lake and bother us no more!" In despair, Undine throws herself into the water, never to be seen again. The Knight and his girlfriend plan on getting married. However, on the night of their wedding a watery figure that looks a lot like Undine pulls herself out of the well and states "You promised to love me for all eternity, now you must pay with your life." and the knight dies.

                ​I can hear a lot of people saying "That's not How the story goes!" My defense is that there are variations of this tale and this is the one I enjoy most.

                If I were to introduce Undine in a Changeling chronicle, I would make her a waterborne elemental, winter courtier, and duchess of the ice bound heart.

                                    Hansel and Gretel’s story begins just before the Black Death burst on the world stage, in the year 1316. After the European economy imploded and the little Ice Age of 1300 had begun. When people were so desperate that they were digging up graves to eat the corpses of the dead….. and they began eating each other. In a part of Europe that would become Germany a husband and wife were talking to each other. They did not have enough food to feed themselves and certainly not their son and daughter, Hansel and Gretel. The wife told the husband to take the children to the woods and leave them there. When they were abandoned and knowing that their parents would would kill and eat them if they returned, they decided to seek survival in the forest. After walking a distance, they were astonished to come upon a house made of gingerbread, cakes, and candy. Starving, they fell upon the house like ravenous wolves. What they did not know was that the house was a lure for a True Fae in the form of a man eating hag. The had quickly snatches Hansel and Gretel, spiriting them away to her realm in Arcadia. There, she fattens Hansel up by forcing him to eat the the flesh of changelings escaping their keeper’s realms, humans, and hobgoblins. The Hag makes Gretel her slave, but also teaches her the secrets of witchcraft for some reason.

                                    The Hag has a minor bane, which she carries with her in her realm in Arcadia. She is blind, which the children use to her advantage, using a bone to convince the terrifying Crone the Hasel in thin, when he has actually become monstrously fat. Her second major weakness is connected to her True Fae nature, she lacks creativity. After growing frustrated that Hansel is not fattening up, she decides to eat both of them. Gretel pretends not to understand when the witch asks her to check the stove. Infuriated, the Hag demonstrates and Gretel pushes her in and shuts the stoves door. This is the major bane of the True Fae and while it does not kill the True Fae, it DOES destroy that actor and removes a great deal of its power. This allows Hansel and Gretel to escape her realm and eventually return to medieval Europe.

                                    When they return to the mortal realm, they join the Autumn Court and build dual identities as witch and witch hunter. In their lives as changelings, they participate in all of the Autumn courts heathen rituals and spreading fear as well as developing their powers over the occult. In their mortal lives, they were fanatical participants in the witch hunts of Germany, personally taking part in the arrests, torture and burning of accused witches throughout the 14 through 1600s. Presently, these grotesques serve as the German Autumn Court’s most frightening inquisitors.

                                    Hansel is a ogre seeming Gristlegrinder with five dots in the potent kith merit. He can do bite attacks without needing to grapple and bite deals +10 lethal damage. He is also capable of digesting non-edible foods like bike tires, car parts, silver utensil, ect. He is also immune to bashing and lethal damagee, though he take aggravated damage normally. Finally, his fat acts a form of armor that protects him not only from edged weapons and fire arms, but also falling debris, missiles, hot and cold effects. He can also hold/hide items in the folds of his fat.

                                    Gretel is a darkling of the Witchtooth kith. She has the potent kith merit, which gives her the 9, 8, and 7 again rule in all occult roles. Her blessing also has the effects of the five levels of the defunct Vampire: The Masquerade discipline Maleficia, the Dark Thaumaturgy Path of Pain, and access to the scrying merit, which she activates with glamor. Her sense of smell is so keen, she can not only smell where people are, but what they are feeling.

                                                Pinnocchio
                                                Along time ago, in the mid1800’s there was an elderly impoverished, woodcarver named Geppetto. Geppetto had a son, who was a rascal, imp, rogue, disgrace, and even Geppetto referred to him as “a wretched boy.” His son’s name was Pinnocchio. One day, a talking crickets, (who has lived in Geppetto’s house for over a hundred years) warns Pinnocchio of the dangers of disobedience and hedonism. Pinnocchio thanks the cricket by killing it with a hammer. Thus, does Pinnocchio’s misfortune begin.
                                                On his way to school, Pinnocchio see’s a puppet theater is being put on. Deciding to sell his school book (which Geppetto sacrificed his only woolen jacket to buy), Pinnocchio obtains a ticket eager to see the show. Much to his horror, however, the Great Puppet Master Mangiafuoco, carves him into wood a ties him up with strings. Pinnochio becomes one of Mangiafuoco’s marionettes’ putting on performances for the Gentry. In his place, Mangiafuoco creates a boy of thread, wool, and hay, with five gold coins for Geppetto.

                                                Day after day, night after night, Pinnocchio is forced to sing and dance for the True Fae, putting on the most painful, humiliating and entertaining acts for the True Fae. However, the Gentry find Pinnocchio duller and duller and he fears that Mangiafuoco will chop in into firewood. While Mangiafuoco is counting his money, Pinnocchio manages to escape. While fleeing into the Hedge, he comes across a freehold buried deep in the brambles. Stopping for a draft of beer, he meets a Fox and Cat, who after drinking and wenching with them, abandon him to pay for their meal.

                                                While traveling through the Hedge, he is ambushed by the Fox and Cat, who are actually racketeers, planning to sell Pinocchio to the highest bidder among the True Fae. Entertaining themselves, by hanging him from a tree and watching him swing. When they leave him, he is rescued by a motley of a Fairst(the Blue Fairy), a Falcon, and a Poodle. They take him to their freehold, where Pinnocchio must work for food. Tired of working, begin’s his quest to return to Geppetto’s cottage.

                                                Along the way, Pinnochio meet’s the Coachman, who has a coach full of children. The Coachman promises to take Pinnocchio to Toyland, a place where children do nothing but play all day. On the Fifth day, however, Pinnocchio and all the children have turned into donkeys and are sold to a circus where they are forced to perform tricks. However, Pinnocchio sprains his leg, and is skinned and thrown into the ocean.

                                                Once again a puppet, Pinnocchio swims through the Ocean, but is swallowed by a Dogfish larger than an island. For an entire year, Pinnocchio lives inside the Dogfish, eating the water the beast swallows. One day, however, Pinnocchio gathers enough wood to start a fire, which suffocates the Dogfish and forces it to the surface.

                                                Escaping, he returns to the brambles of the Hedge and finally finds a doorway back to the mortal world. At the entrance, he meets the Cat and Fox, who have been tortured and maimed for letting Pinnocchio escape. Pinnocchio mocked them and stated that their misfortune served them right for their wickedness.

                                                Pinnocchio finally came to Geppetto’s cottage convinced that he would be able to regain the life he lost. However, there was another boy there who Geppetto loved even greater than he. For this boy was a dutiful son, who took care of Geppetto in his ailing health, studied hard at school, and even works at a farm to help support Geppetto.

                                                Unable to regain his old life, Pinnochio steals the identity of Carlo Collodi, an Italian children’s writer, and writes a HIGHLY edited version of his misadventures in Arcadia and the Hedge.

                                                        I found an old legend that, although focusing on a ghost like character, could easily be reinterpreted as a Changeling. I also think he would make an interesting word 10 changeling though I can see people stating he would make a better huntsman.

                                                        Herne the Hunter ‘Herne’ was one of the keepers of the ‘Forest of Windsor’ in the reign of ‘King Richard II’ and known for his great hunting and woodcraft skills. Whilst King Richard favoured Herne his fellow hunters it is said hated him and plotted to cause Herne’s downfall. One day the royal party were out on a hunt the king was nearly killed when attacked by a stag. Herne stepped in to help the king taking the main blow and fell to the floor. He seemed to be dead. Suddenly a dark figure appeared amongst the party and announced himself as ‘Philip Urswick’. He then proceeded to inform the king that for a reward he would cure Herne. After cutting the head off the stag and binding it to Herne’s head, the party took Herne to Urswick’s own hut which was located on ‘Bagshot Heath’. Urswick vowed that he would take great care of Herne. King Richard then announced that if Herne recovered he would promote Herne to be the chief keeper. Unbeknown to the king the other hunters were later in contact with Urswick and told him of their loathing for the favoured keeper announcing that they were disappointed that he had not died in the incident. Urswick promised the hunters revenge but only if his first wish and the reward was granted. He told them that Herne would recover but would loose all his hunting skills. Satisfied with his answer, the hunters agreed. Keeping his promise to the king it seemed, Urswick soon had Herne returned to court whereupon he was promoted to chief keeper. Herne seemed to have recovered thoroughly until it became apparent that his hunting skills had disappeared. The king was extremely disappointed with Herne revoked the promotion. It is said that this is the reason why Herne, being so grieved by the king’s actions, hanged himself from an Oak tree in Windsor forest. His body disappeared under suspicious circumstances. Urswick did not reveal the charm that he had cast upon Herne to the king. A new chief hunter was appointed but he too, once promoted, lost his skill. The same happened to his successor too. Urswick was asked to remove the charm. Before making any agreement, Urswick informed the hunters that they would have to meet him at the Oak. Once there they would be told what had to be done to dispel the charm. The group of hunters arrived at the Oak as asked and after waiting a short time Urswick appeared. He told the them that Herne’s death was on each of them and that horses and hounds should be brought to the oak the next night. Agreeing they made preparations and returned to the forest. On reaching the Oak Herne appeared on a horse and told them to follow him to another area of the forest. Herne took the party to a Beech tree and whilst there Urswick suddenly appeared out of the tree covered in flames. Herne had summoned Urswick to appear. Urswick then made the party swear an oath to Herne that they would form a band of hunters with Herne as their leader to dispel the charm. Urswick’s promise had been satisfied and the hunters became a faithful if not loving band of men loyal to Herne. For many nights the group would raid the forest taking deer until very few were left. King Richard came to learn of their pursuits, and decided to make a visit to he oak. He was angry and desired revenge upon the men. Once there Herne appeared to the king and learnt of his anger. Herne listened and said that if the king wished him to leave the forest, taking his power with him, the king would have to agree to a request. Doubtless Herne wanted revenge upon his enemies who had desired his death. The king agreed to his request, and the group of men were hanged. Herne was then never seen again. It is reputed though that Herne returned and reigned supreme, taking control of the forest of Windsor for eight years after the death of King Richard. Many versions of the Herne legend exist. Some say that Herne hanged himself after committing a terrible crime, whilst another tells of a forest demon that takes on his appearance. The demon is said to place stag horns on its head haunting the forest still trying to convince keepers that it is Herne and that they should sell their souls to him.
