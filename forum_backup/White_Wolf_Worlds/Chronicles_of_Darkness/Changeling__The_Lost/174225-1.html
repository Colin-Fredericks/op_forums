        Namesake provides some possibilities that could be adapted as “worlds of fiction as Arcadian Realms”, with the Namesakes being abductees who were chosen strictly because of their names, and whose durances resemble famous fairy tales. The oddest feature is the introduction of Writers, mortals connected to the newly-returned Namesakes who wield a storycrafting magic and serve as stabilizers for the newly-returned Namesakes.

                Spirited Away would need very few changes to work great as a Durance in Arcadia. It's a reminder that the Gentry don't necessarily need to steal anyone away: lay out food where mortals can stumble on it and they'll be all too happy to make pigs of themselves (Chihiro's parents to Beasts), while their daughter gives up her name and memories as she sells herself into service (Chihiro to Wizened) at the bathhouse for the Gentry, operated by a greedy and powerful proprietress (Yubaba). Perhaps the girl might have faded away entirely (or at least into a Darkling), and forgotten herself for still longer, had she not gotten assistance from an older Changeling (Haku as a Fairest, likely Dual-Kithed Draconic and Waterborn, and possible Loyalist for a time). Zenibaba could be a rival Gentry to Yubaba, engaged in a subtle war over that realm, with No Face as either a more powerful Darkling or a weaker Gentry.

                    While we're on the subject of Anime, Fullmetal Alchemist would make for an excellent Changeling chronicle. Depending on which of the two Anime adaptations you use, it could be fun either way as well. (If you went for the original adaptation, the entire thing is clearly set in the hedge, with the final ending revealing the real world )

                    I mean, consider how the story starts - Two brothers make a fell bargain to bring their mother back to life and trade their bodies for it. Can't imagine how appealing that deal would be to True Fae. Then followed by a life-long pledge to recover their bodies and discover the truth. The incarnations of the seven deadly sins - clearly Fae Actors and horrible dread beasts ruled by their vice, near invulnerable, but also determined. And then there's the alchemy in the world - "equivalent exchange", a contract with the world to trade one for another. Except when you cheat.

                    Heck, this writes itself.

                        RWBY's protagonists have some interesting seasonal motifs that fit well into kiths. The Snowskin Weiss feeds off the sorrow of her family's slaves; she remembers an Arcadia of chill hallways and hollow, massive knights. When Ruby, a Flowering, expends Glamour, ghostly rose petals dance around her. For Yang, it's flames- burning in her eyes and hair as she vents her wrath on Fae lackeys. Blake, a graceful Whisperwisp, harvests fear from those who would subjugate her people once more.

                            Having re-watched Wreck It Ralph the other day, I was struck by how Changeling-like the protagonists (Tron-like computer programs) are.

                            Ralph is clearly an Ogre of some kind, Felix is a Wizened repairman, the various racing characters in Sugar Rush could be Wizened racers, or perhaps some sort of sickeningly-cute Fairest ...

                            It's an interesting take on how Keepers need not be in Actor form - Realms and even Wisps can create Changelings as well. The fun-yet-brutal lives of the game characters, bound to serve the shadowy giants behind the screen or be unplugged forever, would be a great Durance for a PC sometime.
