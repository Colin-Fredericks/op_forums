    Does Clarity need reworking?

    05-22-2021, 09:46 AM
    There is something that feels wrong with the Clarity rules. Firstly, having a system that closely resembles Health and yet differs substantially, especially considering the two rolls required, is odd.
    But what bothers me most is that the severity of the first roll does not affect the actual quantity of Clarity damage.

    What I mean is that even if you're being dragged by your Keeper back into Arcadia and forced to torture your own Touchstone, you can roll dozens of dice for the Clarity attack, but with low enough Wyrd ratings you have pretty good chances to not get any damage at all.
    All the circumstantial modifiers only determine whether the damage, IF ANY, is mild or severe. Conditions affect the damage roll, but so does having a Touchstone attached.

    Perhaps I have a bias coming from my experience. In the chronicle I'm running, Clarity breaking points don't happen very often, so when they do, I'd like it to be a meaningful moment with an impact, even if they have Wyrd 1 or 2.

    I haven't come up with a houserule (still not sure if it's needed), but I'm thinking about having a single roll, using half Wyrd (rounding down) as a "weapon" modifier, and only having severe damage when mild damage gets upgraded to severe.

      I do agree that the current Clarity system is wonky, but the reason is probably to make the amount of damage much more manageable. There are a whole lot of modifiers to breaking points that can really add up under certain circumstances. Like, anything involving a changeling's Keeper can easily end up with +5 on top of the base dice pool. Not to mention that Clarity attacks from other sources can potentially end up with quite large dice pools, depending on the source. Keeping damage separate from that roll ensures that the damage dealt can be fine tuned by the devs, and not spiral out of control.
      But if that's the case, then the devs overcompensated. As you say, breaking points are rare (though other Clarity attacks might not be, as they're generally caused by NPCs) and it's way too easy to not get Clarity damage even on successful Clarity attack rolls.
      A changeling fresh out of character creation has a chance die on their Clarity damage rolls, which means damage is both incredibly unlikely and also limited to a single damage since you don't reroll a 10 on a chance die. In my experience it's incredibly common to have at least two Touchstones since that grants maximal protection. So the one thing that does actually increase the Clarity damage dice pool above chance die for the vast majority of Wyrd 1-2 changelings, is if they're subject to Clarity Conditions, which they gain from already suffering from enough Clarity damage to fill one of the last three boxes.
      (There is actually one exception: A Seeming Benefit on a Contract that causes a Clarity attack adds the successes on the attack as bonus dice on the Clarity damage roll, but that's the one thing I've found in the core book.)

      So yeah, it's completely underwhelming. If you do apply the successes of the Clarity attack directly, be advised that it's going to result in a dramatic increase of Clarity damage. As such you might not want to add automatic damage on top of that. If you want the Wyrd rating to still play a role in Clarity damage, I recommend adding it as bonus dice rather than as a "weapon" modifier.
      If you keep it as two separate rolls, then automatic damage is definitely something I'd recommend, but since the base dice pool for Clarity damage rolls is Wyrd, you might want to base the "weapon" modifier on something else.

                          I agree about Clarity; I’m not a big fan of how it currently works. In my current game, it seems as though several of the players are constantly going into comas.

                          Before anyone asks, my Storyteller is not over-applying Clarity checks. We usually do one, maybe two, per session. It’s simply that we seem to roll HORRIBLY when it comes to Clarity. Granted, the PCs have decent Wyrd ratings: one is Wyrd 5, and nearly everyone else is Wyrd 4. Average Clarity track is 5-6 boxes.

                          In one scene, right before a major battle, our characters were presented with evidence of our experiences being unreal (a four-dice Clarity breaking point). Even though we all came in with zero Clarity damage, half of the group ended up with enough damage to immediately drop into fucking comas. It derailed the plot for several sessions, and the comatose PCs missed out on an important battle.

                          This has been a somewhat reoccurring thing. Every few sessions, someone falls into a coma and we have to stop what we’re doing to help get them out, or wait for them to wake themselves up. It’s gotten to the point where we can’t stand Clarity... it’s just not fun anymore. AGAIN, my Storyteller is not especially harsh with Clarity rolls... we really just roll THAT badly.

                          I dislike the Comatose Condition because I dislike mechanics that forcefully remove a player from the group and the game. Consequently, in the case of mild Clarity damage, my Storyteller now inflicts the Madness Condition in place of Comatose. MY GOD is it an improvement! It keeps the afflicted player with the group and doesn’t derail active plot lines. Comatose is reserved for taking a full track of severe damage.

                              The odds of taking five points of clarity damage in a single roll at Wyrd 5 (through the one dot of reduction that a touchstone offers) is 0,45%.                                                       If you focus the story on retaking or rebuilding your personal connections and emotional investments then breaking points should come up more often and with higher modifiers (but even then, damage may be rare).
                                                      But if you're mostly dealing with supernatural matters, you won't engage with Clarity as much.
                                                      My own experience running Changeling is pretty similar to Tessie's: mostly supernatural mystery-driven, since my players don't seem to really care about their past lives, their Fetches etc.
