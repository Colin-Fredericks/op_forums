    After sharing three books on the Asian American experience, I thought I would switch back to manga for inspiration. I also want to turn back to an old classic, Inuyasha, one of the first manga series I ever read and introduced me to the concept of Yokai. I also feel the way Kagome uses the well to travel to the Sengoku period is very similar to the way changelings use the Hedge and even how time works in the Hedge and Arcadia.

    In modern-day Tokyo, Kagome Higurashi lives on the grounds of her family's Shinto shrine with her mother, grandfather, and younger brother. On her fifteenth birthday, while searching for her cat, Kagome is dragged into the enshrined Bone Eater's Well (骨喰いの井戸, Honekui no Ido) by a centipede demon that emerges from it. But rather than hit the bottom, Kagome finds herself in another universe which is parallel to her universe - but in the past, during Japan's Sengoku period. The demon was originally defeated fifty years prior by Kikyo, a priestess and exorcist who was the previous keeper of the Shikon Jewel (四魂の玉, Shikon no Tama, lit. "The Jewel of Four Souls"), a powerfully magical artifact created from the sacrifice of the priestess Midoriko, which grants its holder any wish their heart desires. Kagome is revealed to be the reincarnation of the now-dead Kikyo. The Shikon Jewel was burned along with Kikyo's body to cast it out of this world entirely, in order to keep it safe from the hands of those who would use its power for evil. Kagome comes across a sleeping boy pinned by a sacred arrow to a tree, learning he is Inuyasha, a half-human, half-Yokai whom Kikyo pinned to the tree as her final act when he attempted to steal the jewel. Kagome then gets attacked by a centipede demon and desperately frees Inuyasha to kill the centipede demon, but when he turns on her and tries to steal the jewel again, he is subdued with a magical beaded necklace to keep him in line with Kagome saying "sit" or "sit boy", which causes him to violently fall to the ground. The Shikon Jewel is extracted from Kagome's body and taken by a crow demon, which Kagome destroys with an arrow, but in doing so, inadvertently shatters the jewel into many shards that scatter across Japan and into the possession of various demons and humans. Kagome and Inuyasha travel throughout Japan, searching for the shards, finds friends and confronting enemies like Inuyasha’s pure blooded half-brother and Kagome’s previous incarnation, Kikyo.

    The series has been fully translated into English and I recommend borrowing/ordering the book form your local library. HOWEVER, you can purchase the first book here:

        Rumiko Takahashi's current work, Mao, I think is a good source of inspiration for a Changeling chronicle taking place in Asia (Though I can see people arguing Beast is a better premise). The series draws heavily from Inuyasha, with the heroine, Naoka Kiba, using an abandoned shopping district to travel to the Taisho era. I think this can be a source of inspiration for what the Hedge might look like in Japan.

        Eight years ago, Nanoka Kiba was saved from the wreckage of a fatal accident at Gogyō Town Shopping Center. Drawn to the abandoned passage of storefronts on her walk home from school in present day, she becomes inexplicably transported to Japan's Taisho era where she meets the mysterious Mao, a fellow traveler whose arrival at a ghost-inhabited village is simultaneous with hers. Together, they investigate the bizarre events that begin to occur around them, and Nanoka soon pieces apart her broken memories of the accident and how she and Mao may have something more sinister in common.

            I will try not to start a bruhaha, but I believe there are several works beyond The Ancient Magus’ Bride for inspiration on Western Fae making people of China, Japan, Korea, and the other nations of Asia into their Changelings. One is the light novel series “Failure Frame, I Became the Strongest and Annhilated Everything with Low Level Spells,” in which a Goddess teleports a group of Japanese high school students and their teacher to a Western Fantasy DnD world.

            Abruptly catapulted into a fantasy world, Mimori Touka and his classmates have been summoned by the world's resident goddess to serve as heroes. Luckily, most of the students display amazing skills upon arrival--except for Mimori, whose abilities bottom out at a measly E-rank. With no further use for him, the goddess banishes Mimori to a dungeon from which no one has ever returned alive. Yet, as it turns out, Mimori's skills aren't so much worthless as they are abnormal. Abnormally powerful, even. If Mimori can only claw his way back to the surface, nothing will stand in his way from getting revenge.

            The premise is very similar to light novel series like “The Rising of the Shield Hero” and “Arifureta: From Commonplace to World's Strongest”, The reason you write a concept like this is to show how the downtrodden losers in the class that the genre are meant for are unable to escape their social heirarchy even when brought to another world against their choice. Their bullies and tormentors get incredible powers while the loser enjoys a loser salad of loser spells to fend for himself. The odd one out as the rest of the class loses their humanity ala The Lord of the Flies. Children that are suddenly given fire to play with for the first time and they take this to its logical conclusion. The horror of this concept comes to life as most of the class pretty much goes along with this because why should they care? A horrifying lack of humanity or empathy from fellow classmates and teenagers really sells the spirit of abandonment that on children can create. WHAT IS DIFFERENT from the other two series is that the main character of this story, Touka Mimori, is damaged goods and a little sociopathic, which shows in his dealing with other characters after his banishment. He was broken as a child, but tried to be a good guy for the people who saved him and he loved. NOW HOWEVER, there is no longer any reason to wear the mask as he vows revenge.

            I find the Goddess Vysis’ casual cruelty and manipulative nature very similar to that of the Gentry/True Fae. I also find the way she gives the students their classes and powers similar to the way a True Fae gives a changeling their seeming and kith.

                There is another light novel series I recommend for inspiration on playing a citizen from an Asian nation being made into a Changeling of a Western True Fae (Though people might argue that this makes sense for a Prometheus Chronicle). It is not your typical isekai, taking place in a world based off the stories of Mother Goose and the Grimm Brothers.

                Torture Princess: Fremd Torturchen

                I AM THE TORTURE PRINCESS, ELISABETH LE FANU.I AM THE PROUD WOLF AND THE LOWLY SOW.A beautiful woman appears before Kaito Sena following his tragic death and rebirth into another world. Introducing herself as the Torture Princess, she orders him to become her loyal servant. Naturally, his response is..."Hard pass." And so Elisabeth presents him with two choices-become her butler or be tortured to death. After rethinking his hasty decision, Kaito is forced to wait on Elisabeth hand and foot, as well as aid her in her mission as the Torture Princess, which is the subjugation of the fourteen ranked demons and their contractors. But there's a catch. Once her mission is complete, Elisabeth is fated to die alone-forsaken by heaven, earth, and all of creation.

                Torture Princess is one of the better written light novels I've read, with excellent prose and writing quality. The small cast is an interesting one, as well; the main character is absolutely not a bland self-insert isekai protagonist, and there's a lot more going on with the titular "Torture Princess" than you'd expect at first glance. After Kaito’s untimely demise at the hands of his father, he’s summoned to a fairytale world based on the works of the Brother’s Grimm by Elizabeth to be her servant. I’m not entirely sure why she did it if I’m being honest, but his personally make for some very entertaining interactions. And I won’t lie, apart from her absurd dress, I dig Elizabeth’s design, and the art in general.

                Full warning, though; the violence and gore in this book will be a shock to anyone expecting something more like the typical isekai novel. The story is meant to be a fairy tale and clearly draws inspiration from Grimms Fairytales and Mother Goose. Depictions of torture, violence, and dismemberment are on par with the manga "Berserk," and other violent, bloody stories. The story is gothic horror/action, with several very evil characters doing evil things (before they're killed by the Princess), so if you're bothered by this kind of content, this may not be for you.
