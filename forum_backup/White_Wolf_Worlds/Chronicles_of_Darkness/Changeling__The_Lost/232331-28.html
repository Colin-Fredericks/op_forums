    Well, I have to wonder how many people really want to make a character who has a power just to not use it? That fairest gristlegrinder idea doesn't really make much sense unless you're playing them to be something like an ogre by nature who pretends to be pretty and sedate, but breaks the facade on frequent occasion. (In fact, going by the above logic, you might just have a "brawler" kith, and the ogre version is the Gristlegrinder, while the fairest version is the Draconic.)

    Kiths are your most significant, unique, and defining power. Very, very few people would waste them on something they would actively go out of their way to not use. You can throw away some skill points into brawl and streetwise or something to say you were a ruffian in your youth, then the durance turned you around, because you can always get more skill points.

    In fact, I still think that, if we're to separate out a "what you were made into" and "what you chose to become", that kith should be the choice, and seeming should be what was forced. Seeming, at least in 1e, comes from the environment you were in during the durance, while kith comes from what you did. Darklings were the changelings in darkness, spawned of chthonic Keepers. Ogres were the ones that had to be hard for brutality was the only way to survive. I mean, who gets out of their durance, and then chooses to have the power to be a tree? Rather, you were forced to be a tree, but you chose what being a tree meant as your kith.

        It really isn't always super exciting to take a power and then never use it! Which is why the new breakdown makes such a character more viable for more people. Though one of the interesting things about such characters is figuring out how to use it anyway.

        It's more interesting to take what's been described and try to make it work then to try and fit old ideas into it squarely.

                        You don't choose to be an Ogre or a Darkling. You choose to escape. Your method of escape dictates your Seeming in the new paradigm.

                        In many ways, this means that before escaping a changeling is just their Kith. One living in darkness is just a being that lives in darkness. It isn't until they choose to make the effort to escape that they refine themselves into a more specific expression of that. A fighter in the bloody pits is a violent brawler. It isn't until they escape, and the agency they get from it, shapes that into a more specific form via their Seeming.
