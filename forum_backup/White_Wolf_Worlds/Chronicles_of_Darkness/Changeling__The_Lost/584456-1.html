    Mine idea of Seemings based on duel Escape:
    • Beast: Blind rage, animalistic power overpowering the Keeper
    • Elemental: Becoming "one with the wind", you, literally, devolved youself to get lost in the fight, BECOMING WIND, so the Keeper could not hit you
    • Darkling: You made plan to challange to battle, only made other one fight for you and so you could escape.
    • Fairest: You rose the army of fellow changelings and to save them, you challange the Keeper - and won.
    • Ogre: You lost your heart in the fight. Literally.
    • Wizened: By spending next 20 years and training, you finally challanged Keeper when you become worn out weapon master.

        Originally posted by Illuminated Minutia View Post
        Alright, so let's take the escape via duel example. I could see that working for pretty much all of them depending on how you run with it.

        Beast: Focus on embracing your instincts to win.
        Elemental: Reverse of Beast, "no mind," Zen, etc.

        Darkling: Victory by sacrificing others, doesn't mean you had to have killed or left others behind on your way out (though you easily could have), possibly just burned bridges somehow
        Fairest: Heroic victory, probably wound up freeing more than just yourself

        Ogre: Burning spirit, berserk fury, overwhelming passion and emotion.
        Wizened: THE RIDDLE OF STEEL ...
        The fun thing about these is that some of them work for others just fine. Riddle of Steel, as you imply, works fine for Elemental and also for Fairest. Sacrifice of others works fine for Beast as much as Darkling. Berserk Fury works fine for Elemental or Beast.

        Plus, some alternatives:

        For Beast, breaking the rules of etiquette: you find a cold iron blade somewhere, or you gut punch them and run away while they're reeling.
        For Elemental, the ground betrayed the Keeper; you danced away and he kept tripping until you got enough openings and opportunities to wrest a win.
        For Darkling: you weren't supposed to be the duellist; you conned the other Changeling and took his place, and then won.
        For Fairest: the duel happened in the middle of an escape; your Keeper agreed to single combat for all of your companions, and you won.
        For Ogre: your Keeper kept scoring hits on you, but you just refused to feel them. He attacked more and more wildly, but you just wouldn't go down.
        For Wizened: your special skill isn't combat; it's trash talking. You kept talking and your Keeper couldn't focus on fighting anymore.

        Originally posted by Illuminated Minutia View Post
        tbh I'm still wrapping my head around the escape concepts of Wizened and how their abandonment of the Self parallels the Elementals. For now I'd probably go with something focusing on your failing flesh/body and salvation through raw skill. Let the weird replacement of skin and bone with steel become more of a side effect of Arcadia (which is basically what all the Seemings are when you get down to it).
        It is very similar.

        The key difference between the Elemental and the Wizened, IMO, is that the Elemental transcends through unpredictability whereas the Wizened transcends through understanding. An Elemental says, "You cannot expect everything, so expect nothing." A Wizened says, "You cannot expect everything, but you can expect everything that matters."

        I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".

                Originally posted by Taveena View Post
                Or by seducing one of their loyal servants into slipping you the keys?
                Wizened is basically the easiest. Whatever you did to escape, chess playing, swordplay, running really fast, whatever? That's your Wizened's craft. Did your character decide "I need to be really, really good at seducing people if I want to escape"? Bam, Wizened. Would your character have formerly sneered at players and seducers, did they think it wasn't appropriate, civilized behavior, but ended up sacrificing those concerns? You could make a Beast. Did you betray the servant? Did you fake genuine affection so they would lower their guard only to run away as soon as you had the keys in hands? Darkling. Did you inspire the servant? Did you make a stirring speech about how escaping was the only thing you could do, did you make puppy dog eyes, did you touch the servant's emotions such that they gave you the keys willingly? Fairest. Elemental would... let go of their ego, and subsume themselves into the concept of seduction, I guess? I'm having trouble seeing any other interpretation.

                Originally posted by Taveena View Post
                So basically the issue with your point is- well, the first is that Darklings aren't just 'tricksy darkness poisoners' anymore - they are DEFINED as "those who betrayed an ally to escape from Arcadia". If they challenged them alone, then there is no-one to martyr yourself for, nor to betray.
                "In her mind, or in reality [...] It meant taking advantage of the situation and making the hard choice, not the right choice. And in that decision, she let shadows in, she accepted it in and abandoned the light and righteous path." If your character realized that they couldn't beat their keeper in a fair fight, if they decided to rely on trickery or poison, and if that was a significant choice on their part, that they now see themselves as less of an honest person... then they are totally a Darkling. The "in her mind" part is really important. You don't need to have literally pushed another changeling under your keeper's sword for a distraction. As long as the character did something they considered a betrayal, something dishonest, it works.

                Originally posted by Taveena View Post
                Consider the duel in which it is simply the Changeling against the Keeper - a proper duel, for the Changeling's freedom. They are not flowing away as an Elemental, backstabbing as a Darkling, martyring themselves as a Fairest, betraying an ally as Darkling, denouncing humanity as a Beast, or remaking themselves as a Wizened.

                Do you bs your explanation as a Wizened (the... reliance on swordplay is at least a CRAFT, but they're not really remaking themselves...), not get a Seeming at all, or is the fact the character doesn't fit into the 6 seemings mean he's unplayable?
                Who's talking about Elementals flowing away? Sacrifice your ego, become swordplay.*

                All the Seemings represent a psychological transformation. The game assumes your character was changed in some way from this duel. They did something, they acted in a certain way and now they're different, or rather they see themselves as different and Arcadia, in its great generosity, acceded and changed the Changeling in a fitting way. If you just want your character to have fought a duel and not have any epiphany, no realization about what was holding them back, nothing transformative... then yes, you're in trouble.

                *Or take in the strength of a storm and use it to win. Whatever.

                            Personally I think it is a mistake to choose a seeming based soley on how they escaped. The elemental embraced 'they can't hurt you if you don't care'. The darkling took on a siege mentality, "It's a dog eat dog world, trust no one because they will betray you". The wizened threw himself into his work, the ultimate work aholic. The fairest responded by putting themselves out, stepping up and focusing more on others as a way to protect themselves. I would say that the changes of the seeming were wrought by the wyrd in response to the traits embraced by the changeling. In a way a seeming is the first step to becoming one of the true fae.
