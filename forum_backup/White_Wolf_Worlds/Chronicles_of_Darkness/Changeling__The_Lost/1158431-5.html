    A while when the old court creation preview was posted we all started posting courts. I thought I'd adapt the one I wrote, the Flower Courts, to the new Give and Take mechanics. They're a Freehold deeply focused on being a part of mundane society.


    The Flower Courts.

    The Freehold claim that their fonders were born in the iron age, and they lived well and prospered for every generation the village would come togeather and choose the greatest of their number to be a king. They would obey their monarch in all things, and so each became as great as the village's best. But one king decreed upon his deathbed that they would choose his eldest son; for the king loved his son as all fathers do and could not see that his son was not the greatest in the village.

    Under the king's son's rule the village fell into poverty. The walls crumbled, the warriors became lazy and arrogant. The Gentry saw this weakness and took the entire population as slaves.

    By the time the survivors escaped the world they remembered no longer existed. "Do not fear." they told one another,. "We will find a king who understands this new world".

    Following power itself the arrived at Washington where they saw congressmen and senators going about their business. "Surely one of these powerful men will be our king" they said. But the senators and congressmen spoke about elections, and checks, and balances, and the villagers could not understand them.

    Next they travelled to Boston, where they saw factories and industry on a scale they couldn't imagine. "Surely a man who can create such marvels could be a king" they said. But the industrialists spoke of shareholders, and investments, and capitol, and the villages could not understand them.

    Then they arrived at Pennsylvania, where they saw the university. "These men have studied all things, surely one of them must know how to be a king" they said. But the scientists spoke of methodology, and peer review, and the villagers could not understand them.

    Distraught the villagers wondered the streets, they feared that without a king to protect them their old masters would return. By change they came across a lover presenting flowers and this was a language they could understand, and so they asked the flowers to choose them a king.

    Give and take: Each Flower Court forces the Gentry or their servants to sabotage a Courtier's mundane life before they may attempt to attack them, enchant them, or drag them back to arcadia and every flower as two weaknesses: The gentry can either slip through it's open petals by convincing a mortal to break a pledge with the Changeling, which mortals qualify depends on the Court. Having no pledges goes against the fundamental nature of the Flower Courts and provides no protection, the Huntsman are free to target any Changeling who does not have at least Wyrd / 2 appropriate pledges at will. The exception is the Court of Honeysuckle who only ever require one pledge, but require greater intimacy at higher Wyrd. If the open petals do not attract, a Huntsman can instead wilt the vine by attacking the Changeling's place in society.

    As the Flower Courts are all and always subordinate to the Lilly Crown each Changeling always benefits from their own Court's protection.

    The Court of Balsam, The Unyielding Flora, The Red Blossom, The Court of Stubbornness

    I was subjected to tortures you mere mortals couldn’t imagine so I’d like some fucking sympathy please.

    The Court of Balsam do not return to mortal society so much as they expect mortal society to come to them on their terms. Almost every Changeling returns from Arcadia with phobias, supersitions and sometimes even frailties. The Court of Stubbornness doesn't even try to overcome these, they're offended by the notion that they should. Instead they mortal society to accommodate them. While the Freehold considers the Red Blossom to be stubborn gits who refuse to understand every relationship requires some give and take, they are invaluable to the Freehold because they remind the other changelings that yes, they do have issues, and yes they need to ask for accommodation before they snap.

    Give and Take: The Gentry and their servants wilt the vine by convincing a member of the Red Blossom to compromise on some accommodation related to their traumas from Arcadia.

    The Court of Hollyhock, The Proud Bloom, The Gold Blossom, The Court of Ambition

    I’m sure you’ve read all sorts of faerie tales about our trickery, well that’s the old way. Modern faeries understand the benefits of customer satisfaction. So here’s my offer, seven years younger for seven million in cash, and I’ll make the same offer in seven years time.

    The Court of Hollyhock want a life among mortals, but not just any life. Wealth, power or fame, their new life is going to be extraordinary if it takes every Contract they know to make it happen. This Court provides the Freehold with much of it's wealth and influence which makes up for the fact that the Gold Blossom is the least involved in day to day Freehold Life.

    Give and Take: The Gentry and their servants wilt the vine by inflicting a serious career setback to a member of the Gold Blossom; such as a demotion.

    The Court of Honeysuckle, The Choking Vine, The Pink Blossom, The Court of Dependency

    Please, we can make it work. I can change.

    The True Fae break their slaves. Their souls are full of gaps, and the Court of Honeysuckle return to the mortal world looking for someone who can fill those gaps. Some seek out their old family or their old loves, others tumble through relationship after relationship always looking for "the one". The Pink Blossom is infamous for the fragility of their members, especially when they're supressing their true personality for their lover's benefit, however the Choking Vine is invaluable to the Freehold because they're the only Court who, however clumsily, relate to average mortals on their own level.

    Give and Take: The Gentry and their servants wilt the vine by tempting the Pink Blossom into betraying their relationship, such as seducing the changeling into cheating on their partner.

    The Court of Violet, The Humble Herb, The Blue Blossom, The Court of Satisfaction

    Hi Ho, Hi Ho, It’s Off To Work We Go!

    If the Flower Courts are about fitting in with mortal society then the Court of Violet have figured it out. They find some quiet corner, usually one where their faerie talents are useful, and then they keep their heads down and do their job. Typically invisible, and happy that way, the Humble Herb take satisfaction in their work and being valuable to the mortals around them. In the Freehold the Blue Blossom are the ones who keep everything running. Few ever aim for status or influence, and those who have status or influence know better than to take the Court of Satisfaction for granted.

    Give and Take: The Gentry and their servants wilt the vine by successfully tempting the Blue Blossom with wealth or status.

    The Lily Crown The Royal Flower, The White Blossom, The Crown

    A pure white lily symbolises many things. It symbolises virginity and purity, a Changeling’s lost state of grace before the Gentry plucked them from earth. To give someone a white lily says that it is heavenly to be with you, as it is heavenly for Changelings to be back home with humanity. Most importantly of all a white Lily symbolises majesty.


    Once a year the courts oneiromancers create a dreamscape. In this dreamscape there is a road beset by trials. The road leads to a hall with an empty throne with a crown of white lillies upon it. The oneiromancers take the dream and divide it up between as many vessels as they can, but by tradition every changeling must have at least one vessel.

    On the appointed day each Changeling secrets their vessels into people's beds. Some trust chance and Wyrd, others spend months before the day choosing their champions. That night each mortal with a vessel dreams themselves onto the path, they face the trials, and the first to reach the throne and claim the crown becomes king or queen of the freehold. Everyone else awakes with no memory and a good night's rest.

    The trials themselves can be as easy or as challenging as the freehold wishes. Only two rules remain in force, the first is that no king or queen may ever know of or influence the selection process, they're told the flowers, that is to say the Wyrd, chooses. The second is that one trial must rule out any mortal with a connection to the Gentry or to a changing freehold.

    In some desperate years those have been the only trials, but most have many more. A trial so that the new monarch cannot be cruel. A trial to exclude fae-tocuhed (for who wants a king to send his knights to Arcadia?) The Freehold often comes up with lengthy and elaborate criteria, but they must be careful. If no mortal makes it past all the trials the Bulwark shatters for the next year. Even setting too many trials will cause mortals to wake up before completing the quest or failing at a task.

    And thus every year one ignorant mortal suddenly discovers that they're king or queen of the faeries.

    None of the other Flower Courts have a Court Crown, their power is concentrated in the Lily Crown to grant a mortal everything they need to rule the faeries. The Crown provides:
    • The Sight, Lily Kings can see through the Mask. (Former Kings retain this ability)
    • Comfort with the Hedge: Lily Queens have a five die pool to open Hedge Gates and may open or create new ones by spending a point of Willpower. Once inside The Hedge they can navigate, are immune to any mental effect that does not affect Changelings and they are protected from both the thorns and transforming into a hobgoblin.
    • The ability to make up to 5 Pledges, as well as Pledges of fealty and vassalage.
    • Protection from magic: Any dice pool to activate a contract or which has been increased through magic takes a 5 Success penalty when targeting a Lily King, unless his majesty consciously allows it.
    • Status in the courts, just as a Mantle provides social bonuses so does the Lily Crown. Lily Queens get a +3 bonus to all Flower Courts, which can stack with Court Goodwill to a maximum of +5. (Former Queens retain a +2 bonus and any Goodwill their previous subjects feel).
    • Most potently of all, every month when the first sliver of the new moon shines the Lily King must declare one Court exemplary. They may choose based on any criteria they wish, and that Court's protection is enhanced. For the reminder of the month no Courter need fear the Gentry attacking them through the wilting vine.


    Give and Take: The Lilly Crown rests on a mortal head, but even they benefit from protection against the Gentry. No True Fae or servant thereof can attack the wearer of the Lilly Crown unless they are in the presence of at least four Changelings from the Flower Courts who genuinely wish them harm.

                                                I'm not meaning to be surly here, I'm really not. I know I probably sound that way. But after the extended re-shuffles,t he delays, and now what smacks, to me, of obfuscation of the changes made while asking for a financial commitment and support sight-unseen... but twelve or so Kiths? 1e had what, five or six on average per Seeming. What was the word-count needed for if not one of the core aspects of the game?

                                                Now let me preface this next bit with 'I'm not saying this is the case, but rather circumstances make it look troubling in a vacuum: when I hear "a huge part of the character mechanics/customization has not only been cut down from what was shown previously, but (apparently) from 1e 's standards, and then am told "but another book will make up the slack".. well, the only comparison that comes to mind is the old argument against DLC in the video game industry. It could be taken to look really bad, frankly.

                                                Now, just for reference, I'm an old warhorse here. I've played Lost 1e since its release back in '07 - and Dreaming was my game before that. Been with the WoD/CofD for twenty years and change. Every new sourcebook that released for Lost 1E was a bit like a birthday, Samhain, and Christmas at once for me - and when the word came down about Dancers in the Dusk and Swords at Dawn? Hoo boy!

                                                Then we get the Underhill preview and I fell for it, hard and fast, looked forward to a full release, did some homebrew, played in a couple beta venues. Then the game gets pulled back in and reworked under, charitably, messy-feeling circumstances.

                                                And then I hear about the Kickstarter, finally getting a date, waiting eagerly to finally put my mind at ease.. and.. get this whole affair. Which, to be blunt, has soured me entirely. I've not seen or heard a single thing to allay my concerns, and I keep seeing and hearing things that only increase them.

                                                I mean,ultimately, know OP is a business. I know my patronage is, in the grand scheme, completely irrelevant and won't be felt whether present or absent.

                                                But right now, as it stands, 'absent' is where we're at, and given how much I love the game? That hurts.

                                                But that's just my two cents. Best of luck with it folks.

                                                        Second edition kiths are a lot weightier in terms of wordcount than first edition kiths. In 1e, kiths were 50-60 words, essentially each just a name and one power, but there were hints they could mean a lot more. So in 2e, they're around 500-600 words, with a lot more setting info packed into them, plus powers. We're not doing anything tricksy with the wordcount -- we specifically presented fewer kiths in much better depth. We also provide what you need to make your own.
