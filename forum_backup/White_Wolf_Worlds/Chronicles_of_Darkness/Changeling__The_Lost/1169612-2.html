                I'll get to answering peoples' comments when I get a moment - just managed to get this one ready before more family have rolled up and need socialising with



                ​Ignore the names for now, then, and focus on what else lies within. A fragment of something hard, something a little tarry; a lump of asphalt and grit from the road. It appears to be spattered with dried blood.

                Fellowship Of Tar And Grit
                Post-industrial roads have become an extremely dangerous place in the spirit world. The thundering, clattering, squelching spirits of wheel and transport are aggressive and fast. Even the spirits of the asphalt underfoot are resentful of those who would exploit their bodies' bones and flesh without paying due respect. Most Uratha figure it's safer to keep off the roads when one can and cross them as quickly as possible; after all, quite a few of the things that crawl or race along them are bound not to leave the roads' limits. That said, a shaman who gains the favour of the bubbling tar-matrons of the road courts, or who bargains for secrets of the heaving chaos of traffic from lesser entities of fume and gear, can learn a few useful tricks.

                Roads like blood, because they are rivers of carnage. Throw a track of pitch and grit down and run large, heavy lumps of metal along it at high speeds, and things get killed. Animal roadkill gets ground into the road surface, a sacrifice pulverised again and again on the altar of tar. Humans are caught by the cages of metal, or slam them into each other, and pour their blood onto the ground. Even the constant, tiny, petty deaths of insects slapped against glass and metal hide over and over and over again, each just a minute drop of ichor, are taken as offerings. Their little endings add over time, each a grain of sand that fills an immense cup of arthropod slaughter. The roads drink it all up regardless.

                A shaman who convinces a road court to make a covenant likely finds the spirits' price takes this sort of form. Only the most bloodthirsty courts, crazed by an eternal churn of traffic and human passage and pain and smoke, demand actual human sacrifice. Most need an offering of at least a large animal slain on the asphalt, and then occasional obligations over time like ensuring bonemeal is added to the freshly prepared grit of newly repaired road.

                Have your bargain made? Come to an understanding with the cracked ribs and tarry cartilage of the spirit-road? Step onto the street you've made a bargain with, then, and kneel down close to the asphalt. Feel grit and tar under your hand - let the connection form. Cut the palm, press it down, and let the blood flow. The spirit will drink - a point of Essence, guzzled down as the crimson fluid spills on the ground. Now you and your pack can walk the road with the spirit's aid for a little while. In the Shadow, the predators of steel and smoke will ignore you as they go about their daily struggles; it won't protect you if you deliberately get in their way and provoke them, or if they're specifically out to get you for some reason, but you can avoid unneccessary dangers. Most spirit-roads can't help but be faster to travel than the rest of the spirit-world just by their nature, offering Uratha anything up to +5 to Speed while astride them and heading to a journey's destination (just crossing the road doesn't bestow anything), so being able to navigate with less concern for the metal beasts of the highway can be convenient. In the Flesh, the first accident that would befall you is shunted onto some hapless nearby human instead. Some Iron Masters go on pilgrimages, spirit-quests on the roads, just walking on and on, spilling blood and feeding spirits as they go, living with the road and from it. These pilgrims breath engine fumes and eat roadkill at the edge of the asphalt - or the occasional human furious with road-rage or aroused by the notion of indulging brutal passions on what they think is just a luckless hitchhiker.

                The road-blessing is a good trick for prowling your territory more safely, but there's another that's a bit more practical for getting from A to B quickly. Sometimes an Uratha requires transport sharpish, and taxi drivers are either absent or have an alarming tendency not to take well to a werewolf's needs or behaviour. But mundane, real taxis are just a part of the greater, symbolic whole, the notion of the network of taxis and drivers and transport that threads through the fabric of the city. In the phone book, find the taxi service that doesn't correspond to any real agency or company operating in the city - you can tell which one it is because part of the name dips briefly into First Tongue, or because when you look at the ad from the corner of your eye, it looks back with inky little orbs of its own, or because it sometimes moves from one page to another. Don't call it - the phone number doesn't go anywhere good, trust me - but rip it out the book, that little scrap of paper. When you need transport? Reach into your pocket, find that little scrap of paper, fold it three times and then cut your thumb; let the blood and a point of Essence soak into it. And voila, give yourself a minute at most before a curious cab rolls up; blacked out driver windows, a number plate that isn't on any registry anywhere, and a no-questions-asked policy. They'll take you - and whatever baggage you've got - anywhere in the city, no money required. The price is a favour down the line; the request will come through for you to move something from one spot in the city to another. It might be to pick up a green rucksack from the park and drop it off outside the courts, or to just ride round and round the metro five times (the something, in this case, being yourself). It all has meaning to the spirits, and it's always best to pay your debts.

                - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                    Having put away a good half a bottle of very fine quality vodka during the family gathering today, I'm clearly in prime condition for some more writing. Let's delve into something with a bit of a kick to it....





                    ​Put the grit down alongside the other curios, feel its lingering roughness on your fingertips, then look to the next gift from the bag's contents. This one is larger, more ornate; two wolves curled round each other. Their colours are at stark odds. One speaks forth wisdom, an ivory sliver marked with questions and truths. The other... the other intakes, inhales, consumes. The ebon sliver at its mouth marks that it is a devourer, an eater. Where the first twin gives, the second takes. Where the first explores, the second guards. Where the first discovers, the second... culls.

                    Eat Your Name
                    Give up your name, and hide in its shadow. Rip it up, tear the pieces apart, swallow them down. This is not a good feeling, but it'll protect you for now.

                    Most Uratha desperate enough to eat their name are looking for a temporary affair, a time-limited deal, a purgatory rather than permanent damnation. That's what we'll look at here. There are other ways - permanent ways - but you're probably not far-gone enough to consider that. Yet.

                    So let's look at how you profane your identity, shall we? You're giving up your name because you want protection. It's either fear or conviction driving you to such a drastic step, I'm guessing. You want the nasty slivers of hurt that swim through the world and catch their barbs in the narrative fabric of the who-you-are to just slide off instead. You want to give nothing for their lamprey-maws to snare any purchase on.

                    It's not enough to just summon a spirit of darkness or secrets or entropy or annihilation. It's not enough to just find one in its lair in the Shadow. You have to find the right place and the right time, to seal your pact with such a spirit in a place in the Shadow where the light never reaches and where Essence of the right resonance pools thick - a 5-dot locus of such, or one of the Deep Shadow resting places where Death Wolf sometimes pauses in her travels, or one of the strange, Escher-painting tangles of architecture born from the dreams of a stifled architect who could never see their goals made in the stubborn, uncompliant Flesh. You go there and you perform the oblations necessary for that place. There'll be a sacrifice of some sort. There always is.

                    How you shed the name depends on the place. You carve or ink it into your flesh, shuck it off in a bloody excision with knife or talon, and feed it to the eager flock of spirits that wait for your benediction with eager hunger. You take all the photos that you own of yourself, and wash them with your blood until your depicted face smears and dissolves. You burn things that matter to you, that are part of who you are. You reject what is central to yourself, the things that are who you are. You speak your name over and over again until it simply loses meaning, until it is a random agglomeration of syllables with no deeper sense to it.

                    Pay the congregation of spirits what they desire, which will likely be a deep cost indeed. Pay worship to Death Wolf. Lose yourself in the insane architecture. Become insensate, become less than who you are. This is meditation of a nihilistic fashion, an attempt to scour yourself clean of everything that connects you to the world.

                    During this process, the place that you are in and the price you are paying will have very real effects. A scattering of your connections are lost forever, and there's no way to tell who or what beforehand. It won't be one of the People, or one of the Shadowfolk. But the ties will break nonetheless; an ex-lover forgets you forever; an old friend loses touch with finality; a mentor stops fondly remembering you, stops remembering you at all. In the worst cases, the ties that snap are deep and close. A mother forgets her son. A true friend wonders why they ever bothered. An oath born of a saved life is cast aside callously.

                    Now, now you are ready for the hunt.

                    You call a sacred hunt upon your own name, and you hunt it through the Shadow. It will be a dangerous trip, and when you find it, your name will not necessarily be what you are expecting. It might be whispered in the cascade of a spirit-waterfall, or tossed from beak to beak by a murder of crows. It might glint in the churned earth like a gem, or slink through the grim darkness as an oozing worm. Catch it, take it in your jaws, swallow it down.

                    Your name is gone.

                    You are made empty. Firstly, Harmony breaks immediately out of balance; it shifts 1 further to Spirit or Flesh, whichever it is closer to. An Uratha at Harmony 5 breaks one way or the other at random. This isn't a roll; it's a break. Secondly, whatever level of Death Rage trigger you suffer, it's now one stage worse for the duration of your namelessness; lacking such an identity to tie the threads of spirit and self together, the spasming foam of Essence and primal inheritance from Urfarah's mantle is loosened and provoked.

                    Oh, and of course, the name is gone. You can't remember it anymore. If it is spoken to you, you immediately forget it. Any attempt to give a new name slides off too. The best that can be managed are broad titles - 'you', 'Rahu', 'pack-brother' and the like. You don't forget your ties, but they are harder to draw upon; you suffer a -3 penalty to all rolls that would draw on Allies, Contacts or Status, and you cannot gain new dots in any of these merits. No spirit will honour a debt or favour owed to your earlier name, or consider you in a favourable light due to your actions before consuming your identity; you can build up new relationships in this time, though, even if the spirits will likely see your lack-of-name as a weakness to be exploited. Every day, you must spend a point of Willpower or gain the Madness Condition.

                    So what do you get for all this? You prowl the deep Shadow, you tear off who you are and suffer for it, and for what? For namelessness. For reducing yourself to no more than a tumbled stone in the stream of causality, barely registering in the constant flow of existence. You have flayed yourself of who you are, and your caulked blood is a shield.

                    Your name is not a valid method or aid for any means of targeting you for as long as it is gone. It does not count as your name in any mundane, symbolic, or metaphysical way. All supernatural attempts to affect or influence you suffer a -5 penalty to their dice pool and dramatically fail on a failure, regardless of if they require your name, unless they have the forceful physicality of a direct attack that deals damage such as a werewolf's claws or a spirit's Blast numen. Mind control slides off, scrabbling for purchase and slipping away. The winds of fate and prophecy cannot see you; stored records or archived information about you has a tendency to suffer and degrade; new records formed about you during this time lack all identifying information. Attempts to recall you based on only minor recollections or encounters suffer a -5 penalty as well. You are a gap in the narrative of the world; the story is not expecting your emergence.

                    How long is the name gone? Well, it varies, depending on the exact method you used. It might require you perform a Sacred Hunt and eat the spirit who ate your name. It might require a pilgrimage to places sacred to Death Wolf, to beseech her for its return. Perhaps you must convince a Tribal elder who holds your name etched into the bones of a hero that you are you and not one of the shape-stealing horrors who so eagerly seek to assume the void you have left. Perhaps your name is not where you left it, and you must hunt through the Shadow to find where it has ended up, and hope any new owner is willing to reliquish it. When - if - you finally secure your name again, Harmony immediately breaks out of balance again. Go without your name for a full year, and you gain the persistent Madness condition regardless of your best efforts.

                    But even amongst those who know of how to eat their own name, and who judge the dangers a worthy price for a potent advantage and the erosion of the self, there's a reason they are hesitant. Everyone who has eaten their name then vomited it back up knows that there is something else that knows it too. Something is out there, the real dealer in the exchange, something you've offered a vital part of yourself to and that has only returned the name to you bearing the mark of its talons. The Eater of Names is watching you now, and you know it has purpose and intent in its vigil. One day it will come for you, and it will have a duty for you, and your very name will demand you fulfil it. The feeling never goes away, unless you shear off your name forever.

                    There's a few strange Bone Shadow shamans who have no names at all, who have made it so they never did, and who can tell you how to take that final step - the greater, true excision of identity, debriding the existence of their person a from the world. That's a topic for another time, I think.

                    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                            Be careful as you place the twin wolves to one side; it doesn't do to upset that pair with careless disrespect. Onto the next trinket, already dangling part-way out of the bag, and tangled with another that lies deeper; this one here is a set of carefully beaten little silver plates, tiny, each the shape of a phase of the moon. Handle gently - it is silver, after all. Not enough here to ignite the Essence in your blood to real intensity, but enough to give a Pure a sting. On each little moon, tiny glyphs glint - names of revered lunes, the titles by which they are to be called and summoned.

                            Premium Grade Triple-Distilled Lunar Spirit
                            Here is a goblet. It is full to the brim with a roiling concoction, all twisting streams of liquid crystal, quicksilver, and carmine fluid. You're going to take this goblet and drink that weird admixture down. It's going to be your new best friend. It is a lunar elemental.

                            ​The image that most Uratha get in their heads at the phrase 'lunar elemental' is of a lune. Lunar elementals are quite probably lunes, or made from lunes, but they're also not​ lunes; they're quite probably spirits, or very close to such, but they are definitely not​ spirits at the same time. Outside of a werewolf's body, any given lunar elemental is just a squirming knot of fluid mass; it is physical and manifest in both Flesh and Shadow. Without the fire of an Uratha's Essence channels to give it animation and purpose, it doesn't do​ anything apart from stir with some semblance of sluggish life from time to time. Spirits shy away from attempting to consume such an aberrant being. If left dormant for long enough, an elemental will slowly shrivel up and evaporate, but that takes a very long time.

                            ​Tales about as to how lunar elementals come about. They bleed from the wounds of the Warden Moon's flesh as she shatters leviathans of the void in battle, and lunes reverently collect some of the gore of their goddess. They are the tears of a Luna who is not real, a weeping Warden of an alternate reality where the world below is a broken apocalypse, and the weight of a divinity's grief is enough to seep through the seams of this adjacent existence from time to time. There is a factory, somewhere, where they bind lunes and grind them down for all manner of ephemeral produce, from dark powders that burn with brilliant flames of moonlight when ignited to draughts of lune-lymph that ward against curses and, of course the elemental stuff derived from the core of the spirit's self-identity. There's a brewery where lunes imbue the gurgling, dripping tubes and vats with so much lunar Essence that it bubbles out and slithers around, self-animating. A chunk of the moon was shattered off by an impact in aeons past and fell to earth, a piece of the Warden's flesh, now buried deep underground where it struck the earth; veins of god-gore sometimes dribble up into the Shadow's light. When an Uratha makes a greater lune feel true reverence or deep, all-encompassing love, the spirit vomits up an ephemeral child of elemental moon-meat. The ancestors of great Uratha are carried up to the moon to do battle with the terrors of the greater dark and, when they die a second time, their void-riddled spirit-carcasses slough off these elemental cankers from the parts of their defiance and rage that refuse to give up the battle even then. There's any number of other stories too. Lunar elementals are so rare that there's little common ground to connect the various times they have been discovered, been given as gifts by lunes, or appeared in other ways. What matters is what they do.

                            An Uratha who drinks an elemental will activate it; contact with the werewolf's Essence brings it to life immediately. From then on, the elemental dwells within the werewolf's flesh, a familiar trapped in a prison of its masters flesh, bone and blood. If the werewolf dies, so does the elemental. It might be possible to extract an elemental through clever Essence alchemy or nightmarish surgical procedures, but the being cannot extract itself voluntarily.

                            ​As well as the occasional uncomfortable tug, shift or pulse within the body, the Uratha can feel the elemental's presence as a vague presence at the edge of awareness - a tingle or fizz in the neurones of the brain and the fabric of the soul. The lunar elemental weaves itself into the connection between werewolf and Warden Moon - the auspice itself. If imbibed by a werewolf without a lunar Auspice, an elemental will rapidly go mad and toxic.

                            ​A werewolf with a lunar Auspice and a lunar elemental can, instead of using their normal auspice ability, use one of the following powers once per chapter instead:
                            • The werewolf's natural weapons ooze with a slick, metallic venom for 2 rounds, inflicting the grave Poison tilt on any enemies injured.
                            • The werewolf's regenerative abilities erupt into a hyperactive state, increasing the levels of damage healed each round by 5 for 2 rounds.
                            • The werewolf's flesh liquefies and becomes formless, creating limbs, pseudopods, eyes or other body parts as needed. In this form, the werewolf has +2 Strength and Stamina, can slide through gaps of any size, climb walls and cling to ceilings, can manifest Gauru claws and jaws, has any number of limbs required and is unaffected by Tilts; the werewolf also gains a +3 bonus to grapple checks and triggers Lunacy as if in Gauru form. This form can be maintained for a number of rounds equal to 3 + the werewolf's Stamina + Primal Urge.
                            ​Additionally, a lunar elemental will immediately alert an Uratha if she intakes any sort of poisonous or toxic substance, whether physical or supernatural in nature.

                            ​A lunar elemental is relatively low-maintenance - it requires a single point of Essence per week from the werewolf's pool to sustain itself. However, it also adds a very distinct element to the werewolf's scent. Attempts to track or locate the Uratha via scent or spiritual senses benefit from a +3 bonus, and creatures that are particularly attuned to the mystic qualities of blood can immediately detect the Uratha's unusual nature in close proximity.

                            ​Werewolves that lack a lunar auspice often end up with poisoned flesh (inflicting the Poison tilt on those that bite them or are splashed with their blood), horribly warped flesh (a permanent increase to regenerative capabilities, but at the cost of a permanently, obviously monstrous appearance), and a reaction to silver that's positively incendiary, enough to set nearby creatures and objects aflame from the heat that spills from such injuries. Alarmingly, there's a few tales where a hapless Pure arrogant enough to think it could control the chaotic energy of a lunar elemental's bond ended up both mutated and also capable of vomiting up new, corrupted elementals into the meat of other werewolves that it managed to bite, copying its affliction to its victims in a blood-born plague of moon-tainted horror.

                            - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                                Past the chain of tiny silver moons is something else - a spirit-braid. Pull the thread out of the bag, and with it come the little charms tied to its length - little symbolic representations of spirit choirs, each carefully sculpted so that the shaman can feel the lessons of its surface just with fingertips. Each notch, each bump, each carved mark is a mnemonic reminder of a ban, or a bane - of a law, or a crime - of something relating to that choir and its members, of something that can protect against them, or be used as a lever to influence them with.

                                ​Not every shaman can keep every scrap of detail in their head. It can be handy to have a little reminder, when dealing with spirits.

                                ​The Lion's Paw
                                The shaman's task in shepherding the Shadow isn't just about slaughtering hostile spirits and littering the Hisil​ with their ephemeral viscera. Sometimes, a touch more care is required; assuming that one can make friends​ with all the dwellers of the reflected world is a fool's errand, but alliances can be forged, catastrophes averted and harmony spread through the right application of protection or healing at the right time. In some places, spiritual blights slither through the Shadow, prompted by idigam​ or Bale Hounds or simply born from grotesque but natural phenomena; the cycles of the Hisil​ can be set off course by the undesired defeat of a keystone spirit and set matters spiralling into chaos; a wounded ally might be a great asset if not for the horrific injuries it has suffered. The shaman, then, isn't just the wolf who culls the flock and kills the weak so that the whole may be stronger; they are the shepherd who looks ahead beyond simple, immediate and primal hungers for the hunt and the prey, planning with wisdom the careful balancing of the twin worlds they strive to maintain.

                                ​Even for those who do not attempt to sculpt the Shadow's currents through the application of such mercies, concern for more immediate spirit allies and, indeed, the pack totem can mean that rites which allow them to heal and aid spiritual beings are of significant value.

                                Heal Spirit (Pack Rite ••)
                                This rite draws ambient power directly from the Hisil around the ritemaster, weaving threads into the substance of a wounded spirit to reinforce its symbolic strength directly.

                                Symbols: Blood, healing, water, fire.

                                Action: Extended (10 successes; each roll represents five minutes)

                                Success: This ritual targets a single spirit, which must either be willing or be bound by another rite. At the rite's culmination, the ritemaster may heal any number of points of lethal damage to the spirit's corpus, spending 2 Essence per point to do so. In balance, a participating pack member suffers an additional -1 wound penalty for the remainder of the scene.

                                Cleanse Spirit (Pack Rite ••••)
                                This rite draws upon old, powerful echoes of primeval balance underpinning the Shadow's nature.

                                Symbols: Blood, purification, water, fire.

                                Action: Extended (15 successes; each roll represents five minutes); potentially penalised by the Rank of a spiritual source of the Conditions or effects

                                Success: This ritual targets a single spirit, which must either be willing or be bound by another rite. At the rite's culmination the spirit is purified of a negative Condition, and any supernatural effects on it that alter its attributes, nature, or Essence are purged. In balance, a pack member suffers a moderate Sick Tilt for the remainder of the scene. This rite can only remove the taint of a Maeljin from a spirit that is Rank 1 or 2; more potent spirits require greater and likely more unique methods of purification, if they can be purged of the Wounds' taint at all. Removing the taint of a Maeljin also inflicts the Madness condition on the Sick pack member for the scene's duration.

                                Gird Spirit (Pack Rite •)
                                This rite lends some of the symbolic strength of a pack's close ties to a spirit, girding it with Essence.

                                Symbols: Shielding, the pack, loyalty, metal or earth.

                                ​Cost: 1 Essence per spirit.

                                Action: Extended (5 successes; each roll represents one minute)

                                Success: This ritual targets one or more spirits, which must either be willing or be bound by another rite. At the rite's culmination, the ritemaster expends a point of Essence per spirit thus affected, reinforcing each spirit with this energy. Affected spirits gain a single point of armour, add a single point of damage to their attacks, and gain +2 to their resistance when opposing supernatural effects. A spirit may also immediately expend the girding to grant a Clash of Wills against a supernatural effect. The spirits do not gain any of these benefits against the ritemaster or members of the ritemaster's pack, however, and the spirit's appearance is altered to indicate allegiance to the pack in some way, such as depicting the pack's symbol, gaining wolf-like masks of spirit-matter, or changing colours and hues to match those of the pack. Additionally, a girded spirit cannot willingly pay allegiance or swear vassalhood to another spirit unless it is the pack's totem, and cannot be forced​ to pay allegiance or become a vassal for the rite's duration. Acting in a way that serves loyalty to the pack or the totem can restore 1 point of Willpower to the spirit once per story. Unless expended to force a Clash of Wills, each spirit will receive the benefits of girding for a month.

                                ​There are probably several much more potent Tribal forms of this rite, each attuned to the power and portfolio of one of the Firstborn and imbuing a spirit with a fragment of the patron's power.

                                ​Mask Spirit (Wolf Rite •••)
                                The ritemaster shrouds a spirit by tearing away part of its surface symbolism, drinking the sheared-off identity down to hide it from prying eyes.

                                ​Symbols: Jaws, concealment, darkness, chains

                                Cost: 5 Essence

                                ​Action: Extended (10 successes; each roll represents 1 turn)

                                ​Success: This rite targets a single spirit, which must be either willing or bound by another rite. At the culmination of the rite the ritemaster consumes a portion of the spirit's identity, dealing it a single point of aggravated damage; the ritemaster gains the Ban Condition matching the spirit's Ban. For the duration of the rite's effects, the spirit's footprint in the world is reduced both physically and spiritually. The rite imposes a -3 penalty on all attempts to track, locate or perceive the spirit unless it is actively hostile and attempting to attack; by spending a Willpower point, the spirit can force a failure on any such roll to become a Dramatic Failure. Other spirits will assume it is a minor or irrelevant spirit of no interest to them unless it directly attracts their attention or intervenes with their activities; this effect can be overcome by spending a Willpower point, but most spirits will have no reason to do so. Attempts to detect its Ban or Bane will, if successful, answer with the ritemaster's Bane of silver and any Ban Conditions the ritemaster is under instead of those of the spirit. The rite's effects last until the next full moon; the spirit cannot heal the aggravated damage from the rite until it ends.

                                    Words Of An Oak
                                    Young lovers carve their initials in the trunk of an old oak tree. Children growing to objectionable recklessness scrawl rough declamations of their enemies and pronunciations of their own inevitable rule into the bark with their sharp little knives. Self-proclaimed worshippers of the wild amble out of their comfortable lives for a brief dance around the tree in vain hope of finding spiritual succor. The humans don't understand the true nature of the ancient oak that they briefly bind into their lives. They can't really see the depths of its age, the glacially slow contemplation of its Shadow sentience, or the wisdom its boughs sift from the sky and draw up from the earth. They certainly have no idea of what a total bastard your average oak really is.

                                    Oak trees have the gift of prophecy. Half-asleep oak spirits can prognosticate and predict with surprising eloquence, when the fancy takes them. They're better at perceiving the slow currents of change - how a city's expansion will resculpt the landscape and how its growing communities will clash or intertwine with each other, how a government will stumble and fall, how a revolution will slowly build before its inevitable flashfire into incendiary tumult, how an elder Uratha's arrogance will bring about her doom. Still, they're reasonably on the ball when it comes to particularly local events - an oak can accurately predict the time and cause of death of a human who lives within one mile if it will happen in the next year, right down to the day, hour and even minute. An oak can see whose jealousies will burst into flame and how the raging fire of hatred and bitterness will consume households and families. An oak knows where the body will be buried, and who will dance with joy at the downfall of another. Oaks rarely prophecy or even consider pleasant, good things - or just practical and useful concepts like the next week's lottery numbers - and this is because they seethe with their own bitter envies and sour unhappiness. An oak spirit that manages to achieve serenity, harmony or peace loses its ability to prophecy.

                                    Oak spirits lie a lot. They can only utter prophecy when at least semi-dormant, and always demand a price for their services but are somehow capable of uttering falsehood even if they swear oaths and accept gathra for the bargain. How and why oak spirits are capable of violating the sanctity of the transaction isn't clear, but may stem back to transgressions in some past, primeval aeon.

                                    Oaks like to lie because they can use their twisted prophecies to bring about ruin and doom for the people they mislead. On the other hand, if they always lied, even the wise and cautious shamans would never trust them, so the oaks know they must sometimes speak the genuine truth to maintain some semblance of value to their prognostications.

                                    A prophecy can only be elicited once a year, and then only from the eldest oak in the region. The price is cheap enough for an Uratha, but others who would glean the wisdom of the trees have to swear to perform a duty in service to the oak; wise bargainers get the details on a task before they make the agreement, rather than leaving it open to the oak's malice down the line. Failure to carry out the duty will result in the spirit world turning against the transgressor, plaguing them with ill fortune and supernatural malisons; this is the case even if the oak's prophecy was a lie.

                                    Accomplished shamans use the performance of the rite as an opportunity to manipulate the dormant spirit as it comes to full wakefulness for the prophecy, using the full extent of their wiles and cleverness to try and convince the oak to tell the truth rather than a falsehood. Blood offerings are a very effective way to quickly improve the impression that an oak has of the ritemaster; in a few distant corners of supposedly civilised lands, lone travellers have a tendency to disappear when the locals water the roots of a revered, sacred oak with fresh blood for the yearly festival.

                                    ​There is one last wrinkle to the oaks and their prophecy, a scrap of lore so obtuse that only a tiny number of shaman know it to be the case. There are, from time to time, stories of beings wrought from lightning and metal; servants of strange powers indeed. Should such an angelic messenger give orders to an oak spirit, it can force the spirit to utter whatever the messenger desires in place of the augury that would usually spill forth. In this way, these beings can - on the incredibly rare occasions they appear - conscript the sacred oaks and prophetic wood-shrines of some human cults to serve their own manipulative purposes.

                                    Oak Prophecy (Pack Rite •)
                                    Old pacts with spirits of the wood can stir a slumbering oak spirit to brief wakefulness, granting it the ability to utter a prophecy.

                                    Symbols: Oak, bright colours, dancing, awakening

                                    Cost: 1 Essence (Uratha) or a promised favour or duty (everyone else)

                                    Action: Extended (5 successes, each roll represents half an hour)

                                    Success: This rite can only be performed once a year, and only at the oldest oak in a region. At the rite's culmination, the tree's spirit awakens and grants a prophecy, manifesting briefly through the creaking of the tree's boughs and the cracking of its bark. If the ritemaster has asked for a prophecy on a particular topic, the spirit will do so if the topic is something that is within its remit. If simply asked for a prophecy without specifics, it will prophecy on something relating to the ritemaster or other participants in the rite; it might predict the death of a loved one, or warn of a coming doom, or reveal a threat that exists and which the ritemaster is not aware of. However, there is an equal chance that the prophecy will be true, or be a lie; if the spirit's social impression of the ritemaster is Excellent due to leverage, gifts, or other efforts on the ritemaster's part, the odds of a true prophecy increase to two-thirds, and if the impression is perfect it rises to three-quarters.

                                        Pariah Skin
                                        Why take the blame, when you can shuffle the consequences off onto someone else?

                                        It's an old theme in spiritual and religious practices. Hand your sin off to another. Make an effigy onto which a disaster or curse can be shifted. Convince the evil spirits to infest the livestock instead of you, then kill the animals. Anchor the fuck-ups you've made to your home, then torch it and walk away as your past links symbolically go up in flame. It's all about shedding your skin so that the karmic vengeance strikes an empty husk, or better still, getting some other poor bugger to wear the skin for you.

                                        Amongst Uratha shaman, the common parlance usually names such techniques - the ones that are made real by the Shadow and the power of Essence - as 'pariah skins', symbolic scapegoats serving as lightning rods for the occult shit the world tries to throw and for the crimes the shaman commits in the line of duty. There's three broad categories of pariah skin - a literal mantle or coat of skin that the shaman wears, dolls or effigies, and living animals.

                                        It's rather grotesque, but a pariah mantle is a cloak, coat or other covering made of flayed skin. For once, the process of making one doesn't involve ripping it off some poor victim; for the pariah magic to work, it has to be the user's own skin. For an Uratha, this means an agonising process that will inflict several levels of lethal damage as it is performed; thankfully regeneration makes the notion viable at all. The skin has to then be treated, marked with the correct glyphs and invocations, then donned ceremonially; it doesn't need to be outwardly visible, though, and the thin Uratha-leather garment can be easily hidden under clothes. When it works, the skin seethes and smokes for as long as its power can hold out, after which it rapidly crispens and crumples away.

                                        Pariah effigies are simple and relatively easy to produce in numbers, although they're also anchored to a location. These draw on symbolic links that the shaman must imbue between themselves and the doll; some make little figurines that represent them, or weave in pieces of their own hair or bone, or build a mad little wooden or lacquered pantheon depicting spirits of secrecy and misdirection which they anoint in their own blood. When used to protect the shaman from a malison, an effigy will smoke and crumble to ash, or crack violently, or start to gibber before sloughing flesh-like layers off and expiring amidst its own false viscera.

                                        A pariah animal is simply that - a living beast used as the shaman's stand-in. A pig, chicken, rat or other small creature is common. Patterns of warding and false-scripture declaring the animal to be the shaman are marked on its hide; some shaman use paints and pigments, but since the markings need to be intact for the magic to work, more turn to brands, scarification and other permanent approaches to the problem. When the symbolic link brings down doom upon the hapless animal, sometimes the beast suffers whatever fate lay in wait for the shaman; other times the creature simply catches aflame, or hemorrages violently and swiftly.

                                        Pariah Skin (••• to ••••)
                                        ​
                                        ​Prerequisites: Occult 4, Resolve 3, must be Uratha or Wolf-Blooded

                                        ​Effects: Your character has learned the profane arts of the pariah skin, and knows the occult secrets required to make the world believe a scapegoat stand-in is symbolically you. Once per story, when your character would be affected by a hostile supernatural effect that is not a direct attack that deals damage, your character may reflexively enter a Clash of Wills against the effect with the dice pool using the character's highest Renown. Your character does not need to be aware of the supernatural effect occurring to use this protection. If the Clash is successful, the source will not be aware that the effect has been negated; instead, it will be given the impression that the effect has worked successfully.

                                        At ••••​, your character immediately becomes aware of the hostile attempt when it happens, and understands something of the nature of its source if it would not be immediately evident; you can identify the broad type of effect, such as a spirit's Numina, a Facet, etc. The source becomes a valid target for a tracking attempt; your character can follow the threads of symbolic power as clearly as if they were a strong scent.

                                        Drawback: The protection of the pariah skin is based on a physical thing, which can be destroyed or disrupted. A mantle can be torn away, a collection of effigies knocked over or wrecked, and an animal killed.

                                            Sea Dogs
                                            Bear with me on this one. I'm going to drop largely out of narrative or characterful writing here, because it's late, I'm tired and I'm running out of brain juice. I want to talk for a moment about the process behind getting to the end result (or indeed, getting anywhere) with this entry.

                                            ​I made up a bit list of ideas and concepts when starting to tackle this thread, and have been working through it, grabbing one bit or another and writing, rewriting, hacking things apart and writing again. Normally I'd do a lot more drafts and refinements still, but this is meant to be a bit of fun and it'd be nice to get it done at least vaguely within the perimeters of the Christmas holiday season. Having thinned the list down a lot by the time we've reached this point - this entry being number 10 - I had 'something naval/maritime' on the roster still. Someone on Discord mentioned they wanted to see something for the sea, so hey, I thought, handy convergence.

                                            ​Then I sort of flailed around a bit, because I wasn't particularly sure what​ to do. I started thinking about the problems sea wolves have to deal with - the issues with being on a cramped ship while a potential berserker engine of death, the problems with the weather and the fact the ocean is a hungry, dangerous place spiritually, and of course the concern of the vessel itself. It's always an issue with supernatural stuff where there are lots of ways to tamper or damage technological things and structures - the most vulnerable point in a shipful of werewolves is the ship. A lot of supernatural abilities and powers can do a number on a ship quite easily, and potent foes can just tear a hole in it.

                                            ​Problem was, following this line of thinking, I ended up getting mired in defensive, no-sell concepts, which I dislike. What I mean is, looking at ways that werewolves can strengthen their ship and stop it getting trashed, or ways to tamp down on Kuruth problems on a long voyage. Thing is, there's already possibilities covered in these areas - Uratha have the normal set of Death Rage management tools still, and if you want to look at a way to protect a ship from supernatural tampering, take a gander at the Nomad Chain from the Thousand Steel Teeth in The Pack and adapt a version of it for ships (call it something funky like the Albatross Burden or make it a figurehead or something). With eating your own name and pariah skins already being highly defensive, protective measures in this thread, I want to take a different tack.
                                            ​
                                            ​The storms and spirits and that sort of danger are pretty nifty too, but while having a crack at that, I hit a creativity doldrum. I had a notion about bottling weather up to boost rites or control the weather, but a werewolf can already use the Elements and Weather Gifts, summon spirits to do stuff, and generally the ideas I was coming up with were overlapping too heavily with these existing concepts. Still, this is the thread for shamanic stuff, and I definitely felt like something rites or occultism-related should lie at the core.

                                            ​The other problem here is that we don't really have any meaningful framework for seagoing ventures, and I don't plan to write up an entire naval system off the cuff for this one entry.

                                            ​Anyway, after banging these ideas around in my head for a while and ending up feeling frustrated at what I'd written, a few core points rolled out. Something for a ritemaster, yes. But also, refocus this on the central plank we pursued in Forsaken 2e - the notion that werewolves are hunters and predators. What's really cool about having wolves on the waves? The pursuit, the chase, the hunt. So rather than fiddling around with what I was angling for earlier, why not something that helps make the ritemaster on the waves into the consummate predator of the tides? Why not focus on a bit of material that amps up the ritemaster's capabilities as a pursuer on the sea, wielding rites and occult power?

                                            ​Shaking that​ around my head a while longer resulted in it solidifying into a style merit, which follows. I still don't guarantee excellence, and of course we still don't have a solid naval framework to be hanging it off, but hopefully it gets at the main idea. Please excuse the lack of descriptive text!
                                            ​
                                            Tide Hunter (Style Merit, • to •••)
                                            Prerequisites: Occult 4, Resolve 3, at least 5 dots of rites
                                            ​
                                            Storm Rider (•​): While aboard a vessel, your character halves the time on extended action rolls to conjure and bind weather spirits and elementals. When weather spirits or elementals that your character has summoned or bargained with are aiding the ship with their Influences, characters attempting to navigate or steer the vessel benefit from the 8-again quality on their dice pools for such purposes.

                                            Wave Chaser (••​): While aboard a vessel, when your character invokes the Sacred Hunt rite against another vessel, your ship only loses a pursuit roll against that vessel if the prey achieves an exceptional success on the dice; a normal success is treated as the prey losing the contest by 2 instead.

                                            Blood And Salt (•••​): When your character uses the Hunting Ground rite to declare a vessel their sanctified territory, then when pursuing, attacking or boarding another vessel, when prey's blood is spilt on either your vessel's deck or that of the enemy, you no longer need to pay the Essence cost to cause your character's regeneration to heal lethal damage for the remainder of the scene. Furthermore, any of the preys' dice pools of supernatural powers that would heal damage, restore health or ease pain suffer a -3 penalty for the remainder of the scene.

                                            ​I can see space for a 4-dot capstone here that potentially varies in effect depending on the ritemaster's Tribe.
