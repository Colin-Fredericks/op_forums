                        I'd say that if the Gentry are living stories, they need to be killed in a way that makes a satisfying conclusion to their story. The more appropriate and mythical the death, the greater the chance that they'll stay dead. It helps if you've been personally wronged by the Fae you're trying to kill, and if you've sworn mighty oaths against it or otherwise dedicated yourself to its destruction. It helps a lot if you can somehow turn its own power against it, or otherwise arrange an ironic fate. A Fae warlord's former champion slaying it in single combat with the sword it gave her would probably work. A complete stranger sniping the same Fae from cover with a cold iron bullet probably wouldn't work, at least not permanently.

                                    Originally posted by Satchel View Post

                                    It pretty much only works for Titles, seeing as that's how Legend combat plays out beyond the abstracted rules in Game of Immortals.
                                    I'm gonna be honest, if I was the story-teller, I wouldn't care what the rules said on that one. If I had a group of players sit and ponder the nature of the fae to that extent, come up with that idea, execute it, and actually pull it off, I'd give it to them, names and titles be damned. Because that was brilliant.

                                    Maybe call it a house-rule or something. Every Fae has a bane in the form of an event or situation which acts as a dramatically satisfying conclusion to the story they embody. Who determines what's dramatically satisfying? I do. I'm the story-teller damn it.

                                    You could make that really interesting. Like now motleys have a real shot at taking down their old keepers if they can just find this one bit of information and determine what old story or fairy tale their keepers embodied. So that gives them a really strong motivation to go back into the hedge and poke around, looking for clues, having adventures, seeing weird shit. If you're worried about that making the fae too weak or easily defeatable, you could add that since they are stories, and stories exist in the mind, they grow exponentially more powerful when somebody else knows their story, because it gives them more of themselves or something like that. That balances the temptation to search for the story with the knowledge that doing so will make your adversary way harder to actually best, even with knowledge of his weakness. And it also explains why maybe some people around the hedge and arcadia and stuff would know or have clues to that story, because if the fae feels that they can be trusted or (much more likely) controlled, then giving them that information, or even snippets of it, would greatly increase their power.

                                    If you don't like that, maybe rule that since the fae are stories, then they are intrinsically aware of anyone who knows their stories and where they are. Or, if you want to be really mean, it gives them some kind of power over you and your will. After all, stories exist in the mind. If you know the fae's story, and the fae IS his story, then logically speaking, the fae is now inside your mind. Or as logically as anything concerning the fae can be
