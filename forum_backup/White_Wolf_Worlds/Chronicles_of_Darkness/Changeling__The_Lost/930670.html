    Forming a Feud

    07-12-2016, 11:02 PM
    I feel like doing another bit of crowd sourcing for my setting of Buena Vista, this time more focused. I've decided a group of the Gentry have formed a feud, with Buena Vista (and 'nearby' extraplanar regions) as one of their playgrounds / battlefields, so these will be the True Fae the freehold most commonly deals with. This feud is larger than most, and it's open to debate that this caused Buena Vista to have higher fae activity or is merely a reflection of the region's fae affinity.

    An Arcadian realm has formed as a "neutral ground" for its members to congregate. It takes the form of a small kingdom with an enormous castle, where the Fae act out roles in a byzantine royal court. Actions taken there reflect and influence their actions in the outside world, sometimes as a near-literal replay, or cloaked in metaphor. Each member takes on a role fitting for a fantasy kingdom's court. They put up a superficial front of being united for the kingdom's common good, but as expected with the Lords and Ladies of Faerie, each member is pushing their own selfish agenda, forming factions of convenience. Every once in a while, some event prompts a swift, collective response, most often an invasion from other Fae beings on their claimed demesne. Even then, such skirmishes are seen as opportunities for a rival to be taken down by the "barbarians" outside their realm without being blamed. Changelings who served their Durance there might return to the mortal world with useful gossip that hints at the Gentry's agenda in Buena Vista. Sometimes, a feud member will intentionally allow spies enter as petitioners, slaves, and so forth to use against their rivals.

    So, parts I have in mind for creating a feud member if you'd like to suggest one:

    1) Code name: Part of the Autumn Court's duties are to collect and maintain all the information the freehold has on the Gentry. Not knowing their True Names, Ziggy assigns a code name themed on typefaces/fonts that seem appropriate to him. He hasn't isolated all members of the feud yet, but having a fitting code name saves me time picking one out once Ziggy discovers one.

    2) Royal Title: The Title (usually Actor) the Gentry favors using in the neutral ground realm.
    3) Feud Role: The role they serve in the neutral realm's royal court, often appropriate for their title, but some find humor in playing "against type."
    4) Other known Titles.

    5) Alliances and Rivalries: Snapshot of the allegiances and enmities with other members of the feud. Rivalries are more passionate and tend to be longer lasting, while alliances are easily formed and dropped as circumstances and moods dictate.
    6) Taste in tropes: In my chronicle, the Gentry (or at least the members of this feud) often have a collection of favorite tropes, themes, genre conventions, character archetypes, and so forth that generally defines their style. Some enjoy teaching "morals" in their stories as a means of demonstrating the superiority of their favored tropes or exposing the 'falsity' of a rival's trope.

    7) "Weight class": How they measure up compared to other members of the Gentry. Some members are comparatively young and weak, while others are ancient and powerful.

    Members thus far:

    Helvetica
    Royal Title: The Queen of Elegant Blades
    Feud Role: Samurai-Empress who allegedly rules the kingdom. She is always eager to lead a battle from the front lines, leaving opportunity for her "underlings" to conspire while she's away. Naturally, many use her eagerness to lead her into seemingly unwinnable battles, but her martial skills have thus far kept her alive. When she is at the castle, she has no appetite for bureaucracy or "mundane" business, and is used as a figurehead. She conducts herself with a (twisted) sense of honor, however, and uses it as an excuse to pull her puppeteers' strings when they act against her interests. Despite her act at playing a naive warrior who favors brawn over cleverness, she is still a member of the Gentry, and thus naturally devious, even if she's not a true master of the art.
    Other known Titles: The Lady of Ashes (Actor), The Fang of Muramasa (Prop), The Fractal Wilderness (Realm).

    Alliances
    (TBA)

    Rivalries
    Exocet: The details aren't shared yet, but The Old Bird delivered a prophecy of her downfall. With this news, Helvetica declared the Bird's stargazing to be worthless superstition, setting the terms of the conflict: Helvetica wins if she's still standing when the crisis comes. Exocet wins if his prophecy of doom comes to pass as he described it.
    Myriad: Helvetica all but stripped Myriad of their power, now bound under Helvetica's will in some fashion. Myriad, however, is a willing (for now) component in Exocet's machinations against Helvetica.
    Ziggy Zapf: The Lady of Ashes was Ziggy's Keeper, having him tend The Fractal Wilderness for her competitions and hunts. After escaping and earning the title of Sorcerer of the Autumn Court, she's decided to enact a quest to bring Ziggy to "justice" with the Queen, seeing him as an appropriate "wizard villain" to her "warrior hero."

    Tropes: Martial competition, blood sport, the glory of battle, might as a sign of virtue, the warrior mystique, open, direct methods.
    Weight class: Helvetica is considered "young" by the feud's standards. Some allowed her to "rule" as a means of testing her capabilities, while others thought she'd be more easily manipulated than a more experienced Fae on the throne.

    Exocet
    Royal Title: The Old Bird
    Feud Role: Court Astrologer. The Old Bird is an ancient being who has seen the rise and fall of (fictional?) dynasties in the feud's realm (or its backstory), and compelled to deliver prophecies to those who pass his tests. Though he poses as an dispassionate messenger of fate, he sees himself as pulling fate's strings to accelerate the entropy of the universe. Everything is ultimately doomed, so he might as well embody that inevitability.
    Other known Titles: The Great Orrery (Realm)

    Alliances
    Myriad: He sees irony in using a tool as weak as Myriad to bring down the "mighty" warrior empress, and a degree of poetic justice in letting them get revenge on the one who laid them low.

    Rivalries
    Exocet looks down on every dust mote, but he has special contempt for Helvetica. Even if she were to accept her inevitable demise, he believes she would see it as an opportunity to go out in a blaze of glory, where Exocet seeks to cause misery and despair. She won't go out with a bang, but a whimper.

    Tropes: Prophecies of doom, especially self-fulfilling ones, entropy, inevitability, despair, fate, astrological motifs, puppeteer.
    Weight class: Exocet is a particularly powerful and ancient member of the Gentry in "raw" terms, but his power is restricted by the countless pledges he's made over the eons. His indirect methods are as much a result of working around his bindings and frailties as a change in personal tastes.

    Myriad
    Royal Title: Revelry of the Pack (Wisp, destroyed)
    Feud Role: Myriad previously played the role of guardian beasts who patrolled the castle grounds as bound spirit animals. They would often whisper temptations to the 'noble' members of the castle to tempt them into primal, uncivilized acts of vice. As lowly beasts bound in service to the castle, they were initially considered beneath suspicion as the source of the whispers. Helvetica showed unexpected insight into the situation and commanded the destruction of all the unclean beasts and their kind. The ensuing 'genocide' left Myriad with only one Title. Fearing obliteration, Myriad bargained for mercy and was offered some lowlier kind of slavery that would presumably keep them in line and less able to retaliate or manipulate the feud.
    Other known Titles: ???

    Alliances
    Exocet: Myriad accepted the chance to get their revenge on Helvetica and reverse their fortunes. All they have to do now is bide their time, hope their pawns do as expected, and wait for their bindings to break... If they can wait.

    Rivalries
    Helvetica: Facing obliteration at the hands of a (supposed) paragon of civilized, disciplined society, and a weak one at that, has not sat well with Myriad.

    Tropes: Id beast, peer pressure, hive mind, corruption with consent, slaves overturning masters
    Weight class: Myriad has varied widely in power over their life, with impulsiveness being a common cause of their own downfall as well as their enemies. They are currently at their weakest in a long time.

    Collective Noun: Unlike other True Fae, if Myriad's True Name is exposed, they manifest as a Wisp instead of an Actor.

      Wee, worldbuilding fae! I don't actually know what kind of font Exocet or Myriad is; I pulled my names from the fonts in Word (or pieces of them, in the case of Comic and Hand). These are ideas thrown against the wall; if any of them don't fit, let me know.

      Onyx
      Royal Title: Hunter Who Stalks the Sun
      Feud Role: Master of the Hounds, for a given value of "hounds." Currently, he commands a dragon, a demon, and a river (three changelings that arrived with him). He will patrol the borders of the Fractal Wilderness, as well as procuring new "beasts" to seed the grounds (hobs or changelings). When seen at court, he is considered boorish and common, more interested in his hunting and far too caring of his hounds. He's not often seen actually in court, however, and there are rumors of his actual role being Spymaster, though who he is spying for is in doubt.
      Other Titles: Spear of Stillness (prop), Bow of Light and Arrows of Dark (prop), the Intern (actor)

      Alliances
      Helvetica: The Hunter considers the Queen a worthy rival. While outwardly, there is much fighting, arguing, and comparing of strength, the Hunter does not want her dead, or even weakened. He needs to beat her when she is strong; otherwise, it proves nothing.
      Myriad: Every hunter needs prey. As with the Queen, the Hunter wants the Pack strong and worthy of being hunted.
      Georgia: Publicly, these two hardly meet. This leads to rumors that the Hunter is spymaster for Frost. There's no evidence, though. Nothing direct.

      Rivalries
      Exocet: While all that are mighty must be brought low, the weak will, in turn, rise. The Hunter cannot reconcile with finality and inevitability, putting him at odds with the Old Bird.
      Comic: Jo is loud, irritating, and cannot be bested in their arena. The Hunter hates them.
      Hand: The Hunter was publicly scorned by the Clay for having "the wrong kind of passion," and is holding a grudge.

      Tropes: Horned god, hunter, shadows, night, the moon, cycles, change, the Wheel of Fortune
      Weight class: Medium. The Hunter is old, but he's gone through cycles of being stripped of power, then returning to strength. He is currently waxing half.


      Comic
      Royal Title: Jo King
      Feud Role: Jester. Jo would like to be in charge, but doesn't stand a direct chance against the Queen. So they play the fool instead, mocking and pranking everyone equally. Jo strikes a subtle balance, never going too far in their mockery, and never singling any target out for too long.
      Other Titles: none

      Alliances
      None

      Rivalries
      Absolutely everyone, though any given fae's hatred of Jo is not the strongest hatred that Fae possesses.

      Tropes: Jokes, puns, clowns, humor, madness, colors, the Fool
      Weight class: Low. Jo doesn't have a lot of direct power, and tends to save their strength for when it would be the most funny.


      Georgia
      Royal Title: Frost-On-Glass
      Feud Role: Lady-in-waiting. Frost took a subservient role, rather than one of authority. She is always impossibly poised, scrupulously proper, impeccably perfect. To look at her, one could never dream she was more than the ideal supporter. That would be the danger of looking upon Frost-On-Glass, she cannot be doubted. It is only later, when examining her actions, that trust in her wavers.
      Other Titles: Exquisite Doll (actor), Gaze of Neutrality (prop), the Frozen Sea (realm)

      Alliances
      Helvetica: Frost loyally serves the Queen. For now.
      Exocet: Before all this, the Old Bird was allied with Frost in the short term. Publicly, Frost must support the Queen, but rumors spread that it's a ploy to take her down.
      Onyx: Publicly, these two hardly meet. This leads to rumors that the two are deeply involved. However, that's directly at odds with Frost's previous support of the Old Bird.

      Rivalries
      Myriad: Such a being should be beneath her notice, but Frost would rather it ceased to exist.
      Comic: Once, Jo threw a pie at Frost. Once.
      Hand: The imprecision and sloppiness displayed by the Clay would enrage Frost, if she were to be so crass as to show emotion. Still, she/he must be destroyed.

      Tropes: Delicacy, ice, cold, control, lace, glass, porcelain, precision, the Empress
      Weight class: Heavy. She could give anyone up to and including the Queen a fair match, but has so far chosen not to.


      Hand
      Royal Title: Beloved Clay
      Feud Role: Noble Fop. The Clay is a being of sensation, at his/her core, and so plays the role of the flirt, the lover, the gourmand. She/he can shift passions at a moment's notice, but never into despair or despondency.
      Other Titles: Passionate Doughboy (actor), Woman-of-Earth (actor), Army of Love (Wisps)

      Alliances
      Constantly shifting. The Clay likes to pretend everyone is with him/her; the truth varies.

      Rivalries
      Onyx: The Clay made an offer to the Hunter for a time of passion; the Hunter replied he needed to look after his hounds. No one denies the Clay, especially in favor of some cheap mortals.

      Tropes: Passion, sensation, earth, ceramics, wild abandon, hedonism, the Lovers
      Weight class: Medium. For all her/his power, the Clay has an issue with focus.


      (If I was giving tarot cards to the three you had, I'd say Helvetica/the Queen is Strength, Exocet/Old Bird is the Tower, and Myriad/the Pack is Ten.)
