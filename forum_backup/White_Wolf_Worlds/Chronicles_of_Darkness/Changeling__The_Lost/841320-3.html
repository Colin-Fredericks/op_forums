                                Name: Mr. Bushido

                                Concept: Repentant Soldier

                                Kith: Razorhand

                                Seeming: Fairest

                                Description: Mr. Bushido appears to be of Japanese ethnicity with dark black hair that he wears in a top knot and sports a thin goatee as facial hair. He dresses in traditional Japanese robes and always carries a sword on his person. When in his Mask, the sword is either concealed from public notice or disguised as another instrument, and although his features are still Japanese, they are less traditional style and more appropriate to the modern time period.


                                Details: Having abandoned his real name, the one known as Mr. Bushido has a bloody history that he is not comfortable to speak of even among friends. One spring morning while taking a walk through the forest, he was snatched by a True Fae and dragged off to their oriental realm. There he was rigorously instructed in the way of swordsmanship and loyalty, becoming the iconic archetype of a samurai. To serve his Keeper, he was presented with a cursed sword. The moment Mr. Bushido wrapped his fingers around it, he was unable to break physical contact with the weapon ever again. It was bound to him now, as much a part of his body as his arm or leg!

                                For many years (in Fae terms), Mr. Bushido defended his Keeper's realm from invaders while simultaneously crushing the peasants that tried to rebel against his tyrannical rule. But then the unthinkable happened; he failed to defeat his enemy. By the code of the samurai, he should have taken his own life on the spot when his opponent chose to spare him, but something awoke inside him and he began to remember. This was not his home, this was not even his real time period, someone or something had dragged him here by force, brainwashed him to be the perfect warrior. Reeling from the shock of his memories, he chose to join the rebels and help them return to freedom by cutting down the soldiers he once served beside!

                                Since his escape, Mr. Bushido seeks to make amends for the blood he shed serving his Keeper. No longer seeing the honor in slaying an enemy, he kills only when it is necessary instead of when his pride compels him to do so. Even then, he does so with a heavy heart, as it adds more guilt that he must atone for before he can rest peacefully.

                                                Lazlo Blue-Blood, Displaced Patrician
                                                Darkling Earthbones of the Wind Court
                                                Mask/Mien: Guru/Martyr

                                                Description
                                                It’s not easy to look Lazlo in the eye. Even those who only see the Mask can tell two things from such brief contact - he is very old and very guilty. His eyes stare out from above hollow cheeks, his skin is pale verging on bloodless, and his comportment is seldom without a nervous tic.

                                                His fae mien is even more unsettling, from his sunken eye sockets to the facial scar that weeps a turquoise blue ichor to the twitching spider legs that sometimes stick out from the cracks in his gray marble flesh - as a changeling, Lazlo looks like a hideously warped statue carved from the earthen prison of something terrible.

                                                Background
                                                Lazlo inherited a philanthropic legacy rooted in one of the success stories of the American Dream. He used to be as near as one gets to modern nobility in both name and deed, fighting the political corruption that plagued his city’s very reputation tooth and nail at personal expense.

                                                Lazlo was, in short, a veritable pillar of the community. The Stranger Cevexh, in common fae fashion, took this rather more literally than most when she had him hauled off to Arcadia. She made a stone column of his fortitude, to raise her arena above the thorns and briars that crawled across her domain, and left him below to support her decadence.

                                                He stayed like this for a time, listening to the noise of gladiator fights by day and warding off creeping vines and questing roots by night, until one evening Cevexh’s best-loved prizefighter was given a leave of absence. Struck by the injustice of this favoritism, the pillar that was Lazlo set forth a litany of the indignities he had been subjected to while trapped away from his home.

                                                The declamation stretched for seeming hours and was tinged with such poison that the Hedge-plants withered around him, and when he finally came to a stop he was shocked to find that the rose-child was still standing before him. Stranger still, through the haze of his shame he realized that the duelist was offering to take him away from the arena if he would only show the boy the world of his birth.

                                                He accepted, and since leaving Faerie the weight has not lifted from his shoulders.

                                                Personality
                                                Despite his gloomy outlook, Lazlo has adapted easily to the fae currents of favor and obligation, rapidly trading and networking his way to both a clerical position at City Hall and an administrative office as Treasurer of the Looking-Glass Court. His pride demands that he fall no further, and to that end he has become one of the most well-connected changelings in Chicago.

                                                There are several reasons Lazlo has thrown himself so entirely into his role in the freehold. Like many of the Lost, he left a family behind when his Keeper pulled him into her tale, and he does not trust in his ability to step into his old life when his political activity already put significant distance between him and his children.

                                                Instead, he has made a minor habit of adopting newly escaped changelings as protégés, pushing them to strive for whatever pursuits mesh with his own unfulfilled dreams. He commiserates with the bitter Arcadian intern at Newcomb and Razor over the frustrations of fetch-trickery, tutors the Flowering ex-gladiator in the subtleties of the Blue Court’s relation to the Red, and otherwise fans those sparks of similitude he comes across as he quietly worries at the cracks in his flagging ego

                                                    Name: (The) Ziband

                                                    Concept: Aspires to Godhood

                                                    Kith: Witchtooth + Coyote

                                                    Seeming: Fairest


                                                    Description: An anthropomorphic coyote with sandy colored fur and dressed in human clothing. He is fond of the colors yellow, purple, and green, often incorporating these colors into his outfit in some way. His mask appears to be an attractive Caucasian man with sandy brown hair that sticks out in short, spiky tufts about his head. He is frequently seen wearing a smirk that lays somewhere between proud satisfaction and the edge of madness.


                                                    Details: Before he was taken, The Ziband was a nihilist who found the human world to be dull, boring and pointless. Everything was repetitive where nothing out of the ordinary happened anymore. Perhaps it was this melancholy disposition that drew the Fae to him, but one day he was approached by his Keeper who promised to show him a world of magic and splendor. All it would cost him is to abandon the only world he had ever known and come live with a nigh omnipotent, alien being from another dimension!

                                                    During his stay, his Keeper showed him secrets, so very many secrets, and Ziband soaked them up like a sponge. But it was never enough; no matter how much he learned about Arcadia, he was incapable of figuring out most of it on his own. His Keeper had to explain things to him as if he were an idiot child unaware of what he was doing. His human mind simply could not grasp the nature of a realm forged out of dreams and metaphor, where logic and common sense did not apply to the forces of nature. Eventually, Ziband could no longer stand his perpetual ignorance and fled back to the human world vowing he would return when he was more properly equipped to handle Arcadia's magical nature.

                                                    Since his "escape" from Arcadia, Ziband has devoted his efforts towards increasing his power first and foremost. He is a resident of the Freehold, but has not pledged himself to any Court, instead remaining an independent agent. As a Witchtooth, he is constantly researching occult knowledge and hoarding magical artifacts. While as a Coyote, he is fiendishly manipulating everybody who interacts with him, be they friends or enemies. He is still juggling with the idea of what he should do with his closest allies. So far, he's likes the idea of becoming their new Keeper once he feels ready to return to the world of the Fae.


                                                    Ziband's long term goal is to return to Arcadia not as a slave, but as a noble. He seeks to gentrify himself and become one with the True Fae, believing that in doing so, he will obtain godly power and be able to warp reality to his will. Naturally, nobody in the Freehold knows about this plan of his, not even those who he calls friends.



                                                    Additional Note: Ziband has a personality quirk of speaking about himself in the third person, often adding "the" at the beginning of his name. He also tends to have very high opinions of himself, not dissimilar from a celebrity diva that mingles with common folk. To some, this quirk is entertaining (in small doses), while to others, it can grow tiresome and annoying.
