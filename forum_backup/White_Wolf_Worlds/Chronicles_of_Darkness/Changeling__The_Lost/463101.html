    Ideas For True Fae

    03-31-2015, 12:27 AM
    Simply put, this is a thread for us to post ideas for True Fae and their realms!


    Crowned King of Karpes: If one were to say this Fae was strange, you would probably ask them to be more specific. When acting on dry land, the one who goes by the name Crowned King of Karpes often takes the form of a purple scaled karpe inside a fish bowl. The bowl is attached to a deep sea diving suit which appears to have been modified for aquatic life forms and is adorned with a red velvet cape with white, fluffy trims. The suit is filled with water, instead of air, clearly designed for use by a creature who does not function well on land. The king's body is unnaturally flexible, even cartoonish in its abilities. If one peers into the fish bowl, they can see the karpe using its dexterous fins and tail to pull switches and levers to pilot the mechanical suit. When they are in the water, however, this Fae can be found outside of their suit, wearing a majestic crown and carrying a royal scepter in one of its fins. The suit is protected by the magic of the True Fae and is practically indestructible by mundane methods.


    The realm of this Fae (known as Karpe Kingdom) has a steam punk theme, dwelling in an underwater city made of copper and brass with out dated (though sophisticated) technology. Somehow the metals are protected from the erosion that would normally come from being underwater, causing the city to shine brilliantly for those who come to visit. The city's inhabitants are largely comprised of various species of karpe, although the city has drawn tourism from other aquatic or amphibious lifeforms during its existence.


    The fish who dwell in the city are both cultured and disturbing. They tend to wear Victorian era clothing such as top hats, monocles, powdered wigs, fancy suits and large dresses, but they never speak a word. At least not in any verbal communication, but who knows if they're communicating in some kind of Arcadian sign language. They float through the city attending social gatherings, grand operas (which consist of them blowing a sequence of bubbles), orchestra symphonies, ballroom dancing and even dinner parties. It would be quite charming if you could figure out how the hell they communicate with each other! When visitors to the city attempt to strike up a conversation, the general response they get is for the inhabitants to give them fishy stares with their large eyes and open mouths. Sometimes these stares can last for several minutes, waiting for the stranger to give up and swim away!

          The Bunny Wizard: As far as True Fae go, The Bunny Wizard is rather simple in its mannerisms and desires. In appearance, The Bunny Wizard resembles a man sized bunny with white fur and dressed in a purple, flowing robe. Its bestial form gives it a rotund physique, making it seem fat or heavyset in a way. It carries an enormous carrot like some kind of magic staff, which it keeps close to its body at any given time, even when sleeping. As far as others can tell, The Bunny Wizard is driven by one simple objective, to satisfy its carnal desires. Its realm is host to a never ending orgy where males and females alike are passed around from one partner to the next. The only time The Bunny Wizard leaves its hedonistic realm is to add more slaves to it. To accomplish this, it has devised a spell for turning others into sex crazed bunnies like itself. Once they've been transformed, they're practically begging to join his harem, as they skip merrily off through the Hedge with their new Keeper.


          The Bunny Wizard may be a True Fae, but he is also surprisingly lenient when it comes to escapees. A creature who is drawn to sex for the pleasure of it has no real emotional attachment to the endless chain of partners he leaves unconscious on the floor. Once The Bunny Wizard grows bored of a member of his harem, it is possible for their memories of their human life to creep back in. At which point, they find it is rather easy to simply walk out the door and never come back. Their Keeper has already moved onto new playthings to keep him entertained. Of course being allowed to leave and being able to leave are two entirely different things. The moment escapees step outside the protection of his realm, they have to evade the denizens of the Hedge and other Fae in order to successfully return to the human world.


          In theory, The Bunny Wizard is a pretty weak member of the True Fae. He's not a very good fighter and does not hold any significant titles or authority within the ranks of the Fae. He simply exists to satisfy its own desire for pleasure and cares for little beyond that. Despite his theoretical weakness, however, many Hunter cells regard him as a significant threat. The Bunny Wizard's first instinct when pressed into battle is to use the spell on his attackers, and more than one foolish Hunter has ended up joining his harem after launching an attack.


          Changelings taken by The Bunny Wizard can belong to almost any manner of Seeming (Ogres and Elementals are the rarest, though not unheard of), but they all reflect the image of bunnies, rabbits and hares in some degree. While they all serve carnal needs, the types of seeming and kiths they represent often have a baring on additional duties they must perform. Food, drink and music for Wizened, being one such example. According to the escapees, Ogres are the more violent lovers in the harem, while Elementals are used for erotic decorations and scenery.

              Here's the backer for my voodun antagonist:

              The Adept of Bleak Omens: Tick tock, goes the clock of the world. Time goes on, entropy grows greater, and on top of the highest clocktower in Arcadia rests the spade-headed Adept, gazing out impassively into the world as he contemplates the concept of endings. He was born with a full comprehension of eternity, they say, already uncaring of all the golden achievements of the world-to him, the only interesting thing is their end, for the thing that dwarfs all other things in importance is the end of time itself, and even that may be humdrum should more optimistic cosmic predictions prove accurate and the grave of an old universe is the cradle of a new one. Nothing is permanent, not even permanence, says the Adept, so nothing is worth caring for. Sentiment is actually something he finds frustrating and confusing, actually-nothing in his Domain has a true name anymore, only a clear description of what it does, as he finds it patently ridiculous that, say, an engineer, a sorcerer, and a saint can all be named John.

              What does interest him however, is how things fall apart-eternity's constant task, its art. He is even more fascinated by how people attempt to avoid the inevitable doom that so enthralls him; some kill rabbits and string their feet into charms, some have this strange fear of breaking mirrors, and most intriguing of all, prophecies. Prophecies that often involve unalterable doom, but are supposed to bring comfort to those who read them because they know of the doom beforehand. So the Adept has made of himself a function of Fate; he finds prophecies and omens, and observes how mortals react to the doom, his servants and presence giving rise to stories of strange monsters that congregate just before something truly terrible happens. What makes him a threat, though, is his ego-he can conceive of nothing more interesting than watching doom unfold, and thus any change to progression of events for the better is something he inevitably interprets as spiting him, specifically, so he lashes out and attempts to put things the way they were. Actually thwarting a doom actually provokes the Adept to have a tantrum; his fun has been ruined for this temporary hope, and what's more, his own role prevents him from resetting the game board, as it were-there is no doom to correct, anymore, so the arbiter of doom is legally prevented from interference. His entire Domain knows when a project of his has been upset, as he is known to build entire nations purely for the purpose of bringing world-shaking cataclysms and wars against them to work off his frustration.

              He takes slaves too-some of them are people who survived his pet dooms when they pass, taken in the confusion. More frequently, however, is that he buys them from humans; he has forgotten more things about Destiny, Time, and Divination than the brightest sage among humans will ever begin to know, and he finds it is simply easier to outsource to those who would know his lore. Besides, he finds humans who despise sentiment as much as he does pleasant company, for such temporary things; there's nothing particularly dire about selling any animal away, to him. These become his test subjects and enforcers for his own prophecies, those that affect Arcadia. The few changelings who escape, mostly by finding loopholes and oversights, are often some of the best diviners in any Freehold, having been taught as a function of survival-and the greatest advocates of breaking fate altogether, so that doom no longer controls them.

                  Mirror of Reversal: In your life time, you have probably heard the story of clones or doppelgangers who emerge from mirrors as polar opposites of the people who's image they reflect. One of the True Fae in particular, has taken a liking to these stories and has assumed the role of the Mirror of Reversal. It's goal is to take mortals away to Arcadia and turn them into complete opposites of what they were before. Prom queens are turned into garbage munching ogres, while deformed children are refashioned into Greek-like perfection. The homeless raised to the status of kings, and the 1% are cast out into the streets and left to feed on rats and table scraps.

                  The Mirror of Reversal manifests in the mortal world as a mirror, using them as doorways into and out of the Hedge. When a mortal comes within reach, their own reflection grabs them and yanks them through the portal, after which a Fetch emerges from the mirror, and the portal closes behind them. Captives do not view the Hedge as a tangle of thorny brambles, but instead as shards of broken glass swirling around them, nicking and cutting away at them as they are dragged to the Fae's realm.


                  Within the Mirror's realm, everything operates in reverse. It is like a never ending version of opposite day, except much more extreme. It is not as simple as just saying or doing the opposite of what you mean, the very laws of nature have been twisted around. If one did not have their mind previously sliced away by their trip through the Hedge, they would probably go mad as their perceptions were turned upside down and tried to sort out the new information around them. Mirrors are also very prevalent here, and one can hardly look in any given direction without seeing mirror fragments embedded in the surrounding landscape. Prisoners who are coherent enough to strike up a conversation have suggested that these mirror fragments are actually pieces of their keeper, silently observing their realm and the results of their creations.



                  When choosing who to take back to Arcadia, the Mirror of Reversal generally seeks out those who play to their traits with obnoxious levels of indulgence and stereotyping. A simple pretty girl isn't enough to warrant a capture, but a blond haired bimbo who lives off daddy's money and dates the school quarter back is definitely something the Mirror of Reversal wouldn't mind turning into a hideous monster. A typical politician would also be safe, but someone who abuses their wealth and authority either openly or behind closed doors with a throng of whores and cocaine parties would draw the Fae's attention as well. The Mirror of Reversal loves to take the epitome of mortal stereotypes and completely switch them around, whether for amusement, curiosity or malice is unknown to others.

                          Il Dottore

                          Il Dottore. more commonly known as the Plague Doctor, is a truly insidious True Fae. Appearing equally as a crow-masked medieval physician, a vulture-headed carrion god and a smiling modern doctor in a pristine white coat, Il Dottore is as fascinated as he is repulsed by the frailty of humanity, but particularly by the ravages of disease. He views his charges in equal measure of pity and contempt, and those that he steals away from the mortal world are either those obsessed with their own health and mortality or those riddled with the most awful of diseases. He beguiles both of them with the sickly sweet temptation of life beyond the petty handful that fate has offered them, running through their fingers like so much sand, and like all the Gentry he keeps his world to the letter.

                          He keeps it, because he can keep his victims alive for decades in their state of misery.

                          Those unfortunate souls who he whisks away to his Realm finds a vast garden and a hospital, part Victorian finery and part squalid plague pit. The plants are of surpassing beauty, and all of them holding the most virulent poison that mankind could dare to dream of. Many of his subjects tend these grounds, suffusing their very marrow with their vile ichor, or brew up another of Il Dottore's latest 'panaceas', or set forth to scrub the floors and walls clean of the effluvia from those that he experiments on. The healthy and the wretched alike are subject to hideous plagues from the nightmares of madmen, but kept on the vital side of the veil by the concoctions and treatments that the doctor thinks up. survivors of this realm bear scars from blades and acid, turned from proud humans to scurrying lab rats or things more pitiful still.

                              Speaking of Doctors


                              Vivi, The Vivisector: One of the more depraved members of the Gentry, there are still members of the Autumn Court that spit every time her name is uttered in their presence. Vivi the Vivisector was born human and ended up being turned into a Changeling. After her escape, however, she decided immortality and godly power was a sweet deal and ended up becoming a true fae of her own volition. According to those who knew her, she was reported to have watched the Island of Doctor Moreau for a week straight prior to her leaving.


                              Vivi never did get the hang of naturally turning people into Changelings, but unfortunately for her victims, she wasn't about to let that stand in her way. A former member of the Autumn Court, she had a keen understanding of biology and sorcery. If she couldn't turn people into changelings the easy way, she'd do it the hard way. This began her nickname as she used her medical instruments and Arcadian elixirs to perform genetic modifications with surgical precision. She is a mad scientist conducting amputations, surgical grafts and gene splicing on those she drags to her realm. The magic of Arcadia seeps into their wounds, turning these normally impossible hybrids into working bodies!


                              Not all of Vivi's experiments are successful, of course, and some take longer to complete than others. These works in progress and failed rejects are kept sealed inside stasis pods, their bodies floating around inside alchemical liquids until Vivi decides what to do with them. Those who didn't work out the way she planned are terrifyingly left in stasis as a reminder of where she went wrong and how to improve on future attempts. In theory, they will be left in their pods for the rest of their days, unless somebody puts them out of their misery, or attempts to free them.


                              In appearance, Vivi the Vivisector takes the form of a sophisticated and well mannered doctor, dressing in a pristine white suit with a red tie. Her hair, skin and eye colors are never the same two days in a row, changing to a random hue many of which are not natural to humans. A pair of half rimmed glasses adorn her face, attached to a safety chain to prevent them from being lost or stolen. She also wears gloves and carries around a cane with a crystal rat skull as its head piece. With the exception of forcing her modifications upon her victims, she has the demeanor of a motherly figure. She speaks gently to her creations (the successful ones) and even gives them free roam about her realm, after making sure they understand the outside world can never accept them. Staying with her is the only way they'll fit in, and they can live happy and safe under her care. All she asks is that they help out with her work from time to time, and bring her new subjects when necessary.

                                  This idea was inspired by another play by post RP I created a long time ago.


                                  The Producer: In the realm of Arcadia, the one who goes by the name of The Producer is one of the most popular Gentry around. Hobgoblins and True Fae alike flock to his realm to witness the entertainments he constructs for their viewing pleasure. Sometimes you may even find loyalists Changelings accompanying their Keepers to such an event. The Producer has taken on the self appointed role of game show host, and he specializes in the production of war games. In his own words, reality shows are all scripted while television and video games are the same boring story rehashed a thousand times over. The audience wants something fresh, something new, something real. And so he gave them the Blood Sports!

                                  The contestants are Changelings gathered from across the world and forced to compete in deadly competitions against each other for the amusement of the crowd. The winners escape with their lives (often making it back home), but the losers die horribly to appease the sadistic crowds. One of the most popular series The Producer runs is called 'Climbing the Food Chain', in which the contestants hunt, kill and devour each other. The last survivor is declared top of the food chain, as well as winner of the game. Other games he has produced include gladiator battles, a full scale war between two factions of slaves, and a survival challenge in which the contestants are dumped naked on an island full of death traps, forced to build their own tools, clothes and shelters! The hidden tar pits, carnivorous plants, and dangerous predators only being some of the hazards they must avoid while constructing a defensible location.


                                  The Producer resembles a very large man standing roughly 8 feet tall and with a well muscled body of 245 lbs. He dresses in a blood red business suit with a black undershirt and tie. His head is shaved clean and he moves with an energetic air. The kind of passion expected from somebody who's life revolves around putting on a show for others. In fact, he actually seems quite friendly and cheerful when addressing his contestants, despite the fact he's placing them in life and death situations. Despite his friendly disposition, one should not mistake him to be hesitant to fight. Even within his own realm there are rumors which say The Producer was entered himself as a contestant to his own games and emerged the victor. If these rumors are true, then it means not only is he very skilled in combat, but he's also willing to place himself in harm's way just for the thrill of it!

                                      Happy Land Fun Fair

                                      Even by the standards of the Gentry, this Gentry is weird. The Happy Island Fun Fair takes the form (mostly) of Realms, each with its own theme: Futureworld, Jungleland, Yesterplace (itself a composite of myriad Realms: China, Japan, Frace, Germany, etc), Storyrealm (again with many subdivisions), and of course the Midway. Other Titles appear as Wisps tending the booths, and even a few Props to be won as prizes for top scores in some of the games on the midways (every Realm has its own midway, as well as the Midway Realm). Most prizes, if they're magical at all, are either Trifles or Tokens. Each Realm has rides, of course, and food stalls and all the other things that go to make up a large amusement park, though there is the problem of the park not really understanding the concept of "too much of a good thing." The many people, mostly changelings, who inhabit it aren't workers; they're the customers. The park has many entrances, broad and easy the find; it has one exit, which is much smaller, moves, and can be almost anywhere. It could be at the exit to a haunted house attraction, or it could be a simple step through a curtain, or a particular car on a sky tram might let you off outside the park. The park knows it needs an exit, but it doesn't really understand why, and that leads to the real problem: Amusement parks are not designed for long-term residency.

                                          The Hive Mind: The true scope of how large this gentry is has outgrown mankind's perceptions. It does not merely exist on a physical level, but has also manifested itself into the realm of conscious thought. The Hive Mind, as it is known, psychically links all of its servants together, transmitting information and orders instantaneously across vast distances. Those who escape Arcadia are severed from The Hive Mind's control, regaining their sense of individuality. But for those trapped within The Hive Mind's realm, there are no personal thoughts or actions, there is only the collective will of the hive.


                                          The denizens of The Hive Mind's realm take the form of insect-like beings and can be of practically any species. Ants, bees, roaches, flies or whatever else The Hive Mind decides to create. Some of them don't even resemble earthly insects, but instead take the form of mutated hybrids and macabre horrors. The realm is encased in fresh resin, drying into a hive network and expanding its territory in the process.


                                          The Hive Mind is greedy for land and resources, and it's swarm is parasitic in nature. Due to the sterility of most Fae touched, The Hive Mind is forced to indoctrinate other life forms into its swarm via symbiosis. Those taken captive from the mortal world are sealed within chrysalises while the magic of Arcadia mutates them into productive members of the swarm, and linking their consciousness to The Hive Mind. Like a gestating pupa, they emerge from the chrysalis in a more glorious form, one better suited to serve the Gentry's needs.


                                          It is not clear what causes individuality to resurface in those who have been enslaved to The Hive Mind. But evidence has suggested that the further away one is from the swarm, the more difficult they are to control. The Hive Mind is the collective, so when they are in smaller numbers, it becomes easier for the swarm to gain independent thoughts and actions. Without The Hive Mind's constant orders dominating their minds, it is possible for their memories to resurface, and for them to seek out the mortal world again.


                                          The Hive Mind's physical body is an amorphous mass of organic matter. When examined closely, it appears to be a giant brain wrapped inside a chrysalis. Fleshy tentacles protruding from The Hive Mind's body gives it the ability to defend itself if need be, but it appears to be rooted to the ground, making it immobile. This could explain why The Hive Mind is located in the center of its realm, where it has the best chance of reaching out to all those under its influence. It is also shielded with a spiky carapace, leaving only patches of fleshy membrane visible to see the brain inside.


                                          There are many horrors and atrocities that have been associated with The Hive Mind over the ages, but one of its most depraved creations are the Brood Mothers. In an effort to expand its swarm as rapidly as possible, The Hive Mind has fashioned a unique Contract to grant female Changelings the ability to reproduce. However, this contract subjects them to a state of hyper fertility, and they can give birth to several dozen children from a single pregnancy, who are quickly turned into Changelings and added to the ranks of the swarm. Although life as a Brood Mother is a living nightmare, the contract to make one is extremely costly, and only a handful of them exist within the swarm at any given time. Rumors among those who escaped claim that in order to activate the contract, the Brood Mothers must devour their mates, transferring the male's life energy to the offspring within her. This ritual sacrifice must be performed for each pregnancy they attempt.

                                              Cartoon Antics: The first taste of the mortal world this Fae experienced was in the form of television. Somehow it managed to get a working satellite signal in Arcadia! It quickly became entranced by cartoons and sitcoms. Oh the wacky situations and exaggerated scenarios the characters would find themselves in were so amusing, the Fae just had to come here in person and see it for himself. Imagine the Fae's disappointment to find the real world was much less comical and more predictable than the broadcasts it had been so interested in. This couldn't do at all, it was too mundane, too depressing, too boring!

                                              And so the Fae had an idea, it would bring comedy back into peoples lives. And so it began to design its realm, a realm where stringing up Christmas lights resulted in a string of unlikely and disastrous scenarios. A world where the colors were brighter, the inhabitants more eccentric and the laws of physics... mutable. A world where people could get hurled through a brick wall, and not only survive, but make a full recovery by the end of the week (when the next episode airs). A world where superstitions came true, a world with laugh tracks and theme songs and wacky sound effects. Where nothing you set out to do today is ever as simple as its supposed to be!

                                              This is the realm of Cartoon Antics, and now they're in need of characters to fill the roles!

                                                  The Invincible Champion: This Fae has one simple desire, to know defeat on the battlefield of Arcadia. They have spent eons in combat and not once have they tasted humility or shame. Those who are taken by the champion are forced to train and compete against each other until a fighter worthy of the ultimate challenge emerges to knock the champion from his throne. Unfortunately, The Invincible Champion has grown vain and arrogant during his uncountable victories in battle, and deems none of them worthy of such a challenge. And so his slaves continue to fight and maim each other under the watchful gaze of the champion, desperately trying to reach some level of skill where they will descend from the throne to challenge them to a duel. Months of training pass, they earn victory upon victory for years, decades, possibly even centuries, and still the champion remains unmotivated, demanding more tests, more training before they prove themselves a worthy challenge. Sometimes a slave finally snaps under the perfectionist vanity of the champion and decides to fight their way past the guards to escape. Unfortunately for them, that only catches the champion's interest even further.

                                                  The Invincible Champion resembles an enormous man standing 13 feet tall and weighing nearly 500 lbs of muscle. His body is clad from neck to toe in red armor dyed with the blood of millions of victories. His weapon of choice is a pair of swords that would take a trained Ogre two hands to wield even one of them properly. The blades measuring 8 feet long, 4 feet wide, and weighing around 300 lbs each. It is said that The Invincible Champion once used these very swords to split a moon in half, and considering this is Arcadia that is probably true!




                                                  The Forever Young: Some kids just can't wait to grow up. You get money, freedom, your own house, your own car, est. Others see the downside to being a grown up and wish to remain children forever. These are the targets of The Forever Young, a Fae who's realm is designed to reflect a child's paradise. There are no rules, at least none that the human mind is able to comprehend. Nobody ages here, you're free to do as you please so long as it falls within the confines of the inconceivable laws that govern the place. But while it may have all the joy of eternal childhood, it also comes with the bad stuff too. Children can be cruel, especially when confronted with differences and lack of supervision. Children who bully and ridicule the others for being different or weak are known to earn themselves among the Huntsmen of the realm, helping to ensure no child leaves the paradise their master has built for them. The Forever Young doesn't understand why they're so sad. They get to be young and free forever, and really that should be all they need to be happy.


                                                  While the meaner children have the ability to push others around and even hunt down escapees, The Forever Young is the master of this domain. A wild king, it takes the form of a young boy dressed in tattered clothes and is often found swinging through the tree tops on ropes, vines and pulley systems, maneuvering through the tree houses with the speed and grace only a True Fae can manifest. He loathes adults and has forbidden them to enter his realm under any circumstances. The only hope the Changelings have of escaping his grasp is to allow time to run its course and, if they're lucky, they'll be adults by the time the Huntsmen track them down.


                                                  The weapons of the Huntsmen are fashioned in the image of children's toys. Wooden staves, sling shots, a bag of marbles, plastic swords, and so on. But with the magic of the Wyrd empowering them, these toys are anything but. Slingshots fire rocks with the force of a handgun, the marbles explode like grenades when tossed to the ground, plastic blades maintain a very real edge, these are just some of the possibilities!
