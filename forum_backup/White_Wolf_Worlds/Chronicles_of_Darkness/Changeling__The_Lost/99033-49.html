I put one hand around her waist and the other behind her knees and launched myself into the sky. And it’s a good thing that I’m stronger than I look, because carrying another fully-grown person – even a relatively petite person like Joan – is no joke when you’re flying.

I’ve seen her ecstatically happy before (what can I say? I’m good at what I do), but I don’t think I’ve ever seen her look quite so… carefree before. Of course, I’m not one to talk… I can still feel the big goofy grin spreading across my face every time I spread my wings and launch myself into the air. Still… she wears it well.

I didn’t ask for details – Calamity looked a little shaken up – but I understand that she had to weather some sort of custom-made dream to prove that she could maintain her wits and focus amidst adversity.

She used general terms when speaking with Juan-Carlos, but it wasn’t hard to figure out who she was talking about from the context. Still, since she obviously thought it was important to keep things on the Q.T., I let it pass, resolving that we’d have a conversation as soon as we got back to the Dream House and had a little privacy.

It turns out that Molly has been speaking with Jacob for the past few days about the possibility of learning more about who she used to be, before Darke and Grey used the Table to remake her. The problem is that by all accounts she used to be a bad person. Like, a really bad person. So there’s the possibility that she might revert to the Loyalist she used to be if she remembers that part of her life.

In a vacuum, there’d be no question about whether she has that right. But there could be dire consequences for a lot of people if we just blithely go ahead with this course of action. Jacob has spoken with Boss Lowjack about this situation, and he agrees that we can’t really deny Molly’s request… but he’s going to be taking precautions. And though I hate the necessity for things like that, I confess that I’m glad.

My solo number didn’t come up until the second set, so I made my way up to the balcony to speak with Yvette relatively early in the evening. She and Marguerite were flirting with each other pretty shamelessly… but I let it pass without comment. Either I trust my French lover or I don’t, and I intend to err on the side of trust until I have a good reason not to. Marguerite has proven many times that she’s not the same person she used to be.

She had the choreography pitch-perfect, and I’m sure people will be talking about the “dancing cats” in the background for days. Her singing was nothing to sneeze at either.                                     Thanks for commenting, everyone! As always its nice to know that people are interested. It's been a great story so far, and like the rest of you we players are looking forward to seeing where it goes.

                                    So, Lorelei's social dice pool is... complicated. I have a cheat sheet in my character folder to make things a little easier when it comes to rolling up her performances, and I'll try to replicate it here. Keep in mind that this is when Lori is going all-out and using all of her abilities to their utmost, and this is by far her best pool... other social rolls are significantly less impressive, though still potent. This also assumes that some of the contracts and abilities listed below are already active when the roll comes up, and thus their activation cost doesn't affect the cap on Glamour spent in a round.

                                    7 dice for Presence - Lori maxed this out when her Wyrd went up
                                    7 dice for Expression - Lori likewise maxed this skill out as soon as the cap was raised.
                                    1 die for Specialization (Singing, Dance)
                                    1 die for Adroitness - for her recently renewed Pledge to look after the Spring Court's interests in the Dream House
                                    2 dice for Striking Looks
                                    2 dice for Fame 2
                                    2 dice more for Striking Looks when Splendor of the Envoy's Protection (Vainglory 3) is invoked
                                    12 dice when Lorelei spends 6 Glamour on the roll... as a high-Wyrd Fairest she gets 2 dice for each point of Glamour spent on the roll, and she typically spends one less than the cap.
                                    7 dice or 7 successes - depending on whether or not she gets an exceptional success invoking Songs of Distant Arcadia (Vainglory 2).
                                    SUCCESSES dice - when Font of Inspiration (Verdant Spring 1) is invoked

                                    So right there we have either 41+SUCCESSES dice rolled, or 34+SUCCESSES dice rolled +7 automatic successes, depending on the activation roll on Songs of Distant Arcadia. There are also a few other wrinkles to consider. Since there are no official rules for high Wyrd Minstrel kith changelings, it was house-ruled that Lori gets 8-again on Expression rolls not used to activate a power. Her basic Minstrel kith power allows her to spend single point of Glamour to re-roll any failed dice on an Expression roll, essentially making it a rote action (this is why she typically spends one less than the cap to add dice to the initial roll). This means that she is by and large usually rolling around 40 dice, adding seven auto-successes, re-rolling all failed dice once, and re-rolling successes until they come up as failures. As this is a social roll, she also rolls 5 additional dice separately which are only applied to members of the Spring Court, thanks to her Mantle.

                                    So in the most recent journal, she resulted in 44 successes (48 with the Spring Court). She kinda/sorta surpassed that in the most recent session (journal pending), which resulted in 45 successes (46 with the Spring Court). I'll beat that 50 threshold sooner or later!

                                    Honestly, as the Baroness mentioned in a recent conversation we had, it's kind of ridiculous what powerful Changelings are capable of when it comes to Expression rolls. Even Marguerite and Calamity, who have each been performing professionally for less than a year and don't have nearly the number of advantages that Lori does, fairly routinely roll 15-20 successes. If you go by the general guidelines in the Chronicles of Darkness rules, that's the equivalent of a once-in-a-lifetime experience; something you'd witness once and then remember forever. We've essentially had to scale up our expectations when it comes to this particular set of skills in this game.

                                    So that's the breakdown. I'll be happy to answer any questions I can, if anyone is still reading through all the math and mechanics!

                                    EDIT: I realized that I oversimplified above. The high-Wyrd kith blessing the Baroness came up with for the Minstrel kith was actually to give them a more reasonable 9-again on Expression rolls. This becomes 8-again when compounded with the 9-again from Lori's Professional Training merit.

                                                        As for the physical act of rolling dice, well... I do it the old fashioned way. My Sunday night dice bag contains 40 ten-siders (what can I say? I play a lot of Chronicles of Darkness and I have GMed multiple 7th Sea campaigns, both 1st and 2nd edition. I have a lot of d10s). I pour 'em out on the table, put however many I don't need for the roll back in the bag - or alternately borrow one or two from another player - and start rolling. I separate the successes from the failures first, then re-roll the failed dice once (rote action) and separate again. Double-fails go back in the bag. I count what's left on the table, then re-roll (8-again), separate the new failures, count successes, and add to the previous total. Repeat as necessary. After I have a total, I add any automatic successes generated by Contracts and then roll five more dice using the same methods and add that for any Spring Courtiers in the audience. It can take a minute or two to tally up the results, as you can probably guess.
