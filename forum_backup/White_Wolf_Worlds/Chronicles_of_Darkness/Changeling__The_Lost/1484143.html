    Enmantled Tokens

    06-15-2022, 06:32 PM
    A knight of Spring kisses the rose he carries in his lapel and then tosses it to his beloved before riding out into the Hedge; wherever the flower is planted, love is sure to blossom. The Emperor of the West’s favored blade has been passed down from mentor to student for generations, and remembers the fighting style of every noble user. A minor member of the Court of Shady Deals has been using the old quarter-on-string trick for so long, that one day she finds her lucky coin can spoof card readers as well.

    For better or worse, the Mantle has a tendency to leave its mark in the wake of a changeling’s passing through the world. Flowers sprout in her footsteps; the lights flicker in any room he enters. The physical and mystical representation of her bargain with greater forces, it is a source of both protection and power. Some Lost take this a step beyond just the standard perks and Contracts, imprinting their Mantle onto their prized possessions as emblems of their station and the sacred oaths they’ve sworn.

    It takes more than just desire and a little Glamour to imprint a Mantle. The changeling must perform acts in line with the themes and values of her Court, furthering the Bargain with her patron. The greater her standing in her Court, the more powerful tokens she can create, but the more dedication is required to her task as well.

    Many changelings keep the enmantled tokens they create on their person at all times, serving as badges of their position within a Court or as reminders of the ways they’ve distanced themselves from their Durance. Others give them to junior courtiers, motleymates, or loved ones, as teaching instruments, symbols of trust, or as parts of mutual pacts cementing the bonds between Courts. This can be a risky proposition, however, for every concrete trace of the changeling in the world is another breadcrumb for her enemies to follow.

    Systems

    To enmantle a token, a changeling must perform acts that would gain her Glamor from her Mantle (such as a courtier of High Tide opening someone’s last Door, or a Summer Lost furthering a goal through wrath) while touching, holding, or wearing the object in question. She then must choose immediately to transfer the Glamour point to the would-be token instead of keeping it for herself. The token requires (desired dots x 3) points of Glamour in order for the Mantle to take hold, and a changeling can only create an enmantled token with a dot rating no higher than her own Mantle. Once the object is filled with the requisite amount of Glamour, the Lost spends a point of Willpower to seal its effects. The powers and drawbacks of an enmantled token always relate to its Court’s themes in some way, such as an Autumn dagger that lets the user bleed out a subject’s fears in exchange for their ability to act with empathy.

    While an enmantled token is active, the user is treated as having either one additional Mantle dot if a member of the same Court, or an additional dot of Court Goodwill otherwise. This dot does not last long enough to facilitate the purchase of Court Contracts, but otherwise functions normally in all other respects. This can give a user a technical rating in Mantle or Court Goodwill of 6 for numerical effects only, such as building dice pools. In addition, anyone holding the token can regain Glamour as though a member of the Court in question, which serves as a second Catch for the token as well; users must choose whether to regain Glamour or activate the token this way. Changelings who have lost their Mantle through breaking an oath to the token’s Court may not access any of the item’s abilities.

    Fae creatures holding an enmantled token may spend a point of Glamour to gain the object’s dot rating as a dice bonus to their next roll to track the creator or gather information about them, whether through mundane or supernatural means. Holding an enmantled token also counts as fulfilling the Loophole of the Mantle Mask Goblin Contract when emulating the token’s Court.

    An enmantled token purchased as a Merit costs one dot more than its rating.

    Sample Enmantled Token: Hard Drive of Babel (3)

    Created by a particularly erudite member of the Crystal Web who wanted to pass on the accumulated knowledge of the BriarNet to fellow members of his collective, this token takes the form of a black external hard drive covered in myriad silvery scratches that, when squinted at from just the right angle, one could convince oneself form some particularly ornate arcane sigil. Any computer the token is plugged into while activated takes on subtle signs of the Crystal Web Mantle, such as monitor pixelations that faintly resemble a goblin’s face.

    While plugged into a computer, a character can activate the token by opening its driver location and typing in a subject of inquiry into the search bar. This grants the user either a three-dot Library or a repository with a +3 bonus related to the chosen topic as the hard drive rapidly uploads all pertinent data onto the device. This data lasts until the end of the chapter or the token is activated again, whichever comes first. Any attempts to copy or transfer files provided this way cause the relevant information to delete itself automatically, which might result in a reduction in dice bonus if repeated enough times.

    Catch: The user can activate the token without Glamour if the computer to which the Hard Drive is connected has an active peer-to-peer file sharing program running (requiring also an internet connection) and which has provided a file (or piece of one, depending on the program) to another device within the past day. The original creator of the Hard Drive is immediately notified through the closest internet-connected device if the token is activated this way, and may spend a point of Glamour to gain access to the user’s computer for the rest of the scene. Although not a malevolent fae, he is prone to snooping.

    Drawback: One piece of information the Hard Drive provides is always misleading, poorly explained, or incomplete. One roll made when utilizing the Library or repository (chosen by the Storyteller) incurs a three-die penalty rather than bonus. If used as part of an Investigation, the Storyteller may give a Tainted Clue instead. Either way, if this bad intel causes the user to suffer a notable setback, the player takes a Beat.

    Lagniappe

    Merit: Patron's Shield (2)

    Prerequisite:     Mantle

    You can draw extra protection from your Court's Bargain. When targeted by a supernatural power, you may spend a point of Glamour as a reflexive action to substitute your Mantle rating for Wyrd for the purposes of Supernatural Tolerance. If the effect would force you to act in a way counter to your Court's themes, gain the 8-again quality on your defense roll. When this protection is invoked, your Mantle becomes briefly visible even to onlookers normally blinded by the Mask.

    Goblin Contract: Usurping the Crown

    A Hedge-jackdaw taught you the trick to snatching greatness from the brows of rulers. Let's hope your nimble fingers don't land you in too much hot water.

    Cost: 3 Glamour + 1 Willpower

    Dice Pool: Dexterity + Larceny + Wyrd vs Composure + Mantle

    Action: Contested

    Duration: Instant

    Success: With uncanny agility, you steal the Crown of a Court monarch within touching distance. You may don the Crown and gain its effects (though no Mantle powers or Court Contracts), or stash it away somewhere for a rainy day. By spending a point of Glamour, you may pass the Crown onto another individual. In the eyes of the Wyrd, any character wearing the Crown is, technically, the leader of the usurped Court, even if its members vehemently disagree.

    A character with the Goblin Queen Condition who gains a Crown this way may refashion it to suit her own tastes, both aesthetically and mechanically, using it as a legitimizing anchorpoint for her Hedgebound Court. Doing so means the Wyrd does not view her as the leader of the stolen Crown's Court.

    Any character with a stolen Crown suffers the Magpie's Misfortune Condition (see Oak, Ash, & Thorn, pg. 34) for as long as they bear it, with a number of jinxes equal to the rightful owner's Mantle rating. Passing the Crown on passes the Condition with it, though the Storyteller keeps any unspent jinxes to use against the previous holder.

    Exceptional Success: In addition to stealing the Crown, the new bearer can negate a jinx levied by Magpie's Misfortune by accepting a point of Goblin Debt.

    Failure: The Crown remains upon the monarch's brow. What an awkward situation you've landed yourself in!

    Dramatic Failure: The user's bumbled theft offends the very patron of the Court. The changeling gains the Notoriety Condition for all members of the Court, even those not present or living in freeholds on the other side of the world.

                  With the release of Kith & Kin, the rules for Regalia Manifestations have me revisiting this little notion. I want to make a few tweaks to the systems for Enmantled Tokens based on the ideas presented in the book, perhaps for some aesthetically pleasing symmetry or to draw some parallels or contrasts between Arcadia and Courts. The first mechanical change in mind is the cost of creation; considering everything Enmantled Tokens do, a point of Willpower to seal the effect is perhaps a little too cheap. It costs a Willpower dot to finalize a Regalia Manifestation, which would work well enough, but in light of the themes and mechanics (and name) of these tokens, I am thinking as well that perhaps the changeling must quite literally imbue the object with a dot of their Mantle. I'll need to figure what Mantle 0 means for a courtier, but part of me is pleased with this parallel (and on an abstract level, both a Willpower dot and a Merit dot cost 1 Experience, so the scales are more or less balanced there). What do others think?
