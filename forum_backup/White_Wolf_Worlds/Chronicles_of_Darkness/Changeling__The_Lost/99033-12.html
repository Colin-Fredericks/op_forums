                Things are moving fast. Something like the murder of Mr. Grey won’t stay secret for long, and our PCs find themselves engaged in argument.

                Session XXVIII

                Gawain’s Memories

                Afternoon, June 17th

                The hope present in the midst of a long vigil often becomes laughable in the face of the cruel Lady Fate. Yet, for all that it rankles, I must acknowledge that the man has a point. Two, as it happens. Not only is the safety of the Freehold entire paramount, but Lowjack is in a position to make demands. If he removes the Table as promised, and secures the motley’s safety with a strong web of pledges, I must be satisfied. At least until such a time as the Freehold is determined to be safe without Darke’s presence.

                I do wonder if such an affront to justice would sit as easily were I not benumbed by rage at my own lady. On whether or not the man Gray deserved to die I will concede that he may very well have not been innocent. If he willingly carried out a dishonorable order given by a dishonorable liege, or even did so to save his own skin, he certainly was not, and deserved the same fate as our villain. The truth of the matter is, if not lost, inconsequential at this point.

                More pressing is the reckless abandon she showed in this. Her first inclination is thoughtless amoral violence. Fine. I do not fault her on that, no matter what she believes. But that is why you have a priest, and a wisewoman, and a Master of the Guard and a spymaster. Because one’s first inclination is not the only option. Perhaps we would have chosen that course. But we did not consider any others.

                She says that she wished to protect us. Does she think that the sins of one would never rest on the others? Does she truly believe any of us feel the better for not having been consulted? That if she were to be brought before a judge for her crime that I would allow them to touch her without destroying me first? Does she take me for a child, all unknowing of what makes up the world? Gah. At least there is no doubt she does not take me for a partner.


                Early morning June 18th

                Fuck. Fuck fuck fuck fuckity fuck fuck. Jesus, where the hell have I been for the past day and a half? Who have I been? That one’s easier. Lord Bertilak is who. Recently I keep getting flashes, memories of day-to-day life as him. It’s starting to seem more real than before, that life where I was the lord of a castle, with a wife and a witch and a duty and a story. Way more real than being a kid, and having a family, listening to music and watching movies. As for where, unfortunately, I’m pretty sure where I’ve been is right here. For once, I wish I’d been dreaming. I wish this was all a nightmare.

                I hurt Calamity. I... it wasn’t malicious. But I knew what I was saying when I sat where she couldn’t even be nearby for movie night. And sure, she hurt me, but that’s not the way it works, you know? Not the way it should work. I guess it’s justice but... fuck. She drank almost as much as that night with the truth and dare. The way she sighed and leaned against me when I picked her up and took her to her room was heartbreaking. She fell asleep quickly. I could stay calm and comforting until then at least.

                I love her. I can’t stay really mad at her for long. But Lord Bertilak was right about one thing. Nothing about what happened is the way you treat a partner, someone you respect and trust, you know? I can accept that she considers herself a monster, that that is a path she’s decided to walk. I can accept that, if we’d had that discussion, that she would have been arguing to kill someone we didn’t know for certain was guilty, because of what he might do in the future. I could even respect where she was coming from. But eventually, she will have to accept that I am a man, not a fucking kid to be kept away from the scary things. That I get to make decisions about the path I walk, and she doesn’t get to make them for me, you know?

                I didn’t choose the Dawn out of naiveté, dammit. I choose it consciously and with intent. She can’t take that away from me, any more than I can take the monster away from her. I just wish she’d stop trying, you know?


                Afternoon, June 18th

                I think things are better? I don’t know how well I communicated things to Calamity when she woke up. She seemed appreciative of breakfast though, and didn’t object when I followed her up for a shower. So I guess things are mostly back to normal at least. I’m kinda foggy though cause I didn’t sleep. And didn’t really feel like asking Lori for a wakeup zolt again, you know? I might need to before tonight though. See, the meeting to dethrone Darke is tonight, at 8. So we need to be ready for problems there, either from Darke himself, or the Wyrd being a jerk, or maybe even from little grey aliens.

                I guess I Lord Bertilak didn’t explain very well. Lowjack was pretty sure this whole thing was orchestrated by the Mothership - sending the Table down as bait to get Darke eliminated, and Andromeda to distract Joan. Not as a loyalist - Joan’s got a keener sense than that. But if Lowjack is right, Andromeda is more useful to the Mothership by not being loyal. And those two, Darke and Joan (and Percival, but he’s a jackass), are the first line of defense of the Freehold, the one that stops things *before* people get hurt. So his condition for agreeing to become the Winter Monarch was that Darke would be required to stick around and help with the defense, until we could build up better ones. Not... ideal, but I trust his judgement. Perhaps Darke will even see what he’s done wrong for what it is, and not just some ‘difficult’ and ‘well-intentioned’ decision justifiable in the name of the Freehold. Perhaps.

                But anyway, that means we need to be prepared for the Mothership to capitalize on what chaos there turns out to be. Unless that happens though, Lowjack would prefer we stay out of it. So we’re just kinda kicking around, you know? Well, Lori and Jacob went off to check on Tamara, cause she went home with the Handmaidens and then called in sick, so that’s terrifying, but they haven’t called for backup yet. Hopefully she’s ok.


                Lorelei’s Diary

                What separates us from other people and makes us different? What is it about human beings that can cause us to see the same set of circumstances in such completely dissimilar ways? How can two people who both essentially want the same thing choose to go about achieving it using completely antithetical methods? What is it that separates me from someone like D? Or Calamity? Or Annabelle?

                I had crawled back into bed to cuddle with the Darklings for a while, and had started to drift off again when Marguerite stirred. She got dressed and made for the door, clearly intending to head downstairs for breakfast. Then, as she was reaching for the doorknob, she turned to look at the three of us. “I want you to know, I love you all,” she said sharply. Then she turned and fled the room.

                D and I looked blankly at each other for a minute while Julia stretched like a cat. Then we both sort of shrugged and started to get dressed.

                We didn’t mention anything about it at breakfast, and D had to go to work right after she finished eating. After she left, breakfast-table conversation soon turned to Mr. Grey. Both Gawain and I had come to the conclusion that it would be best if we took him into our protection, both to keep him from telling Master Darke about our involvement in this situation, and so that we could produce him as a witness if the other monarchs had any questions about the Table and how it was used.

                I don’t know why she didn’t tell us then. I don’t know why she let us drive all the way out to Venice Beach without telling us what we would find. The police lines were a shock, and the sight of the shattered second-story window was another. I started to freak out, assuming that Master Darke had already heard about what Mr. Grey had told us, and had begun his retaliation.

                Calamity told Gawain to pull into an alley a few blocks away. When we got there, she started talking. I think a part of me already knew what she was going to say. She killed Mr. Grey. She told us that it was to protect the motley; that she did it to keep him from telling Master Darke about our investigation. I wish I could believe that; I really do. And I think that in part she honestly believes it, but the truth runs deeper. It wasn’t her reason, it was her excuse.

                Gawain drove us back to Santa Monica for our meeting with Lowjack. There was a lot of discussion back and forth on the subject of Mr. Grey’s murder. Some of the motley thought it was the right move; a sort of pre-emptive strike to prevent trouble down the line. Others thought that the matter should have been discussed among the motley before action was taken. Jacob mostly kept silent on the subject, which I respect… Mr. Grey was a member of his Court, after all.

                After a while I couldn’t take it anymore, and I confess I sort of quietly exploded. A man is dead. Does it matter if he was a friend or an enemy? How can we calmly sit around and discuss the facts of his murder as though the loss of one of our own means nothing? How can it ever be right to dole out the ultimate punishment for a crime someone hasn’t even committed yet, and might never commit?

                Needless to say, it was a quiet, tense trip to Lowjack’s, and we didn’t present the most united front when we spoke to the once-and-future-king. What he told us when we explained the situation to him didn’t make us any happier, either.

                What Tamara told us after the show Sunday night was true: Lowjack never wanted the monarchy, and he still doesn’t want it. He agreed that Darke has gone too far, and that something needs to be done to prevent the continued misuse of the Table… but he put a new spin on it that I don’t think any of us had considered. He described the roles of the Summer and Winter Courts in the Freehold, using a complex metaphor about antibodies and white blood cells. Essentially, the Winter Court’s role is to quietly protect the Freehold from Gentry incursions during times of peace, while the Summer Court takes up the sword to beat back more obvious invasions.

                Every Court has their spies, but Master Darke is the spymaster for the entire Freehold. And he’s very good at it. Lowjack believes that the Mothership might have allowed the Table to fall into Darke’s possession, knowing that the rest of the Freehold would eventually find out about his misuse of its powers, and would not stand for it. Regardless of our personal feelings about the man’s actions, Darke’s removal will weaken the Freehold’s defenses significantly. Add to that the fact that this is coming when Joan is distracted from her duties, and you get a perfect time for the Gentry to strike.

                The long and the short of it is that Lowjack is willing to take over as the King of Winter… but only on the condition that Master Darke be allowed to remain in place as spymaster. He won’t be punished beyond removing him from the throne, and possible banishment somewhere down the line, when a suitable replacement can be found. Nobody much liked that solution, but after a brief discussion, we all agreed that it was the only way.

                We told Lowjack that we would agree to his terms, and asked him to contact Queen Charlotte to let her know. He also said something rather strange: he told us not to judge Darke too harshly, because his durance was something like living in Sin City.

                “Las Vegas?” I asked, confused.

                The others explained to me that Sin City in this case was a pop culture reference, probably to a movie based on a comic book which was a sort of concentrated look at the noir genre. We stopped at a video store on the way home and picked up a copy. What can I say? It was right next to the liquor store, and I felt the need for a fifth of bourbon in the worst way.

                So while the monarchs met to discuss deposing Master Darke and the Monday night show proceeded downstairs, we of the motley drank ourselves stupid and held an extended movie night. Things started out tense, but by the time the second flick was over we were starting to relax again (I blame the alcohol). Gawain sat by himself for the entire movie marathon, but he carried her off to the room they share at the end of the night when he saw how miserable his isolation was making Calamity.

                I was pretty stony toward Calamity at first as well, I admit. She refuses to apologize, or even to admit that she might have been wrong to take out Mr. Grey without even consulting the rest of us. But she looked so lost and alone sitting there by herself drinking alone… I couldn’t let it stand. I called her aside after the first movie and spoke to her softly.

                “I’m still mad,” I said to her seriously. “In fact, I’m bordering on furious right now, and that’s not likely to change any time soon.” She nodded and lowered her eyes, and I leaned in and carefully kissed her forehead. “But I still like you, I still love you, and I still respect you, Calamity. I don’t want there to be any mistake about that, okay?”

                I think I saw a little of her old spark return to her eyes at that. “Thanks, Lori,” she said simply, and then we went back to start the second film.

                It was an eclectic night of movie-watching to say the least. We started out with the two films which had been scheduled, and then moved on to Sin City and another film Marguerite recommended.

                The first film was called Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves. It was fun; nothing to write home about, but a fairly good mindless action flick. It had its share of problems, sure… some of the acting was pretty wooden, and the leading man seemed to lose his British accent about fifteen minutes in (which was probably a good choice all in all… it wasn’t very convincing to begin with). The villains were good, though… the Sheriff of Nottingham was delightfully wicked and crazy, and the man playing Guy of Gisborne had this sexy gravelly voice that set my loins a-quiver, if you know what I mean.

                Everyone gave me one of those Oh-Lorelei-You’re-So-Out-Of-Touch looks when I asked why the filmmakers had felt it necessary to include the guy that played Dracula in that Electric Company show Brad and Cindy Mullendorf used to watch when I was sitting for them as a condemned Arab, though.

                D got back from work near the end of that first film, and that made everything a little bit better (well, I admit the bourbon helped too). She took in the fact that we were all drinking and watching movies earlier than usual, and noted the seating arrangement, and raised an eyebrow at me in question. I pulled her into my room and explained about Calamity, Mr. Grey, Lowjack, and Master Darke. She was suitably sympathetic, though I think on some level she might have agreed with Calamity about the dangers posed by Mr. Grey. I didn’t pursue the subject.

                Before we rejoined the motley, I brought up the other thing that had been on my mind. “Hey, D,” I began, “about what Marguerite said this morning…”

                She grinned. “I know, crazy right?” she asked. “I never would have expected her to just come out and say it so baldly…”

                “I’ve been thinking a lot about our relationship with those two lately,” I admitted, “and… I mean… they’re obviously more than just friends to us. I was thinking about… reciprocating.”

                D cocked an eyebrow, still grinning.

                I sighed and pulled her close. “You’ll always be first in my heart, D,” I said earnestly. “Don’t ever doubt that. But I can’t deny that I feel something for Marguerite and Julia, too. They’re more than friends… more than just casual lovers, even.”

                D laughed softly in my ear, and it was musical. “You don’t have to explain, Lori,” she assured me. “There’s more than one way to love someone. And just because you and I have fallen in love, that doesn’t mean we can’t feel something for other people, too.” She held me tightly and whispered into my ear. “Hell, I feel the same way… though I’m much too macho to ever admit it.”

                Then came the kissing.

                A few minutes later, we rejoined the group. I sprawled out on one of the sofas with my head in D’s lap as she not-so-subtly groped me, played idly with my hair, and fed me the occasional spoonful of melty ice cream. The second movie was actually a parody of the first, made by the incomparable comic mind of Mel Brooks. Robin Hood: Men In Tights did not approach the hilarious majesty of Young Frankenstein (my personal favorite of his films), but it had more than a few laugh-worthy sequences, and watching it immediately after Prince of Thieves made it a little more topical.

                Plus it had Cary Elwes (Wesley from The Princess Bride) as Robin Hood, and that can’t be bad.

                Next up was Sin City. It was a mostly black-and-white flick with splashes of color here and there to call attention to important plot or thematic elements, sort of like Schindler’s List. But unlike that holocaust drama, in Sin City there were no shades of grey. The shadows were pitch black, and the lights were white as snow. It’s almost ironic, as the characters and stories were all about moral ambiguities. Marv is an ugly man in almost every imaginable way, but there is something redeeming and pure in his quest to avenge the only person who ever saw the good in him. Dwight is a handsome rogue with a checkered past who is willing to do the most unthinkable things to protect the people he loves (even as he betrays them in little ways). And Hartigan – maybe the only truly good person in all of Sin City] – is forced to betray his principles and resort to acts of unspeakable barbarism to save his beloved Nancy.

                It was a shocking film, beautiful in its brutality. Parts of it were painful to watch. Decapitation, cannibalism, sado-masochism… the list of atrocities goes on and on. When I was growing up, the idea that a popular film would include not one but two on-screen castration scenes was unthinkable. If that’s what Master Darke’s durance was like, then maybe his choices regarding the Table are a little more understandable. Not forgivable, mind you… but understandable.

                The final film of the evening (well… it carried us well into the wee hours of the morning, actually) was called Reservoir Dogs, and Marguerite insisted that we watch it for some reason. It’s a gritty crime film; the story of a heist gone wrong… although the heist itself is not shown. The audience only sees the aftermath of that disastrous escapade as the criminals turn on each other in savage ways. I think Marguerite was trying to tell us something in her own way. I confess I wasn’t in the mood for moral lessons about solidarity in the wake of Calamity’s earlier display of “initiative.”

                I was, however, in the mood for debauched bedroom shenanigans once the movie marathon was over. D and I allowed ourselves to be led to Marguerite’s room, and I once again found myself surrounded by my three lovers; the token Fairest in the middle of a writhing mass of Darkling flesh. Before things became too prurient, however, I pulled Marguerite up so that we were face to face.

                “D and I were talking about what you said this morning, ma cheri,” I said softly. I’m not sure, but I think she actually lowered her eyes a bit when I said that, as if she didn’t want to hear what I had to say. I caressed her face. “I want you to know that I feel the same way,” I said with as much warmth as I could muster, “I love you both, and I’m so glad we’ve all found each other.” I stroked Julia’s hair; for once she didn’t seem to have anything to say to that.

                A minute later, to prevent things from getting too mushy, I added “D feels the same way, but she’s much too macho to ever admit it.” My soulmate chuckled, cuffed me a good one in the shoulder, and then decided to shut me up in a more pleasant way.

                The rest of the evening is kind of a blur, I admit.

                Lowjack called us bright and early the next day… well, bright and early for us, at least. Noonish. He let us know that the monarchs had all agreed to our plan, and they will be taking steps to replace Master Darke today. He also asked for a personal favor… he wanted me to check on Tamara Chance for him, as she left the bar early last night in the company of the Warlock’s Handmaidens, and had called in sick this morning. Of course I agreed to look in on her, and Jacob and I dropped by her apartment less than an hour later.

                She seemed to be all right, if a bit tired and… “drained,” I suppose, though that adjective seems a bit on the nose. I used my Contracts to restore a little of her vitality, and she felt much better immediately. We tried to warn her against dallying with the Handmaidens again, but I don’t think she took us seriously. I don’t judge… I know the thrill of loving a Leechfinger myself (though I know D would never drain me in the heat of passion), but I think we’d better keep an eye on Tamara to make sure she doesn’t end up getting hurt.


                So I’ve had a few things to distract me and keep me from thinking about what Calamity did the other day. Every time I think about her treating Mr. Grey’s life so casually it makes me so angry… it’s hard to wrap my brain around the fact that she killed a man – snuffed out his life like a candle – just because he might turn out to be a threat at some point in the future.

                Why would she do such a thing?

                And why does it remind me of Annabelle, of all people?

                Lorelei Grace

                    Calamity’s Little Black Book

                    Tuesday June 17th

                    I have told my motley many times that I am a monster, so why is that when I act like one they're all so fucking surprised? Maybe I shouldn't have told them, but I just couldn't lie to them directly yesterday. I knew it was going to go badly the moment everyone decided that we should take Grey with us to see Lowjack. Fuck it, maybe it would have been better to leave him alive but then none of the bastards would have gotten their just desserts.

                    Oh yeah, the icing on this cake is that Darke is off limits. No one liked that, not even Lori. I do believe that the oaths Lowjack intends to bind him with will be ironclad but I don't trust any oath to keep Darke from finding a way to get back at us. I guess it is fucking fair play since as far as I'm concerned this deal is just a gorram delay on putting that motherfucking sonofabitch down like the Gentry he's trying to become. So yeah, we'll all play nice till the crisis is past and this city doesn't need Darke's toxic ass anymore. But in the end, not a one of us will be well and truly safe while that sick bastard's breathing. And I'll be damned (again) before I take that shit laying down. Lowjack and his damned logical thinking can go fuck themselves if he expects anything different.

                    Fuck I'll be glad when Jacob gets back so we can spar. I'm not dumb enough to think that it's not an opportunity for him to speak to me discreetly but fuck it I need a fight and if he was going to yell at me he might as well have joined in when everyone else was. And by everyone I mean Lori and Gawain. Insincere Marguerite did try to kinda back me up. I mean, she didn't say I was right (and given how painfully clear Gawain and Lori had both made their positions I can't say I blame her.) But she did argue that I did what I thought was right and that I deserved at least a little mercy for that. And Lori did make a point of telling me that she still cared about me, which was part of why she was so mad at me for doing it.

                    The worst was Gawain. He was furious. He shouted and then just drove in silence with a death-grip on the wheel. The rest of the day I could feel the anger coming off of him in waves and every time he had to look at me he was still enraged. Fuck, it would have hurt less if he'd just hauled off and laid into me. I'm good at dealing with that kind of fucking pain. And the fact that I fucking deserved it didn't fucking help. I fucking knew they'd never agree to killing Grey and I knew when I did it they'd be fucking pissed. Still, doesn't matter if you're the one that stabbed yourself or not, it still hurts just as much.

                    So yeah, the mood when we showed up at Lowjack's was absolutely delightful. And then he went and laid out the Mothership's plan and how we'd all been played like a damned fiddle. Seems so obvious once he explained it all. Andromeda was perfect Joan bait and while I have had it with folks whining about it I can't deny that the woman is sorely distracted from keeping an eye out for loyalists. And Darke couldn't fucking resist having the Mothership's Thrice-damned Table right there. You know, even if murdering Darke is exactly what the Mothership wants, for once I don't give a rainbow colored crap about it. Which is why this plan is so absolutely fucktasticly perfect.

                    Like I said, no one liked it. Not one little bit. But we had fuck all for choices.
