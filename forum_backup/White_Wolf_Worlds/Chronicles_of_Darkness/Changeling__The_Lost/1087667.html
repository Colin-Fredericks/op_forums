    Pledges, Consequence, and You!

    05-04-2017, 10:25 AM
    Howdy y'all! So, I've been trying to get myself back into the RP swing of things as of late. Changeling is by far my favorite setting. In part because it gives the player some explicit reigns for steering their own story (the brightest, I think, being the +1 - +3 dice that can be added to Dream Combat just for being really entertaining about it xD)

    Still, I think that pledges are always tricky because it becomes a mechanical benefit with only as much consequence as an ST wants to make about it. I'm curious how other people handle that. I think about them as a way of fielding what you'd like to see happen with the story. 1e's Freehold Blessings did this pretty well, I think. Subtlety means you should be rolling to conceal your nature constantly in the setting. Fortitude is seeing a lot of terrible things -- Fertility for making a lot of crazy things happen with Glamour.

    But some of the issues of actual consequence seems, to me, particularly true with Freehold pledges. The Freehold represents the community the players' characters are a part of. Which means that the only way to bring in the interesting elements of a consequence is from an ST forcing it or needing the player to actively muck something up that is otherwise a right of entry into any story that includes characters being part of one.

    The 2e changes seem to be trying to address this with the idea of Stories and Approaches, particularly with the Huntsmen as central antagonist, but it still doesn't bring up much of what the player character is risking by making a bunch of long term pledges that never really put them at risk beyond a flavorful (I get more punchies because I make shoes for this one guy down the block, you all saw it!) hook. I like the idea of Sealing as a short term way of engaging with that but even it mentions the issue of "Let's all pledge to get each other burgers and get some mechanical bonus for it" business

    Maybe it's a topic that always boils down to ST discretion but I thought it would make for a neat conversation, hear about how other people have handled pledges, things that did or didn't work, just -- ya know, chatter I suppose ^-^

Particularly when delving into the possibilities of rotating ST duties addressed in the Chronicles core.

Between characters, unless they're part of a broad plot, they seem to only have a RP risk. You either choose to break it or don't.

          I guess a kind of extended action roll could be a way to address it, requiring a Changeling to meet a certain threshold with side pledges or else be hit by the failure to uphold but again, I'm just kind of curious about other people's thoughts and how they've handled it/seen it handled.

                          1e had some serious mechanical issues that were very easy to abuse, and I ended up homebrewing new ones. (Basically I removed duration and made boon equal to the lesser of task or sanction)

                          In terms of RP I did have one character who used pledges to great effect. Whenever he needed to persuade someone to trust him he would respond with "I swear on my True Name to tell you no lies this day" which tended to intimidate people into listening to whatever he had to say. For most people the inability to tell a lie paired with the devastating penalties for violating a True Name Oath would be a hugely uncomfortable and frightening lose lose situation with no real upside, but he was an elemental with no dots in subterfuge. It made everybody uncomfortable that he would voluntarily self inflict that kind of handicap just to make a point, but giving up a skill he didn't have in the first place was well worth the benefit of being unquestionably trusted at his word.

                                  Honesty Pledges are also extremely effective in an interrogation context. Terrifying somebody into talking doesn't normally work because they just tell you what they think you want to hear, but if you intimidate them into agreeing to a Pledge to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth you can ensure the information is accurate to the best of their knowledge.

                                  (We did this to my Fetch to find out to what extent he was working with a hobgoblin slaver. The best part was that the only consequence was a minor pishogue. "Nothing much happens if you lie, but we'll know.")
