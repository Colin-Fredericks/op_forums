    Rural freeholds

    05-08-2014, 10:55 AM
    My current group has requested a freehold based on our local area for our next game. The only problem is, we're in the middle of nowhere, scattered across four hamlets and a village on the Cumbrian coast not far from the Scottish border. Does anyone have any ideas of how to design a freehold in this sort of setting?

      I think it would be an awesome idea.

      I can quite clearly envision a tight-knit freehold which took up a farm (an maybe some nice ancient rural custom, as sacrificing oxes or whatever to assure a good crop) and lives together in the same farm - house. Some of them may decide to live directly in the forest, or in some more far-away little house in the woods.

      - The changelings of the freehold come from a quite vast area: there should be at least a couple of freehold members (winter and spring, i would say) which scout around newspapers and strange events in order to save and recruit just escaped changeling.

      - The freehold is isolated: probably, there will be a huge amount of internal politic and bickering amongst the changeling.

      And what happens when a spring member decides to bring a van full of human tourist in order to bring "fresh air"?


      - The freehold may be is "a little bit too strange": there may be reporter which are trying to have a scoop on "Genetically modified organism" (goblin fruits?) which are grown in the land, or industrial spies which would greatly pay in order to have the "secret tecnologies" which the freehold don't has. On the other hand, neighboring farmers may be not so happy to have such "bizarre" neighbors.


      - There is a lot of unexplored real world terrain nearby. Make it memorable. And, of course, you should put there at least some trod. And children. And wolves.

          My longest running game took place in an rural freehold located in the farmlands of Upstate New York. The freehold's area of influence covered the expanse of an entire county, with a population that numbered around 25. Most changelings lived relatively off the grid, off in abandoned farms in the hills, old factories and in small camps out in the woods. Hobgoblins played a bigger role in the freehold and the main antagonists was a collective of fetches and loyalists that called themselves "The Grange."

          You have to remember that rural locales don't have the luxury of anonymity that more urban areas provide. There are fewer people, and as such, the citizenry will take note of strangers and their odd behaviors. A secret society of magical misfits would undoubtedly get the attention of superstitious country folk, especially in the World of Darkness, where the darkness and paranoia of the places outside of cities creates an almost palpable aura of fear.

          Also consider: The old ways and superstitions of the Old World may still exist out in East Bumblefuck, Wherever. I can see motleys of changelings becoming the little gods and the "good folk" of folklore in these places, taking guardianship of entire towns and reinforcing the old rituals. Still, this sort of setup still requires the changelings to remain hidden ... faith and magical thinking has a hard time existing in the presence of proof and taking up the traditional roles of ancient fairies can't be very good for one's Clarity.

          Another thing to consider is how the Hedge interacts with these less "civilized" places. In Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell, the English countryside is dotted with mysterious fairy roads and old goblin manors whose denizens occasionally abscond with well mannered Englishmen. Perhaps, the "country" has more active gates into the Hedge, and thus more fairy activity in its wild places. The relative safety of the city is absent.

                                      Well, for what it's worth, I would think back in history. In my world there are three factors that help make a good location for a freehold or court: access to the Hedge (which tend to be places of great natural beauty), a group of mortals for glamour, and an investment of hollow-type magic by the Changeling. The settlement can continue if at least two out of the three continue to give the place power. A freehold might be somewhere that had a decent mortal population for hundreds of years, but happens to have not been too large in the last fifty or hundred years. It's hard for the group to leave because: Changelings still tend to cross over from the Fae lands here, so someone needs to pick up the pieces or its a prime spot for seasonal magic power (perhaps the rituals of seasonal magics need to be at ley line crossings). Perhaps they guard a sleeping Changeling Queen.

                                      Maybe the group is purposely on the Fringe-- because they have been cast out from the city-based court, so they formed their own freehold. Maybe they hang out here because they are doing some kind of not-so- acceptable business? Maybe they smuggle goblin fruit, do some kind of mortal money/paperwork forgery, or maybe they make tokens that they sell to goblins? Okay, not the best examples, but any actions that they court might want to be able to deny-- how about they "disappear" city mortals that get too much in the way?

                                      I'm wondering if in an area with less population, the Changelings will harvest glamour through dreams as much as possible? It would solve the problem of provoking the limited local population too much by trying to make those high feelings manifest. That could lead to a campaign with more emphasis on dreams and the connection between the Hedge and Dream.
