    I mean, back in 1e I was toying with the idea of offering an incentive for kithlessness by tying frailties to kiths, but "we're changing the arrangement of fixed-inborn and mutable-inborn splat types around" doesn't seem likely to mean "the thing that is [now] a refinement of [what is now] the core concept of the character's supernatural state is going to be the thing that houses any drawback that that state may possess."

                    Originally posted by Omegaphallic View Post
                    You could do that, that way, but it's not the angle David plans to go with as he wants to "Front Load" Kiths as he put it. Your free to house rule it so that seeming comes from you kidnapping.
                    Well, again, so far as the themes are concerned, it seems backwards to what the stated goals are.

                    If the point is to focus more upon how Changeling took their life back, and the focus is now more upon kiths than seemings, why isn't it the kith, the thing that actively empowers what you DO as a Changeling, the one that is the character's own deliberate choice?

                    Seemings deal more with the tone of the magic that pervades you, but not your overall character direction, especially if they are receding in importance. Hence, making seemings the "Abuse Victim" part of the character, which is a legacy of violence that taints your soul and every act or the drip of the inky darkness or the feral mindset of the Keeper, while kiths, the bigger active choice, the specialization, the thing that makes your character unique and gives them that little power edge is the "Abuse Survivor" part. I mean, players build their character's talents around their kith, story-wise. Who picks a kith as a flowerling, and then focuses upon mental skills to the exclusion of social skills? It contradicts the story that characters are reclaiming their own lives if they always play to the life that their Keepers forced upon them, but just pretend it was all their own idea, now. (Because, after all, it IS all the player's idea...)

                    Kiths-as-past also means that the things done against your will loom larger as a part of your character's unique flavor, and come in infinite varieties, while your character's active choice is restricted to just 6 choices you are allowed to make. (Or 5 if there's a consolidation...) If you want to play up the character determination to reclaim their lives part, isn't this backwards? Wouldn't making the "enh, there's only a half dozen different types of you, anyway" thing be more in line with the callous indifference of the Keepers?

                    I mean, yeah, I get that it's something that's been thrown down to us from on high, but I sort of suspect this wasn't just some utterly random, arbitrary decision.
