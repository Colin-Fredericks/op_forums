    Confused about Approaches

    03-21-2016, 11:14 PM
    I really like the idea of Approaches, but I have some questions about the specific workings. True Fae apparently have to abide by all Approaches as if they were bans/banes, which is one of the major hurdles preventing them from destroying freeholds directly, but how far does this extend and how restrictive can an Approach get? Can a character with an unfulfilled Approach force a Fae to back off by engaging with it? For example, if I have two characters, one of whom may only be taken during the day, and one of whom may only be taken at night, if they always stick together does the one who currently cannot be targeted get to attack any Fae coming for the other, with impunity?

    I know this is why they tend to send Huntsmen instead but it just feels weird if you could have situations where a Fae is functionally unable to act against certain Changelings during a confrontation. Am I not understanding the concept right?

      Think of the freehold and courts as a castle. The castle walls are the freehold's Bulwark but every castle needs to have gates for people to get in and out, which leaves the castle vulnerable. These paths in and out are the Approaches. I think there's some flexibility and ST judgement call on how restrictive an Approach can be but courts have several so even if one's extremely restrictive another may not be. In your example with the characters who can't be taken in the day or at night then I think they'd run into a couple different problems. First, if the Fae was coming specifically after the one who can't be taken at Night (Person A), then it doesn't matter if their ally (Person B) can't be taken during the day. The primary target (A) can be attacked during the day and B is just collateral damage. I'm sure a well motivated True Fae could keep person B in one place for a few hours until the sun sets to take them back to Arcadia properly. Secondly, if the dynamic duo decided to go on the offensive with the idea that the Approaches would protect them, they'd run into the problem of them being primarily defensive constructs of their story and by my personal call, they'd concede that protection because they initiated the conflict.

                          It can be, I suppose. In Los Angeles, we had the Freehold Approach be that a True Fae or Huntsmen basically had to make known everything they had at their disposal. I dunno how much I would rely on that mentality for Approaches, though, if I was writing a section in a supplement.

                          For me, Approaches are a crossroads-on the one hand, they should be things that someone who's in the court long enough is actually inclined to have as a weakness, a natural extension. On the other hand, the sheer fact that you know about it means you can plan it out and use it to your advantage.

                          One thing worth keeping in mind is that Approaches aren't walls-I know, there it is again, but it matters here, because Approaches literally are a reference to castle design. Approaches are design details where in you basically highly encourage an enemy to come a certain way, because everywhere else is theoretically too hard to pull off. By it's nature, it should, at the least, actually look weak. Often times, it actually is the weakest part, of a castle or, in this case, a changeling. But it's also the place where perception is highest. The Approaches aren't about secret failings, defects a character isn't aware of. It's the one they know they have, the one they try to shore up with, or have their friends ready to deal with. It's a weak, but controlled, 'environment'.

                          if we were going to argue about forcing a Huntsman going after strengths, Belle's approach would be pitting against her kindness, her empathy, her patience. The intelligence thing is a Disney thing(Not say that she wasn't educated, but it wasn't a hallmark either).

                          The Riding Hood one of shouting distance of people is actually not bad as a strength argument.

                          For me, though, the 'weakest but most watched' is probably my main ruling metric.

                          EDIT: Actually, a not indecent approach to Approaches would be to ensure anyone in a court had at least two Approaches, one for 'strength', one for 'weakness'.

                                  Approaches are, again, functions of the Wyrd. The specifics haven't been figured out(it's supplement material), so I don't know if they can be crafted or not in game. I wrote Paris on the assumption that a certain amount of craftmanship is needed for Freehold building, with the Rose Courts being refined over the decades.

                                  If they are crafted, they're probably still limited by the dictates of the Wyrd, and when your metaphor is built around the "weakest but most watched" part of a castle, you can expect that even the most unfair version of that is not as unfair as you'd probably want. Although, again, you see the enemies coming when they go for an Approach.

                                  From a meta perspective, consider that Approaches are also a tool to encourage fairytale behavior in a way that makes it so that players don't have to rely on Frailties, triggers, and their own inclination to act. Unlike the latter, it is something a player has to acknowledge and incorporate into their behavior, particularly as they rise in the Courts. Unlike the former, though, it's a behaviorial thing they can play with and subvert, deconstruct, reconstruct, invert, exaggerate, and so on to their hearts content.

                                  The Black Rose, as an example, have the Approaches of being caught unprepared for a Huntsman or True Fae unprepared and having to rely on someone else for protection. The ideal for the Arcadians is to catch them in a moment of weakness, and if they escape, to hamstring their ego and vicious nature. THe Black Rose doesn't like dwelling on their Approaches, but they consider it a reasonable loss if the Huntsmen gets two yearning only to find himself caught in a death box of Courtiers with long pointy stick and flamethrowers. So, occasionally, the Black Rose will convince someone(a particularly weak and foolish person in the court by their standards, most of the time) to actually make themselves open for the Huntsmen to Approach. Help the courtier see how much more they need to be, get rid of an enemy for a while, it's a win win for them.

                                  While the Black ROse is probably one of the more deviant from my standards, their Approaches are literally a classic way to use them-the Huntsmen takes advantage of them to get Yearning, only to end up skewered on the Blackguards pikes. And it's so easy for both the Black Rose to set up and to convince the Huntsmen it's worth going after.

                                          Hmm... Arc, I like your take on approaches, but they don't seem consistent with the way they're described in the Court Creation section (or at least, in the version that has been previewed). It might be worth considering some revision there, if it's not too late. For reference:
                                          The courts tell a specific part of the freehold's story and in doing so they create Approaches, a deliberate opening in their defenses to force the huntsmen to face them on their own terms. An Approach is the court's greatest strength and the primary means that they use to fight back against the Fae. When abiding by an Approach in their interactions with courtiers, the Huntsmen gain a point of Yearning (see pg. XX) For example, an Autumn courtier in the seasonal courts fights back by embracing the occult and mastering contracts and the oneiros. Abiding Autumn's Approach, a Huntsman is not likely to challenge her to a duel with pistols or ambush her in the dark and drag her off by force. Instead, the Huntsman may torment her in her dreams before challenging her to a mystical duel.

                                          It definitely reads like the intention is to force Huntsmen to attack where the Court is strongest. Dream combat and mystical duels are certainly not a weak point for the Autumn Court. And I'm not sold on the wall/door metaphor. The invading army attacks the door because the castle walls are too strong to pass. The defending army defends the door because they know it's the only place the invaders will be able to get in. But Huntsmen take Approaches to get Yearning. If they're good on Yearning (which, granted, they shouldn't generally be) there's no actual wall stopping them from going after the Changeling in another way. Or if there is supposed to be, that's not made very clear in the Court Creation section.
