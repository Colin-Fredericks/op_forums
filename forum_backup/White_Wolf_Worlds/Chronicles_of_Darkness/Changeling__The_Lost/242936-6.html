                                Personally, I prefer five subtle (or not so subtle) variations on a theme that I can take in any order I want* over a chain of powers that must be purchased in a specific sequence whether you want to or not. Parallel clauses rather than serial clauses.

                                In fact, I wouldn’t mind if the notion were to be expanded to the occasional Universal Contract as well, with “variations on a theme” that don’t fit the Seeming or Court model in terms of how they vary nevertheless being presented as parallel clauses where you pick one to start with and get additional ones for one Experience each. That said, that’s strictly a hypothetical on my part, as I can’t readily think of anything off the top of my head.

                                The problem I have with Geist’s Keys and Manifestations model is the rigidity involved: if I want Industrial Shroud and Stygian Graveyard, I automatically get Stygian Shroud and Industrial Graveyard as well, whether I want em or not. Contracts with parallel clauses don’t hace at problem.
                                * Assuming I take more than one at all; I see little point in one character taking multiple powers that essentially do the same thing, which is why one more Experience per variation after the first makes sense to me.

                                                I’m a strong advocate for concept-centric character design, to the point that I’ve developed an aversion against thinking primarily in terms of “cost effectiveness”. That is, I’m willing and able to do so when the game system forces me to; but my preference is to put “does it fit my concept?” as the top priority, and “does it fit my budget?” as a distant second. In particular, I dislike the mindset of “the point budget constrains what I can get; so I’m going to take everything that it lets me take, and I’ll feel grateful for it.” It’s a symptom of an adversarial relationship between the player and the character creation system.

                                                Admittedly, it’s nowhere near as serious a problem in Onyx Path’s games as it is in, say, Hero System or GURPS, where I first developed the aversion. Still…

                                                For Geist, I’d want to revise the pricing structure downward while keeping the powers matrix, on the premise that a given Key will only automatically unlock one Manifestation: say, pay for each Manifestation once and for each Key once, choosing which Manifestation the Key unlocks when you acquire the Key; then pay a nominal fee (say, one Experience) for each additional Manifestation you have that a given Key unlocks — not unlike the “pay an Experience for each additional clause of a Contract” setup that David has proposed for Changeling. Which is the only relevance this tangent as to the thread…

                                                        Agreed: creating new Contracts should be an accomplishment — unless it’s a Goblin Contract, the lot of which I feel should be borderline-pledges in terms of ease of creation. That is, I wouldn’t mind in the least if Goblin Contracts occupied a sort of middle ground between Pledges and Contracts.
