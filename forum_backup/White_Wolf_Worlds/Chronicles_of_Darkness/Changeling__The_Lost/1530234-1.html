        Alright, I promised an excerpt, so let's gaze into the Looking Glasses.

        ​One of the reasons I like these so much is that they're very different from what you expect for the Fairest. They're a little creepier and more sinister than this seeming usually gets, and I think that's a fun way to expand on their themes. I also just love the old magic mirror trope, and I think the author did a great job of integrating them into the Chronicles of Darkness.

        Not much preamble today, but I'll be back tomorrow with something about the new antagonists in the book: The Forgotten.

        Looking Glasses: Through a Mirror, Fabulously

        The mirror sits neglected in its corner, covered in dust. Nevertheless, you feel drawn to it, as your own reflection seems to beckon you. You dust off the surface and see yourself within it, more striking than you ever thought possible. You take the mirror and place it somewhere secret. You know, somehow, that no one deserves to look upon it but you.

        One day, while brushing your hair, you ask the mirror a question and it answers back. Not in words, but in images. Visions of what might be? These omens dance across your mind while you sleep, impossible to forget. Some of them come true.

        You gaze into the mirror more and more, the world’s reflection growing more enticing than reality. Your motley wonders where you’ve gone.

        One day, you ask it to find something for you. To see someone. Your fetch, an old friend, a lost love, an enemy… and it shows you what you seek. You spend hours, days even, watching the object of your growing obsession. You pour over their every waking moment. You waste away while your reflection becomes even more beautiful.

        One day, you ask to see your Keeper, and you are never heard from again.

        Since the first Fairest was stolen into Arcadia, mirrors have loved them. They shine and preen and make such beautiful reflections. As the Muses wept through their durances, their mirrors kept them company, and it’s said the mirror world itself became besotted with these poor souls and offered them a gift. And Sovereigns know better than to refuse a gift.

        All Fairest can create Looking Glasses. By pouring attention into any object with a clear, reflective surface, a Muse can transform it into a Looking Glass. In fact, it’s so easy that many of these mirrors are created by accident — which is part of why they’re so dangerous.

        Background

        The first gift the mirror grants is the future. If asked, a Looking Glass can show its creator visions of what’s to come. Its prophecies are always heavy with symbolism, difficult to interpret, but inevitably true. But the more you ask, the more you want to ask. The Looking Glass worms its way into your thoughts more often than you’d like, until you become fixated on it.

        Once a Muse develops an obsession with her mirror, she can unlock its real power. She can ask it to show her the smartest person in the freehold, her ideal lover, or her fetch. Friends, partners, foes — the mirror can show her all of them. She just has to ask.

        And she will, again and again, until the mirror and the people reflected in it are all she can think about. Maybe she starts missing meals. Maybe she starts tearing out her hair when she’s away from the mirror too long. Maybe she starts stalking the people she spies on. Maybe she doesn’t do anything anymore but gaze at her own reflection. By then, the mirror has her, and as her body decays, her reflection only grows more and more radiant. Perhaps, when she’s dead, her reflection will walk off into the world without her. Beautiful and perfect.

        Obsessed with Myself

        It’s common wisdom among the Fairest to never make a Looking Glass. But how often does wisdom prevail? Looking Glasses are dangerous, but their power is tempting. Who doesn’t want to know the future? Who wouldn’t want to know their enemy’s every move? Who doesn’t want to find their true love? Few would admit to creating a Looking Glass, and even fewer would describe themselves as addicts. The Fairest know you don’t talk about the mirrors; you just watch for signs of dependence and take the object away if you can.

        Opinions on Looking Glasses vary from freehold to freehold. Some ignore them, some pretend not to use them, and some allow their use only under supervision. Some courts will keep a few mirror addicts around and feed their habits in exchange for information. Looking Glasses can be exceptional tools of war, particularly against other changelings, and if these mirrors are common in a freehold, hobs might sell charms and wards against them in nearby Goblin Markets.

        A few freeholds take a zero-tolerance stance, shattering every Looking Glass they can find for fear that the Gentry might see someone watching and step through.

        Through the Looking Glass

        Few changelings know how or why Looking Glasses work. They assume it’s another aspect of the Wyrd playing tricks on the Fairest, but the truth is far stranger.

        Whenever a Looking Glass is created, it acts as a beacon to creatures in mirror space, who flock to it to admire the Fairest who created it. Looking Glasses allow mirror creatures to see beyond the frame of a mirror and into the future, granting them glimpses of the people the object spies upon. This is a wondrous thrill to mirror folk, and they shower their love and attention on the Muse who offers them such freedom. It’s just that their affection is rather addictive. Who doesn’t want a captive audience?

        If one of the Fairest dies from starvation or neglect while obsessing over their Looking Glass, a mirror creature steps through the mirror and takes the body into the mirror space to honor them with a grand funeral. Others say the mirror creature steps through and becomes the deceased changeling, transforming at last from reflection to reality.

        Mirror folk loathe when humans steal Looking Glasses and abuse their powers. Ugly mortals were never meant to have them, and are punished harshly for their transgression against the Muses. Only perfection is worthy, and no human soul can have that.

                    Alright, I promised we'd discuss the Forgotten today, so let's take a look outside of biting range.

                    Like our clan books, we wanted to include antagonists, but because we were doing a book of all the seemings, we wanted to do a more cohesive group of horrors. And what better way to do that than through a mirror darkly?

                    The Forgotten are what happens when a changeling escapes Arcadia but can't quite get through the Thorns. Instead of Clarity, they must restore their minds by eating mortal (and changeling) flesh. These quasi-changelings have their own seemings like the Lost, and favor a special Regalia called Maw. I'll let the write-up do the talking here, but I really like these characters. They add a bit of that visceral quality a lot of old fairytales share. This is probably the section of the book I picked over the most post-development. Mostly that was simplifying a few things for each Forgotten seeming and generally cleaning some systems. I think they turned out wonderfully, and I hope you all have fun unleashing them on your chronicles.

                    The Forgotten: The Hungry Ones

                    When a changeling slips free of their durance, they must flee through the Hedge as the Thorns try to tear away the tattered remains of their soul, trying to remove the last fragments of the changeling’s connection to the human world. Those who weather this assault and make it out the other side become Lost. The ones who survive the physical wounds but lose their identities — their selves — are Forgotten.

                    Forgotten are rare, but they have a devastating impact on those who encounter them. With their human essence obliterated, they’re left with a terrible hunger that can only ever be temporarily sated by consuming human flesh, a wretched attempt to regain what the Thorns took from them. Shunned by both changelings and other denizens of the Hedge, most live in isolation, emerging only to feed.

                    Lost scholars theorize that the Forgotten are the origin of many of the cannibalistic horrors found in folklore around the world. What do Scandinavian trolls, Japanese kappa, and Scottish kelpies have in common? They all want to eat you.

                    Systems

                    Forgotten come in as many individual varieties as the Lost themselves, but they have a few universal traits that set them apart from their less monstrous cousins. Treat Hungry Ones as Lost except as outlined below.

                    Seeming and Kith

                    The Forgotten possess their own seemings, reflecting the sort of Lost they might have become if only they had finished their journey home. However, theirs are twisted, violent echoes that instead focus on methods of hunting and devouring prey. They are discussed in greater detail in the following sections.
                    • Disasters (Elementals)
                    • Imps (Darklings)
                    • Knockers (Wizened)
                    • Sirens (Fairest)
                    • Stalkers (Beasts)
                    • Trolls (Ogres)
                    Hungry Ones develop the same kiths as the Lost.

                    Anchors and Clarity

                    Forgotten have no Threads, only Needles. They also have no Clarity or Touchstones. As such, they are immune to any ability that affects Clarity, and they have no seeming curse (being Forgotten is more than enough). Hungry Ones can use kenning, but it is always a chance die.

                    Blessing

                    Each Forgotten seeming gains a unique blessing, rawer in power than those of their changeling counterparts but often more sinister. Unlike the Lost, Hungry Ones don’t gain an additional Attribute dot at character creation, but neither do these blessings have any catch.

                    Prey and Lure

                    Each Forgotten gains a favored form of Prey and a Lure to draw them in. These qualities vary between seemings and are discussed in their respective entries.

                    The Prey is the Forgotten’s preferred meal, the specific kind of person they crave more than any other. If a Hungry One eats someone who counts as Prey, living or dead, she completely refills her Glamour pool.

                    The Lure is the way the Forgotten attract or trap victims, a preferred method of isolating them or drawing them into the depths of the Hedge. Anyone who counts as Prey suffers the Forgotten’s Wyrd score as a penalty (to a limit of five) to resist or otherwise see through any action that aligns with her Lure, including the use of Contracts.

                    Glamour

                    Hungry Ones have only a terrible emptiness where the remains of humanity should be, and it eats away at them with each passing day. Forgotten lose 1 Glamour every sunrise. However, time is inconsistent in the Hedge; weeks in the mortal world might pass between losses, or a Hungry One who appeared full and rational only yesterday may emerge ravenous and snarling tomorrow.

                    Forgotten can only regain Glamour by consuming the flesh of changelings, hobgoblins, and humans. Every time a Hungry One inflicts damage with a bite attack on any creature with a Glamour track, she also steals a point of Glamour from her victim’s pool. Alternatively, if she consumes a changeling or hob’s fresh corpse, she gains Glamour equal to their victim’s Size or Wyrd, whichever is less. The Forgotten can’t steal Glamour from mortals, but consuming a fresh human corpse grants Glamour equal to half their total capacity, rounded up. Forgotten gain the most Glamour from eating their Prey (see above).

                    Beyond their insatiable craving for human and fae flesh, the Forgotten require no other sustenance. They don’t need to sleep, drink, or consume any other source of food. Hungry Ones don’t age normally and will generally persist until someone kills them or they starve to death.

                    Forgetting

                    As Hungry Ones don’t possess Clarity, they can’t even escape the horror of their own existence in the ecstasy of madness. Instead, a Forgotten’s level of rationality is based on when they last ate. All Forgotten suffer from the Amnesia Condition as a result of having the majority of their human identities torn away by the Thorns; this Condition can never be resolved but is temporarily suppressed whenever a Hungry One has a full Glamour pool.

                    If a Forgotten’s Glamour pool is at least half-full, she can spend a Willpower to temporarily suppress Amnesia for a scene. If she has less than half her Glamour pool remaining, she also gains the Deprived Condition. Finally, if a Forgotten has no Glamour in her pool, she gains the Berserk Condition, which cannot be resolved until she regains Glamour by consuming a fresh meal.

                    Hedge-Bound

                    With their humanity almost completely ripped away by the Thorns, the mortal world rejects the Forgotten, and the Hedge becomes their home. Hungry Ones count as hobgoblins for the purposes of navigating the Hedge and being in the mortal world (Changeling: The Lost, p. 253).

                    Regalia

                    A Forgotten’s affinity Contracts are its equivalent seeming’s favored Regalia plus Maw (below). However, some Hungry Ones can access independent Contracts (Kith and Kin, p. 58). Only members of the relevant Forgotten seeming and their Lost counterparts can develop them. In the latter case, normal changelings must have a Forgotten tutor, as per Pupil’s Devotion.

                    New Regalia: Maw

                    A maw is different from a mouth. A mouth opens to eat, certainly, but it also frowns, kisses, smiles, pouts, and reflects the person it’s a part of in myriad other ways. A maw does none of this: It’s gnashing teeth and wet lips that open to a dark, gaping gullet. A maw symbolizes constant hunger, an insatiable need to consume without ceasing. It represents both a terrible ending and an endless desire.

                    Maw Contracts are almost unknown among the Lost, save for a few Ogres, Beasts, and Gristlegrinders who can’t quite forget the crunch of bone between their molars. However, every Forgotten feels an instinctive connection to Maw as their hunger for flesh claws at their ragged minds.

                    Seeming benefits apply to both the listed Lost seeming and its Forgotten equivalent.

                            Alright, let's look at another entitlement: The Lost Pantheon.

                            In a way, this entitlement is probably the closest structurally to its original incarnation. It breaks the rule about legion entitlements having no more than six members, instead suggesting that there's no doubling up on the godly personae these changelings can adopt: only on Zeus, Thor, etc. That said, you could interpret that as this being many, many, many entitlements with similar tokens, but that's up to you.

                            One change I quite like from the original is presenting these changelings as being a bit more self-aware. Members of the 1E Lost Pantheon really did think they were gods, or at least that changelings were gods, and while that might be true of some members in 2E, many/most see themselves as avatars of deities rather than literally being them. The entitlement's stronger focus on individual, named gods is also a bit different from the original, but that made more sense within the structure of 2E entitlements, where your character embodies a particular role.

                            With that, here's the Lost Pantheon. Tomorrow, let's look at the Ogres' Forgotten mirror: the Trolls.

                            The Lost Pantheon

                            Where have all the old gods gone? We’re right here.

                            All myths sprang from the gods. Once, they were worshiped throughout the world, but as science and technology winnowed away belief in their power, they slipped into dreams and dusty tomes. Babylonian, Egyptian, Greek, Norse… their empires have fallen. Their domains have been left untended for too long. They are all forgotten.

                            But not the Lost Pantheon. By taking on godly guises, its members raise divine hands to ensure souls are ferried, new ground is consecrated, and inspiration is meted out to the worthy. Along the way, they restore faith in a world left wanting. The wait has been long, but now, the Ancients wake.

                            Privileges and Duties

                            Unlike most legion titles, the Lost Pantheon theoretically has no limit on its membership, for as long as humans have gods, the Pantheon can stand for them. However, only one Ancient can represent a given deity: one Baldur, one Juno, one Horus. Still, few know how many are embodied within this entitlement at present, and it’s rare for a freehold to have more than one member in its bounds. When they do cross paths, they’re more likely to come to blows over mythological grudges than they are to work together.

                            The specific duties of each Pantheon member vary with the godly roles they take on, but every domain includes the following: the maintenance of at least one Elemental Font (p. 118), preserving or stoking its presence within the Hedge and keeping the paths open so its power can flow into the mortal world; maintaining at least one temple, such as restoring a ruin from antiquity, consecrating a Hollow, or running gatherings for initiates; and protecting the interests and safety of a dedicated type of follower (children, mothers, farmers, scholars, etc.).

                            Wherever an Ancient wanders, they must uphold their deity’s ideals. To embody Artemis, one must forswear the touch of men and join in the hunt; to embody Idunn, one must tend and defend an orchard of goblin fruit. Thus elevated, they attract prayer, and pilgrims seek them to resolve issues within their purview. A member of the Lost Pantheon might rescue an injured animal sacred to them, take in a child orphaned by an eruption of their element, or protect a changeling member of the profession they bless.

                            To better carry out their divine duties, their capacity for the fantastic deepens. Events that might shake another’s Clarity merely force them to reconsider. The Gates of Ivory stand forever open, their consciousnesses alert whether their flesh wakes or sleeps.

                            Mask and Mien

                            An Ancient’s Mask makes them appear larger than life or enchantingly beautiful, or perhaps they strike terror into mortal hearts. Depending on how magnanimous they are, they may fill others with wonder, terror, or submission.

                            The precise nature of an Ancient’s mien depends on which traits their godly self most favors: Poseidon’s armored scales, Hathor’s cow-horn crown, Chantico’s molten magma hair. These traits grow more extreme the more steeped in the Wyrd she becomes.

                            If this sounds unsettlingly like the Gentry, they’re glad you noticed. Members of the Lost Pantheon disagree on the precise nature of the Fae and how they relate to the gods, but the Ancients feel their power and ability to create are worth emulating — if not their methods.

                            Heraldry Token: The Celestial Medallions (• to •••••)

                            In its Mask, a Celestial Medallion resembles an old, battered coin or some other metallic token from antiquity, usually from the nation where the Ancient’s god originated. Behind the Mask, it’s a glorious electrum star with nine points, its central motif representing a specific deity incorporated into the entitlement’s heraldry (Athena’s Aegis, Thor’s Mjolnir, etc.). No two are exactly alike.

                            Effects: Unlike most heraldry tokens, a Celestial Medallion can’t be stolen, at least not for long. The Wyrd ensures it returns to its rightful keeper’s hand within 24 hours of its removal.

                            Those who look upon the Medallion know the truth of its owner’s divinity. If they’re already faithful, they know they look upon the avatar of their god, but even if they aren’t yet initiated, they feel significant awe. The bearer of the Celestial Medallion gains the 8-again quality on any attempt to make a first impression with a human, and this increases to the rote quality if the human worships the god she’s embodying. (Conditional)

                            [REDACTED]

                            Catch: The Medallion must be immersed in or passed through the element associated with its current bearer for one hour as the user recites prayers.

                            Drawback: For the rest of the day, the bearer sees the Wyrd in everything, bombarded by elemental illusions, inverses of character or nature, and grim (possibly true) fates. She gains the Distracted Condition.

                                Today, let's take a look at one of the Forgotten seemings: The Trolls.

                                The Trolls are the dark mirrors of the Ogres. I'll mostly let the write-up speak for itself here, but I think these are my favorite of the six "anti-seemings." They have a fun connection to fairytale monsters, yet they're also the Forgotten who can kind of deal with the Lost on less violent terms (relatively speaking). I also think their sample character is wonderfully tragic/horrifying.

                                Not much to add here! Tomorrow, let's look at another Forgotten seeming: the Knockers.

                                Troll: A Tale of Avarice

                                Trolls are monstrous, cannibalistic cousins to the Ogres. Where Ogres often have gruesome appetites, a Troll’s hunger is a howling, bottomless pit. They are doomed to hunt eternally for more meat and more treasures, for Trolls can feed off the wealth they extort from others just as well as can they chew on your bones. But no fortune, however vast, is ever enough to truly sate them.

                                Once

                                Most Ogres grow to despise the relentless violence their Keepers force them to inflict, feeling deeply ashamed of the gruesome appetites cultivated in them. They want to be forgiven, to make amends, or revel in their newfound freedom. Trolls escape their masters, but never unlearn their Keepers’ lessons. They free themselves from violence, but can’t stop inflicting it. But there are many ways for an Ogre to lose their way and become a Troll: Some refuse to return on the cusp of escape, too ashamed of the horrors they inflicted on the Gentry’s behalf. Some chose to stay, finding the taste of living flesh too delectable to ever quit the habit. And still others decide they’re owed something, deciding to take what’s theirs by any means necessary.

                                Unlike their Forgotten kin, Trolls don’t track down their prey: They wait for it to come to them. Trolls haunt bridges, trods, Hedge gates, and places rich in natural resources, licking their lips when unsuspecting morsels wander too close. Most are happy enough to eat whoever wanders by, but their hunger can also sometimes be sated with riches and tokens, so cunning hobs and changelings bargain in exchange for safe passage. If the offer’s good enough, the Troll might even decide not to eat you — but that’s never a guarantee.

                                Despite their ragged appearances and uncouth table manners, Trolls often amass enormous treasure hoards from their tolls. New Trolls bury their trinkets near their haunts, while the eldest of them build palaces around the bridges and pathways they guard, stuffed with tokens and baubles and delicate things their clumsy hands might break. When Trolls are hungry (which is most of the time), they quite enjoy their lot in life. They revel in their riches, power, and cravings. Now that they’re free of their Keepers, who they can barely recall, they can indulge however they like. More flesh, more stuff, more violence… Just more, forever after.

                                Until they over-indulge, regain their hazy memories, and recall all the faces they’ve hurt and how they tasted on their teeth. Trolls often fall into deep depressions when they’re sated, leaving their bridges unguarded to sob into their treasures.

                                Prey

                                Trolls prey on the prosperous. Something in their hearts is made whole by devouring those who have more than they need, and they consider flesh from the rich to be a treasure equal to gold. Trolls can smell meat and money and draw power by hoarding them. As Trolls grow hungrier, they grow taller, wider, and stronger, and when starving, they can grow as big as a barn. This increased size only fuels their hunger, as Trolls need more flesh and wealth to sustain their massive frames.

                                Lure

                                Though their gnashing teeth and gigantic statures are frightening enough, a Troll’s paths are what truly terrify changelings. Trolls may deny passage to anyone who wants to cross their path, use their bridge, or open their gates, making it impossible for anyone to navigate around them. If you encounter a Troll on your path, you’ll never reach your destination unless you pay, kill, or outsmart the monster barring your way. Your only other option is to give up the quest altogether — which might be the only path that leads to survival.

                                Blessing

                                All Trolls gain the Giant Merit. When one has the Amnesia Condition, he gains two levels of Size and Speed. When he has the Berserk Condition, increase these traits by another two levels. These increases improve derived Advantages as normal and are lost when the appropriate Conditions are suppressed or resolved. The Troll can suppress this transformation for a scene by spending a Glamour, but he can also take an instant action to regain it.

                                Furthermore, Trolls gain Glamour when people pay them to avoid being eaten or hurt: one Glamour per dot of a token or Availability rating of a mundane item. This nourishes them like flesh, and the richer the payment, the more filling it is.

                                                Knocker: A Tale of Deceit

                                                The most successful hunters aren’t always the biggest or the fastest. Cleverness and patience can bring in prey at a much more reliable rate than wildly snapping at whatever crosses your path — just ask a Knocker. These would-be Wizened are the sharp-toothed gnomes and rhyme-talking spinners of fairytales, offering riches, knowledge, and magical favors to those they encounter, but always at a price. What they ask in return is simple, at first: a bowl of milk, some slivers of ash wood, maybe a few drops of blood…

                                                Easy enough to lose, right? But as their victims become more reliant on favors, the price gets higher. And gorier.

                                                Once

                                                Clever enough to escape but not quite clever enough to get home: That’s the tragedy of the Knockers. Having slipped the Gentry’s leash, they think themselves untouchable, too crafty to be caught again. Their arrogance is their downfall, for the Hedge loves to swallow up those who think they can’t fail. Too late, Knockers discover that they’ve only outsmarted themselves.

                                                When their minds are clear enough to hold a memory, Knockers endlessly replay their failed journeys, obsessing over every misstep, picking away at the thought of paths not taken, the choices that would have led them to a happier ending. This twisted regret breeds a resentful, spiteful creature who has nothing but hatred for the Lost. How is it fair that a lumbering Ogre made it home when a being of superior wit is trapped in the Thorns forever? This resentment fuels their attacks on changelings as much as their hunger for flesh does.

                                                Yet for all their bitterness, these Hungry Ones are social hunters. Lacking the brute strength or mystical allure of other Forgotten, Knockers trap their prey via more insidious methods, hooks baited with the keys to their desires.

                                                Prey

                                                Knockers prey on the desperate. The smell of those who have a need so acute they’ll do anything to fulfill it is delectable to these Forgotten. They often target those suffering from addiction and those who are in such dire straits that they’ll make bargains with monsters in the dark. If a Knocker can’t find his favorite prey, well, he’ll make someone desperate — even if they’re only desperate for the Knocker to stop their torment.

                                                Lure

                                                A Knocker’s Lure is deals, and they’re rarely what they seem. They can conjure almost anything a victim desires, so long as they pay the agreed-upon price. These gifts are temporary but addictive, and those who continue to do business with a Knocker will eventually become so dependent they’ll willingly agree to the creature’s hardest sales pitches: first a finger or two, then a foot, then a neighbor. However, while they’re usually relentless hagglers, a well-fed Knocker will sometimes offer fairer deals to those she encounters in an attempt to appease her own guilt and try to prove to herself she’s not a total monster. Soon enough, though, the hunger comes clawing back and devours any notion she may have momentarily entertained about fair play or mercy.

                                                Blessing

                                                This blessing is similar to making a bargain (Changeling, p. 214), except as noted below:

                                                A Knocker can make a deal with changelings, hobgoblins, and humans.
                                                • Making deals with a Knocker is addictive. The subject’s player must make a successful Resolve + Composure roll every time he agrees to a deal. Failure means he gains the Addicted Condition for bargaining with the Hungry One.
                                                • A Knocker can spend a Willpower to craft a talisman that can provide a benefit he wouldn’t normally be able to give, but its power is temporary; it crumbled into bits of detritus after a number of weeks pass equal to the Hungry One’s Wyrd (or sooner, if the Knocker wishes). However, the subject is always welcome to make another deal.
                                                • Knockers don’t gain mortal camouflage from this ability; they don’t need it. Instead, the Hungry One always knows the location of anyone they have an ongoing deal with and the location of the Hedge gate nearest to that person.
                                                • If a subject violates the deal, the consequences are always the same: They’re drawn into the Hedge and deposited near the Knocker, who is always aware when this happens.
