                    Time to come back around. Rowan’s story comes to a head, while, Insincere Marguerite’s troubles boil over. No end of drama at the Dream House. Gawain’s player had to miss.

                    Session LXV

                    Lorelei’s Diary

                    I just looked back over my last entry and had to suppress a bitter humorless laugh. Concerns about my reputation in the Freehold? Bring it on. Uncertainty as to Rowan’s safety with regards to her house? Sounds good to me. Existential ennui? Thank you, world, may I have another?

                    I’m writing this quickly as the others prepare to go. We’re heading to Oleander’s place, and I thought writing in my diary might be vaguely preferable to sitting around feeling useless and tense. We have to take action now, since we’re relatively sure that our opponent now views us as a threat… and I can’t help but wonder if we’re doing the right thing. And even if we are, I have to face up to the very real possibility that by the time this night is over, someone I care about may never trust me again.

                    So the day started off well enough. D came to find me in our room when the rest of my lovers began to wake up, and the six of us headed downstairs for sex breakfast. D was wearing a tank-top, which really showed off her physique, much to my approval (and Julia’s, and Gloria’s). When we got to the kitchen, Gawain was already making more waffles (I forget the type this time), so we sat around the kitchen island and the usual banter began. I was engaged in the talk and responded if anyone addressed me directly, but I let most of it flow past me as I finished perusing an article on endometriosis. After a while I found myself leaning up against D and stroking her bicep idly as I read.

                    Oleander was one of the first to leave, and I walked her to the front door and left her with a deep kiss. “We have to find a way to see each other more often during the week,” I opined wistfully.

                    She smiled. “Just let me know when you’re available, and I’ll do my best to be non-toxic,” she promised. “But… I do have a job to do. I need to head into the Hedge over the next few days to pick up some supplies, and some of the sites I’ll be visiting are… not very nice. So let me know now if I need to reschedule that jaunt, please.”

                    I shook my head. “Of course I don’t want our time together to interfere with your work,” I explained. “I’m sorry. I wasn’t thinking. I was being selfish.”

                    She shook her head. “I’d do just about anything to spend more time with you, angel,” she said softly. “But I know you have a lot of demands on your time. I… I like your other girlfriends. I wouldn’t have slept with them last night if I didn’t. But… I’m maybe not as needy as some of them, and I’m okay with that.”

                    I gave her my best smile. “You’re amazing,” I said breathily. “I’ll see you next weekend if not before, okay?” She nodded, leaned in for another kiss, and then she was gone.

                    Calamity and I were prepared to do a couple of semi-final run-throughs of our number after breakfast, and we wanted Marguerite to be present for at least one of them so that she could get an idea of what sort of lighting and sound would be needed for the act. Calamity went off to talk to Marguerite, and the two of them didn’t show back up for almost twenty minutes. I didn’t mind, I was still blissing out leaning up against D at the time.

                    D had left for work by the time Calamity joined me in the theater. I could tell immediately that something was wrong, and I thought she and Marguerite might have had another fight. “What’s wrong?” I asked, carefully placing a hand on her shoulder.

                    “It’s nothing,” she replied automatically.

                    “Calamity…” I began, letting my concern show on my face.

                    “It’s… nothing I can talk about,” she amended.

                    “Are things okay between you and Marguerite?” I asked after a moment.

                    She nodded. “Well, as okay as can be expected,” she said after a few seconds of silence. “She’s not mad at me or anything like that.”

                    “Are you safe? Is she safe?” I asked.

                    Calamity shrugged. “Nobody is in any physical danger that I know of,” she said guardedly.

                    I quirked an eyebrow. “Emotional danger, then? Spiritual?” Sometimes I have real trouble letting things go.

                    Calamity sighed. “I’m sorry, Lori,” she said. “I’m truly sorry. But I can’t talk to you about this.”

                    That’s when it hit me. “You’ve made a pledge, haven’t you?”

                    She nodded.

                    I took both of her shoulders in my hands. “Just promise me – you don’t have to pledge, but please promise me – that you’ll let me know if there’s anything I can do to help, okay?”

                    She nodded again. “I promise,” she said.

                    Marguerite approached us after this little conversation, and told me that Felicity had texted her to let her know that she’d had her talk with Rowan… who was not taking the suggestion that something might be a little ‘off’ about her house particularly well. I decided to give her a call and see if I could help.

                    As Felicity had said, she wasn’t very happy about the assertion that something might be wrong with her house, but she was prepared to play along if it would make me happy. She seemed particularly nervous about staying with Felicity – whom she doesn’t know very well – so I asked her if she would prefer to stay with me at the Dream House. She hemmed and hawed for a while, claiming that she didn’t want to put anyone out, but eventually I managed to get her to agree.

                    “Thanks, Lori,” she said hoarsely as we were saying our goodbyes. “I’ll see you tonight. Love you.”

                    “Love you too,” I replied without even thinking about it. After I disconnected I realized what I had said, and sort of mentally kicked myself. I’m still not sure how I feel about Rowan, and I don’t want to lead her on. I don’t want to hurt her.

                    In any case, something about her tone when she talked about the house made me nervous, and her extreme resistance to the idea of letting anyone examine it set off all kinds of alarms in my head. I called a motley-wide meeting. Everyone was there except D, who was still at work. I explained the situation to them, and told them that I had invited Rowan to stay with us that night. I also explained that I wanted her to stay with me specifically… both so that I could try to comfort her, and so that I could watch to make sure she didn’t try to sneak out in the middle of the night.

                    Marguerite offered to remain in contact with Felicity to make sure things went smoothly when the Day Court arrived to inspect the premises. Calamity and Julia opted to head out to the Autumn Court’s occult library to see what they could dig up on similar situations. They asked if I wanted to go with them, and a part of me really did… but realistically I’m not much use when it comes to exerting my brainpower, and I’d probably have ended up as more of a hindrance than a help.

                    So I spent most of the afternoon fretting and worrying instead of doing anything useful. Marguerite kept me posted, but there wasn’t actually much to tell for most of the afternoon. When Calamity and Julia got back, they had managed to form a few theories about the nature of the house... none of which were particularly encouraging.

                    First, as I'd already postulated, the house could be haunted. This doesn’t seem likely for a variety of reasons. First, I’ve been in the house a couple of times now – I’ve even been alone in it for short periods – and I’ve never noticed anything that would indicate a poltergeist or other malevolent spirit. Second, Rowan’s obsession with staying in her house seems to be a recent development; she’s left it for extended periods since we’ve met her with no ill effects.

                    The second theory is that the house could be a Gentry in disguise. There’s at least one precedent; a Gentry who was exiled and confined to the form of a house. It exerted its powers over the mortals who lived within it, trying to get them to accomplish tasks which it thought would help it return to Arcadia. That doesn’t seem to be this house’s M.O, but then again, every Gentry is different, so we’re at least prepared to humor the notion until it can be disproved. Jacob mentioned driving an iron nail into the house to see how it reacts. Calamity really liked that idea, while Luna seemed to think it would be safest to simply burn the house to the ground before it could drag anyone off to Arcadia.

                    The third and most likely theory involves something called a “Dionaea House.” Apparently the word is based upon the scientific name for the Venus Fly-Trap plant, and it describes a sentient house which lures people into it so that it can consume them. They’re difficult to detect; the books Calamity and Julia found said that one of the only visible signs of a Dionaea House was their tendency to leave piles of human bones beneath their foundations. They have been known to use attractive mortals as bait to lure prey in, though… and these mortals are rarely harmed.

                    We’d also uncovered a little more information about the house’s previous tenants. All of them were attractive blonde women in their early twenties. Photos bear this up, though it’s shockingly difficult to dig up any concrete information about any of them beyond their names. The thing is, all photos of these residents show attractive blonde women in their early twenties… even those who lived in the house over a period of ten to twenty years. It could be a coincidence… but you’d think there would be a little variety in the photographic evidence. We even batted around the idea that maybe all of them were high-Wyrd changelings who aged very slowly, but something about that theory didn’t feel quite right.

                    I think it was a little before dinner time when everything went completely to shit. Marguerite entered the kitchen a little later than usual, and she was looking unusually grim. I asked her what was wrong, and she told me with a sigh.

                    Felicity’s attempt to remove Rowan from her house had not gone well. About five Day Courtiers from LA accompanied her to the house carrying axes. I’m assured that this was merely a precaution, in case the house tried to trap them inside to prevent Rowan from leaving, but it set my newest girlfriend off in a way none of us had predicted. She absolutely refused to leave… to the point of assaulting Felicity. She actually tried to choke the life out of our Day Court neighbor, and it took two of her companions to separate them. She was taken to Felicity’s house and restrained to prevent her from hurting anyone.

                    I couldn’t believe it. If I hadn’t witnessed Rowan’s distress for myself a short time later, I probably still wouldn’t believe it. Rowan is such a peaceful, non-violent girl… it’s hard for me to imagine her lashing out against anyone like that, let alone a prominent member of her own Court. I decided that I had to talk to her, and asked Marguerite to arrange it with Felicity.

                    A few minutes later my phone rang. I didn’t recognize the number, but I answered it anyway. “Hello?” I asked hesitantly.

                    “Lori?” Rowan’s voice came across the connection. She sounded both tense and out-of-breath, not at all the sweet lilt to which I’ve become accustomed.

                    “Rowan, sweetie, are you okay?” I asked anxiously.

                    “I’m… not hurt,” she assured me after a moment. “They took me out of my House,” she added a few seconds later. She sounded both affronted and a little scared.

                    “I know, sweetie,” I said with as much reassurance as I could. “I’m really sorry about that. I didn’t know they were going to be so blunt about it. But I really think they’re trying to help you, okay?”

                    “Help me?” she asked, as though trying to figure out how that could be possible. “Help me by taking me away from my House? How is that going to help me? I don’t like it here. I’m not comfortable here. I want to go back to my House.” Yes, she kept using the word, and subtly emphasizing it, which is why I’m capitalizing it here.

                    I started to get really scared at that point. “Rowan, honey, I want you to listen to me, okay? I know you’re upset, and you have every right to be. But if Felicity is right, you’re in danger while you’re in that house.”

                    “How could my House be dangerous?” she asked. “That doesn’t make any sense.”

                    I tried a different tactic. “It might be nothing. I hope it is nothing, and you’ll be back in your house as soon as we can verify that. I promise.”

                    She thought about it for a while. “O…okay,” she said at last. “I trust you, Lori. I’ll… I’ll try to calm down. But… but this whole thing makes no sense. I wonder if Felicity wants my House for herself? That makes more sense than claiming that my House is somehow dangerous. Or… or maybe she’s been corrupted by the Night Court, and they’re trying to get their hands on my House…”

                    I was close to tears by this point. “I really don’t think that’s the case, honey,” I said softly. “That place has some kind of hold on you. Listen to yourself… this doesn’t sound like you.”

                    “I…” she began, then coughed for a moment. “I… attacked Felicity earlier,” she admitted hoarsely. “I wanted to make sure you heard it from me. I don’t know why I did it… she was just trying to take me away from my House and I sort of panicked…”

                    “I know,” I said soothingly. “Just like I know you wouldn’t have done something like that normally. The Rowan I know doesn’t want to hurt anyone… not even a jackass like TJ who’s been trying to intimidate her left and right.”

                    I tried my best to soothe her, and I think it worked to a degree. When we hung up a few minutes later, she at least seemed more rational. And if she wasn’t exactly relaxed, at least she didn’t feel quite so much like a plucked guitar string.

                    D arrived a few minutes later, cocksure as ever. She took in the somber looks, walked up to me and took me into her arms. “Is everything okay, babe?” she asked.

                    “Not really,” I replied.

                    “What’s wrong?” she asked. “Who do I have to punch?”

                    I told her what had been going on, and she got a determined look on her face. “I need to check on some things,” she said stiffly as she stalked toward the exit.

                    “Where are you going, hon?” I asked. I… dammit, weakling that I am, I wanted to be able to rely on her strength for a while.

                    She turned back to face me and stroked my cheek gently. “I’m going to call Madame Spidertoes,” she said softly. “I’m going to ask her about this list of tenants you’ve uncovered. If they were changelings, she’d know about them.”

                    I nodded. “That’s a good idea. The more we know the better.”

                    She walked out into the hall. As she walked into an unused room, I faintly heard her say “Gladys… it’s Maria. I need to ask you something…”

                    She returned a few minutes later with a grim look on her face. “None of them were changelings, as far as she knows,” she told us. “Not vampires or other supernaturals either… though that’s a bit harder for her to pin down.”

                    I slumped my shoulders. “Thanks for trying,” I said.

                    Calamity called Dahlia, as by this point it was hard to argue against the idea that the house represented a “danger to the Freehold and its inhabitants,” and Calamity is pledge-bound to report such things to Dahlia. Though I only heard about the conversation secondhand, I gather that Calamity’s handler is also a bit tense after watching Chalcedony for the past few days. She doesn’t exactly seem to be falling in love with Little Girl Blue like Molly did, though, and that’s at least a little comforting.

                    Dahlia sort of impatiently asked if she needed to break surveillance to deal with this situation or if we could handle it, and Calamity opined that we could manage by ourselves (I wish I had her confidence on that score). Dahlia also suggested that we might want to check with Madame Spidertoes to see if she knew of any way to detect and deal with Dionaea Houses, in case that’s what it is. I was reluctant to have D call her again – it wasn’t jealousy this time, I was legitimately worried that we would become a nuisance to her – but D gave me a quick smile and a peck on the cheek and assured me that her former flame wouldn’t mind helping us out in such a situation.

                    It wasn’t long before D returned with Spidertoes’ suggestions. Foremost among these was that if it is a Dionaea House, we might be able to force a reaction out of it by spreading a certain chemical around its base. I can’t remember the name of the chemical right now, but D had written it down, and Spidertoes had told us that we could probably get some from Oleander.

                    “Shit,” I murmured when D reported this information.

                    Everybody turned to look at me. “What is it?” Marguerite asked.

                    “Oleander’s going into the Hedge today. She told me she was going to be toxic for the next few days.” I pulled out my phone and speed-dialed her. “Maybe she hasn’t left yet. I might be able to catch her.”

                    Luck was with us; she answered on the third ring. “I missed you too, angel, but I was gonna play it cool and wait a couple of days before I called you,” she quipped once we were connected.

                    “Hey, honey,” I said by way of a greeting. “I’m afraid it’s not a social call tonight. The motley has to deal with something, and I need a certain chemical, if you can get it for me.”

                    She was all business in an instant. “Whatever you need,” she said seriously. “Just name it.”

                    I did.

                    She whistled. “Is this some sort of a test, angel?” she asked after a moment. “I said I’d get you anything you needed, and I meant it… but I can’t just give that stuff out. I’ll have to inform Charlotte at the very least. Why do you want it?”

                    I told her in brief about Rowan’s house, and said that Spidertoes had recommended we ask her for the chemical. She gave a sigh of relief at that. “If it’s Spidertoes’ idea, then I’m sure Charlotte won’t have a problem with it. I still have to tell her, though, and it’ll take me a while to prepare a batch for transport. And, Lori…”

                    “Yes?” I asked anxiously.

                    “I don’t want you handling this stuff. At all. I haven’t even told Flores that I know how to make it. It can actually weaken the bindings between body and soul, and it’s no joke. I don’t give it out lightly, and I wouldn’t give it out at all if anyone else was asking me.” She chuckled grimly to herself. “God, I must really have it bad for you. In any case, I’d rather none of you handle this stuff if it can be avoided. Give me two hours to get it ready, and then I’ll come with you and apply it myself.”

                    I fretted about that for a minute. “If it’s that dangerous… are you sure about this, sweetie? I mean, maybe we shouldn’t be doing this…”

                    Oleander thought for a minute. “If this thing is a threat to the freehold, then it needs to be dealt with. And if anything can kill a house…”

                    “Okay,” I said. I still had misgivings, but I was willing to put them aside for the moment. “Text one of us when you’re ready, and we’ll come and meet you at your workshop.” I walked out into the hall and pitched my voice low. “Thank you so much for doing this, Imelda. I love you.”

                    There was silence for a few moments. I know she doesn’t like that name, but I wanted to do something to impress upon her how serious I was. “Thanks for agreeing to let me handle it,” she replied eventually. “I’ll feel better knowing that nobody else is going to get their hands on this chemical. And I love you too, Juliette.”

                    We talked among ourselves for a bit as we waited for the signal. When the subject of Chalcedony came up, I mentioned a theory that she might be using Talecrafting to cast herself in the role of romantic heroine… the character in the book who is accused of wrongdoing, but as people investigate and get to know her, they can’t help falling for her. It’s an interesting theory, and it fits the facts, but I have to admit that there’s not much in the way of direct supporting evidence.

                    Rowan called me again while we were waiting for Oleander to get back to us. “Hey sweetie, are you okay?” I asked anxiously as I answered the phone.

                    She sighed into the receiver. “I asked if I could call you again, and she said yes. I want to be with you, Lori. I don’t want to stay here. You make things better. If I can’t go back to my House, can I at least come and stay with you?”

                    I considered it. “I’ll… I’ll have to check with the others, honey, okay? It’s not just my decision. But I will ask them, and I promise as soon as this is resolved we can spend as much time together as you like. I’ll set aside a whole day if you want.”

                    She let out a shuddering breath. “Okay,” she said. “I’ll… I’ll be patient. Thanks for talking to me, Lori. I love you.”

                    I thought about it this time. “Right back atcha,” I said, somewhat lamely, and then the signal was gone. Unsurprisingly, we all pretty much agreed that letting Rowan stay at the Dream House would be a bad idea.

                    After a while, D got a text. She glanced down at her phone, then looked up and nodded. “Green light from Oleander,” she said simply.

                    We piled into Gawain’s van and drove over to Oleander’s lab. Most of this entry was written while we were waiting for her call, but I’ve added these last few paragraphs at the lab. Oleander had a few last things to take care of, and didn’t want any of us anywhere near the chemical, so we’ve been cooling our heels here for a few minutes. And it’s given me time to think.

                    A few weeks ago, we noticed that Marguerite was acting strangely, in ways that seemed to be out-of-character for her. When things came to what seemed to be a crisis point, Calamity took action, believing her “darkling sister” to be both under Chalcedony’s sway and in immediate danger. As a result, their relationship was badly damaged, and they are only just now starting to return to the easy friendship they had before.

                    Is this situation really so different? Someone I care about is acting strangely, and seems to be under the sway of a powerful magical or psychological effect, and we’re about to respond to that violently. But do I really know Rowan well enough to judge her mental state and take this sort of action? What if her relationship with the house is some sort of weird symbiosis? I have no evidence that either of them has ever harmed anyone directly (aside from Rowan’s assault on Felicity earlier, of course). Do we have the right to strike out against it in such a deadly way without proof that it means this community harm?

                    And… whether she’s under its control or not, will Rowan ever forgive me for this?

                    Lorelei Grace

                        Calamity’s Little Black Book

                        Monday, November 23rd
                        Nine years, six months, twenty-eight days
                        Days without stabbing: 5

                        I'm going to stab a house. Well, most likely I'm going to stab it. I've never fought a house before and apparently we're going to spiritually salt the earth it sits on to try to poison it first But once we've pissed it off and weakened it I'll get to stab it. I've got an iron spike and a hammer from Gawain's workshop so I should get to stab it with that (to check and see if it's a Gentry) if nothing else. Beats watching Titanic (well, I guess we might still watch it after this is over. It's not that late. Defeat the monster and then celebrate with a light lunch and movie...)
