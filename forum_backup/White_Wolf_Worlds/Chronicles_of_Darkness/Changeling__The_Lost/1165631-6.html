With that in mind, I could see an Author Kith using the written word to create or enhance emotion beyond what mere words (even with critical successes) could produce. You could also flip it, and let the character read to understand exactly what the emotions and state of mind was for the writer when a text was penned, for instance.

                        Author
                        They say the pen is mightier than the sword. That's garbage of course-I wouldn't bring a pen to a sword fight. No, the trick of it is, a pen in the right hand can make sure your enemy can't even summon up the will to lift their sword in the first place. Then you can drive the pen through their eye, if you really insist on getting physical.

                        The gentry are ego personified. They revel in self aggrandizement-how can you not, they say, when the very ground you walk on thanks you for the honor? But of course, being great is never enough-people have to know you're great. Of course you can tell them yourself, but that's rather gauche now, isn't it? No, far better to have someone else there to tell everybody how great you are. Or perhaps the Keeper is bored. A childlike true fae, easily distracted, needs a good flow of new diversions, and what better way to divert a being of madness and story than more stories?

                        And that's where the authors come in. Humans often chosen for their creativity, they were plucked from their world and dragged through the thorns, then made to use the blood from their freshly torn wounds as ink to write stories to please their Keepers. Some record the feats of their true fae master, some create stories from their own imaginations to delight and enchant.

                        Once they've returned ironside, authors retain their preternatural capacity for storytelling, able to weave tales to astonish and horrify and delight with ease. These changelings are the ones most likely to talk about their durance, filling in the blanks with dramatic detail; sometimes they cleave as close to the truth of their memories as they can, sometimes they inject spectacular falsehoods of one sort or another, but either way, they've found that a story shared is a burden halved.

                        Beast: This changeling looks vaguely like a crow, with black feathers and large, obsidian eyes. They seem to hunch forward, scrawling what they see in one of a half-dozen journals they keep on their person at all times, each one for a specific kind of story divided in a way that only they know about.

                        Elemental: This changeling seems to be made of wood and parchment, words inked over their skin, shifting and changing constantly. They sit in their one spot at the bar, silent and still, until someone sends them over a drink and asks for a story. Then they become as animated and energetic as anyone could hope to be.

                        Kith blessing: When the changeling makes an expression roll that involves writing, they only need three successes instead of five for an exceptional success.

                        Read between the lines: More than any other kith, the authors know that the most important things are the things that go unsaid. The changeling can spend a point of glamour and make a wits+expression roll to either; imbue a piece of work with the ability to evoke a specific emotion in anyone who reads it (the reader can trigger a clash of wills to resist the effect) or; they can understand the subtext of any one piece of writing that they read. Each new piece of writing requires a new instance of initiating this blessing.

                        ...

                        Alright, here's my crack at the author kith. Do you think the kith blessing might be overpowered? Having essentially two abilities could be a bit much, but I thought that given they're fairly situational, that might balance them out.

                                Not so much a conversion, but I had an idea for a Kith known as a Deadeye - they're basically sharpshooters among Changelings, designed to always be armed and dangerous even with an empty gun. I don't know what to make for their Kith Blessing though. Their Trickery is below:

                                A Bullet With Your Name On It: A Deadeye's gun is always loaded. By spending a Glamour and successfully rolling Presence + Firearms, any gun a Deadeye is holding is instantly loaded with ammunition appropriate for its type. Even if the firearm is missing its magazine, it is considered loaded to its maximum capacity. These bullets are unique to the Changeling, being composed of materials such as crystal, ice or even something like compacted star dust and do the same damage as normal ammunition. Any un-fired bullets dissolve into nothing at the end of the scene.

                                                    White Oak Dragon

                                                    Thanks; if your kith gets taken in for an official one, I may change the name to Soul Shooter, as the Trickery implies a Changeling uses part of herself for her weapon. Best of luck.

                                                    Master Aquatosic

                                                    Hmmm, when using Firearms to shoot a moving target is an interesting idea, or possibly for shooting a distant one. I had thought that perhaps when a Deadeye uses the Aim option, since that is a risk/reward scenario, but I'm not sure.

                                                    As for the Trickery, it's more to allow a Changeling both to leave a calling card and options for actually using a firearm. Note that I mentioned that the gun didn't need to have a magazine in it, which was meant to go across all guns - this means a Changeling can simply carry an empty pistol, spend a glamour and have a full weapon at their disposal, or even simply pick up an empty rifle an opponent was using and use that as if it were fresh. This is partially to work around various places not allowing loaded weaponry on the premises. While having a weapon at all would probably raise brows, one not even having the capacity to carry ammunition would grant the Changeling some leeway. Add in the fact that the ammunition they create is unique to them, and at the end of the scene there is no evidence they were personally carrying it, and you have some interesting scenarios for a hitman.

                                                    My initial idea for the Trickery was that the number of bullets a Changeling got was determined on the successes and half their Wyrd, and that it could exceed storage that way. However, I realized that would be pretty lackluster and random if the Changeling got a low die roll, or overpowered if they had something like a shotgun, so I changed it to simply having the normal maximum storage. I had pondered on another idea where it was Glamour and a die roll to simply make a ranged attack, but that felt a little wasteful to me in terms of glamour, so I decided to simply have it affect a normal gun.

                                                    I suppose there could be another aspect added to it, such as not suffering penalties to Firearms rolls from environmental factors when using their Trickery, or maybe choosing to not cause damage to a target even if successful. After all, Gristlegrinders don't require making a Grappling roll to make bites that have Lethal damage.
