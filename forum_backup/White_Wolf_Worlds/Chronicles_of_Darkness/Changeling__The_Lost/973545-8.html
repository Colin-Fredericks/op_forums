                        Originally posted by Axelgear View Post
                        The Hedge is at its best when it's the place of fairy tales; when it's the path to Grandmother's House; when it's got a dragon's lair or a cannibal's larder. A place for Changelings to indulge their fae side. The Thorns existed in 1e, but they weren't out to cause you pain; they were hungry. Hungry for life; for emotion; for warm blood. The Thorns were psychoactive, but the Hedge itself wasn't out innately to hurt you. It just never really had enough in it that justified the risks taken.

                        ...

                        I like the Hedge, but I like it as a place that lets fairytale stories play out, and that doesn't sound like where the focus is at the moment. Right now, it sounds like something that purely exists as a complication for other stories. When it's just contributing to yet more of the "I want to run from everything fae" side of Changeling, I feel like that's getting ignored.
                        Sorry for resurrecting this sub-thread when you seem pretty done with it, but I wanted to weigh in and I've been out of town.

                        As I read it, the Hedge isn't innately out to hurt you. It's not about harming travelers; it's about expressing pain. And pain, when expressed, is empathized with, and that empathy induces your own pain. That's the general case: in the specific case, of course, it's expressing your pain so it doesn't work like that at all. Changelings, more than any other splat, weaponize emotion and empathy, which gives them an understanding of those things that exceed an average human's... and that understanding comes in the form of losing Clarity. They get it, and in getting it, are hurt by it.

                        Someone who's genuinely innocent or ignorant, I think, will have little trouble navigating the Hedge. It would be frightening, but it wouldn't hurt you and once you're through it, you wouldn't have a problem letting go of what you just went through. So someone like Little Red Riding Hood has no real struggle. The wolf will walk out of the brambles and ask her plans and will slink away with her answers. The loss of her innocence happens later, after she's left the Hedge, at Grandmother's House. But no one's really that innocent or ignorant. You've heard things. Things that you don't really understand, perhaps, but you've imagined what they might be. And the Hedge would take those imaginations and magnify them into terrifying realities: thorns that are then capable of shredding your Integrity.

                        Fundamentally, I don't think that has anything to do with running from the Gentry. The Gentry aren't terribly impressed by the Hedge, if you read the spoiler again. Sure, they might fear it, but they aren't harmed by it because they're incapable of empathy. You don't go into the Hedge to run away from Arcadia; you go in when you've accepted that Arcadia is part of you. In a sense, the Hedge can be empowering as the realm that expresses the Gentry's unwillingness to go Ironside.

                        But circling back away from the fluff-based justifications, I would point this out: you can play out fairy tales anywhere. If that's the value of the Hedge, then the Hedge has no value. It is, as noted, simply the Woods on the edge of town, just on the other side of the river, where the outcasts live in their secret forest kingdom. Instead, I would point at the statement (I think Arcane made it? But I can't find it in the thread again) that everything about Changelings ultimately comes back to pain. The Hedge isn't a garrison against the Gentry; it's the one place that fully manifests the themes of Changeling.

                        The Hedge is a borderland you don't cross, because you want to never go back. It's safer at home.
                        The Hedge is the stripping away of your mask, that pretense you put up saying that you didn't go through a Durance.
                        The Hedge is about brutal honesty. You come out transformed because it won't let you deny truths.
                        The Hedge is about dreams: hopes and fears and secret feelings. It's always there, that excuse and wish to explore beyond the border.

                        And all of it hurts. Because what a fairy tale really is? Is about growing pains.

                        I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".

                                    I really, really like the new write up. The writing is more tightly focused and the mechanics are so much cleaner than first edition. I do see the concern on some players being turned away by the predominant focus on how the place can hurt you, which is a tough balancing act, because the Hedge should be scary. Characters should have reasons to be hesitant about going there. At the same time, they should also have reasons to face that fear, to decide the gain is worth the risk. In terms of actual mechanics I don't think the Hedge is nearly as bad as some people are reading it to be. Even wandering around off the trods in most cases you're taking one bashing per hour, and you can mitigate that by traveling more slowly. Or shape a temporary hollow and rest for an hour before pushing on. That just doesn't say "never do this" to me. Some of my best adventures have involved bruises and bloody knuckles and blistered feet. Hell, I work outside and I'm constantly getting nicks and scrapes, and sure they sting for a couple minutes but that doesn't stop me from enjoying the work. At the end of it you come home, and the scratches heal and fade away, or they become scars that you tell stories about.

                                        Oh, and for those curious, this is the short version in the "high concept" introduction section talking in simple terms about game concepts.

                                        The Hedge

                                        “As soon as there is life, there is danger.”
                                        ― Ralph Waldo Emerson

                                        Of course, the Hedge was a place every changeling passed through on the way out of Arcadia. It's a place where every changeling left something behind, a piece of themselves torn asunder by the thorns. But after the escape, the Hedge takes on a whole new role in changeling life.

                                        The Hedge is a dangerous place. Its thorns, its paths, they bend and twist and decay and grow anew at a moment's notice. Off established roads, called trods, the path is a savage garden, a vicious and frustrating jungle. Why would anyone ever return?

                                        The Hedge offers just as many rewards as dangers. Mysterious goblin fruits grow on the Hedge's plantlife, fruits which are Glamour welled up and calcified, easily consumable and offering sometimes miraculous advantages to their consumers. The Hedge offers a remarkably savvy way to elude the True Fae and the Huntsmen, after all, the Hedge is a dangerous and confusing path even for the fae. Also, many changelings carve out safe places within the Hedge, these "hollows" are free of prying eyes and hunting hearts. Also, changelings can walk in and manipulate dreams, and they usually do this by entering an individual's dreams through portals found deep in the Hedge.

                                        The Hedge is a psychoreactive place; it bends, contorts, and reshapes to personify emotions and fears. Of course, this is jarring to everyone the first time, and perhaps even the hundredth time. But, it's also amazing and truly awesome when wielded expertly. Imagine your curiosity the first time you heard of 3d printing technology. The Hedge is all that to the Nth degree ― if you can imagine it, the Hedge can become it. The Hedge doesn't care about petty laws of physics. In the Hedge, you're as powerful as you are creative. Your strength in the Hedge equates to your strength of passion and will. So to the Lost, the Hedge can be just as empowering as it is dangerous.

                                        David A Hill Jr
                                        Freelance Writer
                                        Independent Game Designer
