    Fan Made Courts

    07-09-2014, 05:44 PM
    So I went and made topics for custom bloodlines, kiths, legacies, and shapeshifters, but now I'm curious to see what kind of factions players have come up with. To start us off, I'm posting up my own creation with the Bright Courts.




    Bright Courts: The Bright Courts are comprised of Dark, Light, and Shadow. Unlike the Seasonal Courts who voluntarily share power with the passing of the year, the Bright Courts have very conflicting ideals and are known to battle each other constantly in an effort to gain power and influence within the Free Holds. The name Bright Courts refers to being named after varying degrees of light.



    Dark Court: What's the best way to defeat your enemy? Turn their own power against them, or so the Dark Court's philosophy would claim. True Fae are some of the most twisted, cruel, and malevolent beings in creation, and the Dark Court seek to harness that evil for their own protection. Despite constantly being accused of being evil or being no better than the Gentry themselves, members of the Dark Court would disagree. While they are certainly not on good terms with outsiders, they do have the protection of their members at heart. Indeed, those who join the Dark Court may find that they are shockingly hospitable for a society who's passing often leaves death and destruction in its wake. The Dark Court is primarily concerned with the survival of the Dark Court, and anyone who refuses to join them is little more than a pawn at best. Becoming a member of the Dark Court means dedicating yourself to a life of self indulgence where you survive by your strength, wits, and the desire to do whatever it takes to survive.

    The Dark Court feeds off Selfishness, finding it much easier to harvest Glamour from those who care more about their own well being than how others might feel about the situation. The Dark Court is primarily concerned with the survival of the Dark Court. Everyone else is there to be used, exploited, and ultimately discarded when they are no longer of any worth. They coax society into acting on their selfish impulses such as greed, lust, anger, and so on. Members of the Dark Court often blend in with the criminal elements of society including local street gangs and organized crime, and they often set up their base in places where one can not afford to be generous if they hope to survive. Such behavior warrants them a very unpopular reputation among outsiders, but those who actually join the Court realize they're just trying to survive in a cruel and disorienting world. They will gladly endure the slings and arrows of their fellow Lost, so long as they can remain free another day. Because they have so little friends in which to call upon, the Dark Court members are surprisingly pleasant towards one another, helping each other out when they're not otherwise preying upon society's foolishness.


    The Dark Court contracts are Creeping Darkness, Encompassing Darkness, and Fading Darkness.


    Creeping Darkness are the subtle offensive powers of the Dark Court. It gives them the power to influence the minds of others by stoking their negative emotions and desires, making them more susceptible to carrying them out. Creeping Darkness is often used to make harvesting Glamour easier when wielded by less experienced Dark Courtiers, but in the hands of a veteran, it can be truly frightening.


    Encompassing Darkness are the blatant offensive powers of the Dark Court. It gives them the power to control darkness as if it were physical matter, attacking one's foes with tendrils of shadow being just one of its more basic powers. At the higher levels of mastery, some Dark Courtiers can even summon forth creatures made of living darkness to aid them in battle!


    Fading Darkness are the defensive powers of the Dark Court and primarily deal with stealth and trickery. Its name comes from the idea that there are times when it is best to avoid conflict rather than exasperate it. Fading Darkness can grant them the power to move unseen through the world, provided they do not attempt to interact with it in any significant way, and is primarily used as a means to escape from dangerous situations.





    Light Court: The antithesis of the Dark Court, who have dedicated their lives to preserving all that the True Fae would corrupt. Firm believers in the idea that the end doesn't always justify the means, the Light Court preaches that evil can not be defeated by resorting to evil methods. To this end, they strive to maintain whatever remains of generosity, compassion, and mercy they have left, and do not allow the memories of their durances to corrupt their spiritual purity. While the Dark Court may have a reputation for being universally evil, the Light Court has a reputation for being universally good. However just as with the Dark Court, their reputation is not all its cracked up to be. Those who join the Light Court may find that their dedication to purity can be rather stifling at times. While they are not fanatical zealots who threaten harsh punishments upon those who defy them, they also do not take kindly to selfish indulgences such as base lust, greed, anger and so on. Their society is like an order of pious monks who reject the temptations of the earthly realm in order to strengthen their spirits against the forces of evil. Those who's willpower is not strong enough for such a devout lifestyle often find they can not live up to the Light Court's expectations, and inevitably drop out.


    The Light Court feeds on Selflessness, and they find a much easier time harvesting Glamour from those who bare compassion, kindness, and understanding towards others. They shun violence at least when they are the only ones in danger, but when the innocent are at risk, they will sadly resort to such methods to protect them. Their goal is to strengthen the souls of not only themselves but that of society as well, making their spirits nearly unbreakable when the True Fae come to claim them. Hoping to lead the world by example, they give those around them something to aspire towards. When in their mortal disguises, they often take on forms of people who are skilled at helping others such as doctors, open minded clergymen, therapists, and guidance councilors.


    The Light Court contracts are Dim Lighting, Brilliant Lighting, and Soothing Lighting.


    Dim Lighting are the offensive powers of the Light Court and the name is derived from the idea that by resorting to violence, their light diminishes. Dim Lighting teaches the Light Court how to channel the forces of light into concentrated energy with the power to repel one's enemies. Only the highest levels of Dim Lighting possess actually lethal capabilities however, as killing one's enemies is only to be used as a last resort according to the Light Court's philosophy.


    Brilliant Lighting are the defensive powers of the Light Court and the name is derived from the idea that by embracing the way of peace, they can withstand any punishment. Brilliant Light channels the energy of the Light Court into protective barriers, and empowers the body to make it better capable of withstanding assaults. A master of Brilliant Lighting can be practically untouchable, evading or blocking the enemy's attacks until their spirit for battle is broken or satiated.


    Soothing Lighting are the support based powers of the Light Court and the name is derived from the fact that they use these powers to spread their philosophy. Soothing Light has abilities which can heal injuries, and disperse aggression from others. It enhances their social talents to find a more political way to resolve situations than swinging a sword at your foe.






    Shadow Court: Adhering to the philosophy that putting all your faith in one ideal leads to destruction, the Shadow Court takes a mixture of Dark and Light Court teachings. Most of them are those who abandoned the previous two Courts when they found they were unable to adhere to their extremist views on how to combat the True Fae. The Shadow Court sees their strict devotion to good and evil methods to be foolish. What happens if your philosophy fails, or your enemies find a weakness and begin to exploit it? The Gentry are unpredictable and incomprehensible, and the only way to fight against them effectively is to be as flexible as possible. Plans rarely survive first contact, so it pays to have contingencies in place, and then it pays to have contingencies for those contingencies!

    The Shadow Court feeds on Adversity, and they find a much easier time harvesting Glamour from those who have a strong will and creative mind, who are able to overcome the many challenges that life has to offer. They use their powers in an effort to help others grow and prosper by finding new ways to defeat their obstacles whether physical or metaphysical. Among the three Courts mentioned, the Shadow Court is perhaps the one that is most loosely organized. Each member of the Shadow Court follows their own set of moral code, ethics, and ambitions. This makes the entire court unpredictable, but is also makes them incredibly diverse. Since they do not have a unified goal in mind beyond simply being able to overcome all odds, the True Fae find it very difficult to prey upon their desires and coerce them into servitude. Their desires are random with each member, and it can be a time consuming and expensive process to seduce more than a handful of Shadow Courtiers at a time. Their lack of unity is their greatest defense against those who would exploit their society!


    The Shadow Court contracts are Faded Shadow, Warped Shadow, and Splintered Shadow.



    Faded Shadow are the defensive powers of the Shadow Court and they are named after shadows which appear to be transparent due to the intensity of light around them. As with Brilliant Light, Faded Shadows abilities have protective capabilities that can bolster the user's defense against enemy attacks, but unlike their counterpart, they have a slightly more offensive approach to simply creating an impenetrable defense.


    Warped Shadow are the offensive powers of the Shadow Court and they are named after the event of a shadow altering its size and shape based on the form and direction of the light that is shining upon it. Warped Shadow teaches the Shadow Court various shapeshifting powers from size alteration to sprouting claws and even changing their appearance completely for a certain amount of time. With the ability to alter their forms like shadow puppets, the Shadow Court are able to quickly adapt to almost any combat situation!


    Splintered Shadow are the social based powers of the Shadow Court and they are named after the event of when a person or object appears to have multiple shadows at once. Splintered Shadow grants powers of trickery, disguise, and stealth. The Shadow can appear to be in multiple places at once, or take on the appearance of someone else just to name a couple examples. Splintered Shadow is often used for when the Shadow Court does not wish to openly reveal their motives to others, and instead misleads them with false evidence.

              I like your ideas for the Bright Courts Nyrufa, especially how it's designed to house an odd number of Courts rather than even. I could see these Courts either developing from the Courts of Day & Night or developing alongside their Scandinavian counterparts, and the odd number eliminates the polarization of the power struggle presented by running a Chronicle with the Sun and Moon Court.

              I would make a couple of suggestion though, to me it seems like the Bright Courts lack the core theme of Beautiful Madness. Having Courts designed around the Good/Evil/Neutral archetypes can lead to some interesting Chronicles but I fear they wouldn't grasp the full scope of what Changelings existence is about. For instance the Light Court's emotion is Kindness, which is universally a positive emotion. It has the Beauty but where's the Madness? What's the threat to Clarity from a monastic existence besides separation from ones Changeling nature? And the Dark Court shares the same problem in reverse, Malice is unquestionably a negative emotion. I think the key to fixing this though lies in your solid idea for the Shadow Court and it's ever shifting dimensions. Why not have the Light and Dark Court's emotion and theme be more morally ambiguous but still encapsulating the essence of Malice and Kindness? For instance: Malice is a deep seeded hatred for a particular person or idea and Kindness is an expression on empathy that's usually non-beneficial to the giver of said Kindness. So why not instead of Malice the Dark Court's emotion be Rebellion, and instead of Kindness the Light Court's emotion be Selflessness? Both leave enough room for both righteous and wicked characters and I feel they still keep to the general theme that founded them. They're not perfect examples though but what do you think?

                          I wrote up a series of courts for a game that never went anywhere, 6 courts based around conflicting emotions and the state of the sun/moon.

                          Court of Hope, Guild of Tomorrows, The Sunrise Court- Fosters the belief that all things can be overcome, that the world can get better and that no hardship can last. The Court of Hope is a home to Lost who want to make the world better, or at least inspire others to keep trying. While they are known for encouraging others to fulfill their heart's desires, the Guild of Tomorrows is also known for their steadfast dislike for violence and control. The World keeps turning, the sun keeps rising, and every day is a new chance to do better than the day before.

                          Forlorn Court, Palace of the Lost, The Dusk Court- If Fae can come in and take anyone, what does that mean for the Changeling? Changelings are naturally better than human, more than human; why shouldn't we run the place? The Forlorn Court serves itself, feeding off of the hopelessness, the misery, of mortals and elevating themselves ever higher. Night commands the lives of those who fear it, why shouldn't we?.

                          Court of Joy, Temple of Laughter, The Noontime Court- Life is worthless without something to smile about. Laughter is the best medicine, and these Lost administer a heavy dose daily. They brighten the room like the sun, and refuse to let negativity into anyone's mind. These changelings don't hunger for joy, they thrive on it and spread it like wildfire. Every day free is a breath of freedom, so why not be happy?

                          Court of Anguish, Sanctum of the Suffering, the Midnight Court- Loss is everywhere. It infuses every one, human and Changeling alike. We have all lost something and we all suffer for it. Wounds may heal, but the scars linger, reminding us of what has been taken. These are the Changeling who take from others because they have been taken from; who suffer and in turn inflict suffering on others. Each evening shows in black and white the pain hidden by the sun's warmth.

                          Court of Dedication, Union of Effort, The Fading Sun Court- Hope without drive is simply dreaming and joy without a goal is just revelry. The court of Dedication is home to the drive, the will to shape and survive. One of the few courts to espouse military might, the Union preaches all members should be focused on improving themselves and encouraging others to do the same. As the day ends, the question is raised, was any moment wasted?

                          Court of Hunger, Fortress of Indulgence, Sinking Moon Court-Everyone wants something, and the Court of Hunger happily fulfills that urge. Humanity is defined by their excesses, and so too are the Lost. Every Changeling hungers for the emotions of others to survive, some of the Lost crave revenge on the fae that took them; both are welcome in the Sinking Moon Court. Soldiers and criminals, politicians and religious fanatics, the Fortress of Indulgence calls to all who want beyond their needs. The sun comes to banish the night, but don't you want more?

                                  Betrayer Courts: Not every Lost who flees from Arcadia has the intention of fighting back against the Gentry. Some decide it is better to join with an enemy you presumably can not defeat. The Betrayer Courts are factions who have chosen to ally themselves with the True Fae in some form or another. A Free Hold that belongs to the Betrayer Courts is a nightmarish place in which the Gentry are able to operate in a much easier fashion than they can in other Lost territories.




                                  Court of the Fey Folk: Also known as the Court of Ambition, or the Court of Aspiring Fae, the Court of the Fey Folk have realized that they actually desire to live within Arcadia as full residents. Some do this because they feel the mundane world is to boring and predictable, others do it because they have grown addicted to Fae magic and can not satiate their hunger for power. Others see the Gentry as the closest things to Gods they have ever met, whatever the case may be, a return to Arcadia is their desire. Those who are willing to speak with outsiders (or have been forcefully interrogated) explain that upon becoming True Fae, they will use their combined power to create their own realm inside Arcadia, where they can live and prosper as equals, rather than slaves. They hoard power, causing their Wyrd to escalate quickly within the Court, but at the same time their Clarity dwindles as they are driven deeper into madness. Thankfully, only the lower ranking members of the Court remain in the mundane world. The true leaders of the Court of the Fey Folk (those who have become powerful enough to ascend into True Fae) enter back through the Hedge and return to Arcadia to begin construction of their desired paradise. Some vanish never to be seen again in the mundane world. Others re-emerge as Keepers, seeking to drag more humans off to Arcadia! The desire to willingly become True Fae has made them quite reviled by other Courts, and with good reason, for they are glorifying a race that tortured them and destroyed their lives all for the sake of entertainment.

                                  The Court of the Fey Folk feeds on Passion, both in its lighter aspects and its very darker aspects. They find it much easier to harvest Glamour from those who are driven by impulses, whimsy, and who possess the fiery dedication to stand up for their beliefs. Passion can be good, igniting feelings of love, inspiration, and bravery. But it can also be quite dark, invoking acts of violence, ignorance, and vice. The members of this Court can be both good and evil depending on the individual, but all of them are traitors in the eyes of their fellow Lost.

                                  The insignia for the Court of the Fey Folk displays a regal image of a woman dressed in a flowing robe that drags on the ground behind her. A large pair of butterfly wings grows from her back, and she carries a jeweled staff and wears a gem encrusted circlet around her forehead. Her back is to the viewer, but she turns her head as if glancing behind her, head tilted in such an angle that suggests she is looking on in curiosity to the visitor.




                                  Court of Enthrallers: Also known as the Court of Ranchers, or the Court of Iron Shackles. The Court of Enthrallers have come to the conclusion that the best way to avoid being targeted by the Gentry is to offer them a "replacement" as an apology for escaping. Although Privateers may operate independently, The Court of Enthrallers are an organized slave trade who target mortals and other Lost, bringing them to the Goblin Markets and auctioning them off to the Gentry in exchange for being allowed to remain in the mundane world... for a while longer. The Gentry are always hungry for more souls, and so the Court of Enthrallers are constantly seeking new merchandise to offer up. Among all the Betryaer Courts, the Court of Enthrallers is among the least trusted and one of the most dangerous. Although they consider it to be risky business to target members of other Courts, they have been known to do so when fulfilling their quota of slaves becomes more difficult. It is much easier to organize the disappearance of a Lost than it is for an ordinary human, and so they make good scapegoats in a pinch. The Court of Enthrallers are also quite skilled at what they do, and do not simply haul a prisoner off to the market first chance they get. Instead, they lock them up and study them, taking note of their physical and mental health, their skills and talents, their ability to perform, and their sexual status (gay, straight, bisexual, virgin, male, female, est). After performing a thorough inspection, they begin conditioning them for servitude, using their talents to break down the subject's will and make them more compliant to taking orders. Once the subject appears obedient enough, they take them to market, informing customers of their traits and skills, in order to get the best deals out of them.

                                  The Court of Enthrallers feeds on Submission, and they find a much easier time harvesting Glamour from those who are cowardly, weak willed, and possess low self esteem. They are masters in the art of torture both in physical and mental facilities, plying their talents in the form of breaking a person's will. Hopefully somebody will arrive and free their prisoners from the processing stations of the Enthrallers before they are completely tamed!

                                  The insignia for the Court of Enthrallers displays a human dressed in rags, kneeling on the ground as a clawed hand holds a chained leash that is fastened around their neck. The human's wrists are restrained behind their back and their head is bowed in submission. The human in the insignia can be either male or female, and tends to change from one display to another.




                                  Court of Bridge Builders: Also known as the Court of Passage, or the Court of Hedge Trimmers. Being directly opposed to the Bridge Burners, the Court of Bridge Builders seek to create entrances to The Hedge, making it easier for the Gentry to come and go. Their philosophy is that if the True Fae see them as being useful tools, they will decide to keep them in the mundane world instead of drag them kicking and screaming back to Arcadia. The other Courts have considered them to be extremely dangerous, for their ability to create doorways and passages to The Hedge makes it easier for denizens of The Hedge to escape into the mundane world, and that makes guarding against them much more difficult. There have been times when the Summer Court managed to steer the Gentry's forces into a choke point where their numbers had to be funneled through and were much easier to fight off. Only for the Court of Bridge Builders to fashion a new entrance that circled around and gave the True Fae the chance to flank their enemies!

                                  The Court of Bridge Builders feeds on Surrender, and they find a much easier time harvesting Glamour from those who stop fighting back, and just allow the enemy to overtake them. They are very skilled when it comes to lengthy siege battles, both physical and mental. Wearing down the opponent's mind, rations, stamina, or health until they finally lose the will to keep fighting, and just accept what is to come. Depending upon the enemy they're facing, this can take a while, but then again, even the greatest construction crew can't build a bridge in a single day, at least not one that isn't held together with sticks and rope.


                                  The insignia for the Court of Bridge Builders features an elaborate golden bridge which is surrounded on both sides by a winding forest of thorny hedges. The hedge rises up from the ground, bends over the top of the bridge, and weaves together into an arch.




                                  Court of Silence: Also known as the Court of Death, or more appropriately the Court of Assassins. The Court of Silence are a Lost organization of assassins who receive contracts from the True Fae to find and dispose of particularly troublesome threats within the Freeholds. If the king of a Court is putting up too much resistance for the True Fae's liking, they issue a contract, and the Court of Silence will dispatch an agent to kill them swiftly and efficiently. As members of the Lost, they have a better understanding of how Changelings think than the alien mind of a True Fae, and therefor have a better chance of slipping in and out unnoticed. The Court of Silence tends to make its headquarters close to Hedge entrances for several reasons. First of all, it makes it much easier for them to establish contracts with True Fae since they're right near the gate. Secondly, The Hedge links to practically any place on earth, and if a contract is particularly far away, they can simply enter The Hedge and follow the path towards their destination, emerging half a world away in a relatively short amount of time. Finally, being close to The Hedge puts them close to the Hobgoblins, who sell many of the tools they use in their trade.

                                  The Court of Silence feeds on Trust, and they find a much easier time harvesting Glamour from those who are sure of their surroundings. Trust in their allies, trust in their beliefs... trust in their security! A target is most vulnerable when they believe they are safe, and so the Court of Silence cozies up to them and earns their trust, then promptly disposes of them when nobody's around to help.


                                  The insignia for the Court of Silence is a pair of puckered lips with a curled hand extending one finger upward in a shushing expression. Behind the lips appears to be a blood dripping knife, but it is too obscured to get a full view.

                                      Interesting ideas, and in a game where the organization of the True Fae isn't as noted in the books they might be useful, but I don't think they'd work in a base game, for a couple of reasons.

                                      First, the True Fae are all monolithic entities in their own right, unless it serves them to band into alliances at their whims - alliances which can break up just as unpredictably. To organize their followers in this sort of official capacity would require a degree of cooperation the True Fae just plain aren't suited for by temperament, as a species. They're an innumerable horde of individuals, not a bloc. It's unlikely for the fae captives of multiple Keepers to have banded together on their own behalf for the same reason - their loyalties are going to be pretty solidly set toward their keeper, or toward an opposing keeper who claims that he'll offer a kinder slavery if the Changeling betrays his boss, or to the concept of just surviving until they can escape. They generally wouldn't be *able* to collaborate on organizations this far-reaching, per my understanding.

                                      Secondly, the Courts were all forged by their founders by extracting an Oath from something - one of the seasons for the seasonal courts, a direction for the eastern courts, the sun and the moon for the diurnals, and so on. A given court system features courts based on a unified theme, some manner of dividing the power, which is part of what helps protect the freehold from the Others. These courts don't really have a unifying theme - they're four individual courts without counterparts.

                                      Lastly, this implies a degree of intentional antagonism on behalf of the True Fae toward escaped changelings that isn't necessarily going to be there. Any given True Fae might be upset that his slaves escaped, yes, but he might be proud that they were so clever (and want to show them off to the others) or just want to keep his cuddly toys close, even though they stop moving if he squeezes too hard. A given True Fae might send an agent or two out into the world to act in the ways any of these courts represent, but having enough out to make up a full Court would mean fewer servants and toys to serve his purposes in Arcadia.
