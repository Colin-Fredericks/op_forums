                                Originally posted by Gaius View Post
                                It's possible there have been changes, but in the outline, Dave laid out his plan.

                                Instead of each Contract consisting of 5 powers to be bought in order, each Contract is one power, with an experience cost relative to its dot rating. They aim to revisit all/most of 1e's, but part of the idea is to nix those that are lame or redundant. No doubt when revisiting older Contract powers, they'll buff/nerf/rework stuff so it feels like a worthwhile purchase for its cost. For Universal Contracts (most of them, aside from Court and Goblin varieties), each Contract has one or more Favored Seemings, for which they do something extra. If you're a member of a Favored Seeming, you get its respective perk for free when you buy the Contract; for a Seeming not your own, you can pay 1 experience to learn the associated perk from a member of that Seeming.

                                I think it's going in the right direction. I'd be interested in seeing some samples -- or hearing if things changed in any way -- but I'm in no rush.

                                Hmm... Though a logistical question does have me curious. If this is still the plan, I wonder how Seemings published after the core book will work with Contract Favored Seemings. I guess it would just be a matter of, after the Seeming's main write-up, listing any Contracts for which it's a Favored Seeming and what those extra perks do. Always cool to get new powers -- especially when they can breathe extra life into published Contracts -- though I wonder if it's possible to avoid needing to cross-reference multiple books.

                                On a similar note, I guess also amended to the 7th/8th Seeming write-up will be some examples of Kiths as flavored by that Seeming. I enjoyed those in the initial sample we got, so even more of them after the core book certainly wouldn't disappoint me.
                                It does sounds like it's going to be better than it was, but it's just an outline and as such very vague. I wonder if there's any chance we could get something akin to the Unleashings of DA: Fae?

                                            Originally posted by Gaius View Post
                                            I always like hearing where people are coming from, if you'd be comfortable and interested in breaking it down. No pressure, though.
                                            Sure sure. Excuse the stream-of-consciousness spew here.

                                            The short and long of it is, my mother is a non-functioning schizophrenic. My father fought in Vietnam, and had severe PTSD. I have a few significant issues of my own (without getting specific, because that can cause my family issues) I grew up with extreme examples of mental illness all around me. I dealt with words like madness, psycho, schizo, crazy, and not only were they hurtful, they were universally dismissive of the humanity. My parents weren't people; they were "crazies". I wasn't a teenager, I was "a risk to myself and others". We got defined by these things, and never by people that wanted to help, but always people that wanted to file us away and do their best to close their eyes.

                                            Madness, as a concept, is complex, and has a strained relationship with oppressive politics. It disproportionately affected women, minorities, and poor people, and was used as an umbrella description for any number of issues. Most importantly though, it was an excuse for unwilling captivity. It was an excuse to imprison damaged people.

                                            Most modern concepts of madness spring from those archaic ideas, and in games, primarily Lovecraft. Madness was something that privileged white men experienced when they had to face something outside their comfortable bubble. Unfortunately, there's just no good way to use the concept in madness without coming off as insensitive or condescending, because it's not really one thing; it's a dismissive concept used to blanket a great number of issues, and simplify them to the point of meaninglessness.

                                            You can see this evidenced in portrayals of Malkavians over the years. Often, their "madness" was a joke. Often, it was "scary". You'd hear people say, "Malkavians are great, but only when they're done right." Nobody agrees when that is. But most importantly, "madness" defined them. There's no way to "do it right" when mental illness is the single feature that defines a person. That, to me, is inherently "doing it wrong". You cannot "do it right" so long as what you're doing is diminishing a person's identity in favour of one trait. This is doubly important when it's one trait that's NOT actually congruent from person to person.

                                            If I'm going to touch on mental illness, and I most certainly am because it's important to me, the least I can do is give it the respect of specificity. It's never a punishment. It's never a consequence of being a "bad person". It's why I fought tooth and nail to get "Derangements" removed from the Storytelling System.

                                            Games, to me, are about what you do, not about the state you're in. They're about actions and consequences; trappings like mental illness are not actions, they influence actions. You can certainly act in response to mental illness. So my description of a game is never going to focus on things that have happened to you; it's always about things you are doing, and things you are experiencing and engaging with actively.

                                            Also, frankly, Changeling never really delivered on the promise of "madness". You can't really call it "madness" when everything is really quite literal. You're not "seeing things" if the dragon in front of you can actually eat you. You're just dealing with being able to see very real dragons.

                                            Changeling is not a game about unwilling captivity. Characters in Changeling are not unwilling captives. They were unwilling captives. To me, dealing with these issues and building a new life is significantly more compelling than wallowing in them. Changeling is a game of Beautiful Lies, and Bittersweet Truths. It's a game about sometimes choosing to embrace lies, because they're prettier and softer than the truth. And it's sometimes about choosing to embrace the truth, despite the fact that the truth won't comfort you just by virtue of being the truth. It's about wondering if people actually care when they tell you they understand, or if they're just trying to brush you under the rug. It's about telling the truth, and facing people willing to blame you for your victimisation. It's about that crushing fucking moment when you wonder if it's even worth telling yourself the truth, because you might blame yourself for the awful shit that happened. It's about choking down the truth when you threw up last night, because even though your loved ones care, telling them the truth is just going to mean more uncomfortable questions. It's about escaping Arcadia, and coming back to this boring shithole. It's about wondering if maybe you were better off staying there, then it's about beating yourself up because WHY THE FUCK WOULD YOU THINK THAT.

                                            Changeling isn't a game about madness. It's a game about whether or not the truth is worth it. Because the truth hurts, and there's no escaping that.

                                            David A Hill Jr

                                                    David, I feel you in more ways than one (so I hope you will excuse the random rumblings that follow). My family has consistently been plagued by war, revolution, crisis, and the marks these leave on the psyche and the family and community's shared unconsciousness. I am myself currently undergoing psychoanalysis, after I almost went catatonic a couple of years ago.

                                                    I have to agree with you that Derangements and the old Morality/Humanity system felt...very Victorian. It fit early nWoD but only in a caricature-esque. That said, I think that unfortunately, a single event (let alone a series of events in the slightly-darker-than-our-own WoD) can indeed affect you in ways that, at some point or another, feel unbearable. Someone struggling with depression faces the very real, very defining fight to get out of bed and do something, even if just for today. Even if tomorrow's struggle has already been lost. The stigma associated with mental illness survives and thrives today, even when people more broken than you are the ones looking at you and thinking "that's a lost soul if I ever saw one, and I want nothing to do with them". In this regard, I liked cWoD's flaw system, in that it allowed for characters to carry their personal scars into the present story. And by like, I mean it helped me deal with personal, psyche-related issues. It can be very distressing, not just to have grown up with mental illness and then see someone roleplay Malkavians recklessly, but also with a guilted sexuality, or a multi-ethnic background in a pretty much "homogeneous" (not really, but since the history textbooks say so, most people believe the official story) European country, to face a racist, sexist elder that can turn you to dust just because his mastery of Auspex allows him to see that the WoD is in fact a roleplaying game and you're the player behind the character, and he doesn't like you (I put a funny little hyperbole here).

                                                    That's what fascinates me with roleplaying games. The actions and choices, like you said. It helped me accept myself for who I am to such an extent that I am almost addicted to roleplaying (Vampire, mostly) at times of stress or crisis.

                                                    Now, on Malkavians, well... I like them. Having been the psychologically troubled (but ridiculously harmless) kid at school, it entertains that side of me that wanted to joke with people's weird, funny little looks. Yeah, fishmalks are distasteful (although, a friend of mine suffering with mental illness enjoys playing them immensely), but doing them right for me means using to your advantage that single trait other Vampires would use to step all over you. If Dementation allowed them to remove any and all mental illness from their psyche, I personally would only want to gain control over my issues, not eclipse them. Like the stories about how certain kinds of mental illness are actually a different perspective on reality... Violent outbursts notwithstanding, de-stigmatised mental illness would actually be a totally different story. That's the reason, I suppose, why the Magician tarot card reversed symbolises both the Magus and mental illness. That's what Malkavians felt like to me, I guess.

                                                    So I enjoyed playing them in the same way I enjoy the feeling of relief after I come out of my psychoanalytic sessions, even though they can be tense and almost traumatic at times. I could simply remove elements I consider sensitive from my games entirely, protecting myself from having to Storytell or roleplay uncomfortably close to home scenarios. But that feels too much like how contemporary psychology and psychiatry approaches mental illness today, treating symptoms rather than causes. To quote Sigmund, "Unexpressed emotions will never die. They are buried alive and will come forth later in uglier ways." and "Out of your vulnerabilities will come your strength.".

                                                    Apologies if my thoughts on how roleplaying has been therapeutic for me were insensitive or poorly expressed.

                                                    Edit: Your description of Changeling sealed the deal for me. Once 2nd ed. is out, I'm definitely giving it a try!
