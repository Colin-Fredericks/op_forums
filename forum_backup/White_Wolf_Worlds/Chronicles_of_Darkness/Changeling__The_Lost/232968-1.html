            Well, yes. There are some tweaks to be made, but they're just that -- tweaks to individual catches.

            Contracts I've actually found to be among the least troublesome and most effective supernatural powersets in my game. I mean, yes, some problem contracts (Cobblethought, or much of Eternal Spring), but they generally work fairly well. My understanding is that Contracts are purposefully weaker than equivalent powers that other splats get, but that this is compensated by:
            1) Cheaper XP cost -- any given Lost will have more contracts at hand
            2) Cheaper and easier activation -- Glamour is easy to get, Catches, especially for low level powers, are easy to use

            Most contracts are also a bit different in style -- there's relatively few contracts that are directly opposing other people. Instead, you have a lot of dice boosters, a lot of situational effects. Fae excel at stacking modifiers, and they can more easily affect large numbers of people than anyone else (observe Vainglory 5). Not to mention that there are some serious power-house contracts out there, such as the 5th Clause of Stone (one activation, increases strength, armor, health, and initiative).

            I wouldn't mind evening out activations, since yes, the Wyrd+Attribute thing is odd, but in my campaign it mostly means that Lost tend to run towards Wyrd 4-5 with their XP, and then don't worry much about activation pools (on the one hand, Wyrd is harder to raise, but on the other hand, raising Wyrd raises all of your Contract activations, at least outside of Court contracts, which is very nice).

            EDIT: Oh, and the name weirdness is intentional to give it more in the way of Fae flavor. Which I've kind of liked -- certainly more than Werewolf's tendency towards rather bland power names (Contracts of Stone or Contracts of Oath and Punishment sounds cooler than Gifts of Strength).

                                Well. The obvious response, from me, is that that's too powerful to be a supernatural ability period. Because turning someone into a frog is effectively an insta-kill ability (they're not dead, but they are tiny and helpless and unable to affect the world or stop you from stepping on them -- at least unless you let them retain some moderately un-frog-like abilities or skills). This is especially the case in White Wolf, where activation pools are heavily biased to the offense (the typical pool being attribute+skill+power versus two attributes or attribute+supernatural tolerance -- two contributors instead of three, and those two are more expensive to raise).

                                Thing is, insta-kills generally aren't very fun. Used by PCs, they either work (and end the battle, possibly anticlimactically), or fail (and you look dumb), and used by NPCs they make a character's survival dependent on a single dice roll. Even with White Wolf's fairly deadly combat system, most other forms of combat (actual damage and such) include more options and more dice pools, giving more agency to players on the receiving end and allowing for more complex and thus more fun battles when players are using them.

                                And yes, I know there are plenty of other insta-kill powers in White Wolf as is (most mind-control stuff, basically), and no, I'm not really enthused about it. But c'est la vie.

                                    Well, looking at the source material, "insta-kill" abilities (as you call them) that, of course, actually leave the target alive - Snow White's poison apple, Sleeping Beauty's needle, etc - seem to require preparation and circumstance. If, hypothetically, I were designing such a power, I would probably go with something similar to Mage's rituals, only more so. A lot of possible things that can grant a bonus, with several required to add up to a full effect.

                                    In this case, two common elements seem to be that the casting is a punishment for some (imagined?) offense - a way to induce respect - and that the transformation will wear off after a certain time, or under certain conditions. One could make it more difficult without magical tools and rituals; without an appropriately large slight; and without an appropriately simple turn-back-to-normal condition.

                                    Interestingly, a common variant seems to be transforming someone into an animal to get them out of the way, in which case they usually transform back once every 24 hours. Not sure why, but that could be another source of possible bonuses, to counter out the lack of an appropriate slight or other deficiencies in the casting..

                                    Acquiring enough die bonuses to turn the evil king into a newt could well form the basis of an entire chronicle or, more likely, a chapter or two (obviously, the difficulty depends on who the target is and what resources the characters have.)

                                    But it's just an example. The point is that it certainly seems like fairy-tale spells and powers tend to be more powerful than Contracts. Surely it's not impossible for an ability to be both powerful and fun, right?
