    Chronicle idea needing advice on population numbers of Changelings

    08-07-2023, 04:05 PM
    I stumbled across this in the "Changeling Plot Ideas" thread that I really like, and I was hoping for some feedback on an idea I would like to build overtop them.

    I picked this one to main:

    A true fae appears at the freehold to beg for aid. It seems that a member of the gentry somehow lured a creature into arcadia that fae magic has no power over, and it is now rampaging across Arcadia. Being half-fae, changelings are capable of traveling to arcadia, yet being half-flesh means they are able to battle the beast with more mundane methods. The thought of returning to arcadia or working for one of the true fae is insane, even by changeling standards. But the Fae claims that several of his number have been slain by this beast, and the Gentry are getting desperate. They are willing to offer nearly anything the changelings can imagine as payment, and will offer a (literally) iron-clad contract as proof of their sincerity. Do the motley let the fae enjoy their comeuppence? Or is there something they desperately desire that only a true fae could provide? Maybe they'd just like a closer look at this fae-proof beast...​
    And I also saw this which could incentivize the PCs pitching in and not just making themselves popcorn and watching Arcadia get chewed up and spat out:

    It finally happened, the Bridge Burners successfully destroyed the last entry point to The Hedge, thus ending the Gentry's threat to humanity forever. But things go horribly wrong when Changelings begin to slowly die without their connection to Fae magic. Realizing that their powers are waning and their people dying, the Autumn Court decides they have little choice in the matter. They have to tear open a new gateway to The Hedge. But to do so, they're going to need a lot of power, and the PC's are in charge of rounding up the Glamour needed for the ritual!
    I was considering making the "Fae-Proof Beast" something along the lines of an ironclad warship of similar construction and capability to a Admiral-Class or possibly a Victoria-Class Pre-Dreadnought Ironclad Battleship, crewed by Bridge-Burners with a unified, organized command structure with a dedicated line of succession and designated survivors in place to prevent a military decapitation strike.

    My rationale is that oceangoing ironclads of those eras and of the years leading up to them used wrought iron plates as armour, and built-up naval guns of those eras were generally made from wrought iron and fired common shells or armour-piercing shot; common shells were typically made of cast iron and usually filled with something like black powder to function as a bursting charge. So these would function quite nicely as Fae-plot-armour piercing shells and anti-fae armour. I've been working on preliminary mechanics for warships and I'd like to use them when they're done.

    I'd like to do a full War Campaign. To build a War Campaign usually I'll start with developing an Order of Battle. If we can safely assume that Iron doesn't respond to Changeling Contracts and we can't simply throw Tatterdemalion's Workshop, Dance of the Toys, and Changeling Hours at a bunch of old Museum ironclad ships, and iron high-pressure marine boilers won't be content to run off Hedgespun Coal, this means doing a lot of things the hard way with procurement, operation, and sustainment of naval assets. Heads-up that I consider this to be a highly desirable feature, not a bug because that added potential adds more moving parts to the operation, and allows me to allow for decision making at not only tactical, but also at operational and strategic levels by making the operation and sustainment of a large warship a large, multifaceted operation consisting of many different inputs that need to be very precisely orchestrated by the opposing force, none of which are more important than the other. This presents my players with many options they can take besides attempting a pitched naval engagement.

    The number of Bridge-Burners and possibly mortal allies that I can populate a military Order of Battle and civilian/industrial home front with is going to dictate the amount of manpower and resources they can pool together, so I've been wondering what your thoughts are on the Changeling population globally, and the percentage of which could be Bridge Burners that might be radicalized enough to take the fight to Arcadia itself this way.

    For scale, it would take a complement of more than five hundred officers, sailors and gunners to crew for a vessel the size of an Admiral-Class Ironclad Battleship which is a pre-dreadnought ironclad, and ideally a ship should have a "Blue" and "Gold" crew that can crew the ship alternately. The HMS Dreadnought, which came about ten years after the Admiral Class Ironclads and made them obsolete, cost about 1.8 million Pound Sterling to build ten years after the Admiral-Class, which adjusted for inflation is around 280 million Pound Sterling today, or around 350 million US Dollars. This is the combined approximate total annual income of nearly five and a half thousand average families in the United States as of today before taxes, which we can use to benchmark an approximate value of what it would take to afford the sail-away cost of a ship that made the Ironclads obsolete. I'll look for more precise figures on the Admiral-Class if need be.

    I'd be curious to hear your thoughts on the matter and on what size and scale of the Order of Battle you'd find most appropriate for the Chronicles of Darkness. I can usually scale up or I can scale down as circumstances require, although generally when it comes to war campaigns I prefer to scale up because scaling up helps to keep an industrialized war in an attritional phase, rather than risk it go to a decisive phase prematurely should a critical asset be lost.

          Meanwhile, I would like to observe that Victorian Lost has an entire chapter dedicated to the simple prospect of discovering a crew of bridge-burners are planning to run a train into the heart of Arcadia (by turning a train tunnel into a Hedge gate; the mortal passengers being considered acceptable collateral damage). One of the of the suggested consequences to move to if the players decide they're fine with letting this happen is that in addition to the destruction of the Fae realm at the impact site, the crash woke something up that even the Gentry think twice about awakening.

          Also seconding the "not everyone has to be a changeling" bit; Dogs of War is pretty direct about the Lost not necessarily doing well in direct military roles in any sense that couldn't be managed equally well or better by a mortal who doesn't have to worry about Clarity.

          It's difficult verging on impossible to guess at the global changeling population, not least because some number of them do not live on the globe, and the bridge-burner philosophy as a changeling thing tends to be more about burning the bridges from the mortal world by meddling with the dreams and belief and wonder that they posit attracts the Fae than about commandeering old war machines and somehow working out a means of getting them into the Hedge.

          Regardless of the general degree of population fluctuation among changelings, a headcount of 500 alone is larger than the largest described freehold on the books (unless I've overlooked or forgotten something from Dark Eras). To get that many bridge burners in one place by itself would necessitate some form of plot contrivance, to say nothing of resourcing them.

              Usually in a War Campaign what I like to do is prepare a large map, or even just a point-form list of locations filled with encounters, assets, objectives, and then create an Order of Battle, then allow my players to direct the war efforts at different levels, zooming in accordingly to focus in on specific areas as the need requires. Because this model means establishing a lot of constraints at the start, it's often easier for me to scale up than to scale down, so this is very good to know.

                      This is a wild overassumption, and not just because Courtly titles are not metered out by any actual government; changeling courts are coping strategies that are more playacting made serious by being backed with mystical bargains than anything with real geopolitical heft.

                      Recall that many of these people don't have their own legal identities when they emerge from the Hedge and all of them have a shaky relationship with perceptual fidelity — none of them should be expected to build and run a state without a lot of extra effort to have their new lives in order. Having someone who helps the new arrivals get fake IDs and cheap housing while they're finding their feet and other support group/safety net activity tends to be the extent of most freeholds' baseline legal structure, not least because almost every changeling comes from a background where being mired in lopsided agreements was something they were glad to be shot of.

                      If this idea's a no-go, that's alright, I'll keep it in mind going forward. It would be nice though to know what sense of scale people generally tend to expect in the genre, since I'm at a bit of a loss at how we're supposed to be dealing with kings and queens and courts and royalty without enough of a population to crew a warship, much less require that much of an organizational structure.
                      Like Hermit says, just because somebody is called a King doesn't mean they rule a country's worth of people. It is worth remembering, for good reason, that this is a franchise where vampires are a consciously social species and the most organized supernatural megastructure makes use of mundane society whenever a magical solution isn't required for a problem.

                      You don't need a lot of monsters for the monsters to make big things happen, because this is a setting rife with conspiracies who gather leverage on the entities who can get them the things they need. There is no Changeling Police Chief responsible for ensuring city law favors the Lost because it is much less trouble to have the regular chief of police owe you an ongoing debt for services rendered.
