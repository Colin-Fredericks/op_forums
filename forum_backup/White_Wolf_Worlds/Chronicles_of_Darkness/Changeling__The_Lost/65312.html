    The Lady California
    Mistress of the Hotel of Heartbreak

    Tommy was exhausted. He'd had a show earlier that evening: a sold-out crowd, as usual. The energy had been electric. But now, back at the Hotel, he just felt... empty. Drained. Outside, there was a crashing sound. Another television set, it sounded like. At least this one had missed the pool.

    Someone Tommy didn't know - a man in dark sunglasses, with a face hidden by a curtain of greasy black hair - stumbled over. He smelled of booze, pot, and sex. "H-hey, man," he said, his thick cockney accent and drugged slur making his voice nearly illegible. He pushed his long, stringy hair out of his eyes, revealing a line of injection pockmarks as his sleeve fell back. "Dya wan- d'ya wanna join us up on four hundred? There's a fuckin' orgy going, plenty of hot tail."

    Once, Tommy would have wanted nothing better than to down some whiskey, do some coke, and join in, but lately, it all just seemed so... so hollow. He shook his head. As the Brit staggered back up the stairs, Tommy turned to the black-clad man behind the desk. "When's checkout time in the morning?"

    The man smiled - or was it more of a sneer? Tommy wasn't sure. "Relax," he said in a smooth voice. "You can check out anytime you like." He chuckled. "But first, you need to try this." He pulled out a syringe, uncapped it, and handed it to Tommy. The liquid within shimmered an opalescent, radiant blue.

    The musician stared at it for a long moment. It seemed to call to him. He didn't move as the man walked out from behind his desk and deftly inserted the needle into Tommy's arm. Something that felt like liquid fire started flowing into his veins, the sensation swiftly replaced by a delicious, languorous pleasure.

    As Tommy let his head fall back, the night man watched. He turned back to his ledgers, shaking his head. "You can check out anytime you want," he repeated.

    "But you can never leave."

    There have always been legends of a creature who'll help an artist achieve greatness for a price. From the Leanan Sidhe of Celtic myth to the "crossroads devil" of the old blues artists, artists have always been able to find a muse for their work, if they're willing to pay the price.

    The Lady California (for that is how this Fae styles herself in the modern day) is one of such patrons. She finds those of talent who seek greatness, and then she grants their wishes. She feeds their flames of fame and glory until they lose what they love most - and then she returns to claim them. One woman killed her father in a drunk driving accident. A young man's childhood sweetheart, disgusted by his new lifestyle, left him, never to return. Always, soon after this loss, the Lady comes to take her due.

    The Lady's Realm is a dual place. First are the Stages, an endless number of theaters, arenas, colisea, and music houses. Here, her pet musicians put on frequent shows to packed houses of screaming goblin fans. The performances are always incredible... or else.

    In between shows, the Lady's captives are chauffeured in posh style to the Hotel, a lavish and mountainous place where any luxury imaginable flows like water. Alcohol, heroin, and other drugs both natural and un- are in frequent supply, and inhuman beauties of either gender make themselves available for carnal pleasures. The rooms clean and mend themselves: a musician could leave the walls smeared with semen and food, the television smashed into sparking bits, and the mattress thrown onto the lawn outside, then come back in a moment later to find the place fresh and ready for more carousing.

    The pleasures the Hotel offers are false and empty, though no less addictive for that. Long-time residents find their minds and wills sapped by endless parties, becoming barely more than automata going through the motions without real enjoyment. Some claim that these poor souls soon fade away, consumed by the Hotel itself.

    The Lady appears to mortals as a gorgeous woman with jet-black hair. Her clothing changes according to the trends of the moment, but it is always expensive and fashionable, usually black in color. The lady's eyes are black as well, with no color in the irises. In her own realm, the Lady rarely appears bodily. Some whisper (when they think the music will drown them out) that she *is* the Hotel. Others think that she simply walks unseen, supping on the souls of her captives.

    Changelings who escape from the Lady California are often Fairest, but Darklings come out of the draining parties nearly as often. Sometimes, those who seek the Lady's aid don't have enough talent to be worthy of her stages, but she takes them anyway. Wizened operate the sound, lighting, and stage effects of the show, and Ogrish roadies lug strange faerie instruments back and forth. Bits and pieces of the Lady's story have come to light in common music, raising the question of whether some popular musicians managed to escape and regain their lives after the Lady took them. While this isn't clear, most of the Lady's changelings find jokes that "Elvis never died, he was just taken home" to be in very poor taste.

          The following is a Keeper that I wrote back in 2007 for the now defunct shadownessence forums. It predates Autumn Fears and Equinox Road, so the stat blocks don't reflect the updated True Fae rules. If using them this Keeper possesses two titles: The Dowager, with which she animates her Actor, and The Illustrious Manor, which is invested in her Realm. Drop Empathy to 1 and Expression to 1 and up Subterfuge to 7. Her mein would have the Nightmare Aura, Lyrical Voice and Perfect Protocol blessings.

          The Dowager


          Quote: Oh, no. Your happiness with your new life is all the payment I need ... that, of course, and your consideration ...

          Background: To be beautiful and well-mannered is to be respected and desired. It is to be popular. People, all over the world and everyday, make private little prayers begging for the chance to be prettier, more charming, and to possess better style. Most of the time, the people making these silent pleas don't possess the means or the opportunity to better their graces. They remain the wallflowers of life, isolated from the social bustle of life by a bad wardrobe and poor manners. Other times, the unfortunate soul draws the attention of the Dowager.

          When she first takes notice of a human who needs her help, she brings them comforting words and a chance to change themselves. The Dowager offers beauty, grace and expensive clothes in exchange for nothing more than the right to act as their mentor in the field of etiquette.

          And she is good on her word. Soon, her pupil finds that his life has become more successful. As long as they follow the Dowager's advice, one never has to worry about being unpopular or poorly dressed ever again. All he has to do is follow the rules laid down by the Dowager, rules that often involve strict curfews and esoteric prohibitions.

          As time goes on, these weird rules grow stricter and stricter until one day the Dowager's ward cannot help but break one. It could be that he was a minute late leaving a school dance, or that he forgot to take off his shoes before stepping into a house. Regardless of what the transgression was, the result is always the same; by breaking her rules the human has not only proven that he no longer needs the Dowager's help but that he is also ungrateful.

          When the Dowager goes, so do her fey glamours and all of her blessings and the victim finds himself thrust into a crumbling situation. His cool new friends stop talking to him, his cool clothes start to fall apart, and his cool demeanor just doesn't seem all that cool anymore. For many, this sudden shift back into mediocrity is jarring and traumatic, leading to depression and sometimes even suicide.

          Some victims, however, seek out the Dowager to beg for her aid in their lives again. These doomed souls the Dowager drags through the Hedge to her Faerie estate called the Illustrious Manor. There, she sets them to slave as servants and "entertainment." Everything in the Illustrious Manor is beautiful, and the Dowager does not take well to ugliness or ill-taste. Those trapped in her realm are forced to be as perfect and pleasing as possible, lest they suffer the rage of the Dowager.

          Description: As is common for one of the True Fae, the Dowager's form is as mutable as she desires. More often than not, however, she appears to her victims as a stately mature woman of impeccable taste and class befitting the culture that she is currently immersed in. She is beautiful, in the way that aristocratic matrons tend to be, and she moves with otherworldly grace.
          When she is angry, the Dowager's elegant facade transmutes to that of a nightmarish governess with steely grey hair and long black lacquered talons. To stare into her hollow black eyes is to know every insecurity and imperfection that one possesses and the icy disdain that is evident in her terrible visage is enough to make the most confident soul feel weak.

          Storytelling Hints: When first encountered, the Dowager seems to be infinitely kind and charitable. She is understanding and empathic when it comes to the sorrows that plague her victims and she seems completely geniune when she offers them her assistance. Once her hapless prey agrees to her arrangement, things begin to change. The Dowager grows more and more controlling and increasingly critical of her victims graces and appearance. Inevitably, though, the poor human victim fails to meet the ridiculously high standards set forth by her "fairy godmother" and in punishment is dragged away to the Fair Lands.

          Above all things, the Dowager treasures beauty and decorum. She is offended by the sight of ugliness and bad manners invite her wrath. She is clever and manipulative, but always gracious. Even when she is inflicting pain. The Dowager is one of the most apt Keepers in Faerie, and she is always attended by a retinue of Loyalist changelings of the Fairest and Wizened seemings whose souls she utterly holds in thrall.

          Mental Attributes: Intelligence 3, Wits 6, Resolve 5
          Physical Attributes: Strength 2, Dexterity 3, Stamina 2
          Social Attributes: Presence 6, Manipulation 6, Composure 7
          Mental Skills: Academics 3, Investigation 5, Politics 6
          Physical Skills: Athletics 2, Brawl 4 (Talons), Larceny 3
          Social Skills: Empathy 6, Expression 5, Intimidation 5, Persuasion 4, Socialize 7, Subterfuge 3
          Merits: Eiditic Memory, Encylopedic Knowledge, Barfly, Inspiring, Striking Looks 4
          Willpower: 9
          Virtue: Charity
          Vice: Pride
          Initiative: 10
          Defense: 3
          Speed: 10
          Health: 7
          Wyrd: 7
          Contracts: Dream •••, Hearth •••••, Vainglory •••••, Fleeting Spring ••••
          Glamour/per turn: 20/7

          • Immortal Flesh: As one of the True Fae manifested in the
          mortal world, the Dowager can shrug off some of the lesser
          slings and arrows of the mundane. Bashing damage does
          not affect the Dowager at all, unless delivered by a cold iron
          bludgeon. Cold-forged iron causes aggravated damage, should
          someone be skilled enough to strike her with such.

          • Ruled By Passion: The True Fae are entities almost
          embodied by their passions and vices, with higher morality
          and self-denial little more than a whim to them. For them,
          passion is virtue, and the denial of passion an affectation.
          Their supernaturally amoral nature is reflected in an
          inverted ability to regain Willpower. The Dowager regains one
          Willpower point from indulging her Virtue, but refreshes her
          whole pool when satiating her Vice.

              I let my Changeling player come up with some of her own ideas on her time in Arcadia. This is a little blurb she wrote...


              "Every new mortal brought to the Drosselmeyer's realm was made to dance. If an ankle or foot were not strong enough, they were cut off with white-hot scalpels and replaced with delicate ones of clockwork and sculpted glass, forever, effortlessly en pointe. If backs were not straight enough, or strong enough, violent hands wrapped us in prisons of metal, frames of wire. In the end, only my right hand had enough grace that I kept it.

              Perhaps The Drosselmeyer took me because he liked my eyes. He would have "fixed" them if he had not. Later, when he would break my fellows--throw away old toys that no longer pleased-- it was then I learned to make new hands and feet, legs and eyes. Hearts and souls and mouths and tongues. My eyes saw the way. And when my fellows were broken beyond repair, in shame and desperation, I took their grace and made it my own. I canabalized their bodies and distributed what remained to my fellows... Oh but I saved the best for myself. And I ran away with it."

                      The Land of Ash and Shroud

                      Take a trip to the catacombs beneath Paris, the Sedlec Ossuary, Rochester's sprawling Mt. Hope Cemetery. Wander around, get lost among the human remains and monuments. If you take the right turn at the right time, with a stick of incense or freshly-picked lily in your pocket, you might come across something--a mausoleum not on the official map, a door set back in a corner made of some striking material, or what appears to be a newly-dug grave resting at the foot of an ancient and weathered tombstone. Go on through, it's alright. Everything's going to be okay. You're only apprehensive because modern society has taught you that death is something to fear rather than embrace.

                      Taking up the invitation will put you on a one-way course to the Land of Ash and Shroud, a Fae Realm as tranquil as it is labyrinthine and desolate.

                      The Land of Ash and Shroud is a land of sprawling hills, manicured lawns, azure skies, placid reflecting pools, along with endless rows of graves, mausoleums, memorials, chapels, columbaria, charnel houses, and bone pits. Tombstones sit side by side inscribed in Cantonese, Gaelic, Basque, Mingrelian. The air is filled with silence, broken only by the rustling of leaves in the wind, the cawing of a crow, or...what is that faint scratching sound? Is that a bell ringing?

                      Sometimes a visitor might come across a funeral in progress, a body being laid into the ground or going into a crematorium. Best not to interrupt. It might be yours.

                      If you don't want to die of starvation, wandering endlessly through the cemetery maze, you should probably seek out the Director and ask for a job. He's a somber man, dressed in all black (though sometimes, for reasons unknown, he appears in a white suit). It's a big graveyard, and there are always lawns to mow, statues to polish, graves to dig. Sometimes he decides you'd make a nice willow for the Garden of Renewal. There's a rumor that the Director's been stealing copper, so maybe you can make an offering of it to gain passage out of the Land. Or maybe he'll just get offended.

                      Despite the morbid nature of the Land of Ash and Shroud, changelings of every Seeming have been reported. True, there are plenty of Gravewights (to commune with the spirits of the dead), Lurkgliders (to act as guardian statuary) and Antiquarians (to keep records of the interred), but Darklings are far from the dominant strain. Ogres make excellent gravediggers and pallbearers, and one Witchtooth claims she was charged with keeping out certain intruders. "Not human ones, though they may have once been human. They were like ghosts who weren't welcome in that hallowed ground." The Wizened trim shrubs, fashion coffins, and preside over funerary protocol. There are Fairest mourners, eulogists, and decorations. The Elementals are varied: a Woodblood topiary, a Fireheart pyre, a Waterborn pond, a Stonebones grave marker, a Blightbent miasma. Beasts are the rarest Seeming from the Land; their wild natures clash with the cemetery's peace. When they are produced, they're often of the Windwing, Skitterskulk, or Riddleseeker kiths.

                      Whence come the Land's dead? As far as outside observers are concerned, most of the graves have always been here and will always been here, bits of backdrop encoded into the realm. Sometimes other Fae send their dead to be buried here. Changeling slaves often end up in one of the paupers fields, but a favored servant or lover might receive a lavish farewell and wind up in a grand mausoleum of marble and gold. Anyone who dies in the Land of Ash and Shroud will, obviously, get their own sendoff, the grandeur of which is often (but not always) in proportion to their standing, both with the Director himself and in whatever makeshift community to which they belong. Some changelings, desperate for any sort of escape, have dug up some of the graves to find the coffins below empty, or full of things like oranges, rubies, or swan feathers. Some of the tombstones have nothing below them at all. There might be a second labyrinth beneath the cemetery, connected to the surface through mausoleums, trap doors, false graves, and the like. What's down there is anyone's guess.

                      Do not believe the reports about a tower of lead that can sometimes be glimpsed beyond the fence when standing on the Land's highest hill. It is a lie.

                                      Here is a Homebrewed Keeper that I came up with with an interesting background.

                                      Vellos, The King of the Crimson Flame, Ruler of Infernus

                                      There once was a man (whose name is forgotten in time) with the heritage of the devil in his veins who used his dark heritage to fight the creatures of darkness. One day his exploits attracted the attention of a keeper known as Valendria, the Lady of Leaves. She quickly became infatuated with this man and for the brief time took human form under the guise of another hunter and slowly deceived his heart into lust and love. Valendria fooled the man into binding himself to a pledge and forced him to return to her realm in Arcadia, the Valley of Leaves. In Arcadia the man's infernal heritage mingled with the glamor of Arcadia, transforming into a creature representing a demon of mythology. The man took the name Vellos and at first proved himself a loyal servant to the Lady of Leaves, often being a warrior in battle against a number of her enemies. But as his powers in Arcadia grew, he had ambitions beyond being a mere servant of Valendria and catching her in a time of weakness, cut out her heart and devoured it, destroying the last of his humanity and becoming one of the gentry.

                                      Vellos with his newfound power, reshaped the realm turning it into a hellish dominion. The lushful forested realm burned away in a massive conflagration, leaving the realm as a scorched wasteland of volcanoes, coal, ash, brimstone, obsidian and magma. After reshaping his realm into a hell of his own making, he set about capturing humans to turn into changeling slaves. Those captured by Vellos were put to various jobs such as mining the mountains for gemstones, feeding infernal bonfires throughout the realm or being servants in his obsidian fortress. He also required some to be his military force as his ascension to demonhood as he calls it made him a good deal of enemies. Those captured by Vellos typically take on a fire-aspected bent. Fire based elementals and ogres are the most common changelings found, although occasional others are created as Vellos often needs a wide variety of servants with various skills. Unlike many Gentry though, he still has vague memories of the mortal world and involves himself in mortal affairs more often than many other Gentry. He does occasional trips to the mortal realm to set up a number of holdings there both to entertain himself and expand his power bases. At times he uses changelings as agents to administer his affairs and is not beyond making use of mortal agents through deception and bribery.

                                      Infernus

                                      Infernus is as close to a literal fictional hell as one can muster. The place is a place of fire and brimstone. The smoke from the the countless fires and the volcanoes cough up so much smoke that the sun never shines though the black roiling clouds overhead. Not to say there is not light though as although the realm is perpetually dark, numerous fires burn bright enough to light large tracks of land. Massive volcanoes spew copious amounts of lava, creating entire rivers of red magma. What little water there is in the realm is provided by Hot Springs and Groundwater that nonetheless is always boiling and always tainted with the taste of sulfur. Food for the occupants is scarce but Vellos knowing the value of living slaves usually does not allow his people to starve to death. Various trees of coal grow in the realm, providing Flame Fruit and other goblin fruits and in a bountiful enough supply to feed the realm. Nevertheless, Mortals do not live long in Infernus and unless they "Adapt" to the realm by becoming a changeling the food and water of the realm will end up killing them. The realm has a vast amount of mineral wealth however and many changelings toil in the mines to extract these materials. There is often a high turnover rate as many changelings are either worked to death or die from mining accidents. When their numbers dwindle, Vellos will often do a recruiting trip to replace those that have been lost. At the center of the realm is the Palace of Obsidian, a massive fortress crafted out of obsidian shaped by Arcadian Magic. It lies in the center of a massive lake of lava making escape a difficult and dangerous proposition. The Crimson King himself sits on throne of Rubies that constantly burn a bright crimson colour. He often induges himself in numerous luxuries depending on his fancy at the time.

                                      Vellos

                                      Vellos himself is a 7 foot tall monster who looks like the Devil Himself (Well at least in Fictional Biblical terms). He has cloven hooves, a tail, massive bat-like wings, red skin, a bestial face and two massive goat horns. When disguised in human form he appears as a tall man, dressed in a white suit with red trim, a top hat, a ruby cane and fiery red hair. His favorite trick is to trap people in engineered fires and appear to them, offering them a deal in exchange for service. Those that are resistant find themselves getting burned with fire until they either agree to the terms or die. Either way Vellos sheds no tears over the dead as there is plenty more worthy recruits to find.

                                          Heh, that's pretty great. One of my players just dealt with his Keeper, who was not too dissimilar (in terms of source material, anyway). Actually, this is certainly the thread for it, so:

                                          The Devil Who Haunts the Souls of Hypocrites

                                          The religious leader who preys on his followers' faith. The cop who harms the people he claims to serve. The politician who claims to stand for the people, and yet undermines them at every turn. These are the Devil's prey.

                                          He is not always horned, with red-skin and black wings. This is just the Devil's preferred form for dealing with prey of certain faiths. In other cases, he is more than happy to tailor his appearance to come down as whatever form of divine retribution his chosen slaves-to-be most fear. What remains the same is his taste for hypocrites. Those that say one thing, and do another. Before he takes them, he watches them. Every now and then, they will see him spying from a mirror. It is necessary for his work, for the Devil wants to leave the world a better place.

                                          The Devil's tortures are wretched, to be sure, subjecting his prisoners to an eternity of just punishment. They witness the results of their actions, the tragedy it brings, and experience it for themselves as the Devil conjures scenarios for them to act out. At other times, they suffer under whatever punishment seems most appropriate for their religious beliefs. Some Changelings become Elementals of fire and brimstone, lashed by tongues of flame as they burn in a Christian Hell. But this is not the end to it. When the durance ends, and the Changeling bursts gasping and finally free from their captivity out of the Hedge, they discover that the world has not noticed their disappearance. Something even worse has happened.

                                          A Fetch, those wretched half-men spun from a scrap of shadow, has taken their place. Not only that, but their Fetch has become a better person than they ever were. This is completely genuine. The Devil, amused by this final, cruel irony, has made the Changeling's replacement into a true hero. Someone who will not only fix the wrongs their Changeling once committed, but will go on to achieve greater and better things. The corrupt cop returns to find his Fetch has dismantled the ring of his old contacts, arrested them, and gone on to serve as a distinguished and honoured member of the community. The politician has delivered his promises, and exposed the business leaders who once had him in their pocket to the fury of the public. The Fetch is honoured and praised, and as the escaped slave looks at their Doppelganger's happy face they can hear the laughter of the Devil.

                                          You should have stayed gone, he whispers in the Changeling's ear. They are all better off without you.
