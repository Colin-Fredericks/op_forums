        I guess what I mean is, what should I be looking for when making a seeming effect for contracts? I also tend to struggle with Elementals as I can only think in terms of stuff like "the Hedgewall takes on properties of the Sprite's element"
        I've been systematically going through all the contracts on-and-off and adding in Seeming benefits for those that don't have them yet (because it's fun to do and my players never mind it either ) and I know your struggle! It's very, very easy to get in the trap of "Well, beast should do something with animals... I guess?" or "Well, elemental should do something with some element..." but after a certain point those very specific ideas are too confining and we need to open it up a bit.
        When looking at the official Seeming bonuses, you'll see that no matter what "umbrella" you come up for any particular Seeming, one Contract or the other will assign a Seeming benefit that you would give to Seeming X under a rigid adherence to theme to Seeming Y instead. This tells me that there is a lot of leeway in assigning these bonuses, and a perfect fit is impossible to achieve.

        There's also various Contracts that have similar uses with some pros/cons, so when you start giving Seeming bonuses to all of those it's really hard to not homogenize them, but that's another story. ^^

        Some suggested themes for each Seeming:

        - Beasts: Instinct, pack dynamics in a broad sense (benefiting members of your "pack", or bonuses related to taking out/challenging the "alpha dog" of a group), senses, tracking, animals (both actual animals as traits and behaviours linked to animals), mobility, the Hedge. Tenacity (in a "won't let go once I got the scent", sort of fashion.)

        - Darklings: Shadows, misdirection, fear, etherealness, visions, mysticism, the sinister, knowledge ("secrets"). Corrossion, rot, etc... Sinister things.

        - Elementals: Malleable, fluid, elemental. Objects and scenery want to help, even the air will help carry sounds you wish to hear to your ears, sort of stuff. Powerful: if I want a Seeming to just be able to pump more juice into a Contract and get a more explosive result than anyone else, Elemental is going to be a strong contended for where that bonus should go. Unrelenting, unstoppable, energetic. Plants. Timeless. Seldom subtle, but lots of bang means lots of buck.

        - Fairest: Beauty, charisma, inspiring. Emotions, particularly positive ones, but it's seldom hard to justify a Fairest bonus for a wide range of emotions. Command, you decree, he/she/it/the world obeys. Sentimentality. Shoe-in for many bonuses that help dominate social situations.

        - Ogres: Toughness. Protection. Taking one for the team. Brutality. Intimidation (shared with Beast, though it manifests a bit differently. Also different from the "fear" of Darklings). Mental health. Healing. Strength. Straightforward: sometimes it just is that simple, even if a non-Ogre has to try and make it all complicated. Brute force. Bodyguard. Largesse and largeness.

        - Wizened: Creativity, ingenuity, cunning. Knowledge, of all sorts. Surprisingly (to some people) manipulative, and much more active on a social level than you'd think, often a power-behind-the-throne type, chessmastering from behind the curtains while others are seen as the "face". Changing one thing into another, which can be literal "things" (objects) or much more vague concepts. A special connection to the things they made themselves. Complexity. Analysis.

        Some Contracts are harder than others for this or that Seeming, but I find Beast the overall hardest, and you really need to let go of "animal" as the core idea often to make it work, which is good! Elementals can be tough on some Contracts (especially if you hold too tightly to the "elements" idea), but write themselves on others. Ogres can sometimes be tough if already written examples (often for Beasts) muscle (hehe) into their territory a lot, but often not too bad. Darklings, Fairest and Wizened tend to go quite smoothly.

        I find that once you add more bonuses, you'll at some point need to add a little bit of clarification of how different bonuses work together, too. I see it as good practice to ensure that a member of a certain Seeming might be very interested in learning 2-3 of the other bonuses due to synergy with the bonus they already have. If members of all Seemings would love to get their hands on almost all of the Seeming bonuses other Seemings get for a certain Contract, then you've generally got a winner. ;D
        It's often not possible though, so just making sure at least some of them synergise is a good start. ^^

        I shared what I had for Crown a bit less than ten months ago, but I've devoted a lot of time to other gamelines so haven't been able to finish it since then (though I have Jewels and Mirror too now, but I have to go over them again sometime to see how ready-for-critique they are). Here's the thread: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...eeming-bonuses

                                                Originally posted by Falcon777 View Post
                                                Taidragon Yes, it's true that the term has been used that way, but really it shouldn't be. If the reason you're called a sorcerer is because you know a lot about the supernatural then you need to get your title fixed.                                                 Honestly, though, your reply is ignoring the main thrust of my question: how many contracts must a changeling know before they are considered magically powerful and wise in the ways of Fae magic? Neos has his take of 7 common or 5 royal, but that's only concerning a single regalia. How many regalia must a changeling "master" by that definition to be considered magically gifted/strong/informed?
                                                Your question was how many contracts a Changeling needed to be a “decent sorcerer”. That is not the same as being magically powerful and wise in the way of Fae magic - sorcerers would require a good grasp on it, yes, but the point of the Autumn court sorcerers is that they are knowledgeable outside of contracts, of things like abjuration, making an environment more habitable for a spirit or ghost to manifest or to banish them. The difference between a mere human occultist and a Changeling sorcerer is that a human is for the most part in the dark on what a supernatural being is actually like, stuck with a human perspective and resources. Changelings innately have an advantage because they not only know of the supernatural pretty intimately, but they have the means and tools to disconcert truth from fiction. Clarity and Kenning both grant large advantages on their own for ferreting out details about the supernatural, hob goblins often have loads of things that a Changeling would want including tomes of lore from other beings and their Kiths can easily grant them advantages in themselves (Helldivers by themselves can access different realms and speak to spirits/ghosts through their Trickery). Autumn in general has this lovely habit of documenting things so that others in their court (and those with Goodwill) can research it and become knowledgeable in these things, hence why Autumn courtiers tend to be the sorcerers. Both occultists and sorcerers dabble in the occult beyond their natural reality, but the difference is that Changeling sorcerers have the context to actually know what they’re looking into - if your definition of Sorcerer is simply “can do magic”, you’re including pretty much an entire freehold and thus losing distinction.

                                                And even if you’re looking to ignore all of that, contracts are still a bad benchmark to go off of. Being wise to the ways of fae magic doesn’t result in having lots of contracts, nor does having lots of contracts mean you’re wise to the fae - it just means you’ve spent lots of experiences and have made a bunch of deals with the entities that empower your contracts just as others are. Being wise to the ways of the fae means that you’re knowledgeable about how the Hedge functions, about Arcadia and the nature of Gentry Titles and Names, about Bastions, dreams and Goblin debt. Contracts help, but how many someone has doesn’t denote their experience and wisdom. You could have an expert in searching the Hedge who never went past having the initial six contracts you start out with simply because those six contracts are exactly what they needed to become an expert in that field. Having tons of contracts may make you powerful (depending on the selection), but wise/knowledgeable?

                                                To put it simpler, you’re asking about how many cars someone needs to own to be wise and knowledgeable about automobiles, which ignores what experience they’ve had, how long that experience is in and what they specialize in. Having different cars helps in that, but you could have had the same beat up truck for most of your life and still be a better master at maintaining vehicles than some rich yahoo with a dozen sports cars because you’ve worked in several different garages with a half dozen tutors and hours upon hours of experience under the hood.

                                                What you’re looking for is what contracts would serve to exemplify what the Changeling is wise and powerful in. As has been noted, Changelings have rather a lot in their world to know and learn about and become wise in, and no matter how much time and what durance they’ve had they’re going to have to specialize in what they know. The number of contracts merely denotes how much experience they’ve devoted to the deals that make them able to do various things that relate to that wisdom.

                                                        Originally posted by Taidragon View Post
                                                        Apologies for the double-post, but the Wizened Blessing has me confused. It notes that if you’re using the proper tools to turn one object into another “ This counts as a five-die bonus for purposes of determining the required successes.” Does this mean that the player gets +5 to the roll for making the transmutation, or that they take a -5 penalty to it... or that they simply have to get 5 more successes to succeed on the build action?
                                                        I think they messed that up a little, since the Build Equipment rules just use an Instant Action lasting up to a few hours, not extended rolls, so you don't need multiple successes.

                                                        Anyway, I believe this is what they're referring to:
                                                        Built-In Penalty: The roll always suffers a penalty
                                                        equal to the intended equipment bonus.                                                         Each other
                                                        intended benefit, like a new function, an increased
                                                        trait, or access to a resource, counts as a one-die bonus
                                                        for this purpose, as well as to determine whether
                                                        to roll in the first place. Weapons impose a penalty
                                                        equal to their weapon modifier; armor imposes a
                                                        penalty equal to its highest armor rating (general
                                                        or ballistic). Penalties can’t exceed −5.
                                                        So using the Blessing counts as trying to make a piece of equipment with a five-dice bonus (which doesn't mean the end product gives an actual five-dice bonus, it's just a unit of budgeting in the Build Equipment rules) and therefore you take a -5 penalty to your Build Equipment roll. On a simple success you transmute the material. Actually even on a failure you still do, but it'll be Fragile or Volatile.

                                                        Note that with the blessing you can only turn one material into another material (straw into gold) not one object into another object (fridge into microwave). Just pointing it out due to your phrasing. ^^
