    It’s “Off Week” again, and time to cover the other two Courts. I have two journals in had, 3 coming down the pipe.

    As before, they are spoilered as some info is not known by some players, though admittedly it is a little better known now it’s mostly for consistency sake with the previous document. As before, It is the original handout plus bonus material, so there may be a slight tone change.

    Summer Court Handout

    Some stereotype the Summer Court as religious fanatics, but that is not true. Some are religious, some not, but they enjoy the trappings nonetheless. The Summer Court is based out of the Old Methodist Mission just outside Santa Monica between Santa Monica and Malibu. The Church is entirely run by Changelings, and has no regular human congregation but sometimes hosts gatherings.

    Viscount Augustine is the monarch of Summer, a powerful Ogre Gargantuan Render. Known for his gold armor, he is named for the Saint who, among other things, has his mother’s name attached to the city, St. Monica. Viscount Augustine is Christian but not terribly fanatical. He sees the Gentry as abominations, but Faerie he does not judge so long as they do not side with the Gentry.

    Joan may be the best-known member of the Summer Court. A Fairest Bright One and Soldier, she is fairly religious and very popular, and often pursued. She tends to distrust the Spring Court, given the number of members who have tried to relieve her of her virginity, and otherwise pledged to steal her heart. There is even a betting pool. Fortunately, Joan is a formidable woman as well as a master of melee weapons, which with her beauty only has more people pursuing her.

    The Summer Court is on guard against the Faerie but also the chaos in LA. Santa Monica and Malibu have more Faerie coming and going than they can track, but at least the Courts are organized. The chaos in LA and Hollywood, with the Day and Night Courts closer and mad Hollywood Seasonal Courts farther, are seen as influences to keep out. The Summer Court is not concerned about the Directional Courts, who seem disinterested in expansion and who dislike the same courts the Summer do for the same reasons. They do not necessarily want to bar members of the Courts from the city, but distrust them, and come down hard on any attempt to establish a Court.

    Viscount Augustine and Joan are in favor of the Cabaret, of course, but worry others, especially the Winter Court, have agendas with it. They have warned Ace to not trust Master Darke for any reason. Also to watch out for Vampires, as they know some are out there and may be driven West by misguided hunts that are all the rage in Hollywood right now. They may look for new feeding grounds. While Augustine is not crazy about vampires, he has bigger problems than wanting to provoke them.

    While at Court you briefly met Old Billy, A Beast Steepscrambler and Riddleseeker. Old Billy is, well, old; he is apparently well over 90, which makes him a decent advisor to the Summer King despite his being a Beast. Old Billy seemed to see a lot of potential in you, so don’t prove him wrong.

    There is also a rather aggressive Beast Roteater/Broadback named TJ. You don’t know much about TJ just yet, just that he is around, an enforcer, and does not seem even remotely Jesus oriented.


    Winter Court Handout

    The Winter Court in Santa Monica is not as powerful as it is in many places. Let me rephrase that, it is not obviously powerful compared to some places. In fact, its tendrils are all over both cities.

    The base of the Winter Court is a local rehab center, which is ironic given the Court’s role in drug trafficking. Drugs are an excellent source of sorrow, after all, possibly the biggest in tinseltown. The Santa Monica Community Rehabilitation Hospital makes a pretty good cover, and lets them keep an eye on how much the local drug use is, or is not, in check.

    Master Darke is the local Winter King. He is a Manikin Blightbent, looking like a man in a dark suit, fedora and sunglasses smoking heavily. His features seem almost plastic, and he never raises his voice. Master Darke is a quiet voice when the monarchs meet, but when he talks, they listen. It is fair to say many fear him more than any Autumn King.

    Master Darke is interested in how the Cabaret can further the Winter Court’s goals, and is interested in supplying Jacob with drugs in exchange for a small cut of the profits. Master Darke guarantees there will be no trouble from law enforcement. If Jacob is interested, he can keep him supplied. If not, He won’t consider Jacob an enemy, but he may not…work as hard to look out for the club if it serves fewer of his needs.

    Also at the SMCRH is Doctor Bright, a second Manikin Blightbent. While it is difficult to say for sure, he really seems to bear a strong resemblance to Master Darke. Of course, no one has ever seen Doctor Bright in anything but his white work clothes, and he never wears a hat or sunglasses. Doctor Bright also never raises his voice, but his voice is gentle, not cold like Master Darke. As far as you can tell, he actually tries to take care of the patients at the clinic. As far as you can tell.

        Lorelei’s Dairy

        I had four beers before I turned in last night, and that was probably about three too many. I guess I play the part of the party girl pretty well most of the time… but I need to remember that I’m kind of a lightweight when it comes to alcohol.

        So I took the gang out to dinner last night to celebrate our one week anniversary as a motley. We had great Chinese food and good conversation. Insincere Marguerite put shoes on before we went in and took them off under the table while we ate. At some point, Insincere Marguerite suggested we play a game when we got back to the house… a sort of “questions in the dark” scenario. We Americaned her idea up a bit and settled on Truth or Dare.

        As anyone who has ever played this grade-school staple should have expected, things started out innocently enough, but quickly devolved as the night wore on. Before the game started, Insincere Marguerite laid out a simple pledge binding us all to tell the truth. If anyone lied in response to a direct question as part of the game, that person would be plagued by a terrible odor for the rest of the night. We played by the commonly accepted rules: one person would ask another a question, and their target could either tell the truth in response, or accept a dare to avoid answering entirely.

        Things were innocuous enough at first. Gawain asked me if I’d ever been in love, and I had to admit that I have not. I asked Ace what he missed the most about his life before being Taken, and he got this sort of wistful look on his face and said that he missed his dad.

        I couldn’t possibly remember every question and answer that was exchanged last night… even aside from the alcohol, we played long enough so that everyone got to ask everyone else at least one question. I think it was Ace who turned up the heat a bit when he started asking questions about the rooms in the Hollow House. I think we all got a little nervous at that, but most of us managed to cover it up pretty well.

        Marguerite explained that she used to be a thief in Arcadia… the greatest non-Gentry thief in all the realm. She said that she occasionally kept the things she stole for her Mistress… and some of those things were Changelings, hence the restraints in her room in the Hollow. She was quick to point out that she never stole mortals for the Gentry… she just took those who had already been Taken from other Gentry.

        Gawain’s space in the garage was full of severed heads because he was forced to act out some old medieval fairy tale called “Sir Gawain and the Green Knight,” which was apparently pretty gruesome to hear him tell it. It was obvious that the big lug felt nothing but regret for the things he did in Arcadia, and the conversation quickly moved on after his awkward admission.

        Once the ball was rolling, everyone seemed to feel more comfortable with asking the deeper questions. Jacob asked Calamity about the blood in her room, and she took a very long swig of rum before choosing the dare option. Jacob dared her to kiss Gawain (which I think we all wanted to see). She didn’t give him some half-assed peck on the cheek, either… it was a real, deep, soul-kiss. Both of them looked pretty pleased afterward.

        After that, Calamity asked me what I missed most about my life before I was Taken. I had to think about that for a minute before I answered, but there was really only one true response I could give. I tried to keep the moisture out of my eyes as I talked about my two best friends, Melissa and Jane. And with every word, I saw their faces more and more clearly, staring at me through the gilded bars of our cage as I flew away into the Hedge.

        The next time the game came around to me, Jacob asked me why I was so afraid of going into the Hedge, and hazarded a guess that someone was hunting me. His guess was too close for me to edge away from that part of it, so I told him about Hunting Hawk. I don’t know if he’s still after me, but he certainly was when I left the Hedge. Jacob gave me a kindly, concerned look, and opined that we might need to do some hunting of our own soon.

        I was actually relieved when the game moved away from questions of Arcadia and the Gentry, and began to relax a bit. That sense of contentment evaporated when Insincere Marguerite turned her captivating eyes toward me with another question. “You are obviously a lover of women,” she began innocently. “What is the most depraved thing you have ever done – consensually – with another woman?”

        I was shocked into silence for a minute or two. My mind drifted back to that night with Imogene a couple of weeks ago… and what she asked me to do to her. It was easily the kinkiest, dirtiest thing I’ve ever done. The fact that she obviously loved it – and that doing it to her turned me on more than a little bit as well – was completely beside the point. It was a private moment, as intimate in its way as the time I spent with my friends in Arcadia, and I wasn’t entirely comfortable discussing it.

        And there’s also the fact that I didn’t want my new friends thinking of me as some cheap, depraved whore, of course.

        I knew that I could take the dare and avoid answering the question… but honestly, I sort of wanted to save that option for when talk inevitably turned toward my time in Arcadia. I figured that if I told the truth about my sex life here in California, no matter how depraved it was, my friends would take the hint when we came to something I really didn’t want to talk about. It’s sort of ironic, in retrospect.

        So I downed the rest of the beer I’d been drinking, took a deep breath, and spat out the vulgar term for the sex act in question. There was a vaguely uncomfortable silence from most of the group, followed by Insincere Marguerite applauding my daring and imploring me not to be ashamed of my dedication to my bedmates’ pleasure. I appreciate that she was trying to help… but at the time I sort of wished she would just shut up and let the game move on.

        I thought I would lighten the mood a bit after that, so I asked Jacob what his greatest regret was. It surprised the hell out of me when he refused to answer and took the dare option. I hadn’t expected him to balk at what I considered a softball question, so I didn’t have a good dare ready. The beers were starting to go to my head, and the memories Marguerite’s question had stirred in me were making me a little hot, so I just told him to take off his shirt.

        He has a lot of scars, and a brand in the shape of a snowflake. I should have known that even silly-horny-girl dares would be revealing in this context, I guess.

        The questions and answers seemed to skip me for a while after that. I remember Marguerite asking Gawain how he made love… like, physically, how it was possible. I’d been curious about this myself, since he’s basically a suit of armor. The answer was cryptic and not particularly satisfying (“it’s like a codpiece but it feels real”). Calamity also asked Gawain if he likes Monica, and I had to giggle at his teeny-bopper answer (“I like her but I don’t like-like her”).

        I’d started to relax when Ace turned to me with a question. “What did your Keeper look like?” he asked, as though it was the easiest thing in the world to talk about. I have to remember that his time in Arcadia was easier than mine, and he doesn’t mind talking about it as much.

        “The Empress,” I answered slowly. “She was beautiful. And she was purple.” That didn’t seem to satisfy people, so I elaborated. “It wasn’t just that she wore purple, or that her skin or hair or eyes were purple. She was purple, in every sense of the word. It’s like… every symbol or concept or emotion that we associate with the color purple… we think in those terms because she embodies those things.” It didn’t really come close to describing Her Majesty, but it was the best I could do with three beers in me.

        I asked Calamity if she had ever been in love, and she told me that she thought she had, but that it had been before she was Taken, and she didn’t give any details.

        The game sort of wound down after that point, but the evening didn’t end there. We were just sort of chilling out on the balcony deck, still drinking and chatting, when talk turned toward our schedule. Since we’ll mostly be operating the Dream House at night, and Insincere Marguerite and Calamity are more comfortable working in darkness, most of the group is resolved to keeping third-shift hours. I told them that I’d be happy to adjust my schedule most of the time, but that I’d need to get up on Sunday mornings for church.

        My religious habits had obviously been bothering Calamity for some time, so she came out and asked me why I go to church, as I apparently don’t seem the type. I didn’t have to answer her. I could have even lied without breaking the pledge, since the question wasn’t posed as part of the game. But for some reason – maybe the moonlight, or the beer, or the fact that I actually like these strange new friends of mine – I decided to tell her the truth.

        I told her about dad. I told her about growing up under his strict Methodist preacher parenting, and how I’d hated every minute of my childhood. I told her about how living in Arcadia had given me a new perspective on those years, and how his estrangement from the fetch who replaced me seemed to have mellowed him somehow. I told her that he was the preacher at the church I attend, and that we have a good relationship now, even though he doesn’t know I’m really his daughter.

        Jacob chimed in at some point with a similar story. He has a twin sister (who is now ironically much younger than him). She goes to college in Santa Monica, and he visits her from time to time. She doesn’t know who he is either.

        These stories set Calamity off in a way I couldn’t have anticipated. She… she killed her parents. When she got back from Arcadia, she went to see them, and they had never realized she had been gone. She had been a quiet, studious girl before she was Taken, and her fetch was more outgoing and vibrant… more interested in making friends and being popular. It was exactly the sort of person she had always hated… and apparently exactly the sort of daughter her parents had always wanted.

        I imagine that was pretty hard to take. Calamity snapped, and… did something she can never take back. I admit that a part of me was horrified to hear this from her. A part of me wanted to rail against her, and accuse her of actually being the monster she believes she is. But most of me… looking at her…

        I pity her. I feel so sorry for her. I know she’s not the vicious beast she claims to be. And I know just as surely that none of us will ever be able to convince her of that. She killed her parents. In her mind, there’s nothing more to say on the subject. At least not yet. But there’s something her friends can do for her. Even if we can’t convince her that she’s a good person, we can let her know that we still love her and want to associate with her anyway.

        I wanted to show her that we all have secrets. I wanted to commiserate with her. And my defenses were down due to the alcohol. So I blurted out my darkest secret; the one thing I would have taken a dare to avoid talking about while the game was still going on.

        Calamity had told me earlier in the evening that I should watch out for Austin. Now, Austin has always been a real sweetheart to me, so I asked her what she meant. Apparently, she can sort of sense when someone is an oathbreaker, and she used that power on Austin (and most of the other performers) just before our opening night show. Austin has broken a solemn oath to another Changeling at some point in the past.

        I reminded Calamity of this conversation, and then told her that I’m also an oathbreaker. I told the group about Jane and Melissa, and how we all swore that we’d escape Arcadia together or not at all. And then I told them about how I left them there to rot when I won my own freedom. A part of me wanted to mention that Jane told me to go, that she forgave me even before I left… but bringing that fact up would have just been making excuses for my selfishness.

        My friends’ reaction stunned me. They started talking about mounting a rescue mission into Arcadia itself. Marguerite hemmed and hawed about how she had vowed never to steal another Changeling, but Calamity assured her that this would be a rescue, not a theft. They were all so gung-ho about going back to get Missy and Jane that I’m not sure if they noticed my less-than-enthusiastic response to this plan or not.

        Of course I want to free them. I’m going to get that out of the way up front. They are my best friends, and they have been my lovers and my sisters since I was first Taken. But… I don’t know if I can go back there. Hunting Hawk is still looking for me; I know it. And if we came across the Empress, I know in my heart that she’d never let me go again. And I’m not sure I could bring myself to leave her this time anyway.

        And then there’s the question of Missy and Jane. Missy was already so far gone when I escaped that she couldn’t have left the Empress even if she’d won that stupid contest. And Jane won’t leave Missy; she’s already proven that she’s more loyal and faithful than I’ll ever be. What if we go back into Arcadia only to find that the girls I knew are gone forever? What if I get my motley captured or killed in a vain attempt to rescue the ghosts of two memories?

        I finished my fourth beer with thoughts like that running through my head. After a while, I made a decision. The Empress turned me into a songbird. In Arcadia, all I had to do to survive was make myself beautiful and sing pretty songs. And it occurred to me that I’m still doing that, even now that I’ve gotten away from her. Is that all there is to me? Am I nothing more than what she’s made me? No, dammit. I refuse to believe that. I will go back to Arcadia. I will rescue Missy and Jane, if I can. And if it turns out that the whole thing is pointless… then at least I will have tried, and maybe that’ll earn me some redemption.

        I felt better after that little epiphany, and so I did something I’d wanted to do for weeks. I spread my wings and jumped off the deck, gliding across the grounds in lazy circles until I lightly touched down. I had to go back in through the front door and climb back up to the balcony, of course, but I felt better for having stretched my wings a bit. Gliding isn’t as nice as flying, but it’ll do in a pinch.

        I said my goodnights after that, and the group started to disperse. I’d been sort of idly flirting with Jacob for a while, but he didn’t seem interested, so I let him be. He and Gawain carried Calamity to the room she’s been sleeping in while we de-ghostify her old bedroom, and Ace tore off his pants in a fit of high spirits and made a grand exit I don’t think any of us are likely to forget.

        I stumbled back to my bedroom, stripped, and flopped onto my bed. I had thought I wanted to be alone, to absorb everything I’d heard and come to terms with everything I’d said. Apparently, that wasn’t the case. I found myself crying a little, wishing I had someone with me. I didn’t want sex; not really. After the night’s emotional roller-coaster, I so wasn’t in the mood. I wanted someone to hold, and I wanted to have someone hold me. I wanted to kiss and cuddle and chase the dark, serious thoughts away with warm fuzzies.

        As I drifted off to sleep, my mind’s-eye gave me a flash of purple… not the violet and indigo of the Empress, but a softer, lighter pink-purple streak popping out of a sea of lustrous black strands.

There was another alien from their keeper, Mr. Gray, who was here in the area for a little bit, in the Winter Court, so he might have told them that other aliens might have magic tables maybe? Even Lucian wasn’t sure who might have grabbed it. He did give Ace a hand grenade, in case the Winter Court came after us. And he’s getting him a bulletproof vest.

                                                        So, plans. First off, I think we should convince the non-super creepy Changelings in town that Andromeda’s alright, just kinda, well, alienish. She hasn’t hurt anyone since she got out of Arcadia and since she got out of their control proper she hasn’t even talked with the Keepers. I mean, Joan, is pretty sure Andromeda’s not a loyalist and she thinks everyone is until she had a reason not to, it seems like. That’s got to count for something, you know? But anyway, that way, her court and everybody could help protect her from the Gentry. Then we need to find the table. The Summer Court wants to destroy it, but it does seem useful, as long as people agree to be changed and everything, you know? Regardless, we can’t let bad guys have it, and if we don’t know who has it, bad guys might. So we gotta figure that out.

                                                        For now though, Andromeda is staying here, in the basement room. At least for tonight. We’ll have to see how tomorrow goes. She was really tired and quiet, after she cried herself out a little. Hopefully she’ll feel better tomorrow. Maybe she’ll want to come to the pier with me and just relax and have fun. Though, if I’m leaving via getting tossed in the water, I don’t want to abandon her or anything, so maybe not the best plan.

                                                            Calamity’s Little Black Book

                                                            Tuesday May 6th Days without Stabbing: 1

                                                            Love. Katt used to read all sorts of fanciful poetry about it and listen enraptured to every love ballad there was, the more saccharine the better. She dreamed of love, yearned for it. Then Snow Globe taught me the danger of love, of any trust. Taught me to show nothing, to give nothing that might be used against me. I thought of how utterly foolish that naive little girl had been to crave something so maddening. It really is a drug, it warps your thoughts and your perceptions. It makes you do things you would never consider otherwise. It takes control and drags you along like some wayward carnival ride. And then, inevitably, it's over. Only it still doesn't let go. It clings to your soul and whispers in your ear like some twisted little devil on your shoulder. It makes you forgive the unforgivable. It makes you do the unthinkable.

                                                            I know these things to be true. I have seen it this very night. It was love that allowed Haley to trick Andromeda. Love that drove Andromeda to forsake everything she knew and was comfortable with to pursue the object of her affections. Sure, she is now free of the Mothership, but that wasn't ever her goal. And, blasphemous as it might be to even think it, is she honestly better off here than she was? She's lost the place where she belonged and is in danger from both the servants of the Mothership and her fellow changelings. I sat there as she explained how she knew that Haley had betrayed her. As she explained that she understood how the other girl had used her to make her escape. And then cried and told us how much she still loves Haley. That she would still do anything for her.

                                                            It was love that drove me to kill. Hearing it in their voices as they spoke of the sinister bitch that stole my life. I survived the hell of Snow Globe and they never even missed me. They loved that superficial whore more than they ever loved me. They knew she wasn't the same and THEY FUCKING LOVED HER MORE FOR IT. All I wanted was to go home... to be safe again... to be able to let my guard down... To be that stupid little trusting girl that believed in love again. But they betrayed me and they died for it.

                                                            Fuck, I shouldn't have sent Gawain to bed and stayed up alone. I wish I was there instead of here. Curled up against him with that hard metal skin under my fingertips and those strong arms around me just laying close and talking about anything and everything... even the idea of children. Me? A mother? There is an absolutely terrifying idea. But for him... with him... He loves me so much. It's there in the way he looks at me and speaks to me and in his touch... I tried to warn him, but it was too little too late. Love had taken root and would not be dislodged. And oh holy fuck did it feel good to have him standing there at breakfast with his arm around my waist or to see the fury when he struck down the vile shrike that wounded me Monday night. He would never betray me the way Haley betrayed Andromeda... the way my parents betrayed me. He would never hurt me like that. I can trust him. I just wish I could say the same about myself.

                                                            Insincere Marguerite tells me that I just need to listen and he'll tell me what he wants and needs. That he is a boy and an honest and straightforward one at that. That I can protect that innocence from the truths of the world. But Gawain is not stupid or dumb. He knows... just somehow he is still able to be... bright. The way Katt used to be. I can remember waking up before daybreak almost every day just so I could see the dawn. I can remember how it made me feel. I can remember it, but I can't feel it anymore. The Whispers took that away from me. Cut it out with Their razors and disappointed voices. I remember how it hurt, how I had to learn to be cold and numb inside to survive it, to endure it. Gawain's Keeper made him kill. Murdering over and over, spilling a river of blood that haunts his dreams still. He stopped fighting because of the pain it brought him and it did not save anyone. But somehow, somehow he did not lose his soul. Gawain would not have killed that night in Belleview. He's murdered more than I have... more than I ever will most likely... yet he's not the monster I am. How can he love me? Hell, how can he even stand to look at me? I kept the truth of that night from him for much too long and yet his only reaction was to comfort me when I did admit the truth of it. Insincere Marguerite thinks I can protect him from the world, but what he needs protection from most is me.

                                                            Damn I am fucking maudlin tonight and I haven't even gone near the bar. This is why I hate being goddamn idle. Too much time to think... to remember... And as much as I fucking need to stab the ever-living fuck out of something right now I do not believe the Winter Court is stupid enough to send assassins or kidnappers after Andromeda while she's here. Not when she's been alone in her home and most likely is going to be again soon. But I brought her here and someone should watch over her. I cannot allow her to be taken or killed on my watch. I must remain awake and alert just in case. I don't know what will happen tomorrow but for now, she is safe.

                                                            Maybe I should look for Insincere Marguerite. I'm sure she's awake still and her girl isn't here. She was willing enough to try to answer my questions about being in a serious relationship and she did seem troubled. I don't recall her ever mentioning her dearest before but her feelings clearly run deep there. I have no idea what I could do to help, but maybe just talking about it would be of use... Or maybe I should mind my own business. It's hard to be sure if it's something she wants to talk about or not. And I'm sure she knows I'm awake and alone, so I guess if she wanted company she'd have sought me out.

                                                            Fuck, I am not good at all of this. I don't know how to be her friend. I don't know if I'm doing any of this right. I like her. I like all of them. I want to help but fuck, unless it's something that needs to be gutted in the dark what in the ever loving hell am I going to do about it? What do I know about anything? Katt was too young and too sheltered. Snow Globe was hell. I have no idea how the real goddamn world works. I have no idea what normal people feel and do. Fuck, I drug Gawain to bed because hearing about how Lucian Torrez murdered a man got me hot and bothered. (To be fair, it was fucking epic. The man may be evil but I am jealous as fuck of his style right now.) I am not afraid to stand up to him or Master Darke or even the fucking Mothership to help Andromeda, but what good does that do when it's her broken heart that hurts her more than anything else? Haley tricked her, used her to escape and never looked back. If Andromeda wasn't here now, how would I feel about that? I'm not sure, but I doubt I'd find myself thinking of how to make sure that her ghost couldn't identify me as her killer. But I know it won't actually fix anything. It would hurt Andromeda even more and there isn't any proof Haley has done anything but fear that an agent of her Keeper had come to take her back.

                                                            What I need to do is find the goddamn magical table. Andromeda managed to coax the Mothership into letting her bring it with her to fix Haley but it was promptly stolen. So, all of the ones that we know want it aren't suspect because if they had it they wouldn't be looking for it still. I think Ace is still annoyed that I told the others about it, but we're the damn Musketeers. One for all and all fucked over if one of us does something stupid (like deciding to harbor a suspected loyalist.) I know Queen Charlotte is interested and... well... I know when I talk to Andromeda about this I am going to be trying to convince her that the Autumn Court should have The Table. But first we have to find it. Andromeda told us it's not simple to use, that it would be beyond even Haley. But the ones after its power don't know that and I imagine that given time whoever claims it will be able to unlock The Table's power. The only person she knows of that would understand it is a Mr Grey, who was the Mothership's doctor before her. He was apparently a member of the Winter Court but has not been seen or heard from in some time. If they have him stashed somewhere, it could be why they want The Table. Or maybe he dropped out of the courts and joined up with some other group... loyalists or bounty hunters or something? Maybe they have the Table? Or maybe I am just spinning wild conjecture and have absolutely NO fucking idea where to start looking for the damnable thing.

                                                            I hate this feeling of having no idea what to do. I hate being idle. I need to do something.. anything... I'm not a patient monster tonight. But there are no good leads and Andromeda already told us everything she knows. She said she needed to be alone, so I have left her be. There really is nothing to be done right now, but I can't just sit here any longer or my head is going to explode. There's room in the unused part of the basement to practice my walking and Annika will be here tomorrow. I'll still be close enough to watch Andromeda's room and a few hours of strutting should help clear my head.


                                                            Insincere Marguerite’s Whispers

                                                            Today was a fairly relaxing day, but full of awkward conversations. Mostly.

                                                            For example, at breakfast we had some discussion of Andromeda, who was rather the subject of the hour, as Calamity returned from, well, “spending time” with her, which did not seem to bother Gawain. Maybe. Calamity inadvertently revealed information from a secret conversation she had had with Ace about a Table and Andromeda. I covered my ears, but saw them write a Calamity and Gawain heart sort of thing on the whiteboard, and thought I heard something about Calamity becoming a parent but with my ears covered I don’t hear as well and apparently it was something else but I thoroughly embarrassed both. Anyway, Andromeda is coming to the show tonight, probably, and we will interview her after. That was the plan, anyway. Mostly.

                                                            I must remember Americans like guns, but don’t like Napalm. It seems if you like one horrid violent weapon you should like another. Also, with people from three different countries and probably even more different eras, cultural references are a minefield. Also apparently my turns of phrase are amusing to the Motley. Ace in particular was more amused by the good-natured jibs about the British I made and took them in stride. Probably. It was how I intended them, anyway. I have nothing against the British, after all, even though they are terribly strange.

                                                            I was working on ideas for my Brotherswing show when Calamity entered the stage area and wanted to ask me some questions. It seems I am being sought for romantic advice, due to I’s clearly excellent relationship with Julia. Probably. Calamity is indeed dating Gawain, and does not want to hurt him. She does, however, like sleeping with Andromeda but was not in love with her. I was sort of surprised Calamity was not gay myself. Mostly. Of course I was also surprised Lorelei was queer, so I am clearly bad at this. Usually.

                                                            I’m…not in a good place to give romantic advice. Mostly. I am, after all, trying to force myself to give Julia space and not give in to the urge to keep and abscond with her, despite her being regrettably okay with the concept. Still, I shared what advice I could. Gawain is a boy, and a straightforward one, so her path seems free of stones so long as Gawain does not bore her. Probably. Indeed, I helped not if she is worried about his innocence, she can protect him from many of the dark truths of the world by murdering them without his knowledge.

                                                            Of course, Gawain is not really innocent. Indeed, he has killed far more people than I have, wrecked more lives than I have. Probably. Well human lives. Mostly. Maybe. I reserved my thefts for those in Arcadia, who were already compromised by their role as servants and playthings of the Gentry. I was not going to bring that up to Calamity of course. I suspect she would take it badly, and her hands make knives. I am soft despite being boney and would make a poor, if adorable, pincushion. Probably.

                                                            Also Calamity was apparently stuck at thirteen for some time. Thirteen is a terrible age to be stuck at. No wonder she is angry all the time.

                                                            I spoke some of my problem. It’s hard to bring up without sounding egocentric, really. “Oh dear, this beautiful artists absolutely loves me, wants to be with me in clothing optional situations, and seems okay with almost anything I want, however kinky.” That is the sort of problem one would get slapped for complaining about. Usually. And yet, I am worried about my soul, and I think, or hope, I conveyed that to Calamity. Some odd looks crossed her face when I started mumbling about my Dearest, so I think perhaps I did.

                                                            The show went well, and we met Andromeda at the hedge house. I won’t use the word innocent for Andromeda but she is terribly naive. It seems she came here following Hailey, another Wizened whom she had turned into her alien-looking self for the “Mothership.” Hailey sensed her captor was smitten with her, and seduced her (or allowed herself to be seduced) and slept in Andromeda’s lower security part of the “ship,” escaping that night. Clever, I think. I have to say outwitting the minions of the Gentry is a fine pastime. Generally. Andromeda went after her, confused why she would want to escape (for some reason) and took an artifact called The Table with her…a “tool” she used to reshape humans into Changelings. She brought it to “fix” Hailey when found, and made the mistake of sending her Keeper a message back to let it (she?) know she arrived in town. This letter was, apparently, intercepted by the Summer Court, who was watching Andromeda at Hailey’s insistence. Wisely.

                                                            It seems, however, a wrinkle appeared…the Table the Summer Court was seeking had been stolen from Andromeda, very shortly after arriving. The Summer Court allegedly does not know its location, neither the Ogre Lucian Torrez nor Hailey. Calamity implied the Autumn Court may be interested in it, so apparently they don’t have it, and the Winter Court also appears to be seeking it. It is an issue, probably. The odd thing is Andromeda claims it would be useless to them as she is one of the few who can use it…apparently even Hailey can’t. Andromeda’s odd behavior, early comments about her Keeper and preoccupation with derrieres have not lead her to be well accepted even in her Court. Apparently.

                                                            We decided to look at her for the moment. She will be sleeping downstairs. This does beg some questions; are we sheltering a Loyalist? She has not betrayed her Keeper. Does that make me a Loyalist? I came here on Mama Lion’s orders. I do not wish Mama Lion harm. I would be some racist Parisian pseduointellectual without her intervention, after all, and probably fat. Probably. At the same time, I don’t wish to work for her again either. Most would say we are not Loyalists. Probably. But the Lost are seldom even tempered about such things. Maybe.

                                                            Ace met with Mr. Torrez, to let him know Andromeda did not have the Table. Apparently he witnessed Mr. Torrez killing a human, then they had coffee. Summer Courtiers are strange. Mostly. Ace did discover that Joan, of all people, wasn’t suspicious of Andromeda…she originally suspected Hailey, in fact, before Andromeda’s presence made that seem unlikely. She still apparently dislikes and distrusts Hailey, but then she is as friendly as a hornet’s nest covered with manure. I don’t know why the Spring Court is so fixated on her.

                                                            It’s all rather confusing. This should help me keep it straight in my mind. Now I must prepare answers if Gawain comes seeking advice for romancing Calamity. I will give him good answers. Probably. I should also make plans in case it happens for me to steal the Table from someone.

                                                            Hopefully.

                                                            End Session VIII (with Session VII Overflow)

                                                            “The Table” if you have not guessed is actually a Prop. Andromeda lacks the ability to turn people into Changelings or alter Changelings, but The Table can allow it’s Operators to use it’s power as a Kindly One to do so, as per the Contract with the Wyrd that created The Table.

                                                            In terms of RP, Insincere Marguerite made a lot of random stereotypes of people in conversation (including her usual distain for French Canadians and, for some inexplicable reason, Albanians) so it came up she got up on “the racist side of the bed that morning.” It came up OOC in the IC conversation with Calamity about boys how very differently the two Darklings see their pre-kidnap time. Calamity misses her old self, pines for it a bit, and sees her transformation as a terrible thing. Andromeda reminds her of Katt so she feels protective. Insincere Marguerite was a privileged teenage Front National supporter who, while it comes out in her attitude sometimes, loathes her old self deeply, and lashes out at people who remind her of her old self at all. It’s an amusing and unplanned dichotomy.

                                                            The scene with Torrez was they saw him walk out in front a car to stop it. Get hit, then jump through the windshield, pull the driver out, interrogate him, and punch through his head. Lucian is a Stonebones/Render (and, as noted, played by Brian Thompson) so really good at a) taking damage and b) breaking shit. It was also intended to send a strong “fuck with this guy at your peril unless you do a lot of preparing” message to the PCs. The message was received.
