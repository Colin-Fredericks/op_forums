    Session CXX

    Lorelei’s Diary

    Sigh. I know I’ve said this before, and I hate repeating my sentiments… but with everything that’s happened over the past twelve hours or so, I just can’t help it, so here goes.

    I do not have time for this shit.

    So the last time I wrote we were preparing for the Saturday night show and I was seething about the fact that Calamity had signed up for another demon-killing mission in L.A., putting paid to my own plans to seduce Rosalita into my bed and share her with Maria. And yes, I could have simply let Calamity and Jacob see to the demon-killing, but considering how our last effort in that arena went, it seemed logical to assume that my presence would be helpful for healing and crowd management. I would have felt awful if I had stayed home and people had been hurt as a result.

    Prior to the show, Marguerite was walking around in an… interesting outfit. She was wearing all bright pink, including a pair of comically oversized sunglasses. She held a small transistor radio as she danced about, blaring out 60s music. Calamity and I asked her what was up with that, but all she’d say is that Julia hates that outfit, so she was taking advantage of her absence to flaunt it (you might recall that the other Mrs. Oldacre was spending the early portion of the evening speaking with Willow Black at the Rose & Thorn).

    My solo number last night was, in my opinion, pretty sub-par. The audience still ate it up, so I didn’t let it get to me, but as distracted as I was by recent events, it wasn’t my best effort by a long shot. So I put it behind me and decided to focus on mingling with the crowd, as there were a fair number of notable guests present.

    First, T.J. was there, looking as big and boisterous as ever. D had already been by his table a couple of times by the time I made it out there, and he seemed to be okay, but I thought it was incumbent upon me to make my pleasantries as well. “Hey, T. J.,” I said softly as I pulled up a chair.

    “Highness,” he said with a nod.

    “How are you?” I asked after a moment.

    He shrugged. “Can’t complain,” he said noncommittally.

    I sighed. “I meant, how are you physically… how’s your recovery coming?”

    “Oh,” he said, and seemed maybe a little embarrassed. “I’m doin’ okay, Highness,” he said after a moment. “Pretty much patched up now.”

    “And Percival?” I asked.

    “He’s an asshole,” T.J. said without missing a beat, “and there ain’t no cure for that. But I hear he’s healed up okay.”

    I chuckled at that, and we chatted for a few minutes about Percival and his role in the Summer Court. Oddly enough, we eventually landed on the idea that he acts as a sort of opposite number to Flores. It’s not as odd as it sounds at first… Percival can start a fight with just about anyone, and Flores can defuse any violent situation with those big puppy-dog eyes of hers. Plus, they pretty much loathe each other (as much as Flores is capable of loathing anyone, that is).

    Eventually I couldn’t avoid it any longer. “Look, T.J.,” I said finally, “I’m sorry about what happened to you at Rowan’s place. If I’d known it was going to get that out of hand, I’d never have asked you to do it…”

    He shrugged. “And I woulda done it anyway. Honestly, I still feel kinda bad about how I treated Rowan a year ago…”

    I smiled at him. “Don’t worry; it’ll be our secret. But seriously, thanks T.J. You put yourself out there because I asked you to, and you got hurt in the process. And I won’t forget that.”

    “Thanks, Highness,” he said. “Means a lot.”

    I bought him a refill (chocolate milk and vodka… what the fuck?) and moved on, allowing Calamity to slide into the seat as I left. Julia caught my eye as she returned from her sojourn to the Rose & Thorn, so I walked across the floor to speak to her before she took her seat.

    “Mission accomplished,” she said simply, “I talked with Willow about the investigation and dropped… that name a few times. I think she got the message; she left right after our conversation.”

    I kissed her. “Thanks, sweetie,” I said, “I hope that makes things a little easier on John’s people.”

    “So,” she said after an indecently brief pause, “who’s that?” She indicated Rosalita’s table by inclining her head.

    “Oh, she stopped by earlier today to talk with Calamity. I introduced myself, and…”

    Julia smirked. “You made friends?” she suggested with a raised eyebrow.

    “Yeah,” I agreed. “Actually, I feel kind of bad about that whole situation, because I invited her here tonight so that I could, umm… ‘introduce’ her to Maria, and now I’m probably going to have to bail on that whole situation because Calamity signed us up to kill another demon in L.A…”

    So then I had to explain that whole mess. Julia valiantly offered to entertain Rosalita until the rest of us returned, but as I still didn’t know at that point who was going with Calamity and who would be remaining at the Dream House, I was noncommittal with my response. “I imagine Maria would still like to enjoy her company tonight… though I doubt very much if she’d mind you joining in as well.”

    A quick conversation with D later and everything was set. She really didn’t want me going into L.A. on a demon hunting mission without her, but I could see that she was also looking forward to spending some time with our tough-girl guest. Eventually she accepted my insistence that she stay and enjoy herself. “After all,” I said, “I’ll be with Calamity, Jacob, Becky, and Caoimhe… and I can take care of myself.”

    She smiled and stroked my cheek so gently. “I know you can,” she said, leaning in for a kiss. “If you’re sure…”

    So with that out of the way, I only had one more important conversation to see to before the end of the evening, and it was one that I was kind of dreading. I mean, I spoke with other people – Missy and Jane and Trinh all passed a few words with me, and I shared a quick kiss with Gloria – but there was only one other person with whom I needed to have a serious talk. And the bitch of it is that it was probably nothing compared to the talk I’m going to have to have with her tonight.

    Oleander sat alone at her usual table and threw me a dazzling smile as I approached. I’d seen her talking with Calamity earlier, so it didn’t actually surprise me when she said “I understand we’ll need a rain check for our usual date night, eh Angel?”

    I grimaced. “I’m so sorry, sweetie… believe me, I’d much rather spend the evening with you…” It seemed impolitic to mention the hot Latina biker chick I’d been lusting over for most of the afternoon, so I let it lie.

    She leaned in and kissed my nose. “Calamity explained the situation to me; I understand. I can… if you want me to, I can take another day away from my research and come back tomorrow…”

    “Of course I want you to,” I said fervently, giving her a smile. “Look, I promise I’ll keep tomorrow night free, okay? If you can make the time to be here…”

    She nodded. “I’m non-toxic right now, so all I have to do is put off injecting myself with anything interesting for another day. Not a problem. My… my project is coming along. Maybe not as quickly as I’d like, but I am making progress…”

    “I know you can do it, honey,” I said warmly. “I have total confidence in your abilities. And if you ever need anything from me, all you have to do is ask…”

    She gave me a squeeze, and we chatted about simple things for a few minutes. I confess that I unloaded a bit on her… I didn’t spill state secrets or anything, I just sort of let her know that the responsibility had been weighing on me, and that the stress was starting to affect my performances.

    “Don’t worry, Angel,” she said when I brought this up. “On your worst night, you’re still better than anyone else I’ve ever seen…”

    The sheen must have shown in my eyes, because, she sort of sighed and looked slightly panicky. “Did I do that wrong? That was supposed to be a compliment. I know I’m not very good at being romantic…”

    I leaned in and kissed her. After we separated, I did my best to reassure her. “I think you’re very good at being romantic,” I said. “You always seem to know just what to say to make me feel better.”

    Before we parted, she gave me a gift. Reaching into her handbag, she pulled out a syringe and a small bottle. “If you get into bad trouble, Angel,” she told me softly, “inject someone with this. It will make their bones grow long, sharp thorns. It should be enough to take them out of whatever fight you’re in. If you want to end the effect, force them to swallow this,” she handed me a single pill out of the bottle. “If you give it to them within the first minute, they should recover.”

    “How long does the effect last?” I asked, feeling slightly nauseous.

    She thought about it. “The thorns will quickly start to pierce their organs; after that the question is moot.”

    I chose to focus on the positive aspects of the situation. “Thank you so much for looking out for me, sweetie,” I said, very carefully stowing the syringe and pill in my purse and leaning in to give her a kiss. We said our ‘til next times after that, and I sort of loitered around the exit chatting with D, waiting until Caoimhe showed up so that we could all head into L.A. together.

    Calamity approached me before then with a serious look on her face. I thought I knew what she was going to say, but she surprised me big-time. “Lori,” she said soberly where nobody could overhear us, “I talked to Oleander a little while ago, and she… said some things. I thought you should hear about it.”

    I’m sure I blinked a couple of times at that. “Okay,” I said, “what sort of things?”

    “Well,” Calamity began, “I was talking with her about tonight’s mission… trying to let her know that it was my fault the two of you weren’t going to get your usual Saturday night together. And Rosalita and Kathy came up in the conversation, and I mentioned maybe helping Luther out with those gangsters in his neighborhood. I told her that I was trying to make sure I only did violence for the greater good.”

    I’d heard it before, so I just nodded.

    “Well, she said some things that made me think. She told me that it was kind of a slippery slope… that even if violence is committed for a good reason, it can be damaging to one’s sanity. So that gave me pause…”

    I smiled. “I’m glad to hear that,” I admitted.

    “But then she started talking about how damaging your sanity could be dangerous to the people closest to you, and how you have to keep a tight rein on that sort of thing to avoid hurting those people… unless things had gotten to the point where a great sacrifice was necessary to protect the ones you love, at which point your sanity doesn’t matter anymore.”

    She looked helplessly at me. “Lori… I don’t think she was talking about me there at the end…”

    So there we have it. Of course it didn’t take me long to figure out what that little speech meant. My sweet Oleander is close to completing the toxin for use against Hollywood, and she’s convinced that she’s going to have to deliver it personally. She doesn’t expect to survive the experience, but she’s willing to do it to protect me and everyone else in this Freehold. So she’s taking risks in developing the toxin… doing things which could damage her Clarity. And she’s convinced that she’s doing it for the greater good.

    I may be off when it comes to some of the specifics, but I’m convinced that the above paragraph is at least close to the truth of the matter. I couldn’t breathe for a second. I couldn’t meet Calamity’s eyes. I turned away. “I need a minute,” I said as I started to walk away.

    Her hand landed gently on my shoulder, carefully placed so as not to risk cutting me. “Lori… are you okay? Was it something I said?”

    I turned back to her and forced a smile, though my vision was pretty shimmery by that point. “I’ll be okay. Thank you for telling me, Calamity. I just… I need a few minutes to compose myself, okay? I’m going up to my bedroom… I’ll get ready to go while I’m up there. Come find me when Caoimhe gets here, please.”

    So I retreated to my bedroom, shut the door, and started to cry. I hadn’t been at it long when Marguerite found me and asked me what was wrong. As she’s one of the two other people in the motley who know what Oleander is actually doing for the Freehold, I felt like I could talk to her. I tried to keep my composure as I hastily gave her the basics of what Oleander had said, and my beliefs as to what that signified.

    She held me, and did her best to comfort me. “We will not allow this to happen,” she assured me. “We will save her. If nothing else, you can order her not to sacrifice herself, non?”

    I shrugged. “I… don’t like giving orders to… to people who are close to me. And… well, usually, with Oleander, all I have to do is ask, or even just hint, and she tries her best to please me. But this time… I’m not sure if even an order will be enough.”

    “We will save her,” Marguerite said again, stroking my feathers in a comforting way. “You will not be alone in this, Juliette. We will all help, and we will save her.”

    I let the tears flow freely, and I held her, and I kissed her, and I think I eventually got around to thanking her once my wits had returned to me. Once I’d regained my composure, she bade me adieu and returned to the others downstairs. I touched up my makeup and put on some traveling clothes (a leather jacket, a tight red top, and black miniskirt, fishnets, and boots). I was just heading downstairs when Calamity came to meet me.

    “Everything okay?” she asked, concerned.

    “I’m dealing,” I replied. “Sorry to worry you.”

    So Calamity, Becky, Jacob, Caoimhe and I piled into the van and headed for L.A. just after midnight. Calamity explained along the way that we’d be parking in the lot of a large club, where our “clients” would be waiting for us in a limousine. Two of us would be allowed to meet with them directly in the limo, though the rest would be able to listen and contribute to the conversation over those Bluetooth headset thingummies.

    And that’s just what happened. Calamity and Jacob went into the Limo (mostly because Becky refused to allow herself to be separated from me, I think) and got the whole story from an important-looking vampire in a suit who called himself Uncle Peng (Kathy, or “White Bone Princess,” was there too). They wanted us to enter the nearby club, then descend to the secret club beneath that club, seek out a creature named Da Ji, and put an end to her. They were willing to pay us $125,000 for this… or $1,000,000 if we could do it without damaging the club or involving the authorities.

    Calamity and Jacob were a little wary of this deal… it seemed a fairly large sum for what we were being asked to do. (Or is it? How much do people pay to contract-kill demons these days? What’s the going rate for that sort of thing?) Peng gave us a few more hints as to the nature of the beast… namely that she styles herself as a nine-tailed fox, a powerful malevolent spirit from Asian mythology. He also told us that it often seems that there are multiple versions of her wandering the club, and that it would be part of our job to determine which of them was the real Da Ji.

    And so began the evening’s adventure. Kathy led the five of us into the crowded dance club, and we quickly made our way across the floor to an unmarked door between the restrooms. On the way, I bumped hard into someone. When I turned to apologize I found myself gazing into the hooded and cloaked chest of Mr. Gone. “Can I help you with something, Your Majesty?” he asked, a hint of annoyance creeping into his voice.

    “Sorry,” I said, flustered. “I… dropped your name a few times earlier today while speaking with Ms. Gold about the matter we discussed last night. I thought it might help to loosen her tongue a bit when Boss Lowjack’s people question her. I didn’t mean to inconvenience you.”

    “Ah, I see,” he replied. “I’ll just be off, then.” And he faded away into the crowd.

    The mysterious doorway led to a small room containing a short stairway leading down to the real club. We had been told that the place catered to supernaturals, specifically Kindred and “Yao Guai,” as they call changelings. We saw a number of representatives of both camps last night, as well as a variety of mortals. I didn’t get the sense that anyone was there against their will. Apparently some mortals really get off on it when vampires feed off of them. Go figure.

    The club itself was… red. I mean, like really red. Lush red carpeting covered many of the floors. Red draperies covered the walls and hung from the ceiling, stirring slightly with the air currents. The furniture was red. Everything was lit by red-tinted lights. There were rooms with aquariums filled with exotic fish, and the red lighting made it look like they were swimming in thin blood. I’m sure it was a perfect example of vampire-chic, but honestly the overall effect was a little nauseating to me.

    Oh well… at least my top matched the décor.

    We were led to a lavish sitting room and asked to wait while our hosts ascertained whether Da Ji would consent to meet with any of us. Kathy translated most of this, as many of the Kindred and changelings present spoke primarily in an Asian dialect with which I was entirely unfamiliar (I think it might have been Thai, but that’s just guesswork based on what I’ve gleaned from various menus over the last couple of years).

    At one point a darkly beautiful changeling woman entered the room and beckoned to me coyly, implying that she would like to spend intimate time with me. I glanced over at Becky, who sort of rolled her eyes, and declined as politely as I could. The fae woman sidled up closer, leaned down, and whispered in my ear at that point. “You are seasonal,” she said in thickly-accented English. “This is not your territory. We need to talk privately… now.”

    I nodded in understanding, then gestured to Becky. “My guardian?” I asked simply. Becky stood up and drew nearer to me.

    The fae woman seemed to consider, glanced at my crown, and nodded. She strode from the room, beckoning that we should follow. She led us to one of the oddest boudoirs I’ve ever seen. The floor seemed to be a thick pane of glass, under which flames were burning brightly. The floor was warm, but not as painfully hot as it should have been.

    The woman – whose name turned out to be Malai – sat on the bed in the center of the room and looked me up and down. “Where did you come from, and what are you doing here?” she asked bluntly.

    I nodded to her. “Mistress Lorelei of the Spring Court, from the Lady by the Sea Freehold,” I introduced myself. I held a hand out to indicate my companion. “Becky Wan, Claviger of the Spring Court.”

    Malai raised an eyebrow. “Not from Hollywood, then?” she asked.

    “No,” I said with some finality. “And we’re here hunting a demon on behalf of some of your business partners.”

    Her eyes widened. “Da Ji?” she asked. “Shit! Best of luck,” she said wryly.

    “Look, we’re not here to step on anyone’s toes,” I said in what I hoped was a soothing voice. “Part of our mandate on this little excursion was not to disrupt the activities of the club any more than absolutely necessary. And I’m sorry to show up here unannounced; we weren’t aware that any changelings were involved in this place until we got here.”

    “Okay, I got it,” Malai said after a moment. There was a bit more banter… some of which included the not-so-subtle suggestion that we use the bed to prevent people from speculating as to the reason for our little meeting. In the end I declined, deciding that it was more important to get back to my friends.

    I returned to find Kathy negotiating with an androgynous person in a smart suit to determine how many of us would be allowed into an audience with Da Ji. Calamity was having fun with one of the serving girls, tugging on a short length of rope which was attached to her collar whenever she wanted to freshen up her drink. The girl seemed into the game, so I can’t really fault her for taking advantage of the situation. There was a part of me that longed to revel in the desire and lust of that place too… I think something about it was bringing out all of our darker urges.

    They had really good bourbon, though.

    Anyway, eventually it was declared that Da Ji would speak with two members of our party, and we were left trying to decide who should meet with her. As both the highest-ranking member of our party (politically speaking, at least) and our resident social butterfly, you’d think I would have been a shoe-in for that job, but ‘twas not to be. See, I can’t go into a potentially dangerous situation like that without my Claviger present… which would have precluded anyone else from sitting in. So in the end, once more, it was Calamity and Jacob who ended up in the hot-seat and took on the burden of meeting our unknown opponent.

    Caoimhe, for her part, was starting to rethink the whole undertaking, and let us know quietly that she thought we should cut our losses and get out while we still could. I was inclined to agree at that point… one of the monarchs from a neighboring Freehold getting involved in something which might damage a prominent Night Court establishment could cause trouble for the Lady by the Sea. I asked Calamity and Jacob to exercise caution, but told them both that I trusted their judgment. And I had the syringe Oleander had given me in my purse if things got ugly.

    Now, I will confess that I don’t know the first thing about what went down in that meeting… all I know is that Calamity and Jacob came out of it about half an hour later in agreement that pursuing the contract on Da Ji would be a bad idea. Something about how there were nine of her, and one of them wasn’t even on the premises, and we’d have to kill all of them before she regenerated any of them… it was this whole big thing. But if Calamity of all people decides that trying to kill someone is a bad idea, I tend to accept her judgment on that.

    We had no idea how long Jacob and Calamity were going to be in their meeting, and there’s only so much you can do in a club like that without succumbing to its ‘charms,’ so when a commotion started up in the hallway outside of our little waiting room, I sat up and took notice. A number of security guards were rushing past the door, along with Malai and another changeling I didn’t recognize (he looked like a vaguely humanoid shaggy beast with a skull for a head). I asked Kathy what was going on, and she inquired on my behalf. It turned out that a small group of people were in the club upstairs, and they were looking for someone, and they were making trouble. Like, ‘call the police’ levels of trouble.

    My heart dropped like a stone when I heard that. “Who are they looking for?” I asked with a sigh.

    Kathy posed another question to one of the security guys outside, and then turned back to me, her expression carefully blank. “They appear to be seeking an attractive blonde woman with wings,” she deadpanned.

    “Jesus wept,” I muttered under my breath. “Look, I’m sorry about this. If you’ll let me up there, I’ll try to defuse the situation before it gets any further out of hand…”

    Kathy relayed this, and one of the security guys started shaking his head vigorously. Mr. Skullhead, though, looked me up and down and laughed contemptuously. “I’d like to see these guys try to stop you from doing whatever the hell you want,” he said good-naturedly. “C’mon, let’s see what’s going on up there.”

    I followed him down the hall and up the stairs with Becky in tow. Caoimhe opted to wait for the others, and I asked her to let them know where I was and what I was doing. Mr. Skullhead led the way, occasionally moving security guards out of the way as if they were chessmen, and then held the door to the dance hall open with an “after you” gesture. I walked out of the stairwell and into the back of a mob.

    Okay, I’m overstating. But everyone was leaving the club in a big group, making it really hard for anyone to actually move. I glanced over at Becky, who gave me an “I got nothing” shrug, and then did something I’ve always wanted to try. I raised myself up with all the authority I could muster, and just started walking forward with a purpose. And you know what? It worked! People who saw me coming practically stumbled to get out of my way, which caused a sort of peristaltic motion in the crowd as a whole. It just… parted for me, and soon I found myself in the open air…

    …being blinded by flashing blue and red police cruiser lights. So much for deescalating the situation before it got out of hand. I found Uncle Peng speaking to the officer who seemed to be in charge of the situation. Well, that and yelling at his chauffeur, who had apparently allowed the troublemakers to drive off in Peng’s limo… which contained the payment he had promised Calamity in return for killing Da Ji. Once he had disentangled himself from the authorities, I pulled him aside and let him know that we could probably recover the limo as soon as Calamity emerged from the club.

    That happened about five or ten minutes later. Calamity spoke to Peng briefly, while Jacob and Caoimhe made a bee-line for me and Becky at the van. I gave Calamity the quick and dirty version of the evening’s events when she joined the rest of us, and she invoked the Contracts of Oath and Punishment with an air of resignation, and climbed into the driver’s seat. “They’re not far,” she said as we pulled out onto the street.

    “Not far” turned out to be a dilapidated building a couple of miles away. An old man sat on the front stoop, grinning toothlessly and holding a hand-made sign which read “It’s not a crack house, it’s a crack home.” The limo was nowhere in sight. I looked at Calamity doubtfully, but she insisted that the troublemakers were inside.

    “Hello?” I called out with some trepidation as we stepped into the building’s front room. I caught snippets of whispered conversation from nearby, but couldn’t make out more than one word in three (though I could have sworn I heard a female voice indignantly tell one of the others to “put that crack back where you found it!”).

    I sighed. “Look, it’s obvious that you three have something you want to say to me. Why don’t you come out here so we can discuss this situation like civilized people?”

    They shuffled out into the entranceway looking a bit sheepish. And yes, it was the same three from the previous night… the old man in the shabby suit, the muscular young guy with the soulpatch and the guns, and the busty young blonde with the amazing blue eyes.

    “Okay, what’s this all about?” I asked.

    The older guy fell to one knee. “How may we serve you, oh Divine One?” he asked.

    I think I responded with a witty rejoinder along the lines of “W… what?”

    The blonde took up the thread at that point. “You’re clearly here on Earth doing God’s work, like Michael Landon, and we want to help you!” she said with obvious enthusiasm.

    I blinked a few times, vaguely conscious of the fact that a giggling Caoimhe was recording the whole scene on her phone, until something the girl had said penetrated. “Michael Landon? From Little House on the Prairie?” I asked.

    She shrugged. “I dunno; I never watched it. I think he was on Touched By An Angel or something, though. One of those shows about angels doing God’s work on Earth.”

    The older man said something under his breath that sounded like “Highway to Heaven,” but offered up no further commentary.

    “But, umm… I’m not an angel,” I said, feeling absurdly apologetic about that fact.

    Three slightly out-of-sync variations on “yes you are!” bombarded me from all quarters.

    “No, I’m not!” I protested. “Look, it’s true that I’m… I’m not like other people, but I’m not divine. I’m just a regular girl trying to do the best I can… just like you.”

    “Ah, I see,” the older man said with an infuriatingly knowing wink. “Yes, of course. Just a normal girl. And no one shall hear any differently from us.”

    Okay, so it became obvious at this point that I wasn’t going to convince them of the truth with logic. Caoimhe was snickering behind me, and I got the impression that the others were only just holding it in. I decided that the time for subtlety was over.

    “Alright, fine,” I intoned, spreading my wings and drawing myself up to my full height. “You have passed the test. Your faith shall be rewarded. I am indeed an agent of the Divine Work, and I may well have need of your talents ere long. But we must be cautious; for the Enemy has spies everywhere…”

    “I’m not afraid of them!” the young guy said, drawing a pistol from the waistband of his pants.

    I gave him a sad smile. “But they might seek to strike at you through those you hold dear,” I explained. “So it is important for now to maintain our secrecy. We must not act too soon. Please, my friends, return to your homes. I will call for you when I have need of you.”

    They gave me awed looks and solemnly agreed to return to their homes and lie low until they heard from me. I am… not naïve enough to believe that this will be a permanent solution, but hopefully I’ve bought myself some time with that little bit of theater. I took their names and contact information, and asked them where they had stashed the limo. It was only a couple of blocks away, and Calamity set off to reclaim it, asking us to meet her back at the club.

    Okay, so my (sigh) loyal followers include:

    Charles Nelson Grissom, a septuagenarian religious fanatic who is convinced that the Rapture is imminent, and that he must prepare for the Glorious Ascension’s arrival by shedding all of his worldly possessions (most notably his clothes).

                                                    “Worse,” I said.

                                                    There was a pause. His next question was posed lightly, but I could hear how deadly serious he was behind the joke. “Did you find a massive theater with duplicates of all of us in residence?” he asked.

                                                    “No,” I said. “I did a little kenning while we were there, and the place wasn’t Arcadian. It was both more mundane and a lot stranger, in a weird way.”

                                                    He chewed on that for a moment. “I can be there in forty-five minutes,” he said.

                                                    “We won’t be far behind you,” I promised him.

                                                    The ride back into town was pretty quiet for the most part. I spent much of it snuggled up between Maria and Marguerite, trying to give and receive comfort in equal measure. Maria came back to herself for the most part during the ride. She was still very quiet, but she was definitely coming back from wherever she’d been.

                                                    We arrived at Lowjack’s in time to witness Halley’s exit. She was stuffing something that looked suspiciously like a laser rifle into a duffel bag as we entered, but did everything she could to draw attention away from that fact. She apologized for not coming by the Dream House much lately, and promised that she’d rectify that situation as soon as she could.

                                                    John had Tamara lock the door and put up the “Back in Five Minutes” sign after Halley left, since there were too many of us to comfortably fit in John’s office. We gave him the run-down of everything we had seen and learned in the facility, and he considered it carefully. “You did exactly the right thing,” he said after a moment. “You assessed the threat, then got out to report it. Most importantly, you sealed the complex off so that nothing could get out. I’ll probably call a meeting of the four monarchs within the next few days so we can discuss a more permanent solution to the problem. I know your schedule, Lorelei, and I’ll try to work with it. I’d just ask that Luna let me know what she learns from this missing hiker as soon as possible… we might have to make it an emergency meeting, depending on the results of that investigation.”

                                                    He had noted aloud at one point that Maria wasn’t acting quite like herself. I know why he pointed it out; we were discussing a supernatural threat that possessed people and caused them to act oddly, after all. Maria responded as you might expect, asking him if he would prefer it if she threatened to kick his ass through the bar’s front window. John seemed to accept that, and moved on.

                                                    He and I spoke privately in his office before we left, and I explained why Maria was acting differently in general terms. I told him that I would keep an eye on her over the next few days just to be on the safe side, but that he shouldn’t worry about her being a danger to the Freehold or anything like that. I don’t know if he took me at my word or not, but he at least had the grace to pretend that he trusted my judgment on that matter, so I’ll count that as a win.

                                                    When we got back to the Dream House, well… I probably technically could have participated in the show if I’d rushed to clean up and get into costume and makeup, but… nah. As Queen, we exercised our royal prerogative to play hookey and relax for an evening. I took a luxurious hot shower, washing my hair at least twice to make sure I got all the dust and cobwebs out of it. I considered taking John’s suggestion and using something called a “bath bomb,” but I’m not really much of a bath person anymore with the wings. The only reason I’m able to indulge with Oleander is that she has a detached tub at her place, so it’s easier to drape my wings over the edge.

                                                    After my shower I threw on a t-shirt and some sweatpants and headed to the Media Room with Maria. We decided to watch a couple of silly, mindless action movies on Netflix, and I snuggled up close to her and just basked in the love. After the first flick was over, she leaned in and apologized. I didn’t have to ask her what it was for.

                                                    “You never have to apologize to me for that, Maria,” I said. “I love every part of you and I always will. And we all struggle against our Arcadian selves… do you know how hard it is for me to be in this house while a show is going on and not rush down there to prove that I’m better than the rest of the performers?”

                                                    We left it at that and put on another movie. As midnight approached, I remembered that I actually did have another commitment that evening, though it was one that wouldn’t take me away from Maria for long. After the flick was over and the House had started to empty out, I made my way down to the theater and found Dakota. We made small talk for a minute or two, and I let her know that Dad would be coming back into town the next day.

                                                    “Look, I just want to thank you guys again for putting me up like this,” she said sincerely. “Your dad is a wonderful guy, but… there’s not a lot to do at his place. I really appreciate your hospitality.”

                                                    “We’re happy to have you,” I said, “though that does remind me of something. I know you’ve been kind of limited in your ability to feed lately. Are you feeling a bit… peckish?”

                                                    She grinned shyly. “I could eat,” she said softly after a moment.

                                                    I held out both of my arms, stroking along my inner wrists, and then inclined my head at a coquettish angle.

                                                    “Where do you want it?” she asked.

                                                    I tugged at the neck of my t-shirt, exposing my throat. She leaned in, bared her fangs, and… golly.

                                                    I mean… wow.

                                                    I’d heard about the heightened sense of pleasure people feel when vampires feed on them, and it was one of the reasons I’d been so curious to experience it for myself. The reports were not exaggerated. While it might be a bit much to refer to the experience as “orgasmic” – and I consider myself something of an authority on the subject of orgasms – I can certainly see how some people might become addicted to the sensation.

                                                    I was pretty woozy, so Dakota offered to carry me up to my bedroom. I’m not sure if it was the blood loss or the post-feeding haze, but I don’t think I could have walked under my own power at that point. In fact, I have a dim recollection of seeing Marguerite, Julia, Missy and Jane frolicking in the upstairs hallway wearing nothing but edible body paint, though that might have been a hallucination.

                                                    Dakota tucked me in and went back downstairs, and D showed up a few minutes later. By that point I’d used Spring contracts to replenish my blood, leaving only a warm blissful feeling. We peeled off our clothes and slipped under the covers, sharing our love as only two people who know each other exceptionally well can. Then I set the alarm, slipped back into her embrace, and let everything else fall away from me until it was time for class this morning.

                                                    Life can be pretty grand, even when it decides to throw you the occasional curve ball. Like, y’know, Bulgarian teleport zombies.

                                                    Jacob’s Journal

                                                    Well that could have gone better. Not that anything bad happened to us. Not really, but we did find out some stuff that just makes the problem of the industrial building in the middle of the woods a little bit more pressing. Part of me wishes that Stasia hadn’t been so clever in figuring out how to open that hatch, and another part of me is glad that we know about the problem so we can do something about it. Lori made a few calls before we left to take care of things in case we were out longer than expected. She called Dark Kimberly to manage the show and Lowjack so someone in a position of authority would know where we had gone. So, we all piled into the van and headed towards the woods. It was me, Luna, Calamity, Insincere Marguerite, Lori, De la Rocha, Stasia, Elena and Becky. Becky was coming along so she could wait by the entrance with a walkie talkie in case we needed her to call for help. The trip through the woods was uneventful; the trip into the complex was not.

                                                    Getting in was fine. We started laying a trail of tea candles so we could find our way back out. We packed a good bit of supplies, which I thought was a good idea. We brought flashlights, tea candles, glowsticks, food, water rope, and empty bottles (just in case). We started where Stasia and Elena had already been and found the same signs that they told us about. They were in fact in Cyrillic but they were not in Russian. I wasn’t exactly sure what they said, so we kept going. We found our way to the break room which had four other doors leading off from it. There was a flag on the wall, but I couldn’t remember which country it went to. I took a picture so we could look it up later. We decided to start down a hall that Stasia and Elena had not been down. We knew which hall led downstairs, but we wanted to check out the level we were on first. We found what appeared to be an infirmary, but the doors were shut and locked with chains and padlocks from the outside. They were trying to keep something or someone inside for some reason. Around that time I noticed Lori talking to De la Rocha, who wasn’t looking very good. De la Rocha was very quiet and stayed in the shadows. I’d say her face was expressionless but her eyes were very much alive. Like she was a big cat in the jungle hunting prey. Lori asked De la Rocha to go back to the surface, and she eventually agreed and left the way we had come.

                                                    Stasia picked the lock on the door to the larger area and we were about to go in when Calamity got a look at something through the window on the door. It looked like a human figure sitting huddled on the floor with their back to us. I looked through the window and saw the same thing. After making sure that we were all seeing that we walked inside. As soon as we got inside we didn’t see the figure any more. Lori used an ability to see if there was anything magical in the area. She said that something really big had happened there a long time ago, but it wasn’t Arcadian in nature. Meanwhile Stasia and Elena were looking around and found a map of the facility and the surrounding area. It depicted someplace close to Sofia in Bulgaria. Calamity then used a contract to see if there were any ghosts in the area and apparently she spotted one. She tried talking to the ghost but they didn’t speak a language in common. I guessed that if the ghost could read the Cyrillic on the signs then she might understand another similar language. So, I asked Calamity to ask the ghost if she could hear me. She said yes. So I said “Hi” to the ghost and asked her how she was doing in Russian. The ghost apparently understood because she answered back in Russian. Calamity then used another contract to give us all the ability to hear the ghost. I then proceeded to ask her questions about what was going on.

                                                    As we discovered it seems that the ghost and this facility were originally from Bulgaria. At first the ghost woman, I never did ask for her name, kept trying to show us how to get out of there, but eventually answered my questions. It seems that they were doing experiments using radio waves to try and move people from one location to another. As it turns out they were successful, but something went wrong. When the person they transported came back they came back changed. Their eyes were dark, as if clouded with grey or black cataracts. They also had a different inflection when they spoke, and certainly not least, they were just plain crazy. And the last experiment that they did moved the entire structure from Bulgaria to the United States. This caused almost all of the people in the building to go crazy and start attacking those that had not changed. I think the ghost that I was talking to had been killed by her husband. I also asked about the flooded lower levels. She said that they lower levels had been sealed off and flooded to kill the people that had gone crazy. The ghost also told us about a machine that was still running that was keeping her spirit there. She said that there was a way to drain the water if we wanted to get down there and turn the machine off. She suggested that it was the machine that had caused the people to go crazy. I got directions on how to do that should we want or need to.

                                                    Around that time Calamity heard another ghost scream from an adjacent hallway. The ghost that we’d been talking to said that the ghost down that hall was crazy and could hurt the living. Makes me wonder how many other people found this place, and if so did they make it out alive? One would think so considering that the hatchway was closed and sealed. Marguerite offered to go down into the flooded area by herself if we could find a scuba tank for her to use. I think this idea was universally shot down by everyone there. Insincere Marguerite offered to take a mirror with her so she could escape quickly if the need arose. Stasia argued that diving into that place would be akin to cave diving, which is extremely dangerous. It would be hard to find her way back out before her oxygen ran out. In the end I think that we talked Marguerite out of going alone.

                                                    We decided as a group to at least take a look at the hatch, and that is where we ran into trouble. Calamity started walking down the stairs and almost immediately something grabbed a hold of her ankle and yanked hard. Calamity started to fall down the stairs and Marguerite and I reached out and grabbed a hold of her before she could fall. I held onto Calamity’s hand while Marguerite grabbed her waistband. Together we managed to pull Calamity back up and onto her feet. The thing that latched onto her ankle was a woman, with an obviously broken neck and dark eyes. The woman fell after we shook her off and she landed several floors down on the metal stairs. There was a splash. Insincere Marguerite, who had a contract active that allowed her to see in the dark, said that the woman broke her leg, but seemed to be undeterred. This was not heartening. The woman was dead, but not a ghost and physical damage didn’t do all that much to slow her down. We talked for a bit about what we should do next when Marguerite said that the woman was creeping back up the stairs holding a flashlight in her hand. Insincere Marguerite said that it looked like the woman was going to throw it. So, Calamity waited until she did and then caught it when it came flying up. The woman scampered a way down a hall when she saw that. I suggested that we go back to the surface and close the hatch so we could talk in peace and not have to worry about being attacked while we made our plans. Lori was all over that idea and we eventually convinced the others that it was the right choice. Calamity was a little reluctant, but in the end she agreed that we needed a safe place to formulate a plan.

                                                    As if the body attacking us wasn’t disturbing enough Calamity said that the woman that attacked her on the stairs was the body of the ghost that we’d been talking to. I wish I had gotten her name. The interesting thing was that the body of the ghost was barely wet, only up to her ankles, and it was really well preserved for someone that had been dead since 1962. Yeah, I asked the ghost what the last year she remembered was and that’s what she said. Her body should have been falling apart at the very least, skeletonized at the most, but she wasn’t. That means that there could be more mobile bodies down there like her. Out of the water and still moving around. After we had locked the hatch again we wanted to make sure that no one had escaped the lab, so we searched for tracks. It turned out that there were child sized footprints leading away from the hatch. They led us away and into the woods and then back to the hatch. So, whoever had left realized that they were in the middle of nowhere and went back to the lab. Calamity used a contract to see if she could find out exactly where the owner of those footprints was. She saw that the child was inside the complex with two or three other people making booby traps. So, I guess that means that they know we’re coming back. Or they have a suspicion that we are. I guess that just means that if we come back to this place, or if someone comes in our stead, we or they will need to be extra cautious. It’s not just urban exploring anymore. It’s something else entirely.

                                                    Since we knew that the child thing had gotten out Luna called the Forestry Service to see if anything had happened while the door was open. They said that a hiker had gone missing but had turned up that evening unharmed. That was good news but we wanted to make sure that nothing had attached itself to the hiker. Luna offered to do what she called a wellness check to make sure that the man was okay. We agreed that tomorrow would be a good time and that she should go alone. Having non-rangers with her would be suspicious to anyone and especially to whatever beings were inhabiting those bodies.

                                                    So, we discussed what we should do with the lab. Someone suggested that we just fill in the whole place with concrete. Someone else suggested that we go inside and try and kill all of the zombies. I’m not sure of a better word to use. They are definitely dead and yet they are still moving around. I think the going theory is that when the building was transported something replaced the souls of the people with something else. Something alien or supernatural we don’t know. It kind of makes sense seeing as we talked to the ghost of a woman that just tried to kill Calamity by pulling her down the stairs. We came up with several people to call, but in the end we decided to start by telling Lowjack what was going on. Lori took it upon herself to call the reigning monarch and give him a preview of what had happened. She asked him if we could all meet as soon as we got back to the city. He agreed and we all decided that Lowjack’s bar would be a good place to talk.

                                                    Before we left the woods Lori also checked her voicemail and found that she had several messages from her father. She called him and found out that he had a feeling that she was in danger and kept trying to reach her to see if she was okay. Lori assured him that she was fine and they made plans to meet for dinner tomorrow. I think he also might have some names of the vampire hunters, so Calamity can track them down. I want in on that if she goes after one of them. Even if it’s just recon, I want to know what they’re up to.

                                                    We all met at the bar and talked. We told Lowjack everything that we’d seen and learned. He suggested that he call a monarch meeting in the next few days to discuss what to do with the Bulgarian facility. After that Lori talked to Lowjack privately in his office. Not sure what that was about. Probably monarch business. I will admit that I’m curious about the things that Lori now has to deal with, but I get a feeling that it is probably something that I don’t want to know about anyway.

                                                    After our meeting with Lowjack we went back to the Dream House. We decided that after the day we’d had that we didn’t want to have to deal with the show so we let our back up people take care of everything. Luna also said that she was thinking of going to the hiker’s house tonight and doing a stealthy check to see if the guy was really okay. I said that I could be invisible, literally, and offered to come along. Luna agreed and we went to the guy’s house. We parked a little ways away and Luna transformed into a moth to go check out the house. She came back about twenty minutes later saying that she had seen the man and his eyes and that they were normal. Reassured that nothing untoward had actually happened we headed back to the Dream House. I left the bar in the good hands of my back up and went upstairs with my lovely Luna. She is a pretty moth and a beautiful woman. I’m lucky to have her.
