                              Alan Wake (including the American Nightmare expansion) manages to pretty much nail many of the themes and moods of Changeling, I feel. Everything about it, from the narrative-driven nature of the monster and its obvious bane towards light, to Alan's time beneath the lake being a perfect example of a durance, to Mr Scratch and his shadow powers being the perfect example of a murderous Fetch, combines to give me a terrifically strong Changeling feel the entire business. There's even a conspicuously large amount of time spent running around shadow-infested forests that give me a pretty strong Hedge-vibe.

                                          Originally posted by Baroqo View Post
                                          Lady Gaga and Katy Perry screams to be changelings. Or Others.
                                          What about their managers?
                                          I always thought it would be a funny direction to take a Changeling chronicle to follow one of those "alt-tween" bands or musicians: create some NPCs blatantly parodying Jeffree Star, Brokencyde, Fall Out Boy, whoever...one of those musicians who targets the preteen audience with material meant to be shocking to their parents and make the kids feel like rebels.

                                          Showcase an NPC who is a parent getting into some strife with their kid and blaming it on "that awful music she's listening to-you can call me old-fashioned, but when I was her age at least the music targeted at us wasn't that explicit." Showcase a young NPC, maybe a little cousin of one of the PCs, getting more and more introverted, wearing more and more all-black band merchandise with...weird symbols on it, symbols that raise the hair on the back of the local Autumn Courtier's neck. They start acting more and more alien, distant from their peers and the adults around them, eventually ending friendships with the kids who don't listen to the same music they do-they were all "posers," or they "didn't really care about me," or the age old and eternally popular: "They just didn't understand me." This culminates when the band or artist comes to town and holds a concert which ends disastrously: dozens or hundreds of preteens dead or missing, and the ones that survive come out acting like completely different people than they were even before listening to this music: Angrier, nastier, even more destructive and alienated.

                                          The mortal explanation, of course, is some faulty wiring that burned out at the exact wrong moment and led to a catastrophic accident, or perhaps a drug slipped to a lot of concert-goers by some unknown person that caused a mass bad trip, and a lot of press releases are made and the band's name is on the front page of the news every day for weeks. What really happened (unless the PCs were given an opportunity to stop it that they took) was this: During the culmination of the set, a gateway to Arcadia was opened right there in the middle of the theater, and the brainwashed youth were easily herded through it, certain that this was the way to a world where they would be understood and appreciated as they weren't at home. The Faeries got as many fetches made to replace the taken children as possible, but even if a Fae lord had the resources to make so many duplicates of so many humans, it would grow bored with the task quickly: Thus, many of the children are declared dead or "missing" as no replacement for them emerged or the one that did was so shoddy and unstable that it collapsed in on itself within hours of appearing in the mortal world.

                                          If the PCs couldn't stop this, they obviously have a responsibility to stop it from happening again, and so they go after the musician/musicians...only to find that there's nothing supernatural about the musician themself. They're a puppet, a mortal Glamour-junkie, and that whatever did plan for the deaths and disappearances of all those children wears a business suit, is nowhere near the tour bus they've just stormed, and has gained power not only from the lives it's just stolen but from the grief and rage and other emotions being broadcast (even amplified) worldwide by the endless media coverage of this tragedy.

                                              While watching "Gankutsuou: The Count of Monte Cristo" (an anime adaptation of "The Count of Monte Cristo" set in the 51st century including mechas and aliens and the titular count is possessed by a vengeance demon called Gankutsuou), I felt that this would work quite well as a Changeling story. I haven't read the original novel, so most of my knowledge of the story is from the anime.

                                              Edmond Dantes, instead of being sent off to some prison somewhere, his rivals manage to send him to Arcadia. Or he actually is accused of a crime he didn't commit and sent to prison and while there, he is taken by the True Fae. I also considered the possibility of replacing Fernand Mondego's role in the story, the man who marries Edmond's old lover Mercedes, with a fetch of Edmond (this works if his rivals send him directly to Arcadia, but if he is sent to prison first his fetch would probably still be there).

                                              While in Arcadia, he encounters Gankutsuou, the king of the cavern (or Gankutsuou might be his keeper). If Gankutsuou is the keeper, he releases him because he thinks this whole vengeance-thing will probably be amusing. If he isn't, then he probably helps Edmond escape somehow.

                                              Edmond Dantes returns to the human world, using his changeling powers to hide his true identity, and calls himself the Count of Monte Cristo, secretly plotting his revenge on those who sent him away and took his life from him.
