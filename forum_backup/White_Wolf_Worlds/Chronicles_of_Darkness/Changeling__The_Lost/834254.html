          I think this has to do in part with what you and your players are into, as well as what the game needs. I'm a pretty political guy and like dealing with large group dynamics; the kind of populations people often posit seem incredibly small and restrained to me, an artificial social horizon just as forced as the hero of the story always needing to deal with friends and rivals instead of institutions or the needs of the many. So I tend to bulk numbers up for games like Vampire or (often) Changeling, where I want to see parties and factions and so forth. For a game like Mage, the size of a university's faculty community is a feature. For Werewolf, numbers often detract. So: think about what purpose groups serve in your game, how numbers work with the game's themes, and what kind of social canvas your players are into.

          I often think of Dunbar's Number as a handy dividing line; if you're keeping it below 100-150, everybody in the community is going to know each other somewhat personally. Start going above that number and you're talking groups instead of people. Most WoD games steer towards below that number, because it's easier for people to keep straight in their head, less work for the ST, etc.

              Half year ago we have the discussion about supernatural populations numbers based on the Beast: The Primodrial SAS "Morning After" estimations. Here is the most accurate sum up we come with

              Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
              At least if we don't assume even more splats, those seem as pretty good numbers. As a rule of thumb, so, we can agree that a regular city will have 0,05% of its population as Major Monsters. That is, 1:2.000 people. Any given splat that forms a comunity will be little more than 1:20.000. I personaly would like even less, but can't complain about it now. Among other things, I don't see the need to account every splat for every urban area. Any city can be free of some of those guys.

              From them, we have how to figure variations. Those numbers should work best on big cities and metropolitan areas. Big places, with enough people, variability and travel routes to effectively run toward middle numbers.

              The smaller the place, the more it can change from the expected numbers. The thing here isn't to avoid this, but to account for consequences. A community with more of any given splat than it should support will be more affected for it. Well, the kind of stories told by the WoD does have as a common trope the Secrets of the Small Towns.

              So when dealing with a less populated region, decide cautiously about the numbers. Mantaining those numbers or even reducing them should make for a game of few splats. That isn't bad. With few vamps, wolfs, mages or fae you would have a game with themes of isolation, strangeness, or even pioneering, as well as possible incestuous societies, where you have to deal constantly with the same faces every time. Such a theme can make great games.

              Making for more splats on such places isn't bad, either. The thing here is to remember that you just broke the security line. Your chosen splat will have a greater impact, more people will know and this society will be even culturally affected by this situations. Imagine a town where everyone knows that you shouldn't cross the path of the people of the Hill. Or a city where the Mayor is chosen by unknown hands, and he and his officers learn of the true powers that be.

              Even if you don't want this, most people want moar numbah without it being really necessary. Some chronicles won't need more than 40 vampires living in the city at a time, never, not even accounting for the nameless guys to give the impression of a full party. You can give the impression of a complex All Night Society with just that much. And if you ever need a new guy, remember that you can bring him into the fold from the outside. The PCs aren't the only new Embraces, specially when people die. And remember those outcities numbers. Travelers can easily account for new characters when you need them and left quick enough for you to not have to bother with the numbers.

              By my side, I would use 1:25 instead of 1:10, but again, personal taste. With that I can move more freely the numbers and cut out some splats to give room to the ones I think should live there. I don't see most cities housing every splat, or even every social splat.
              General numbers are 1:20 000 Major Template to mortals ration. You can make those number fluctuate to the needs of your setting.

                                      My eyebally rule of thumb is to have a number of tiers:

                                      Tier 0: Limited number in existence (Mummy).
                                      Tier 1: One per hundred million (Promethean).
                                      Tier 2: One per hundred thousand (Sin-Eater, Demon?, Beast?).
                                      Tier 3: One per ten thousand (Changeling, Mage).
                                      Tier 4: One per five thousand (Vampire, Werewolf).
                                      Tier 5: One per thousand (Hunter, Minor Template Mortal).

                                      Take New York City. The latest census estimated 8,491,079 people. Let's round that to 8.5 million for convenience.

                                      This gives us:

                                      As many Mummies and Prometheans as the ST deems fit.
                                      8 Demons?
                                      85 Sin-Eaters (and Demons? and Beasts?).
                                      850 Changelings and Mages.
                                      1700 Vampires and Werewolves.
                                      8500 Hunters and Minor Template Mortals.

                                      All of this adds up to 11143 people "in the know" about the supernatural, the vast majority of which have some supernatural capability of their own. This is 0.131% of New York's population. If we narrow it down to Major Templates only, the number is reduced to 2643 creatures, or 0.031%.

                                      I am personally quite happy with these numbers.

                                          Changelings are made, not born, so the ratios can vary widely, and things can happen to drop or raise the population.

                                          Vancouver has 2 Changelings, because something bad happened there for example.

                                          I think the .5% of population, with 3/4 being minor templates is good ratio for most cities that do not have something disrupting they're numbers.

                                          So in the GTA the population is 6,000,000 people, that's a Supernatural template population of 30,000, not including those with a supernatural merit.

                                          7,500 of that is major templates. Promeathans nomadic, so I won't include them as long term inhabitants for this calculation and mummies are usually too low in population to worry about, out side of a few cities like Washington DC so I'll leave them out too. Ghosts, Hobgoblins, Spirits, Angels and thst like I don't even include in the 30,000.

                                          That leaves Vampires, Werewolves, Mages, Changelings, Sineaters, Demons, and Beasts.

                                          That's 1,070 per group, but since Changeling is the main splat for Toronto and Werewolves tend to have smaller populations, I'll say make the Changeling population 1,430 and werewolves (and Changeling Breeds) 720.

                                          This 1,430 population is divided between 3 freeholds, a with the Sultana's freehold being by far the largest and the Seasonal and Seelie freeholds being much smaller rebel Freeholds, but which have access to outside allies in other cities.

                                          So the Seasonal Freehold and Seelie Freehold could have say 200 each.

                                          That leaves 1000 for the main Freehold.

                                          Divided between the main 4 courts thats 250 each, while if the Ruby court isn't extinct anymore, then the last 30 could be members of the elitist Ruby Court.

                                          Breaking that down further, the Jade Court could have say 8 monarchs answering the Emperor of the Court, with each Monarch having roughly 25 members of their ethinic group, the rest answering directly to the Emperor.
