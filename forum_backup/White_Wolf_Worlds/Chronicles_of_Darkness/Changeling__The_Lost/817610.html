    Thoughts on the Beast Preview

    01-27-2016, 11:12 AM
    I've been reading the Beasts preview and for some reason part of it just bugs me. It seems to focus a whole lot on being a sexual predatory animal, and less on the whole throwing away your dignity and self-respect from an overriding need to escape by any means. It seems to raw-raw on the whole gleeful abandon of human civility, rather than it being a consequence of throwing away your dignity in the urge to escape. I mean I've read instances where people will do all sorts of insane things to escape confinement, including breaking bones or sawing off their own arms to escape bondage. One specific example that comes to mind is the story of Aron Ralston. However, beasts in their current state don't seem like they'd be able to represent such individuals.

    Maybe I'm just not getting it, but I do think it could help to focus a little bit more on human base instincts, and less on the sudden development of instincts from entirely separate creatures.

    EDIT: Not to mention, if they're throwing off all the trappings of humanity wholesale, how would they even return to the material world? Why trade one cage for another? It doesn't make a whole lot of sense based on the writing as is.

              1) No. The sexual predator might be easy to see, but it's not all sex, nor is it all predatory. It's screaming in a disorienting fashion to bamboozle the cop getting on you for a jaywalking ticket before running off, it's scratching your itchy crotch in a business meeting with a short skirt on, it's smelling people when you're introduced to them. I wouldn't award the Clarity bonus for all of these, but it's a variety of responses.

              2) The thing of the matter is that the Beast needs to make choices to remind themselves of how they freed themselves to ground out their sense of being in a sense of what belongs to them. I used animalistic stuff above because it's easy to communicate the idea, but really, the write up does skew towards the human side of taboo-breaking. Really, though, it opens up the range for players to define their characters. There's the punk who disrepects the norms of any sub-culture and place he's in, and there's also the wild man living in the sewers on rats. Beasts can be both.

              3) The thing to remember is that paranoia of being captured is, as a norm, higher than the risk of actually being captured. THe divide between the two is smaller than in first edition, but for the most part, as a psychological horror game, the simple fact is that the fairies are going to come less for you than you think they will, because the dread of them coming is one of the biggest emotional drives of the game.

              With this in mind, the Blessings aren't about flight responses, not exclusively, but rather tapping into the archetype of the moment you freed yourself to remind yourself that you have power over your own life. It's not about giving into the lizard-brain to run away, it's about indulging in the base to remind yourself that you aren't as restrained as you think you are. It's being unafraid of touching shit because the key is hidden in it and being okay with crying in public, even in Japan*, because fuck it, you need to cry. It's about being yourself, even when circumstances tell you the particulars of such is inappropriate for modern civilization.

              4)That said, it's an exercise of archetype, not a way of life. A Beast is not compelled to flaunt the niceities all the time. A Beast can go to the five-star restaurant and go all night without digging into the meal with hands and face or finger her date behind the table or anything-the Beast can honestly just have a nice, elegant evening. The Clarity Blessing is more a personalized focus for when the Clarity attacks come in. Going back to a Jessica Jones reference, it's the Beast going reciting street names from their childhood home area. You don't do it all the time. You do it when you really need to get your Clarity back up.

                      I look at it as not of abandoning people or connections, but of abandoning the more restrictive or "unnecessarily" complicated social rules of society. A Beast still wants friends and will act to protect them, but they're disinclined to play by the labyrinthine rules of polite society, especially when those rules get in the way of a simple, pragmatic method.

                      Table manners: "Why should I bother with the difference between all those forks? In fact, why should I use forks when I've got perfectly good hands?"
                      Sex: "Why all the complicated emotions, jealousy, marriage, and so on? I'm hot, you're hot. Can't we just do it?"
                      Combat: "Why can't I fight dirty or hit below the belt? This is a fight, not a game of checkers!"
                      Clothing: "I'm too sexy for my shirt. And shoes. Therefore, I can do without your service."
                      Tact: "Why can't I just blurt out what we're all thinking instead of dancing around the elephant in the room?"

                      That's the inner conflict Beasts have that makes it harder to simply disappear in the crowd of humanity. They get a stronger feeling of identity and agency by cutting through the social red tape because it echoes their escape and the feeling of liberation that came with it. It's not about becoming anti-social, it's about defying the social conventions that don't make sense or have no immediately recognizable purpose or benefit. They might look at some rules as being too reminiscent of the irrational rules their Keeper imposed on them. They might see some social rules as unjust laws tyrants use to keep their power and keep the common people unwilling to stand up for themselves because it'd be 'impolite' or subject them to public shaming. They might see social rules used as excuses to avoid thinking and thus lose agency in your own actions. Sometimes they might break rules just to prove to themselves that they're acting on their own, instead of just caving into the will of the public or of authority figures.
