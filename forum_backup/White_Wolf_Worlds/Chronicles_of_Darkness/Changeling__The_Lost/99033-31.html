                    Session LXII

                    Gawain’s Memories

                    November 20th, afternoon

                    Well that was a hell of a way to start the day. I mean, the start-start was fine - interfering in a tickle/pink lemonade fight between two pretty women isn’t so bad. I didn’t even get pink lemonade poured on me. I don’t think Gloria accepted that me tickling Desiree made up for Desiree tickling her though.                     That’s bad. That’s real bad. I figured it would be an S&M kinda thing. Calamity likes that stuff, and Leanne’s a Leechfinger and not leery of it like De la Roche is with all that. But that’s too much. After Leanne ran out of glamour, Calamity was still hurt bad enough Desiree had to heal her. I mean, maybe Leanne’s just a shitty healer, you know, or she’d been coming to the Dream House and not gathering glamour for multiple nights so she was low, but probably not. Probably that means she was close to dead. That’s.. that’s not OK.

                    The others chewed her out. Well, not really. Like Lori said, they were mostly mad at Leanne. It was, you know, Calamity’s choice, like she kept saying, but Desiree did talk a lot about how good both she and Leanne are at doing bad things to people and it never being their fault. You know, Desiree and Leanne’s fault. I don’t know. She seems nice, you know? But she could have killed her. I can’t lose her. And I’m not good at telling when people are, well, when they’re doing what Desiree said she and Leanne are really good at.

                    So, that was breakfast. After that, I, you know, let off some steam at the forge and Calamity worked on the routine for that In This Moment song with Lori. It was a few hours later, I think, when Calamity came and got me cause Moon and Juan Carlos were there.

                    They wanted to let us know that Chalcedony’s programming was gone and Queen Charlotte was letting her go. That was good, especially that Queen Charlotte was sticking to her guns with that, despite some of the other monarchs calling for death/exile/imprisonment. There was apparently contention since they still didn’t know if she was a loyalist by choice as well. But now at least she gets to make that choice, you know? It’s a choice most of us made. Admittedly, the fact that the Director seems to be a better dude than most Gentry makes it weird, but still. We can’t just kill anyone whose Durance wasn’t utter shit out of hand. Anyways, Moon and Juan Carlos came by to let us know special, in case she came after us. They didn’t think she would, but she was still upset with us, me and Calamity and Insincere Marguerite particularly.

I really like the books, and Jennifer Lawrence is pretty awesome. Just benignly being a threatening jerk face, stealing Rowan’s seat and just... being there. Threateningly. All I had was my Contracts of Potential, but still. Then Calamity and Insincere Marguerite got all darkling about it, like maybe she was luring Lori out to where we couldn’t get to her easily. They wouldn’t tell me whether we were really worried about it, so i went ahead and used my Contracts of Potential to see why she wanted her out there.                     Lorelei’s Diary

                    So I woke up late yesterday morning as a big ol’ ball of happy, surrounded by my darklings, safe and warm and loved… just the way I like it, in other words. I basked for a while, snuggling up against D’s chest and listening to her heartbeat until the others started to stir.

                    We all got kinda-sorta dressed like we usually do (Marguerite started to put one of my shirts on again, but Julia gave her a dirty look and she thought better of it) and headed downstairs in a big knot, murmuring happily to each other in a sleepy yet contented way. I mostly let Julia hang on Marguerite’s arm while I stuck with D, hoping that this would reinforce Julia’s confidence in Marguerite’s feelings toward her. I’m pretty sure D caught on to what I was doing, because she played along beautifully, whispering sweet nothings and occasionally pulling me into a random kiss on our way downstairs.

                    Unfortunately our good mood pretty much evaporated when we entered the kitchen. It was obvious that there was some tension in the air, and a few minutes later we learned why: Calamity had spent the previous evening with Leanne Sheets, and had barely survived the experience. Leanne had used her leechfinger abilities to drain Calamity while the two of them canoodled. The Hollywood refugee had healed Calamity as much as she could, but Calamity was still obviously flagging by the time she got home, and Desiree had to patch her up the rest of the way.

                    I was livid. I had the distinct impression that the rest of the motley felt the same way. And Calamity just stood there sort of meekly, defending Leanne by saying that she had known what she was getting into and it had been her decision. Desiree chimed in at that point, telling Calamity that Leanne is an expert at subtle manipulation, and she’s made a habit of maneuvering people into making bad decisions which they were convinced were their own ideas.

                    In the midst of my rage at how close I’d come to losing a friend, I looked at Calamity. I mean, I really looked at her. I didn’t like what I saw. She looked lost and a little bewildered, like she couldn’t quite process why we were all so upset. I could see her withdrawing, and I didn’t want that to happen. I reached out and took her hand.

                    “Hey, Calamity,” I said softly, “you know none of us are mad at you, right? We’re upset because someone hurt you, that’s all. Think about how you’d feel if someone hurt one of us…”

                    She nodded. “Thanks, Lori,” she said just as softly. “I know it was stupid. It was a bad decision.”

                    It didn’t occur to me until much later that she hadn’t actually said she wouldn’t do it again.

                    In any case, not everything was doom and gloom at breakfast. Gloria and Desiree seem to have mostly patched things up between them, which I know makes them both happy. There was some light-hearted threatening between the two of them (Desiree had been tickling Gloria mercilessly, and Gloria in turn threatened to pour pink lemonade all over Desiree’s head), but anyone could see from the twinkle in their eyes that it was all in fun.

                    So before we split up to take care of Dream House business, I pulled D aside for a moment. It wasn’t really private, but I don’t think either one of us cared much about that.

                    “What’s up, babe?” she asked curiously.

                    “You free tomorrow night?” I asked nonchalantly.

                    She rolled her eyes. “I’m always free if you need me to be, Lori… you know that. You always come first.”

                    I couldn’t help but smile at that, and I think I might have blushed a little too. I will never get used to hearing that amazing woman tell me how much she loves me. I don’t think I want to get used to it.

                    “So I was thinking, if you didn’t have plans... well, I still have that ecstasy that Marguerite picked up at Disneyland, and I’ve been wanting to try it. But, well, I don’t know how it’ll affect me, and…”

                    She just kept looking at me, quirking an eyebrow in that infuriatingly sexy way she has.

                    I sighed. “Dammit, D, I want to be with someone I trust when I do it. And I trust you more than anyone else in the world… certainly more than I trust myself.”

                    She chuckled and leaned in to give me a kiss. “It’s a date, babe,” she said lightly, “but does this mean you’ve already got plans for tonight?”

                    I shrugged noncommittally. “Not really. Certainly not if you need me for something. But, well, Rowan is supposed to drop by again tonight…”

                    She laughed again. Sometimes she really is insufferable. “Say no more,” she said with a grin.

                    I looked away. “It’s not like that, and you know it. She’s a really sweet girl, and she’s insecure, and she’s new to this whole scene. I don’t want her to think that I’m avoiding her after what we shared on Tuesday, okay?”

                    D smiled more seriously. “Okay, I get it,” she said. “Go on, do your thing. Make her feel like the luckiest seal on the beach. I’ll be here for you tomorrow.”

                    “Thanks,” I said, kissing her again.

                    A few minutes later Gloria walked up casually. “So… does this mean you’re free tonight?” she asked D shyly.

                    D smirked. “I’ll have to check my calendar. I might be able to work you in…”

                    They both looked supremely happy with this arrangement, and I managed to push down the twinge of jealousy I felt with only a minimum of effort. D loves me above and beyond everyone else. Gloria is a friend, and doesn’t want to see me get hurt. I repeated those facts to myself a couple of times like a mantra, and the tightness in my stomach seemed to ease up.

                    So after that Calamity and I got together on the stage and started choreographing our act. She’s going to be the main focus of course, as the dancer, while I mostly stand in the background and sing. We paced through a couple of options for a few hours. It’s still going to take a lot of work to get it perfect, but I think we have the beginnings of something memorable here.

                    We had a few visitors as Calamity and I were strutting and fretting our hours upon the stage (wow… ain’t I cultured?). First, Felicity stopped by to speak with Marguerite. I don’t claim to know what that was about; I was under the impression that the matter of the Day and Night crowns had come between the two of them, and their burgeoning romance had sputtered and died. And things did seem tense between them, so maybe she had some other reason for gracing our doorstep after all.

                    As she was leaving, she ran afoul of Desiree. I know the two of them have never liked each other – at least not since the matter of Princess Jolie came between them – but I could have sworn that the temperature dropped ten degrees when they saw each other. I mean, I admit that Felicity hasn’t been my favorite person since the incident with Marguerite a few weeks ago, but Desiree must really hate her.

                    Our next two visitors were much more pleasant… though they were here on somewhat somber business. Moon and Juan-Carlos dropped by. I was about to utter my usual squeal and jump into Juan-Carlos’ arms, but the look on his face brought me up short, so I had to content myself with a sober embrace as I welcomed the two of them into the theater. Calamity and I dispersed throughout the house to get the rest of the motley together, and we let them say what they had to say.

                    There’s good news as well as bad. Chalcedony’s dream therapy is finally over, and Juan-Carlos is convinced that she’s now completely free of the Director’s programming. The only issue is that he doesn’t know how extensive that programming was, so we have no way of knowing for certain whether she was a Loyalist by choice. So the Autumn Court has been keeping her sedated and away from the rest of the Freehold… until now.

                    Queen Charlotte put her foot down; she won’t hold another changeling against her will when there’s no proof that she’s done anything wrong. She also won’t allow any other punishments to be meted out (such as exile or execution)… and I say good for her. Sure, it’s more dangerous to give people the benefit of the doubt, but if we start punishing people for things we suspect they might be guilty of, then where does it end?

                    So Chalcedony is being released into the wild, and Juan-Carlos and Moon begged Queen Charlotte to delay that release until they could let us into the loop. Because Chalcedony is pissed at us. Very pissed. And honestly, who could blame her? Nobody really thinks she’ll act against us in a violent way, but it can be hard to tell in these circumstances, so they wanted us on our guard.

                    I took Marguerite’s hand during this conversation, because she looked absolutely stricken. I still think Chalcedony was manipulating my French lover, whether through magic or just raw personality, but I also know that she had a major crush on the blue girl, and hearing that Chalcedony is holding a grudge against her couldn’t have been easy. She fled the scene quickly after that conversation. I would have gone after her to make sure she was okay, but I got the impression she wanted to be alone (and anyway, Julia followed after her, and I’m on thin enough ice with her as it is).

                    It was almost time to get ready for the show at that point, so I was prepared to head back to my room to get showered and dressed. Gawain approached as Calamity and I were finalizing plans to continue working on our shared act the next day, and told his girlfriend that Gloria and Desiree had invited them to go see some movie premiere at midnight. Reading between the lines, I think she might have appreciated the chance to help out around the Dream House in an official capacity; alone among the motley in residence there, she doesn’t actually work at the theater. I sidled up next to her before the evening ended and sort of casually asked if she was thinking about quitting her job and working security for us full time. She was cagey about her answer. I think she’s privately torn between maintaining her independence and spending more time with the rest of us. I let her know that the option was always there (which might have been the teensiest bit beyond my authority to promise, but I don’t think anyone else would challenge the decision) when and if she was ready for it.

I scanned the audience quickly and located Rowan across the room and a little further back than she usually sits, and tipped her a wink in response to her hesitant wave.

                    After my number I made my way around the crowd, stopping in to talk to Rowan first to make sure she was okay and to promise her that I’d join her shortly. That sweetheart had bought me flowers and had them delivered to the bar, and I gave her a kiss to let her know how much I appreciated the thought. After that I made the rounds, making sure to talk to all of our regulars (including that mortal guy who keeps asking me out every other night).

                    I stopped by Princess Jolie’s table for a while. It was the first time I’d seen her at the Dream House in quite some time, and I wanted to pay my respects. As has happened all too frequently recently, she looked deeply depressed. This time the source of her malaise seemed to be Leanne’s recent tryst with Queen Charlotte. I commiserated with her for a little while as best I could (in the wake of Calamity’s encounter, I found it remarkably easy to bad-mouth Leanne), and made sure she knew that I’d always be around if she needed to talk, but in the end there really wasn’t much I could do to help her.

                    Molly was at the bar as usual. We chatted for a little while, but all I really got out of her was that she was going to be shadowing Chalcedony, and that she was worried about that for some reason. Reading between the lines, I wonder if Molly maybe has a little crush on Chalcedony? I’m not sure if that would be a good thing or a bad thing. On the one hand, it could interfere with her duties, but on the other hand… well, it can only be a step forward if she forms meaningful relationships with other changelings, right?

                    In any case, all such thoughts were quickly driven from my mind when I joined Rowan at her table. She confessed that she was pretty spooked by TJ, but I once again assured her that the Summer Courtier wouldn’t dare bother her in the Dream House. She confessed that her anxiety was more about what he might do outside of the Dream House. Apparently he had delivered her paper to her doorstep that morning.

Unfortunately, TJ is pretty good at skating the lines of propriety, and he hadn’t actually done anything actionable against Rowan yet. D promised to escort the two of us to Rowan’s car when it was time for us to leave to ensure that he wasn’t lurking in the parking lot.

                    Before too much longer it was time for Gawain and Calamity (and Luna, apparently) to head out to the film. It sounded like fun, actually… I haven’t been out to the movies since we all went to see Guardians of the Galaxy in August. But this film (Mockingbird or something like that, I think) was part of a series, and I hadn’t seen any of the others yet, so it didn’t seem like a good place to start. Apparently Gawain’s a fan of the books those films are based on, and he was almost giddy with excitement.

                    After things wound down, Rowan and I took our leave as well, and D was as good as her word, walking us out and giving me a tender kiss goodbye (which didn’t seem to bother Rowan much, to her credit). I’d never been sailing before, and I don’t get many opportunities to get out and play in the sun with my schedule, so I eagerly agreed. She sort of hesitantly asked if I might like to go the next day, and I told her that it would be fine as far as I knew, but I wanted to check my schedule with the rest of the motley before making any promises. She allowed as how that would be fair, and we drifted off breathing softly into each other’s hair.

Most of the others were happily chatting about the movie they’d seen the night before, and they seemed to have enjoyed themselves. D and Gloria had hooked up when she got back to the Dream House, of course, and they also seemed to have enjoyed themselves, but I didn’t let it bother me.
