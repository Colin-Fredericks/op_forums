                    Session XXXVII

                    Gawain’s Memories

                    Jun 28th

                    Man, today has been the day for coming up with hard decisions. I mean, it’s gonna be hard enough finding something both in my idiom and symbolically appropriate to the capture of dreams, but I also have to come up with a movie that epitomizes the 90s. Such I hard life I lead, you know?

                    The second bit there came up at breakfast today. Insincere Marguerite decided that for our next move in the war of the decades, we should all pick a movie that will serve as a champion for our identified decade. Lori is the 70s, Calamity the 80s, and Jacob represents for the 00s. Me and Insincere Marguerite are sharing the 90s. It’s a serious responsibility, you know? I haven’t gotten any good ideas yet, but I’m sure one will come to me.

                    We all were pretty busy the rest of the day. Insincere Marguerite and Julia were off to Anaheim to visit Julia’s parents. Lori and De la Roche went off to Muscle Beach to work out, then Lori sat with Princess Jolie while Andromeda used the Table on her. Calamity and Jacob visited Boss Lojack to explain about Jacob’s past, before it came up in a more damning light. Calamity was worried about me rattling around the House, you know, with only Ace for company, but Juan Carlos had told me I could look him up for some lessons on making dream prisons and dream combat, so I assured her I’d be fine.

                    Folks’ things went well, from all accounts. Julia was afraid her parents like Insincere Marguerite better than her, mostly cause they told embarrassing stories about when she was a kid and drew boobs or lip synced heavy metal songs. But that’s what parents do, you know? I guess... Jacob and Calamity were fairly tight lipped, but no one was hurt and Boss Lojack was still cool with him being in his Entitlement, so I guess it went well? The Table worked (Lori thinks) but Andromeda was really drained. Still, we all knew that was a possibility, so Joan came and picked her up and is, you know, taking care of her.

                    Juan Carlos and me were at it for a few hours, over at his townhouse. It was my first time and all, so it took me a bit to get the hang of it. See, he makes clay pots, to trap the dreams in, painted in a Grecian style to represent the kind of dream within. That’s just his way though, they can be all kinds of things, so I oughta, you know, come up with my own thing. I mean, throwing pots was fun and all, but I was thinking something in metal. It will probably involve my jeweler’s tools more than my heavy duty stuff. Especially for a ‘stealth’ version, for when I need to sneak something like that in somewhere. I did make one in a pot version, so he could give me pointers on the dreamy part of it. I think it turned out pretty good, myself. We’ll see if it’s strong enough to catch one of my dreams.

                    Calamity was schooling noobs on Gears of War when I got back, so I joined her for a few maps until, you know, it was time for the show. We even got an audience for a bit. The turn around on Lori’s part from ‘why are you being so mean to these kids?’ to ‘shoot them again Calamity!’ was almost worth listening to the little twerps talking to Calamity that way. Almost.

                    The show was awesome. Calamity came out of getting ready looking like a priest who raided a velvet factory (Becky was coming by) and so Insincere Marguerite mentioned she’d put in It’s a Sin to the line up. And Calamity decided to dance to it! It was super great. She managed to even put a distinct air of spooky into the incredibly sexy dance Insincere Marguerite has choreographed and it was just, you know, awesome. Lori did some good songs too.

                    Calamity made a beeline for Becky and I’d gotten the impression she wanted to, you know, blow off some steam, so I hung out with De la Roche and the girls. Something killed the conversation at some point, I don’t remember what, so I asked folks for ideas about dream containers. That didn’t do much to help cause it made Lori think of birdcages, but then Insincere Marguerite theatrically complained again about De la Roche calling her gringo, even though she called all of us gringos really, and it’s true anyway. That did cheer things up better, and soon we were talking about girl piles with Insincere Marguerite cherries. You know, the usual things. We were talking about them being in the Hedge House, which is not so usual, but I guess Lori just wants to get more comfy with being on that side of things.

                    We also decided what to have for breakfast tomorrow, and by ‘we decided’, I mean that Insincere Marguerite indicated she wanted crepes and looked at me with big eyes until I said OK. That got De la Roche talking about her family some, which she hasn’t really done much apparently. She wasn’t real close, you know, cause they have kinda strict ideas about gender roles and stuff, and she doesn’t really fit into their ideas of right. It seemed like maybe she missed some of the foods her mom makes and I can maybe help with that, you know? I mean, I’m sure mine won't be as good as her mom’s, cause that’s the way these things work, but I could at least make that kinda food.

                    Thinking back over all this and getting it down, we did talk a lot about families and parents and things today (De la Roche is gonna get dragged to church one of these Sundays). Poor Calamity was getting a little depressed and drink-y about it. Hopefully Becky’s treating her good. Tomorrow’s bound to cheer her up some too, since she’s taking Lori and Jacob to the shooting range for practice. It didn’t throw her off her game with the dancing either, so I’m sure she’s alright.


                    Lorelei’s Diary

                    Something has changed within me. Something is not the same.

                    Okay, I admit it: I downloaded the soundtrack to Wicked after Oleander recommended it to me the other day, and I might have listened to it a time or twelve since then. But that doesn’t make the opening strains of “Defying Gravity” any less appropriate.

                    Maybe I’m getting stronger. Maybe now that some of my other responsibilities have been fulfilled, things look clearer to me. Maybe I just finally realized that I’ve messed around for long enough, and it’s time to get serious. Whatever the reason is, I know that I can’t put off rescuing Missy and Jane any longer.

                    D and I got up yesterday morning and prepared to head down to Muscle Beach for a workout, as agreed. I put on a snug sports bra and a pair of bike pants, figuring I could soak up a little glamour while we exercised. My outfit got a few appreciative ogles at breakfast, and Calamity made some salacious comments about our plans for the workout.

                    D grinned at her in a mock-exasperated way. “Lori may be a nympho, but we are capable of focusing on other things,” she explained as we walked away.

                    Is that how my friends see me? Okay, I know I like sex… I might even technically be a nymphomaniac; I’m not sure how you diagnose or classify something like that. But is that really my defining feature? Is that as deep as my character goes? I know D didn’t mean to hurt my feelings with that statement… ordinarily I wear my libido as a badge of honor, but at that time it felt like a slight even if it wasn’t intended as one.

                    So maybe I was a little more focused during our workout than usual. I got advice from D and Monica (and Juan-Carlos, who was also there) about how to target specific muscle groups to start building up strength and stamina. And while I know I still have a long way to go, I think I got in a good workout before it was time to shower and head out.

                    I accomplished something else while we were there, too. I asked Monica about her duties for the Bishopric of Blackbirds. She seemed almost eager to talk about it, and when I asked hesitantly if they had any sort of “ride-along” program, she quickly informed me that there was nothing formal in place, but that she’d be glad to have me along if I wanted to go with her someday.

                    “When are you heading in next?” I asked nonchalantly.

                    “I thought I’d do a small patrol on Monday,” she returned.

                    “That would be perfect,” I said eagerly, “I don’t perform on Monday nights, so I wouldn’t have to be back early or anything.”

                    “It probably won’t be very exciting,” she warned me, “things in the local Hedge have been pretty quiet since we beat back the Mothership.”

                    I nodded, and refrained from mentioning that I like it when the Hedge is quiet. “But you’ll be able to teach me a few things about navigating in there, right? Like how to find trods and spot dangers and such?”

                    “Sure!” she said with a grin. “I’d be glad to.”

                    D shot me a couple of inquisitive looks during this conversation, but she refrained from asking about it. I think deep down she knows what I have in mind, and isn’t really surprised that I’m finally working myself up to it. I dropped her off at her apartment with a promise that I’d see her at the show that night, went back to the Dream House to change, and then headed across Santa Monica to… brrrr… the Children’s Library.

                    Okay, so writing it out like that doesn’t really cover just how creepy that place really was. In my own defense, I’ll just note that Madame Spidertoes calls it home, and it suits her. Andromeda, Princess Jolie, Becky Wan and I were ushered into a secret room at the back of the library, down a set of stairs, and into a chamber containing the infamous Table. I thanked Andromeda and Madame Spidertoes for allowing us to do what had to be done, and the little alien girl went to work.

                    It took a few tense hours of work. Andromeda strapped Jolie down and began manipulating the Table, and eventually reaching into Jolie’s body to make the alterations. Jolie didn’t seem to mind it most of the time… her moans and screams didn’t really sound like pain if you know what I mean. But Becky was as tense as a guitar string through the whole process. And sometimes Jolie’s familiar beautiful visage seemed to fade, and a pale grey alien face took its place, and Becky really didn’t like that.

                    I did my best to calm her down. I took her hand in my own. “Andromeda knows what she’s doing,” I assured her. “This is a good thing, Becky. Jolie wants it, and she’ll be better off afterward.” Becky nodded and squeezed my hand, but she kept her lips pursed tightly the whole time.

                    When it was over, both Jolie and Andromeda looked exhausted. Jolie yawned and stretched and said that she wanted to go home and get some sleep. Andromeda nodded that this was a good idea, and stated that she thought the operation had been a success. Jolie gave me a hug and thanked me for my part in helping to cure her condition, and then allowed Becky to quickly usher her out of the creepy secret basement room.

                    I turned to Madame Spidertoes and solemnly thanked her for her hospitality (she gave me a regal nod in response) before turning my attention back to Andromeda. “You okay, sweetie?” I asked, concerned.

                    “I am fine,” she replied in a dead monotone.

                    “I’m sorry you had to do that, Andromeda,” I said desperately, “I wouldn’t have asked if it hadn’t been so important…”

                    “It is fine,” she replied in the same empty voice. “I will require rest now.”

                    I called up Joan, truly concerned. Joan swung by the library a few minutes later and picked Andromeda up. I tried to apologize for putting her girlfriend in such a situation, and Joan waved it off. “You and your motley have been true friends to us; she was happy to help you in return. And don’t worry, she was like this after she helped me as well. I am confident she will recover.”

                    I remembered how thin and stretched Mr. Grey had seemed before his untimely demise. “Joan,” I said softly, “don’t let her use it again. Not for anything, okay?”

                    Joan nodded, and for a second I saw how worried she really was, but then it was gone. She ushered Andromeda gently into the passenger seat of her car – I’d never seen her be that careful with anything before – and said her goodbyes. I watched them drive off before heading for my own car. I really hope they’ll be all right.

                    I got back to the Dream House an hour or two before the doors opened to the public to find Gawain and Calamity playing some video game online. It was pretty violent, and Calamity seemed to get off on spouting profanity at the people they were playing against. I was frankly shocked at some of the language I heard from her, but the guys on the other end seemed to give (almost) as good as they got, so I assumed it was all in good fun.

                    Marguerite and Julia had gone up to Anaheim for the day to meet Julia’s parents, and they returned about half an hour after I got back. Apparently things went well. Marguerite certainly looked pleased with herself, and told us that Julia’s parents had shared all sorts of embarrassing stories about her as a child. Julia was mortified, but I think in an odd way she was pleased with the day’s events as well.

                    We had a little time to talk before the show, and one of the things that came up was Marguerite’s idea that each of us should pick a movie to represent our signature decade, and then the whole motley should watch these films. Calamity will represent the 80s, Marguerite and Gawain will choose for the 90s, and Jacob will pick for the 2000s. Naturally, yours truly will be choosing a film to represent the 70s. It’s not as easy as it sounds. Most of the best films of that decade seem to be set in other decades, so they’re not really representative. “The Godfather,” “M*A*S*H*,” “Grease,” “Cabaret,” “American Graffiti…” There’s also “Star Wars,” of course, but that doesn’t really say much about the world of the 1970s.

                    I think I’ve narrowed it down to four films. The obvious choice for a film to demonstrate culture in the late 1970s is “Saturday Night Fever,” but that seems a little on the nose. There was more to the 70s than Disco culture, after all. “The French Connection” reinvented the crime drama, bringing a gritty, real-world feel to a genre which had previously been filled with idealized black-and-white cops n’ robbers stories and sneering noir anti-heroes. “Annie Hall” was a revelation in its day, and I’m told it sort of set the stage for the future of the romantic comedy genre. And according to the internet, “Jaws” is almost single-handedly responsible for the development of the summer blockbuster.

                    In any case, as showtime approached I had to bid adieu to this lively discussion and go off to ruin a cheap dress. I’d found a cheap frumpy white dress at a local Goodwill and purchased it for about $2 a couple of days previous, and I spent about ten minutes carefully cutting a dashed line down its back. With considerable effort I managed to squeeze into it without ripping the fabric further… which was made more difficult by the fact that I had tucked my wings in tight against my back and pulled the dress on over them.

                    My first number came up early in the show, which was good, because while I was wearing that dress I had to be extremely careful whenever I moved. As the opening strains of Halestorm’s “Ms. Hyde” played over the club’s speakers, I strutted out onto the stage in my virginal white dress and a pair of prop glasses (thick black frames with no lenses), with my hair pulled back into a tight bun. Then when the first chorus started up, I spread my wings energetically, tearing the dress away and revealing the lacy black lingerie underneath to the audience. The glasses came off next, and then I shook my hair out of the bun.

                    Okay, so it was a little more stripper-y than I usually go, but the crowd seemed to enjoy it, and I didn’t let the disrobing go any further than that.

                    I only had one other solo tonight, and I toned it down a bit with a sultry version of the Classics IV song “Spooky.” I suppose I could have flipped the genders like Dusty Springfield did in her cover version, but at the last minute I decided to leave ‘em as they were originally written. I like girls. I’m sure this brave new world can deal with it.

                    Oh, I should mention that Calamity also made her stage debut last night, and she was fucking amazing! She strutted and danced around the stage to one of the songs Marguerite choreographed (“It’s a Sin” by the Pet Shop Boys) in a black velvet number that had pulses racing all over the club. I don’t much like Annika, but I have to admit that she must be a good teacher; Calamity looked great up there. While she was on the stage, she owned that audience, and I couldn’t be happier for her. Becky – who had stopped by to see the show after escorting Princess Jolie safely home – looked pleased at the performance too.

                    In the wake of Calamity’s command performance, Insincere Marguerite suggested that she might want to try her hand at singing for an audience in the not-too-distant future. She mentioned a French song called “Non, Je Ne Regrette Rien.” I told her that it sounded like a wonderful idea to me, though I’ll want to hear her sing it before I give my final okay to her proposal. Privately I’m sure she’ll be fine, and Calamity was similarly enthusiastic in her support. It’s nice to see so many of my darkling friends coming out of their shells.

                    Calamity and Becky made themselves scarce pretty much as soon as the show was over, and as usual I found myself seated at a table with Jacob, Gawain, and my darkling lovers. Talk ranged all over the place. Gawain was looking for inspiration and advice on constructing a “dream container,” something he can use to trap nightmares and other dreams. He said that Juan-Carlos uses pottery vessels, but that he wanted to do something a little more personal (which we all understood meant something metal). I sort of half-heartedly suggested a miniature cage, but that got me thinking about birdcages, which sort of killed my enthusiasm for the conversation.

                    Jesus, I must be the only person in the world who routinely gets depressed thinking about birdcages. But with all due respect to the recently departed Maya Angelou, I also know why the caged bird sings… because her owner will fucking murder her if she gets bored enough.

                    Talk turned to Julia’s parents again. They’re apparently hippies (they still have hippies these days!). This led to D mentioning something about never taking me to meet her family, as she didn’t feel like suffering the same embarrassment Julia had just gone through.

                    I took her hand gently. “That might be the first time I’ve ever heard you mention your family, D,” I said softly.

                    “Well, there’s a reason for that,” she said, not meeting my gaze. “They’re very traditional. They have very definite opinions about gender roles and how proper young women should behave. I don’t see them much.”

                    That might have been the saddest thing I’ve ever heard her say. She made it back from Arcadia, had no Fetch, and was able to reconnect with her family… only to become estranged from them for perfectly mundane, human reasons. I squeezed her hand. “Well,” I said, “you can probably look forward to hearing my dad embarrass me horribly someday, even if he doesn’t realize he’s doing it. I think it’s like a universal law that parents have to do that. That is if you ever get around to joining me for a church service…”

                    She tensed again, and I sighed. “When you’re ready, sweetie.”

                    “I will,” she promised again. “I just have to work up to it.”

                    After that, I asked Jacob if he wanted to go out camping or hiking some time. I figured I ought to at least try to pick up the basics of wilderness survival if I’m going to venture back into the Hedge, and he knows more about that stuff than anyone else I’ve ever met. I may never be Aragorn, but I’m hoping he can at least teach me to recognize the difference between a pothole and a footprint, or show me how to set up a shelter that won’t collapse on me while I sleep. He seemed willing to try, though he gave me a look that indicated he was questioning my motives.

                    I also asked the girls if they would mind sleeping in the Hollow last night. That got a lot of raised eyebrows, let me tell you. I haven’t exactly made a secret of the fact that I don’t like going into the Hedge… even into the relatively safe area around the Hollow. This, of course, is pretty much why I wanted to try to spend more time there, to acclimate myself and get used to the way things work there.

                    So we did sleep there. It was nice to spend the night with Marguerite and Julia again. Julia in particular has been very supportive lately, and sometimes I feel like she doesn’t get enough of my attention. I tried to reassure her last night that she is never far from my thoughts, and I think I succeeded.

                    Once again I got up much earlier than any of the others, headed back to the Dream House (alone!) and got ready for church. The others were awake and munching on crepes when I got back, and I had time to scarf down a few before I had to change and head out again. I’d asked Calamity to take me down to the firing range for another lesson in marksmanship, and Jacob decided to come with us as well.

                    I asked D if she wanted to come along, and she got kind of sheepish. “Gun ranges around here tend to be sort of… unofficially segregated,” she explained. “I doubt your NRA buddies would appreciate a Latina chica hanging out in their little clubhouse.”

                    I was disappointed, but prepared to leave it at that. I should have known that my honey wouldn’t let something like that lie for long, though. After a few minutes of talking the situation out with herself, she decided that she wasn’t going to take that sort of attitude lying down, and insisted that she come with us just to spite these theoretical racists.

                    Ordinarily I find my love’s in-your-face attitude endearing, but I think she took it too far today. She was taunting the guys in the lanes next to me as I was shooting, trying to throw off their aim and rile them up. I know she’s Summer Court, but honestly, those boys were just minding their own business and not bothering anyone else in the least. I think I even caught her hexing one of them to spoil some of his shots. When the two of us retrieved our targets, she started ridiculing him about how he couldn’t even outshoot a girl.

                    I was seething all the way home, and I sort of retreated into my room and started writing this diary entry as soon as we got back. I think D is hanging out with Marguerite and Julia… she must have taken the hint that I wanted to be alone.

                    I don’t get it, I really don’t. She was so determined to see a problem where there wasn’t one that she ended up being the problem in the end. I know her Court gains glamour from anger, but I was trying to improve my skills, and she was causing a distraction I couldn’t ignore. And dammit, it’s important for me to learn to defend myself if I’m going to mount this rescue mission.

                    So I’ve been stewing alone in here for about an hour, wondering what I should do next. A part of me wants to just let D chew on things for a while, and maybe spend the night with Oleander, but I know I can’t do that. Both D and Oleander have been very accommodating in letting me have my cake and eat it too, and the minute I start using them as weapons against each other is the minute this whole open relationship stops being okay.

                    I love D. Being mad at her doesn’t change that. Nothing will change that. I just need to make her understand that my priorities have shifted, and that I have to stop fooling around and focus on getting ready for the trials to come.

                    Survival. Jacob can teach me that, which will help me cope with the dangers of the Hedge itself. Monica and Oleander can help me acclimate to the unique challenges the magical borderlands present. Insincere Marguerite can probably help me with the basics of stealth, which will have to be my go-to option for encounters in the Hedge. If I have to fight, I’ll fall back on my Contracts of Vainglory to scare the enemies off, and in an absolute last resort I’ll have my pistol. I’ll continue working out with Monica and D to build up my strength and stamina in case I have to run for it.

                    I’ll also need to work on improving my healing abilities. The Contracts of Eternal Spring offer miraculous curative abilities, but my mastery of them is still limited by my lack of knowledge of the human body and how it actually works. I need to learn medicine, and I need to learn it quickly. There’s only so much that online classes and CPR lessons at the Y can do for me, but I don’t have time to go back to school for real. And let’s face it, I’ve never been the smartest girl on the block. Maybe if I’d spent more time in High School studying and less time partying and trying to get laid…

                    No, this isn’t about me. It’s about the girls I left behind in the Empress’ Realm. For them, I’ll find a way.

                    No, I haven’t told the rest of the motley what I’m planning, but I haven’t been exactly subtle about it either. Hell, I even asked Calamity this morning if she could get me access to the Autumn Court’s library so I could see if they have any intelligence on the Empress. The motley knows about Missy and Jane. I told them that I’ll have to go back one of these days, and they even offered to help me rescue them at the time.

                    I can’t ask them to do that. This is my responsibility, and I’m not going to risk my friends’ lives or freedom to correct my mistakes. Now don’t get me wrong; I’m not going to sneak off alone in the middle of the night and try to do this myself. If the others insist on helping me, I won’t be so ungrateful as to demand they stay behind. But I won’t ask. Accepting help that’s offered is one thing. Roping someone into a mission like this unwilling is another thing entirely.

                    D will want to come with me, and my God in heaven that terrifies me. I can’t ask her to stay behind, but I will not let the Empress have her. I’d rather see us both dead than risk that.

                    Lorelei Grace


                    Jacob’s Journal
