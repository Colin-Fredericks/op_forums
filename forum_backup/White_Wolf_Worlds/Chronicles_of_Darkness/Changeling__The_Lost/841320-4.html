    I've got a huge pile of unused/not-completely-read books. Been trying to do something about that recently, though. Getting started with a mortal, hoping to try a wolf or 'ling next. Mage looks fun, but it's rules heavy, so very rules heavy.

    As for that Sphinx, he was the kind of person who'd give great advice, insightful in what people wanted and needed, he just expressed it by baiting and antagonizing people until he had them pointed in the right direction. Lots of leading questions and teasing one-off comments. Fidgety as hell too.

    His durance saw him as one of the various monsters tasked with guarding a castle from a group of invaders, led by his keeper in the form of a shining knight, who would defeat them all time and time again. After the first several cycles of getting himself wounded or killed trying to fight them off, and he learned exactly what the punishment for not trying was, he settled into an 'Answer me these questions three' routine that the keeper found entertain enough to play along with, eventually earning him the form in which he'd escape.

    That escape came when, after an uncountable number of repetitions on the same scenario, he challenged the knights, as he always would, lost as he had done many times before, and threw himself off the tower, which was new, dramatic sure, but more importantly, it left the knights declaring loudly that they had 'vanquished the monster', and continuing on their way.

    They had apparently forgotten he had perfectly good wings at the time. He'd fly far, far away, over the moats, the outer walls, and the invading army. He'd fly until the distant plains gave way to thorns that tore his wings apart, dragged him down to the ground, and then he'd run, and run, and run, and never look back. Until he found himself in the outside world.

    That would make him a... Grimm in 2e? Or something else?

            Second Chicago NPC writeup.

            Terri Caulfield, Reclusive Surveyor
            Beast Levinquick of the Fire Court
            Mask/Mien: Survivor/Monster

            Description
            The rare occasions that Terri’s human face can be seen tend to take their toll on her presentation; a scattering of premature grey hairs break up her ragged black bob-cut, her eyes are sunken and wide, and what can be seen of her teeth are pitted and chipped within bloody gums. A tattered red shawl hangs on her slight frame over one of various outfits picked from a handful of cheap dress shirts and pairs of worn-down denim pants. Terri is usually barefoot, which makes it hard not to notice she’s missing a toe on either foot.

            She’s more in her element in the Hedge, where her mien need not go through such an awkward translation. Caulfield looks mouselike beneath the Mask, her glass teeth and glass eyes glinting in the patchy neon light that lines her Hedgespun cloak of bloody sawgrass. Despite being smaller than some children she still cuts an intimidating figure — her black pelt fades into the background as the jagged white markings upon it stretch off her ears and tail into the sky. She is seldom empty-handed.

            Background
            If she is honest — and she usually is — Terri thinks the Storm-Serpent Marquess might have taken her out of a misplaced sense of pity. The Levinquick recalls catching glimpses of the Fae during her strict apprenticeship as an electrician in attics and basements. The Huntsman sent to pick her up orchestrated the power outage that was her first job as a journeyman, and the older residents of that building still whisper stories of the freak storm that brought her there and grumble over the fallout from the fumbling repairs her fetch left it with.

            Terri lived for a time on the stormy scrubland of her Keeper’s vast estate, herded with her fellow captives from outpost to fencepost to lonely outpost by ill winds and low-hanging thunderstorms. Many of the changelings in her company were broken by the years of highly regimented drills and harrowing near-miss evacuations, eventually fleeing to the plains to meet whatever fate the Marquess had in store for deserters; the Beast saw some of their twisted remains on her desperate flight through curtains of thunder the night she stole her handler’s key, scattered her comrades, and made her escape in the ensuing chaos to the hills above the advancing thunderheads.

            Caulfield returned to Earth in the same attic the scarecrow of rat skulls and copper wire was left, during the last of many blackouts enabled by the imposter’s substandard fix. It would prove a sign of things to come that she was discovered by one of the rare few mortals who could see through the Mask in the middle of an attempt to sort the matter out; the ensuing panic only ended when she joined the Court of Fire some hours later. After a few months of trying and failing to adjust to active life in the freehold, she largely retreated from society until the early twenty-tens. Her renewed involvement with the Underwood began with her formal claim of the role of Surveyor, citing inspiration from the previous appointee’s grandson and one of the many visiting fae that pass through Chicago.

            Personality
            Terri has gotten moderately far in life on her ability to divert attention away from herself. Despite her burgeoning reputation as an ill omen among the freehold, the Beast is a natural fit for the post of Surveyor, being well-practiced at cold-reading and keeping a conversation going. She does poorly with direct confrontation, being both noise-averse and a terrible liar, but neither of these qualities interfere much with the aura of panic that pervades the Hedge around her and empowers her bond to the Lantern Court.

            Despite her forthright nature and the social requirements of her appointed task, Caulfield is functionally a recluse for much of the year; her brief stint as Crown Princess and her key role in maintaining the Bulwark have instilled the reserve Lamplighter with a firm belief that the best thing she can do for Chicago proper is to keep her visits as short as possible.

            Resident Lore-Hound
            Currently Consuming: Demon: the Descent 1e

                So keep in mind I'm fairly new to Changeling and I'm lacking basically everything from 2.0 since I'm having a hard time finding the resources when people point out stuff like Potent Kith mentioned earlier, so this is mostly pure fluff and doesn't have a dot-by-dot breakdown of the sheet or anything since I don't even know where to begin.

                Name: Toski, the Dream Delver
                Age: Mask is his 30's of a person with dwarfism, bright red hair and green eyes. Like a knee-to-middlethigh-height Weasley.
                Kith: Still working this out, but likely Chiurgeon / Truefriend or some kind of one like a gardener.
                Court: Spring

                Toski is a bit of an unusual one. For one, to changelings he looks like a humanoid squirrel, complete with snout, bushy tail, ears, and fur. But that's not all. He wasn't originally human. Technically, he's a hobgoblin, but mechanically built as a changeling, spawned from a rather unlucky squirrel that happened to wander into an open hedge gate under the roots of a large oak in a city park that had been there since its founding and was the main way the local Fae abducted people. You know those little shreds of left-behind soul from a changeling as they're yanked through the thorns? He ended up finding a piece and, being a dumb squirrel, tried gnawing on it thinking it was food. Since the Wyrd has a sick sense of humor, it rather unpleasantly hot-glued that essence of humanity to him with a dose of glamour, giving him fragmented, but usable memories of humanity and twisting him into his current shape. Once he managed to get back out after a few months of wandering, he took up the name Toski, after Ratatoskr, the great messenger squirrel that darted among the branches of the world tree, delivering messages. In his case, the branches are the dreams of others, delivering warnings of incoming attacks, delivering message of new changeling arrivals, and generally being a very valuable informant for the Spring Court by keeping an eye on the local hedge. Takes his payment as bottled, honest laughter (as opposed to forced, fake laughter. The laugh and the joy that comes with it have to be genuine) and distills it into a healing goblin fruit farm for his motley, since as they say laughter is the best medicine.

                No slouch helping out physically, either. That Spring control of plants as well as a few contracts of the Hedge are very useful for, say providing food or growing gnarled roots almost a foot thick as a wall to block pursuers.

                His spring mantle also manifests as a strong, flowery perfume that seems to follow him, and occasionally flowers will bloom from his fur similar to a fashion accessory. Like one might bloom on his upper chest as if he had a lapel pin. Wears a necklace with viking runes as a kind of reminder and affirmation of his Spring Court allegiances. Possibly a token, but no clue on potential effects:

                Barkanan: symbolizing fertility, growth, sustenance, and healing.
                Ingwaz: Fertilization, the start of something new, actualization of potential.
                Dagaz: Hope, happiness
                Naudhiz: Need, unfulfilled desire
                Jera: Harvest / reward
                and Wunjo: Joy, ecstasy.

                                        This is a idea I was working on for a predessigned character for a one shot of a Changeling game. I ended the character development here, I got inspiration

                                        Name: Isabella
                                        Seeming: Fairest
                                        Kith Idea: The Dark Knight (Female Fairest dual kith Razorhand / Metalflesh). Her razorhand weapon is a sword masked in the form of a sword pendant, and when she invokes her Metalflesh armor, it apears in a suit of dark plate mail.
                                        Concept: former Gentry enforcer, leader of the Dark King's armies
                                        Merits ideas: Fae Mount (Tintaglia), Dual Kith, Potent kith.
                                        Motto: Those who abuse the weak are weak themselves. Abuse me, if you dare!!

                                        There are villains in the throne that need its enforcers. Palpatine got Darth Vader, Queen Bavmorda got General Kael. The Gentry needed an enforcer, a guardsman, a lackey, a general for his armies, and got it in the Dark Knight, an inspiration for his troops, and a terror for his enemies, be it other gentry or his own vassals.

                                        Isabella, bullied as she was in her childhood and adolescence, always wanted to be strong, and imagined herself taking revenge on her abusers. She got imagination, and loved fantastic stories of heroes slashing villains, but with time she fell in love with tragic heroes falling to the dark side for seek of revenge and lack of comprehension from their kind. She began writting tragic fantastic stories of fallen heroes, and she was good at it. So good, that "something" was attracted to her by her stories, most of all for the passion, hatred and pain behind them.

                                        And one day se received a proposal in dreams, a hand lending help, a paternal figure she never had, wispering: "Do you want the power to exact revenge on those who mistreated you? Do you want to make them suffer for what they did to you? Do you want those who refused help you see their big mistake?"

                                        She said "Yes, I want", and the pact it was done.

                                        Isabella remembers more things than she would like to remember in her durance. A King, a paternal figure that teached her to be strong, to be merciless... and he gave her presents (a sword, an armor, a loyal horse), and love. He cared for her. She was a daughter for him, and he was the father she never had.

                                        For him, she leaded armies in bloody battles, killed farmers that only demanded not to starve, suffocated rebelions... She was known as the Dark Knight, not a very original nickname, but one she liked, not for the sound of it, but for the tone it was speaked: with fear.

                                        In a moment of her durance, she realiced that the tale of the "tragic fallen knight seeking revenge on humanity" was not so romantic at all. Something inside her broke, and she became aware of all the bloodshed and misery she brought in the name of her King... no, in the name of herself, of her anger, hatred and frustation. She realized she was used, and worst, she allowed it.

                                        So the repentant Dark Knight seeked redemption and leaded a rebellion... and she lost.

                                        She fled to the Hedge, with her loyal mount, Tintaglia, back to the world she belonged, crying for all she lost, all she allowed and all she became, but swearing to use that power for something good, to redeem herself, if redemption was something possible for her.

                                        The King didn't forget her daughter's treason. Someday she will see her mistake, and she will return to him, repentant. And then she will die at the hands of her substitute, a new Dark Knight replacing the fallen one.

                                                I had a vague for a character, and I'd like some help with fleshing him out.

                                                I've been wanting to play an ascetic wandering occultist who used to be a Loyalist in the role of cultist. His Keeper was a hoarder and dealer of mystical knowledge in the style of Hermaeus Mora from the Elder Scrolls. His Durance probably involved the studying, crafting and organization of Tokens in the form of grimoires and such, which would be used by other changelings to tempt people into coming to the Realm.

                                                His first escape consisted of promising to act as a recruiter of cultists in the mortal world. He would come to regret leading people to Arcadia, but at the same time resent the lack of freedom in pursuing the knowledge and mysteries he desired. I am not sure how he would earn his second Seeming, but I would like it to be Wizened. His Kiths would be Antiquarian + Witchtooth.

                                                Now aware of how the hunger for power can ruin lives, he foregoes a traditional lifestyle in favor of studying the occult all over the Hedge and the Ironside. Occasionally he visits Freeholds to share his expertise and solve mysteries in exchange for minimal goods and services, as well as leads into more mysteries

                                                He is a brave scholar, a patient teacher and a gifted explorer. On the other hand, he still hasn't gotten rid of the common flaws of cultists, so he is excessively curious and not above using his powers to solve problems. He is not ashamed of admiting his former status as a Loyalist, and prefers that other changelings be similarly frank with him. Unlike many cultists, he makes it a point to treat the objects of his studies with objectivity and keep sane, so his Clarity would be high. He'd probably join the Lord Sages Of The Unknown Reaches. He'd also make it a point to interact with normal folks often so as to not be consumed by his fascination with the supernatural.

                                                His Mask would probably be something like Nomad, while his Mien is Scholar.

                                                What do you all think? Is he too much like a typical cultist? What sort of Contracts and Court would best fit him?
