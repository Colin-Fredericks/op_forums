                      In several of the 2 edition books there is the use of the word &quot;zie&quot; and &quot;zir.&quot; What do these words mean they seem to be in places where &quot;she&quot; and &quot;he&quot; would go
                      I'm not sure how versed you are on gender studies and transgender issues, so I'm briefly going to go over some basics and then try to answer your question within that context.

                      In short, in gender studies, gender and sex are two different things, with sex referring to certain anatomical and physiological features and gender referring to culturally defined concepts of masculinity and femininity. Gender is often perceived as binary, "man" or "woman", "he", or "she". But the truth is it's more of a spectrum, with each individual human being having a unique blend of traits that are culturally associated with masculinity or femininity. You've probably heard of transgender people, who identify with a gender other than the one they were assigned at birth. Some such people identify with the binary genders of of "man" and "woman", but others prefer to occupy a space somewhere inbetween those extremes, or even fluctuate where they fit on that spectrum.

                      Now, with all that in mind, it becomes clear that the pronouns "he/him/his" and "she/her/hers" alone are insufficient to describe everyone, as many people don't fit neatly into one of two black-and-white categories. Fortunately, language is ever-evolving, so there have been many attempts to expand the English language's vocabulary with respect to personal pronouns. Zie/zir/zir's is a gender-neutral personal pronoun, typically used by people of nonbinary gender. Another such nonbinary pronoun is hi/hir/hirs. If you see such pronouns used to refer to a character in a Chronicles of Darkness book, you can safely assume that character is transgender and outside the gender binary.
