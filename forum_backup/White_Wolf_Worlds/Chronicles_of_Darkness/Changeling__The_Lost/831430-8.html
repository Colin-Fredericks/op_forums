                                            Finally read through all the bits that weren't previously spoiled, though my allergies + beta language are making actual comprehension hard.

                                            Hostile Oaths make me sad. I wish it wasn't so completely absolute. I don't care how hard it is: there's got to be a way of breaking that other than zeroing at least one participant's Wyrd. I get that, mechanically, two reconciled changelings could simply not mess with each other, but "undying enmity" still makes me sad. I can't see a way to do it satisfactorily though. I'm going to noodle on that.

                                            I like the general sense I'm getting from the Dreamwalking stuff, but... there's an implication in the text that a Changeling who infiltrates a Dream Bastion without a pre-established agreement isn't committing an act of violation. They totally are. Parleying with someone's subconscious isn't consensual. I'd like it made more clear that that's an unethical thing to do, even if many Changelings nevertheless have no qualms about doing it. It's certainly more ethical than what Huntsmen and Gentry do, but it's in the middle of the spectrum, not on the saintly end of it.

                                            I'm also rather sad that Changelings who have a restful sleep disappear from their beds at night. It's a profound gulf in intimacy there, and it is filled with melancholy. Not to mention how creepy it is if you have casual one-night stands with mortals and they wake up in the middle of the night, conclude you've taken off, except all your stuff is still there... which... makes me wonder how many Changelings dare to sleep naked.

                                            Lastly, trying to puzzle out the cosmology of dreams here. There seems to be an unnamed dreamspace that acts as a conduit of sorts between the mortal world and the Hedge (so a dream is less a "gateway" and more a tunnel?). A mortal dreamer basically opens up the conduit from one end, and the Hedge naturally creates the other endpoint in return. A Changeling who dreams is basically digging, so there's no "other end" and waking up means just walking back up the hole. Bastions, in this sense, actually kinda feel like scars created in response to the "wounds" where a dream is like a needle that pulls some Hedgestuff out. And Hedge Gates are basically bypasses around the dreamspace that blow it so wide open there's no Bastion formed in response. Am I understanding what's going on here correctly? I feel like I am not.

                                            I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".

                                                About Clarity Triggers...

                                                Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                Last paragraphs of the "Crafting Triggers" and "Triggers in Play" sections.
                                                Oh, you mean this?

                                                Triggers should ideally be simultaneously specific and broad. If your character lost his husband in the abduction, that husband could be his second trigger. However, the very concept of marriage might be the trigger’s broader expression. So, while confronting his lost love could trigger a breaking point, so could being invited to a wedding, or passing by bachelorette party on his way to the club.

                                                Maybe it's me not being Native Speaker, but could not be refrain more directly in final text?

                                                Now it sounds like the Trigger could be ( not is in all variant ) as:

                                                A. Confronting lost love,
                                                OR
                                                B. Being invited to a wedding
                                                OR
                                                C. Passing by bachelorette party.

                                                It's just reads like it must be chosen like A, B or C and only one of it.

                                                When dealing with the general application of a trigger, the player should receive full authority on when it does or does not apply. However, if a character faces the specific trigger (in our example, if he confronts his former husband), that’s a definite breaking point.

                                                This rule should be hashed out in to "Crafting Triggers" section, it clearly points that Trigger should be both Common and Specific - it only lost in "examples" part of text.

                                                I would also encourage to mark Triggers as something like "Lost Love - Confronting the husband" on the char sheets, to make situation a more clear on both players and ST sides.
