                                                    Based on our initial character creation efforts, here is the feedback we've got on some of the kiths that we've been considering. Most of them are quite excellent, and we wouldn't change a thing about them. We did have a couple thoughts on some of them, though.

                                                    Brewers have always been popular for our games. I was disappointed to see that they've lost their resistance to poisons, though. It seems like that would still be fitting for them, given that the rest of their Blessing requires them to spend Glamour.

                                                    I could see Coyotes being able to spend a point of Glamour to add Wyrd to Subterfuge rolls made to lie or inspire trust.

                                                    Since Gremlins are treated as pranksters and jesters rather than merely destroyers, it would be nice if they didn't require a complex machine to be used in order to use their Blessing. After all, a jester making a mockery of someone for tripping over their own two feet would be quite appropriate in one of the Keeper's lands that lacked complicated machinery. Even if the object was still required, perhaps it could be any object rather than a complex one, such as a walking stick that breaks at the worst moment. At the very least, it would be nice to see their Blessing being able to be used more than once per scene. A bonus to rolls attempting to humiliate others could also take care of the jester angle in the absence of machinery.

                                                    Playmates seem like they could use a little bit of a boost, given that the entirety of their Blessing is reliant upon them engaging in teamwork efforts. I could see giving them some sort of benefit to either Socialize or Empathy rolls, giving them an easier time finding companions.

                                                    Does the Render's ability to ignore armor function only against material armor, or would it work just as well against armor created by magical effects?

                                                    Are the Thussers able to choose some people to be immune to their performance? If they're not, their Blessing's mechanics don't seem to fit with the flavor text. The implication I'd gotten from it was that they could distract with their performance while either the changeling or someone connected to them stole from the entranced audience, but the mechanics seem to indicate that nobody could act at all unless they first managed to resist the effects.

                                                    Airtouched and Lurkgliders both have Blessings that protect them from falls until terminal velocity. At that point, do they suffer the same damage anyone else would suffer, or does their falling damage begin like they were a normal mortal suffering a fall starting at that point?

                                                    Jason Ross Inczauskis, Freelance Writer

                                                        I have been absolutely delighted at the dreaming systems. They have all been excellent, and I can't wait to see the Merits that relate to them.

                                                        The only thing that stands out as odd to me is that a changeling is unable to dream without vanishing from the physical world, and that to maintain their presence there they must hide their supernatural nature at the cost of their Willpower. I think that I would prefer it if choosing the Gate of Ivory resulted in them having shallow dreams that function as ordinary dreams like a mortal would experience. They would still gain their Willpower for slumbering, but while doing so, they would be just as vulnerable as mortals in their dreams to those that would seek to meddle with them. This would still give them a good reason to choose the Gate of Horn, but it would make the Gate of Ivory a valid option for changelings that wished to maintain the illusion of normalcy without it proving to be untenable for those that did return to their mortal lives.

                                                        Are Hedgespun items permanent creations, capable of being taken out of the Hedge?

                                                        The Succubi are delightfully insidious, and interesting problems that really require helpful changelings to solve. I assume that the damage that they deal after the Willpower is gone takes the form of extreme fatigue rather than actual wounds. Do their victims start to realize something is wrong, or do these Wish Fulfillment dreams keep them coming back for more even though they haven't gained the Willpower from it?
