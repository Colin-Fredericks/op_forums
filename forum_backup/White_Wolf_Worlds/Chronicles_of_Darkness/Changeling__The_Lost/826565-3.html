                Originally posted by ShadowKnight1224 View Post
                It also casts the Fairest in another light, since now their Breaking Point can be framed as less of a leader's burden and more about the Fairest recognising that he can't hold himself accountable for other people's actions, even if they were performed under his guidance/leadership. It helps the Fairest understand that he's not a solipsistic, Gentry-like creature that believes all consequences stem from his actions alone. He acknowledges that people have agency as well, and that they make their own choices even when under someone else's guidance.

                EDIT: Holy shit the Beasts. The Beasts now can gain Clarity from "cages" and "restraints." The Beast can understand that not every home is a prison, that not every binding is a chain, and that tying yourself to another person doesn't mean being their slave or their pet. That is so awesome.
                I agree, I love the new light this casts on all of the Seemings. Like Wizened, their curse of Perfectionism makes failing at their craft into a Breaking Point. That failure can either lead them into deeper depression, or they can learn and grow from it, realizing that it's okay to fail, and the fact that they can still fail is part of what makes them human.

                But there's an important thing to remember here as well. It's not just succeeding at the Breaking Point roll that gives you Clarity back. All you get from that is the Acuity Condition. The Beast doesn't just realize that not all captivity is bad and feel immediately better. That realization is only the first step, and while it gives them a temporary moment of sharpened lucidity, that feeling will fade if they don't do anything more with it. If they want to heal, they have to resolve the Condition. They have to take what they've learned from the Breaking Point and grow from it as well.

                Just facing your fears and surviving isn't enough. It can be empowering, no doubt, but you've got to make something of that power. You've got to use it to better yourself. And you've got to do it while the experience is still fresh in your mind.

                                    So, here's another thing that I really like about these rules. It's not quite as much of a pivotal piece of Changeling's thesis as the method of regaining Clarity, but it's a nice touch that I really appreciate.

                                    Most Chronicles of Darkness characters we've seen so far (I think everyone except vampires) roll Resolve + Composure for Breaking Points. Changelings roll Wits + Composure. Where other Integrity stats use the default self-control dice pool to determine if a character slips, Clarity uses the default perception dice pool. It's a little thing, but I think it's kind of subtlety brilliant. It's an understated comment about what exactly Clarity is about. It's not about sanity, it's not about morality, it's not about keeping control of yourself. It's about being able to see the world for what it is. I think that's kind of touching.

Any time she could spend Willpower to add to a Contested roll, or subtract from a Resisted roll, her Clarity can replace the normal +3 or -2 it’d confer. This does not protect her from fae magic, curiously, but other forms of supernatural power. Roll Clarity as a dice pool. The Storyteller may make this roll on the character's behalf as a reflexive ability. At Clarity 4 or less, this requires a Willpower point and an instant action. It can no longer be done reflexively. If the magic is intended to hide, obfuscate, conceal, disguise, or confuse, instead add her Clarity to her dice pool in a Clash of Wills (see p. XX)
