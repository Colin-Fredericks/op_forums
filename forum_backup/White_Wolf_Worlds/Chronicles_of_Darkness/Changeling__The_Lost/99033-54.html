            Session CXVII

            Lorelei’s Diary

            Habbibiddy.

            Ahem. So the good news is that I actually did manage to get another couple of hours of sleep following my last journal entry; nuzzling up to Maria always calms me down and makes me feel at peace (and Julia and Marguerite’s presence didn’t hurt either). The four of us tromped down to Sex Breakfast a few hours later, groggy and fuzzy but generally fairly happy.

            I took up the frying pan this time and made pancakes for most of our little extended family (though Marguerite requested French Toast, and I – accommodating pushover that I am – obliged her). I may not be a top quality chef, but I flatter myself that I can flip a flapjack as well as many a short-order cook at least, and things turned out pretty well. Julia made herself an omelet but I think that was mostly so she could spout out “omelet du fromage!” a few more times. And no, in case you’re wondering, people still haven’t explained to me why they think that’s so funny.

            Talk turned to what we were going to do for the day. I had no particular plans myself, but figured something would probably come up if I sat and listened to other people’s plans for long enough. Marguerite announced that she was going to spend the afternoon rehearsing, as she intended to return to the stage that evening. D had some nebulous plans to spend the afternoon with T.J., but wouldn’t tell me what that was all about (frankly I was afraid to ask). Trinh said that she was going to spend some time apartment hunting… a daunting task, since she wanted to get a nice place that wasn’t going to rope her into an extended lease since she might be merging with her Fetch soon.

            We talked about that for a little while too… things are going well between the two of them, and they think they’re almost ready to merge. They just need to work out a few things. As strange as it seems, the vegan thing is really one of the major roadblocks coming between them… the Fetch wants to exact a promise from Trinh that she won’t ‘taint’ their shared body by eating meat after they’ve merged. Trinh is reluctant to make that promise, which is keeping the two of them from arriving at the sort of harmonious trusting relationship that makes the merging process possible. I’m sure they’ll work something out eventually; it’s just going to take a little time.

            Calamity initially stated that she was going to do some digging on Luther Henderson, the brave soul who tried to tackle her in the middle of that rush hour fiasco a few days ago. It was eventually suggested that she might want to check out his dreams first, as that would be a good way to ascertain his true character without possibly provoking another fracas. Calamity allowed as how that was a good suggestion, and put her espionage plans on hold for the time being.

            Missy expressed an interest in learning more about working with dreams, and Calamity generously offered to teach her a bit when she was ready. Jane commented softly to me that whenever Missy starts to feel overwhelmed by reality, the two of them have taken to retreating into dreams for a while. That sort of environment is a little more familiar to Missy, and helps to calm her down until she can face the day again. That sounds like a fine idea to me, as long as Missy doesn’t become too dependent on it… but it doesn’t sound like that’s what’s happening. She just regards dreams as a safe place to fall back to until she can gather her wits.

            So Missy and Jane mentioned that they’d like to spend some time out at the pier, and asked if I’d like to come along. Well that sounded like a fine way to spend a free afternoon, so of course I said yes. Julia had been planning to paint on the roof, but changed her mind when she heard us talking, and asked if she could come along and bring her sketchbook. Well, that pretty much KOed my idea of taking the ‘vette, but that was okay. When Calamity and Becky decided to join us as well, we opted to take the van anyway.

            And yes, I asked Jacob and Luna if they wanted to come with us, and as usual, was turned down. I know that Luna doesn’t like crowds, and I understand it on an intellectual level… but it’s so far from my own personality that I have a hard time wrapping my brain around it. I can’t really sympathize with the idea that someone would rather spend their time cooped up in relative isolation when they could be out enjoying a beautiful day with their friends. Add to that the fact that Luna’s seemed a little down recently, and you can understand my concern. I mean, she’s always very reserved compared to the rest of us, but I think something’s been bothering her lately, and I wish she’d talk with me about it. That’s supposed to be my role in this motley, isn’t it? To help support the others emotionally? Well, I guess Luna’s got Jacob for that, but still…

            Nope, not gonna get mopey about that afternoon. I had a wonderful time at the pier, and I think the others did too. Julia quickly clambered up on top of one of the fishing shacks and started sketching the crowds. Becky and Calamity found a relatively quiet corner and quickly got cutesy and lovey-dovey, which was adorable. I hung out with Missy and Jane, the three of us strutting our stuff and making everyone aware that we were the hottest girls there. And yeah, we got hit on a lot. And yeah, we ate it up.

            “No,” I told one guy with a grin, “those two are engaged. I’m married to a different hot chick. But don’t worry, we’re all very… open.” Once he’d picked his jaw up off the planks, I blew him a kiss and handed him a Dream House business card. What can I say? There’s no harm in doing a little advertising while I’m having fun, right?

            About an hour into our stay I got a text. When I checked my phone I was surprised to see that it was from Olivia Gold, the Day Court’s ambassador to our Freehold. “I need to speak with you,” the text said, “when would be a good time?” I sent back that I was out of the office at the moment but would be back about 4:00, and she acknowledged that she would see me then. So I had to inform the others that our fun afternoon had acquired a deadline, but everyone took the news with good grace.

            Olivia was at the Dream House when we arrived. She said that she wanted to talk to me, but didn’t object to the motley being present. “I know that I’m not a member of the Spring Court,” she began formally, “and I know that you’re not obliged to do anything for me. But I had a good working relationship with Princess Jolie… she sort of looked out for the affairs of Day Courtiers in the Freehold, and I thought I would see where we stood with this matter.”

            I nodded thoughtfully. “I’ll help you if I can, Olivia, as long as it doesn’t compromise the well-being of my own courtiers,” I said.

            Olivia sighed. “Thank you. Actually, I thought you’d want to hear about this anyway, considering your history. It’s Rowan. Somehow, word has gotten out that she’s involved with a Night Courtier, and… well, people aren’t taking it well. There’s been some minor vandalism at her house. Nothing too severe so far, but I’m afraid it might escalate.”

            I pursed my lips. “What’s been done? And who’s responsible?”

            Olivia shrugged. “Nothing has been permanently damaged. There’s been some spray paint, both on her house and her car. As for who… I don’t know. My first impulse is to say it’s Night Courtiers from L.A., but I’d be lying if I claimed that there aren’t certain members of the Day Court who are capable of this as well.”

            I sighed in turn. “Thank you for bringing this to my attention, Olivia. Rowan and Neon are both friends, and neither one of them deserves to be treated this way. I’ll think it over and do what I can to help them.”

            Olivia nodded and thanked me. “One more thing,” she said before she left. “I know you’re close to her, but I hope you’ll consider the likelihood that Insincere Marguerite is the one who spread the news that the two of them were involved.”

            D surged forward at that, but I caught her shoulder in a gentle but firm grip. “I doubt that very much,” I said rather coldly. “She’s on friendly terms with both of them, and I doubt she’d want to see them hurt or bothered in this way. But I’ll certainly bring this matter to her attention.”

            After Olivia had vacated the premises, I waited for a break in Marguerite’s rehearsal and gave her the news. As I expected, she was as surprised and chagrinned by the situation as I had been. She offered her opinion that the vandals were most likely Night Courtiers, and were probably not acting at the behest of their monarch. “They see it as the betrayal,” she explained, elaborating on the antipathy between the Day and Night courts, “and they are afraid to lash out at Neon. She is a member of the Unseelie Court motley, and they are all afraid of Corvus and Corbin. Particularly Corbin. Rowan is meek; she is an easier target.”

            I sighed. “I should probably talk with Rowan and Neon tonight,” I said. “I’ll give Neon a call and see if they can drop by the show this evening.”

            D pulled me aside for a moment. “I got an idea, babe,” she said quietly. “See, I can probably convince T.J. to stake out Rowan’s place and keep an eye out for these vandals. He’d be happy to do it. He can knock a few heads, maybe break a few bones, and make sure they know not to pull that shit in our Freehold again.”

            I nodded. “I’ll consider it,” I said after a moment. “But if we go ahead with that plan, I don’t want anybody killed… or even too badly hurt. The last thing I want to do is provoke a conflict with our allies in L.A.”

            She nodded. “T.J. knows when to pull his punches. He can make someone wish they were dead without getting close to actually killing them.”

            So I had that to think about. I actually called Brightstar first to see what she knew about the situation. She was happy to hear from me as always, of course, and had gleaned quite a lot from Neon. Her roommate was at work when I called, but Brightstar was sure she’d be happy to hear from me, and would be happy to drop by the club that evening if it was possible. Brightstar herself is going to stop by tonight, so I’ve got that to look forward to.

            I called Neon next. I told her what I’d found out about their situation and asked if the two of them could drop by the club that night. She tentatively agreed, stating that she’d text me or something if their plans changed. I also warned her that T.J. might be hanging around Rowan’s place that evening as a sort of deterrent, and asked her to let Rowan know so that she wouldn’t get too freaked out by his presence. The last time he was hanging out around her house uninvited he nearly scared the daylights out of her.

            So yeah, I gave D the go-ahead to put T.J. on the job. I’m hoping that by setting an example with the vandals, we’ll send the message that messing with Rowan is just as dangerous as messing with Neon (though I doubt T.J. will take a bite out of anyone’s face… yeah, Neon gave me a few more details about the Corbin anecdote). Hopefully he’ll send a few of the scumbags running back to L.A. scared shitless and maybe a little battered, and that’ll convince the rest of them that fucking with members of my Freehold is a bad idea.

            Jesus, would you listen to me? What the hell is happening to me lately? Is it simply the fact that I’m now responsible for the well-being of others that’s causing me to make these violent decisions? Or is it the old adage about power corrupting already at work? I mean, I don’t want to hurt these people… do I? I just want them to stop screwing with my friends and constituents. But… if this situation escalates, someone could get really hurt; maybe even killed. I can’t allow that. I have to nip this in the bud, and that means I can’t afford to use soft options. I have to act decisively.

            Maybe I should have asked the other monarchs for their advice before making this decision… but I can’t just go running to John every time a crisis comes my way. I’ll never earn the Freehold’s respect that way. No, in the long run I think it’s better to act on my own and make a mistake than to let myself be led by the other monarchs. But Jesus… I just asked an angry pig-ogre to break bones for me. Not my finest hour.

            Anyway, the show must go on, and last night was no exception. I’d spoken with Missy and Jane several times about performing for us, and they both seem to like the idea… though there are certain caveats. They’re happy to back me up on the stage for now, but neither is comfortable performing solo for the audience yet… it’s too close to what the Empress required of us back in Arcadia. I completely understand that, and I’m more than willing to let them adjust to the idea at their own paces.

            The three of us got up on stage together during the early part of the show for a little a capella vocal harmony with an old favorite. “Afternoon Delight” by Starland Vocal Band is a lot of fun to sing with those two, and the three of us used to spontaneously launch into it all the time back when we were in high school. It was sort of a vicarious thrill… the lyrics are very sexually suggestive, but the tune is so jaunty and innocent sounding that a lot of people don’t catch the meaning behind the words at first.

            I didn’t mention it to Missy and Jane. Maybe they’ve forgotten about it. Maybe they’re getting over their trauma. Who knows? But that was the song the three of us were singing just before we were Taken. That was the song that drew the Stranger’s attention to us. You’d never know it to look at the two of them, though… they were obviously having a lot of fun with me out there. Maybe the happy memories of our high school days overpowered the song’s negative connotations.

            I did the queen thing for a while and wandered the crowd talking with people and making myself available to hear their opinions and complaints. For a wonder, most people seemed to just be there to enjoy the show, and I only had to “don the crown” for a couple of guests.

            First on that list, obviously, were Rowan and Neon. They both thanked me for doing what I can to help them even though they’re not courtiers, though Rowan expressed chagrin over the fact that Olivia had gotten me involved in her troubles at all; she’d hoped to spare me that stress.

            “Sweetie,” I said seriously, taking her hand, “you may not be a member of my Court, but you are and always will be a dear friend. Both of you are. Part of the reason I took up the Crown was to serve the people of this Freehold, so I’m glad Olivia came to me when she did. If I can fulfill my duties as a monarch and help a friend at the same time, then I’m happy to do what I can.”

            The three of us chatted for a bit after that, and I got a few more details about their situation… most of which seem inconsequential now. Rowan was quick to tell me that nobody had actually hurt or threatened her, they were just defacing her property. Neon reiterated with a sigh that the culprits were probably Night Courtiers from L.A. who disapproved of their relationship.

            “The conflict between Day and Night is usually a good thing,” she said as the conversation moved on. “The rapidly shifting leadership and attitudes of the courts make it almost impossible for the Gentry to understand us. They can’t plan an effective strategy to take us down because a plan that works against one Court almost certainly won’t against the other one, and the balance of power goes back and forth so quickly. So it pays for us to be opposed… it’s just irritating when it targets someone I care about.”

            I suppose that makes sense, though it seems to me that the Seasonal Court model of government works well enough with far less problems. But it didn’t seem politic to state it that baldly.

            Before moving on, I mentioned that Brightstar would be spending the following evening with me, and suggested – on T.J.’s recommendation, actually – that Rowan might want to stay with Neon that night. That way, any retaliation the Night Court might plan against T.J.’s vigilante activities wouldn’t land on their heads. They agreed that this was a good plan and decided to go with it.

            I’d noticed one guest sitting by himself in a shadowy corner… a corner that wasn’t usually shadowy, in fact. It’s been a long time since we’ve seen Vic Twilight in our little cabaret, and knowing what I do about his recent activities, I thought it would be best to welcome him back personally.

            “Your Majesty,” he said softly as I pulled up a chair and sat beside him.

            “Mr. Twilight,” I acknowledged.

            “Just thought I’d stop by and pay my respects,” he said politely.

            “I appreciate that,” I said, flashing him a grin. “And I’d just like to say, knowing what I now know, that I appreciate all of your efforts on behalf of the Freehold. I know you’re putting yourself at great risk on a daily basis to make sure that the rest of us remain safe… and you’re doing it without much in the way of recognition.”

            He flashed me a toothy smile. “Just doing my job, ma’am,” he said. “You got any special requests the next time I go out?”

            I shook my head. “I trust that you know your business far better than I ever could Mr. Twilight,” I replied. “If that changes, though, I’ll make sure to let you know. So how are things going on that front?”

            He shrugged. “Not much to report. Only thing I’ve noticed is that there’s a new party drug on the street. Not sure what it is, but when a mortal takes it, they become like a fountain of glamour for a few hours, then completely dry up for a few days.”

            Interesting. Is it possible that the Hollywood Courts are trying to counteract the effects of Gunnar Friday’s starvation tactics? Or maybe they want to distribute this drug in a particular location to create a beacon of glamour… something they could use to lure the Grand Maw where they want it to go after they unleash it. Neither is a particularly comforting possibility.

            “Friends of yours?” Vic asked after a moment, indicating Missy and Jane with a nod of his head.

            “Yeah,” I said. “You might recall that my motley went back into Arcadia last year. They’re the reason why. We brought them back with us.”

            “I see,” he said. And then, after a somewhat awkward pause, “may not be my place to say this but… you be good to Oleander, okay? That lady is one of the only people in the Freehold I feel like I can talk to, and she’s been so happy since the two of you got together… she deserves something like that. She was terrified the whole time you were gone. I never seen her like that before.”

            “I know,” I said. “I felt horrible putting her through that. And I promise that I will be good to her. You’re right… she’s special, and she deserves the best I can give her.”

            I caught Jacob’s attention at the bar and gave him a pre-arranged hand signal. He nodded, and I turned back to Vic. “Your next drink is on me, Mr. Twilight,” I said. “Thank you again for your service, and please come back any time.”

            Though I hobnobbed a bit more, I found myself gravitating toward the girlfriend table, where D and Gloria were making a spectacle of themselves. They were apparently in a somewhat giggly and playful mood, and each was attempting to drop an ice cube down the shirt of the other. It was amusing to watch, but was perhaps not the most dignified way they could have comported themselves in our place of business (and the current seat of the Spring Court).

            I had considered trying to rope D into bed with me, Missy and Jane last night… it’s something I really want to do at some point, and everyone seems open to the possibility. But looking at the two of them, I could tell that D and Gloria were planning to be together (with or without me), and I just couldn’t bring myself to sabotage their evening. Plus, dammit, I wanted in on that action.

            “Okay,” I said once I’d finally managed to get their attention. “Here’s what we’re going to do, ladies. I’ll make a wager with you. If I win, you two have to act your age and conduct yourselves like grown women for the rest of the evening.”

            D nodded suspiciously. “And if I win?” she asked.

            I shrugged. “I’ll do anything the two of you want for the rest of the night. Anything,” I reiterated unnecessarily.

            D’s eyes lit up. “Okay, I’m in.”

            “Me too,” Gloria piped up.

            I put my elbow on the table and extended my hand in the classic arm wrestling pose. “Let’s rock,” I said with a twinkle and a wry grin.

            Of course, my wife can casually pick up and toss a Pontiac, so the result of our contest was a foregone conclusion… as I’d intended all along. Those two ladies are… very strong. I mean, I’ve known that D is a powerhouse for years, and I knew intellectually that Gloria’s Summer Court contracts make her stronger than she appears, but… wow. They put me through my paces last night, and I couldn’t be happier.

            The usual suspects went to therapy this morning, so Gloria and I were alone in the bed when she started gently shaking me to let me know that breakfast was ready. I think I responded to her hungry entreaties with something witty like “nuh-uh. Legs don’t work no more.” She rolled her eyes in mock exasperation, called me a big baby, made me put on a long shirt, and then carried me downstairs while I leaned into her shoulder in a stupor.

            D got a text while we were eating and started chuckling. Apparently T.J. had met with some success while staking out Rowan’s home. A couple of guys pulled up and prepared to continue their campaign of vandalism. T.J. punched the hood of their car, driving his fist into the engine block and effectively scrapping the vehicle. He then told the two perpetrators to decide which of them would live and which would die. He didn’t actually harm either of them as far as I know; they ran away and he didn’t bother to chase them. He continued to watch the house for the rest of the night in case they came back for a little retribution. He doesn’t think that’s likely, but wants to be thorough just in case. He did ask that someone spell him in that duty for an hour or two so that he could take care of a few necessities. D and Calamity opted to take care of that duty.

            So here I sit, glad that the Rowan situation seems to have been resolved so quickly and painlessly, and looking forward to another energetic evening with my lover from L.A. And in the back of my mind, with every day that passes, I become just a little bit more terrified of the fact that I’ll be attending college classes starting next week.

            Lorelei de la Rocha


            Jacob’s Journal

            Today was the day for questions that I don’t have the answers to, but that will come later. The day started like usual with Calamity going to train with White Maria early this morning while the rest of us slept. When we did get up we made breakfast like usual. Lori cooked pancakes for everyone except Insincere Marguerite. She made French toast for her. I made coffee like usual. The conversation is what made breakfast interesting. I suppose it always does, but today we touched on some topics that we don’t usually visit. Someone asked Marguerite why she doesn’t sleep in a bed very often, and why she sleeps in the vents when she doesn’t. Insincere Marguerite replied that after having had a concrete slab dropped on her chest and her apartment set on fire while she slept she’s entitled to be a little wary about sleeping somewhere so vulnerable as a bed. I think the rest of us had to concede that. It would be a shitty way to wake up.

            After we ate we talked about what we were going to do for the day. Insincere Marguerite said that she was going to spend the afternoon rehearsing since she hasn’t performed in a few days. Trinh said that she was going to look for apartments online. She wanted to find a place that wouldn’t lock her into a long term lease. The others ended up deciding on going to the Pier to hang out. Lori asked if Luna and I would like to join them but we declined. Luna is not a big fan of crowds so she tends to not like the pier so much. I can respect that. I am not great with crowds either. The Lady’s realm was not the most crowded place so I only ever had to deal with a handful of people at a time. Even in Her court there were only ever a few people. Most of her subjects were just plain terrified of her. The rest of us were wary of course, but I didn’t truly fear her until after I betrayed her. That was the first time that her anger was truly focused on me.

            Before everyone left though there were a couple other things brought up. Calamity said that she was interested in checking out that guy that attacked her during the highway incident. He seemed a pretty selfless type, putting himself in danger like that to stop Calamity when she couldn’t stop herself. The rest of us talked her into checking on him in his dream before actually meeting him in person. She took his driver’s license the other day when he was unconscious so she has his picture. She can go into his dreams any time. After some consideration she agreed.

            Trinh also had some news. Apparently she and her fetch are close to an understanding that will allow them to merge into one person soon. They just need to work out a couple of details. Like whether or not the merged them would eat meat or not. Her fetch wanted her to promise that she wouldn’t eat any meat at all after they merged, but Trinh was a little hesitant to make that promise. Things like that tend to be binding for us, so it would be a big deal if she promised not to eat meat and then went back on that later. I guess we’ll see how they get that worked out in time.

            After everyone left Luna confided in me that she had thought about merging with her fetch, but there are obvious problems. One of the biggest among them is the fact that if they merge they will be in Luna’s body, which doesn’t look anything like her fetch’s body. He is a “he” for one thing. And he would be missed. He has a life. He has a career as a stand-up comedian. Not to mention his mother would miss him. And Luna can’t really get involved in his life as a friend. She might slip up and call their mother “mom” by accident. Plus he tends to get fixated on Luna, which might cause problems. Especially if he doesn’t know what their true relationship is. After our serious chat we just sat down to watch some Netflix. It was a light, fun afternoon.

            Later in the afternoon, around 4:00pm Lori and the others got back from the Pier and met Olivia Gold, who showed up right before they did. It seems that someone has taken an issue with Rowan and Neon dating and has decided to vandalize the selkie’s house and car letting their displeasure be known. Olivia had an understanding with Jolie about Day Court issues and thought that Lori might take up where her predecessor left off. Olivia thought that it might be Night courtiers from L.A but didn’t rule out the possibility that it could be Day courtiers doing it. After Olivia left Lori talked to Marguerite about the issue and she agreed that it was probably Night courtiers that did the vandalizing. After that Lori called both Neon and Rowan to see if they could come by the club tonight and talk about the situation. She also asked De la Rocha to talk to T.J. about defending Rowan’s house tonight to prevent anyone else from doing any damage to her house, however minor.

            The show is where all the conversations happened and where the long time absentees showed up. We haven’t seen Vik in a long time, not since he was sent to Hollywood to deal with problems there. But he was at the show tonight. Lori went over to talk to him. At one point during the conversation she made a signal to me that his next drink should be on the house. I acknowledged that and made sure that we had the spirits that he likes. Lori also talked to Neon and Rowan about what has been going on lately. I’m assuming that she let Rowan know about T.J.’s presence at her house and what he was doing there. It would be remiss of Lori to leave that bit off. I’m not sure what else was talked about, but I’m assuming they got all the details worked out.

            Calamity made her rounds as well. She stopped by to talk to Jolie and Desiree who were sitting together. Again, I’m not sure what was said, but Desiree at least seemed to be in a better mood than before Calamity arrived. Perhaps she got to purge some negative emotions on her friendly sounding board.
