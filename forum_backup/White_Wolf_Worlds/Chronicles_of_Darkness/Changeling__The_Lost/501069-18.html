    Originally posted by A_Newfie View Post
    The way you describe them in how unique they should be they feel more like they should be an entitlement consisting only of fairest rather than the entire fairest seeming!
    With the risk of repeating myself, it does sound cool for a specific scenario but not so much for an entire seeming.
    You know, this exact argument came up in the Darkling thread too. The idea was that while the lost loyalty angle was cool for a specific character, it was too narrow for the whole seeming and would work better as an Entitlement. I think this comes from taking a writeup that was meant to depict one example of a character of the Seeming and assuming it's meant to be representative of the entire Seeming.

    Take another look at their Blessing. All Fairest regain Clarity from taking charge when they don't actually have a fully-formed plan. Their sense of agency is reinforced by the knowledge that people will follow them, even if they're not actually prepared to lead. What this tells me is tha they're not actually good leaders at all, they just like the feeling of being in a leadership position, especially when they're not a good fit for it. I don't know about you, but to me that speaks volumes about their vanity.

    Now their curse. All Fairest experience a Breaking Point when their ill-conceived plans (unsurprisingly) go awry. I'm not sure why people asaume this means they have to be good guys. I mean, I guess they could be hitting that Breaking Point because they feel guilty about hurting their followers. But I read it as they're breaking because the plan failed horribly. They don't have to care about the people who got hurt, it's just that the fact that people got hurt is pretty undeniable proof that their plan was shitty, and they're not the perfect leader they think they should be. They're reminded "oh yeah, I'm not a great leader. I'm just a useless waste of a life, just like I've always been, and people only look up to because they don't know any better."

    The background and durance are just one typical example of the type of situations that might lead one to become a Fairest.

        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post


        When I read the part about the Fate placing them in the position of leadership, I thought about stories regarding destiny. Remember Alistaire from Dragon Age? He didn't want to be king at all, like, adamantly opposed to the idea. But just because he was the King's bastard and his only remaining son, his blood mattered more than his wishes. The people who raised him and fought alongside him made the choice for him, that he was going to be king, even though he kept protesting it. That's how I view the idea of a fairest always finding themselves the leader of a situation, even if they don't want to be.
        He's a great example, particularly when you factor in the few directions his story can take.
