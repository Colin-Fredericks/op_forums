            It's fairly easy to make capture impossible or not worth it.
            But such circumstances are quite rare in RPG's at least in my experience barring either massive blunders or ST's plot points that require it to happen. Generally though most players will try to avoid captivity whether or not they have a Curse pertaining to it.

            The Beast Curse's breaking point is limited to once a Story.
            Nice catch I somehow missed that. But such a limitation seems lacking in the Fairest Curse which ironically is a lot more likely to happen often than being captured is. Even the Fairest Curse becomes quite meaningless to me, and most of my issues are about it are resolved, if it's once a story as well.

            At the pace we play once a story tends to be mean a rather impactless thing anyway since our Aberrant Chronicle has ran for over 10 years of real time and it could be divided into around 3 stories. Naturally the game has had some hiatuses as well, hence the story divides, but still anything applicable only once per story is likely to be quite meaningless on our table. Similarly our long running Exalted game, that lasted 2-3 years I think, could have been about 3 Stories if I'm being generous.

            Definitely a thing where YMMV.

            I have trouble imagining a session where a Fairest is making multiple leadership calls that would qualify for the Curse even in theory. That seems to be a game moving at much faster pace - and much higher octane - than the default the books go to.
            And I have trouble seeing a session where it wouldn't. This is even more true of high octane action scenes but it is a problem in normal scenes and situations as well, see my real life example in a previous post.

            As far as my comment goes, it still merits question-just how often does social, mental, or emotional harm occur to your characters in game play?
            Do note that it isn't limited to harm that happens to your characters quite on the contraire it is about what happens to anyone who might befall upon some harm because of your decision or counsel. Such things can and do happen fairly often in RL let alone in an RPG.

            The Fairest Curse punishes making mistakes. Yes, mistakes are inevitable. But that standard isn't being applied to the Beasts.
            If only that were true. Fairest Curse does not take into account acceptable collateral damage caused by a plan, MachineIV even said he liked that (the fact that it doesn't matter if the plan succeeds if someone got hurt you are struck by it) about the Curse. So it is not about making mistakes but for example in a combat situation it's about pure luck since that is the only way you are, even with a perfect plan, going to avoid any and all harm befalling your side. Similar can be said of plans in a non-combat situation where the harm is likelier to manifest as hurt feelings, which count for the Curse. So if it was about mistakes, that it struck only when your plan failed (which would be appropriate when looking at many villains, and some heroes, reactions to their plans failing), instead of being tied to any harm befalling anyone due to your leadership I would be much less critical of it.

            Again for the Beast part. I have been in countless games in over two decades of playing and I still can probably count the number of times I have been captured using my fingers. Times when plans have caused harm to someone, regardless of whether they fail or succeed, innumerable. The two cannot be compared really. But like said there is another major difference between the Curses, unless it is a mistake is the difference of frequency. Like you pointed out the Beast Curse clearly says it only applies once per story while the Darklings and the Fairest Curse is clearly said to apply anytime they trust someone or harm someone through their leadership. This makes the Beast Curse least likely to cause trouble during the game while the Darkling has easiest time avoiding his, even if it can strike more often than once a Story, while the Fairest is stuck bleeding Clarity all the time, unless they withdraw from decision making and such.

            But the ultimate point is this. If the Blessing is supposed to be something that enforces your self image by repeating the task that helped you win your freedom the Curse should be about rattling that self-image. Beast is about freedom hence it is rattled by captivity, Darkling is about betrayal thus it is rattled by trust, Fairest is about leadership and so it is rattled by... leadership? That just makes no sense at all. It would make sense if it was rattled by poor leadership, well more sense than it does now, but as pointed out it doesn't care whether the plan succeeds or fails only about harm befalling someone. It would be more sensible perhaps if it was rattled by anarchy, they risk their Clarity if they cause dissidence and chaos by rising against appointed leadership but even in this case usurpation of power should be possible without triggering the Curse if the actual structures of power remained in place.

                                I think... what I'd rather see than the Leadership thing that is being pushed, is if it was about Perfection. Either the illusion of it, or creating it in the world around them. The person whom seems noble, and 'better' would come across as being Fairest, but the steps they have to take to make sure everything is Perfect is where the darkness starts to creep in. Those up-all-night perfectionists, trying to show their value, trying to enforce their will, putting all of themselves into making that project they want to complete perfect! But, their drive to do so shows how fallible they really are. When someone puts all of themselves into something, sure, it can be pretty. But that scraggled look of three days without sleep, hairs scattered all about as they try to stitch something together that no one else had the ability to do so (or, so the character believes) kinda feels like it's more in line with what Fairest are supposed to be in this edition, but somewhat different from the first edition, an interesting middle ground.

                                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                    One of my big problems with the assumptions comes from the apparent high level of violence that happens in peoples games. Most people don't go out of their way for a fight, so it's not like someone getting hurt under the Fairest's direction is something that happens every other game.

                                    But maybe that's just my games.
                                    I ran a game (mixed supernaturals) weekly for about a year and half, maybe two years. In all that time, the players were responsible for about 8-10 deaths (not counting ghosts/spirits). At least half of the NPCs they killed were killed in self defense. Almost all of the rest were very intentional, with one being an actual execution that was debated within the party. A handful of bystanders and allies suffered some sort of significant harm due to blowback/collateral damage, one did die, but it wasn't something that happened every time they tried to execute a plan. The rest of the deaths in the campaign were all due to the independent actions of the various villains, and even they didn't rack up crazy body counts. If I had to guess, I'd say that the Fairest curse would have only come up about half a dozen times, maybe a few more if you wanted to be really liberal about what constitutes seriously hurt. That game was focused on mystery and investigation, though. Conversely, I played in a vamp game recently where we were responsible for at least twice as many deaths in just a few months worth of weekly games. In that game, the curse would have come up every 2-3 sessions. So, it varies quite a bit. This might be something to tweak with house rules based on group play style. If you run a game where combat is used sparingly and/or the players' plans causing serious harm is rare, maybe keep it as is. For most of the games I run, I think it would work fine as written. If combats and collateral damage are a regular feature of your games, maybe be generous with bonuses to the breaking point roll, or limit the curse to once a session, or give them a way to avoid it, like burning all of their temporary glamour to re-roll for the breaking point.
