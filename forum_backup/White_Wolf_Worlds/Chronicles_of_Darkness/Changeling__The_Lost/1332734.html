    [Dark Eras Imperial Rome] The Marble Court

    08-26-2019, 05:37 PM
    ​Hello folks! I'm currently working on a Play by Post Changeling game here on the forums. The game itself is set in Imperial Rome and one of his gimmicks is the existence of a fith court, the Marble Court, which acts as a sort of cultural counterpat to the seasonal courts. Its details, in short, are the following:

    Originally posted by Cinder View Post

    The biggest difference when it comes to the Lost in Rome would be the existence of the Marble Court, an organization born during Republican times which quickly rose to prominence. The Marble Court embodies a simple truth: while changelings triumph and die, the Lost Courts of Rome endure eternal. The Marble Court is a reflection of the Roman ideal of conquest and stability. Its core belief is that the greatness of Rome is a blessing changeling should claim as their birthright, that by giving their trust to the traditions and structure which allowed Rome to prosper the Lost honor the incorruptible foundation that allows all of them to exist in safety. It is an ideal which found its natural evolution with the rise of imperial power and nowadays is pretty much the changeling’s response to the cult of the empire. Seasons might change, but the marble under the Lost’s feet stands untouched. Technically, the Marble Court has no right to rule, but the change of seasons cannot happen unless the Court formally allows it, with proper rituals and investitures. The Marble Court can keep the passage of crowns on hold and “dutifully govern Rome’s Lost until the situation returns to acceptable conditions”. Its is a huge social and political circumstance which does not happen often and rarely lasts more than some weeks, but makes it so the Marble Court remains just as an important player in Roman Lost’s politics as any other, perhaps even more so. The ancient oaths which empower the Marble Court seem to play an important part in Rome’s protection from the Gentry, as shown by what happened when seasonal monarchs tried to govern without the Marble Court’s blessing. The responsibility to decide whether or not to allow the passage of seasons lies within the hands of the most respected changelings of the Court, who behave as their position requires more often than not, but also means their criteria can be painfully mundane, ranging from old grudges to simple corruption. The Wyrd, as always, does not care for such banal, human details
    I originally planned to send it only to my players, though since I do think the results are decent enough to submit to criticism, I though that sharing its write-up here could not hurt. There's some stuff I'm not writing here yet because it counts as spoilers (not to mention I'll need to write a bunch of Contracts as well), but here's a start. Let me know what you think about it!


    The Marble Court
    The Amethyst Laurel, The Court of the Eagle and the Gladius


    The hunter lowers his spear. As it touches the marble under the hunter’s feet, the iron tip of his weapon makes a blunt sound which reverberates in the silence of the night. He looks at his surroundings. Behind him, a heavy door locked from the other side. Around him, people in full armor drawing closer, their weapons pointed at him. Either soldiers or mercenaries, working for someone with enough gold to pay for their time and experience. Above him, on the seating of the small arena he found himself trapped in, a dozen of changeling looking below. They stare in silence. Among all them, sitting in the prestige box, his quarry.
    He stands up. The light of the torches shines through his crystalline skin as he moves, but true light bathes the place only when he starts to talk. “You thought me to be an easy prey, didn’t you, fiend? That I would flee as I did from the mines of your master? But here,” he says while making a wide gesture to the whole audience, “here you’re in Rome. And I’m Lucius Cornelius Scipio. My ancestors triumphed over Hannibal at Zama. I’m not going anywhere.”

    STRENGTH THROUGH PRIDE

    True strength does not lie in the fickle, transient qualities of a single individual. It is what happens when such qualities are put into service of something greater, another stone in walls of a bastion which entire generations lived to preserve. Men and women die for these ideas, armies go to war and world changes, so it’s hard to deny their power, and no idea proved to have more power than the glory that is Rome.

    Changelings are citizens of the empire just as any other person born on this side of the Hedge and, as such, they can draw from the strength of Rome should they prove up to the task. Such strength does not come easily: it’s born out of honor, stoicism, and sacrifice. It’s molded by traditions, structure, and abnegation. It comes with more duties than benefits, but blood and tears shed in its pursuit all contribute to the greater good. This is the eternal truth the Marble Court understands.

    Just as Rome endures thanks to its solid foundation, so do the Roman courts. The changelings of the seasonal courts might have a hard time admitting it, but without the stability offered by the Marble Court, there would be no Roman Lost. There would be no celebrations, no armies to move against the Gentry, no knowledge to preserve, and no safe havens to hide within. There would be changelings, perhaps, but they would be emotional, weak things, unworthy of being called Romans. It’s the Marble Court that provides an unchanging center around which the turn of season can happen. With the Marble Court’s changelings being the keepers of the traditions and qualities which allowed the Lost of Rome to prosper for this long, the very same beliefs which made Rome great in the first place, they exalt and encourage the other courts to be a better version of themselves.

    Being a member of the Marble Court grants comfort and a reason to be through the pride of being Roman. Its changelings see themselves of the inheritors of the armies who triumphed over Carthage, the birthplace of Caesar and Augustus, and the empire which unified so many different countries and people under a single banner. The Losts of the Marble Court are reassured that the traditions who helped to shape Rome will also protect and empower them, that the Roman way of life is the correct one.

    The Amethyst Laurel determines who is worthy of ruling over Rome, how to behave in time of crisis, and acts as impartial judge in matters of the utmost importance. The Marble Court might have no right to rule, but it’s through its blessing others do and, should it need to take charge of Rome until other courts regain enough dignity, so be it. It is the Marble Court which reminds changelings of what is Rome and what is not, setting a boundary that even the Gentry must respect. When the Marble Court wields the duty of leadership, it imposes the ideals of Rome on the Gentry as well. The Fae, enemies at the gates who come from infinite kingdoms of thorns and madness, need to pay due respect to the empire if they wish to wage war to Rome when the Amethyst Laurel leads. The Others have to enact the proper rituals, make sacrifices, and even make tributes to changelings before the conflict can start for real. It’s a declaration of intent accompanied by tangible efforts to ask for fate’s approval for it. The mercurial nature of the Gentry is forced into a somewhat recognizable shape by centuries of Roman traditions, which often leads to jarring and bizarre results, but also forces the lords and ladies of Arcadia to ascribe to the changelings of Rome a degree of dignity and prideful importance because they are citizens of the Roman empire, a truth so strong even the True Fae have to respect it.

    Changelings of the Marble Court harvest the pride which gives them strength by putting such strength into practice. They remind others authority and command are a birthright of theirs. By doing that, they behave as the representatives of the empire they’re expected to be, an attitude which shaped everything that came before them and everything that will come later. It might be a source of trouble, with so many ready to hold a grudge when you show them their place in the world, but it’s either for their own good or they’re foes who deserve to be vanquished.

    FANATISM

    Pride blinds. The belief that your way of life, your ancestry, traditions, and nation are not only valuable but the only correct way to exist, it’s easy to dismiss everything else as inferior. At best, it’s worthless babble which wastes your time. At worst, it’s something which menaces to poison everything you value from within.

    The Marble Court is the worst enemy of itself. It takes pride in its stability and firm strength, but such pride also leads towards self-destruction and stagnancy. After all, if the Roman way is the right one, then there’s no reason to adapt to the passage of time or take some effort to reconsider how to behave. If it worked in the past, it’s going to work forever. It’s how we’ll make it work now. This treacherous way of thinking lends itself both to corruption and mental closure, flaws which are going to eventually cause enough cracks in the foundation of the Marble Court for it to collapse on itself. Empty shows of force like games and parades matter little if everything is crumbling and those with the power to do something about it refuse to see it.
    In the meantime, this attitude also translates into not-so-veiled hostility towards all who dare to criticize the methods of the Amethyst Laurel or the actions of its members. To attack them means to attack Rome itself, regardless of the intentions of the opposite part, and to attack Rome marks someone as coward, pariah, traitor. Enemy of the empire.

    The Marble Court owes more than it is willing to admit to the Imperial cult and it’s not always clear what it’s true and what is propaganda. Courtiers themselves, even the most respected ones, often lack the necessary perspective to deal with challenges with the objectivity they require. They might allow other courts to make their voices heard but, when it comes critical decisions, it’s their way or nothing.

    Another ugly byproduct of the Marble Court’s dogma is how exclusively Roman it is. To say only Lost of pure Latin ancestry are allowed in the Amethyst Laurel would be a lie, but it’s also true the Marble Court is the less cosmopolite court by far and that its members who come from noble Roman families are seen as first among equals, even inside a court which already considers itself as such. It should not be like this: every durance is different but all have the dubious virtue of making it possible for changelings to relate to each other from a shared suffering, to rebuild a new life from themselves regardless of who they were before the Thorns tore away their soul. But old habits die hard and there’s still a distinction between who is Lost and who is instead a Roman Lost. It veers into true xenophobia at times, a fear which has consequences and whom enemies can use to deliver insidious but fatal wounds.

    The most subtle weakness of the Amethyst Laurel is another one, though. With its changeling being so enraptured by the noble tradition they’re part of, their individuality often pays a price. If all that matters is Rome, a dream which will outlast them all, then what does a single person matter? Just as it happens for humans, the empire thrives thanks to sacrifices, control, and conquest. Rome does not care about the scars a changeling bears. It does not care about its dreams, its fears, nor about its enemies. If offers cold strength, sure, but sometimes those who are Lost need more a warm embrace and someone who does not consider their emotions as a weakness to be ashamed of.

    Mantle: Marble

    Mantle Effects: The Marble Mantle is magnificent and imposing. It speaks of strength and honor, of glorious aggression and blood spilled by great man. Each step made while wearing it is made of solid ground with the uplifting certainty it will lead towards a great destiny. The sound of marching armies and triumphant music accompany it. The clothes it wraps around shine like gold. Wherever the courtier goes, he is never alone: he is of Rome and Rome walks with him.

    Recover a Glamour point whenever you establish dominance over people with different allegiances from yours.

    • Gain bonus dice equal to your character’s Mantle dots on mundane rolls to convince others you’re the right person to take charge of the situation at hand.
    •• Gain the benefits of the Gentrified Bearing Merit even if your character does not qualify.
    ••• Once per chapter, you may spend a Willpower point to grant your character additional dots of Court Goodwill Merit equal to her Mantle dots, to a maximum of five per court, for the scene.
    •••• Your character can regain one Willpower point whenever he regains Glamour thanks to the Marble Mantle.
    ••••• Once per chapter, you may spend 1 Glamour and roll Mantle + Wyrd vs. Composure + Wyrd to inflict the Awestruck Condition on a target for the remainder of the scene.
