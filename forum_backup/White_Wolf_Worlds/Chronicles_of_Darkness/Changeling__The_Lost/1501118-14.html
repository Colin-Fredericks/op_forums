                            Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                            Does other courts have Bargains that the Huntsmen need to respect like the Seasonal Courts? I may have missed the part that explains why this is exclusively a Seasonal Court thing.
                            They do. It's not exclusive to the Seasonal Courts. The sidebar in the Huntsmen section is talking about how although a Huntsman counts as an agent of the Fae for the purposes of a Bargain's constraints, loopholes exist in those Bargains that mean the Bargain offers little obstacle. The sidebar talks about this in relation to the Seasonal Courts' Bargains because the Seasonal Courts are the ones with full writeups in the corebook and thus the most directly needing addressed re: their usual defensive measures versus an unusual antagonist.

                            By way of a comparable example, the Wind Court of Chicago's Bargain doesn't help much directly against Huntsmen — it renders the Gentry and their agents easily memorable and their witnesses indelible (still not something you want when dealing with a Court of gossips and reputation-builders who rely more on personal connections than institutional ones), but a Huntsman who doesn't care about leaving an impression has little deterrent compared to, say, hobgoblins and privateers who have to do business with the black mark of having meddled with the assets of a famously litigious and possessive demon even if the more concrete magical warding methods in circulation around the freehold didn't do the trick. (Notably, the Fire Court's Bargain doesn't have any such readily available loophole, because Fire rules the roost while those wards are down and being compelled to help restore them simply means picking one's moment and maybe giving the rest of the Court conflicted feelings about any concessions one might ask for.)

                                    Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                                    Can someone explain in simple terms how the Digital Hedge(BriarNet) works?
                                    It's a space within the Hedge accessed through its own Hedgeways (called nodes), much like the Halls of Mirrors or the Dreaming Roads; it allows characters to engage with digital data in metaphorical dressing the same way they would with the rest of the Hedge.

                                    How difficult is to get in/out?
                                    Traveling through a node to reach a destination in the Hedge makes travel very slightly harder (per The Hedge's notes on accessing BriarNet Goblin Markets), but reaching a node doesn't have to be a declared goal like the Gate of Horn or the Hall of Mirrors would be.

                                    Outside the context of its interfering with navigation, you have to Hedgespin a means of access symbolically appropriate to your current location in the Hedge with a six-success paradigm shift, and in order to physically get from the BriarNet to the real world you need to exit to the Hedge first. Locations referred to as BriarNet Cafés can provide a ready place to make the jump from fairyland to the sprite-cities.

                                    How do you travel outside the Trods on the Information Superhighway?
                                    The same way you travel outside of trods in the regular Hedge. The Information Superhighway is just the spine of cyberspace, much as there's more to the Dreaming Roads than its eponymous trods.

                                    What exactly the Autonomous Payload contract does?
                                    Lets you poke around in the actual data of a networked device using Hedgespinning instead of simply meddling with the liminal space of the internet.

                                    Can you use a computer to open a Digital Hedge portal?
                                    A device used to access online Goblin Markets often enough will eventually develop into a dormant Hedgeway that leads to the BriarNet and can be opened from the inside by one-time Keys from its Goblin Markets (referred to as broadsides).

                                    Popular BriarNet Goblin Markets might develop trods routing metaphorical traffic toward them and doing too much online shopping might let a hobgoblin into your home through your PC, but developing a stable connection between reality and cyberspace would be huge news for the Lost.

                                            Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                                            So, it is a 6 success paradigm shift to enter and another 6 success paradigm shift to exit the Digital Hedge?
                                            There is nothing in the books indicating you need a paradigm shift to leave the Digital Hedge. The paradigm shift to enter is to leverage suitable digital symbolism like driving on a highway (of information) in an urban environment or (web) surfing in the vicinity of an ocean or climbing fiber optic cables between the points of light in the starry night sky; there isn't really a consistent analog counterpart to that since the rest of the Hedge is the full-spectrum breadth of symbolic options.

                                            Nodes are described in Kith and Kin as milestones representing mortal-work hardware on BriarNet-specific trods representing network connections, and are then described as analog-digital Hedgeways within the Hedge used to access the BriarNet in The Hedge, which says that passing through a node across the divide isn't significant enough to necessarily qualify as a goal for navigation purposes by itself.

                                            Given that The Hedge's additional information comes in the context of accessing Goblin Markets and most characters who aren't changelings, enTitled Huntsmen, or Fae can't use paradigm shifts, and given that the products of Hedgespinning tend to persist until shaped away (mechanical bonuses or penalties notwithstanding), it seems reasonable to parse the combined mechanics such that the Hedgespinning method is a shortcut allowing you to make use of the pervasive nature of the internet rather than an essential step in going in either direction across the digital divide.
