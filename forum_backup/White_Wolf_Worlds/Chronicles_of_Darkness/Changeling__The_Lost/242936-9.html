    Originally posted by EmperorClydeIV View Post
    Here is my own idea on the new contract system :

    There is no "universal" or "seeming" contracts. There is just "contracts. Each contract has a power, a cost and a catch. For what would be a "universal" contract, that's it. For what would be a "seeming" contract, you add a spin on the contract for a few (or all) the seemings. One of those seeming spin can be a little stronger than the others (to make it "favored"), but I personally think they should all be about the same power level. Some seeming could be skipped, but I don't think there really is a need to skip one and only one all the time. Sometimes it could be more than one, sometimes there could be none missing. Also, you can never buy a contract with the seeming spin of an other seeming than yours.

    What we get from that is a system that reinforce some thematics and liberate the mechanics from some artificial constraints.

    - A changeling with no seeming can buy any contract. Their contract is just a little less powerful than a changeling with a seeming. If the seeming is a factor of the changeling's agency, the changeling with no agency does not get to put his personal spin on a contract. The contract is law and you follow it to the letter. You did not assert you individuality, so you're still just a prop in someone else's story (your keeper's).

    - Your seeming is the expression of your agency. You're not only a prop anymore. You have taken some control over the rules that were imposed on you. That's why you can put your own spin on your contracts; you can bend it a little in your own way. That also means you cannot "fake" your agency, meaning you cannot invoke a contract with the spin of an other seeming.

    In one of the books (I don't remember which) there was rule to shift your kith/seeming over time to something new. If at some point we allow a changeling to buy an other seeming's version of a contract, in my mind that would mean the changeling evolved and changed to that seeming first.

    Also, all contracts are accessible in some form, even if a contract has no spin for your particular seeming.

    Agree. All Changling should be able to learn all Arcadia's magic, only some Seemings could benefit more from some powers. But there is a problem with that take also - to this day Contracts were lists of powers. With "it's all universal now" we can only have one power per Contract - so not 6 powers for Contract of Strenght to choose for your character, only one per theme. So powers will be more... boring.

    But I like approche with Seeming Contracts having many "Facets" ( to still from Forsaken 2e name ), and you can buy any one you like, only it could be more costley for you.

            One thing that must be brought up in the Facet comparison that makes it an improper fit is that those Facets are keyed to Renown, not Auspice. This factors a few things.

            1)Renown is something everyone can buy and invest in, Seemings aren't. Yes, you can buy a merit that makes it so you count as another one, but I've always had a minor feeling that's a 'pick one and settle' sort of deal. The dynamic is therefore different and more limited for players-which is fine in ways, but...well, let me make the other points.
            2)Renown is a thing you buy, Seemings aren't. Now, granted, buying Renown means you get either a Moon Gift and a Shadow Gift or you get some Facets, and of course it makes those powers more potent, but the point is that you are first investing in something not inherently related to the gifts you're using-okay, I need to explain that a little better.

            THe point I'm getting at, with Renown, Gifts, and Facets, is that, from an EXP expenditure point of view, isn't exactly the same as buying gifts and facets, although it is related. You can just go and buy facets(barring renown qualifications), but the setup leads you to buy Renown first. It's an extraneous buy that has become very beneficial to have regardless.

            How that relates to seemings is that a Changeling doesn't sink EXP into other things inherently, Court Contracts aside. They just buy the Contract. When we use the Renown system as a mirror, this says a few things about EXP and power. All the Shadow Gifts are three dot powers, effectively, because Renown serves this sort of buffer state between the powers. I imagine Renown is still fairly expensive, even if Gifts and Facets aren't so much themselves. So without that diversion of Renown, Contracts either become more expensive, or the power drops, or some mixture of both(which, I'm sure, is fine for some people, as that fits Changeling). Having Seemings fill in the same place just-

            -....Goddamnit, I lost my train of thought.

            Anyways, here's where I'm going. Seeming as an underline to Contracts is pretty cool, and Facets are a good comparison to keep in mind, but I don't think we should have Facets be the absolute guiding star here. It can inform, it shouldn't direct. I think Contracts benefit a lot from having the power that seemings underline, rather than something more directly facet-y

            I mean, if nothing else, Werewolf has done Facets. Changeling can do something else.

                Originally posted by eberg View Post
                One of my concerns is that Seeming Contracts can inform a fairly narrow concept of the Seeming. All of the building-focused contracts for Wizened aren't really applicable to the non-builder kiths (which is most of them). Contracts of Elements can similarly get weird when you stray into non-conventional elements. I just don't see how they add much by being associated with Seemings. I'd much prefer the space be used for more Universal Contracts which players can pick and choose from to get the character they want. As much as I love Changeling, one of my main gripes is that I found myself scraping for contracts which fit my character's concept most of the time and a lot of this was because of the need to wed most of the contracts to Seeming or Court. Since kith is going to do the heavy lifting in terms of the specific manifestation of your changes and they are being uncoupled from Seemings, I think Seeming Contracts should follow suit.

                Really, I suppose the common theme in what I want is the ability to have a wide range of bits to build a Changeling. Faeries are traditionally depicted as a really diverse lot and part of the joy of it to me is just how creative you can get with your concepts. I can come up with dozens of Changeling NPCs to populate a setting and have none of them be at all alike. You can be almost anything. Lost was a real improvement over Dreaming in this respect and I'd like to see the last few impediments towards really free-form building removed.

                Okay, I'll shut up now. Carry on.

                The idea would be that we could actually use the Seeming variations on Contracts to branch out and expand the ideas of Seemings. Seeing what Contract of the Shadow Cloak might mean to each of six different Seemings gives us the chance to flesh out the setting and the Seemings bit by bit with very few words.

                                    Amazing idea. I am 100% behind the general concept here as well as the kith before the seeming idea in the other post. It sounds like nice upgrades to me. Where I'm not so sold is on how the XP is used regarding contracts. It is something I always felt was fuzzy in 1e (and I mean no offense to the writers) and I have concerns that it may remain the case here. Please allow me to explain, as my intent here is to openly share and hopefully help, not to criticize.

                                    Spending XP for skills make sense, as it reprensent the actual experience you gain from the time spent working on becoming better at something. No need to expand on this concept. But, contracts are... well, deals. Made long ago between two parties. Without ever having read the contract to know of its intricacies (because it was likely verbal and never written) you manage to claim yourself as eligible to receive the benefits of that deal via a technicality and you pay for it with the non-existing currency of XP?

                                    I may have completely misunderstood the concept, but that was how I read the core book so I never could understand how you suddenly came to know how to make yourself eligible and why it should cost XP to state out loud "because I'm made of leaves, I should be able to do X". Or for that matter why allies couldn't just tell each other "BTW, you're made of leaves. So, you're eligible." and then contracts get spread like cookies.

                                    Don't get me wrong, I love contracts. The idea is brilliant and so perfectly in tune with the spirit of Changeling: The Lost. In my games, I usually have each courts have a changeling as the role of Keeper. People who are tasked with the keeping of lore on ancient contracts and of protecting their secrets. Gaining access to contracts requires favor from a court, or a keeper willing to look away) so that you could spend time reading them and getting to know them intimately (spending time and effort = XP) so that you could eventually find the loophole allowing you to use it.

                                    But to go back to my point, here you have contracts tied to five seemings. So it becomes "Yeah, I'm made of leaves, so I should be able to do X (1XP) and also, I'm pretty so really you should also let me benefit from this second aspect of this contract too (2XP) and and you know I have a primal heart, so, it makes sense that I could also do X (3XP)". From a technical standpoint looking at the possibilities I honestly adore this approach. However, as a storyteller or a player I find it difficult to maintain immersion.

                                    If I may suggest other ideas:

                                    1 As others have, may be untying the contracts to the concept of XP or gaining them by technicality might work? Suppose, for example, that contracts aren't gained by way of being something but also of having accomplished something. Surmise, for example, that they require a price from the character. Suppose, the creator of the Vainglory contract had to abandon his only love to seal the deal, maybe today changelings who are eligible by virtue of a technicality must also pay a similar price to become party to the original arrangement? Then, entire sessions and stories might erupt from the simple need of the motley for one of their own to gain a certain ability. "Guys, we need our Ogre to gain the favors of Vainglory for this plan to work so... how do we arrange for a women to fall in love with him?"

                                    2. May be the idea of the keeper (explained above) or any variation deemed more appropriate could be worked into a mechanic?

                                    Well, that turned into quite the all of text. Sorry about that and thank you for your time.
