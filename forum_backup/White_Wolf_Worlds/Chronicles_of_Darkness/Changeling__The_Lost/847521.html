    Crowd Sourcing Buena Vista

    03-14-2016, 11:58 PM
    I'm still new to Changeling, and Storytelling in general, so I'm thinking about trying some collaborative world building to speed things up and give my ideas some quality control and (hopefully) constructive criticism. I'll probably be updating this opening post as things shape out, and I either get new ideas or integrate those of posters into the setting. Ultimately, it's my setting, so please don't take it personally if I don't accept every idea.

    Themes: Technological hubris and folly, the unfulfilled promises of science fiction.

    Buena Vista is a Texan city located on the coast of the Gulf of Mexico, straddling the Matagorda ship channel. The city was something of an attempt at a planned community by several business leaders and politicians who styled themselves as futurists, technocrats, and visionaries. Disney's "Epcot" was originally an acronym for "Experimental Prototype Community Of Tomorrow." Buena Vista certainly tried to beat Epcot at that game. While the city is doing relatively fine economically, there are always features in the city's design, architecture, and layout that bring a bittersweet nostalgia for the days when someone could believe a science fiction utopia was going to be here by the not-too-distant year 2000. The twenty-first century hasn't lived up to that hype (yet), and like a child who's outgrown his once-favorite cartoon, many people in the city are embarrassed at the lingering Zeerust. Some embrace the oddness and put "Keep Buena Vista Weird" bumper stickers on their cars. Others celebrate it ironically.

    One phrase that fits many of the landmarks in Buena Vista is "ill-conceived." The Matagorda Tunnel that joins the two halves of the city under the Matagorda ship channel was largely made to demonstrate simply that it could be done, rather than the much simpler solution of a raising bridge. Residents joke that drivers going through the tunnel should turn on their windshield wipers. Port Atlantis, a hybrid of theme park, tech expo, and aquarium was designed by committee, without a coherent mission statement, much less a marketing plan. True to its name, it sank, financially speaking.

    Though most of the business ventures starting during the city's founding have fallen through, the city itself works pretty well as a resort and tourism city. The tech industry retains a strong hold in Buena Vista, but ironically, many of the worthwhile innovations developed there tend to be incremental improvements or 'boring but useful' technologies that the average person might take for granted or not be aware of.

    Landmarks:

    Buena Vista Tech: "BV Tech." Mascot: Beaver. (Sometimes a cyborg beaver)

    Port Atlantis: Now run-down, the property was bought by an eccentric sci-fi fan who holds mass LARP sessions. (Actually Ubuntu Serif's Goblin Market)

    [The Occult Quarter]: Inspired by a trip I took to New Orleans, where I saw many occult stores, fortune tellers and the like in the French Quarter. One of the influences for the "Keep Buena Vista Weird" motto, since it's "fantasy" juxtaposed with the "sci-fi" environment, and superstition right next to BV Tech's modern science. (De facto run by the Autumn court.)

    Matagorda Tunnel: Underground tunnel joining the southern island to the mainland, underneath the Matagorda Ship Channel. Some people find it unnerving to drive under a body of water, and people joke about it leaking, but sometimes they do need to pump some water out of the tunnel before it interferes with traffic. Urban legends spring up around cars vanishing inside the tunnel, but are brushed off as tall tales born of anxiety. (The tunnel sometimes leads into the Hedge)

    [Hotel and Resort]: A particularly attractive hotel and resort. (Spring Court headquarters, owned and operated. Court business is often done in areas sealed off for 'private parties.' This isn't exactly inaccurate.)


    Nearby Features:


    Sundown Island Bird Sanctuary

    Mad Island Wetlands Management Area


    [The Freehold of Buena Vista]: Seasonal Court System.

    I'm not sure how the courts are going to be organized beyond having a Monarch and an appointed [Class] of [Season] position. I'm also not sure how big the freehold should be, but I have decided that Buena Vista is becoming one of the more 'fae dense' spots on Earth. Being a tourist location, being weird has a higher degree of tolerance from the community, making it fairly Changeling-friendly, at least in the nicer neighborhoods. Despite the name, Monarchs are elected on a yearly basis by the members of their court.

    Queen of Spring: []

    Druid of Spring: []

    King of Summer: []

    Knight of Summer: []

    Queen of Autumn: [] (Draconic Fairest)

    Sorcerer of Autumn: Ziggy Zapf (Gameplayer/Gremlin Beast)

    Queen of Winter: []

    Spymaster of Winter: []


    Court holidays: The freehold celebrates a number of 'transition holidays' that are designed to maintain goodwill between the courts on both ends. The Spring Court hosts a series of games and talent shows that transition into contests of martial strength and athletic skill overseen by the Summer Court. Summer joins Autumn in the Hunt of Fallen Leaves, which ends with the Autumn Court's Festival of Fallen Leaves, a celebration of the (perceived) safety caused by spreading fear to the freehold's enemies, featuring trading of occult tips, tricks, and trinkets as well as any arcane spoils from the hunt. Autumn joins Winter in the Night of Enigmas during the Winter Solstice, where allies trade secrets using all manner of cryptic clues, ranging from lighthearted scavenger hunts for obscure trivia to impenetrable cryptographia for truly dangerous knowledge. Finally, The Winter Masquerade is held in a different hidden location each year, transitioning into the Spring Revel when disguises are discarded (or not).

              I guess I should unpack some of my meaning and generally rephrase. It's not technology itself that causes hubris and folly, it's certain people's attitude about technology that produces a particular flavor of folly I'm referring to as "technological hubris and folly." There are times when simple, "low tech" solutions are more practical. Sometimes, certain "high tech" solutions are favored instead because of hype, spectacle, or because it's a fad. Sometimes overly optimistic innovators forget to include backup plans or failsafes in case things don't work out as well as they do in theory.

              It's the difference between reckless optimism and empty promises versus cautious optimism and calculated risk. Buena Vista has the former built into in its history and foundation and matured by transitioning to the latter. Technology isn't bad, it's just that many people pin too many hopes on it producing utopia overnight. The Gentry are in Buena Vista because they can exploit people using the fantastic spectacle of science fiction / future fantasy of that alternate vision of the wondrous Year 2000 over the comparatively "boring" 21st century we have in reality. You want a flying car and a light saber? Sure, hop on over to Arcadia and we'll get you some.

                          True Fae cataloged as "Exocet" by Ziggy:

                          Known Titles:
                          "The Old Bird" (Actor): He appears as a colossal six-eyed, six-winged bird in Arcadia. His eyes constantly shift around independently of each other, observing the threads of fate being pulled all around him. He's a particularly old member of the Gentry (Wyrd 10) and mostly entertains himself by playing Oracle for other Fae beings. While he does his best to pose as a neutral agent of fate who simply delivers prophecies, he prefers to manipulate things to his ends. Specifically, he enjoys self-fulfilling prophecies of doom. When dealing with purely fae beings, he knows how to subtly line up the dominoes to get them to doom themselves in spectacular fashion. With mortals, he has to perform constant adjustments, to his displeasure. The thing he has trouble figuring out: Mortals just aren't as ruthless or as cowardly as he keeps expecting them to be.

                          Greatest failure: He intended to issue the final nudge during the Cuban Missile Crisis. When the missiles didn't start flying, his frustration got him pushing his agents harder, which ironically destroyed those agents' credibility, scaring some cooler heads into realizing fear could easily doom humanity.

                          Notable influence: He once dream-poisoned Edgar Allen Poe, and became the inspiration for The Raven. Since then, he's developed a fondness for retorting "Nevermore" to people's optimism.

                          "The Great Orrery" (Realm): A vast planetarium-like realm filled with mechanical solar systems and moving star charts. Here, The Old Bird examines the fates of the countless realms of Arcadia, and occasional glimpses of the mortal universe. He has seen the Degenerate Era, the Big Rip, and the end of the mortal realm as we know it, and he wants that feeling of doom and inevitability to extend to the comparatively small world of individual humans. He "knows" it's all pointless and futile, and he delights in proving it to himself by causing doom and destruction on multiple levels. With his solipsistic perspective, he's convinced he is fate, and that the mortal realm will end because he decreed it, not because he observed it.

                          Favorite Kiths:
                          Telluric, for reading the Orrery's signs.
                          Author, for putting wiggle room, twists, and ambiguities into his prophecies. Without them, his prophecies often devolve into meaningless word salad. Which sometimes works, anyway.
                          Antiquarian, for researching the past so he can calculate how it will influence the future.
                          Riddleseeker: For interpreting or "de-interpreting" prophecies in his library.

                          Prospective PCs: Keeper for one of my potential players (Author/Wizened) who was his most recent favored author.

                          So... Anyone got suggested frailties?

                              Ziggy's Keeper, cataloged as "Helvetica," known titles:

                              The Queen of Elegant Blades (Actor)
                              The Lady of Ashes (Actor)
                              The Fractal Wilderness (Realm modified by Ziggy as his Durance)
                              The Fang of Muramasa (Prop)

                              Helvetica is a relatively young member of the Gentry who views herself as a noble warrior in her many guises. She has an obsession with dueling and other violent forms of competition. She favors straightforward methods, and her Actors usually have high Strength and Presence so that she can plow through her enemies or intimidate them with her "warrior spirit" in impressive displays. For this, she has a reputation as somewhat of a foolish brute among the Gentry, but she has her cunning and subtle side, often using agents in the shadows to set her enemies up so she can sweep them off their feet with her brute force methods while they're vulnerable. She sometimes uses her agents to frame her enemies for "dishonorable" behavior so that she can get the social force to challenge them in the open.

                              With the rise of anime in the West, she's decided to relive some of her Japanese-flavored fashions, and dug up one of her oldest weapons, The Fang of Muramasa, created by the legendary mad swordsmith himself. The Queen of Elegant Blades currently manifests as a female samurai, while the Lady of Ashes takes on the guise of a youkai-summoning Miko. These days, the Lady's summoned monsters often resemble collectible monsters. She had Ziggy modify her realm into the Fractal Wilderness so that she could collect "fresh" monsters.

                              Virtue: Honor
                              Vice: Pride

                              Helvetica recently received a prophecy from Exocet, who is currently orchestrating her downfall. Helvetica is now focused on eliminating perceived threats so that she can nobly fight against her fate.

                                  Codex Shredder: Once the PC's motley has one of the book MacGuffins, various changelings and hobs begin encountering a large stone lion with metallic shredder teeth patrolling the local trods. It always demands that Hedge travelers surrender their books and other written materials to feed it. It can smell writing, so smuggling a book past it will require creativity. Its personality subtly changes with its diet.

                                  Frailties: Gets horribly sick if it eats a book with a large "hatedom" toward it (Twilight, for example). Television, movies and other modern non-text media "empty" it.
