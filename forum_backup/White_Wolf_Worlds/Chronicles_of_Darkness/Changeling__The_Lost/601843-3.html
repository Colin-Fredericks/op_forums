                        Bein' trans is - I mean, I think, I'm not a neuropsychologist with access to my entire mental history - a big part of WHY I'm so interested in this story. Before I transitioned it was wish-fulfillment (and it still is, because I am a bundle of barely functional neuroses) and it never really stopped.

                        It's tricky though - there are... a few aspects of it. The first is that I want the character to be changed unwillingly - I've played characters who WANTED to be changed, but they ran out of steam really fast, because ultimately I didn't... know how to play a trans character whose dysphoria had been resolved. There wasn't really any arc there, so it's got to go fish-out-of-water. Second is that it needs to be a STRUGGLE to get back - again, because otherwise the story ends too fast and I'm left with a character I don't know how to play. Third is that they DO need to go back - as mentioned, I'm trans, and if they DON'T want to go back, then that implies the mind is a plaything of the body and that gender is a product of biological sex, which... is not something pleasant to think about. I tend to play characters who identify as male - even though I don't - because that means the transformation also acts as wish fulfillment (for me, not them). And then the reaffirmation of their gender - the continuous desire to return to being male - while somewhat out of character, is also slightly DISTANCED from me, which makes it a little easier to deal with.

                        So I'm trying to write a character arc that includes all those elements. And as a result of the discussion in the thread, it currently seems like number 2 is the hardest, though...

                        I mean, the example for 'permanent cosmetic changes' is a change of haircolor, and a rewrite of someone's entire biological sex seems somewhat beyond that level. (... Also, given how Goblin Markets work, it doesn't seem impossible that sex would be the ONLY thing that's changed, resulting in them still APPEARING female. Getting a Y chromosome back doesn't necessitate the sudden disappearance of female secondary sexual characteristics. Or primary.)
