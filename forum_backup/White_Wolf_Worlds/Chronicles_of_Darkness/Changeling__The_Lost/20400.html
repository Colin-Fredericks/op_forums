    Re-tellings of Adventures and Backstories

    11-29-2013, 06:26 PM
    I am sure many of you have taken more than just notes of sessions you have played or written. Perhaps you have detailed the background of a character or NPC of a story. Mayhaps you have written a bit of poetry or song for or about something that happened in game. There are very few places we can share these things in which the readers will understand the darkness, the subtle hints of changeling life or appreciate the depth between the lines we have written. I have a couple of such stories, but rather than just posting them I hope to make a thread for the community to post their works.

    ~I ask that any feedback on the material posted here be PM'ed to individuals rather than posted in the thread. That way we can simply have a collection of works rather than pages of potential bickering.
    ~Also if you wish to leave feedback please think about your wording, may authors welcome constructive criticism ( "I found your dialog hard to follow due to the lack of reminders of who was speaking." ) as opposed to jack-arsery ( "You sux at writing!" ). So, please remember people who post here have put their time and energy into the pieces there is no need to be hateful    .
    ~Please keep in mind these pieces are based in the World of Darkness and may contain graphic or mature material.


    I will start off with my two pieces, but for easy of reading I will post them each in separate posts.

      Please, little girl don't! Thana's Backstory
      ~LadiSilverfox



      "Please, little girl don’t!”

      Her mother’s long slender fingers wrapped around her arm, tearing her free of the circular clothing rack. A stern and fierce look upon her face as she grit her teeth and began to speak.

      “Thana, you’re almost seven. Your birthday is in two days, act like it please. You know better than to hide in clothing racks. You could poke your eye out or there could be broken metal or worse, someone could pull you out the other side and I would never see you again. Is that what you want?” Thana stood before her mother who was glancing around trying not to make a scene, her face covered in some light dirt. Her mother had given up long ago trying to clean her face, as any amount of water or rubbing only ever seemed to make it worse. In a huff mother returned to browsing the shopping racks.

      Thana stood quickly for a moment while she looked around. Her mind began to wander and imagine the possibilities of the veritable urban jungle before her. Taking a step backwards, she slipped back into the circular rack and deep into the dense jungle brush. The men and women bustling about the over crowded store became monkeys swinging from the trees high above her head. The insolent employee who barely listened to the customer in front of them grew thick and stone-like while the native priest danced before it, jumping and hollering to appease the stone being.

      She was now the greatest explorer in the world and the only one who was able to enter and learn the secrets of the lost Colocca people. Shoving a dense group of green, leaf pattern Bahamas shirts aside to gain deeper access to the heart of the jungle. Consumed in her play, she didn’t notice as the sounds of her world drifted away. A sweater arm felt from above, the fabric fighting for her arm. ‘Snake’ she thought but this snake grew thorns of blood red. She slipped her arm free, somehow missing the thorns with ease.

      “Oh no, quicksand! And it’s not just quick, it’s like lightning. One toe in that and you are dead.” She explained to the imaginary camera men in a hushed tone. Measuring out her jump she sent herself flying over the large group of black tiles. Having made a death defying leap and landing safely on the other side of the innocuous looking “lightning” sand she gave herself a loud cheer. She quickly turned to see her mother’s scowl but it was not her mother she saw. There were nothing but trees with a dense canopy that could blot out the sun and underbrush so thick she could not move back through it. Her eyes wide and brimming with tears, “Mommy?” Her trembling voice called out over and over, returned only by the sound of hollering monkeys. Stricken with panic she began to run. The undergrowth became rough and hard to move, vines entangled her and their deep crimson thorns made sure to bite hard. The night fell and she continued to run, her arms as red as the thorns that tore at them.

      A clearing appeared before her, a campfire light flickering with much needed warmth. Her stomach growled and tightened, which drew her eyes to a chunk of meat roasting upon the spit. She hung back, but saw no more evidence of anyone. She was unable to hold her self back long. A rush of joy filled her heart as she stepped into the ring of stones that surrounded the camp. There was a canteen, a medical kit, food and a bed, all laid out. But now she stood frozen for on the other side of the fire, where she was sure had only been a rock before, sat a man. He seemed as old as time its self. His wrinkles were deep like canyon crevasses on his beardless face.

      “It’s okay child. What are you doing out here?” He paused, but she didn’t talk or move. “You seem like a good child. I’m sure your mother or father wouldn’t mind if you talked to me. I just want to help you. Why, just look at you. You're all cut up from the woods. If we don’t get you cleaned up you will get infected. Won’t your mother and father be cross then? Besides, I have some nice meat here. I would be willing to share some with you. Why you look hungrier then a starved dog. When is the last time you ate?” He pulled the meat from the fire and yanked a piece off. She watched anxiously as he put it into his mouth, chewed and finally swallowed. Her tongue lashed across her dry lips. Her eyes studied his every movement as she then watched him take a drink from the canteen. “Just water dear, no need to worry. What I provide will be shared.” He held it out for her. “But so shall you, too, provide.”

      Unable to bear it anymore, she got close enough to take it. The water was like a cold rain on a hot summer day. Soon she found herself sharing his meat and receiving care for her wounds, talking for hours on end of what had happened that brought her to this strange place. Her favorite part of the story was in the small details, as some of it now seemed buried: her mother’s perfume, the joys and pains she shared with some of the kids from school, the hopes she had for her presents which she would be receiving in a couple days for her birthday. He offered her his sleeping bag and she was pleased to take it. That night she dreamed of the old man returning her to her mother. She could already hear her mother’s lecture but she was going to be happy to have it.

      However, her dream became a nightmare. The old man was not a man at all but a creature made of cold hard stone. He ripped her from the sleeping bag, pulling her by her hair. The words he spoke before she drank his water, he now mumbled over and over. She was pulled off to a quarry were all the people were odd and twisted. Men and women with half bodies of goats dragged carts loaded down with stone up ramps along the walls. Others were made of rock, like the old man, forced to mine out small crystals from the walls with their bare hands. She watched as those with buckets full of the grape sized crystals carried them into a dark cave on the other end of the quarry. She was tossed to the ground towards a fiercely dressed trollish looking man. The old man walked into the dark cave as well.

      “Stand!” the Troll barked, his voice an odd high pitch squeak. She quickly did as she was told. His thick, pudgy fingers overlapped her cheeks as his beady eyes stared down at her. A few moments of awkward silence passed between them. “To the walls!” She stood frozen with fear, tears ready to stream down her dirt covered face. As the Troll raised his thorn covered whip a passing rock woman grabbed Thana's hand and pulled her towards the wall. The Troll lowered his weapon, satisfied that she would be working as was told now. The features of the woman were half of stone and half of flesh.

      The nightmare wouldn’t end but she would talk with the woman of stone while she worked. The woman was kind; she would ask all sorts of questions about her previous life. Then she would spend time learning how to mine the crystals. Each night the Trollish overseers would go around and pass out a handful of grape crystals to each person. They were hard but chewable after a long time of sucking on them. Each night the woman would have her go over the stories of her life again and say, “Never forget these things.”

      Time seemed to pass quickly now. Mining became less painful for her each day. Soon she was bringing her own buckets of crystals to the dark cave. She felt like it had been forever since she saw the old man. This time as she walked deep into the cool root-bound cavern he hardly seemed the same man. He was far more an unmovable pillar. She held out her bucket for him to take but he simply laughed and motioned her over. He had her sit beside him and tell him wild stories until he grew hungry again. As he ate he would give her extras. She grew to like going to the cave and now understood why the woman would try to do so for her and why she would have her tell the tales of her life. Tales from the world of mortals seemed to be his favorites.

      One day while entertaining him with stories, she began a story of an explorer in a deep jungle. This tale filled her with familiar feelings. Soon she was thinking of a sweet perfumed that smelled like nectar from heaven, orbs with a shiny iridescence that could dance on the wind, bits of paper covering beautiful boxes, tiny bits of crinkled string falling from the sky and the laughter of many children. She thought, too, of the old man’s words from the campfire which now seemed so long ago. She felt tired now. He offered her a crystal grape for her tale. She took it and pretended to eat but did not.

      The next few days the keeper would call for her. Each day she would tell her stories and would be rewarded with crystallized foods. Each day she pretended to partake but hid the food instead. Her hunger was enormous by the third day. She desired to indulge in the treats he gave her but she did not. She was tired of this life and wished for either her old life or for death, anything but this endless dream.

      On the third day again she was called to see the Keeper. She stood alone with him, this time. He was angry and did not want to hear her tell tales.

      “You have paid what you owed and have taken no more from me. I have no use for you! leave my cave!”

      She awoke with a start inside a circle of stones. The camp was gone as if it had never existed at all. Her movements were stiff, her memory hazy. Stumbling, she walked. The forest opened to her. Where was she to go? The sweet scent of perfume, like the nectar of the heaven, that is where she wanted to be. With laughing children, pretty bits of paper covered boxes, iridescent orbs playing on the wind and bits of crinkling paper raining from above. Running now, the sights, the sounds, the smells, it was crisper and crisper. Never did she falter, even as the thorns again tried to grab at her. Laughing and crying, tearing open of papers and clothing! CLOTHING! Several pairs of jeans hit her in the face. Tears ran down her cheek, she was so happy to see them. She pushed her way through into the outside world. To her left was a mirror and she found she wasn’t who she thought she was anymore. Her body was rough and made of stone, much as the women who had helped her in the dream had been, only even less of her was flesh. She was tall, taller then the long mirror. She had hair, scragglier and filled with clumps of soil. A glimmer of light caught her eye, the donging bell as someone entered the store. Turning to look around she saw someone familiar digging through clothing racks. Mother. There was no doubt, as she moved closer she smelled a hint of that perfume. She suddenly stopped. It was her. Younger and smaller but herself and she was heading into a clothing rack.

      If only she could remember her name but it wouldn’t come out. She had to act quickly; perhaps this could all be prevented. If she could avert this fate she had to try.

      “Please, little girl don’t!” the urgency in her plea was lost from her cold rocky voice.

      “Thana, you’re almost seven.” She heard her mother scold. That’s right she thought to herself. My name was Thana. Thana. I still am Thana… aren’t I?"

          The past of Tommy Williams. NPC Backstory.
          ~LadiSilverfox


          The weather was sticky and hot, just like yesterday and the day before. There was nothing pleasant about summer, Starflower, the young twig of a girl, thought to herself. The cries of her child floated from the back room of the beaten up apartment.
          “Arg! Would you shut it already?” despite her angry pleading the child continued to cry. She flopped down on the couch and pulled the small tic-tac container from her back pocket she looked at the two tiny pieces of amber colored bits of joy. “Ambrosia.” She mouthed to herself, but Justin would be back soon and she was supposed to wait for him. The baby continued to scream, she just couldn’t take it anymore. “You little shit...” she popped open the top and downed the last two pieces. They melted like candy on her tongue, a super sweet sticky syrup. The change was almost instant, as she sat there the baby’s cries became the beautiful music a symphony orchestra. Her rat eaten clothing a ball gown glistening in the light, she danced and laughed with handsome gentlemen around the grand ball room.
          She sat on the couch humming, her eyes half closed. She didn’t notice the front door open, Justin a man in his mid-forties, walked in carrying an arm full of supplies that he had managed to get out of people’s shopping bags.
          “Star? Starflower?” he growled realizing his words wouldn’t reach her for a while. “Fucking cunt. You couldn’t even wait an hour? Why the hell do I even let you stay here? Little bitch!” Throwing the supplies on to a side table he marched angrily down the hallway to the room with the screaming child. Tossing the door open, sneering as he looked down at the young boy padlocked into a animal crate. The room smelled of human waste and flies buzzed from pile to pile of excrement and filth. The boy stopped for a moment and stared in wonder what the man might do. “Shut your fucking hole you little twat!” With that the man slammed the door and headed back to the living room.
          “Time to earn your keep Star.” He grabbed her by the arm and pulled her out the door. Using her body to earn them the money to buy more of the latest street drug, lovingly named after the food of the Gods by those who marketed it.

          The day had gone poorly for the two junkies. Cops had made regular patrols chasing off Johns and thwarting attempts for the two to make money. Standing in their living room they fought over whose fault it was that they were low on cash and that they were out of Ambrosia. A knock echoed from the door. Pushing herself out of Justin’s grip, she tripped and stumbled her way to open it. However, there was no one there. Slamming it shut again she turned and froze. Standing next to Justin was a figure. Its body hit the ceiling forcing it to bow it’s head to fit with the low ceilings. It wore clothing that looked to be a mixture of cobwebs and fog, the face covered by a mask that was a deer’s skull that would be the pride of any hunter. With pale white hands it gestured, tracing shapes in the air with it’s nearly 4 inch long fingernails.
          “You have things you do not want.” Its voice was slow, deliberate and reminiscent of death walking over your grave. Yet the sounds seemed to come from everywhere but from the creature. “I have things that I could flaunt.
          On my own I am without,
          I believe a trade can be struck with no doubts.
          The child you possess is what I crave,
          I offer up the Nectar of Gods, the rest of your life will be nothing but a rave.”
          Laughing out loud at the rhyming beast, Justin and Starflower did not need long to choose. Enough Ambrosia to last them for the rest of their lives, they wanted it. No they needed it. Though the two answered “yes” a dozen times the beast did nothing. After several moments the creature vanished, leaving the two to wonder if what had happened, was it nothing more then a symptom of withdrawal.

          Over the course of the next week, each night at 8:00pm the creature would come with a knock at the door. Each night it would repeat its offer, however would not respond to the couples pleas for the drug.
          On the fifth night Starflower had an idea. Again the knock came, again the creature spoke it’s rhyme. And this time Starflower had her answer rehearsed.
          “The child is more trouble than it is worth,
          I hate it though I gave it birth.
          Agree to this trade we do,
          For all the Ambrosia we can use we give you the child and its cage too.”

          The creature let out a screech like a rabbit in thrall before vanishing. The house was quiet no sound of the child’s cries and on the coffee table two dose of Ambrosia sat. Starflower and Justin screamed and fought over the two pieces. Shortly there after the police arrived to investigate the domestic call a neighbor in the building had made. They found a gruesome scene. 43 year old Justin Williams, had died of an overdoes after beating his lover 15 year old Mary Anderson, street name “Starflower”, to death with the broken leg of a coffee table. Despite reports from the neighbors of the couple having a child, no proof was found. However the corpus of a mutilated rabbit was found locked up in a backroom covered in filth.
