                Here are my thoughts on pledges.

                My first impression was that plddges had gotten beaten hard and put away wet by the nerf stick.

                This was a mix of misreading it and not having the key info on goblin vows.

                The weak verison sealings can be very powerful when used right. If one keys to the right sort of promise, tricked out of the target usually, one could sabotage an assassination attempt, cock block someone, at a key moment, a small trick that could have huge implications depending upon the timing.

                If some promises never to drive on black ice, then does and takes a plenty to driving skill, you could get them killed.

                If used frivilously then yeah, its usually going to be a minor annoyance unless the target is really unluckly, but with the right timing, it could be deadly.

                Even the minor supernatural effect could be deadly, especially if your being hunted by well Hunters, and they see this tell that your reflection went weird for no reason. Possibly other things depending on how creative and generous one is with the definition of minor (I'd say minor is anything that wouldn't directly require rolling or dealing damage, or long lasting, no mechanical effect, although that doesn't mean no conquences).

                Then you have the strong sealing and most of the above is even more true for Strong Sealings naturally enough.

                But the key difference isn't the choice of lethal damage, which will only be a major issue when close to death or unconsciousness.

                No the big daddy is the ability to unleash a contract on the sealing breaker.

                Possible effects include Luna's Bedlam, Paraylyizing Shudder, the various contracts that deal or risk clarity damage, Terrible Harvest, The Fairest Effect verison of Phantom Glory, in some cases Pipes of the Beast Caller, Creeping Dread, Changeling Fate, Know the Competetion, Mask of Supierority, Read Lucidity, Murkblur, Flickering Hours, Portents and Visions, Riddle Kith, Shared Burden, Temporary Lucidity, Fairest Effect of Hidden Reality, Leaping Towards Nightfall, Thief of Reason, Tying the Knots of Fate.

                At the right time unleashing most of theses on someone can be brutal.

                But here is the twist, for a handful of them you can REWARD someone for breaking a sealing.

                Example a loved one promises to never allow themselves to come close to death by violence, accident, or disease and you seal their promise, strengthen its and here's the kicker, you tie the contract Shared Burden to it.

                They break that promise by getting cancer or AIDS and Shared Burden activates and heals them for example.

                Another example person X promises never to get into a fight, you seal the promise, strengthen it, and tie it to a use of Phantom Glory with both the Fairest and Elemental Effects and now next time they get into a fight you basically gave them a superpower, the ability to turn into the Human Tourch or Thing from the fantastic four (abit only for one fight, but still cool).

                I don't think this twist was intended.

                Bargains could be more exciting and should grant glamour to the changeling each time they are kept (with none fae or fae touched only).

                Oaths are kind of cool, could be stronger, but weirdly forever so Changeling would rarely want to give them. Also given how the court oath works, a court goodwill oath makes sense as well (instead of getting a dot of Mantle, you get a dot of Court Goodwill by pledging to be a loyal ally.)

                Goblin Vows got all the best stuff. Its a mix of nerf and buff. Only being able to make deals in reguards to the purview of the goblin vows you have narrows ones choices, but not by a alot.

                What's bigger is that you can make direct deals between people like in 1e Changeling and this doesn't change that. So you can't grant a mortal say resources ••••• anymore in exchange for services to you (you might be able to seal said mortals words to the wyrd instead of yourself, effectively granting the mortal the use of your goblin vow and grant him or her say ••• resources at most).

                But flip side of that is you can at least grant yourself magical merits and contracts now, which you could do before.

                This means you can gain alot of powerful effects from say Riddle Kith ••• contract (aka the gift that keeps on giving), a longer life or even immortality with long of days merit, a hedge beast companion, a Fae Mount, a giant hollow filled with Luxuries (Hollow size •• can be the size of a town or castle, + Luxuries is Hollow •), Dual Kith, or you could go even weirder into stuff Changelings technically qualify for like Sleepwalker Merits from Mage.

                Or you could magically enhance yourself at everything you do for +3 dice, buff your basic abilities for up to +5, or basically master a skill for +7, which while it only effects your rolls, will in practice make you a major master of that skill.

                So if you pick Crafts and gain the +7 dice to all crafts rolls, your being to be awsome at carpntery, plumping, metallurgy, Dreamweaving, Hedgespinning, contracts that require a crafts roll, ect...

                So in that sense its both narrower and tighter, but also more fantastic and powerful.

                Its also interesting that all Fae (abit Fae Touched only partially, and Fetches not at all) can use pledges. So your Incubi, Succubi, Hedge Beasts, Changelings, Huntsmen, True Fae, various hobgoblins, Cambions, and who knows what else can make pledges.

                And if they can make pledges they can do things like join your motley (well normally only loyalists would invite a Huntsmen to join their motley), join a Changeling Court, which means they can get access to goblin vows, and so on.
