        Master Aquatosic: Thanks for your interest; we always like hearing that people are reading. As far as Lori's various relationships go, it's not so much that she's braving these shark-infested waters with open eyes and a keen mind... it's more like a failure on her part to recognize or properly deal with the symptoms of her addiction. Lorelei's vice is something I originally termed an "Aphrodite Complex." Put simply, she feels an intense need to be loved (or at least admired) by just about everyone in her life. And while she does know that this is a problem she has in broad terms, she often ignores or misses the specific cases in which it applies.

        To her current thinking, yes, taking Rowan as a lover in the first place was probably a mistake. She likes Rowan, but at the moment she doubts she'll ever be able to give her the kind of attention she reserves for Maria, Marguerite, Julia, and Oleander. Rowan even told her flat-out when they stated seeing each other that she didn't want a one-night stand; she wanted something which at least had the potential to blossom into a full-blown romantic love affair, and Lori jumped into the situation anyway... because Rowan's feelings for her are intense, and that feeds the part of her that's still trying to be the prettiest bird in the cage. And now, well... she feels like she can't break things off just yet, because Rowan has just suffered a significant trauma and she doesn't need any further shocks just now.

        Her mind is a little clearer as far as Joan and Andromeda are concerned. Yes, Joan's intense passion for Lorelei feeds her addiction as well, but Lori feels like she has a safety net where Joan is concerned. Lori can stop herself from taking that affair too far by reminding herself that she doesn't want to do anything to damage J&A's relationship with each other (her virtue is Loyalty, and they're both good friends whom she really doesn't want to hurt).

        On a related note, the loyalty her many paramours show Lorelei has not gone unnoticed in the Freehold. See her next journal in a few short days for full details. :-)

                                                        Another parallel is between Lori and Calamity, primarily in their getting into relationships that they really shouldn't due to addiction. It has been noted that Lori is addicted to the love and adoration that her lovers give her, which she managed to balance between Insincere Marguerite, Julia and Maria fairly well, and with a bit of turbulence with Oleander being added in. However, that same addiction has led her to bring in Rowan, which has hit and gone over the peak of what she can handle without overt consequences, and as PenitentTom noted, she can't exactly drop Rowan without causing further damage. Likewise, Calamity though is normally satisfied with with Gawain and Becky as partners, Gawain for his gentleness and Becky for her ability to take Calamity to the extremes she desires (though that is really a gross oversimplification), her torture during her durance has led her to more...unhealthy relationships. Even when knowing that the Handmaidens are not to be given any length of rope lest they hang her, Calamity has let herself fall into their grasp before, and without the rest of the motley would have several times over. Though the Handmaidens didn't go too far, Leanne only stepped in as a medic after she nearly became Calamity's hangman, and even then she required others to completely undo the damage. Even then, Calamity has noted an urge to go back to her, even though she's aware at just how fine and sharp a line she is on.

                                                        Both Lori and Calamity's pasts have resulted in unhealthy relationships brought about by their respective addictions, Lori for love, Calamity for punishment, and both of them are supported by their motley lest the problems grow out of control. The key difference is that while they both derive pleasure from satisfying those addictions, Lori's puts a strain on her partners as they vie for her attention, albeit with Maria having some precedence, while Calamity's puts her own life on the line because she can't resist those who have the means, and lack of restraint, to kill her.

                                                        As noted, I am simplifying these aspects to varying degrees, but I love how they keep evolving over time, and how the characters are growing while making further parallels with one another. It really adds to the idea that everyone is amplifying the growth of their wyrd due to their connections to one another (and, admittedly, having a wellspring of glamour at their disposal doesn't exactly hurt).
