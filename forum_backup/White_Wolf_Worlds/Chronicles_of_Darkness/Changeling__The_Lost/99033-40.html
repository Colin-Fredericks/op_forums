                                        In the aftermath of the battles with the loyalists, the Motley faces their pasts. Also, finds out why Madame Spidertoes does not do diplomacy.

                                        Not that I am saying anything bad about Madame Spidertoes.

                                        Session LXXIV

                                        Lorelei Diary

                                        Okay, things have once more settled down a little, and while I won’t claim that everything is peachy-keen, at least we all seem to be in a better place than we were this time yesterday. We’ve gotten some closure on the matter of Hawk, we’re hopeful that things are in a stable place as far as the Maria situation is concerned, and I’ve managed to learn a bit more about my father’s mysterious secret. Oh, and we made the first real, solid step toward rescuing Missy and Jane, too… which is both exhilarating and terrifying for me.

                                        We called Madame Spidertoes just after I finished writing my last entry, and once she understood what was at stake, she arrived at the Dream House a disturbingly short time later. We told her about the situation, and asked her to intervene on D’s behalf… by speaking with the Winter Court and assuring them that Maria is not a threat to the Freehold, and that any action taken against her will invite the Madame’s most extreme displeasure. She agreed to do what she could at once, and asked if any of us would like to accompany her to speak with Boss Lowjack.

                                        I decided to go along with her, figuring I could just drive on over to the clinic afterwards for my interview with Hawk. A few of the others had the same idea, so there were a fair number of us in Lowjack’s bar to witness what happened next. I never want to get on Madame Spidertoes’ bad side. I mean, I’ve known that for a while, but I’ve never actually seen her angry before, and it makes an impression. She told Lowjack in no uncertain terms that certain things were going to come to light about Maria in the near future, and that Maria had reformed and was not a danger to the Freehold. Then she went on to suggest what a bad idea it would be for anyone to act against Maria in any way.

                                        Lowjack’s whole body was tight as he addressed Madame Spidertoes’ concerns. “I assure you, your word is good enough for me, Madame,” he said in clipped tones. “If you say that Maria is not a danger to us, then we’ll take no action against her. I’ll speak with the other monarchs about it. It’s my understanding that the only pledges in place require the Court to protect Twitch from Maria, not to harm her in any way. I’ll double-check on that, of course, and make any necessary adjustments as quickly as possible. Now, could you please send the spiders away?”

                                        I had been so focused on their confrontation that I hadn’t noticed the thousands of spiders creeping into the room from all corners. They kept their distance from most of us, but seemed to have no compunctions against sidling right up to Lowjack’s feet. After a few tense seconds, Madame Spidertoes smiled, and her little pets flowed out of the room like water. “Thank you,” she said softly. “I appreciate your cooperation in this matter.” And just like that it was over.

                                        I thanked Boss Lowjack before we left the bar, and apologized for Madame Spidertoes’ in-your-face manner during the preceding conversation. While I’m grateful for anything Gladys can do to help my sweetie out of this predicament, I hadn’t expected her to be quite so threatening during our opening salvo. I guess it’s an Autumn Court thing. Anyway, Boss Lowjack didn’t seem to hold it against me, and was certainly motivated to make sure D is kept safe from any fallout of Hawk’s debriefing, so I’ll call that one a win.

                                        As we walked back to our cars, Calamity drew me aside and offered to take my fear away again. I thanked her for the offer, but once more declined… albeit for a very different reason this time: I’m not afraid of Hawk anymore. He was dangerous when we didn’t know what he would do, when he was hiding in the shadows hoping to swoop in and spirit me off to Arcadia without alerting the others to his presence. But now? He’s sedated, confined, and under heavy guard. I have my motley all around me. I have the power here, and I’ll be damned if I’ll let him terrorize me under those conditions.

                                        Dr. Bright met us at the clinic. He asked us very politely to leave Hawk in a condition to answer more questions following our visit. He also asked if he could leave a closed-circuit camera running in the room… he didn’t insist, but I didn’t see the harm in allowing it. Maria is as safe as she can be with Spidertoes’ protection in place, so I didn’t think Hawk would say anything that would implicate us unduly. Also, despite the somewhat bloodthirsty tone of my previous entry, I no longer had any reason to kill him.

                                        My gun was still in my purse, though.

                                        The interrogation was much simpler than I thought it would be. Hawk really didn’t have much information that I didn’t already know, though in the end it was Marguerite who really salvaged the situation. Hawk glared at me disdainfully as we entered, sitting perched on the edge of his chair like a dumb-ass.

                                        “I suppose you’re wondering why I’m here,” I began.

                                        “Not at all,” he interrupted. “Your plan is obvious. You intend to trade me to Her Majesty in exchange for the other whores’ freedom.”

                                        That stopped me short, and I had to take a moment to recover. Of all the plans I had conceived to bring Missy and Jane home, that one had never occurred to me. And for good reason.

                                        “Hawk,” I said after a moment, “do you really think she would make that trade?”

                                        “Of course,” he said immediately. “I am Her Majesty’s most trusted servant. I am far more valuable to her than a couple of empty-headed twitterers.”

                                        I sighed a little at that. “Hawk… you were the help. I’m sure the Empress trusted you a great deal, but… she trusted you to guard us. We were her most treasured possessions. Do you really think she’d give us up for a glorified watchdog?”

                                        He sniffed derisively. “You don’t know what you’re talking about, whore,” he said, but he didn’t offer any further arguments. Someone clearly has a very high opinion of himself.

                                        Yes, yes, I know… kettle, thou’rt black.

                                        “Look, Hawk,” I said seriously, “there are only so many ways you can come out of this situation. The Freehold is going to try to rehabilitate you, and when they realize that they can’t, they’re going to execute you. If you can offer me something – some bit of information that might help save Missy and Jane, maybe – I’ll talk to the people in charge about changing that outcome.”

                                        He thought about that for a moment. “If you will not trade me for your friends, then I do not know what I can offer you. You know where they are within Her Majesty’s domain. I followed you into the Hedge less than an hour after you left, so very little had changed.”

                                        That brought me up short. He’d left less than an hour after me, but arrived almost a year later. This rescue mission instantly became a lot more complicated.

                                        He was still talking. “There are other guards, of course, though none as trusted or capable as myself, and none of them are allowed near the songbirds.”

                                        Marguerite cleared her throat. “Perhaps a bargain could be struck after all,” she mused. “If Monsieur Hawk returns to the Empress’ realm, he would return to his post guarding your friends, non? Perhaps he would be willing to… look the other way when we arrive to liberate them…”

                                        I thought about it for a moment, and it seemed like the best option. “What do you say, Hawk? Would you make a Pledge to allow us in and out of the Empress’ Palace? We wouldn’t ask you to help us, just refrain from stopping us. If you’ll agree to that, I’ll talk to the monarchs about releasing you.”

                                        He pondered the offer for close to a minute, but I’m pretty sure inside he was jumping at his chance to escape the Freehold with his feathers intact. “I agree,” he said at last.

                                        Marguerite crafted the Pledge, which also placed a time limit upon our rescue attempt. The Pledge is good for a decade (I’m assured that it will remain in place until the proscribed time has passed for everyone involved, which is helpful). Hawk will not try to stop us or alert the Empress’ forces to our presence when we go to rescue the girls, and in return I’ll speak to the monarchs on his behalf. We made sure to inform him that the monarchs might place their own conditions on his release (like a pledge that he’ll never return to the Freehold). I also told him that the Winter Court was probably going to ask him about the creature that attacked him in the parking lot, and that it would help his case if he was both honest and thorough in the information he provided. He was still willing to agree to our terms.

                                        We informed Dr. Bright that we were ready to leave, and made sure that he was aware of the deal we made with Hunting Hawk so that he wouldn’t die during the Winter Court’s interrogation (it turns out the caution was unnecessary; he had seen the whole thing on the closed-circuit camera).

                                        It was getting close to showtime by that point, so we headed back to the Dream House and began setting up for the evening’s entertainment. I realized after a bit of thought that I’d have to let Dad know what was going on at some point before I left to rescue Missy and Jane… it would be unforgivably cruel to disappear for God-knows-how-long again without warning him in advance. He’d expressed curiosity about the shows we put on before, and as it was a Thursday, I figured it would be safe enough to invite him over. A short phone call later and everything was set.

                                        I… hadn’t counted on Calamity’s new act. She had decided to perform an AC/DC number called “Mistress for Christmas,” and one look at her outfit was enough to firmly indicate that she was choosing to interpret the title’s first word in a very specific manner. I confess that I panicked for a second and rushed up to her when I first saw her, telling her that my Dad was in the audience. She looked sort of chagrinned and asked if I wanted her to change and do a different act, but we were only a few minutes to curtain at that point.

                                        Thinking things over for a minute, I decided to let her go through with her original act. Dad’s appearance was a last-minute change, and it wouldn’t be fair for me to pull rank as the theater’s Artistic Director and disrupt our set schedule because I’d invited him over on a whim. In the end Dad was a little rattled by Calamity’s act, but he weathered it fairly well (Gawain was good enough to distract him through the worst of it). And at least she didn’t single him out specifically with any of her innuendo. And hey… anyone could tell that she was enjoying herself out there, so I guess it all worked out for the best in the end.

                                        As for me, I debuted my own Christmas number… my own smoky rendition of the Eartha Kitt classic “Santa Baby.” I strutted out in a fairly brief Santa Claus outfit, complete with hat and red fishnets and played to a pretty warm reception from the crowd. I might have toned down the sexual aspects of the performance the teensiest bit… but hey, my nearly 80-year-old Methodist preacher father was in the audience, so sue me. It still went over very well.

                                        After I had strutted and fretted my hour upon the stage (have I used that one before? I feel like I’ve used that one before.), I schmoozed for a shorter-than-usual time and then joined Dad at his table. Consequently, I didn’t get to meet the two Night Courtiers who had come from LA to check out the Dream House and invite us to their own club… but I’ll talk more about that when and if we take them up on their offer.

                                        Oh, I should mention one other thing here. Elena has been a little down recently, due to both coping with Jacob’s revelation and struggling with questions about her own identity. I introduced her and Natasha to my Dad last night, and suggested that they talk quietly about their experiences while I worked the crowd. Dad’s always been a good listener, and I’ve found recently that he can be a very calming influence on us troubled souls.

                                        Anyway, as the evening drew to a close, Dad smiled as I sat down across from him. “You are very talented, J… Lorelei,” he said after a moment. “But then, I have always known that.”

                                        “Thank you, sir,” I said in response. “Are you enjoying the rest of the show?”

                                        He frowned slightly, but nodded. “You and your friends have achieved something remarkable here. And while I cannot claim that everything I have seen is entirely to my taste, the joy you bring to your audience is obvious.” He cleared his throat softly before continuing. “Your friend Calamity’s act was… unexpected.”

                                        “I know,” I said quickly, “she debuted it tonight. I hadn’t seen it before, and I didn’t know what she was planning when I invited you to come over. By the time I found out about it, it was too late to change the lineup. I’m sorry if it offended you.”

                                        “I was not offended,” he was quick to reassure me, “merely caught a bit off guard. She has a lot of passion.”

                                        I had to laugh at that. “That she does,” I agreed. Then I turned serious. “Reverend, could I speak with you privately for a few minutes?”

                                        “Of course, Lorelei,” he said, and took my hand as I led him out of the public areas and into an empty room.

                                        “Now, what is this all about?” he asked when we were alone. “I’ve noticed that you have been a bit distant of late… it’s why I was so glad to receive your invitation tonight. What’s been bothering you, Juliette?”

                                        I gathered my thoughts. “I’m sorry about that, Dad,” I said first. “There’s been… well, there’s been a little bit of danger in my life recently. We discovered just before last weekend that there was a Loyalist in town… that’s what we call changelings who work for the Gentry willingly. This Loyalist was someone I knew… a man we called Hunting Hawk. He worked for the Empress, and it seemed likely that he was in town to try to take me back to her. We didn’t know how closely he was watching me, so I didn’t want to draw attention to our relationship.”

                                        “I understand,” Dad nodded. “I assume this is also why Maria joined you at church last weekend. Are you safe? Is there anything I can do to help?”

                                        I smiled and took his hand. “It’s over now, Dad, though I appreciate the offer. We managed to capture Hunting Hawk yesterday, and the Freehold is deciding how to deal with the situation now. But there’s something else…”

                                        He waited patiently for me to continue.

                                        “I might have to… go away for a while,” I said hesitantly. “I might not be able to give you much warning. But I don’t want you to worry if you don’t see me for a couple of months, okay?”

                                        He frowned, and his brow creased. “You are being suspiciously vague, Juliette,” he said with disapproval.

                                        I had to chuckle. “Yes, I suppose I am. Sorry, Dad… old habits die hard. Let me start over. We learned some things from Hunting Hawk, and we managed to strike a bargain with him. I think this bargain might help us rescue Missy and Jane. It’ll be dangerous… but I have to help with the rescue when the time comes. I’m the only person in the Freehold who’s ever been to the Empress’ domain, and… I promised.”

                                        I was trying not to let my tears show by the end of this little speech, but I think he saw them anyway. He reached out and pulled me into his arms. “I understand, Juliette,” he said. “Of course you want to help your friends… it’s the right thing to do. I’m proud of you,” he added gruffly.

                                        “Thanks, Dad,” I said, “but remember that time flows differently in Arcadia. So even during a quick trip there, months or years could pass on Earth. I just… I’ll tell you before we go so that you won’t have to wonder, but I don’t want you to worry even if it seems like I’m not coming back, okay?”

                                        He nodded. “I… might be able to help you,” he said hesitantly. “I am far too old to participate in such activities myself, of course, but I have some... ‘supplies’ which might be of use to you. You are welcome to them if you think they would help.”

                                        “What sort of supplies?” I asked, confused.

                                        He shifted uneasily. “Pistols, rifles, armored clothing, ammunition… explosives,” he said after a moment.

                                        “What?” I asked, dumbfounded. “Why do you have all of that stuff, Dad?”

                                        He didn’t meet my gaze. “It’s from another time,” he said after a moment, “a chapter of my life which is long since over.”

                                        I raised an eyebrow at that. “You’re being suspiciously vague, Dad,” I commented with the hint of a grin.

                                        He gaped at me for a moment before laughing with what seemed like genuine amusement. “I suppose I am at that, Juliette. Very well. After your fetch left home, I was… lost, I suppose. Directionless. My congregation began to drift away from me, because I had lost my drive. They came to me when I was at my lowest point, and offered me something I desperately needed: they offered me a purpose.”

                                        There was something wistful in his eyes, like he was remembering good times, but there was also a sadness in his voice. I realized that I’d been hearing it all along. “They called themselves The Long Night. They told me that there were monsters in the world, and over the course of the next few weeks, they proved it to me. Monsters stalked the city streets at night. Ageless, soulless horrors feeding upon the innocent, ruling from the shadows, manipulating the world over the course of generations…”

                                        “Vampires,” I said softly. “I’ve never met one that I know of, but I’ve heard that they’re real. There’s supposed to be this club in L.A…”

                                        “Those days are far behind me now, Juliette,” he said, the sadness more pronounced now. “I took to my new life with gusto, and I was good at it. I killed many of them, and left no clues behind as to my identity. And then one night…”

                                        “What?” I prompted gently after a few seconds.

                                        “She was about your age. She was one of them, but somehow different. She was frightened… not of me, but of what she had become. She had never asked to be a vampire, you see. She had not wanted to be ‘embraced,’ as they put it. She was trying to make the best of her impossible circumstances, and she didn’t want to die. I let her hide from the Long Night in my home… but they eventually found her.”

                                        He turned away, and his shoulders slumped as if with weariness. “I lost my taste for the hunt after that. After all, if one vampire could be a good person, who is to say that there are not other good vampires out there? Who is to say that some of those I killed were not…”

                                        I took his hand and squeezed it. “It’s okay, Dad,” I said when he turned to look at me. “There’s no rulebook for dealing with this sort of thing. You thought you were protecting people at first, and when you learned more about the situation, you stopped. And for what it’s worth, speaking as someone who was kidnapped and mystically transformed against my will, I think you made the right choice.”

                                        He smiled grimly. “Thank you, Juliette,” he said softly. Then he seemed to remember something. “Oh… there is one more matter we should discuss before I take my leave. Your friends Elena and Natasha…”

                                        “Yeah,” I said, “Elena’s having a hard time dealing with the whole changeling thing, and I thought you might be able to help see her through that.”

                                        Dad shook his head. “Actually, I wanted to speak with you about Natasha. She has been living with Elena for a while now, but is unable to contribute to her host’s expenses, and this is beginning to put a strain on Elena’s finances.” He looked away for a moment. “I have offered to take her in for a while. I thought you should know.”

                                        That brought me up short. “Okay,” I said after a few seconds had passed. “I know your heart’s in the right place, Dad, and I know you want to help her. I’d never say anything against that impulse. But there are a few things you should know first.”

                                        He raised an eyebrow. “Is she… supernatural in some way?” he asked. It’s so weird that my life is at the point where that’s the first question my father thinks to ask about one of my friends.

                                        I shook my head. “Not that I know of,” I said. “But there have been people looking for her… dangerous people. She claims that they’re agents of her father’s political enemies, but privately some of us have wondered if her family might be involved in Russian organized crime. So just... be careful, okay? If anything strange happens around her, promise that you’ll give us a call so we can look into it.”

                                        He nodded. “I will. Thank you for telling me.”

                                        Then we hugged. Is it weird that a hug from my Dad is still the high point of my day… even on a day which included significant personal landmarks? I mean, it sounds horrible to say it, but when I was growing up back in Defiance I never particularly liked my father. And I always got the feeling that he didn’t particularly like me either. I mean, I knew even then that deep down we loved each other, but there’s a difference. We irritated each other; grated on each other’s nerves. And that was even before I started actively trying to embarrass him.

                                        Now… well, things between us are like they were in my earliest memories… before we lost Mom. I can’t lose that. I need to talk to Natasha before she moves in with him, to make sure that she’s alert to all threats; that she won’t let any harm befall him. I can’t let anything happen to my Dad now that we’ve finally found each other again.

                                        So in any case, we said our goodnights and I made my way up to Marguerite’s room, where she, Julia, and D were waiting for me. They helped me release some of the tension that had built up in my muscles over the course of the day (and I returned the favor, of course), and then I slept like a stone for about eight hours.

                                        Sex Breakfast the next morning was interesting… and I confess I was a little giddy about learning Dad’s secret past, and might have bragged about my badass vampire-slayer father the teensiest bit in front of my friends. It occurs to me now that Dad might not like me talking about that, since he played it so close to the chest even in front of me. I’ll have to ask the others not to bring it up around him; I don’t want to dredge up painful memories unnecessarily.

                                        I also mused that a story about someone who went around killing vampires for the greater good might make a good movie or TV show, and several of my friends gave each other meaningful looks. I can only assume that such a film or show became popular at some point during the three and a half decades I missed, but I’m sure I’ll catch up with it sooner or later.

                                        I also caught myself perching on the edge of my chair a couple of times. It felt very natural to sit that way. My friends were all good enough not to point out that this was the very behavior for which I had earlier mocked Hunting Hawk, and I’m grateful for that. Besides, Marguerite really seemed turned on by the pose, if her smoldering gazes are any indication.

                                        Luna asked to speak to us as a motley after we’d eaten our fill, and the news wasn’t good. She’d been going over the video footage from our new surveillance cameras, and had discovered that Yvette had been in the Dream House again. She’d gotten in through another mirror, of course, and in the footage she appeared to be casing the joint. At one point she approached one of Julia’s posters… one that depicted me. Though it was difficult to tell what she was doing without sound, she appeared to be assuring my image that she would “rescue” me from Marguerite’s clutches. Joy.

                                        We need to deal with this, and we need to do it soon. Marguerite is adamant that Yvette not be harmed, and I get that, though I think Calamity believes she’s being unreasonable. We need to find a way to approach her without spooking her so that we can talk to her about what she’s been doing. We might be able to convince her that Marguerite has really changed, and that we don’t need her protection. We might be able to solve this one peacefully.

                                        One idea we discussed was the possibility of asking Night Queen Skitters if we could use Lizette’s path through the Hedge to approach Yvette in Paris. She might feel more comfortable meeting with a few of us on her home turf, though Marguerite probably shouldn’t be present for that initial meeting. I like this idea a lot, for a variety of reasons… not the least of which is, y’know, that it would allow me to visit Paris!

                                        Moving on. I called Princess Jolie after we’d discussed the matter of Yvette for a while, and let her know about the deal I’d struck with Hunting Hawk. She was really supportive, and suggested that I speak with Queen Charlotte next, since she’s the current reigning monarch. Jolie didn’t think Charlotte would object too strenuously to our plan, and felt confident Lowjack would go for it as well. Augustine would be the only real hurdle we’d need to vault. A part of me is thinking that we can leverage the apology he still owes me against any objections he might raise… but on the other hand, it’s probably not a good idea to antagonize him when we need his approval. Y’know, especially when D’s position in the Freehold might still be precarious, and he’s probably still a little sore at her about that whole assault and battery thing.

                                                            Calamity as a character has a tendency to rail against and try to change the consequences of her actions and despair when she cannot. She killed her parents, but is learning powers to try to get forgiven by them. She counts down time till she ceases to operate under the consequences of her killing Mr. Grey and brings it up almost every session. She made repeated attempts to get her relationship with Insincere Marguerite where it was after betraying her over Chalcedony without actually going to Chalcedony and took time to approach it really considering her feelings. None of this makes her a bad character. Calamity is a good character and I like her. her behavior makes sense and is consistent.

                                                            Calamity damaged her relationship with Insincere Marguerite both with her part in grabbing Chalcedony and how she handled the affair after. Both permanently damaged her friendship with her Seeming-mate. Insincere Marguerite believes that if Calamity does as usual with perceived threats, she will kill or anger Yvette and then beg for forgiveness again and sulk visibly around the house until Marguerite gives in in frustration like last time, as Calamity's track record is not good with these things.

                                                            To be fair to Calamity she is correct that like many abusers, Insincere Marguerite underestimates her victim's ability to fight back. Speaking as GM, she is certainly well mentally and physically capable of hurting or killing Insincere Marguerite. Granted, to be fair to Insincere Marguerite and Lorelei, I as GM expect if Calamity confronts her, it will lead either to violence or threats of violence that will make it hard to have a peaceful resolution, because Calamity nearly always does one of those things. She is the Quietus of the group to make a Night Train comparison.

                                                            Again, none of this is a condemnation of the character. She was taken as an adolescent, was essentially trapped in an adolescent role in Arcadia, and is still somewhat trapped in an adolescent mindset. This means she irritates various other characters including Insincere Marguerite and Lorelei, the former of who is, to be fair, the easiest to irritate in the group, and NPCs like Julia who is, to be fair, easily irritated by whatever irritates Insincere Marguerite. Conflict makes things interesting, and Calamity puts the most ripples in the water of the PCs. This means she contributes well to the drama, as if everyone did the right and mature thing, the game would get dull. And that Calamity is trying to do the right thing makes her sympathetic.

                                                            But it means things like Lorelei and Insincere Marguerite not wanting her to confront Yvette makes sense too.
