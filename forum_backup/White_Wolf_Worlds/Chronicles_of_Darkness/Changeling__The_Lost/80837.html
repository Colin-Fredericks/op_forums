    GMC and Revamping contracts

    02-27-2014, 02:34 AM
    So since there's like a year or two before we get a full revamp and I have a game going up now I decided to just start making a homebrew change.

    So I started with contracts first. Specifically dreams. Which in all honest. Was stupid. No one is taking it but you live and you learn. So I changed a few things and have a different approach to it. But as to the design philosophy. Narrow contracts are cool but the problem is when you have situations like Goblin contracts dealing with dreams when you have a Contract dealing with dreams there is a problem. So when dealing with something so broad it would be better to treat each contract as a mini arcana. Granted, I think clauses should still be purchased individually but they shouldn't be limited to a five power, that's it approach. So this is a little more open ended. I also have my design considerations in the comments so it's clear why I went with what I went with and what I'm still unsure of. Still some criticism would be nice.

    Next one is probably going to be elements or spring. Since I'm going to be working on the contracts my group has.

      Dreams bugs me as it is. I've never been able to work out exactly what the limits on Forging the Dream are meant to be, or how it interacts with Oneiromancy. The way it's worded makes it sound like a Clause that flat out lets you ignore and override all Oneiromancy and just do whatever the heck you want. Not to mention the fact that Pathfinder is, as you say in the document, completely unrelated. The fourth and fifth dots don't have anything to do with dreams either, and Phantasmal Bastion is literally the only one that seems to interact with the Oneiromancy rules later in the book. It's always struck me as the most poorly designed Contract in Changeling, which is really frustrating. Dreams are fantastic, but the Contracts of Dream don't really want to have anything to do with them.

      While I don't really agree with your design philosophy (I like Contracts being rigid, defined, and inflexible with a regimented order in the way you purchase them, because I feel it suits the nature of rules-based Fae magic), I far prefer a lot of what you've done to the original Contract. I do think I'd just remove Forging the Dream entirely, bump Dream Stride to a two dot power, and make the one dot power a Pathfinder-equivalent, but instead of facts about the Hedge it gives you facts about the dream/person you are in. This is mostly because Dream Stride appears a little good for a one dot Clause, and (as mentioned) because I really don't like Forging the Dream. Other than that, I quite like what you have there.
