    Twilight Gleaners and Sensing Fate's Strands

    01-07-2015, 11:27 AM
    So I know that Changeling is always dealing with Fate in one way or another, kinda the name of the game, but usually it's in very reasonably "hard rules" ways (get a bonus here or there; roll twice, take the better result; redo that last turn cause you knew what was going to happen, etc). All easily managed and really without a lot of storyteller input needed (I like it better when the rules handle the rules baggage and I can focus more on other things).

    One of my players wants to play a Twilight Gleaner. And honestly, their privilege is a bit daunting. Not because it's a super powerful ability, or it will derail any carefully constructed plans or anything. But because I honestly have no idea how to decide if a particular plan is "fated" to succeed or not. I feel like if I go in favor of "what I think will happen over the course of the chronicle" it's simply going to be "+1 for everyone!" because I very rarely (if ever) plan for the PCs to fail. Sure I'll set up obstacles, but my players are usually smart and often have good ideas with a fairly good success rate. Its the dice that muck it up.

    And I feel like the ability loses it's pizazz if it simply reads "spend a glamour to give people +1 to stuff".

    So how do I decide when a plan is "fated" or not? (Without, of course, resorting to anything so simple and random as rolling dice. I don't mind thinking, I just want some directional input)

    Thanks.

              (I'm the same way, I have to admit.)

              Well, I mean "come up with a general idea of what the NPCs would do if the players weren't around."

              Something like one or two lines for each major NPC, along with a loose arc.

              If the players completely negate a particular "supposed to be" (they reconcile two sworn enemies, they kill a particular major NPC, whatever), just revise it after the session.

              Basically, don't think of it as a script for the chronicle; think of it like another step when writing up a situation.

              Example Situation - A Midwinter Party
              A few Winter Courtiers on "Winter Break" have decided to throw a party on Midwinter. With some help from the PCs (and a few Spring Courtiers), everyone who is anyone at the freehold will be there.
              Fated Outcome: NPC 1 and NPC 2 will drink too much, get into a fight, and challenge each-other to a Hedge duel in two days time; NPC 3 and NPC 4 end up going home together, while NPC 5 embarrasses themselves. The party otherwise goes off without a hitch.

              In this case, goading NPCs 1 and 2 to fight each-other would get the bonus, while trying to pick up NPC 4 would suffer the penalty. Attempts to ruin the party would take the penalty as well.

              It might seem like some extra work on your part, but I'm pretty sure it will become automatic after a while (you probably know how your NPCs would react, in general, to each-other.)

              Edit: To summarize:

              • Write 1 ~ 2 lines for each Major NPC spelling out a general idea of their fate over the course of the chronicle. Revise this whenever they become completely inaccurate.
              • Write a simple outline of what would happen in a situation if the PCs didn't intervene.
              • Ad-lib from there.

                      You don't need a plotted course for Twilight Gleaners to work. All you really need to do is keep in mind it tells you whether or not a PLAN is fated to succeed, not whether or not a GOAL is fated to be accomplished.

                      Say your players want a token currently held by an ogre known for his physical might. You might intend for them to get the token, that is to say to succeed at the goal, but that doesn't mean that every possible plan is fated to succeed.

                      If the motley's plan is to have their sneaking darkling start a fight and take the token after she beats up the ogre? Well that plan is just plain destined to fail.

                      On the other hand if their plan is for said darkling to carefully pick-pocket the ogre while he's drunk? Well that sounds like a solid plan favored by fate.

                      Basically you need to always be clear as to the actual plan being tested. As ST you should have a good idea of which side has the better dice pools and other factors that are likely to intercede. Based on this knowledge you can predict whether or not a given plan is likely to succeed or fail and declare fate's intention accordingly.

                      This power can "see" things the players don't. For example if their planning a raid on a loyalist camp at dawn but you know there will be one of the Gentry there, well that raid isn't fated to succeed even if the PC's think they'll hold the upper hand. As such it can be a good tool for cautious players to probe possible failures in their missions.


                      It is true that once the PC's have a Twilight Gleaner amongst their number they'll likely have that +1 for every major action they take for the rest of the chronicle but that isn't necessarily a bad thing any more that an ogre using Stone 1 every single fight is a bad thing. It's a tool at their disposal that helps differentiate the good plans from the bad.
