    Fanmade Goblin Fruit Index

    11-24-2013, 01:39 AM
    So I want to compile a list of fanmade goblin fruits for use in games.

    Can you add any Goblin Fruit you have made or have seen anyone make onto this list? I would be interested, even if they are variations of some the general Goblin Fruits like the kind that heal you or give you Glamour, it would nice just to have a name and description of them that can just be picked up and used for any game.

      Whisper Mint -

      Whisper Mint is a silverish elongated leaf growing in the whispering woods of the Hedge around Seattle. Changelings of that region that share a leaf of Whisper Mint by brewing it into a cup of tea are said to be able to share their thoughts without ever speaking.
      The whispering woods derive their name from the soft hushing noise that the leaves produce when somebody walks by.

          Kaleidoscopic Lotus: Usually found in the deepest parts of the Hedge, near to the tenebrous borders with the Nocturnal Garden of Ten Thousand Wonders, this fruit strongly resembles a glowing flower made out of colored glass. Brave gourmet changelings describe its flavor as "a thin lollipop-sheet", but there is no general consensus whether the colour of the fruit is, in some way, linked to its taste. Eating a Kaleidoscopic lotus is a dangerous proposition: its petals are brittle and sharp, and often end up cutting the tongue and the esophagus, making difficult to feel its flavor among the abundance of blood (1 lethal). Afterwards, it follows a deep state of waking trance: until the next dawn, the changeling see shades of colors not of this world, giving it +3 to all perception rolls and the ability to see in complete darkness.
          Plants of kaleidoscopic lotus are to be grown in complete darkness, and feed with broken dreams.

          Oraclange: feared for its powers, this fruit seems just a common orange, but the interior of its thick peeling is covered with bizarre looking runes. To activate an oraclange, a changeling must whisper a question, and concentrate on it (roll Intelligence + Composure), then eating the interior. Slowly, the writings on the peeling will start to make sense, in the form of a cryptic answer to the question (if you don't have time to create the answer, use the Common Sense Merit). There's a catch, anyway: for the following scene, the changeling becomes foul-mouthed, spitting more insults and blasphemy than actual words. (-3 to most Presence and Manipulation rolls)
          Plants of Oraclange are to be feed with petty secrets.

              Here's three I wrote a while ago for Dream Catchers (so Fiar's Lantern should be read in a Hunters Context)

              Friar’s lantern
              This fruit resembles a light yellow, soggy, furry walnut and can usually be found floating in stagnant water where the hedge resembles a swamp. Friar’s lantern constantly emits a strange luminescent gas making it very easy to spot, but this is something of a trick for the fruit grows just deep enough into the thorns that it’s nearly impossible to reach without some sort of specialised tool. Many travellers have lost their way, and their lives, attempting to pick a lantern. Eating a friar’s lantern gives you a nice buzz. It feels like the smoke is rising from your tummy, through your throat and curling around your brain with a mild and pleasant tickling sensation. While under the influence mortals gain the ability to navigate the hedge, as well as suffer a one die penalty on all mental attributes and initiative. This lasts for four hours, so several may be needed on a long trip. When eaten by a faerie (or if you give mortals the ability to navigate the hedge) a friar’s lantern gives a +1 bonus to navigation rolls.

              Lover’s remorse
              A beautiful flower looks like nothing more than a red rose crafted out of icing sugar and is found only in the most scenic parts of the hedge, legend has it that lover’s remorse grows in the spot where lovers have lain together. Do not be fooled by the lack of thorns, a single bite of lover’s remorse shatters the petals into sharp shards that cut the mouth and the throat all the way down. The name comes from the plant’s medicinal properties, lover’s remorse is one of the most powerful abortives known to man or fae; a single bite can terminate a pregnancy at any point up to the final moment before birth with no harm to the mother. It is prized by faerie courtiers who make potions (just boil lover’s remorse in water, the result is easily disguised as ordinary sugar water) to ensure their rivals never have an heir, less commonly it is used by doctors to treat malign pregnancies that are immune to more common methods. Even the most powerful “chestburster textquotedblright can usually be harmlessly destroyed with a single bite of lover’s remorse.


              Stooge
              This fruit looks more like a warm brown apple pie, though attempting to move it will quickly collapse the pie into a doughy mush. Eating stooge, which tastes like dough mixed with overcooked apples, grants an enormous resistance to all manner of deception and any magic that directly influences the mind or emotions. On all such rolls the 8-again rule applies. However on any other rolls that use a mental attribute the influence of stooge removes 10-again and causes 1s to cancel out successes. If the roll turns up more ones than successes, the result is a dramatic failure. Stooge lasts for one scene before the effect wears off

                  An Oddment I came up with for a contest a bit back:

                  Snaptwig

                  These oddments are found on strange, incredibly tough trees and bushes in the Hedge. In order to activate the Snaptwig, it must be broken into two equal pieces. Either half of the twig may be broken fairly easily, and this piece will crumble quietly into rotted splinters. When this is done, the other piece immediately snaps in half with a loud, ringing crack and a puff of acrid yet brightly-colored smoke. This happens even if one piece is in the Hedge while the other is in the mortal world, but both pieces immediately go inert if either piece is taken into Faerie.

                      Just came up with this one off the top of my head.

                      Timbregerine

                      It looks normal enough, sure. A big, juicy orange, fresh from the orchard. One bite, though, and the Timbregerine makes its purpose known. For a short time after you eat the flesh of the Timbregerine, you can see sound. You can see where it comes from, and where it is going. Tone quality, intensity, all of it, drifting in front of you, in lieu of actually hearing it. Mechanically, give the partaker the 8 Again on all Perception Rolls involving sounds. Impose a -1 on all rolls involving sight, as the drifting colors obscure the unfortunate Lost's vision.

                                          Sherlockian Shambler - the Sherlockian Shambler can be found all over the Hedge, but seems to have a preference for the hollows and hedge-ways of the U.S. state of Georgia. It is found on vines that completely cover large boulders, with baby blue fruits grown sporadically throughout the length of each vine. Beware grabbing the fruit for that will cause the Shambler vines to attack and strangle the target, thus causing a murder scene that no human or fae will understand.

                                          Eating a Sherlockian Shambler gives a static +3 bonus to any and all Investigation rolls for a scene. A second one can be eaten to increase the bonus to +5, or +6 if the ST lives on the wild side, but this comes with the drawback of the character talking with a British accent for the rest of scene as well. The characters unintelligible limey drivel is not limited to one region of Great Britain, and in fact could be any accent the player or ST can think. Alternatively, you can roll on the chart below to see which accent you get. Due to how ridiculous you sound, as well as your new found knowledge of English idioms, a -3 will be placed on all social rolls for the remainder of the scene. The only ones this does not apply to is other people who have eaten 2 Sherlockian Shamblers. The negative modifier even applies to actual English people, as the character has become so top-hattingly British even they can't understand him.

                                          Vines roll 5 dice for grapple
                                          Vines roll 5 dice for strangle

                                          +3 on all Investigation roll for a scene
                                          +5(6) on all Investigation rolls for a scene
                                          -3 on all social rolls for a scene

                                          1-2 Cockney
                                          3-4 Queens English
                                          5-6 Scottish
                                          7-8 Irish
                                          9-10 Scouse

                                          But that would be *shudders* fun!

                                              These are some we made up for a game I played in, Perchance to Dream.
                                              Hedge Fruit

                                              • Clustersnap Berries look like tiny grapes clustered and with their skins fused together. They have to be carefully handled otherwise one berry will snap, thereby creating a chain reaction where the whole cluster pops. The prime ingredient in Sparkling Clustersnap, Clustersnap Berries are unique to New England, and make for a delightful beverage that is mildly intoxicating and that feels somewhat fizzy without being carbonated. Drinking an entire bottle of Sparkling Clustersnap will get one totally plastered, and causes a bitch of a hangover, but also restores all lost willpower.
                                              • Midnight's Repose is a delicate black flower that blooms in the Hedge every new moon. The actual flower is useless, as it is the leaves of the Midnight's Repose that are eaten which give the subject the rote quality on all Manipulation and Subterfuge rolls for the rest of the hour. Found only in Boston, Repose is something of a rare find around the Beacon Hill area, with only a harvest of five or six flowers every lunar month. The leaves can be dried and turned into a powder, soluble in water, which has the same effect.
                                              • Mandranthine is a large red eggplant like Goblin Fruit identical to the equally rare Amaranthine except for purplish striations through the meat of the fruit. When eaten, Mandranthine is a powerful prophetic agent, sending the Changeling into a deep sleep in which he has prophetic dream after prophetic dream. It is also a powerful poison, eating away at the dreamers future with every vision granted. In addition to its insights, each prophetic dream inflicts one level of aggravated damage. If the dreamer is awakened before death, the dreams and the damage both stop, but the subject must roll one die for each level of aggravated damage he has taken. A success on this roll indicates that he will die within one month.
                                              • Thorn Slugs are tiny slugs that live off various Hedge Fruits known to have healing properties, sometimes considered a pest and sometimes delicacies. The slime of a thorn slug is thick and rich, and easily hardens into large sharp crystals that can be harvested. When used in the production of liquor, these can make a thick substance that has to be cut with a knife to separate. With some salt, it can be thinned further until it is drinkable. Some Knights of the Tongue use them for gravies, as well, though their secrets are heavily guarded. The slugs themselves are also considered a tasty treat, and heal one bashing damage when eaten raw.
                                              Seasonal Fruit

                                              • Winterwilly appears as a small reddish plum, covered in frost, with a delightfully minty flavor. When eaten, Winterwilly makes the Changeling completely comfortable in even the most horrible cold for the next eight hours. This can allow her to go skinny dipping in Winter Seas, walk naked through the snow, or otherwise take activities that would normally be most uncomfortable or swiftly lethal. Winterwilly can be made into a tasty jam that retains its special ability, and only grows in Winter.
                                              Oddments

                                              • Pocketful are shiny polished stones, always found under some amount of fast moving water within the hedge. Both beautiful and useful, Pocketful are called such because you're supposed to keep them in your Pocket, and are particularly sought after by particular members of all four Seasonal Courts. If kept in ones pocket when a Changeling uses Cupid's Eye, Baleful Sense, Witches' Intuition or The Dragon Knows a Changeling ignores any Resistance trait that would normally be applied to the Clause (or adds +3 dice, for Baleful Sense) and gains the benefit of the 8-again rule on the Contract Activation roll. After being used once, one stone melts away into wet mud. If you have any pocketful, you'll automatically use it when you activate one of the above contracts.
                                              • Hell's Bells appear like tiny silvery bells, dangling from certain flowering hedge vines. If very carefully cut away from the vine itself, and then stored properly for five minutes or so, the Bell itself hardens and the sap from the "wound" solidifies into the Bell's plunger. While lacking in any overt magic, the tinkling of these little bells is commonly associated with Gentry that are hunting in the Hedge around New England. A Changeling who strings three or four such bells to his clothes, mimicking these hunters of the Gentry, might thus scare off various threats within the Hedge. Of course, the downside is hopefully obvious.

                                              ​When noise turns to silence, when colors dull and pale, when reality no longer makes sense, there shall you find me. There, in the dreams of the River of Faceless Millions, do I dwell.

                                                      Blue Razzberries: One of the most unnerving Hedge-fruits to modern Changelings, blue razzberries are exactly what they sound like: fruits that look like unusually large, bright blue raspberries and taste exactly like the "blue raspberry" flavor of various candies. Eating a blue razzberry will immediately rob a Changeling of two points of Glamour (if the Changeling has one or zero Glamour left, they will suffer levels of bashing damage which will not begin to heal until the Changeling regains Glamour) and causes a -2 penalty to all contract and token activation rolls for the rest of the scene. Most Changelings who learn of the existence of blue razzberries will be very suspicious of the mortal candies that bear their flavor, and some say that such candies are in fact made with the juice of these Hedge-fruits, circulated among the mortal population to drain them of their emotional energy and make them susceptible to Fae or...other influence. (The truth of such rumors is up to your GM, I hope someone has fun with this one )
                                                      (A Changeling who, for whatever reason, chose to grow Blue Razzberries would find that they require a sacrifice of a memory to grow)

                                                      Juju: Juju Trees grow deep in the Hedge, along the trods that some say are closer to Arcadia than the mortal world. Rather than forming flowers that grow into fruit as most mortal trees do, the bone-white Juju Tree forms a seed at the end of a long, straight, leafless branch and excretes a sweet, sticky coating that hardens over the seed over the course of several weeks until the branch resembles a large lollipop-the "juju." Putting the sweet mass of sap and plant matter into one's mouth has the following effects:
                                                      -First, the Changeling won't want to spit it out. Left to their own devices, someone sucking a juju will keep rolling it around in their jaw for about 24 hours, at which time the sweet shell around the seed will have fully melted. The juju seed itself has a bitter flavor that will shock anyone into spitting it out by reflex; this is how Juju Trees spread. Anyone attempting to spit out a Juju before it is completely spent must spend a point of Willpower and succeed on a Resolve+Composure roll.
                                                      -While sucking the Juju, the character feels unexpectedly energetic and has an uncomfortably sunny demeanor; all their problems seem illusory to them, and they're certain that if their compatriots would just stop being so glum and stop worrying they would be just as at peace with the world as the character themselves. They gain a +1 bonus to all physical rolls and and a +2 bonus to social rolls while sucking the Juju, although the social bonus may become a penalty in certain situations (if the character has to appear at a Winter Court social function while high on Juju and is unable to appear sufficiently solemn, or perhaps talking to a particularly bitter and angry Hobgoblin who is repulsed by people smiling...although in the latter case, one could potentially gain the bonus to an Intimidation roll as the character's extreme cheeriness unnerves the Hobgoblin) Additionally, the character will suffer no penalties for exhaustion or deprivation for the 24 hours that the Juju remains, and no wound penalties. Sleeping with Juju in the system is impossible, and although one could theoretically roll the sugary mass to one side of the mouth to chew other food (including other Goblin Fruits) most will not think to eat or drink if left to their own devices.
                                                      -Juju plays hell with a Changeling's perception of reality, perhaps bringing out a bit of the True Fae in them. First, a Changeling sucking a Juju delays all Clarity rolls for sins commited during the 24-hour duration until the moment the Juju is spent and spat out, similar to how a mortal will delay morality rolls for sins committed in the Hedge. Furthermore, if a Changeling sucking Juju sees an act that could damage Clarity as the path of least resistance towards their goal, that is the path they are compelled to take. Suppose that Changeling is expected to watch over a mortal and keep them safe for a few hours: they could use a few Contracts and a bit of mundane stealth to keep an eye on said mortal, but that takes so much work. It's so much easier to just crack them over the head and hide them in the Hollow until everything cools down. Besides, when he wakes up he'll freak out and that'll be hilarious, they might even be able to get some Glamour out of it. Resisting such base urges requires a Willpower roll. (I'm sure someone here will know what this was inspired by)
                                                      (Deliberately transplanting a Juju Tree requires an offering of blood, and incurs a -3 penalty to the Wits+Crafts roll; Juju is wild by nature, and even the most attentive and thoughtful cultivator will find that the plant accepts their aid grudgingly at best)

                                                          Golubaya Roza: This Goblin Fruit is extremely rare, and extremely valued. It's a small rose-like plant with no thorns and five blue petals on its head, which seem to almost glow in the dark. It always grows in the shade of another much thornier plant, and is always found in the darker parts of the Hedge along with a small number of roaming hedge beasts. They possess no seeds, and how they germinate is a complete mystery, so foraging for them is very difficult despite the fact that they predictably grow in the most dangerous parts of the Hedge. But, the effects are often considered completely worth the risk of foraging for these fragile flora.

                                                          By removing the head of the plant, and using all five of its blue petals to brew tea, the changeling or other fae creature can restore their youth by two to three years depending on the level of their wyrd (two years for wyrd level 1-5, three for wyrd levels of 6-10). Or, for those with the Merit of Age Reversal they may actually gain two to three years to their age. The effect is permanent (or as permanent as a person's age is anyway) and even scars and crippling effects can be effectively healed if the damage done was within the two-to-three-year timespan which the Golubaya Roza restores (this won't, however, regrow lost limbs and such other missing parts). The tea brewed from its petals tastes different every time it is drunk, and creates a burning sensation all the way down the throat that spreads throughout the body creating a warm, light-headed, and slightly intoxicating effect as it rejuvenates the fae creature--the entire process takes about an hour.

                                                          *inspired by the Blue Rose from the myth of Baba Yaga*
