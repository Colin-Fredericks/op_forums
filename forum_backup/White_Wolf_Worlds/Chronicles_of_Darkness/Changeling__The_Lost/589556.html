                              The Hedge, yeah... That's a lot in terms of a 'friendly discussion' sort of thing. I'll try to keep my comments on point, but I have some thoughts that are less-than-productive when I apply my mind to what I've seen about Second Edition. Sorry in advance if I sound like a tosser.

                              My biggest joy about using the Hedge in a game is also my biggest worry. That is, how I describe it to players always feels right on the edge of saying too much and not saying enough. I don't want to ever say, 'Remember the movie Labyrinth? Yeah, that's it...' BUT I also want to give my players a real feel for what the Hedge is like. The 'psychoactive' properties of the Hedge make it possible—at least at MY table—for the Hedge to look, feel, smell, generally present itself in slightly different ways to different observers. I try to give broad-strokes descriptions of various Hedge locales and allow players to fill in the blanks. But then there's the whole notion of the Hedge reflecting the 'real world' around it.

                              Our first chronicle was set in New York City, and I wanted to allow the Hedge to reflect some part of the Big Apple. I never quite felt like I'd done that effectively, no matter what the players in our troupe thought. So, does the Manhattan skyline appear above the Hedge in Central Park? Can the plucky Darkling from Jersey hear traffic from the Lower East Side when she's stalking a hobgoblin? Does the stink of garbage or the perfume of cherry blossoms make it through the Thorns? My take on all of those was to lean on that same 'psychoactive' nature of the Hedge as justification to just let it do what-the-hell-ever I needed for the scene.

                              Maybe that's what the Hedge is there for, I dunno. It's not a PLACE really, but it feels like a place. The Hedge isn't alive and sentient, but it reacts to moods and desires, even going so far as to trick the unwary sod who enters its borders. It's not a part of Faerie, but the Others can survive there and command the Thorns in ways changelings cannot do. It's all kinds of possible.

                              My reason for posting here is that I wonder if anyone else uses the Hedge like that. I remember thinking long and hard about the whole notion of the Hedge from the first book for Changeling: the Lost that I saw—Autumn Nightmares, if you need to know—and it straight up preyed on my mind for weeks. When I actually read it, I was delighted to see how the Changeling core book—and subsequent books—dealt with the Hedge by offering direction without mandating onerous mechanics. All I'll say here about my hopes for Second Edition is that I do not want to lose that freedom to make the Hedge whatever works in my game. I mean, I KNOW that I'll keep doing that, but I want others to feel like they can do too!

                                              I've always looked at the Hedge as a Pseudo-Dream Subspace. Where eternal emotions and thoughts of dreams merge and combine with a mortal realm foundation. Always twisting and changing for the "Dreamer" or "Traveler" yet keeping some general elements to itself. Hardest part for my group is always wondering how it looks each time we go in.

                                              I considered the entrance to each "Door" would reflect the Hedge area you pass through. A semblance of the mortal area you leave before you go in. (Ex: Going through a garden will lead to a bizarre luxurious garden, Diving into a pool after you come up, to see a beach full of bright white sand that sparkle like bits of diamond over a alien sky or turning down a city alley would lead you to a desolate metropolis or a peculiar shantytown.) Different area's to pass through, different dangers to face and even if you go through the same gate, doesn't mean the dangers will be the same.

                                                          Hardest thing? Hmmm. One of the issues that I've come across with using the hedge is more of a reflection on the Storyteller.
