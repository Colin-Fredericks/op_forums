            I have developed Alice Liddell's back story a little bit. I am hoping people can give some advice and thoughts on how to incorporate it with my idea for the fairest seeming.

            On Jul 4, 1862, Charles Dodgson (who wrote under the pen name, Lewis Caroll) went on a boat ride with the three Liddell sisters, Edith, Alice, and Lorina. While Reverend Robinson Duckworth rowed the boat, Alice, the middle sister, began telling Dodgson an entertaining story. She told Dodgson and her sisters how she had followed the white rabbit down the rabbit hole, drank a potion that made her smaller than a mouse, than ate a piece of cake that made her bigger than a house, and nearly drowned in her own tears. Deeply amused, Dodgson went home, thinking of the imagination of children. That night, he had strange dreams of Alice being the maid servant of the White Rabbit, growing to the size of a house, and Alice asking the caterpillar for advice. The next night, Dodgson dreamed of the Cheshire cat with his mad half-moon smile, a baby turning into a big, and Alice chasing after the march hare. The fourth night brought the mad tea party, time stuck eternally at 6 o’clock, and Alice escaping through riddles.

            Dodgson found himself being driven mad by these images; of the queen of Hearts lopping off heads, roses painted red with the blood of her victims, of Alice becoming the queen’s chatelain. He first tried to deal with the visions by writing them down. Eventually, this drove Dodgson to a sexual obsession with Alice and asked her father for her hand in marriage. The family responded by cutting all ties with Dodgson. This did not stop the obsession or visions as Dodgson dreamed of Alice escaping through the looking glass only to end up in the land behind the looking glass and the war between the Black and White Queen. Eventually, Dodgson took the pen name, Lewis Caroll, published his visions/dreams under the duology Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland/Through the Looking Glass. For the Rest of his life, Charles Dodgson would find himself obsessing over his creative angel/muse.

            Twelve years after her fetch revealed what was happening to her in Arcadia, the real Alice escaped the Hedge and found herself back in London. No longer the naïve and innocent young girl who had followed the white rabbit down the hole, into the land of hearts, and bondage under the Queen of Hearts. She was a hardened warrior who had first escaped through the looking glass and fought in the war between the Black Kingdom and White Kingdom, who fought her way to freedom. Although, you would expect her to join the Summer court, she instead went to the Antlered/Spring court and offered her services. Thanks to the fame brought to her through Lewis Caroll,s writing, she rose very quickly through the Spring court, first joining the Satrapy of Pearls and then joining the Eldritch order, The Charmed Circle. As her fetch acted as a muse for Charles Dodgson/Lewis Caroll, Alice tries to inspire artistic creativity in London’s young folk. In present day, she is one of the Antlered Court’s greatest influencers of art and writing in London.

            Storytelling hints:

            Alice is still a women of the Victorian era and is almost always unfailingly polite. Noted exception is listed below.
            Minor frailty:
            1. Minor compulsion: Curiosity. The first frailty she developed, Alice’s curiosity still gets her in trouble. Whenever something catches her attention, she drops whatever she is doing and chases after it. Ironically, this has led her to being one Britain’s leading experts on the region’s hedge. She also knows a great deal about London’s secret’s lost history, and forgotten places.
            2. Minor Compulsion: Riddles. Alice is compelled to tell riddles. If she genuinely wants to help the motley/characters, she will often give information in the form of a riddle. Her help can also be compelled by solving one of the riddles.
            3. Minor bane: Pepper. Alice suffers bashing damage from pepper. It also causes her to sneeze uncontrollably. This can by used to stop Alice without harming her.
            4. Minor Compulsion: 6 o’clock. At six o’clock, Alice is compelled to stop what she is doing and have tea time. While not dangerous in and of itself, depending on the time and situation, it can put the characters in a great deal of danger. You can break Alice out of this compulsion by solving a riddle.

            Major Frailty

            1: Major Compulsion, Lunacy. On the night of a full moon, Alice’s clarity drops below five and she is essentially one of the made.What makes this especially frightening is that her madness affects the physical world, warping physical bodies, minds, and thinning the barrier to the hedge.She can summon hob goblins, create affects associate with the hedge, and even use hedge spinning.

                I am currently designing Edgar Allen Poe as a high wyrd monarch of the Autumn court and would like to hear some suggestions. Here is what I have for his back story, so far.

                Edgar Allen Poe was a struggling writer when his wife, Virginia Eliza Clemm Poe, died of tuberculosis. Her death drove him to despair, and on the night of her funeral, he refused to look at her corpse, so he would remember her living. Drinking himself into a stupor that night, that was when The Raven came to him. The Raven told him that he could find his Anna Belle Lee, in the Kingdom by the Sea, but first must travel through the Dark forests, and Underworld, and then enter the land of dreams and stranger things. And so Poe followed The Raven, not just through the hedge, but also the underworld, filled with ghosts and geists, and avoiding the Kerberoi and Cthonians. And finally, he came to The Under Dark, The underworld of Arcadia. While there, he slew the Tell-Tale-Heart, helped the black cat escape the murder trying to bury them both alive, escaped the Pendulum, escaped the huntsmen/murder of Rue Morgue, discovered the true identity of the Red Death trapped one of the Underdark’s Gentry in his own catacombs, before coming to the Kingdom by the Sea.

                The Kingdom by the Sea is one of the darkest and blackest seas of Arcadia, for it is formed from the tears of broken-hearted lovers, tears of the plague dead whose bodies were wracked by the agonies of the Black Death, the tears of orphans who cry for the parents that abandoned them, and tears of soldiers dying on the battle filed as they call for absent family members. It is hear that The King of Dreams rules, and it was he who had taken Edgar Allen Poe’s wife, Virginia. To rescue her, Poe had to confront and overcome his internal fears; first his fear of dying in poverty, second his fear of being a failure as a husband, and finally his GREATEST FEAR, which the raven represented, his fear of LIFE. Overcoming these obstacles, he demanded that The King of Dreams release both he and Eleanor. Yet the King of Dreams was selfish and greedy and moved to prevent Poe and his wife from escaping. However, through his trials and adventures of The Underworld, Arcadia, and The Under Dark, Poe had become a Darkling of the Author Kith. Using his powers of talecraft, Poe wove frailties into The King of Dreams mien, which he used to not only defeat The King, but also write him out of existence.

                When Edgar Allen Poe and Virginia Eliza Clemm Poe, returned to their home, they found Edgar’s Fetch with his physician, Dr. John Joseph Moran. Upon seeing him, his fetch cried out, “GOD HELP MY SOUL!!” and moved to use his echoes. But the challenges of The King of Dreams, Poe had come to understand himself, and was able to fuse with his fetch. Poe was able to convince Dr. Moran, who happened to be a fae-touched of Poe’s, to keep a secret of what had happened and help him fake his death. For Fae was determined to move beyond the tragedies and poverty of his past and start a new life and identity with Virginia. Poe spend the next 50 years traveling through North America (Though he did take a hiatus fighting in the American Civil War) meeting with Indian tribes, tribes of Uratha, Vampires, covens of mages, Krewes of Sin Eaters, and even had a chance to meet Mr. Verney, the founder of the Frankenstein Lineage.
                In 1901, he settled down in Richmond, Virginia joining its Ashen court and becoming it’s King.

                Storytelling Hints

                He has fought in the American civil war and both world wars, so he is as skilled a warrior as he is a witch king.

                He has traveled through the underworld as extensively as the hedge, so he is one of the Autumn Courts leading experts on the Underworld, ghosts, spirits, even the Kerboi.

                He is famous, not just among changelings, but also vampires, mages, and Sin Eaters. Vampires tell stories of him being invited court of owls, mages claim that he has climbed the ladder and had polite conversations with the Exarchs, Sin Eaters claim he has met with the Lords of the Dead. Which is true, he is not saying.

                                    Here is my idea for Mordred, as a wyrd 10 Changeling and King of Winter in the British Isles.

                                    While there are many retellings of the death and aftermath of Arthur and Mordred, here is its true telling. As Mordred lay dying, the King of the Tuatha De Danann, Nuada of the Silverhand, spirited him away to Arcadia, for he believed that Mordred could be the tool with which the Bristish Isles were returned to the Tuatha De Danann and made a part of Arcadia. Mordred was transformed into a terrible creature with the strength of an ogre, yet who flows like shadow around mundane and magical defenses. He was also trained in fighting styles that were ancient by the time the Tuatha De Danann came to Ireland. He was to be the champion who would drag not only Ireland, BUT ALL of the Isles; England, Scotland, and Wales into Arcadia. Yet before he had been a rival for the crown of England, he had been a hunter dedicated to the protection of mortals against the forces of darkness. It was the vows that he had taken as a member of the Order of the Black Knight, that allowed him to break the magical compulsions and controls the Tuatha De Danann had on his mind, and fight his way out of their hollowed hills and back into Britannia.

                                    When Mordred returned, the seasonal courts were in disarray from the death of King Arthur and the fall of Camelot. Hundreds of former subjects of Camelot were being dragged into slavery in Arcadia. Mordred came to the halls of the Silent arrow and declared himself its new King. He knew that neither Spring nor Summer would accept him, for they blamed Mordred for King Arthur’s death and the fall of Camelot. Yet Mordred was determined to do good and protect the people of Britannia, whether Changeling or mortal. He made pacts and alliances with Morgana Le Fey and Nimue, two of the oldests and most powerful members of the Autumn court. For the next hundred years, Autumn and Winter would be the most powerful seasons in Britannia. The Ashen court would use their secrets of the occult and knowledge of the magics and laws of Arcadia and the Hedge to keep the True Fae and their armies at bay. The Silent arrow fought like a wounded and hungry wolf during winter. They launched guerrilla attacks against loyalists, racketeers, and even vampires of the Legion of Green and their Green Knights. Eventually, The Spring court grew brave and hopeful enough to be joy and revelry, while the Summer court rallied and slew many of the terrible beasts of the Hedge, including the Questing Beast, which foretold Arthur’s death. It was the servants of the True Fae’s to feel fear and sorrow. Stability was returned to Britannia and the Seasonal courts.

                                    In this way, Mordred became one of the unsung heroes of the British Isles.

                                        Here are some story telling hints about Mordred.

                                        Thanks to the works of writers like Sir Thomas Malory, Mordred is reviled as a traitor, spoiled brat, and criminal by most changelings and mortals. Despite this, Mordred has spent that last 1,500 years protecting the British Isles from not just the True Fae, but also Vampires, Pure tribe werewolves, evil mages, and ghosts.

                                        Mordred has three frailties that greatly affect his action.

                                        His first minor frailty compels him to protect the British Isles as long as he lives.
                                        His mien is so monstrous that mortals that gaze upon it suffer lunacy.
                                        Changelings that gaze on his mien suffer disquiet.

                                        To protect against this, Mordred wears the armor of the Order of the Black Knight.
