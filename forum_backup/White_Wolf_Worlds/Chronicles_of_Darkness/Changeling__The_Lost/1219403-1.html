    [Many information below applies to my own "world shard", called Autre Monde - I mark these parts with the (AM) to avoid misunderstandings]

    Living in Paris
    No matter how beautiful city Paris is, its called Trou Noir (English: Black Hole) in the Changeling communities of Gaul (Belgium-France-Luxembourg-Southern Netherlands) for a reason.

    Approximately 12 million people lives in the greater metropolitan area, six times denser than France - generating many problems and calamities.
    Mortals - Too many crime syndicates operating in the capital (the Albanian, Corsican, Italian and Russian mafia, the urban gangs composed from second-third generation descendants of Maghrebi and post-colonial immigrants, various occasional or permanent local gangs and some outlaw motorcycle clubs). Their counterpart is the many racially biased, trigger-happy, corrupted or simply exhausted and tense cops and gendarmes. Supplemented by politically active or just simply dissatisfied population ready to be manipulated, enraged and misguided in any given moment.
    Magi (AM) - Thanks to the active participation in mortal affairs in the past, after three or four internal conflicts, the Consilium of Paris is heavily divided and not "The" just "one of" the great Consiliums of France, since the early years of the 20th century. However it is still a great community hub and many Awakened loitering in around the city.
    Kindred (AM) - Nearly a hundred Vampires residing in the greater area, playing the Danse Macabre. The city is governed mostly by the Carthian Movement, under the nominal suzerainty and occasional power demonstrations of a benevolent (uncaring) Prince (with a choleric laissez-faire attitude - let them do as long as there is order).
    Uratha (AM) - Two major Forsaken packs in Bois de Boulogne and Bois de Vincennes, one Pure pack from the North-East, several smaller packs swinging back-and-forth between them in a perpetual fight with both the Spirits and each other.
    Hunters (AM) - One local, government backed Conspiracy is firmly active in the capital, the DLSH - Directorate for Liberation and Security of Humanity (Direction de la libération et la sécurité de l'humanité) but fortunately (not for them) they are heavily infiltrated and misguided by many supernatural factions. Occasionally supplemented or obstructed by an another, military backed Conspiracy, the RSSCMS – Special Security Regiment Against Supernatural Threat (Régiment de sécurité spéciale contre la menace surnaturelle), unofficial name: Les Vautours - "Vultures".
    [I'm in love with the long and overly complicated naming practice of French organizations. I think it's funny. ]
    So it's an "ideal" environment for more than two dozens of individual Cells to operate.
    Bound (AM) - Three large cemeteries are guarded by Krewes: Montmartre, Montparnasse and Père Lachaise. They are more than happy to prove any meddling supernatural that death is the ultimate power of the Universe. Fortunately they are very busy and absolutely territorial - no pestering, no retaliation.
    Arisen and Created (AM) - Lurking and wandering below the city, meaning big trouble on occasion but not a constant threat.
    Begotten and Unchained (AM) - No mainstream players. They are there for sure… or not… or whatever…

    Changeling concerns - Long story short: this is a shit-hole what can not be ruled, conquered or governed. Four permanent and very strong Trods (and our thoughts and prayers for nobody ever opens the Grande Arche de la Défense to the Hedge), near a thousand smaller but equally important gates (She resembles more to a Swiss cheese than to a proper city in this case), a "Fairy Tale" castle with a world famous garden and the thousand times cursed Hall of Mirrors. A nightmare-inducing Necropolis. An important Mortal traveling hub and many-many tourists - ideal for Loyalists and Privateers. So… a nightmare.
    Oh, but She looks very good!

        Living in the Freehold - the Court
        The courtiers' lifestyle has been developed by the regulations and customs of the Clock Court and the goals and interests of the Founder.

        Clock Court
        (the Flowing Sand, the Both-Enders, the Court of Remorse)
        The Court of the measured time revolves around the losses of the Changeling experience and the obligation of living the life purposefully. The main philosophy of the Court is that a day lived without doing something meaningful is a day wasted completely. Tide and time wait for no man - the courtiers of the Flowing Sand living an overly active, agile and most often exhausting and short life. In one way or another they are spiritually connected to the Seasonal Courts and sympathize with them - they have the desire and drive to pursue their wants and dreams, enjoy the day and every single moment for its fullest and to take every opportunities given; they have the stubborn determination to take up the glove and teach the ageless Fae an important lesson - all good things must come to an end and time spares no one; they know that time brings all things to light and they are ready to use every small dust of knowledge against their former keepers; they have the patience to sway back one step and hide in the shadows, reducing their presence to the point where they are just the faint, ticking sound of the impending doom, waiting for the right moment - because all good things comes to those who wait.

        The Clock Court is about the passing and inevitable disappearance of the one person. The inconceivable and steadily decreasing time that has been given to all living thing. Each heartbeat is an unrepeatable ticking of one's own unique clock until the very last moment. The main driving emotion of the Court is remorse and guilt - the personal guilt for all the wasted time they've missed. They were resting when they've had to act, argued when they've should listen, remained in silence when they should have said it out loud, rushed when they've supposed to stand… The remorse for all the time and life unappreciated before the caption, lost during the durance and unreachable after the escape. The guilt for all the remembered and unknown comrades left behind or lost during the long way home.

        The symbolism and spiritual bonds of the Clock are strongly tied to the numbers of 12 and 24.
        The number 12 is associated with the ominous card in Major Arcana of the Tarot - The Hanged Man. This card represents both sacrifice and the inner conflict of the person. Both the number and the card are associated with the astrological sign of Pisces, symbolized by two fish swimming in opposing directions. Fae Mien and Mortal Mask, good and bad, past and future, bad decisions and lost opportunities resulting a constant inner turmoil of guilt and remorse, without enough time to waste on mourning.
        In the I Ching, the 12th kua is translated as "obstruction" or "stagnation" - the inner conflicts of the Changelings most often resulting that they will not be effective enough in the outer world. The courtiers of the Flowing Sand accepting and understanding the guilt and embracing the feeling, using it as a constant reminder of all of the "what ifs" and the values of the present, transforming it to the true creative potential, symbolized by 3, the root number of 12. The Hanged Man represents their sacrifice both of their past life and a possible peaceful life, they are pragmatically and sometimes opportunistically sacrificing their moments of emotional weakness, their need for a normal life, the riches for its own good on the duty and the obligation of doing something for the greater good every day.

        The 24th kua of the I Ching is the "return" and "the turning point", the thunder in the bowels of the earth. The escape from Arcadia taught the courtiers that they can survive. Their Durance served to build character and they have learned that they can persevere as long as proper action is taken in the proper time. They weren't lucky or powerful enough to avoid the Gentry at first time, but coming back from Faerie indicates true strength of character. Their self-control and steady course, their willingness to abandon the herd instincts of the weak may lead to some lonely moments, but leads to great success with the help of the like-minded comrades. They are honest with themselves and more than often brutally honest with others as well because when the turning point arrives, only the open-minded and noble at heart could find the proper course of action. They are rejecting blind obstinacy and always ready to adapt to the circumstances, but if the golden opportunity has been missed, the courtiers accepting it humbly and always trying to learn from their mistakes.
        In the Tarot, 2 is the introverted High Priestess and 4 is the materialistic Emperor. The root number of 24 is 6 - The Lovers, indicative of the need to make a decision. The courtiers of the Clock don't mind if they are misunderstood or loathed for their ways and decisions as long as they have the support of their inner circle. They act and do everything to gain for the community and build the proper heartland and support. They know that they work for the good of others, because living for your own benefit only is a life wasted after all - but they don't have the spare time for explanations and moral debates.

                Clock Court (part 2)

                Courtiers
                Almost every Changeling who joined the Clock Court has lost more than her freedom - a beloved family member died while she was away, her Fetch ruined her most important relationship irreversibly, she was pregnant when abducted and lost her child. In other cases the following circumstances make it impossible to regain their former lives - the talented and emerging DJ with his first label contract returns to that his Fetch both had borrowed serious amounts of money from the Russian mafia and ridiculously died in a banal staircase accident in the same year; the infamous international con artist realizes that she had so many false identities and pretended lives that she doesn't know anymore who she really is, above all of these her brother, the only relative she remembered has been died two years ago and buried under the name of Nelo Selim Zanatti (Ne želim znati is Croatian for "I don't wanna know"); the eugenicist, abducted not much after the Great War is angered by the fact that her male colleagues had stole all of her research materials and essays and published them under their own names, and equally horrified by the fact what it has been used to by the Nazis and relieved by the fact that at least her name wasn't dishonored.

                The ideal member of the Court is pragmatic, opportunistic and quite often bitterly cynical. The main question to them is not "why me" and "what happened to me" but "what is it good for" and "how can I turn it against you". The time is now, this is here, happened what happened - what's next? They are determined and stubborn, usually well-intentioned extremists or simply just arrogant pricks. Their behavior is often in parallel with the Bound: life is short, we will all gonna die eventually, one way or another and they are both terrified and relieved by this fact at the same time. They are usually urged to make a meaningful impact, a lasting mark with their new abilities and possibilities and becoming somebody who is really larger than life. They want to be missed when they are gone at all costs - maybe that's why they have an unstoppable, burning hatred for Fetches.

                The members of the Court are called "both-enders" for a reason, since they can not return to their previous lives anymore for different reasons, many of them living multifaceted new lives. Store clerk at the morning, baby-sitting in the evening, hacker at the night. They not just burning the candle in the both sides but in the middle as well. Most courtiers are rightfully accused with social negligence because seemingly they don't value human relationships anymore and always ready to sacrifice them when it became more a burden than joy. In fact they value them more honestly than ever - they evaluate everything and everyone instinctively, have no taste for white lies and developed a no-nonsense attitude. Everyone in their life got the attention and effort only for what they worth and if somebody want to go - let them go. Time well spent, hopefully.

                Pledging to the Clock Court is not an easy task. It takes time - pardon me for the pun. At first the potential member swears an oath on his own blood in a short (but equipment demanding on the Court's side) ceremony and accepts that his time as an Initiate may be completely erased from his mind if he fails. Then he spends a meaningful day with all of the whole-hour members of the Court and runs through different riddles, physical tasks, errands and other evaluations, based on the member's taste. The time period of the process varies because one can do an extraordinary performance worth 24 hours in just 3 or he can work for a week to reach the goal. Meaningful is a rather ambiguous term after all. When the "days" have passed each and every member votes with a definite "yes" or "no" and based on the consensus of the majority the Initiate is accepted or his memory rewinds completely. The Court leaves the fallen Initiate at the doorsteps of an another Freehold, welcome him as a newcomer with poker face and guide him away from the troubles of the city or simply toss him to the Hedge - based on his behavior during the tests.
