    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post




    Because let's be honest, the Strangers aren't actually that close. Not at all.

    Here's what you've been waiting for-the point. It's about how the themes inform the game, the atmosphere, and the reality of the threat.

    Returning to the abuse point, Changeling is about the abuser being behind bars, or being in another state. You've escaped, you've gotten away, they don't know where you are, they can't touch you. You should know this. You did it.
    While I greatly enjoyed your essay, I do have to say this reasoning ( and the opinion that the game is more optimistic, it does have optimism, but canonically it's admittedly on an individual level- even then it tends to be rather fleeting)does make me raise an eyebrow in bemusement due to it being the least accurate part of your explanation ( to me at least). One of the biggest sources of dread in the game is the fact that you really AREN'T far from your abuser, and they have myriad ways to get to you any time, anywhere despite the distance between you. Now whether or not they WILL come for you again is another matter entirely, but a big part of the game is that your character knows he will never truly be safe; there is always the chance that the Cousins will simply decide that they want you back, and in all honesty unless you arm yourself to the teeth with cold iron and hand forged iron weaponry, spend year upon years studying Pledgecraft, or do everything in your power to try to be as mundane as possible.. there is very little that any one individual ( or hell even a court) can do to stop the Others if they are determined.

    Not only that, do not forget that every Lost who does escape Arcadia does so because their master allows them to. This might seem to be a bit incredulous to consider at first, but it is the truth. The True Fae have absolute power over their slaves, this is a fact. Their power over their slaves is so great that the Laws of Hospitality do not apply to a kept changeling, only the servant ( or free Lost) who visits the Demense, This fact alone nearly crushes any hope of a changeling escaping simply through their own power; it's almost, nay, virtually impossible should their master not wish them to leave. But... They could not feed off of the strife and conflict generated, so naturally they allow a slave to escape now and then- it is the rebellious servant that is the most cherished after all. When you consider that, it's not hard to also consider that a True Fae would also keep tabs on that "escaped" changeling, looking for a new way to create a story from the resultant conflict between it and said slave. The vast majority of the Lost do not know this of course, and for better or worse it does allow them to have hope, however false that hope is.

    So to reiterate, I disagree with your view that one is more or less "free" just because they have escaped, because in reality that haven't escaped at all- they were simply let go. Now you demonstrated that you do recognize it as a possible danger, but I feel like you down played it far too much in your explanation. Other than that, I really liked everything else that you said, good work!

        The short hand response.

        1) Maybe, but that doesn't stop the fact that a lot of the game is still in the changeling's head.
        2) Compared to the God-Machine,who is literally in your backyard(Don't look at it, it'll provoke it!), the Strangers are at least metaphorically a county away.

        The long response:

        1) Well, let's go through the list. The True Fae stay in Arcadia a lot of time, namely because the most rewarding Legends they can enact are done against other True Fae. The Hedge itself is a maze they still have to navigate as much as anyone else. They can't stay in Ironside for long before they start gagging on it. They're capricious and most of the time not singularly focused on any one escapee. If they think the fight is too much, they'd rather just grab someone new to fill in the situation. A working Court system hides those involved with the freehold somewhat, and even if it doesn't that entire grouping together makes a decent fight.

        Yes, the Strangers are scary mofos, and there are a lot of ways to grab their attention and show yourself to them, and that will bring them running. And they do make regular runs to Ironside, so the chances aren't bad. And yes, they want to get you back. But there's a lot of reasons why the True Fae just aren't the big worry in Changeling-they really aren't likely to show up personally, being reserved for Big Boss appearances in games. Now that said, they are big bosses and therefore really hard to stop, but still, they don't personally show up all that often.

        Now their presence is extensive, from hearing about deals made with Arcadia in the Goblin Markets to running into loyalists. There's a threat to be returned at any point. and the Keepers are always heard of. Their presence is inescapable, and that's a large part of why they feel like they'll come and get you at any time. Add in the memory of how they just swooped in the first time to take them, and the atmosphere, both external and internal, is dripping with the fear of the Keepers, and you will deal with enemies that always show how their fingers are in your life....but it's mostly just the fingers.

        The danger is real, don't get me wrong. The Keepers can come at any moment. That is true. But that's also part of the point: the Keepers can come at any moment. It's not a given they will.

        2) Changelings escape under a lot of terms. Some are let go. But some exploit a frailty or clause. Some are given a legitimate chance. Some are literally so brutal that it'shard to say the Keeper is the only storm in the Domain.

        The idea that changeling are only ever released on the Keeper's inscrutable terms is nonsense, it defies the stories it draws on where people really do beat the Fae. Now, changelings worrying and thinking that their victory was actually handed to them? That's very in theme with what Changeling is exploring and dealing with. Again, they are immensely powerful-how could you ever really do so.

        Changeling is a game of perception. Clarity is the morality stat for a reason. Just because the other guy is big doesn't mean you can't win. How you see it has a lot to do with what you'll do.

        That's what the stories are telling you.

        3)Yes, True Fae are essentially living worlds, but once again the way they get away with that is they willingly take on frailties. By definition, their power comes with catches that must be exploitable.

        Again, the True Fae are powerful, they are the big bosses in this game. But they really aren't omni-present or infallible. They are a lot like high powered changelings, at the end of the day-tough, but not insurmountable.

        There comes a day, in recovery, when you realize that the one who tormented you was flesh and blood just like you,and was just as susceptible to torment as you were. Changeling has a lot of that.

If a Fae were to use their full power normally, there would be no conflict to speak of and they would be absorbed back into the Wyrd having lost their identity. The Dragon can only use as much power as he does because of the sliver of iron in his heart. He is doomed to die, perhaps quite soon, and no matter how much power he calls upon he cannot change that. No rule is broken.

                                The above rule is also why a Changeling always has a chance of escaping from Arcadia under their own steam. There has to be a chance, and not one so small as to be just about impossible. Without a real, maybe unlikely, but still a real, tangible threat for the Fae to work against if they want to continue their existence. Some Changelings even speak of destroying their Keepers on the way out, and the rules of Faerie mean that it's entirely possible that they're speaking the truth. Maybe a Fae limited itself by not putting perfect security around the Prop that could kill it. Maybe the entire Realm was set to be destroyed should any of its residents feel true love towards one another. There are a myriad of frailties and weaknesses that could exist to bring an end to one of the Fae even at its strongest. No Fae is going to make such an event a likely occurrence, but the Changelings who escape are the lucky few.

                                Not to say that a Changeling always destroys their Keeper on the way out or anything. One great thing about Changeling is the sheer scope of possible backgrounds each character could have.
