    Originally posted by Arref View Post

    To orient the Seemings to the idea of Agency, I wanted some poetic 'price' and 'advantage' in each description (not unlike a Contract, as it were). And yet we want to retain flexibility to access a whole lot of Kiths. So again, flexibility being interpretation of the 'price vs advantage'.     So with the Fairest, imagine they are sucked into the Glory of the Fairytale Story. They warp close to Fae themselves, beautiful, uncanny, manipulative, and they carry away this veneer with them when they escape. The Fairest have to pull themselves back from becoming collaborators and believing it all. They fooled the Keepers, but they now resemble moreso the thing they were fighting. "My Manipulation will set me free."

    So with the Wizened, imagine they obsessed on the expertise that was giving them power, gained frightening skills, but became twisted in pursuit of that. They lost parts of themselves as they chased the secrets. They learned more than the Keepers could guess, pushing themselves into a love of arcane skills and craft that change them but allow fantastic ability. "My Craft will set me free."
    Hmm, interesting. Your explanation for Fairest certainly works. Wizened... I do admit that, yes, their talent would probably be magical, so I'm guessing I'm thinking of their expertise still in a much more mundane sense. Hmm.

    But also, isn't the Price part, somewhat, now covered by a Kith? To be fair, a Seeming probably shouldn't be all roses, so there's that. (That, and I subscribe to the viewpoint that becoming a Seeming meant you had to change yourself physically, not just mentally or magically.)

            And now, my attempt to get Gristlegrinder for every Seeming. While, of course, this is all rather mutable, and will probably be completely changed with time, I think this is an interesting thought exercise to go along with how we now think of the Seemings.

            The Beast Gristlegrinder is like the Big Bad Wolf, always entrancing more to their beds to be devoured. Their smile holds wicked sharp teeth, and their bodies have the irresistible sensuality of a beast.

            The Darkling Gristlegrinder is the monster under the bed or in the closet, waiting to swallow up children the minute their parents leave. Their form is like the shadows that dance around one's bed at night, hard to define, and seeming to warp with need.

            The Elemental Gristlegrinder is like quicksand, swallowing up those foolish enough to step in its grasp. They almost seem a little sandy whenever they're around, or appear.

            The Fairest Gristlegrinder is the caring one, waiting to offer treats to those who'll have a stay in her home for the night. Once her victims are asleep, she prepares to dump them into a pot to boil and gorge on.

            The Ogre Gristlegrinder is ferocious, willing to savage opponents with teeth and fists, before devouring their remains. Their teeth are immense, all the better to rip and tear with.

            The Wizened Gristlegrinder is an amazing chef, always knowing what their guests need for sustenance. However, don't ever ask them where the best ingredients come from...

                For the fun of it, how about a Kith that's usually never thought of with anything BUT darklings; Gravewights?

                A Beast would be those who take the superstitions about animals to the extreme; a black cat is a harbringer of death, the crows and ravens act as a messenger between worlds, and an owl is able to see the lingering souls in the world, and perhaps sense when new ones are to form. Acting as psychopomps and instinctive guides, these companions do their best to move the dead on. Some may then attempt to keep the living from interacting with the dead, while others might take their duty as harbringer of death seriously, choosing who among the living should be moved on with little more than a whim as guidance.

                A Darkling has severed their proper ties to the living in their mind, instead taking a grim comfort in consorting with the dead. Slowly losing touch with the living, the comfortably predictable patterns of earthly spectres seems to comfort them in a way, a reminder of how they've managed to escape the world of the ever-changing Fae. This separation, of course, has a cost, making it difficult for them amongst humankind, and they all too often find themselves mirroring the haunting games of spectres and shades in order to keep their solitude.

                An Elemental is as much flesh as ephemera, a being half formed of the ectoplasmic essence that makes up the lingering dead. While a Darkling consorts with the dead, an elemental becomes them, not purposely withdrawing so much as simply finding their minds forming an entirely different sort of thought process than any human would. They find it difficult to do more than follow and watch their loved ones at a distance, unsure how to connect firsthand.

                A Fairest commands an otherworldly allure, pale skin and blackened hair somehow enhancing them instead of detracting. The shades flock to them like moths to the swamp light, and their reach extends beyond the realm of the living to networks of undead informants and allies, working to spread word of their beauty and worth. That's not to say that the deceased are their only followers; their eerie grace and exotic form of beauty seems to captivate the living just as easily, putting a shiver down mortal spines while they long for the courage to get closer.

                An Ogre finds themselves as brutal protectors of the sanctity of death, stalking graveyards to punish those that might vandalize graves or uproot bodies. The spectres act as their eyes and ears, guiding the fury of one who knows all too well what it's like to have one's body taken and mutilated with no care for proper respect. No small number of tales of creatures guarding graveyards are actually about the Ogre guarding them, rather than the dead themselves.

                A Wizened is a necromancer and wisdom keeper, performing small favors for the deceased to learn of their secrets, or perhaps to get the chance to study one of these ghosts directly. Blunt and to the point, they rarely see the shades they study as deserving much more respect than an odd bit of flora or fauna, a little cold to them even when they work to assist them. Rumors spread of those that even go so far as to warp the still-aware spectres into odd shapes and forms, creating otherworldly artifacts fueled by the essence of the dead.

                    So when it comes to "Seemings as how you escaped", what I want to know is this:

                    Say you have a changeling who was taken to be the perfect lover for a True Fae. At first it was grand, yadda yadda, but then it wasn't so great, and the changeling wanted to leave, but they couldn't for as long as they could love, so they cut their heart out and replaced it with ice.

                    Would this Changeling be an Elemental (icy/inhuman heart), a Darkling (sacrificing a very human part of themselves) or a Fairest (since the focus is so heavily on interpersonal relations/love specifically?)

                    An Artist is taken to be the official poet of a Fae court, and escapes by weaving a bridge from his Keeper's hair -- they're obviously a Wizened, but what about someone taken to be a soldier who "died" to escape, setting down their weapon before an enemy who was innocent but still dangerous, and letting them cut the soldier through? Darkling (losing their life), Ogre (letting themselves be killed violently by someone who was "good" in comparison), Fairest (again, interpersonal relations / focus on purity)

                    What about the Hunterheart who literally hunted their Keeper down to their lair and killed them? Ogre (escaping through violence), Beast (it is natural for a hunter to hunt, after all), or Darkling (for the fear caused by the Hunterheart's pursuit?)

                    I think "seeming as agency" is confusing the issue a little. . . but then again, I may be overthinking a little.

                            From the pen of my player Isabella, who does not have an account here yet:
                            It all sounds cool but I'm seeing a few issues. The first is there will always be some conceptual overlaps: If a changeling shoves their keeper off a cliff on an impulse, are they a beast or an ogre?

                            The second is this format by necessity locks out certain character concepts, which I tend to hate. for instance, if your standard faithful butler decides to poison their True Fae master, it seems like they're going to end up a darkling instead of a wizened, and you can't be an wizened without changing your backstory - I hate this. It could be fine if it weren't for contracts being tied to Seeming.

                            I also do not know if I like the idea that someone "let go" has no Seeming, just because of the mechanics aspect - likewise, it seems like Loyalitsts are essentially going to be screwed mechanics-wise, if they're not careful.

                            This system may also lead to some weird idiosyncrasies, where a changeling who simply snuck out isn't able to be a Darkling. Not necessarily a gamebreaker, but kind of weird. Another character might wind up out in the cold without a lot of contrived explanation (there's no real "agency" behind "my Keeper got bored", and there's no real Seeming for "I broke out of the abuse victim mindset and left", unless you claim it counts under ogre).

                            There's also a minor silly issue in that the "No Seeming" merit means you're "more human", which would be directly at odds with the idea that the changeling never escaped on their own.
