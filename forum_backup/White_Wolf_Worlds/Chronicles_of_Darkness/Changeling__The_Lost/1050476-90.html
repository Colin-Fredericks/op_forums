            The book doesn't specify, but I believe the service a changeling can perform should be on par with the task a mortal has to complete. Mortals also have the option to lose something important (which, imo, should also be available for changelings) while changelings also have the list of ways the ST can spend Goblin Debt to mess with them.

            When it comes to the actual negotiation, you could either have the goblin be vague about an unspecified future favour, or have them be more like "now that I've given you this, it's time to discuss your payment" but unfortunately the lack of immediacy for Goblin Debt does create a disconnect between the concept of being indebted to weird supernatural creatures/forces and the pressure you'd expect to feel while indebted to them.
            In short, hobgoblins who makes trades are basically loansharks who are more interested in putting people into debt than actually collecting that debt. Given the lack of drawbacks (especially for mortals, but there are multiple Merits for changelings to get rid of it for free, allowing their players to just say no to the ST if it's too inconvenient), just having Goblin Debt is largely trivial as long as you don't rack up too much of it.

            One way to make payment actually feel important (like how it works in most of the source material) is to just skip the middle step and have the hobgoblin request the payment for Goblin Debt immediately as part of the transaction, meaning you technically gain the point of Goblin Debt and resolve it instantly.

                Originally posted by Neos01 View Post
                Just to be sure, if a human makes a deal with an hobgoblin, and he exchanges a chocolate bar for a potion of sweet dreams, he ALSO accrues a point of Goblin debt?
                Yup. The Goblin Debt isn't the payment so much as the representation of the goblin (or its backer) having a foot in the door — remember, none of these deals are one-and-done unless you actually need a single temporary fix. There's a reason Market Sense's effect is "you can reduce your Goblin Debt by one" and not "you can ignore one point of Goblin Debt."

                There's a story in Hunter's Horror Recognition Guide, "The Market," that makes a pretty solid distinction about why you can't pay for things with regular money at a Goblin Market:
                "The chipped mug, how much? I held 10 bucks out as I asked.
                “What? What for?”
                “These.” I waved the bills, feeling too conspicuous for my own good.
                “Pah! No. They’ve got no worth, only value. You want something with worth, you offer something with worth. What use do I have for value?”
                I backed away before I caused more offense.
                The people walking the floor all had something in their eyes, a burning need. After a while, I saw the pattern. They each fixated on one thing. Maybe it was a piece of trash or a bite to eat. A few wanted in to the strange, enclosed tents on the outskirts. One lusted after something I couldn’t quite see to start with, but when I looked, I saw a man’s arm, wrapped in old newspaper. Everyone there had something they needed. Watching for longer, I could see nobody got what they went in for. People traded for things, then traded them away, but the market’s not running a fair game. Like a casino, the house always keeps its cut. Apparently, I’ve become part of the market.
                "But how can that work? I mean, without money, how do they profit?"
                "You’re still new. They’re not in it for profit. They’ve got a need, same as everyone else. They need our desperation, same as I need another lung. That little sinking feeling in your gut when you realize they’ve got you over a barrel? That’s what they’re really after."
                I see other people walking around and they trade as best they can, but they don’t understand their situation — they’re working toward an ever-changing goal. It’s not like I could haggle on a price for my marriage then work my way back along the chain. Value isn’t a constant here, so there’s no way of mapping out a path to your goal.
                The Market’s prices, the acceptable trades, vary according to need. If you really need something, you need to trade more for it. If it’s just another step on your journey of transaction, then you get a better deal than if it’s your end goal. It’s the Market making sure it always has plenty of need to feast upon.
