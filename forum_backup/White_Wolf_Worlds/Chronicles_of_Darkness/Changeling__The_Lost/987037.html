    Advantage of Hedgespun Raiment

    10-13-2016, 09:43 PM
    So, this is a question I've had since 1e changeling, and going into 2e I figure it's something I'd like to figure out. Hedgespun Raiment, at it's most basic (not enhanced with extra defense or what have you), is basically clothes, armor, etc. With changelings, it grants a +2 equipment bonus to social interactions - which is cool, but also something you could do with normal clothes. Armor that is hedgespun is vulnerable to iron. It's also very notably armor.

    So what's the advantage of spending a merit dot for hedgespun armor? If your armor is just going to show up as flak armor or football gear, why not just get flak armor? I mean, I know if you take extra dots being that initial payment you can make it clothes + or armor +, for bonuses to init/speed/defense, but from where I'm standing it seems like it'd be safer to have normal flak armor (as that doesn't have the weakness of iron), and oddly enough less likely to draw the eyes of someone than if your unlucky enough that your fae plate mail looks like football gear. Am I missing something?

                  In the last game I played(1E), we had a house rule that all hedgespun automatically possessed the same stats as it's mundane equivalent. This encouraged players to craft interesting gear via hedgespinning or use their Resources to purchase cool "0-dot" stuff from one of the freehold's artisans, rather than simply crafting/purchasing normal equipment. If a player spent xp on the Hedgespun merit, they could then add a bonus per dot. Our house rule was very similar to the rules written for 2E, except that the hedgespun possessed normal stats without spending a dot on the merit. You could adopt a similar house rule if you think that dropping xp on hedgespun raiment seems rather pointless.

                          That's true, and I understand if the first dot of the merit is intended to be the "price of admission" for hedgespun, but it just seems kinda useless to me. According to the rules as written so far, one dot hedgespun(such as a blade of moonlight or a dress of snowflakes) only gains the ability to exist. That's it. Most of the players in my last game agreed that it seemed rather foolish to dump xp on equipment that was only cosmetically magical rather than simply purchase the mundane equivalent with their Resources merit(compared to other merits that grant a tangible benefit). We really liked the storytelling potential of 'lings running around with knives of frozen flame or caps knitted out of children's fears(even if they granted no mechanical benefits), so we agreed to make the rules for hedgespun less restrictive and it led to some pretty cool stories. I'm just saying that compared to other merits, I can see Jakondite's point. Perhaps the benefit of Gossamer Raiment could extend to all hedgespun, so that a one dot hedgespun sword cobbled from nightmares would be treated as a normal blade during combat, but give a bonus to Intimidation rolls against other fae. Otherwise, the one dot version of any hedgespun just seems rather pointless.

                                      I also understand that we have only seen scenes from the "rough cut" of the 2E rules so far, but I would really like a clear line to be drawn between tokens and hedgespun. I'm not sure if they are meant to be two totally different things or not, but my understanding was that all hedgespun would be items crafted by changelings that would be "always on" and would not require activation(and thus have no need for a catch). I found the whole hedgespun/token rules of 1E fairly confusing(and hard to explain to players), so I wish they would be firmly two different things in 2E.
