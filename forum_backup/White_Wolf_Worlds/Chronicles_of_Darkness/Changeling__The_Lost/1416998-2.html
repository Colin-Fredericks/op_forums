    Originally posted by BetterWorldz View Post
    Good news everyone! Looks like we actually had some sort of official stance on the issue all along, you may remember this here interview from a few years back. In it, Meghan Fitzgerald does confirm that she and Rose Bailey made the deliberate decision to let changelings (and fetches) freely have babies in 2e, and that the intention was to make them “special”, in a way she hoped to develop in future books. Now whether you agree with that approach or not, at least we’re past twisting the text like a bretzel to try and make sense of what the devs were aiming for.
    You know, I was going ignore this thread, but this little bit here bothers me, so I feel it should be laid out:

    That Meghan and Rose have mentioned that this is a subject they want to develop further in future books has no bearing on the fact that you're raising a fuss over it not being a subject of interest in the books released since the Changeling 2E core release, and it has no bearing on what's going to be more the priority for the two books that have released since then. Kith & Kin, in particular, has been a book that's been in development since even before the current Changeling 2E Core was finished, and it's prioritzation was always geared towards what was going to be more important for players. That, in all that time, we ended up with a product that doesn't have details on pregnancy and special changeling babies should answer the question to begin with. It's also rude to imply that since Meghan and Rose said it, they can't then change their mind, or otherwise have to be held for it even in scenarios in the future where it's not really a viable course of action, such as Paradox simply saying no. I dislike the overblowing of this "find", is what I'm saying.

    Also, since we're here.

    More power to you guys. Your particular PCs are one thing, but as STs we’re expected to portray a realistic world where not every NPC is an asexual monk, and hate it all you want but humans are supremely sexual beings. Now add the spring court to the mix and tell me with a straight face sexuality is unheard of in CtL.
    Speaking as one of those players and one of the people who strongly and personally suggested that the BPG should have Horrorspawn in it, I have to say that your understanding of the issue is really sorely lacking. I personally enjoy games that do have romance, sex and sexuality(One of my favorite characters, who contributed towards my transition, had sex as one of their preferred stress relievers, and had a character arc that involved her reconciling her bisexuality and homoromanticism with her previously ironclad understanding of self), and even pregnancy, and this is something shared by the players in our group-but not all games need that, not all groups are comfortable with that. For Satchel's group in particular, it didn't fit into our characters when we started-two of our characters are children, and the other two have got their own problems that would be distracted from if we also worried about their love life. It just doesn't fit our themes and interests for this game.

    Spring Court may has sexuality as a part of it's core of desire, but to bring that up as though that's the whole of the point is it's own form of immaturity. Positing that games need to include or feature sex to be realized is immature in the same-and-equal vein of thinking that all games must be sexless.

    Chronicles has grown up with it's audience, and I think it's really cool that they have, across various products, been willing to discuss and work in themes and stories of parenthood into it-but most gamelines that aren't about that to begin with usually aren't going to have it be a hot topic must-play thing. It's a cool thing to have, but it's not a deal breaker.

    It's also not a hard thing to homebrew in the meantime. Honestly, I get more out of the parent-child relationship if they child is just human, both positive and negative-but between the Fae Touched Template, easy application of 1e and 2e material, and now Heirlooms, I can just make implement Wyrd babies if I run a game that needs it.

    I also have to ask how this material is important to any game you are running or about to run, because if this is just general interest, the sheer personal thrashing on the subject seems excessive, and given the initial post seemed excited by eugenically-interested freeholds and how this interest in children has slowly translated into a more sex-oriented concern, I'm not gonna lie, it's also started at creepy and is steadily getting worse. I trust that's not the impression you want to actually give, so some context in how this actually matters to your table might help.

    But yeah, otherwise, like, yes, I too am excited to see if and how Changeling handles the topic-hell, I would enjoy writing it. Do I need it now? No, not really. Do I think most players need it? No, not really. I, and I imagine the majority of the player base, will be pleasantly surprised when(/if) it comes up, and but in the meantime, we're not exactly stopped by it's lack.
