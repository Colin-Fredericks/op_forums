        Originally posted by MachineIV View Post

        I'm not entirely in disagreement here. I'm gonna give it some thought. But that's probably getting chopped.
        Oh, good. Because I was just thinking about it more, and realized that the reasoning verges on an internal contradiction. It says ‘she has not really be adapted to hold and use Glamour the way a changeling has’ and that’s what causes her shortened lifespan. But what about Fae-Touched that escapes and never uses Glamour again? And isn’t part of the point of being Fae-Touch is to...have a character adapted to holding and using Glamour? That they become natural users of it as a matter of course?

        The writing here makes it seem like the ‘force’ of Glamour use shortens their lifespans, but by all indications they do it almost as naturally as the Lost. If they were using, say, magic rituals or something to access the Wyrd and Glamour, then it would be more understandable.

        Also, there was this line earlier in the write-up, "The Glamour of the place entered her easily and changed her." Which stands in almost direct contradiction to the idea that 'not being adapted enough to Glamour' is a reason for the Fae-Touched to have shorted life-spans. Because again, the statement it takes a 'toll' on the character implies a certain degree of forcing Glamour through them, but, well, see what I just wrote in this paragraph, and the above.

        The Wyrd has been associated with twisting things, and this is reflected nicely in the Fae-Touched gaining their own Seeming when/if they escape. Wouldn’t this twisting itself be indicative of adaptation? I’m still mentally working out the metaphysics of how the new take on Seemings work, but in general, the Wyrd tends to force something to adapt, rather then allowing someone to fail to do so. I could understand if a Fae-Touched who never found their way out of the Hedge had a shortened lifespan, provided they live/avoid being captured that long. But for one who’s escaped and gained a Seeming, the reasoning is much more iffy, and harder to accept.

        Not having very long to live is a tragedy in of itself. A big one. One that can easily threaten to overwhelm the already-present tragedy of a Fae-Touched whose 'promised changeling' is still trapped in Arcadia, or the Fae-Toucheds' addiction to the Hedge. It gets even more egregious when one notes how, in 1st edition. exposure to the Wryd increased the lifespan of changelings. Fae-Touched could have normal lifespans, but a greatly shortened one through channeling the Wyrd just doesn't match up with what I know and believe about Changeling.

        All of this, of course, is mostly based on what I've read in the blog posts, and what I've read in 1st Ed. Hence why my previous post had the addendum 'Unless there's some obscure bit of worldbuilding in 2nd Ed. That we aren't aware of yet.'If you want a theme of 'Fae

        Phew. Sorry, I kind of went on there. But I’m glad to hear that it’s probably going to be eliminated anyway. You guys have really done a good job otherwise.
