        I want to preface this by saying I do like Rose's Hedge. But I think what some people are struggling with is understanding its narrative role. The Hedge is a fairytale World, yes, but fairytale worlds tend to have intent behind their weirdness. The Labyrinth may look at a casual glance like a place where strange things happen at random for no reason, but everything that happens reinforces the underlying theme of "where anything seems possible and nothing is as it seems". The labyrinth exists to break Sarah's (and the audience's) expectations - more often than not, expectations it sets up itself. Wonderland seems like a psychedelic trip with no rhyme or reason, but upon closer analysis the vast majority of the apparent nonsense is actually jokes about math and logic. The 10th Kingdom was a very silly mini-series, but as lighthearted and zany as the kingdoms were, their purpose was to deconstruct common fairy tale tropes.

        The point is, fairy tales mean things, and this except of the Hedge writeup does not make it clear what this version of the Hedge is supposed to mean. David's Hedge was about pain, and about how pain, like magic, can be every bit as alluring as it is repellent; every bit as dangerous as it is wonderful. It may have been a little on-the-nose with its metaphor, but I prefer that over being too vague to be recognizable under critical analysis. This Hedge has the superficial trappings of a fairytale world in spades, but it feels shallow because, from this excerpt alone, I can't tell what it's about on a deeper level.

        Of course, knowing Rose's talent at conveying metaphor through mechanics, I am giving her the benefit of the doubt and assuming that this Hedge does mean something, and that meaning will be more clear in the complete text. I'm guessing there's key information in the redacted segment that would help it all come together.

                Originally posted by Omegaphallic View Post
                I guess the deeper meaning might be that the Hedge is a nexus of sorts between Dreams, Arcadia, and Ironside and so it inherits elements from all their natures.
                The problem with this is that it turns the definition of the Hedge into a circular citation:

                "What is the Hedge?"

                "It is the world between Earth and Faerie."

                "What is Faerie?"

                "It is the world on the other side of the Hedge."

                The other prominent magical neighbor-worlds in ChroD can be summarized by their reflective principles and the nature of their inhabitants — even the mysterious nature of the Underworld is mitigated by its being a a place where obsessive creatures grasp at life and color dwell and struggle not to fade out while mired in death and rot shut away from the lands of humanity by walls that are thinner around funerary traditions. There's no such unifying feature for goblin things besides a general sense of untrustworthiness and a more personal/immediate version of the reflective nature of the Shadow and the Astral.

                The God-Machine's Infrastructure approaches some of this problem by virtue of its tells explicitly including "weird and out-of-place," but that's a world-spanning force with a mysterious agenda and a specific practice of using occult confluences to advance its goals, whereas the Wyrd has historically been a little more reactive than that and doesn't have the assumption of an independent plan behind most of what it does in aggregate — a monster born from someone's nightmares isn't engaged with by changelings as something that the Wyrd created to engage with them, but there needs to be a reason of similar weight and simplicity to "someone reached an emotional tipping point and woke up the spiritual reflection of their kitchen knife" or "the mysterious conspiracy-engine that sits astride the material world found it most efficient to engineer an artificial ghost to keep people out of the study of one of its wealthy pawns."

                Changeling's metaphysics have always had an uncomfortable bit of Calvinball occupying the disjunction between "this makes sense in context but looks weird out of it" and "this looks weird and there seems to be no way to acquire the context for it," and I feel like having an independent working explanation for how and/or why the Hedge operates would do a lot to help with that.
