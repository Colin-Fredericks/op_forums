    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
    Did you miss the part where the devs opening thought was "Betrayal"?
    Mainly using this as a jumping off point:

    I think that is -- and some of the derivatives are -- still what people are focused on. As you said, it's kind of uncomfortable. It may feel to many like taking options away. Before, one could have a character with all the aesthetics that Darklings have, without seeing it as the game insisting they have a moral judgment in their backstory. I think getting people not to read it that way may be a hard sell. It's not to say there isn't a fascinating wealth of story and character options to explore there.* But I can understand people feeling like they're losing more than they're gaining on this. Certainly, of all I've heard -- while I still need to digest some of it -- this is the first thing that gives me pause.

    * A character may hate himself for making that call, and resolve to be as morally upright and outstanding as he knows how to make up for it, even if he's sure he never can.

    I mean, if it's now a certainty that the Darkling betrayed someone, that changes the social context around them tremendously. Changelings are already rather paranoid sorts. Many are likely to see past behavior as a predictor of future behavior, and that creates huge barriers to trust a Darkling character. Again, it's got interesting possibilities -- makes it all the more meaningful when a Darkling wins that trust -- but it lays out a particular area of stories that I'm not sure everyone who likes the Darkling aesthetics wants to explore in that way.

    That's part of why I'm not sure laying down one overriding means of escape that a particular Seeming represents is the way to go. Doing something dark, dirty, and underhanded to get out feels like it fits Darklings to a tee and gives a broad swatch of possibilities. But definitively making it "betrayal" feels like it's too narrow, in a way that will turn off too many people. If there are concerns that giving Seemings broad possibilities will overlap in places, I don't see that as a bad thing. Players and Storytellers can pick whichever of the Seemings makes the best fit for the character's story.

            THis is part of the reason I went with exploitation, the idea that they cheat the game. It could mean other changelings, but it could mean that they did the equivalent of hiding cards or lying about rules. It could mean they exploited the True Fae, making them afraid or misled or confused. It could mean taking cheating the domain. Could mean blackmailing hobgoblins. Could mean tripping changelings.

            I felt like exploitation had a range, from the DnD "Oh, they do what rogues do" to "OMG, these people are all con men and traitors". There's room for heroic exploitation and horrific exploitation. I also felt like that idea neatly encompassed many of the ideas put forth here.

            Of course, one thing about Darklings is that they've never been considered the most trustworthy. It's really bad in the Victorian Era, but even in Winter Masques they make a point of noting how people assume a Darkling is available to do the more morally ambiguous stuff. Hence another reason for exploitation as a uniting factor-if people know you took advantage of others before, why not do it again?

            On a different front, exploitation is a thingthey all do, but again, it's who or what's exploited. Wizened certainly exploit the system, but that's simply taking advantage of the Fae's rules and domains. A Darkling exploits aren't as informed, and what's more they are more inflicting than others. An Ogre beats, a Darkling steals, as it were.

            I dunno, I'm running with what makes sense to me until something better comes along.

                See, here's the big thing: if Seemings are when you grab the reins, that the actions that tie into them need a certain drastic-ness to them. Victims often feel like they need to go to extremes, and in the case of Changelings, they really do. THey have to do something that jerks control out from Them long enough to escape, and that often requires the heights and depths of what a changeling can convince themselves to do. It's part of the reason why they debate if they were let go or not-if they had to go that far just to get out, how do they even know if it's enough? It's also part of the reason they end up falling into the cycle, too-when you learn the extremes solve your problems,you tend to use them a lot.

                The feeling of drasticality or whatever the correct word is goes to different places, of course.Lots of changelings play the long game. The important thing to remember is that long games are often desperate, thread-thin shots that can easily fall apart. They are fraught with failure,and therefore drastic in their execution. They're tension ridden things.

                                                Being "let go" as a thing for No Seeming could be cool.

                                                @Killer

                                                Re: Beast.
                                                Been trying to find a niche for Beast that isn't "I am an animal," which would now be handled more by Kiths. Circling back to fables and fairytales for inspiration, being turned into an animal was rarely a good thing. It usually denoted a curse, a loss of humanity, or something similar. So a theme of degredation or losing something boiled to the surface. But Seemings can't be about losing something, they are about agency and empowerment! So how can you lose something but it represent agency? If you give/trade it away.

                                                Re: Darkling
                                                There is a difference between "contains running" and running defining the escape from the durance. Darklings didn't play the Fae's games or try to conquer them or figure out the rules. They took the first opportunity they had to get the hell out of dodge.

                                                Re: Elementals
                                                I don't feel being socially inept is an important or evocative enough concept to be worth preserving.

                                                Re: Wizened
                                                Technically in that writeup expertise would fall under Ogre, not Wizened.

                                                @ArcaneArts:
                                                Counter-Counter point. Keys and Manifestations. Single(-ish) book Splat vs Splat designed to sell more than one book.

                                                That said, its a tangential conversation, so I don't want to waste too much discussion on the topic in this particular thread.

                                                AN IDEA

                                                So. I have a smidge of a game I think may really help with this. Why don't people post example Durances (like a paragraph, two at most) that lend themselves strongly to a Kith. Lets say one of the current Kiths because we don't know the new ones, just for the sake of example. Then post how you feel each Seeming would escape from that exact same durance. Then we can start looking for emerging trends.

                                                Note: If you want to just put a Durance without how each Seeming solves it, that's actually better because...
                                                Bonus Points: Post how each Seeming escapes from someone else's Durance.
