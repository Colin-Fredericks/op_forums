        I've been brainstorming ideas for a San Francisco Freehold; it's been observed before that the Bay Area seems well suited to being a setting for Changeling, and I live in nearby Santa Cruz, so I have a fair amount of familiarity with the area. Here's my basic idea; more to come soon, hopefully:

        The Freehold of Gold Mountain

        San Francisco's freehold emerged in the waning years of the California Gold Rush, when the heady days of the 49'ers were already starting to fade into legend and the new city was settling into a modicum of stability. The first Changelings to emerge from the Hedge into the Bay Area were mostly recent arrivals who had been drawn to California by the promise of wealth and a better life, particularly poor Chinese immigrants whose relative invisibility made them easy prey for the Others. The name "Gold Mountain" is itself borrowed from the Cantonese Gam Saan (金山), and Chinese folklore has had a major impact on the structure and rituals of the freehold.

        Gold is the central metaphor around which the courts of Gold Mountain orient themselves, representing a whole host of physical and philosophical treasures which Changelings of the freehold seek. The highest law of the land is the Golden Rule, which states that "he who has the gold, makes the rules." The Changelings of San Francisco hold periodic elections, during which each court nominates one of their own to be the leader of the Freehold as a whole. Candidates gain the support of rival courts by buying it; each makes an offer of some sort to those who would endorse them as ruler, and whoever makes the best offer is ushered into power - after they pay up, of course.

        Four courts vie jockey for power in the Freehold (though I haven't quite come up with names for them), each of which grounds its philosophy in a unifying narrative:

        The Court of Greed claims to be the oldest of the courts, and this may well be true. They tell the story of the Gold Rush itself, when brave men risked all in the pursuit of unimaginable wealth, and the most capable among them seized it and built legacies of power and prestige. Unapologetic capitalists and masters of the strange economy of the Fey, the court does not see greed as a sin, but rather a fundamental part of human nature that motivates people to compete and achieve, and in doing so build a better world.

        The Court of Fellowship tells the story of the Golden Nugget, in which a pair of true friends and hard workers refuse to bicker over a treasure that cannot be shared, and in the end are rewarded twofold. They stress the importance of pulling ones own weight, but also of helping out one's fellow Changeling, so that the community as a whole may know strength and prosperity.

        The Court of Anger tells the story of an outlaw, Joaquin Murietta, who is said to have lost his brother and wife to a drunken mob. After ruthlessly hunting down the men responsible and exacting his revenge, he took to robbing Anglo miners of their gold and distributing it to poor Mexicans struggling under the yoke of an unjust society. While many in the other courts challenge accuracy of this tale, the Court of Anger cares less about the historical Murietta than it does about his role as an archetype of righteous anger and and rebellion. They seek to embolden the poor and the powerless and stoke anger and resentment against the injustice of the status quo, and aren't above using violence and robbery as tools in pursuit of their goals.

        The youngest court is the Court of Pride. They tell the story of the Magic Brush, in which a poor but virtuous young man is granted the ability to paint anything he likes into existence. He uses this power to bring prosperity to his people, but when a cruel and greedy lord takes him captive and demands that he paint a mountain of gold, the clever peasant sees to it that he is destroyed by his own blind avarice. The court encourages charitable souls who take pride in doing good works for their community, and artists who take pride in the art they create. Although less concerned with accumulating wealth than the other courts of the Freehold, they are able to compete for power through their willingness to give away all that they have, keeping nothing for themselves.

                The Hollywood Freehold

                Few places are quite as invested in the idea of presenting stories as Hollywood; there's new movies coming out all the time, in every genre. And the Changelings of Hollywood use this ever changing collection of stories to confuse Huntsmen and Fae - the Courts of Hollywood change every six months. That's because the ritual performed is that of creating a movie and then having an awards ceremony: positions in the Court are handed out based on accolades. Freehold Approaches are based on the winning film, Court Approaches are based on the film being worked on, and personal approaches based on the role currently being played.

                Traditionally the director of the "Best Film" is the King or Queen - titled "Producer" - of the Freehold and non-honored cast and crew make up the bureaucracy; other honors such as Best Protagonist, Best Deuteragonist, Best Antagonist, Best Music, &c receive other positions of power such as Champion or Enforcer.

                New Courts are formed amongst those with no official position at the beginning of each movie season (Summer for the Winter awards, Winter for the Summer), and courtiers are encouraged to move around and explore new avenues of expression, new roles, new costars, and new genres of film.

                The Summer movie season is upon us, and there are currently three competing courts:

                The Court of My Life on Credit - an introspective look on the dangers of modern consumerism and an over reliance on Goblin Contracts, My Life on Credit explores the existential weight of the debt cycle even if one has the ability to pay it off. (PG - moderate language) Emotion: Angst.

                The Court of The Coldest Iron - a semi-historical drama set in a European city that's being ruled by a True Fae, The Coldest Iron has enough character development, sex, and violence to wow any discerning moviegoer. (R - nudity, language, violence, gore). Emotion: Tension.

                The Court of The Huntsmen Hunter - when Thane Thorgron's boyfriend is taken across the Hedge by Huntsmen, Thane pulls out all the stops to get him back by becoming The Huntsmen Hunter. Not even the Hedge is tank resistant! (PG-13 - violence, explosions). Emotion: Excitement.

                    We're doing Freeholds now?

                    This one is for the archetypical USA small town.

                    The Anytown Freehold

                    The Freehold claim that their fonders were born in the iron age, and they lived well and prospered for every generation the village would come togeather and choose the greatest of their number to be a king. They would obey their monarch in all things, and so each became as great as the village's best. But one king decreed upon his deathbed that they would choose his eldest son; for the king loved his son as all fathers do and could not see that his son was not the greatest in the village.

                    Under the king's son's rule the village fell into poverty. The walls crumbled, the warriors became lazy and arrogant. The Gentry saw this weakness and took the entire population as slaves.

                    By the time the survivors escaped the world they remembered no longer existed. "Do not fear." they told one another,. "We will find a king who understands this new world".

                    Following power itself the arrived at Washington where they saw congressmen and senators going about their business. "Surely one of these powerful men will be our king" they said. But the senators and congressmen spoke about elections, and checks and balances, and the villagers could not understand them.

                    Next they travelled to Boston, where they saw factories and industry on a scale they couldn't imagine. "Surely a man who can create such marvels could be a king" they said. But the industrialists spoke of shareholders, and investments, and capitol, and the villages could not understand them.

                    Then they arrived at Pennsylvania, where they saw the university. "These men have studied all things, surely one of them must know how to be a king" they said. But the scientists spoke of methodology, and peer review, and the villagers could not understand them.

                    Distraught the villagers wondered the streets, they feared that without a king to protect them their old masters would return. By change they came across a lover presenting flowers and this was a language they could understand, and so they asked the flowers to choose them a king.

                    Bulwark Ritual

                    Once a year the courts oneiromancers create a dreamscape. In this dreamscape there is a road beset by trials. The road leads to a hall with an empty throne with a crown of white lillies upon it. The oneiromancers take the dream and divide it up between as many vessels as they can, but by tradition every changeling must have at least one vessel.

                    On the appointed day each Changeling secrets their vessels into people's beds. Some trust chance and Wyrd, others spend months before the day choosing their champions. That night each mortal with a vessel dreams themselves onto the path, they face the trials, and the first to reach the throne and claim the crown becomes king or queen of the freehold. Everyone else awakes with no memory and a good night's rest.

                    The trials themselves can be as easy or as challenging as the freehold wishes. Only two rules remain in force, the first is that no king or queen may ever know of or influence the selection process, they're told the flowers, that is to say the Wyrd, chooses. The second is that one trial must rule out any mortal with a connection to the Gentry or to a changing freehold.

                    In some desperate years those have been the only trials, but most have many more. A trial so that the new monarch cannot be cruel. A trial to exclude fae-tocuhed (for who wants a king to send his knights to Arcadia?) The Freehold often comes up with lengthy and elaborate criteria, but they must be careful. If no mortal makes it past all the trials the Bulwark shatters for the next year. Even setting too many trials will cause mortals to wake up before completing the quest or failing at a task.

                    The Court of Balsam The Unyielding Flora, The Red Blossom, The Court of Stubbornness

                    I was subjected to tortures you mere mortals couldn’t imagine so I’d like some fucking sympathy please.

                    The Court of Balsam do not return to mortal society so much as they expect mortal society to come to them on their terms. Almost every Changeling returns from Arcadia with phobias, supersitions and sometimes even frailties. The Court of Stubbornness doesn't even try to overcome these, they're offended by the notion that they should. Instead they mortal society to accommodate them. While the Freehold considers the Red Blossom to be stubborn gits who refuse to understand every relationship requires some give and take, they are invaluable to the Freehold because they remind the other changelings that yes, they do have issues, and yes they need to ask for accommodation before they snap.

                    The Court of Hollyhock The Proud Bloom, The Gold Blossom, The Court of Ambition

                    I’m sure you’ve read all sorts of faerie tales about our trickery, well that’s the old way. Modern faeries understand the benefits of customer satisfaction. So here’s my offer, seven years younger for seven million in cash, and I’ll make the same offer in seven years time.

                    The Court of Hollyhock want a life among mortals, but not just any life. Wealth, power or fame, their new life is going to be extraordinary if it takes every Contract they know to make it happen. This Court provides the Freehold with much of it's wealth and influence which makes up for the fact that the Gold Blossom is the least involved in day to day Freehold Life.

                    The Court of Honeysuckle The Choking Vine, The Pink Blossom, The Court of Dependency

                    Please, we can make it work. I can change.

                    The True Fae break their slaves. Their souls are full of gaps, and the Court of Honeysuckle return to the mortal world looking for someone who can fill those gaps. Some seek out their old family or their old loves, others tumble through relationship after relationship always looking for "the one". The Pink Blossom is infamous for the fragility of their members, especially when they're supressing their true personality for their lover's benefit, however the Choking Vine is invaluable to the Freehold because they're the only Court who, however clumsily, relate to average mortals on their own level.

                    The Court of Violet The Humble Herb, The Blue Blossom, The Court of Satisfaction

                    Hi Ho, Hi Ho, It’s Off To Work We Go!

                    If the Flower Courts are about fitting in with mortal society then the Court of Violet have figured it out. They find some quiet corner, usually one where their faerie talents are useful, and then they keep their heads down and do their job. Typically invisible, and happy that way, the Humble Herb take satisfaction in their work and being valuable to the mortals around them. In the Freehold the Blue Blossom are the ones who keep everything running. Few ever aim for status or influence, and those who have status or influence know better than to take the Court of Satisfaction for granted.

                    The Lily Crown The Royal Flower, The White Blossom, The Crown

                    A pure white lily symbolises many things. It symbolises virginity and purity, a Changeling’s lost state of grace before the Gentry plucked them from earth. To give someone a white lily says that it is heavenly to be with you, as it is heavenly for Changelings to be back home with humanity. Most importantly of all a white Lily symbolises majesty.

                    The Lilly Crown belongs to one mortal a year. Even though the Freehold has some influence in their monarch, they still end up with an ignorant mortal suddenly discovering that they're king or queen of the faeries.

                    None of the other Flower Courts have a Court Crown, their power is concentrated in the Lily Crown to grant a mortal everything they need to rule the faeries. The Crown provides:
                    • The Sight, Lily Kings can see through the Mask. (Former Kings retain this ability)
                    • Comfort with the Hedge: Lily Queens have a five die pool to open Hedge Gates and may open or create new ones by spending a point of Willpower. Once inside The Hedge they can navigate, are immune to any mental effect that does not affect Changelings and they are protected from both the thorns and transforming into a hobgoblin.
                    • The ability to make up to 5 Pledges, as well as Pledges of fealty and vassalage.
                    • Protection from magic: Any dice pool to activate a contract or which has been increased through magic takes a 5 Success penalty when targeting a Lily King, unless his majesty consciously allows it.
                    • Status in the courts, just as a Mantle provides social bonuses so does the Lily Crown. Lily Queens get a +3 bonus to all Flower Courts, which can stack with Court Goodwill to a maximum of +5. (Former Queens retain a +2 bonus and any Goodwill their previous subjects feel).

                                Okay, here's my first crack at Court Creation. Its been home for the last three years. Abu Dhabi.

                                **********************

                                Step 1: Founding a Freehold's Story

                                "Alright, where to start? What is it that the desert wolves of Ar Rub' al Khali say? Ah yes... This story is true. Except you know it fucking is, because you can see it all around you. Go to any bookstore, listen in any majilis and you will hear this tale of two boys. They may tell it a bit differently, but all of them are true. This is the tale of Shakhbut and Zayed. Its also the tale of Abu Dhabi. Both were brilliant young men, charming, faithful, well read and true Muslim poets, worthy each of being the Khalifas of old. When their uncle Saqr fell the bullets of a gunman, the title of Sheikh passed to the elder of the brothers, Shakhbut. Ruling for 38 years, Sheikh Shakhbut was all that the desert people of Al Ain and Abu Dhabi could desire in the time after the Great War. The Pillars of Islam were upheld and our way of life prospered, but those days did not last forever. They something under the sand dunes. Black gold. The Oil the British so craved, the very thing that made the Sauds so powerful was sitting under our land, waiting to be tapped, yet Sheikh Shakhbut would not allow it. He feared that to do so would be to end our way of life. Zayed say something else though. He saw what his brother couldn't and wouldn't. Times were changing, and unless our people changed with them, we would be lost to the desert forever."

                                "Zayed would not have a return to the days of wars and feuds amongst his own family. He and the high council removed Shakhbut from power, but they did it bloodlessly with exile The former Sheikh would return within the decade and live our his life for another two. Now Zayed was Sheikh and he was only bound by his ambition. Overtures were made and in just five years, Abu Dhabi no longer stood along. Six other Emirates joined with them, none so powerful as Abu Dhabi's former rival, Dubai. Together, they forged themselves into a new nation that would stand together and prosper from their shared resources. Sheikh Zayed was no longer just Sheikh, or even High Sheikh of Abu Dhabi, and President of UAE. No, he was greater by far, he because Our Father Zayed, the Father of a Nation: The United Arab Emirates."

                                "Why does our court focus on such a human story? Our Father Zayed may not have been supernatural, but the Wyrd respects those who make their own destinies. It was immediately clear to myself and the other Changelings who lived in the sand that we could use Zayed and Shakhbut's tales as our Bulwark. They were both amazing people. I wept for week in the sands when I heard of Our Father's passing in 2004. He was the man we needed to build ourselves a true nation. Ironically, it may be Shakhbut we need now, but we have lost forever. Many of his fears are becoming true. We have lost our tradition of pearl diving and now we are losing our Arabic language amongst our youths. It is the challenge that lies before our leaders. That is the way of the world though. It moves on. We can try and bring as much of the past with us as we can, but ultimately, we must move with it. I am confident they will keep us moving."

                                -Abdulla bin Uthman bin Abdel, Imam of the Falcon Court


                                Step 2: Building the Bulwark

                                The Bulwark of Abu Dhabi (which applies to the entire Emirate, not just the city of Abu Dhabi) is a series of monthly rituals that evoke both Zayed and Shakhbut. The first is both simple and sage. The Emir, head of the Courts of Abu Dhabi, must attend all five prayers on the three nights of the new moon at Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque. This means that the Emir must Muslim, or convert to Islam prior to becoming Emir, so that he is permitted within the Mosque during prayers. The Mosque was Zayed's Magnum Opus, completed posthumously and above and beyond being the third largest mosque in the world, it is also a powerful and possibly unique location within the Hedge. It is almost tranquil there, as if the Eden Imagery from within the mosque as impacted the Hedge itself.

                                The second half of the Bulwark is far more dangerous, both mentally and physically. During the Full Moon in the middle of each Islamic month, the Emir must hold a court within the Desert, far from civilization. This is a highly traditional Arabic affair, with one exception. Any being who knows of the event and possesses any manner of supernatural power is allowed to attend. This can range from the simplest child who can see ghosts to the utterly terrifying prospect of the True Fae themselves wandering out of the desert as Jinn. Most frequently though, it is an opportunity for the changelings to connect with the werewolves of the desert, who lead a far more rugged lifestyle. There is are also visits from the Unchained of Iran, Russia, and Canada, as well as the occasional Mummy. The Vampires never risk being caught in the desert sun. Part of the ritual is a pledge to defend the hospitality of the camp, and no one may partake unless they agree to it.

                                    The Courts of the Circus:

                                    These Courts were originally created by a band of wandering performers back in Europe. It's been long enough that even the oldest Courtiers aren't certain exactly who the creators were. There are stories of a band of motley Roma who gathered together in Romania after escaping from their Durances, a group of minstrels from France, or a Commedia Del'Arte troupe from northern Italy, and each has its believers. Wherever the Circus Courts began, they spread among wandering performers. There are several nomadic Freeholds based on these Courts, and while some are still circuses, there are also wrestling leagues and at least one Fae rock band whose tour personnel are made up of members of the Courts of Ring and Road. The Story this Freehold tells is that of the wandering band of strangers and rakes who appear in town amid fanfare and spectacle, who draw in those around them with merriment and astonishing displays, then disappear once the performance is done, possibly never to return.

                                    The Courts divide their rule based on whether the circus is in motion or has stopped for a performance. From the moment the first member of the troupe sees the first occupant of the place they intend to perform until the dawn after their final performance in a given venue, the Court of the Ring holds sway over the Freehold. From that dawn until the troupe arrives in a new city, the Court of the Road rules. The Ringmaster and Roadmaster of the troupe generally keep in close conversation, deciding on locations for performances, discussing safe routes through the Real or the Hedge, and dividing the jobs for the Freehold among the courts. While all members of the Ring Court perform on stage and all members of the Road Court have responsibilities offstage (such as ticket-taking or security work), members of each Court also often have duties in the other Court's bailiwick: For example, the whole of the Freehold helps to set up the circus in a new location, Ring and Road courtiers working in concert to set up their stages. Likewise, Road Courtiers often serve as assistants or stagehands for performance, and may also be pressed into service as clowns, chorus members

                                    The Circus Courts' Bulwarks are twofold. First, there is the tradition of wandering performance. A freehold troupe will never remain in a single location for longer than a fortnight, and will never travel for longer than a month before stopping to perform. The other Bulwark is a harder, and sometimes darker, affair. At least once a year, the troupe must take with them a young member of the community they've performed in to become part of their circus. Much of the time, this is simple - there have always been those who wished to run away from home, and the Circus Courts are happy to oblige. After a year as a hand in the circus (and often left utterly clueless regarding the nature of their circus-mates), the youth is generally disillusioned by the hard work and struggle of the circus life and is happy to wander away in peace - and those who remain are often Ensorcelled and become members in full standing in one of the Courts, holding nearly the same status as one of the Lost. When willing enlistees are unavailable, however, some Circuses are happy to take matters into their own hands, and either manipulate people to join or outright kidnap children to serve as virtual slaves beneath the canvas of their tents for a year. At the end of this time, some of these kidnapped youths are released. Others are never seen again.

                                    The Court of the Ring: the Court of Wonder, the Scarlet Robe
                                    The Wonder Court encompasses many of the performers of a traditional Circus Freehold. They believe in dealing with the trauma of their Durances by ignoring it, painting over it with the laughter and tears of their audiences. Despite a reputation for flightiness, Courtiers are generally incredibly hard-working, always testing a new routine, writing another song, or training their beasts to perform a brand new trick. That said, they play as hard as they work, if not harder, and there's a saying that if you see a Ring Courtier before noon the day after a show, it's because the husband came home early. Ring Courtiers approach new situations with unbridled optimism, enthusiasm, and curiosity. They find beauty and joy in the smallest things, and love nothing so much as to discover something or someplace new. They're not fools, however, and they often learn powerful magic with which to defend themselves. The Court of Wonder serves to remind their counterparts of the Road to take joy in life - they *have* escaped, and there is much beauty in the world, if you just look around to see it.


                                    The Court of the Road: the Court of Worry, the Oaken Staff
                                    The Court of Worry, in contrast to their more colorful cousins, make up the administrative and technical staff of the circus. They cope with their Durances by careful consideration and planning of the future, accounting for dangers and trials that may come so that they can be prepared for whatever they may face. Road Courtiers tend to be dour, pessimistic sorts, but they often have a sense of wry, even gallows humor about their circumstances. After all, if the situation's as bad as it can get, at least it can't get any worse, right? The Court is split fairly evenly between nervous intellectuals and cautious brawlers, and more than a few members of the Court turn out to be much smarter than they first appear. The Road Court values mundane skill, considering it more reliable than unpredictable Fae magic, though divination is a prized exception. The Worry Court tempers their counterparts' enthusiasm, reminding them that dangers uncounted lurk in the World of Darkness, and that walking into strange places blindly is an easy way to lose life, mind, or freedom.
