                Originally posted by Arrested Scrimshank View Post
                How strict is the “short ritual”? Can it be performed during an action scene (eg. in the middle of combat)?
                Going from the ritual needed to make a temporary manifestation on the same page, one turn, or six seconds outside of an action scene, but considering example rituals include stuff like filling a Chalice with wine and putting it in a cabinet or burying it, narrative and Initiative should probably also be considered re: whether it's feasible in a given moment.

                How hidden must the manifestation be? Is simply slipping it into a pocket or leaving it in plain sight acceptable for a dramatic reveal later on?
                I would say plain sight is probably too much unless you're doing a needle-in-a-haystack sort of thing, but the crux of it to my mind is that the ritual you're performing is thematically appropriate to the Regalia in question; you're gating your death behind a puzzle for the scene, and the solution to that puzzle requires whoever wants to kill you to know, suspect, or figure out that you possess a Regalia capable of such a thing.

                The manifestation doesn’t have to stay on the changeling’s person. But how is it handled if the manifestation is an item they're actively using, like a sword or amulet they’re wearing?
                Considering both the famous example you yourself cite at the top of this post and the description of the ritual as being performed "over the spot where [you hide] it," I would say you have to hide it somewhere that is not on your person; the effect only says it doesn't have to be on hand because that's a universal requirement of Regalia abilities unless otherwise stated.

                        Originally posted by Arrested Scrimshank View Post
                        Still, am I overlooking any thematic or mechanical reasons for why strict concealment might matter more than I’m giving it credit for?
                        Separation of a vulnerability and the same dramaturgical forces that a galoshin uses to confuse the Wyrd into thinking you are your hated enemy: from a practical standpoint, this use of a manifested Regalia exists to force someone who wants to kill you to either leave you alone or commit resources to being in two different locations (or at least physically moving you or preventing you from being moved), and the narrative being spun for "find my death and crack the egg of its potential" doesn't work if your death is held in a thing you are still holding on your person or standing directly in front of; it's there as a way to help you not die, not to make you bodily imperishable as long as you keep a particular ring in your pocket — this is a different process from becoming one of the Empty, after all.

                            Okay, so correct me if I’m mistaken; the ritual isn’t really about sealing away the changeling’s death as some kind of death-ward. That kind of magic would be too blatant a violation of the bargain between life-and-death, far too gauche for a little changeling. No clause entitles them to such authority.

                            What it does instead is trick death with sleight-of-hand: symbolic misdirection that hides the changeling’s life just long enough to temporarily fool causality, like a ball hidden under a magician’s cup. In my analogy the changeling’s body and their regalia manifestation are the cups, dressed up to look the same, and their life now exists in some quantum state between the two.

                                Originally posted by Arrested Scrimshank View Post
                                Okay, so correct me if I’m mistaken; the ritual isn’t really about sealing away the changeling’s death as some kind of death-ward. That kind of magic would be too blatant a violation of the bargain between life-and-death, far too gauche for a little changeling. No clause entitles them to such authority.
                                Essentially, yes. You would need an actual Contract or the more dramatic alteration of becoming Empty to have the effect be a straightforward "I can't die unless break this rock you can clearly see" sort of thing.

                                It lasts for a scene as a concession to it being a limited-use single power of an item that can have multiple such powers that can't be stocked up far ahead of time and incentivizing you to drop the reveal to your assailant in some way to keep them from sticking around you long enough to shank you when the cameras start rolling again, moreso than as an arbitrarily long countdown to renewed vulnerability. You do this before a potentially lethal situation that you think you can get out of with this extra bit of insurance rather than before diving into a lake of fire.

                                From what I understand, once your health track is full, you begin rolling to stay conscious and start bleeding out. As long as you're conscious, you can continue acting in the scene (even with a full track of aggravated damage). There’s a narrow window to reach safety before the curtain falls on the scene. If you lose consciousness, you're effectively dead, but in a kind of “playing possum” way. If your attacker walks away, there’s still a slim chance an ally could step in and revive you.
                                I'd say mechanically the effect stops your last box of lethal damage from converting to agg before the scene ends, but otherwise yes.

                                But… doesn’t it kind of make you bodily imperishable, at least for a little while? Just with the ring “over there” instead of “on you”? That’s the vibe I’m picking up.
                                I will clarify my meaning: The effect is clearly not meant to make "you are personally serving as an obstacle to the destruction of the thing that needs to be destroyed to kill you in a human-mortality version of putting a key behind its own lock" a legitimate narrative outcome — if you want to make it harder for someone to get to your Regalia, you put other people and obstacles in front of it and keep away for the rest of the scene, and if you want to take advantage of your Regalia allowing you to not die, you do it away from the Regalia instead of adding a particular called shot mechanic to the duel to the death you're about to engage in.

                                                That’s fair. It is a substantial option, exactly why I’m asking the question.

                                                I’m wondering if kith "trickery" is maybe more of a 1e holdover? But in 2e, the wording around the societal oaths open some interpretive space.

                                                Thematically, I immediately thought of a Court herald going to negotiate with another Court’s ruler. Even though the rest of their motley isn’t physically present, the symbolic weight of their oath is. All the motley’s presence is felt through the herald’s words and actions. Maybe the group is known to have painted marvellous dream landscapes. So, when the monarch asks for a portrait as the price for aid, it makes sense that the herald can use their Artist mate’s tools of the trade, even if the Artist isn’t physically there.

                                                The social focus of the oath is clear; social oath, social impact… but I don’t think that necessarily restricts the scope only to social manoeuvring. To me, the lowered exceptional success threshold seems like it's intended for more than just schmoozing monarchs or winning debates. It should apply to things like hedgespinning and oniromancy.

                                                I picture it like: “I’m up against a wall, but I saw my motleymate once convince a wall to turn into stairs. They’re not here, but through our oath, I can channel a bit of that same skill, once per chapter, and maybe have an easier time of convincing it too.” The power of friendship has made the obstacle easier to overcome.

                                                It reminds me a bit of how the pupil’s devotion personal oath works, which lets you access an oath bound teacher’s contact seeming benefit across any distance, as often as needed. In contrast, the societal oath gives you a single use of a single motleymate’s kith blessing once per chapter. That’s a much more limited mechanical effect, but still thematically consistent.

                                                Maybe what I’m describing veers more into storyteller-approved personal oath territory? Similar to how a pair can share damage or conditions over distance. Given how broad magic in changeling is and how important motifs and metaphor are, I think there's room for it. If not RAW, then certainly RAI.

                                                Curious how others would run this at their table.
