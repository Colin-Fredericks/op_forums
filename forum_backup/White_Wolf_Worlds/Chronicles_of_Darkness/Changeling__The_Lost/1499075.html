    What Are Your Changeling House Rules?

    12-07-2022, 01:16 PM
    I was wondering what people's house rules were, so I'll post mine first (feedback is welcome):

    Bonus Seeming Benefits

    Beasts - Animal Speech
    Darkling - Trained Observer 3
    Elemental - Indomitable
    Fairest - Striking Looks 2
    Ogre - Air of Menace
    Wizened - Eidetic Memory

    Systems

    Elemental Blessing - As long as your character touches or is surrounded by his element, she may use actions from the Psychokinesis 3 merit without spending willpower. She may instead roll Resistance + Wyrd for determining the dice pool.

    Fairest Blessing - When you spend Willpower points on another's behalf, you can choose up to your Wyrd in targets to get the benefits.

    Kenning - You can ask questions from Goblin’s Eye equal to successes rolled.

    Arcadian Body Merit - Works outside of the Hedge as well.

    Contracts

    Blessing of Perfection (Jewels, Common) - The object or person instead adds her Wyrd rating to the equipment bonus or skill, instead of replacing it. (thanks to DeviantDharma for this one)

    Touch of Wrath (Sword, Common) - Success: Each success causes two points of damage to the object’s Structure, ignoring any Durability that it may have. | Exceptional Success: As well as the additional damage inflicted, the Changeling may choose to transfer successes onto other nearby objects.

    Elemental Weapon (Sword, Common) - Thrown weapons reappear in hand after being thrown, and bows/slings/etc. are automatically supplied with ammunition.

    Vow of No Compromise (Shield, Royal) - Completely heals the leftmost health box of the target.

    Fortifying Presence (Shield, Royal) - Act as the target’s touchstone when healing Clarity damage.

    Seven League Leap (Steed, Common) - Loophole becomes “Jump up and down multiple times in a hopping motion for a turn.”

    Boon of the Scuttling Spider (Steed, Common) - Loophole becomes “Be outside at nighttime.” (my players hated the idea of eating a live spider)

      Alternate Fairest Seeming Blessing:

      The Fairest never suffers any circumstantial dice penalties for Social rolls. It doesn't matter if they go into a masquerade ball covered in blood and feces, because observers are subtly compelled to view the Fairest as beautiful, though each observer might conjure different details in their mind due to subjective notions of beauty. Furthermore, the Fairest can, once per Chapter, attempt to Force Doors in Social Maneuvering without destroying all possibility of further attempts if they fail their roll. The penalty to Force Doors is equal to [Remaining Doors] - [Fairest's Wyrd]. This costs 1 Glamour if the Fairest is suffering from a Condition such as Embarrassing Secret, Leveraged or Notoriety.

      New Ways To Regain Clarity:

      Whenever a Changeling exemplifies his Needle or Thread in an extreme way, he can forgo the replenishment of all Willpower Points in exchange for downgrading Clarity Damage equal to [Highest Between Wits And Composure]. Severe is downgraded into Mild, and Mild is fully healed. For example: If the Changeling has Wits 4 and Composure 3, and is suffering from 6 Severe Clarity Damage, his Clarity Meter now has 2 Severe Damage and 4 Mild Damage. Likewise, whenever a Changeling successfully completes a Long Term Aspiration related to repairing or reaffirming his worldview or self-image, he downgrades Clarity Damage equal to [Highest Between Wits And Composure], but does not have to forfeit the Beats earned from that Long Term Aspiration.

      Amaranthine Goblin Fruit:

      When consumed, it can either heal 1 Aggravated Damage, or ALL Lethal and Bashing Damage.

      Size Matters Benefit Of Hollows:

      It is rated 1 to 3 dots. 1 dot = a small house (about Size 20-35), 2 dots = a large house (about Size 40-55), and 3 dots = a mansion, castle or equivalent estate (about Size 60-75). Anything larger requires combining multiple Hollows via methods like Hedgespinning, Contracts, Tokens and assistance from Hobgoblins, as if they were homes in the same region of a city or equivalent. A single Hollow cannot be the size of an entire city.

      Bargains:

      The subject agrees to provide something in return, but it doesn’t have to be commensurate with the service the changeling is providing, since the point of the bargain isn’t what the human can provide anyway. Some changelings simply ask for money or valuables, while others ask for goods they can sell or trade.

      Clever changelings word their bargains so that the subject has to provide a seemingly innocuous service or undefined payment sometime in the future, and build up a bank of favors. The game mechanics of such favors, if any, are up to the Storyteller, but an appropriate use would mimic the effect of a one- to three-dot Merit, once per Story.
      Goblin Bounty is a Merit rated 1 to 5 dots, and it gives you [Goblin Bounty Rating X 3] goblin fruits per Chapter. Most goblin fruits simply replenish 1 Glamour, so by swearing a Bargain, the Changeling has an effective Goblin Bounty rated 1 to 3 dots. This represents the effort of the service rendered to the mortal being approved by the Wyrd, rather than eating actual goblin fruit.

      So you can gain 3 to 9 Glamour per Story per Bargain, depending on the terms you set. The greater the service, the more Glamour. A basic service gives 1 dot of effective "Goblin Bounty", while a medium service gives 2 dots, and a greater service gives 3 dots. You may extract these points of Glamour by asking the mortal to provide a symbolic offering of gratitude within the site of the Obligation Condition, such as milk and cookies, or gasoline and scrap metal.

      True Fae:

      By default, all True Fae have access to the "Ruled By Passion" and "Divine Flesh" Fae Aspects.

      Ruled By Passion gives them a Virtue and Vice, but inverted in how they play out. In other words, True Fae approach their Virtues as whims, favors, amusements, liabilities, or equivalent, and approach their Vices as righteous, fulfilling, regal, wonderful or equivalent. Otherwise, select Virtue and Vice for them as you would for a mortal.

      Divine Flesh means that whenever they are targeted by a mundane attack, they only suffer 1 Bashing Damage regardless of how many Successes are scored. They can also spend Glamour to heal Lethal Damage on a 1 for 1 ratio, or one Glamour to heal all Bashing. Even magical attacks aren't much more efficient: their type of damage is downgraded by one step (Aggravated into Lethal, Lethal into Bashing, and Bashing into nothing). Cold Iron and Frailties of the Bane type bypass this Fae Aspect, but unless the Bane-Frailty is Major, the True Fae can heal Lethal Damage caused by it via Glamour expenditure.

      They gain Skill dots equal to [Wyrd x 10] and Merit dots equal to [Wyrd x 4].

      Whenever you do not wish to give them all of those Skill dots, every 5 dots of Skills can be exchanged for 1 Dread Power / 1 Numen / 1 Influence Dot, which are listed under their Fae Aspects. Such abilities can mimic those of Bluebook Horrors or Hobgoblins, as well as a Kith Blessing + Trickery

      The True Fae activate their Fae Aspects with Glamour, and often buy dots of Influence over certain emotions to make up for the fact that they cannot have Court Contracts. Any such Fae Aspect is subject to the limits of Cold Iron. In other words, they are bypassed or negated entirely if struck by Cold Iron, and not a single True Fae can have any magic related to Cold Iron. True Fae can have a maximum of 3 Kith Blessings + Trickery, and their Kiths are often monstrous in their Mien and effects.

      You do not have to give them all of their possible Merit dots, but don't exchange leftover dots of Merits into Fae Aspects. Instead, leftover Merit dots may add Skill Specialties on a 1 for 1 ratio.

      True Fae can have as many dots in Skills like Crafts, Expression and Empathy as they want, despite their solipsism and creative sterility. However, any use of such Skills or equivalent ones that is not based on theft, imitation or plagiarism is automatically reduced to a chance die, which cannot be modified in any way. Therefore, such Skills find more use for activating their Contracts/Fae Aspects.

      Last but not least, True Fae can have any dice pools for any Contracts/Fae Aspects, as long as they fit the Title currently being used to manifest. And since they have access to all hypothetical Contracts within their Favored Regalia, the ST is free to come up with new powers for them on the fly, as long as they fit the purview of the Regalia and Title. However, True Fae must use the same Loopholes as Changeling for Contracts.


      Additional Sources Of Beats:

      1) Avoid directly confronting a Huntsman in a way that causes meaningful complications and hardships. Gain a full Experience if you manage to return the Huntsman's Heart.

      2) Successfully confront your Keeper face-to-face in a manner that reaffirms your freedom and identity. This gives a full Experience if you manage to do it inside the Hedge or Dreams.

      3) Get significantly lost while travelling through the Hedge or Dreams, with all that entails.

      4) Have a full scene of meaningful interaction with your Fetch, be it positive or negative. If you successfully merge with your Fetch [Autumn Nightmares Page 106], you may develop a custom Persistent Condition that gives Beats in addition to the usual benefits of Fetch-Merging.

      5) Each Motley possesses a shared and additional Long Term Aspiration, which is to be phrased as a recurring pattern, theme or motif of fairy tales, urban legends, or equivalent dramaturgical medium. The Wyrd arranges events for their lives to manifest narratives with symbolically resonant conflicts that would generate Beats. Whenever a PC gets a Beat from this additional Long Term Aspiration, they also get 1 Glamour. This Long Term Aspiration may be changed once per Story.

      Hedgespun Automatons And Artworks:

      Consult page 141 of Rites Of Spring for the original version of this system.

      Spend 1 Glamour and roll for a Crafting Equipment Action [ChroD Page 100], with bonus dice equal to your Wyrd. Relevant Specialties and Areas Of Expertise also apply. Certain Contracts, Tokens, Hedgespinning, Goblin Fruit, Merits, Blessing & Trickery of Kiths and so forth can help with the creation of the automaton, usually by providing the adequate materials and tools (e.g. Hollow's Workshop), or removing obstacles and limitations (e.g. Tatterdemalion's Workshop).

      Hedgespun Automatons can only be created inside the Hedge, and the Skill depends on the type of automaton. Examples: Crafts for assembling clockwork soldiers, Medicine for stitching together homunculi, Occult for reciting magical chants to animate puppets, or Expression to create artworks that harvest Glamour from the emotions of observers. The Kith Blessing applies for the purposes of Exceptional Success with 3 Successes, as long as it enhances the same Skill used for creating the automaton.

      For each -1 you take to your dice pool, your robot counts as a Retainer worth 1 dot (up to 5 dots), which also gives them free Merit Dots equal to [Retainer Rating X 2]. This includes Changeling Merits at the ST's discretion, but not ones like Court Mantle, Parallel Lives or Touchstones. The automaton lasts indefinitely, but each one has a battery of Glamour equal to your [Skill] X 2, can spend as many points of Glamour per turn as the Changeling, and starts with the 1 Glamour spent upon rolling. If the Changeling has a relevant Specialty, this battery is increased to [Skill + 1] X 2, and if he has a relevant Area Of Expertise, it is increased to [Skill + 2] X 2. They do not have a Willpower Pool.

      Each automaton depletes 1 Glamour per week of functioning inside of the Hedge, or 1 Glamour per day when within Ironside, and goes dormant without Glamour. Each automaton must be refueled manually through your own points of Glamour, which does not require a roll but takes 1 minute per point of Glamour. Each automaton has access to one Common Arcadian Contract possessed by the creator, and 1 suitable Dread Power, or 1 Numen, or 1 dot of Influence.

      This does not include any powers that would make it impossible to permanently destroy the automaton (e.g. Immortal), would allow it to grant others permanent traits without the expenditure of XP nor narrative tasks (e.g. Miracle), or are exclusive to specific entities (e.g. Resurrection Numen). The automaton has the same Seeming as the creator for the purposes of Affinity benefits in their Contracts, and its activation dice pool for its powers is [Retainer Rating X 2] + [Creator's Wyrd]. If targeted by a supernatural ability, the Hedgespun Automaton contests it with [Retainer Rating + Creator's Wyrd].

      The Changeling can give additional features by taking more penalties to his dice pool on a 1 for 1 ratio, usually in the form of Merits or Merit-like properties. The Changeling does not need to have such Merits himself to include them in the automaton. The creation roll takes one day per -1 taken, before factoring bonuses. Consider using Fae Mounts and Hedgespun Items as guidelines for the features of automatons.Teamwork with other Changelings can reduce the time taken in proportion to the Successes of the secondary actors: every 2 Successes offer a reduction of 1 day, rounded down.

      When creating an automaton, the main actor must decide whether Teamwork gives bonus dice OR reduces the time taken. Every automaton possesses Durability equal to [Skill], but does not heal Structure Damage passively.Therefore, a Changeling must Repair it, and their dice pool for Repairing receives bonus dice equal to their Wyrd. Each -1 imposed on this roll removes 1 Structure Damage, and takes 1 hour. If the automation is biological or made of components that are comparable to living tissue, then it heals Health Damage passively like a mortal would, but does not benefit from the inherent damage reduction of Durability. Cold Iron bypasses all Durability, Armor, and mystical protections of an automaton, and deals Aggravated Damage, like it does to Changelings.

      You can have up to your [Wyrd] in active Hedgespun Automatons simultaneously, but you can store inactive automatons inside your Hollow or equivalently secure location. Automatons cannot regain Glamour on their own unless the creator includes an ability or property that allows it. Even so, automatons might struggle or cause problems if attempting to regain Glamour via Emotions if their Retainer purview is not focused on social interaction, and so forth for other methods of regaining Glamour. Discovering how to create a specific model of automaton is its own process, which usually requires an Extended Action of some sort. As a suggestion: Intelligence + Occult, Target Successes = [Total Dice Penalty Imposed Onto The Crafting Equipment Roll] X 2, one hour per roll, with Merits like Library being applicable.

      For automatons whose designs are especially difficult, the ST can request that creating blueprints or equivalent documentation requires something more involved, such as haggling in a specialized Goblin Market, Uncovering Clues through the Hedge, seeking Dream Beings to request inspirational ideas, and so forth. Huntsmen can capture and analyze these automatons to identify the magical thumbprints and tells of the Changeling, and follow that clue through the ebb and flow of the Wyrd until they find their prey.

      In other words, the Huntsman gains bonus dice to find and capture the Changeling equal to the automaton's Retainer Rating, which also applies to activating their supernatural powers, including Hedgespinning. Hedgespun Automatons tend to become gradually stranger as time passes, especially if exposed to excessive damage, abuse or neglect. The Changeling must perform inspections and maintenance on his automatons at least once per Chapter to make sure they do not develop beyond their base programming, which takes 15 minutes per automaton.

      An Exceptional Success on the roll to create an automaton gives it the capacity to understand and follow complex instructions that would normally confuse it and lead to dangerous choices based on its misinterpretation. Furthermore, the Exceptional Success grants additional Common Arcadian Contracts and/or Dread Powers equal to the Changeling's [Wyrd/2 Rounded Up].

      Creating Hedgespun Art uses the same mechanics as Hedgespun Automatons. The Skill used by the creator is usually Expression, and the artwork rolls a dice pool of [Retainer Rating X 2] to cause a specific type of emotion, such as anger toward authorities, fear toward spiders, carnal desire, or sorrow of lost opportunities. This has the same suggested modifiers for when a Changeling personally harvests Glamour from an emotion. Other modifiers are also possible, especially if the observer has a Virtue/Vice, Needle/Thread, Aspiration or Condition which makes him more susceptible to being emotionally influenced by the artwork.

      The artwork starts with 1 Common Court Contract possessed by the creator, and 1 Dread Power or 1 Numen or 1 Influence dot, all of which must be based on emotional influence. The artwork uses [Retainer Rating X 2] + [Creator's Court Mantle] as its dice pool for activating its Common Court Contracts, but it cannot acquire any Physical or Mental Merits, and neither can it physically affect the observer in a direct way. However, it can emotionally influence him to make decisions with physical consequences.

      On a Success, the artwork can harvest Glamour from the emotion it causes in the observer. Each Success scored by the artwork drains 1 Glamour, and removes 1 Willpower Point from the observer, as per the usual mechanics for harvesting Glamour from emotions. The creator or another Changeling can retrieve this Glamour by simply touching the artwork (or the object that produces/contains the artistic experience, in the case of mediums like music and movies).

      If the creator has the Fair Harvest Merit, its benefits also apply to the roll of the artwork. On an Exceptional Success for making the artwork, it can harvest Glamour from an emotion in its entirety, and it has additional Common Court Contracts and/or Dread Powers equal to the Changeling's [Wyrd/2 Rounded Up]. Any emotions caused by the artwork's powers cannot be used as a source of Glamour, as usual. Changelings can notice that the artwork is supernatural via Kenning, Goblin's Eye and equivalent powers, which is not contested nor resisted.

      Other supernatural entities can accomplish this with their powers of detection. Supernatural attempts to remove or alter the emotions caused by the artwork are contested by [Retainer Rating] + [Creator's Wyrd], and mundane attempts are penalized by [Retainer Rating]. The artwork uses its own reserves of Glamour to activate its Contracts, and can spend up to [Creator's Wyrd] Glamour at once for this activation.

      As compensation for not being able to physically affect the observer in a direct way, the artwork does not need regular maintenance like an automaton does, and the Changeling is not subject to the limit of [Wyrd] active instances that applies to automatons. In order to include Common Contracts he does not possess himself in the automaton or artwork, he must swear an Oath to another Changeling who possess such Contracts. This Oath must be related to cooperation with tasks within the purview of the [Skill] used to create the automaton or artwork.

      Hodgepodge Designs Style (•-•••••)
      Prerequisites: Changeling

      (•): Gain bonus dice for creating Hedgespun Automatons/Artworks equal to [Style Rating]. Furthermore, add [Style Rating] to [Wyrd] to determine the maximum number of simultaneously active automatons, and add [Style Rating] to any Social roll the Changeling makes to harvest Glamour if he personally uses the artwork in some way to cause the emotion.

      (••): The Hedgespun Automatons acquire a Willpower Pool equal to [Retainer Rating], as well as a Virtue and a Vice if the creator wants, which are selected by the creator and allow the automatons to replenish Willpower Points like a mortal world. However, these Virtues and Vices do not have to match the ones typically displayed by mortals (e.g. Justice or Wrath)

      However, since the automaton lacks Clarity or Integrity, it will likely fulfill its Virtue or indulge its Vice in literal-minded ways, with the only rules being the ones chosen by the Changeling when creating the automaton. Hedgespun Artworks instead acquire the capacity to Incite Bedlam, using its [Retainer Rating X 2] as its dice pool and being subject to the same suggested modifiers for when a Changeling personally Incites Bedlam.

      (•••): All rolls to create Hedgespun Automatons/Artworks achieve Exceptional Success with 3 Successes.

      (••••): Whenever the Changeling scores an Exceptional Success to create the automaton or artwork, it acquires additional Common Contracts / Dread Powers / Numina / Influence Dots equal to [Creator's Wyrd + Style Rating/2 Rounded Up].

      (•••••): Once per Story, you may suffer 1 Severe Clarity Damage and sacrifice 1 Willpower DOT when creating a Hedgespun Automaton. This imbues it with sapience and free will, with all that entails. For example: subjectively interpreting commands, making independent decisions, learning dynamically and generally demonstrating agency. This person can be represented as a full-fledged Hobgoblin with a Wyrd equal to [Creator's Wyrd/2 Rounded Up], but barring truly extreme or unusual circumstances, it does not acquire Clarity.

      Creating life like this is almost always a Breaking Point for Clarity if it is done with the intent to enslave, abuse, imprison or otherwise treat the person like a Gentry would. If the Changeling finds a suitable Icon in the Hedge and sacrifices it without boosting her Clarity, he does not have to sacrifice 1 Willpower DOT. Furthermore, once per Story, you may suffer 1 Severe Clarity Damage and sacrifice 1 Willpower DOT when creating a Hedgespun Artwork to give it the capacity to physically and directly affect the observer with its powers, as if the observer is being targeted by an Oneiromantic action while suffering from the Flesh Too Solid Tilt. This can likewise make the creator and the observer suffer from relevant Clarity Breaking Points.

      If the Changeling finds a suitable Bauble in the Dreamlands [Swords At Dawn Pages 114 To 117], and uses it as part of an Oneiromantic action inside his own dreams that enacts a Paradigm-Shift related to the nature of the artwork, he does not have to sacrifice 1 Willpower DOT. The Changeling can keep the Bauble and use it normally after using it for this Paradigm-Shift. If the Changeling gives up an Icon or makes use of a Bauble, and sacrifices a Willpower DOT regardless, he can change the Loopholes of the automaton's or the artwork's Common Contracts, including those imitated by swearing an Oath to another Changeling. However, each such changed Loophole incurs 1 point of Goblin Debt.


      Enhanced Kith Trickery:

      In addition to spending 1 Glamour when activating her Kith's Trickery, a Changeling can suffer one of the following drawbacks to significantly modify or augment the Trickery's effects.

      1) Suffering the bare minimum of Severe Clarity Damage to inflict a Persistent Clarity Condition. For example: a Changeling with Wits 4 and Composure 2 has a Clarity Meter with 6 Boxes, so this would inflict 4 Severe Clarity Damage. This represents the Changeling requesting that the Wyrd allow her to work her fey magic as if she was still in Arcadia and serving her Keeper, which allows her to bypass certain limitations and obstacles for the Trickery but seriously harms her already fragile sense of what is real because it brings back memories of her Durance in full force.

      2) Suffering an additional Major Frailty chosen by the ST, which only goes away at the end of any scene in which it caused enough hardships, complications or setbacks to warrant at least 1 Beat. This represents the Changeling opening her already tattered soul to being influenced by the Wyrd, which weaves some of those tatters into a suitably dramatic curse of some kind.

      3) Suffering points of Goblin Debt equal to her [Wyrd]. This represents the Changeling postponing the price demanded by the Wyrd, and having to bargain with suitable Hobgoblins to pay it off lest she suffer a terrible fate sooner or later.

      For example: the Mercurial Visage of a Mirrorskin could allow the Changeling to reflexively and completely change both her Mien and Mask during this scene a number of times equal to her Successes on the roll of Wits + Subterfuge + Wyrd, which is in addition to her initial transformation. The Tools Of The Trade of an Artist also allow the Changeling to perform tasks normally beyond the scope of mundane Equipment, as if he had 3 dots in an Influence related to his Specialty, which does not cost additional Glamour to activate.

      Trickster's Grace (• To •••••)
      Prerequisite: Changeling
      A number of times per Story equal to [Merit Rating], the Changeling can activate an Enhanced Trickery without suffering any of the usual drawbacks. However, he is considered to have effectively sworn a Deed Vow upon a Long Term Aspiration of his choice, usually one that symbolically resonates with his Seeming, Kith, Court, Entitlement, Needle, Thread or any combination thereof. This additional Long Term Aspiration does not count toward the usual limit of 3 Aspirations, but it does not give Beats, and the possession of great personal significance is the Changeling's Trickery. In other words, if he violates the Deed Vow, he loses access to his Trickery for the rest of the Story. He can instead keep his Trickery if he suffers the Oathbreaker Condition.


      New Type Of Frailty: Tells

      The Changeling's Mien has a trait that his Mask does not hide at all. For example: his footsteps sound like cloven hooves, his joints have exposed clockwork mechanisms, lightning flashes across the sky if he screams, his presence curdles milk and makes babies go terribly quiet, and so forth. Tells can result in circumstantial penalties for relevant rolls (e.g. Manipulation + Persuasion to calm down a frightened mortal, Dexterity + Stealth to hide from a Huntsman), or even be represented as Environmental Tilts, especially in the case of Major Tells. The Changeling cannot directly suppress his Tells via Contracts, Tokens, Kith Trickery, Hedgespinning, Oneiromancy or equivalent.

      However, the Changeling can suppress a Tell much like a Taboo: if it is Minor, it can be suppressed for the scene with 1 Willpower Point. If it is Major, it can only be suppressed for one turn per 1 Willpower Point, or for the scene with the sacrifice of a Willpower DOT. If a Tell results in physical changes to the environment, such changes can be bypassed or undone with the use of tools or weapons made out of Cold Iron. Another supernatural character can attempt to bypass or undo these physical changes to the environment with suitable powers, but this triggers a Clash Of Wills against the Changeling. However, another supernatural character cannot use their powers to directly suppress a Tell that reveals a part of the Changeling's physical form or equivalent.

                  The Changeling game I'm running is actually centered around the Fetches, which I expanded into a more complete template.

                  The Characters have Bundles, which are the effective Splats of the template. Each Bundle represents different types of ingredients used to create a particular Fetch, based on the idea for Fetch ingredients in 1e.

                  Fetches have a Persona and a Shadow instead of Virtue and Vice. These work very much like a Changeling's Needle and Thread, except that the Shadow is kinda halfway between a Thread and a Vice. Fetches can regain Glamour through fulfilling their Persona instead of Willpower, while their Shadow acts like a Mantle of the appropriate emotion and adds successes to Harvest rolls (in addition to regaining Willpower). Fetches also have an Obligation which acts like an Aspiration that grants Glamour in addition to a Beat when fulfilled, but can cause Disquiet (talked about below) if a Fetch fails to live up to it. These additional sources of Glamour reinforce the "we're here to play a role" aspect of the Fetch's existence. Fetches lose 1 Glamour per day, so there's a Vampire-like tension that compels the Fetch to act in their counterpart's stead.

                  Fetches have a lot of Promethean durability traits, either as innate parts of the template or purchasable as Merits. A lot of my Fetch-Specific Merits are reskinned Promethean, Changeling, and Supernatural Merits.

                  Fetches can cause Disquiet. Their Disquiet triggers are a lot less frequent than a Promethean's Disquiet (usually doing overtly supernatural stuff or performing acts that are grossly out of character for their assumed Persona), and the initial condition is Spooked instead of Stage 1 Disquiet (unless they are dealing with their Counterparts, which is where Disquiet really starts to kick in -- most social interactions with their Counterpart can trigger Disquiet). The system is largely the same as it is for The Created. Fetch Disquiet is largely based on Unfleshed Disquiet mixed with Capgras Syndrome.

                  Fetches can convert limited amounts of lethal damage to bashing damage by spending Glamour and eating handfuls of their primary element (based on Bundle). This is limited to (Wyrd) points converted per Chapter. While this can also convert Aggravated Damage to lethal, it's more expensive and none of the characters currently have enough Wyrd to spend the necessary Glamour.

                  Fetches can Harvest Glamour (and gain additional Glamour when they Harvest emotions in line with their Shadow), regain Glamour through acting out their Persona or fulfilling their Obligations. I made the ability to Reap Glamour an Echo, but Fetches can't do it natively.

                          Tricks

                          A Trick is almost anything a character does to make things more difficult for their foes. Tricks include embarrassing an opponent, throwing sand in his eyes, staring him down with a steely gaze, or anything else a clever player can think of to rattle his enemy and put him off-balance. To perform a Trick, the player describes the action and works with the ST to determine the most appropriate skill to roll for it.

                          Dice Pool: One Power or Finesse Attribute + One Mental, Physical, or Social Skill vs. an appropriate Finesse + Resistance combination.
                          Target: One foe within Close or Short Range
                          Action: Contested

                          Roll Results

                          Success: You embarrass your opponent, throw sand in their eyes, stare them down, kick them in the genitals, confuse them with technical jargon, or anything else you can think of to rattle your enemy and put them off-balance. The foe gains your choice of the Distracted or Vulnerable Tilt.
                          Exceptional Success: You inflict the desired Tilt, plus an additional Personal Tilt appropriate to the action (defaults to adding both the Distracted and Vulnerable Tilts to the target if nothing else is suitable).
                          Failure: Your trick fails to achieve the desired result.
                          Dramatic Failure: Your foe turns the tables on you. You gain the foe's choice of the Vulnerable or Distracted Tilt.

                          Special: Modifiers might include cover, certain Conditions, situational factors (using a slur you know the foe reacts strongly to), and possibly Equipment Bonuses. Using the same or similar action repeatedly quickly grows less effective; subsequent attempts at the same Trick in the same Scene count as Successive Attempts and suffer the appropriate penalty.

                          DISTRACTED
                          Personal Tilt

                          Description: The character is briefly irritated, angered, pained, confused, or otherwise unable to put their full concentration on what they're doing at the moment.
                          Effect: The character suffers a -2 penalty to their next rolled Instant Action. If they would suffer this Tilt again before they've acted, they gain the Vulnerable Tilt instead.
                          Causing The Tilt: Tricks, environmental factors, sudden surprises.
                          Ending The Tilt: This Tilt lasts until they've taken an action it would affect or until the end of the character's next turn.

                          VULNERABLE
                          Personal Tilt

                          Description: The character has briefly left themselves wide open and struggles to defend themselves against their foes.
                          Effect: The next action taken against the character gains a +2 bonus. If they would suffer this Tilt again before they've acted, they gain the Distracted Tilt instead.
                          Causing The Tilt: Tricks, environmental factors, sudden surprises.
                          Ending The Tilt: This Tilt lasts until an action is taken against the character or the end of the character's next turn.

                          Support

                          This is an alternative to the Teamwork rules, allowing for more flexibility. If you want to help an ally with a task, and you can think of some method of doing so with a Skill not normally associated with that task, you may attempt a Support action instead of a normal Teamwork action. You and the Storyteller should be creative when making Support rolls. You might use Survival to find useful herbs to help a Medicine check, or use Science to calculate a Tokoyo Drift to aid a character's Drive check, or provide general encouragement ("You can do it!") with a Persuasion check.

                          You can use a Support action to narratively affect an opponent, but the only effect it can have is aiding an ally's rolls. If you want to "trip up" a feral vampire to help a friend make a Weaponry attack against it, the character you're aiding gains the bonus but you don't actually inflict any Tilts or Conditions on the vampire. For that, use a Trick instead. The advantage of using Support over a Trick in this manner is that the roll is not Contested.

                          Dice Pool: Any justified by the narrative.
                          Target: One specific Skill used by another character.
                          Action: Instant

                          Roll Results

                          Success: You add +1 die to the character's next roll with that Skill within the Scene. To add more than this, use a regular Teamwork action instead.
                          Exceptional Success: The character resolves the Inspired Condition on that roll; the roll is considered an Exceptional Success on three successes instead of five, and they gain a Beat.
                          Failure: Your attempted Support does not aid the character.
                          Dramatic Failure: Your attempted support backfires, inflicting the Distracted Tilt on the character.
                          Big Iron
                          • Iron is a Bane: Iron ignores fae-born defenses and magic. A Contract, Echo, token, or other type of fae magic that provides Armor or a bonus to Defense will not shield a fae creature (changelings, fetches, hobgoblins, and The Gentry included) from an iron weapon. Changelings and Fetch with the Portaling Echo cannot escape iron bonds or iron enclosures. Hedge gates do not form if the arch, portal, or reflective surface would touch iron (one reason horseshoes were traditionally hung above doorframes was this property). Iron presented as a protective charm (rather than being wielded as a weapon) forces the fae to spend a Willpower point to act against the warded person or object, and the character suffers a -3 dice penalty to such actions.
                          • Iron is not Steel: Steel contains Iron, but that's like saying hydrogen peroxide contains water -- technically true, but you can drink one without issue while the other is poisonous. The power of Iron is in the mythic concept of it, and fae creatures can tell the difference by touch -- iron has a "bite" to it, like a static shock, that items made of steel lack.
                          • Cast Iron and Wrought Iron are the most common usable forms: Cast iron is generally used in cookware, pipes, car parts, and heavy machinery. Wrought iron was once the most common form of iron in the Western world, used in rivets, chains, railway couplings, water and steam pipes, raw material for manufacturing of steel, nuts, bolts, horseshoes, handrails, straps for timber roof trusses, boiler tubes, and fine art ornamental ironwork, furniture, doors, gates and hardware. Today, most "wrought iron" objects are made from mass-produced mild steel, and thus aren't any more useful than any other improvised weapon. You generally have to go out of your way to find genuine iron, either from antique shops, museum displays, old estates, or have them specially made. Fortunately, Bradshaw is rife with old historic buildings, particularly near the Courthouse Plaza and along Mount Earl Street and Governor Highway, and there are tons of antique stores downtown. If you want a tool made from Iron, the Availability is generally 1 point higher.
                          • "Cold Iron" has to be specially made: "Cold Iron," iron that actually deals aggravated damage to fae creatures, never occurs by accident. Cold Iron is commercially pure iron that has been hand-forged (not mass-produced, cast, or created by a machine), and magic cannot have played a role in its creation in any way. So-called "thunderbolt iron" (iron from meteorites, as well as iridium, which isn't technically iron at all but has mystical properties of its own) is especially useful. Cold Iron implements and weapons have an Availability 2 points higher than normal.


                          Getting Into And Out Of The Hedge

                          Where you can create a Hedgeway: A Hedgeway can be created out of any archway, doorway, window, or reflective surface big enough to fit through. This is a change from how 2e normally does things, more in line with how 1e Hedgeways worked. There's a minor but important thing to keep in mind -- you can open any door or window into the Hedge, even if it's locked or barred (as long as it is something you could open or close). This means that beings who can naturally open Hedgeways have a lot of options and are difficult to keep contained anywhere. It isn't as easy to get out of the Hedge, though. Only the Gentry can create portals into the mortal world -- anyone else needs to find an already existing Hedgeway. The Hedgeway creates a copy of whatever was used, so if you find a freestanding door in the middle of a Trod, chances are that's a Hedgeway out.
                          • You can create Hedgeways on doors, windows, arches, and mirrors that can then be moved. As long as the frame (or reflective surface, in the case of mirrors or ponds) remains intact, it will always function as a Hedgeway, no matter where you move it. This means, for instance, that a car door is a perfectly viable conduit into and out of The Hedge. Note, however, that momentum is conserved between portals. In layman's terms, speedy thing goes in, speedy thing comes out. If you open a Hedgeway while in the back of a cop car going 65 MPH down the highway, prepare for a rough landing on the other side.
                          • Once a Hedgeway is created, it always links the same two locations Ironsides and Hedgesides, even if destroyed. This does not mean, however, that two Hedgeways will open in the same vicinity. Two doors might be ten feet from each other Ironsides, but open up to completely different areas of the local Hedge, often at least a milestone apart. There are limits to this -- a Hedgeway opened in Boston won't open into the Hedge near Calcutta, for instance -- but it's usually randomly separated enough that you can't reliably chase someone who went in one Hedgeway by opening another.
                          • Some naturally-occurring Hedgeways form from things like mushroom rings, hole-like rock formations, animal burrows (such as rabbit holes), and the like. These can't be created artificially with any reliability, even by The Gentry.


                          The States of Hedgeways: There are 3 states of a Hedgeway: Open, Active, and Dormant. Hedgeways can also be Destroyed or Blocked.
                          • Open: Currently active and open, and can be traversed by anyone and anything in either direction. A gate remains open for the opener's Wyrd in turns. They may extend this by either playing 1 Glamour or making a Resolve + Wyrd roll as an Instant Action; this keeps the gate open for another [Wyrd] turns.
                          • Active: Not currently open, but has been charged with Glamour. Usually newly created, or one that sees significant use. Changelings, Hobgoblins, Hedge Beasts, Huntsmen, The Gentry, Fetches with Over The Hedge, and anyone with a Key can open the gate with a knock and a request. They don't even need to spend Glamour to activate it. A Hedge Ghost can Reach through an active gate even if it's closed. A gate remains active for one season (about 3 months) after it's created or charged while dormant. You can spend 1 point of Glamour to extend how long the Hedgeway is active by a season when opening it.
                          • Dormant: The Hedgeway is there, but it's neither open nor active. It needs to be charged with a point of Glamour to function, which opens it. This is the state of most Hedgeways that are used rarely, perhaps even only once. These gates can still be opened without Glamour if you know or possess the Key, and can still spontaneously open if a mortal fulfills their Vice while near it. Only Changelings, Gentry, Huntsmen, or Fetches with Over The Hedge can open a dormant Hedgeway without a Key. Hedge Ghosts can still Reach across a dormant gate.
                          • Destroyed: A Hedgeway can be broken. Destroying the frame of an arch, window or doorway, breaking a mirror, or drying up a reflective pool will cause the Hedgeway to stop functioning. It can't be opened unless and until the arch, doorway, window, or reflective surface is repaired or replaced. After a season, the Hedgeway fades away completely and has to be recreated from scratch.
                          • Blocked: Blocking a dormant or active Hedgeway is possible on one or both sides, if you can impede movement through it. For instance, in the Tunnel Market there is a Hedgeway made from an arch that was bricked over Ironsides -- you can get in Hedgesides, but would need to remove the bricks Ironsides to get out again. Iron can also be used to block a Hedgeway -- a bit of iron set on either side of the gate prevents it from opening unless and until it is removed (hence the practice of hanging a horseshoe over a door as a ward). Iron causes dormant Hedgeways to degrade, sealing them as if they were destroyed after a season (though they can be reopened if the iron is removed and a creature capable of portaling reforms the Hedgeway).

                          Keys: Creating a Hedgeway also creates a Key for that Hedgeway. They can be physical objects, such as a turtle shell carved with the right sigils or the broken-off star of an old Mercedes. They can be specific songs sung at certain times of day, or a particular confluence of events, or the presence of red-haired twins — nearly anything can open a Hedge gate, but the would-be traveler must find out what each gate's Key is separately. A curious seeker can take an extended research action to do so, with access to the right kinds of information sources, or can learn about it from a fae who knows. A few Keys see steady use, by individuals or groups.
                          • The creator of the Hedgeway can designate the Key for one or both sides of the gate when they create it. If they don't, the Wyrd chooses a Key on its own. The Key that opens the Hedgeway Ironsides does not have to be the same Key that opens it Hedgesides, and frequently won't be if left to the Wyrd.
                          • Physical keys can be detected with powers, merits, or abilities that detect supernatural abilities. This doesn't necessarily reveal anything about how the key works, or what actions it takes to make the Key work. If you need to hold a misprinted Magic: The Gathering card above your head while spinning widdershins at midnight to open a Hedgeway, just knowing the card has a faint magic aura isn't going to help much.
                          • Legends such as fairy circles, alien abductions, or the enthralled woodsman following the beautiful hulder into the land of the Fair Folk can become Keys when a human heart holds the tales dear. These stories can play out when a human soul in the right place believes they should, triggered into existence by this belief. It requires a successful Resolve + Composure roll to open a Hedgeway this way, but the belief must come first. Hobgoblins can also attempt this method of opening a dormant Hedgeway.
                          • Any time a human indulges their Vice near a Hedgeway while Ironsides, it acts as a Key that subconsciously lures them into the Hedge. The character may roll Wits + Composure to resist the lure, though they can waive the roll instead (treating it as a failure). The Hedgeway opens, presenting a temptation that appeals to the character's Vice. They may answer the call or not; if they do, they gain another Willpower point for fulfilling their Vice again, beyond the normal limit of one per scene, and their player takes a Beat. The gateway slams shut behind the character once they pass through. This Key does not function Hedgesides. Instead, if a character fulfills their Virtue near a Hedgeway, they can immediately make a Wits + Composure roll to force the gate open for one turn per success.
                            • Characters that have alternate Anchors (Needle & Thread, Persona & Shadow, Elpis & Torment, Etc.) can't activate Hedgeways this way. This is a temptation strictly for those who still have human value systems.

                                              I've also worked out a rough system for Goblin Bargains, based loosely on the 1e Goblin Market book but using 2e Mechanics.
                                              Goblin Costs

                                              How exactly do you calculate the price of a memory, your eye color, or a good day from your future when the Moppum Family comes calling? Will they accept American Express (no. no one accepts American Express anymore)? What if you want to buy a friend or the respect of your peers?

                                              While a lot of these things can be fudged, it's nice to have a guideline for these sorts of things. For the most part, you can break a cost down into three categories: Consumable, Roleplaying, and Permanent.

                                              Consumable Costs

                                              Consumables tend to be the lightest costs. They're resources that a given character has that they can replenish fairly easily, usually within a given Chapter.
                                              • Glamour is the universal currency of Fae creatures. Glamour can represent a lot of different things -- emotions, small memories, abstractions, and so on. Glamour is the basis of trade, and most consumables will have a cost rating of between 1-5 Glamour, similar to an Equipment or Service Availability.
                                              • Willpower can be used as an acceptable substitute for Glamour in most cases. This is all regular mortals have in the way of spendable resources, for the most part.
                                              • Other Supernatural Fuel such as Vitae, Essence, Mana, Pyros, Aether, etc. can more or less substitute for Glamour, though the exchange rates may be off.
                                              • Goblin Debt is usually a temporary cost...for Changelings and The Gentry. Goblin Debt is much harder to shed for others, and is covered in greater detail in the Roleplaying section. Goblins know that Changelings can welsh out of Goblin Debt easier than most by getting some temporary bad luck, so they value it lower when it comes to them.
                                              • Temporary Equipment includes any limited-use or single-use item, such as Goblin Fruits and Oddments, Jury-Rigged Equipment, Trinkets (as in, single-use Tokens), or similar items from other supernatural sources. Most Goblins won't accept these things except as a deal sweetener or as part of a larger trade, unless the item in question is particularly useful or rare.
                                              • Permanent Mundane Equipment Might or might not be acceptable -- this sort of thing is generally covered under Collectibles in Roleplaying.
                                              • Components covers things useful for various magic rites and rituals. If they're the sort of thing that can get used up or consumed during the ritual, they count as Consumable. If it's something that can be reused, it's a Collectible.


                                              Roleplaying Costs

                                              Roleplaying Costs cover things that are more involved than Consumables and require more out of the character, usually in terms of incorporating the cost into the narrative somehow. You can gain Beats from some of these events, unless the Beat itself is part of the cost paid. It can take from several Scenes to entire Chapters to entire Stories to replace things traded this way.
                                              • The basis of this currency is points of Goblin Debt. Goblin Debt is a lot more difficult to shed if you aren't a Changeling, which makes it useful as a fungible currency among Goblins, and can be bought, sold, or bartered for many things. As a bookkeeping matter, certain things can stand in for Goblin Debt -- a given Roleplaying Cost might be "worth" between 1-5 Goblin Debt Points.
                                                • Goblin Debt is essentially an IOU -- "I owe this goblin one minor task of their choosing at some point in the future." While Goblins will try to milk their debtors as much as they can, these tasks usually follow the Task scale - a minor task to shed 1 point of debt, a medial task for 2, and a greater task for 3. If there's any additional restrictions or limitations, they add to the points of Debt shed, depending on how onerous the restriction is.
                                                • Goblins want you to pay off your debts, and can "tug" on their connection to the Wyrd to give the player a reminder. This works just like it does for Changelings -- The Storyteller may allocate Goblin Debt points towards penalties, Conditions, or Tilts at any time. However, unlike Changelings, doing this doesn't shed the debt. The Debt remains, though it can't be used against a player more than once per Chapter. A player may reject the Storyteller’s expenditure, but each time they do so, they gain an additional point of Goblin Debt.
                                                • However, Fetches have a trick they can pull. When a Goblin tugs at their Debt, the Fetch may reflexively spend 1 Glamour and roll their Wyrd as a Dice Pool. On a success, they force the consequences of their debt on to their Changeling counterpart. Doing this means they don't have the option of refusing the expenditure, though. Also, the changeling becomes aware that their sudden bout of bad luck is coming from their Fetch. It also doesn't expend the debt -- it just pawns off the consequences temporarily.
                                              • Since a point of Goblin debt is essentially "IOU one favor," let's talk about Tasks. Tasks detail what the sworn must or must not do to discharge their obligation. The one who owes the Task is bound by Wyrd to grant it, so long as it is within the bounds of what is owed, when it is asked at that later date, or suffer a sanction of the appropriate power, levied by the Wyrd itself. Tasks come in 3 flavors: Minor, Medial, and Greater.
                                                • A minor task is rarely difficult to accomplish. It consists of either frequently doing something that requires hardly any time or effort at all or the performance of a single task that makes slight demands on the one so bound. Some lesser endeavors involve keeping a patch of flowers in one’s yard clear of dandelion blooms or carrying a package from one part of town to another and delivering it to someone there. This is generally worth 1 point of Goblin Debt.
                                                • Medial tasks take some effort to accomplish. They consist of some kind of constant activity that requires a small sacrifice of time or resources to accomplish or the performance of a difficult task (or one which may result in harm). Some medial endeavors include keeping an item safely hidden, doing some manner of tedious chore on a daily basis or carrying something either through dangerous terrain or to another city or geographic region. This is usually worth 2 points of goblin debt.
                                                • A Greater task can be quite epic in its requirements to accomplish. Such endeavors consist of an activity that can only be accomplished through significant attention and effort, or a single task of tremendous difficulty (or one that may result in death). Some greater endeavors include time-consuming and dangerous chores such as regular guard duty or traveling to other continents to deliver something. These are worth 3 points of Goblin Debt.
                                                • Sometimes a task might come with limitations or restrictions on how it should be approached or accomplished, or there's something that needs to be avoided or ensured in the completion of the task ("do it without magic," "avoid waking the manatee," "Eldred must live," etc.). Depending on how difficult the restriction is to abide by, it can cover an additional 1-2 points of Debt.
                                                • A Forbiddance is a task that outlines what the oathbound are forbidden from doing. As long as they are abiding by the forbiddance, it counts as satisfying the Debt. Breaking the forbiddance means the Debt comes back, along with some sort of backlash, complication, Condition, or Tilt. Unlike a Taboo Frailty, there's nothing interfering with actually breaking the Forbiddance aside from a person's desire to avoid Wyrd-enforced consequences. Forbiddances come in the same three forms that other Tasks do.
                                                • A Minor forbiddance prevents the oathbound from performing some action that is simply avoided. Situations in which the opportunity to perform the action come up only rarely, and there is no difficulty in not taking that action. Agreeing to avoid entering a certain building, not consuming a certain specific food or drink or avoiding using a specific name or phrase are all examples of Minor forbiddances.
                                                • The actions censured by a medial forbiddance are somewhat more difficult to avoid, whether because they are more common or because the oathbound is likely to desire to perform that action. Situations in which the opportunity to perform the action come up more often, and it may serve as some inconvenience to avoid that action. Never entering a particular neighborhood or using the subway, not eating a specific category of food (such as beef, wheat or citrus) or not speaking to a specific type of person (such as policemen) or about a specific topic (such as sports) are all examples of medial forbiddances.
                                                • Greater forbiddances are truly dire, and invariably change the way an oathsworn lives her life. Situations in which the opportunity to perform the action come up frequently, and it is quite difficult to avoid taking that action. Being banished from a city or geographical region, never again touching fruit or meat and oaths of silence and chastity are all greater forbiddances.
                                                • Durations for tasks are typically a day, a week, or a moon (one lunar month). The time is measured from where the task is supposed to take place -- stuff to be done done or avoided Ironsides is measured in Ironsides time passed, while stuff done in The Hedge is measured in Hedge time. Longer durations, such as a Season (three lunar months) or A Year And A Day (as it says on the tin) will account for an extra point of Goblin Debt. Durations longer than that (a decade, lifelong, generational) are worth Experiences instead of Debt.
                                              • Uses of Merits such as Resources, Allies, Contacts, etc. are either treated as Consumables or as worth one or more points of Debt, depending on what the use is. If the use would put a long-term strain on the Merit, it's usually worth points of Debt.
                                              • Collectibles are the most commonly traded things next to Tasks. Collectibles are generally any physical, tangible object.
                                                • Equipment is permanent equipment, either acquired through Merits or made with a Build Equipment action. Any of the 5 equipment types can be bought or sold -- many also fall in to the Curiosity category if they're made out of strange or fanciful components, though this doesn't necessarily increase their value. Be aware -- Goblins aren't known for consumer-friendly practices, and Equipment sold by them might wear out, break, or vanish sooner than one expects. Or they might be unexpectedly dangerous to the user. Caveat emptor.
                                                • Curiosities are items that are generally nifty. They might have a minor magical effect, unusual construction, interesting history, or emotional significance. Goblins like collecting oddities, especially oddities that have emotional significance to someone. Objects that are (or were) Ghost Anchors and Mementos, for instance, are always worth something. Icons are often traded as Curiosities, too. The value of these in Debt varies wildly and is often down to a Goblin's personal peccadillos and interests, or even if they think they can find a buyer or not.
                                                • Components are anything that has magical applications but don't themselves have powers. Usually this means stuff that fulfills a specific criteria that can be used to power or shape rituals, or else items that have enough of a Resonance about them that they can serve as the basis for new Tokens or other magic items. The value of these items usually lies more in its potential than anything intrinsic.
                                                • It is entirely possible for collectibles to fall into any or all of these three categories. A pistol used as a murder weapon that is also a ghost's Anchor might make a powerful Token, but also has value as a curiosity and has natural value as from its Equipment bonus. The more categories it falls into, the higher a Goblin will likely value it.
                                              • Beats are a common stand-in for abstractions like significant memories, fates, potential, etc. Beats are a semi-permanent form of currency; trading full Experiences is covered in the next section, but Beats can represent permanent losses of memories, feelings, and the like without dipping into full Experiences. A Beat is typically worth 1 point of Debt.
                                                • Some game lines include special Beats that are only earned for specific activities, usually specific to a specific supernatural creature. Cover Experiences provided by Demon Pacts, Vitriol Beats earned by Prometheans as they progress on their Pilgrimage, Magic Beats acquired by mages...these beats might be worth a lot to the right Goblin. These Beats might also wind up being sold by Goblins in the form of Abstractions or Curiosities. Goblins are very good at appraising the value of the abstract and intangible, but they don't know everything, so the potential value of such things might go unappreciated. A goblin that knows the worth of such a special Beat (or even a full special Experience) might charge (or pay) 2 or more Debt for such things.
                                              • Curses are another common cost. These usually come in the form of Conditions and Tilts, ones that either affect the character immediately or after some sort of trigger event. It might be possible to buy items that place Conditions and Tilts on others...a love potion might give someone the Swooned Condition, for instance, or a vial of rainbow smoke might inflict the Darkness Tilt on an area. Conditions usually pay for themselves over time in the form of Beats, but you could make a deal where the Beat is actually part of the cost...instead of gaining a Beat when the Condition resolves, it covers a point of Goblin Debt instead. That allows you to get more bang for your buck, as it were. Curses are usually worth between 1 and 3 points of Debt, depending on how hard it is to shed. You could also get Conditions removed by trading them in to Goblins for debt -- this is usually how they get the raw material for the magic that makes Curse items. Beneficial Conditions, on the other hand, are more along the lines of Beats in a trade, as you're giving up the potential Beat and benefit for something else.
                                                • Persistent Conditions are covered under Permanent Costs.


                                              Permanent Costs

                                              The greatest and most potent goblin wares aren't purchased with Debt, for the most part. No, these sorts of costs demand a sacrifice. This is the proverbial "arm, leg, and firstborn child" sort of cost. Making deals like these can really set back or stunt a character, and should always be carefully considered...Goblins aren't big on refunds.
                                              • The basic currency of a Permanent cost is the Experience. All other costs in this section boil down to their Experience cost in the long run. An Experience can cover five points of Goblin Debt or the equivalent, if you have a lot of lesser purchases to make. There's no set exchange rate for Glamour-based consumable costs...those should be covered with Beats or Debt instead.
                                              • Merits are the most common non-abstraction thing traded by Goblins at this level. Experiences spent on Merits can represent anything from training to buying a literal skill set to a fortune enhancement. Merits sold to a Goblin aren't protected by the Sanctity of Merits unless what you're buying with them is also a permanent cost item (like other Merits, Skills, Attributes, etc.). If you want to pay part of your Status in an organization for a bushel of Goblin Fruits, that's your business.
                                              • Tokens and other permanent magic items (Fetishes, Gadgets, Relics, etc.) are worth their rating in Experiences at the very least. Mementos are an exception...Goblins usually value them as Curiosities and don't realize the true worth of Mementos as Bound Keys. Tokens are the magic items they're most adept in trading, though, and many of the other items they acquire might be turned into Tokens and sold at a profit. Characters who take the time to make their own Tokens can also score profits, since what they're really trading is time and a couple jaunts through the Hedge.
                                              • Skills and Attributes can be bought and sold through Experiences. The standard price is 2 Experiences per Skill Dot and 4 Experiences for an Attribute Dot. Use caution -- a Goblin might use a poorly-worded bargain to take an Attribute from you when you meant to sell a Skill, because of course they might. Dealing with a Goblin cheat to get what they actually owe you is probably going to be a Story of its own. You can't trade away your last dot of an Attribute, but you can trade away all the dots of a Skill.
                                              • Permanent dots of Willpower and Integrity can be sold, and if you've lost Willpower dots or Integrity dots, they can be purchased as well. A Willpower Dot is worth 1 Experience, an Integrity Dot is worth 2 Experiences. Selling your Integrity out can get you a lot from Goblins, but at what cost? What cost?
                                                • The Integrity equivalents of other Supernaturals may or may not be worth anything to Goblins. Clarity, for instance, is not something they typically care to trade in at the Permanent level -- Clarity damage is more equivalent to Debt. Goblins care about Humanity, Wisdom, Harmony, Pilgrimage, or Cover only as much as the person they're buying it from seems to value it, and typically its only something you can sell to them, not purchase (with the exception of Cover...it's rumored that a Goblin can sell the memories and relationships required to make a Cover, usually in the form of scavenged Icons).
                                                • Speaking of Icons -- Goblins love Icons, and it's their exception to buying and selling Clarity for cheap.
                                              • Dots of Wyrd can be bought or sold from some of the more powerful Goblins, if you are willing to make that deal. Prices on that are typically high, even beyond the 5 Experiences you normally pay, because it requires messing with the Wyrd directly. Goblins who can do this are rare, and those who are willing to do it are even rarer, so it's usually more cost-efficient to buy the Wyrd yourself.
                                                • Goblins can't even touch the Supernatural Tolerance trait of other supernatural creatures, though many claim otherwise (and as much as they might desire such a rare prize)
                                              • Contacts, on the other hand, are relatively easy for Goblins to peddle. They can sell any Goblin Contract at all and any Common Regalia Contract they know. Some Goblin merchants might even have weird supernatural powers from other supernatruals to sell as unique Goblin Contracts or Tokens. Goblin Contracts have a flat cost. Goblins typically sell Arcadian Contracts for the Regular rate and buy them for the Favored rate unless you haggle. This goes for any other Supernatural power they buy -- they aim for the highest price they think they can get away with. Dread Powers and Numen are worth a flat 3 Experiences to a Goblin.
                                              • Tasks and Forbiddances with durations longer than a Year And A Day are worth Experiences instead of Goblin Debt. If the duration is a Decade (ten years and ten days), the amount of Experiences is usually equal to the task's difficulty (1 for Minor, 2 for Medial, 3 for Greater). Lifelong tasks can be worth two or even three times that much, depending on your negotiating skills. Always, always get a duration for the completion of a task asked by a Goblin, because they will gleefully bind you to a lifelong task on the cheap if they can get away with it (though, in these cases, one can appeal to The Wyrd to turn such a swindler from a creditor into a debtor -- you still need to perform the task, but they'll owe you to make up for it). Only The Gentry can make or enforce Generational tasks, and even then it requires them to tie it to one of their Titles, so they don't do it often.
                                              • Goblins with the Miracle Dread Power can literally buy your Aspirations, Vices, Virtues, Touchstones, and other Anchor Traits in exchange for using Miracle on your behalf. In all cases, this burns out the slot...you can never gain Beats or Willpower from that kind of Anchor again. Selling your Vice doesn't cure you of the ability to indulge in it, just the ability to get anything out of it. Fetch can sell their Personas, but not their Shadow unless they want to fall apart.
                                              • How might you actually sell your arm or leg? Persistent Conditions and Tilts transformed into Persistent Conditions, of course. Prices typically depend on how difficult the Persistent Condition is to remove, averaging around 2 Experiences. Greater curses fall under this category -- see Curses in the Roleplaying Costs section.
                                              • What about your firstborn child? Selling yourself or other people into Goblin slavery (or to the Gentry) is possible, if reprehensible. The price of the person bought or sold depends on how much use the Goblin thinks they can provide. This is almost always an open negotiation, but is one of the more lucrative trades you can make. You monster.
                                              • What about your soul? Goblins typically don't know what to do with souls, but the Soulless Condition exists, so this is generally covered under Persistent Conditions.
                                              • Other, even more esoteric cases are generally handled on a case-by-case basis.

                                                  One major important point -- in my chronicle, hobgoblins can only use Active gates without a Key, and can't charge Dormant gates to make them Active (nor can they create gates). They need a Changeling, Huntsmen, or member of The Gentry to charge the gate, or else they need to have a Key (permission for passage is often a Key for the sorts of gates hobgoblins like to use frequently). The hobgoblins in my chronicle really like fucking with humans, so this limitation keeps them from getting into the human world as much as they'd like to.
                                                  Fun fact: RAW Hobgoblins and Huntsmen cannot create or open Hedge Gates naturally, only changelings and True Fae can create and open Hedge Gates.

                                                  I have a different limitation on my Hobgoblins to prevent a full-scale Hobgoblin invasion of the real world:

                                                  Hobgoblin Deprived - Instead of taking a -1 to Resistance rolls, they take a -2 to ALL rolls while in the Ironside. This usually is represented with them getting nauseous and sick while in the mortal world, which is a very unpleasant feeling and powerful deterrent to most Hobgoblins. Those determined to stay for long periods in the Ironside can seek out a special kind of processed Hedge Fruit/Medicine called Ironside Pills, which allow the user to stave off the effects for 24 hours. They are rare and pricey to come by in a Goblin Market.

                                                  Hedge Gate Sense - All fae can identify a Hedge Gate upon sight. However, knowing what Key to use still requires an extended research roll or a Kenning roll.

                                                  Huntsmen Hearts - Bastions become empowered by having the Huntsman's Heart inside of it, adding the Huntsman's Wyrd to the Bastion's Fortification rating. They are near-impossible to Hedge Navigate towards, unless you know which mortal has the heart inside of them, OR the name of the Title hidden inside of the Huntsman.

                                                  Oaths For Other Fae - Hobgoblins and Hedge Ghosts can be part of Oaths, as long as a Changeling or True Fae is the one who initiates it.
