    Summary of my impressions:
    • Needle and Thread are really fun in concept but, like Virtue and Vice, not terribly useful in play, nor terribly critical to how a character is portrayed or played. Touchstones, as they always do, suffer from the "Good thought process behind it, but bad implementation" issue as well. Maybe the better solution would've been to just scrap Touchstones and turn the Needle and Thread into Clarity armour, or ways to restore it, or both?
    • The Wyrd's longevity change just feels weird. In practice, it's not meaningful (Changelings aren't likely to die of old age), but the idea that the more powerful Changelings can be truly ancient just by being what they are is fun.
    • For pity's sake, get rid of anything that only kicks in at Wyrd 6+. There is bugger-all point in having a feature that only exists for a vanishingly rare percentage of games; most are not going to make it to Wyrd 6+, so anything that only affects that is largely going to be wasted word count. Either integrate them as features lower down (like Changelings starving unless their Glamour is above their Wyrd at every level) or don't use them at all.
    • (While I'm at it, why have Changelings starve from Glamour hunger when they are far more likely to die of other things in any scenario when they have 0 Glamour and can't readily recharge, long before they'd die or even suffer the effects of starvation?)
    • Clarity as something that can get damaged and healed is fun; it plays with a lot of what Changelings are. However, tying that healing to Touchstones is a terrible idea, for the same reason Touchstones themselves are. Having scenes happen "on camera" for 3-5 players means 3-5 scenes after an adventure in which 2-4 players are likely just sitting around. Party-splitting being a dicey proposition at the best of times, this basically encourages it. This is even worse in chat-based games, where longer posts can render scenes glacial in pace. And if it's happening "off-camera", well... What's the bloody point of Touchstones at all? There are better ways to encourage PCs to connect to those around them and, importantly, involve them in the story.
    • On the subject of Clarity... Putting something so difficult to heal the damage of as the primary way to be harmed in dreams is a fast way for players to be scared of dreams, when they should ideally want to revel in it.
    • The Court Mantle merits are awesome.
    • Cloak of Night is a Contract that every Stealth mechanic ever should take notice of. Might tweak it by allowing a +1 Glamour surcharge to affect additional characters?
    • Hedgespinning. I am very happy with this. It might get mechanically heavy in play, but the hefty Beat reward for Changelings getting inconvenienced and the rewards for finding things make it heavily worthwhile. The Hedge's active, slightly hungry nature is also nicely presented in this aspect, and in its participation in Chases.
    • I'm kinda meh on the Pledge systems. They seem a bit lackluster, except for Sealing.

    Overall, despite the majority of my comments being negative, the Hedge being so much more interesting is all I ever really wanted out of 2e. Much as I criticize, the problems I have are things I can fix on my own for my own games. The ideas in the Hedge section were what I was after, so I'm gonna say I'm happy with my pledge.

                                                        And I say it very much is appreciated in the corebook, thank you. Having actual baselines is, y'know, better than by the seat-of-your-chair guestimation. See the Omega Point system from Unknown Armies-that is literally a spell-design system, because creative magic with limitations is the whole point of Unknown Armies. Does that make it bad that there are already schools present in the core? Fuck no, it means you don't have to devote the first session to creating what magic you're using to begin with if nobody wants to fuck around with Omega Points.
                                                        You don't actually need an intricate build-your-own system in order for custom courts to work, or at least be significantly more approachable.

                                                        Alright some examples here:

                                                        Say I want to make a new mage order, I literally need to pick three skills that are appropriate. That is the only thing that mage orders give you that doesn't key off of some other part of the system. If I want a new order to stand as tall as its canon counterparts I need to add one merit. Eyeballing the merits that do exist, the most complicated order merit clocks in 220 words, plus another 40 for the dot that swaps between ministries. So basically 300 words should cover absolutely everything you need to implement a new order mechanically. If you don't want to write-up a merit, it has the rules for Nameless Orders, which is the paint-by-number mystery cult initiation with added option of rote specialties. It won't necessarily be as potent or unique a proper Order merit, but it should still give you something functional and distinct from its counterparts.

                                                        This incidentally, is what I'm hoping the Mantle creation guidelines will be like. Enough fill-in-the-blank mechanics that someone who doesn't want to worry about the math can still can create a Mantle that adds to a character in a way the canon courts don't.

                                                        If I go to werewolf, all I need is a favored renown, three favored gifts, and an extra blurb in the Siskur-Dah condition. The canon tribes are structured to have a unique proprietary gift, but there isn't actually a "common-uncommon" dynamic so having a tribe with "Death,Rage, Technology" as an affinity list doesn't break anything. And since they draw from a commonly available pool every new Shadow Gift that gets published makes designing a new tribe to be "distinct" easier. So about 100 words should cover a fully functional custom tribe, though obviously if you do want it to have unique gift that makes things complicated.

                                                        If court contracts were structured more like "unlock this third regalia" this structure could apply, though it wouldn't necessarily be the best for the game at large.

                                                        Now I can't say for certain that the underhill version was actually better in this regard because we never actually got to see what those court contracts looked like, but the structure presented was one generic contract, one contract with a "fill-in-the-blank" component, and one "other" contract. In essence, you only needed to write-up one custom power, which should put you word-count-wise in the same range as the above examples. If you do want to reskin, you only need to be able to find one example contract that fits rather than figure out which combination of seasonal contracts best fit your vision.

                                                        Now word count is certainly not the end-all-be-all of how easy it is to implement something. Strictly speaking the easiest creation method is probably the nameless order, which'd likely be longer than a new tribe. However, all the mechanics of a whole new mage pentacle could fit in just the contract list of one seasonal court.
