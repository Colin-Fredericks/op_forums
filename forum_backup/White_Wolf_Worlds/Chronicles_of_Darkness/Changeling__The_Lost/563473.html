          Huntsmen are a new antagonist that will be debuting in 2nd edition, filling a similar role in the chronicle as Strix fill for vampires and Idigam fill for werewolves. We don't know much about them yet, except that they are free agents who work for the Gentry to track down and recapture escaped changelings. From what we've heard, they will have some ability to change their appearance, or possibly shapeshift, into just about anything. They can also use Icons, bits of a Changeling that she leaves behind in her escape, in order to track the changeling no matter where she goes. In a very literal sense, you can run but you can't hide from a Huntsman with your Icon. But, the Icon can only last so long outside of the hedge, and eventually deteriorates and reforms where the changeling it is linked to first left it. We know that they will be truly formidable antagonists, but more because of their ability as hunters than their combat prowess. They're scary because they can find you anywhere and be anything, not because they beat face. We know that Courts use things called Approaches, which give Huntsmen some kind of advantage called Yearning if they go after the court's members on their own terms (for example, going after an Autumn Courtier in her dreams), and we know that Mantle can give individual characters their own personal Approaches. But we don't know what Yearning does yet.

                  Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                  Huntsmen are a new antagonist that will be debuting in 2nd edition, filling a similar role in the chronicle as Strix fill for vampires and Idigam fill for werewolves.
                  Because this has been misunderstood many times before, I want to elaborate on this statement.

                  "Antagonists" doesn't really do justice to the essence of what these title-figures are. The devs have used the phrase "story catalysts", but I'd describe them as the things that upset the story cart. You have a number of fairly standard stories for each game line. Into each of these, you can throw the title-figure: the Strix, the Huntsmen, the Idigam, the Fallen World, the Firestorm: and suddenly things skew sideways. The story isn't the same story anymore. A changeling story is about figuring out how to reassemble a self-image, trying to make the broken glass shards fit despite suffering from echoes of trauma. Into this, the Huntsman is about spiking your paranoia, exploiting your successes, risking all your psychological comfort. The original story is broken; you either force it back or you let it unravel and see where that takes you.

                  Imagine a story where you hear about a Huntsman in the Hedge. You do some research and realize the target isn't in your motley; he's an NPC. And he's succumbing (or however that works), on the verge of being snatched. It's a race against time! You have to find the Huntsman and murderize it first! And you do get there in time. And... you fall in love. And that love is requited, and... nothing. It decides to stop Hunting, because of the effort you put in, whyever and however its original reasons, how you convince it to do so, etc., etc. There's no betrayal of trust where it makes off with you towards Arcadia; it even seeks out ways to recompense. Sure, there are hitches; disbelief, distrust, and dismay from fellow Lost. But... is that really an antagonist?

                  I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".

                      One of the biggest shortcomings of 1e Changlling is it's lack of clear motivations for threats that Changelings face.
                      The biggest threat being Fairies. They make a great example. Their motivations range from obsession, to the lazy boredom. As a nemesis this makes them foreboding and mysterious. But at a ground level of design, Are they really the most apparent threat too a Changeling? Gentry could want ANYTHING. Sur,e they might be presenting themselves a physical threat. But is could all just be ruse to test the Lost for some mad motivation.
                      A good chunk of the supplements were spent defining ways Gentry were limited in scope to be actual threats a Changelings to could best.
                      Meanwhile. Huntsmen existed in core but were never elaborated on. There is the Great Hunt. Bun no defined way it affected anything.

                      So what are the Huntsmen now? A clear Threat with a clear Motive. It isn't just toying with the fae on a whim of fantsy. It is going to take them down. It's goal is to defeat it's target.

                      Of all of the Threats in Changeling. It is easiest to understand the Hunters and easiest to motivate a rally-cry against them. Gentry might be reasoned with. Huntsmen are not. Hobs migh be misunderstood. Huntsmen are not. Human's might just be ignorant cat's paws. Huntsmen are not. Fetches might just be bystanders. Huntsmen are not.

                      Another shortcoming of 1e was that the Hedge is too forgiving. It has the danger level mostly right. But the danger only lasted as long as you remained inside the Hedge. Huntsmen seem to fill a need to make traversing the Hedge an unknowable risk long after you leave said Hedge. It's not just what you leave behind, but also who might find what you left behind.

                      I am eager to read the full depth of development behind the Huntsmen and how they relate to the Gentry.

                                                  Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                  Let's not do this song and dance.
                                                  Awwwww.... .but they're totally playing my tune!

                                                  Okay, serious time. The titular characters are supposed to be some kind of "out of context" issue that does change things. It doesn't have to be an antagonist, remember! With Mage, we've got the Fallen World Chronicles. Promethean is the Firestorm Chronicles. Hells, even Idigam don't have to be antagonistic.

                                                  So, the entire approach of looking at titular characters as antagonists in the first place is flawed. Yes, strix and huntsmen are antagonists, but that's not a rule. So, we should be looking at this from another angle entirely.

                                                  Now, assuming that Fallen World is actually refering to paradox effects, these titlular things are what the games designed to challenge the status quo. Or call it a story catalyst, or upsetting the apple cart. The point is that, in general, we have context and situations in place for handling the usual. Invictus elder getting power grabby, or the Brood and VII come to town? We got ways of dealing with it. The five sacred prey? We know how to hunt them. The Gentry, we have methods. Seers, banishes, liches, scelesti, ephemeral foes? Pick your arcana. The titular events (and, even if we're talking people, their arrival is an event) are out of context. Its something that you can't really prepare for. It throws everything into chaos. Even if something else could serve, these titular events are designed to work that way, while the others are not. That's why they get the title.
