            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
            The Wizened? They gain agency through hard work and expertise. Shawshank Redemption.
            My thought is "Agency through (systematic) understanding, power from expertise."

            The Wizened are associated with craft-work, even the Oracles, Drudges, and Fatemakers. And, as everyone knows, how a thing is made has a lot of layers to it. But understanding the process and the systems of a thing, be it a book or a human body or Fate itself, you learn how to subvert those crafts. A Wizened is someone who probably comes to understand the game the Strangers are playing, and takes advantage of the flaws in the system. If anyone is going to deliberately use a Stranger's frailty to escape, it's going to be a Wizened, because he knows the rules of their game, and he can break them or twist them to his advantage.

                    Ogre's easy enough, so I'll do them too.

                    Agency through violence, power from might.

                    The Ogres are the ones who flip the game board. They might start it up with "I got a 23, what does that mean" and then punch the Stranger while he's looking up the result, but either way, someone's getting hit with a fist the size of a stump. Of course, might's not really the point alone-it's violence. An Ogre doesn't have to be strong to be violent, and violence is the great decider. If you can violate Them like they violated you, then how can they stop you? To threaten their lives and livelihood, to put the fear of death or maiming in them, that's Ogres. It's the abuse victim who goes nuts and stabs the guy 50 times and yells at them to leave them the fuck alone before storming out(and that abuse need not even ave been physical in response, it could have been social or emotional-Fiona is a princess who becomes an Ogre doesn't correlate, but that image does). They never needed to be deep stabs individually, so long as they can choose their own will by creating a dire consequence for any other answer.

                    Of course, Might just makes it easy. And when you have it easy, why bother with the social niceties?

                                    I don't think there's one answer to take from how a Seeming represents agency. I agree with Arcane's bit on the Wizened (which is great!) being the most likely to use the rules of the game to turn a frailty against their Good Folk -- it fits especially well with the idea that the Wizened are lessened; of course they know deficiencies! Still, I'd argue the Fairest stand a good chance of the tactic as well. While beating the Gentry at their own game has an excellent feel, I can also see a Fairest seizing on a frailty. They are the ones playing a game most like the Stranger's, after all, and are most likely brutalized for not playing perfectly. The Fairest are the closest of the Lost to being their Keepers, but they're not there. Even if they know they're close. That's important to them. And they may just use this tremendous insight into their own weaknesses with their human viewpoint to make their Stranger crumple for the lessons.

                                                    I'd suggest True Fae can be scared (or at least act the part). Some Darklings sneak away from their Keepers, but others frighten their Keepers into releasing them. What do the Fae fear? Loss of power, loss of titles, becoming lesser beings, having others gain dominion over them. The truly cunning Darklings spread whispers of the discovery of their Keeper's bane, weaving these whispers into the story of the Keeper's play until the True Fae cannot help but believe them. Desperate and afraid, these Keepers expel the Darkling from their domains before the wicked changeling can use the bane to destroy or limit the True Fae.

                                                    Simultaneously, the True Fae gorges on the passions and thrill generated by the whispers, gorging on the unrepeatable meal as the Darkling escapes. With its nemesis gone, the fear quickly fades, leaving the Keeper empty, listless and less alive than ever before. It casts its net out to find another victim to bring to its domain in the hope of experiencing such thrills again. But the Darkling doesn't care about any of that - she tricked a True Fae with shadow and misdirection, and if she can flee back through the thorns into the real world she may have a second chance at life.
