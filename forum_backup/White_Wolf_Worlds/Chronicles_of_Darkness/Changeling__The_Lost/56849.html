    Beast: what do all those mental minuses mean in character play?

    01-24-2014, 01:13 PM
    Here's a small quote about kidnap victims from the link:

    But slowly, victims surrender to powerlessness, something that is accelerated if the kidnapper shows a willingness to inflict pain, but also to withhold it. “Instead of being tortured, you [receive] kindness,” says Walch.”You are given a drop of water, you’re not beaten. You begin to develop feelings of gratitude. Over time it wears on even the strongest person.” The human need for affiliation asserts itself too. Being in the physical company of someone—anyone—is better than being utterly alone. “If this is your only contact, you come to value that contact, so that even when given the opportunity to run away or get help, you don’t.” That fact is important for the victims themselves to remember as they recover, in case they ever—as they well might—blame themselves even a little for the years they lost.

    Read more: The Minds of the Kidnap Victims: What Comes Next | TIME.com
    New to Changeling, I'm playing a Beast. I'm in a campaign where the Seeming was chosen by the GM, and by factors written into our backgrounds rather than selected by Player choice. My comments here are not dealing with selection of the Seeming, rather with all the thoughts I've had in making the Changeling PC seem more vivid from the ruins of the mortal she was before.

    I think the kidnap and training aspects of the Durance particularly reinforce the stereotype Seeming of Beast because you are mentally degraded by rules and option offerings.

    We know that Beasts have handicaps when dealing with mental skills they never had training in. They suffer negatives to these tasks. The rules provide that Beasts surviving the Durance are quicker of reflexes, faster to act on their instincts, and have a harder time reconnecting to the mortal framework. Beasts are very physical, and less mental than they may have been before they were Taken.

    challenge 1: locked into play of a PC that may be disruptive to the group, because now long term thinking, or just slowing down to consider analysis, is no longer your first reaction to anything.

    response: I don't think you have to play 'beserker wolverine' with the Seeming stereotype. Yes, you can be physical, but the nice thing about the Beast is how many variations are offered. Flyers, swimmers, climbers, and such are not all warrior based or implicitly violent. It seems equally valid to play off mythic variations of clever animals and gods.... and on the web you can find Beast Seemings based on trickster myth. Felines can be anti-social, canines may want a leader to assert dominance over them, reptiles and water based animals might seem cold, but hunger for their previous mortal connections.

    challenge 2: social opportunities are enhanced and/or diminished by the change, perhaps depending on whether local culture has a bias towards the Beast type (dogs vs snakes, say)

    response: more than Ogres or Darklings or other Lost? no, I don't think so. I think human culture celebrates the Fairest as the easiest transition, and perhaps the Wizened as the most difficult, but beyond the stereotype of first impressions, I don't feel the Beast is really at disadvantage in social matters with humans or the other Lost. I think Humans are quick to judge, and perhaps if the Lost have learned anything significant in the trauma of being Taken, it is to not judge quickly. Plus the Mask protects you from judgments on your change, so focus on how proactive your PC is for reconnecting, or how optimistic their attitude is. A 'glass half empty' person may be disadvantaged as a Beast, but that is not the Seeming's fault.


    Anyone else here have RP advice for a new Beast?

      Beasts aren't stupid. Beasts are creatures possessed by passion and instinct. As animals, they experienced senses and thoughts that the human mind could barely fathom and although their Durance was often full of terror and pain, Beasts are blessed with these heightened instincts. Their experience of the world is even more changed than the average changeling. Where a changeling of another Seeming has to contend with fairy kenning and their own delusions, Beasts also deal with echoes of being able to perceive the world through bestial lens. They can smell fear and understand the gossip of chipmunks. No, Beasts aren't stupid. They just have more information they have to deal with before they make a consideration. I imagine that, for Beasts, the temptation to just completely succumb to life as animal is a constant threat. Mortal humans are so dumb, smelly and noisey. They are prude and fear blood.

      Beasts make ideal social manipulators. Their heightened perception and infectious passion allows them to get all up into a person's interest. They can be sexy, compelling or frightening. Animal/Human hybrids are a very powerful mythic archetype ... associated with all sorts of mystical and sexual importance. Honestly, it would be a waste to play a Beast as a "berserker wolverine" ... there are better Seemings for that.

          On a mechanical level, I think of the minuses to mental skills as one of pour impulse control, or slight ADD. All changelings get -3 and beast are slightly worse off at -4. I think of it like changelings are generally twitchy and hyper aware of their environments, always watching for the Keppers to leap out and reclaim them--plus, distracted with trying to readjust to a mortal world that may be very different than the one they had left. Braasts are additionally always getting so much more immediate sensory input. It is real easy for the Lost to loose focus on logic puzzles and memory tests. If the character has a dot in craft or politics or whatever, then that represents enough training to say that the character has learned to focus through the sensory/emotional distractions, thus the -4 goes away.

          As a side note, since animals tend to be all instict and reaction, I assume that makes Beast types more likely to be more emotional. But giving them the ability to shift emotional gears much faster, as well.

              I have one beast among my PCs. He decided to play the beasts aspect as overly impulsive, trusting only your instics type of character.                   It might be helpful to come up with a few "instinctual" responses your character has to certain situations, something related to seeming, kith, durance, or just general personality.

                  For instance, I have a Venombite who is kind of...twitchy. The idea of getting close to people frightens him, and even though intellectually he knows that most people aren't trying to hurt him, he has a tendency to freak out when someone touches him. So, someone reaching out suddenly and unexpectedly for a handshake has a good chance of getting spiderbit. Like others have said, being a Beast doesn't make him stupid, but it's just a reflexive, unthinking reaction he has. He doesn't want to be hurt again, and that is how that desire ends up being expressed.
