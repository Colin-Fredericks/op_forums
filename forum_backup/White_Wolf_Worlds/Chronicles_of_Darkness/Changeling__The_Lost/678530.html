      They are specifically not as powerful as they want to be outside of Arcadia.

      Fae outside of Faerie are essentially Changeling's equivalent of Royal Avatars — they're still significant threats for any but the most experienced group of changelings, but they lack access to the godlike powers of their core being. Autumn Nightmares covers the behavior of the Fae on Earth and in the Hedge and dreams, though 2e's decreased emphasis on the Fae as direct antagonists may render this information less useful in the long run.

          In 1E, their power outside of Arcadia is determined by the number of Titles they possess, Actors(and Props and Wisps) in the range of Wyrd 6-10 respectively always. Their attribute/ contracts, and other scales are accorded to this. For more, read Equinox Road(With advisement from Autumn Nightmares).

          In 2E,....Well, Dave hasn't talked 2E Strangers yet, so I can't talk about their default mode, though my advisement for them is, mostly, run. However, in a maintained freehold, they are actively limited by the Approaches set by the Freehold, the Courts, and the Courtiers with a high enough Mantle. They must fulfill each one as is relevant. Most don't bother and send Huntsmen and Loyalists who can ignore Approaches. So, either ZOM, A G! or possibly really tripped up....or dangerously savvy.

                          There's more in this than might be thought. Let me break into it.

                          1)True, not everyone has all the supplements, and the standard, right now, is the first edition core book, which does not stipulate a Wyrd level, though examples are fairly high in there.

                          2)Strictly speaking still, the game's assumption within canon is that a Stranger's incarnations are immensely powerful. But as you say, there's not a clear line of explanation in just the core alone.

                          3)As always, a GM has the right to just do what they want.

                          4)Changeling has various levels of authority when it comes to Stranger creation, with the core, Autumn Nightmares, and Equinox Road all positing different things and the latter two written with the assumption of surplanting the core books' rules.. Equinox Road is the most defined and comprehensive, and so if we ignore the core book base assumption, it's the clearest authority, and that books says the minimum a True Fae can even be is Wyrd 5, and they have to be in a shitty, shitty place for that to be the case. The way this book is written is to strongly suggest it to be the default canon as soon as one owns it, which is creates a kerfuffle for the entire "Only the core book is considered needed" attitude.

                          5)Certainly within fandom discussion that uses canon as a centerpole for discussion and the basis for deviation, Equinox Road is treated as the authority above the core. Any conversation within the fandom quickly shifts attention over to that book.

                          6)Changeling Second Edition has a very....definite stance about this, with an emphasis on making them the big bad in the background, more menacing as an atmospheric element than an active piece on the board. I can't explain what exactly this stance is in mechanical terms, so I will simply say this- unless you are pretty damn powerful yourself or have a really damn good plan or argument for your freedom(and a way to get that argument out), your default answer when dealing with them should always fall on the side of running the hell away. You can fight a Hobgoblin, a Fetch, a Loyalist, or even a Huntsman, but only legends and lucky kids get away with confronting the True Fae when they move.

                          TL; DR, First Edition is a kerfuffle that heavily relies on the idea that True Fae are powerful but doesn't necessarily enforce it, Second Edition does not have weak Strangers. All of this can be ignored if you're willing to ignore canon, which is certainly the right of any play group.
