            Assuming they know about the materials, can a Wizened blessing turn Metals into their Perfected versions, like Gold into Orichalcum?
            Given that Mage doesn't allow unalloyed perfected metals to be made uniformly Lasting via spell and the Wizened blessing doesn't have a duration on its effect (to say nothing of the fact that the blessing doesn't have any requirements on what material is being turned into what material), I'd say definitely not on its own.

            You might develop a Merit that enhances the blessing's scope or get ahold of a rare Contract that enables it instead, but the Wizened blessing already treats turning normal mundane materials into other normal mundane materials as a Build Equipment action of the highest possible difficulty, and allowing it to make lasting specimens of a magical material that takes at least a couple dozen trips through other dimensions with no more difficulty than turning twigs into plastic straws feels firmly out-of-scope with a power placed equivalent to being very intimidating, spending Willpower on behalf of other people, treating nearby elements as extensions of yourself, merging with smoke, or being faster and more dangerous in hand-to-hand combat.

                                Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                This seems similar to Demon Pacts, is defined anywhere what counts enough as yours to be sold?
                                It is not.

                                I want to sell my capacity for aging, feasible?
                                Not without some serious changes in assumptions about how the setting works. Agelessness through a goblin bargain is typically a knock-on consequence of agreement for something else. The effects of time on mortal biology (or reasonable facsimiles thereof) are terms you agreed to by being born living.

                                But what if I want sell my lung cancer at least?
                                Selling something that is an impediment to you is more typically referred to in terms of buying relief. You'd be trading for the means of getting rid of it or convincing whatever creditor benefits from you having lung cancer to sell a debt they're theoretically collecting on by your disease, when selling debt is conventionally done to get reduced returns from the buyer rather than none at all from the debtor.

                                Or my marriage? The marriage case sounds interesting because if it is yours, it is also your spouse's, how much can you sell something that is shared?
                                You can sell your side of a relationship to something with the means and motivation to buy it, and that's managed in whatever way is contextually appropriate to how they collect.

                                Could a Vampire/Werewolf/etc sell its Vampiric/Werewolf/etc condition?
                                No hard "no"s, but see the note about disease and the note about relationships. Few if any goblins are likely to have the pull and gall to disinherit Moon's children or prune the family trees of the great Curses, especially given that if they were able to undo the Fae's handiwork in the template closest to their own metaphysical framework (outside the context of a unique plot device) it would've come up in the fiction somewhere in either edition.

                                The theoretical universality of fae trading does not override the fact that it's a market built on incentives for buying and selling. Being theoretically capable of selling personal possession of the worst luck in the world doesn't matter if you can't find anybody willing to buy it even for a pittance, because the handling equipment for various maladies and abstractions is not part of every goblin's toolkit and arranging for it has costs.
