    So it's like... let me get this straight.

    It's like when you're a changeling, you're an antenna. You receive information from the Hedge, dreams and Arcadia. All this information is real (or as real as it can be). If you make a conscious effort, you can "tune" yourself to specific information, and you can also tune out/off to avoid sensorial overload or distractions (I assume this comes with a greater or lesser degree of success and may never be fully successful). Changelings parse this information through their senses due to being still human-like creatures with mostly human-like senses and minds. So this ends up with different changelings "translating" the same info in different ways because of their experiences and particulars. And different changeling probably tune into different info as well.

    Finally, Low-Clarity changelings have a hard time distinguishing the info they receive from their Wyrd-tenna from actual reality, while High-Clarity changelings do so easily.

    I think this is how it works?

                    The thing is, hallucinations can actually hurt you. That's what, I think, has everyone confused (or at least me). A hallucination can give you a heart attack or a stroke, or create psychosomatic stress damage. And that's not even taking into account "psychic" damage, that already exists in the CofD and has precedent in gamelines like Mage (Mind magic).

                    Personally, I feel that hallucinations are powerful narrative devices that, when used correctly, can add an amazing amount of depth and emotional resonance to a story. That said, I'm choosing to interpret the new rules as less "we don't like hallucinations as narrative tools" and more about reminding a changeling that they can't actually run away from Arcadia and their fae side. You can take the changeling out of Arcadia, but you can't take Arcadia out of the changeling.

                                    A hallucination is a sensory experience that occurs in the absence of external stimulus.

                                    Many of the things changelings experience as a consequence of their Wyrd and must filter through their Clarity are by definition signs attached to external stimuli, and the supernatural worlds the Lost most regularly deal with are fickle and labyrinthine places where internal states are reflected externally and the past and possible/immutable future intrude upon the present, which obviates most otherwise-reliable metrics for determining whether something is or is not happening somewhere within the ambit of your wandering mind's-eye, which by definition is wider than the scope of your immediate bodily presence.

                                    Getting out of the scripted kingdoms of the Keepers meant losing your center and your place in the universe; when one describes a low-Clarity changeling as "not all there," it's not just a figure of speech — to lose the Gentry, you have to deal with the consequences of becoming Lost.
