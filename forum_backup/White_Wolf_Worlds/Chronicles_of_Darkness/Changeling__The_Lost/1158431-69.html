                For Environmental Tilts created by Contracts, how powerful is the effect? # of Successes?

                Overall, Contracts are pretty well written and balanced, but there are some outliers that may warrent another look.

                Some of the Wizened Seeming bonuses completely remove size restrictions. Does this mean Tattermedalion's Workshop with both blessings can blow up a balloon until it is a functioning Deathstar or other planet sized things. They can also change the appearance of the entire planet. Even barring the insane, this allows for buildings, monuments, etc. Shouldn't it have *some* size limitation?

                You run into a similar issue with Loopholes on Contracts that have a scaling effect based on the Glamour spent. Elemental Fury allows you to, with the loophole, blanket the entire planet in every environmental tilt simultaniously. Even barring apocalyptic uses, dropping *every* environmental tilt for free on a "large enough" area feels disproportionately strong.

                Changeling Hours is definately better than the playtest, but it runs into some serious balance issues. I don't see anything stopping you from freezing someone's shirt in time, effectively trapping them. I also dont see anything stopping an Air Elemental from trapping people inside solid blocks of impennitrable air. Changelings themselves can clash against this effect, true, but almost anything else is screwed.

                Hidden Reality is a plotbuster, as written. You can just conveniently find a signed confession of the killer or some other really easy clue to solve the plot. You could argue that a changeling can't create something based on information they don't know, but then how could they create carkeys with ridges in a pattern they don't know, or a functioning gun when they arent a master gunsmith?

                Stealing the Solid Reflection with both seeming blessings seems to let you copy fully powered supernatural creatures. Is that the intent?

                Whispers of Morning still allows a Changeling easy access to reflexively going incorporeal. Is that less of a problem in actual play than it seems on paper?

                    Originally posted by SoulGambit View Post
                    Some of the Wizened Seeming bonuses completely remove size restrictions. Does this mean Tattermedalion's Workshop with both blessings can blow up a balloon until it is a functioning Deathstar or other planet sized things.
                    No. The Build Equipment action, of which Jury Rigging is a subset and which Tatterdemalion's Workshop operates as, is still subject to the need for materials (not necessarily proper materials, but the Contract very specifically works in a way that requires you to be working with materials) and the limits of adding features, to say nothing of how a balloon is not a complex object and there is no "normal version" of a planet-sized object for the product to work as well as.

                    They can also change the appearance of the entire planet. Even barring the insane, this allows for buildings, monuments, etc. Shouldn't it have *some* size limitation?
                    Let's talk for a moment about how you're applying a Contract that affects a mundane item by mimicking some of a discrete actor's process to a thing that no non-hyper-exceptional changeling could possibly work in a scene, much less an instant action.

                    You run into a similar issue with Loopholes on Contracts that have a scaling effect based on the Glamour spent. Elemental Fury allows you to, with the loophole, blanket the entire planet in every environmental tilt simultaniously. Even barring apocalyptic uses, dropping *every* environmental tilt for free on a "large enough" area feels disproportionately strong.
                    "She may also spend additional points of Glamour" suggests to me that this comes after the Loophole applies, even before getting into how a Loophole removes the Glamour cost for "the Contract's basic effect."

                    Changeling Hours is definately better than the playtest, but it runs into some serious balance issues. I don't see anything stopping you from freezing someone's shirt in time, effectively trapping them. I also dont see anything stopping an Air Elemental from trapping people inside solid blocks of impennitrable air. Changelings themselves can clash against this effect, true, but almost anything else is screwed.
                    These are both solved by not allowing fiction-breaking things like "you can affect someone's clothes to get around the person themselves being resistant to the effect" and "air is an element that is nigh-omnipresent, so any power that affects an element can be used anywhere."

                    Specifically concerning the latter, we have two things to stand against it: First, that "air elementals" are more properly wind elementals or other things associated with air, like clouds or storms or gusts; and second, that the Elemental seeming bonus references "an ever-burning flame" rather than "an unstoppable fire" — an Elemental with Changeling Hours is going to be making breezes that keep blowing or mist that doesn't disperse, not invisible walls.

                    Hidden Reality is a plotbuster, as written. You can just conveniently find a signed confession of the killer or some other really easy clue to solve the plot. You could argue that a changeling can't create something based on information they don't know, but then how could they create carkeys with ridges in a pattern they don't know, or a functioning gun when they arent a master gunsmith?
                    Anything you make goes away at the end of the scene or when someone stops paying attention to it, neither of which is generally going to solve any give plot that doesn't rely on resolution that can be handled immediately

                    Stealing the Solid Reflection with both seeming blessings seems to let you copy fully powered supernatural creatures. Is that the intent?
                    Costs Glamour and Willpower regardless of the Loophole, has all its stats capped by your Wyrd, and will explicitly require at least one oath to another changeling to allow you to pull it off in the first place on top of only working for a scene.

                    Whispers of Morning still allows a Changeling easy access to reflexively going incorporeal. Is that less of a problem in actual play than it seems on paper?
                    I should say so. It's only reflexive if you pay Glamour and it's a Royal Contract.
