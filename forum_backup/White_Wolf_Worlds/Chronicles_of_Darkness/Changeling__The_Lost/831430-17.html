    Pledges
    • Sealing – As I understand, character can have any number of Sealings, she just need to pay in Glamour, yes? No roof top limitation here on instances?
    • Oaths – Do I get it right that character can have any number of Oaths also? Wording seems implying that any time I can make Oath – for motley, freehold, personal or any other long term dedication – I just can have my Oath on this.
    • Oathbreaker Conditon – The penalty of -1 on Social rolls with changelings is automatic? So that other Lost just see your Mien and understand on Wyrd level “Oh, he broke the Oath, I see it on him”? Or it’s more a gossip things – “You heard, this new Darkling in town broke an Oath. – Typical Darkling...”?
    • Motley Oaths – We have all listed game effects or only one is chosen with the creation of Motley?
    • Ensorcelled mortals – What happen to them? In 1ED it was specific Plegde. Now I do not see this. Fae-Touched and Fetches are Minor Templates and bit more powerful than mortal seeing changeling for what it is.

                        On Pledges: Yeah, Sealing penalties, especially ones you invested Glamor in/trick Huntsmen into making, need to be a little harsher in some areas. One Lethal isn't very much, especially for a Huntsman for whom death is "back to respawn." Unless forcing them into breaking a Pledge is (the beginnings of) a way to permanently kill them...?

                        Also, Bargains need to have a bit more mystical power than "Whatever I, as a changeling can already do", because Huntsmen aren't around all the time (just enough to be justifiably paranoid, but rules should emphasize there's more than their mutual game of cat-and-mouse). If Goblin Vows are anything like the Rites of Spring version (ie, freeform magic based around a concept), perhaps a die bonus/effective Catch (no Glamor cost,in other words) to completing the Lost's end of the Bargain. It'd also fit with the "Huntsman Cloak" effect; according to the fluff they see changelings under a Bargain not as mortals, but fae who aren't changelings and are native to Earth, ergo seeing one using their powers to fulfill a pact isn't going to trip their "wait a minute..." instincts at all.

                                        I'm currently not digging the odd restriction on Oaths. I'm writting up a character for a game, an Ifrit/Fireheart Darkling of the Black Rose Court. I'm taking The Love as his first touchstone, a boyfriend that helped lead my character back from Hell. Jay there might just be my character's The One. There's these wishes for marriage.

                                        Oaths are real, they're solid, they're meaningful. They're invested with hope and emotion. Parts of what makes the changeling is invested into each other. It changes and shapes you. That's what an ideal marriage should do - completing each other. But... you can't. Oaths can only be made by changelings, for changelings and with changelings. You can't actually swear a Personal Oath with your lover. And I find that really, really odd and dissonant. No, you have to swear a Bargain, which is just about what you do. Not about the heart, the soul, or the blood. This is creating an odd situation where it feels like your relationship is only a shallow one. That the only way to have a deep, lasting relationship is with another changeling. I feel like its being told that no, you can't get married.

                                        Anyways, that's my impression from making up a new PC. Haven't actually gotten around to playing yet, but its something that crossed my mind, and I thought I would share.

                                        On a related matter, mostly because it crossed my mind while reading the pledge section, where would other supernaturals lie on this continuum? What about the others that might be involved here?
