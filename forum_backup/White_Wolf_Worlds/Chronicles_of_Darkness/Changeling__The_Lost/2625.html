    How do you handle Courts in low populated Freeholds?

    11-04-2013, 11:05 AM
    As I stated in the title, I have no idea how to work a court system in a freehold with say, under 30 changelings. Most of my games have a freehold populus of around 70 to 120 changelings.

      This is something that I've been working on actually. At first I had a lot of trouble with how to construct it but I think that is because I was thinking of the freehold in terms of potential politics like I would when creating a Kindred Domain.

      But I think that might be a mistake for smaller freeholds (ie apparently most freeholds). I think it becomes easier (at least for me) once you start to think about it as a support group first and foremost. so lets take, a small example of 17 changelings that belong to the Crystle Center, a freehold in Little Rock, Arkansas.


      The demographics could be:
      Winter: 4
      Spring: 5
      Summer: 2
      Autumn: 4
      and 3 courtless

      and there lets say there are 3 more who are not apart of the free hold but live in the area so there is contact.

      The Freehold is still rather young, about 50 years old and was started by the Spring courtier Emily Gold, an Oneirophysic who visited from boston and ended up rallying the Lost in the area so that they had purpose and weren't so afraid. Because of this spring has always held the most power. And 3 it's members were a few of the ones that she found first.

      For the first couple of years, through contacts made it so that others could choose one of the four courts through a "neighboring" freehold in texas and from that they made the free hold (at that time only 8 members strong)

      The free hold has marginally grown since then but for the most part the courts function as more of a buddy buddy type of thing within the freehold while the ruler holds all of the power. The ruler is chosen democratically within each court each time the season changes so there is only ever one ruler. It's pretty much always been Gold during Springs.

      How do they work if they are so small?
      Winter: three of them are good friends with the political mindset of hiding. They pretty much provide much of the support of the freehold. They provide homes and places of hiding. there really isn't much funds but they do what they do what they can.

      Spring: in charge of meet ups and events

      Summer: usually relies on Winter for help (intelligence) but for the most part they (known as the twins even though they arn't related in any way) provide defense for usually hedge related stuff.

      Autumn: the ones leaving for the hedge most often, most of them, however want to help the freehold integrate better with society, on that end they focus mostly on fetches which many of the members still have to deal with.

      The courtless are all apart of the Oneirophysics and so are 2 Winter courtiers. The Health is a pretty big deal in their group.


      So that's how I would build a Small member freehold/4 courts. in a support group/club kind of mindset.

              My key setting has several small freeholds with similarly small Courts. Each Court has a major focus, not just in its approach to living but in its performance for the freehold. Generally speaking, since they have so much to do and fewer people to do it with, Court members tend to be recognized more for their function than for their titles, and some folks serve multiple functions for the freehold.

              The Courts end up working together fairly often just to get things done, and there are fewer cliques to cause strife and/or to run away from the main freehold with. Court gatherings are more intimate, their response times tend to be more immediate, and politics are more personal than group-based.

              The philosophical differences that keep the freeholds apart lead them to run their Courts rather distinctively, so it is more common for the Spring Court of one freehold to disdain the Spring Court of another than it is for people within the same Spring Court to turn their noses up at one another.

              Additionally, while two freeholds have seasonal rulers, one does not, relying instead on freehold elders who make suggestions that are usually obeyed if only because they have been free for so long. All three freeholds rely on non-changelings, mortal and otherwise, ensorcelled or simply pledgebound, for many services.

                      I'm running a game with a freehold of about 20 changelings. 3 Summers, 9 Autumns, 6 Winters, and 4 Springs. Including PCs. The courts end up more like small clubs or cliques than real organizations. Each court still has its agenda, and its way of doing things, but it's more like like-minded friends than anything else. The MG describes about how it's turned out for me: Everyone knows everyone else, so the relationship between the courts is more intimate.

                          Another interesting thing to consider is what happens when new changelings show up. Politics probably aren't as big a deal in these smaller freeholds as they may be somewhere like miami, but in a freehold with four or five changelings per court, one or two new changelings can represent a real shift in the balance of power. How that's handled sort of comes down to what the other changelings in the freehold are like but it could be interesting.

                              Imbalance. Our freehold is in a small area, a bunch of changelings were either wiped out or went into hiding when hunters started snatching them up. The freehold broke apart and in the end one person (summer court) essentially took charge and started a new one.                                       In my game, the Freehold had an initial population of 28 Changelings. I purposefully went for a lopsided freehold, where Spring (which had 11 members) had an incredible amount of influence even when they weren't ruling. Winter had a measly four Changelings, all involved in a single polygamous relationship. Summer and Autumn had 7 and 6 members, respectively. Summer and Spring both then supplemented their power by bringing in large numbers of ensorcelled mortals to help guard the local trods. Each Court has a ruler, and then usually a lieutenant/enforcer figure. For the most part, there's hardly any internal politics within the Courts, but a lot of bickering. When we started, Winter was just taking power. With only four members though, the King found that most Changelings would look to their own Court Rulers first before following his commands, and the other Courts stepped up the politics by agreeing to follow his commands only when it suited them. They usually tried to convince Winter to offer them perks in return for following his orders. Spring was particularly troublesome, with their own rule just around the corner and their general attitude that they should always be in charge.

                                      As others have said, when you have low numbers of Changelings then power can swing rather drastically over only a few Changelings more or less in a given Court. Giving one Court a lot more members than any other is a good way to throw in some politics into a small Freehold. The increased manpower from the ensorcelled mortals provides some interesting options as well.
