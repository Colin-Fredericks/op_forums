                                    Mostly I mean that hobgoblin deals tend not to be material trades — the relevant section gives two examples where the exchange is service-for-agreement. Following the second example in that section, Bob's trade of chocolate for Sweet Dreams didn't incur Debt because the train starts rolling on him when he comes back for the follow-up fix, just like the goblin cobbler's agreement to repair the worker's shoes. The sample court system built around a Goblin Market runs through a short list of valid "currencies," and material exchange is only one item on that list for a reason.

                                    Remember: hobgoblin deals accrue Debt slower than Goblin Contracts, but Goblin Debt eventually turns someone into a goblin with Goblin Contracts that they can use "for free" while they're in a state that allows them to make hobgoblin deals and freely change their goblin toolkit every morning. The advice for designing Goblin Contracts begins: "Imagine a problem. Now imagine a goblin. How does he solve it?"

                                    Maybe The Hedge will say differently as far as how Goblin Markets work with regard to Goblin Debt, but by my reckoning the point of the system is not to cover every possible act of exchange a goblin partakes in.

                                    Hobgoblin deals are made to keep people coming back. If that means neglecting to mention that there's unlisted side-effects to that night of pleasant dreams that you've got the cure for, fine. If that means offering a customer's first visit at a cutthroat price, so be it. The point is to get your hooks in them and extract worth.

                                                Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
                                                1) So Changelings get turned back into a human form when they first emerge from the Hedge, described as “roughly humanoid”, but what are the limits of that? If I wanted, let’s say a Centaur, is that roughly within the limits?
                                                The general trend is that the further from an ordinary human body plan you go, the higher your Wyrd tends to be; if you want to hew closely to that trend and/or lean into gradual body horror, I'd start with just having horse legs and progress to the extra feet from there.

                                                1.5) if so, how does the Mask handle the horse bits, since they should take up more space?
                                                Same way it handles the giant rack of antlers on the Hunterheart — people might notice that you have to take extra care not to bump into things while turning around or bump your head on doorframes a lot, but to a casual observer the inhuman bits will be interpreted in a way attributable to mundane (if notable) proportions.

                                                2) Can someone trade their memories for a services in a Goblin Market or to pay Goblin debt?
                                                Memories of your grandmother (as an example of "something dear to [you]" is specifically given as an example for what you would have to part with to pay down Goblin Debt, so yes, almost certainly.

                                                2.5) If someone has some memories they would rather be rid of, can they try to be clever and trade those memories and get something else, or is memory removal it’s own service?
                                                See the quotes from "The Market" — you can try to play the system by getting something for someone else through the Market, but price is a matter of need, so if you don't need the thing you're giving up, it's going to trade for lower worth than something you don't want to get rid of.

                                                There might be a deal with unpleasant fine print to it that would accept seemingly-unwanted memories as a valid payment for something substantial, but as I mentioned earlier, that's Step 1 of a hustle that ends with you losing the shirt off your back.

                                                2.75) Is it conceivable that a Mirrorskin might trade enough of their memories away, in order to help them cope, that they no longer remember anything of who they were?
                                                Sure is, to the point where it's adjacent to at least two offhand-mentioned example characters in 1e. Remember also, though, that the Thorns can offer this "service" as an incidental effect of taking a wrong turn towards a different goal; it's definitely ironic enough as a bad end to a journey to be comfortable with oneself.

                                                3) While looking though homebrew and conversions of kiths, I found one of Chimera that uses the basic Skin Deep Atavism from Beast to represent mutations. While Chimera now has a (cool) official kith for 2e, I think the idea of a constantly mutating kith might not be a horrible idea. What do you think?
                                                Run through the design process on p304-6 and figure out what you want the kith to be mutating for. Note also that the first piece of kith design advice in Winter Masques (which is from 1e, but still reasonably applicable in 2e) is as follows:

                                                A blessing isn’t a Contract. A blessing is a neat trick or a particular affinity, not a showy supernatural power or a dramatic full-body modification. There are exceptions to the rule, of course, but a blessing should mirror the fact that the kith is for a changeling, not a True Fae. Changelings haven’t been fully changed into the stuff of Faerie. A particularly dramatic change such as a Gargantuan’s ability to grow should probably have a tradeoff as dramatic.
                                                2e's version of Chimera gives you a limited version of one of the benefits of being a Hedge Denizen without the tradeoff of losing access to the benefits of your Mantle, and the cost is that you have to cycle through all the Goblin Contracts you know on a chapter-by-chapter basis before you can benefit from the same Contract Debt-free for another chapter. Replicating a highly visible and variable power from another gameline is going to be iffy without a suitably interesting counterbalance, particularly since most of Skin Deep's sample listing encompasses Athletics, Brawl, or Perception rolls or just having the right Merit.

And the Gristlegrinder has a lethal bite for free. Actually, looking at the two, Changeling’s trickery is more powerful, but with a glamour cost. I don’t really know what to make of this.
                                                        The Gristlegrinder kith blessing only works on things with a lower Size than you, apart from the ability to make bite attacks without a grapple, and even without a lucky roll a target who can survive long enough to knock the Gristlegrinder out from inside (with attacks ignoring Defense and Armor and Changeling being a game where setting yourself on fire or having claws are commonplace abilities) can allow you to use a Killing Blow to break free.

                                                        Ravenous Maw's equivalent to To Serve Man is the Satiety Expenditure effect as a prelude to using the Low Satiety effect for body-disposal. The Low Satiety effect alone in this context establishes that if you eat something, its presence is not going to be causing you problems later, given that its precondition for using it on living creatures involves subduing them and "success" in an ongoing grapple to consume them means one of you stops grappling the other; if they're able to grapple with you, they're consumed when they're dead or unconscious, which you can help along by using the Damage move on each successful grapple roll.

                                                        The takeaway, though, is that a kith Trickery about being able to swallow stuff that's smaller than the character but gives no special resistance to the complications of eating nasty stuff shouldn't be looking to an Atavism whose core basis is in being able to safely eat stuff that would otherwise really hurt to eat (including stuff much larger than them) as a mechanical comparison.
