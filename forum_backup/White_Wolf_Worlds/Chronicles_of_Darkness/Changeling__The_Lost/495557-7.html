                Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
                Seeming, at least in the perception I currently have, is a much less concrete thing. Depending on your Kith, you could probably pass for a given Seeming without one. You might be a lacklustre example, but an example none the less. For example, if you're a Seeming-free Draconic, you might pretend to be a Beast with just some heavy behavior acting.
                It does feel like it could be tricky to infer at a glance what parts of a Changeling come from her Seeming, what parts from her Kith, and what parts from other relatively superficial changes her Keeper induced. Like the example of the Keeper who's big on living statues and animalistic humans: all would fit that aesthetic theme, but for some, the Kith's mechanics would reflect those inhuman shapes, while others would have the bestial or sculpted look in their appearances, even as that look is more-or-less incidental to the Kith's actual mechanics and what the Changeling actually did there.

                You could conceivably have three Changelings who are beautiful tiger-people made of fire, and have a hard time picking out which one is a) the Elemental <insert "professionally good-looking" Kith> whose Keeper really liked animal people, b) the Beast Fireheart whose Keeper insisted on beauty in all her "guests," or c) the simply very beautiful, Seeming-less Skogskra whose Keeper just couldn't get enough of everything appearing to be on fire.

                If it's anything like 1e, each Changeling will also get at least one free extra specialty to reflect quirks of their fey bodies smaller than the Kith blessing. e.g. an animalistic Kith that doesn't focus on heightened senses (or Beasts or incidentally animal-like Changelings, for that matter) could use that extra specialty or two to reflect the improved hearing and/or vision. Obviously, these specialties aren't as impressive as a 2e Kith blessing, but between that and Merits, some Changelings could "fake it" or be mistaken for something other than they are without much trouble.

                        Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                        In werewolf the extra speciality they got morphed into an entire dot, so who knows.
                        As of now, we don't have anything like that. If people are interested in seeing it, I'm not against the idea. I don't think I'd do a Specialty, though. I've always found adding Specialties to be really superficial. It's typically a Specialty the character concept should have anyway, so why not just add a general Specialty? Like with Hunter, you get a Specialty in an Asset Skill at creation. But like, I don't think I've ever seen a character NOT get a Specialty in those Skills without the designation of Asset Skills, so it's functionally identical to just adding an additional Specialty.

                        I considered a Resistance or Finesse Attribute. Not sure though. Not given it much thought.

                                        Essentially all of them ended up doing the same thing to different degrees of effect. (Waterborn can breathe water and swim well for a scene with a point of glamour, but can't leave the water for that scene. Swimmerskins can hold their breath for 30 minutes and swim well for no cost. When you get down to it Swimmerskins seem the best at what they did, while everything else was a way to play the same concept as a different seeming.

                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                            Speaking of the Gravewight blessing, another note: Reaching isn't a thing for non-spirit ephemeral beings, seeing as it's specifically a way to operate through the Gauntlet, and thus should probably not be on the list of Conditions the changeling can add or subtract. Relatedly, Materialized applies to the ghost, and thus should probably not be on the list of Conditions if the Gravewight's target is the thing supporting the ghost's manifestation.
                                            I am also of the mind that the Gravewight's choices of Conditions should be limited to Ghosts. It would seem odd for them to create Open Conditions for Angels or Spirits.

                                            I also echo previous statements about the Razorhand and Aggravated damage. It doesn't match the design previous games are enforcing in terms of difficulty to achieve said forms of damage. Increasing the cost or creating more stipulations could help with this, or have it cause more Lethal damage using dice tricks or other modifiers.
