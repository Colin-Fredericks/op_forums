                Sorry for the necrobump guys, I find myself revisiting this thread after learning that the children of changelings won't be addressed in kith and kin in any meaningful way.                 So am I alone in this? Is that part of the game just not that important to the rest of you? What house rules have you adopted in your chronicles over the years?

                    Originally posted by BetterWorldz View Post
                    Sorry for the necrobump guys, I find myself revisiting this thread after learning that the children of changelings won't be addressed in kith and kin in any meaningful way. And well, if it doesn't appear in a book that's got 'kin' in the title, three years in, I guess it's fair to expect the CtL2e line will leave this aspect voluntarily unclear for the foreseeable future.
                    Kith and Kin was the Player's Guide. It's named for a phrase that includes a word that Changeling uses as a ludonarrative term, on account of old English words being a major contributor to Lost's lexicon, particularly in 2e (see also Cockaigne, galoshin, Bedlam, hobgoblin, and so on). I would no more expect the Lost player's guide to talk about changeling fertility from that title than I would expect Oak, Ash, & Thorn to talk about forestry from the inclusion of trees in its title.

                    Am I alone in thinking the ability to have children would be a crucial part of the character's lives and drastically affect changeling society globally?
                    We really don't need more supernatural eugenics in Changeling after Werewolf and Vampire moved away from specifically entailing it and Demon and Deviant have it as a point of liability or a potential basis for antagonist ideology, and the magical changes to the Lost's physiology being real means being automatically sterile isn't a requirement to lock in the drama of changelings who want kids not being able to have kids. (Even in 1e, childbearing was metaphysically unremarkable enough among the Lost that it was addresses in Rites of Spring's section on Clarity breaking points for extreme unexpected life changes.)

                    If the children of the Lost do have supernatural advantages, wouldn't procreation be encouraged by freeholds by necessity, be it only to even the odds against the Gentry and possible rival domains?
                    For much the same reasons 1e talked about when it addressed changelings bearing or siring children, no, trying to meddle in your membership's major life decisions in a way that messes with their self-determination (and thereby their Clarity) is a good way to break your freehold apart.

                    If there are difficult requirements (such as merging back with the fetch as was suggested in this thread), wouldn't that be an essential right of passage for many members of the courts?

                    And if the daughters and sons of changelings end up being wholly mortal, wouldn't that be a tragic dilemma many Lost would have to struggle with? Making the decision to shield them from all things fae or not, staying young as they grow old and die…

                    As much as I adore many of the new ideas that were brought in for 2e, the omission of this core aspect prevents me from getting a clear idea for the setting the developers intended to create.
                    My inference is that the developer's intention, as with many of these sort of things in 2e, is that it's not a consistent enough thing to make definitive statements. Some changelings lack the biomechanical qualities necessary to create progeny. Some changelings are simply sterile. Some are fertile. Some will want to have kids, some will not. Some of the ones who have kids will make babies who are as tied to mundanity as you could hope for in a Touchstone. Some will be touched by the Wyrd and draw worse attention than they'd normally get from associating with a changeling. Some might be the mortal equivalent of a scissor or 1e's fetch-spawn and break down pledges around them.

                    Changeling isn't Werewolf, which came into 2e from a previous edition of "werewolves can't have children with each other and the resulting would-be child is a spiritual nemesis that wants to kill them and everyone else"; previous material can still be used with minimal massaging, but Changeling is not a game that particularly relies on the biological children of its supernatural protagonists as a metaphysical talking point.

                    The world is wide and strange for the Lost, and covering all those possibilities is beyond the scope of the Player's Guide. You'd want another Night Horrors book for those sorts of things, because Night Horrors is the subline for dealing with weird rare-to-unique phenomena.
