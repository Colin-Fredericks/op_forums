            Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
            1) Is sound a appropriate element for a Elemental? (Made a concept for a Elemental Whisperwhisp without thinking about it.)
            Much as I wouldn't make an earthquake elemental whose element was "quaking," I'd recommend using the expected medium of the sound instead (most likely air, barring a particular sort of undersea durance).
            2) Would using red capsaicin from ogre peppers instead of normal capsaicin in pepper spray have a mechanical difference?
            ST's call. Maybe it takes twice as many turns to reduce the penalty, maybe it only needs one success to Stun a target, maybe it isn't meaningfully different in that use-case; goblin stuff is weird about the particulars of its application sometimes.
            2.5) Would spray paint count as a improvised version of pepper spray?
            Possibly, though I'd require a targeted attack to the eyes or reduce the penalty or both, given that spray paint does not have the particular chemical qualities that make pepper spray effective at what it does.
            2.75) if one had hedgespun “pepper spray”, could it do damage?
            Its classification as a weapon notwithstanding, I feel like the specific quality of "does no damage" means it'd either get a dice bonus to the attack roll instead.
            3) does the Parkour merit effect hedge navigation successes needed?
            The tricky thing about this is that the Parkour Merit pre-dates the Chase system, let alone the Hedge Navigation mechanics, and is largely ported over from the 1e version, which modeled chases as a contested Stamina + Athletics action; meanwhile, several forms of Hedge Navigation simply don't operate on principles for which Parkour is applicable. Make the call for your game and be consistent about it.
            4) Is it possible to add hollow merits to a wagon? Or have a mobile hollow?
            1e had Mobile Hollow as a scaled Hollow add-on Merit from Swords at Dawn (p92-3) with prerequisites in Wyrd and Hollow Size; considering that Hollow is now capped at five dots, you could certainly try and gin up a scaled (maybe 1-3 dot) Hollow enhancement following a similar progression (though I'd recommend modeling it using variable stats to plug into the Hedge Navigation mechanics instead of porting the 1e version, which doesn't have a terribly smooth gradation on account of it juggling three or four different metrics of what one would find desirable in "mobility."
            4.5) or have a token wagon that’s bigger on the inside?
            This is much less hassle, yes.
            5) Does the Ogre blessing work Hedgewall and the Field of Thorns from Thorns and Brambles?
            It's not the Ogre dealing the damage, so probably not.
            6) How powerful is the Notary blessing?
            That's a bit of an open-ended question, but: they can prevent characters from having to spend the fuel they need at least one point of to keep from starving to make the sort of agreements their society runs on, get perfect memory of its wording for as long as those agreements last, and can more easily get Conditions (including the likes of Informed, Inspired, and Steadfast) from negotiating, reading, or interpreting those agreements. It's by no means game-breaking, but it definitely gives them a niche to exploit.
            7) How does extended actions work with hedgespinning?
            This is spelled out on page 205 of the corebook: "Extended actions in the Hedge can allocate successes to Hedgespinning as well, but each roll must immediately apply its excess successes to shaping during that interval; they don’t accumulate over the course of the action."

                                Originally posted by Omichli View Post
                                When a Changeling enters the Hedge, does she look as she did when she was created by the True Fae in Arcadia, or does she still appear as a half-human, half-thing when she escaped from Arcadia? How does the Mask/Mien actually work in the Hedge?
                                The Mien is the true appearance of a Changeling. It is somewhat malleable, but real. It may happen that your Mien once you reach Earth again isn't the same as it was under your True Fae, but that would be the exception, not the rule. Most of the time what the True Fae manage to make with the Changeling is a half-human, half-thing, not something completely inhuman. Mantles, Entitlements and stuff may affect your Mien, as it is still malleable, and those changes are still all real changes to your true form.

                                Now, the Mask is mostly an illusion. It tends to conform to the human appearance you would have if never taken by the True Fae, with a bunch of adaptations as needed, but ultimately the Mask isn't real in any way. Non-fae see the Mask when looking at you most of the time, unless something drops the illusion. But your Mien is still the real you and is physically there even if not perceived. Changeling on Earth will see a superposition of sorts of Mask and Mien when looking at you, but that's not what you are, you are still the Mien, solely.

                                What happens in the Hedge is that the Hedge is one of the things that drop the illusion. There's no Mask at all in there, so neither fae see you as a superposition nor non-fae see you as human anymore, they only see your Mien.

                                    The changeling is basically putty in the hands of their Keeper, and can be forced into any type of form during their Durance. This includes forms that strictly speaking are impossible or lack actual physical presence. You could be stuck in a painting, have non-euclidian geometries, or be a song propagating through the air.

                                    When the changeling escapes, they become humanoid again, but with traits from the form they were forced into by their Keeper. This is their Mien and it's their actual form.

                                    Outside the Hedge, changelings automatically put on their Mask, which makes them look mostly like their pre-Durance selves. But the Mask is only an illusion that can be seen through by any kind of supernatural sense.
                                    The changeling can drop the Mask to reveal themselves to those who can't see through it, which has a truckload of various effects no one could reasonably remember during a game.

                                    Note that changelings don't see the Mask of other changelings by default. A changeling can make an effort to temporarily see the Mask of other changelings or to fortify their own Mask so other changelings can see it, but otherwise changelings have no idea what other changelings' Masks look like.
