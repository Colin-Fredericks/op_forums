                                        Originally posted by Xenesis View Post
                                        I think that's the tipping point for me.

                                        The Seeming Blessings and Curses suddenly make actual sense to me. Without that information beforehand, they just seemed incredibly punitive. Especially in one of the earlier seeming threads where I basically asked &quot;Why would you ever play a Fairest?&quot;.

                                        Now I can see the actual train of thought, I can understand why you'd actually want to play a character who'd do things and hit their breaking points, compared to a lot of the time in 1E where you never wanted to ever hit your breaking points if you could help it.

                                        I mean, it's a shame that this couldn't be shared first as I think it sets the stage and expectations of the whole thing, but that's the realities of writing!
                                        Yeah, it significantly changes the way we have to look at Seemings. Instead of one behavior you want to engage in and one behavior you want to avoid, we now know it's one behavior that's a guaranteed Clarity increase and one that gives you a chance to either gain Clarity or lose it.

                                        I really wanted to bring this up in the discussion about Darklings. There was an argument made that Darklings can't trust people without risk of their grasp slipping, and that made them unsympathetic characters. The thing is, trusting people makes Darklings uncomfortable, and it can cause them Clarity loss. But it's also the best way for them to heal.

                                        Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.

                                                        Definitely in love with this whole new layer and how it changes the narrative feel. A Beast may recoil at being shackled, but if they're strong willed, they can calm down and think their way out, and have the catharsis of breaking free, tricking their captor, or using their captivity for their benefit, and grow stronger because of it. Breaking points are an opportunity to overcome a microcosm of their Durance and the memory of their trauma.

                                                        I'm now imagining a scenario where Ziggy gets captured, and after the initial panic, calms down and meditates, thinking of it as an 'escape the room' puzzle game. He takes an inventory of his resources, works out a solution, and enjoys the catharsis of running free, possibly without spending the additional glamour Beasts need to magically brute force their way out of bonds. He gains beats, and directs the experience he gains towards a new dot of Larceny.
