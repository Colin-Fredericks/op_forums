    Entitlement: The Sorority of Stepsisters

    11-01-2013, 05:55 PM
    The Wyrd needs a villain, and I deserve a villain's fate.

    With thanks to helel and special thanks to TrickyBoots

    Every tale needs a villain, without conflict there is no plot. A good villain drives the story, and is quite possibly the most interesting character. She almost certainly gets the best song.

    Therefore, says the Sorority of Stepsisters, it is no surprise that the Wyrd is enamoured with villains. When the opportunity for a good tale comes across but everyone is just too damn nice, then sometimes the Wyrd creates it's own villains. A father marries and his daughter is blessed with two older stepsisters. They get on well at first, until a tragic accident disfigures both stepsisters. While they recover the daughters does her best to take care of them, as any decent person would. In time they start to take her for granted, until she is treated little better than a slave.

    The Sorority of Stepsisters doesn't try to fight the Wyrd, instead they bargain with it. They offer themselves to take the villain's role, and suffer the villain’s karmic defeat at the end of the story. Half way between an actress and a diabolical mastermind the Stepsisters commit evil so other's won't have to, and if sometimes their bark is worse than their bite; so much the better.

    It might seem like an odd idea, but it seems to work. When you look at a freehold with a Stepsister you’ll see just as much conflict. Look closer though and you'll see that the conflict is managed. The heroes prevail. The freehold is strong and united. All curses are broken. All the stories have happy endings. All but the Stepsisters’. It’s the price they pay for everyone.

    Titles: Sister, Stepsister/brother, Stepmother/father (older), Your Evilness (or some variant thereof)

    [Picture: A woman who looks much like Cinderella's Stepmother (Disney version) sits on a high chair. She looks into a sinister cracked mirror she holds in a right hand, in her left hand she holds a skull topped staff with flames coming out the eyes]

    Requirements: Subterfuge 3, Wyrd 3, Persuasion 2, Presence 3, Occult 2

    Joining: Villains are often asocial creatures, most stories only have one. The Sorority is an Entitlement with barely any organisation, any member is free to induct Changelings into their ranks. The formal apprenticeship can be as long or as short as the Stepsister wishes. Apprenticeships that lasts for any length of time usually require spending time as some manner of minion.

    Mein: A Stepsister's attire is defined by her role, the Sorority always dresses for the occasion. A Stepsister who is seen one day dressed in rags and a hag's unkempt hair can be seen the next day plotting with members high society while dressed in a conservative ball gown. Unless the story requires it the Sorority typically make no attempt to hide the fact that they are the villains. Dark reds and blacks are popular and Stepsisters often adorn themselves with skulls or other sinister motifs.

    When a Stepsister swears herself into the entitlement her fae mein shifts. The angles of her bodies becomes sharper and formally soft curves taper to sharp points. The colours of her body and garments become richer, deeper but also few shades darker. Stepsisters also develop a terrific sense of presence and can dominate a room simply by being there; their eyes are often emphasized by large size, scars, marks of power, or just an usual colour. Even when there is nothing usual about a Stepsister's eyes it still feels like there is, a stepsister's piercing gaze is a terrible thing to behold.

    Sidebar: An F in Evil

    The Spring Queen is celebrating the birth of her daughter. The Stepsister has planned everything out: She used her Contracts to make sure she never got an invitation, she turned up to the party and announced her intention to curse the child as her revenge, and five different Summer courtiers shoot her on the spot.

    Problem solved right? The Wyrd got it's villain, the Stepsister escapes using Villain's Fate, and nobody had to get cursed. Well no, it might work the first time, even the second time, but soon the Wyrd will simply stop seeing the Stepsister as a villain: The villain dies at the end of act three, the stepsister died in the prologue, therefore she can't have been the villain. Try it a few too many times and the one day the Stepsister will dramatically enter the throne room to see somebody else swearing vengeance, and her connection to the Sorority fading away.

    Some Stepsisters try to die in the prologue of really heinous stories, and make up for it with top notch classy villainy everywhere else. If they're really good they might even pull it off.

    Background: The Sorority like to claim that their members are martyrs who sacrifice themselves to the lonely life and inevitable defeat of the villain. All to protect some innocent soul being dragged into that role by the Wyrd, and the heroes from having to face a real monster. In truth only some of the Stepsisters have such a noble motive.

    A lot of stepsisters think of themselves, if not as villains, than as monsters. They look in the mirror and see the pointed ears and horns they carry from Arcadia and they call their reflection a freak. Others might have a more justified reason for thinking of themselves as monsters, they may be suppressing cravings for human flesh or have escaped justice for some crime in their past. Whatever reason they have for calling themselves monsters, some Changelings join the Sorority because they want to be punished. It's not a healthy attitude and suffering a villain's fate rarely provides the Changeling with what she needs, but such people do claim the title of Stepsister.

    But the very worst of the Sorority are every bit the villains they pretend to be. Not all Stepsisters try to play the villain with a minimum of actual evil, some dive right in. “Why it's not my fault I ate that child, the Wyrd was going to make somebody do it. In fact you should be thanking me for making sure none of you had to eat her.” Such monsters are easier to defeat than villains outside the Sorority, but though the Entitlement bids them suffer for their evil, they always seem to come back the next month.

    Stepsisters come from a wide verity of courts. Many of the old faerie tales are somewhat xenophobic with foreign villains and this, combined with the Sorority's tendency to have few or just one member to a freehold (and their tendency to get chased out of town) has resulted in the Entitlement spreading far and wide.

    In the Seasonal Courts a Stepsister is most likely to hail from the Autumn Court. The Leaden Mirror's mastery of fear and understanding of the Wyrd are both invaluable to a Stepsister, but many come from the Spring Court seeking desires they cannot fulfil on the straight and narrow. The Winter Court often baulks at drawing attention to themselves, as most villains do, but a few grow to like spreading sorrow just a bit too much and find refuge from their Court's usual justice in the Sorority. Stepsisters hailing from the Summer Court are nearly unheard of.

    In the Diurnal Courts the Sorority has found favour among the Court of the Moon, their may be more Stepsisters walking The Wayward Road than in all four Seasonal courts put together. While the White Hill approves of sacrificing oneself for others, it would go against the fundamental nature of the Diurnal Courts for a sun courtier to publicly declare for evil, and thus far every member who wished to join the Sorority has first joined the Moon Court.

    The Stepsisters have even spread as far as the Directional Courts and indeed solitary members of the directional courts can be found in seasonal freeholds (or vice versa), usually playing up cultural stereotypes and strange foreign magic for all their xenophobic villainous glory. In the Sorority members of the North Court are held in high regard, members join the Sorority to experience the suffering of a villain's life and to help others in the process. The South Court also join the Sorority, for it's courtiers seek to experience every sensation even those you'd have to be a monster to try. The ideals of the Sorority do not fit with either The Sword or The Trident; but though the Court of the East would rarely support an Entitlement where you're supposed to loose, envy can so easily turn to spite.

    Concepts: Self sacrificing martyr, true monster masquerading as a fake one, flamboyant masochist, closet sadist, hammy method actor, self-loathing changing yearning to be punished, chessmaster who profits from every defeat.

      Privileges:

      Mirror of the Villainess (••••)

      After the Stepsister commits her first evil act as a member of the Sorority the next mirror she sees will reflect the ugliness of her deeds. In fact her reflection is ugly enough to shatter the glass, creating a Mirror of the Villainess. This token is the source of a Stepsister's power, and a reflection of every evil deed she has ever committed. At first, it simply shows unattractive features like a crooked nose, lazy eye or acne, as the her list of crimes grows her reflections manifests the appearance of a hag with claws and grey fizzled hair.

      Once per story, and only when attempting to slip into the role of a villain, a Stepsister can take the evil of her dark reflection into herself. This has the effect of gaining up to Wyrd merit dots, however no more than three dots may be added to any single Merit. The Stepsister cannot gain supernatural merits aside from the following: Hollow, Mantle and Token. If the Stepsister takes any Mantle other than her own Court then she transforms all dots in her own Mantle into Goodwill, they will return when the effects end. Borrowed dots of Mantle do not satisfy the perquisites for learning a Court's Contracts, but functions normally for any Contracts the Stepsister already knows.

      Drawback: Every villain is doomed. The heroes always win. Not only are the merits gained useless when used directly against the hero, in the presence of the hero they twist to and turn against the Stepsister: Allies become bumbling incompetents, betray the villain to the hero, or are just plagued with bad luck. An encyclopedic knowledge lacks one vital fact, or turns out to be a hindrance against the hero's “street smarts”. A beggar woman gives the Hero a talisman that reflects the Stepsister's “staff of power” back at her.

      Examples: The Dame of Destitution has 3 dots in Allies (Police) from her mirror and pulls a favour to get the heroine arrested. She gets two successes, and so the storyteller runs a scene where two policemen try to arrest the heroine. The heroine gets +3 (equal to the Dame's dots) to all rolls to fight, to escape, or to convince the policemen to turn against the villainous Dame. Dame Destitution is comfortable sending policemen with Fate against them, because she knows the heroine is most likely to use persuasion, not the sword.

      The heroine is cursed with ugly looks, and can only break the curse by kissing Prince Charming. Harriet has two dots in Striking Looks from her Mirror of the Villainess and is attempting to destract Prince Charming, however seeing the heroine attempt to attract him with kindness side by side with Harriet flaunting her body Prince Charming realises that true beauty comes from within. The heroine, not Harriet, gets that +1 and wins Prince Charming. Just as Harriet planned.

      Action: Instant

      Mein: When displaying it's faerie nature a Mirror of the Villainess emits an aura of pure evil. As it's owner's Wyrd rises the occasional wisp of fire, smoke or brimstone can be seen to escape from between the cracks of the broken mirror.

      Catch: To use the Mirror without Wyrd or Glamour requires a sacrifice appropriate to at least one of the Merits you intend to acquire. Sadistically disfiguring someone prettier than you for Striking Looks, or cutting the heart out of an official to gain Status.

      Villain’s Fate

      Once per story a Stepsister may invoke a villain’s fate when she is about to die or go unconscious from damage. The Wyrd makes her ultimate fate ambiguous. She falls off into darkness, disappears in a puff of smoke while screaming, or her body is pulled into the earth. In reality, she is transported into the Hedge with as much health as had at the beginning of the scene. The Stepsister always arrives at a location she prepared earlier, usually her Hollow. If she hasn't prepared a destination then she cannot benefit from this Privilege, usually with lethal results.

      Using Villian's Fate counts as dying for the purpose of the story. The villain is dead, the heroes can now live happily ever after. Any merits granted by the Mirror of the Villainess vanish quickly (but not so quickly that she can't safely depart a borrowed Hollow).

      Villain's Presence

      The Wyrd notices the Sonority, more than it should do. If a prophecy says that a blue eyed blond maiden is going to break Prince Charming's heart and plunge the Freehold into chaos then, with a bit of hair dye and contact lenses, the Stepsister becomes the favoured candidate. The one who the Wyrd starts nudging to the prophecy's conclusion.

      This isn't a guarantee. The effect is actually quite small, but when a Stepsister is actively trying to hijack the leading role it can be quite helpful. (For more on Fate Storytellers should head to Dancers in the Dusk starting at page 55)


      Rumours of the Sorority.

      Swanning around in black clothing adorned with skulls, intentionally being the villain, that sort of behaviour gets people talking. Here are some of the rumours told about the Sorority.

      All that talk about necessary evil is nothing but a scam. The Sorority are wicked, every last one. Sure their powers make it easier for the good guys, but that's just part of the deal, in return the Wyrd agreed that they'll escape every time they loose. They'll always have another chance, with enough chances even a Stepsister might win, and we'll all suffer under her tyranny.

      What did Cinderealla's ugly stepsisters want? The charming prince, the hero. The Sorority is like a dating service man, matching heroes an villains. The more evil the stepsister acts, the bigger the reward the hero gets for stopping her. Then they meet up afterwords to share the prize.
