                @Dataweaver: Instinct works way better actually, I like that.

                Also, to everyone else, keep in mind that Seemings don't seem to be as... literal as they were before. Every kith can potentially fit into most (if not all) Seemings. (Re)Looking over what David's said, they aren't going to be something that's completely obvious, so it'll probably a bit more subtle than "You're a Beast have some ears."

                So to take the Muse idea and run with it a bit:

                The Wizened Muse plays beautifully complex and intricate melodies. You can listen to the same song repeatedly and always hear something new.

                The Fairest Muse plays what you want to hear, when you didn't even realize you wanted to hear it. You can listen to the same song repeatedly and always find yourself loving it.

                The Elemental Muse plays simple things, and yet in those basic notes you find infinite depth. You can listen to the same song repeatedly and never truly understand it.

                The Beast(ial?) Muse plays songs that seem alive, like a reflection of the soul of the artist. You can listen for years and never hear the same song twice.

                The Darkling Muse plays in the background, subtle tunes that set moods and guide emotions. You can listen to the same song repeatedly and never know what you heard.

                The Ogre Muse punches you to "Shave and a Haircut."

                    Hmm. Interesting. Let me see what I can up with using Earthbones, and what that interpretation could bring.

                    The Wizened Earthbones always seems to know just how to repair a building, even when the flaw that causes so many problems confounds everyone else.

                    The Fairest Earthbones smells of polish, and their voice is as rich as fertile soil. Their body is chiseled like a marble statue, and even smoother. Their presence always gets attention, regardless of occasion.

                    The Elemental Earthbones is like a gate that will only budge for those worthy. They are the staunchest of allies, and the most stubborn of enemies, refusing to back down, or break.

                    The Beast Earthbones always seems to be ready to hide, always ready to curl into the nearest crack in the wall or hole in the ground. If a suitable hiding spot is lacking, they can just easily create one through digging and effort.

                    The Darkling Earthbones is like a warped mirror, reflecting the sins of yourself and others, while they sit back, silent as a rock. When building themselves seem to reveal the lies of their owners, they may have played a part.

                    The Ogre Earthbones is a giant of brutal might, ready to overwhelm an opponent like a rockslide, and smash through anyone who dares to help their enemies the same way.

                    Thoughts? Darkling is kind of weak, but I had a much harder time connecting rocks or buildings with their paranoia theme(which I've latched onto, that and fear. Perhaps connections to haunted houses are in order?)

                                        To orient the Seemings to the idea of Agency, I wanted some poetic 'price' and 'advantage' in each description (not unlike a Contract, as it were). And yet we want to retain flexibility to access a whole lot of Kiths. So again, flexibility being interpretation of the 'price vs advantage'. Each player can explain the approach within a framework of the Seeming.

                                        So with the Fairest, imagine they are sucked into the Glory of the Fairytale Story. They warp close to Fae themselves, beautiful, uncanny, manipulative, and they carry away this veneer with them when they escape. The Fairest have to pull themselves back from becoming collaborators and believing it all. They fooled the Keepers, but they now resemble moreso the thing they were fighting. "My Manipulation will set me free."

                                        So with the Wizened, imagine they obsessed on the expertise that was giving them power, gained frightening skills, but became twisted in pursuit of that. They lost parts of themselves as they chased the secrets. They learned more than the Keepers could guess, pushing themselves into a love of arcane skills and craft that change them but allow fantastic ability. "My Craft will set me free."
