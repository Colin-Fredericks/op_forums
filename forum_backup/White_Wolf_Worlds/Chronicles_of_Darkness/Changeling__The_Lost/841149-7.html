    Originally posted by Deionscribe View Post

    Interesting concept. But the Blessing could use some polishing. Maybe even a little revision.

    If I may?

    Blessing: Runeskins are especially adept at acquiring and wielding the magic of Arcadia. Upon learning a new Contract, they may gain the clause of another Seeming, which can be in addition to the clause they may have an affinity for. Furthermore, they may reflexively use a given Contract a number of times equal to their Wyrd + 1 in a scene.
    Thank you for the feedback. Interestingly enough, your suggestion for a revision to the favored effect part of the Contract was exactly what I had originally thought of when I was first working on them. The kith's blessing has gone through several iterations over the course of creating them, and I decided to limit it to a single Seeming so that they didn't get what amounted to a bonus Experience every time they purchased a new Contract. I had actually worried that even with that limitation, it might be too much of an experience break. I had had some other ideas for it that ultimately I removed, such as a monthly ritual for a Glamour infusion or other dice effects for Contracts (9-Again, bonus dice equal to Wyrd, etc.). I settled on the target number reduction as a way of giving them a bonus to Contract activation that would still benefit those with Dual Kith to one of the kiths that have a bonus to activate a single Contract. Your idea of giving them reflexive Contract activation rather than an easier time wielding them is an interesting one, and one that I had never even considered previously. I'll need to give that one some thought after looking through the Contracts again. Thank you again.

            Tradesman

            "I assure you, she'll be a valued slave for your collection. And I'm offering her for the low, low price of my continued freedom!"

            In the world of the Fae, everything is conducted through bargaining and contracts. But making deals is dangerous, even for the Gentry. Changelings who became Tradesmen were used to conduct business proposals in their Keeper's stead, while allowing their master to reap the profits of their dealings. They bargained with Hedge Markets, Loyalists and even the True Fae themselves, until eventually they became experienced enough to cut a deal that resulted in their escape.


            Fairest: A merchant prince dressed head to toe in extravagant jewelry and top of the line clothing.

            Beast: A raccoon-dog who carries their wares in a large, fur covered bag slung over their shoulder.

            Darkling: A shifty street peddler who sells stolen merchandise in dark alleyways.


            Blessing: Their time spent in Arcadia has instilled the Tradesman with a supernatural talent for business sense. They gain +3 on attempts to barter with others. Additionally, they may spend a point of Glamour to add their Wyrd on attempts to appraise items.

                    Apollonian
                    These changelings come to embody the physical ideal of perfection rather than the aesthetic, like the heroic athletes of legend. Often fiercely competitive or highly ambitious as mortals, they were taken to excel at all manner of physical contests for their Keepers, whether it's cleaving a block of stone in two with a single blow, running for days on end without so much as losing their breath, or skittering catlike through the most deadly obstacles.

                    • Beast: A man whose features are of an animal that embodies sportsmanship
                    • Elemental: A golden Colossus endowed with the strength of ten men.
                    • Fairest: An attractive, short-haired woman who can outrun most people with ease.

                    Blessing: An Apollonian gains an extra dot in Strength or Dexterity, which can take her above the usual limitations imposed by her Wyrd. She also benefits from the 8-again rule on all Athletics, Brawl, and Weaponry rolls.

                        Okay, here is another one I've been working on. I'm pretty proud of it, but I'm worried that it might be a bit underpowered. Any feedback would be welcome.

                        Faerysue

                        Don't worry, I can handle this. I've done something just like it once before.

                        Most of the time, when one of the Gentry takes a mortal, they intend to change them into something very specific. Then there are those who wish for a single changeling that can excel at a large variety of tasks, either because the Keeper has an extremely varied list of tasks that must be accomplished or because the Keeper wishes to have a servant that can quickly adapt to any situation that arises. Towards this end, the Gentry choose mortals of great skill, adaptability, or potential to become their faerysues. The trouble with such an adaptable servant, though, is that there is generally nothing that can be done to stop her when she finally decides that she is ready to return home.

                        -Fairest: A beautiful and inspiring figure, inevitably with some unusual trait that it quite striking. The faerysue is such an impressive changeling that they inevitably inspire others to follow them, often beginning a mass exodus of a given Keeper's changelings. Because of this, all faerysues end up as Fairest, regardless of their actual plan to escape. Some may show some hint of what they might have become if they'd been a member of a lesser kith, however, such as a pair of adorable cat ears for one that might have otherwise become a Beast.

                        Blessing: A faerysue is intended to excel at everything they do, and their Blessing reflects this. A faerysue gains the 8-Again effect on all Skill rolls, and achieves an exceptional success on 3 successes instead of 5. For any Skill roll involving one of her Specialties, she receives the rote quality. Additionally, the Wyrd is so impressed by her awesomeness that it lends the faerysue a little more power than other changelings receive. By spending a point of Glamour, a faerysue can gain access to a Contract or Merit that she doesn't possess for the duration of the scene. The faerysue is only able to gain a number of these additional traits equal to her Wyrd in a given scene, and the Glamour spent on acquiring a new Contract does not count towards the cost of activating the Contract.

                                        Orc

                                        "Yeah, I drink the blood of elves, whatever. Now piss off before I start feeling thirsty!"

                                        As terrible as warfare is in the human world, it still can't compare to the glorious nightmare that is a war between rival Fae armies. Changelings who became Orcs did not simply participate in war, they lived for it! Chosen from the ranks of the blood thirsty and the adrenaline junkies, Orcs were commonly male, although there were occasional females seen among their number. The only opinions Orcs considered valid were those of the strong. Cowards and weaklings were not worthy of consideration, which is why they abandoned the army once they felt their Keeper was no longer worthy of their allegiance.


                                        Beast: The head of a boar on the body of a man or woman. Their pot belly serves well to mask the brutish strength coursing through their limbs.

                                        Ogre: A green skinned humanoid with porcine tusks and hulking muscles. Even the women of their clan were more than a match for human soldiers.

                                        Darkling: Gray skinned, pointy ears and a hoard of battle scars, this barbarian raided enemy settlements at night and returned to base before sunrise.


                                        Blessing: Orcs lived and breathed combat during their time in Arcadia, turning them into savage warriors. Orcs gain the benefit of the 8 again rule on all contests involving Strength and Stamina. Additionally, they may spend a point of Glamour to add +3 damage on melee attacks (weapon or fists) on its next attack.

                                                Damsel

                                                "If you expect me to put out just because you saved me, I'll show you what happened to the last guy who made assumptions about me."

                                                Some Fae like to play the hero in their realm, defeating the forces of evil and saving the people while being showered with praise. Unfortunately their alien mindset is unable to distinguish genuine acts of heroism from a common PR stunt. Changelings who became Damsels were taken from women who lead abusive or dangerous lives for the soul purpose of being rescued over and over again. During their time in Arcadia, they were constantly kidnapped, threatened, assaulted, or imprisoned from the world, only to have their Keeper come to their rescue. After being celebrated for their heroic deed, the Keeper would take the Damsel to his bed and the cycle would repeat again. One day, they decided to save themselves instead of waiting around for others to do it for them!


                                                Fairest: Her perfect beauty attracted the scorn of those vying for her Keeper's affections. One day, she took advantage of her position and staged a preemptive strike on the evil queen, denying the Keeper his chance to save the day.

                                                Ogre: She was told that only true love's kiss would break the spell that turned her into a hideous monster. But now she's done looking for romance, she just wants revenge on the one who cursed her.

                                                Elemental: A beautiful nature spirit kidnapped by evil wizards who desired her power for their own. One day, she decided to show them what happens when you antagonize mother nature.

                                                Beast: Isolated from social contact, she often found that animals were her only companions in life. One day she used her connection to them and became the savage princess of the wild that she is now.

                                                Darkling: Her Keeper trapped her in a continuous loop of watching her friends and family slaughtered by a tyrant who claimed her as his bride, only to have the Keeper avenge their deaths and free her from the unwanted marriage. That kind of trauma can do terrible things to a young girl's soul, like giving her the strength to murder her husband who was still exhausted from sex.

                                                Wizened: A life sized toy brought to life, she was the key piece of a play set orchestrated by her Keeper. Before her escape, she showed the puppet master why men often have a psychological fear of dolls.


                                                Blessing: Damsels were made to be rescued until they learned how to rescue themselves. Their blessing gives them the Rote quality on attempts to persuade others that they are weak or foolish. They also add half their Wyrd rounded up on attempts to maintain their Wits during unprovoked confrontations, even if they pretend to have done otherwise. Additionally, by spending a point of Glamour, they can add +3 to Subterfuge rolls for the rest of the scene.

                                                Note: As the name implies, Damsels are stereotypically female characters. However, there have cases where eccentric or female oriented Keepers have chosen male Damsels as well.

                                                        Originally posted by Deionscribe View Post

                                                        Glad I could help. And now that you mentioned them, letting them have Contract affinity with one other Seeming does make more sense if you want to avoid giving them free passes with new powers. The reflexive Contract use were derived from the Contract mechanics in the 2E playtest draft, which (I think) allowed you to reflexively activate a Contract once in a scene/chapter.

                                                        I looked over the draft, and it is actually something that can be done by spending Glamour anyway when fulfilling a Contract's Catch. It is actually something that can be done multiple times per scene anyway, as the limit is based on how much Glamour you can spend and how many Catches you can activate. I've been working on a different version of the blessing, though, and here it is:

                                                        Blessing: Runeskins are especially adept at acquiring and wielding the magic of Arcadia. Each runeskin has an affinity for a second Seeming, and will gain the favored effect for that Seeming as well as their own when acquiring new Contracts. Additionally, by spending a point of Glamour, the next Contract that the runeskin activates within the scene will benefit from the 8-Again effect. The changeling may only use this blessing a number of times in a scene equal to their Wyrd, and the Glamour cost to utilize the blessing is not obviated by enacting a Contract's Catch.
