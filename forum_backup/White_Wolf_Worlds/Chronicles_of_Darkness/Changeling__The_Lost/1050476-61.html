                                Originally posted by Unahim View Post
                                I might be able to borrow it from a friend... Could you mention what you think I'd find if I did so? Is it better than 2e for my purposes/might I be able to borrow from it, or do you mean it in a "Well, it used to be even more rolls!", which wouldn't be much solace I have to say. :P
                                It used to be that you literally couldn't enter the dreams of anyone who hadn't given you permission with a pledge (besides your fetch) and getting there was an extended meditation roll (Wits + Composure + Wyrd, each roll takes half an hour) that required a point of Glamour and sixteen successes (dropping to twelve successes and a point of Willpower for your fetch).

                                You had to alter the dream before you could act in it as more than an observer, rolling more successes on a dream-riding attempt (the least intrusive form of oneiromancy) than the dream's Intensity (successes on a Wits + Resolve roll, subject to dice tricks if it was a recurring dream or a dream of a memory) cost you Willpower to keep the dream from unraveling altogether, and most of the things you could do with dream-riding had activity timers measured in nights. More involved oneiromancy cost Glamour and required you to hard-reboot any dream that was going on already.

                                Oneiromachy (dream combat) was effectively resisted by two Attributes (since Dream Defense was equal to your highest Finesse Attribute and Dream Armor was equal to your highest Resistance Attribute; regular attacks used your highest Power Attribute and an appropriate Skill along with a bonus equal to your Wyrd, while Wits + Empathy + Wyrd could be used to attack by manipulating the environment). Since this was in an edition without Dream Health systems, damage was dealt to your Willpower and taking too much more than your remaining Willpower risked giving you temporary derangements.

                                Getting into dreams that you don't have a pledge for didn't show up as an option until the Skein was introduced Dancers in the Dusk — two books past the game's initially projected limited run — and the only ways to start getting anywhere involve giving nightmares to your entry-point dreamer, getting help from an appropriate dream-creature (who usually needs payment in Glamour or dire threats), or making an extended action with a required obscure Skill Specialty to learn who would be having a suitable dream in as much as a month's time.

                                Even then, unless you're using a guide, you still need to find the gate into the wider net of dreams, you have to make your way through a number of intervening dreams dependent upon how closely related your starting point and your target dreamer are, you can't use oneiromancy more involved than dream-riding and dream combat (both of which suffer penalties), and you can't just wake up like you can with dreams you have permission to (it takes hours of laying low to drift back to your own dreams, and if you ever run out of Willpower you're stuck in whatever dream you're in until you recover a Willpower point).

                                Personally, I'll take the soft wall of "you can get into anyone's dream, technically, but it will probably be difficult and expensive without a Key."

                                    Thanks for the detailed write-up, Satchel! I must say it sounds horrendous! But it was a very interesting read. ^^

                                    Mind you, I don't mind so much the part about totally blocking off dreams unless you have a Pledge or something. That's a setting/power level decision. But if it's possible to get into a dream, I must be prepared for my PCs to potentially wish to try it. And after doing many rolls to resolve a chase scene through the Hedge (a part of the game that feels similarly over-systematized to me right now), arriving at the Bastion and then working up to some sort of semi-convoluted teamwork roll (you're basically doing teamwork just to do teamwork, in a way :P) rolling a failure on that roll is... quite boring! It doesn't move the plot, any plot, forward at all. It's just "Well... you fail." and then blankly staring at one another across the table. :P

                                    I had one Oneiromancy session so far, where my players were infiltrating the dream of a neo-nazi. I had his Bastion basically be a sort of guarded entrance to a WWII camp. They got past it by knocking some of the "guards" nearby and taking their clothes, changing them a bit and pretending to be officers. I ruled it all as one "attempt" to get in, so one player rolled Stealth for the teamwork test (sneaking and taking out some of the guards), one rolled to socialize to read the overall mood/observe the functioning of the guards and give some tips for them all to fit in better, the third was the primary actor and rolled Subterfuge. They barely succeeded in "convincing the gate guards" (so, getting a paradigm shift) and got in. The moment they got in they were in D-Day among the americans, while the dreamer was a nazi general and pretty determined to win D-Day for the nazi party in his dreams at least. Getting to him was... challenging.

                                    It was an awesome roleplaying scene! But what if they had failed the roll to enter in the first place? It'd have been a pretty anticlimactic "You may not pass" moment.

                                    I got a pretty neat idea from this brainstorming!
                                    I'm thinking that if you fail, you should wake up the next morning (or "several hours later" in case this isn't happening at night) with a slamming headache, vague memories of having been sucked into the dream in a helpless state, and a Condition that blocks you from trying again for some time. However, if you Resolve the condition, you find out the Key to the Bastion. Resolving it likely involves helping the Dreamer in some way, perhaps helping resolve one of their aspirations or doing another big service to them.
                                    That evokes the "enter Dream with a Pledge" feeling of 1e a bit, is very fey, but most importantly: keeps the story moving and adds to it.

                                    I don't know if it's suitable for anyone else's table, but I'm going to try it out at mine at least. Once we can actually play together again. xD
