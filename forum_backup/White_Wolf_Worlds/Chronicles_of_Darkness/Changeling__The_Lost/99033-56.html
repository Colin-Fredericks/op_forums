    Session CXXVI

    Lorelei’s Diary

    Things seem to be settling down a bit… oh God, did I actually just write that down? I should know better than to jinx myself like that, particularly right before I head out to my first official meeting with all three of the other local monarchs.

    So by yesterday morning, my online classes had already faded into a sort of routine, and I managed to plow through the video lectures and assigned readings with an hour or so to spare before most people woke up for Sex Breakfast. I decided to do something useful, found a recipe online for a tasty-looking breakfast casserole, discovered that we had everything we needed in our well-stocked kitchen, and set to work.

    Fry up some hash browns and a couple of pounds of sausage, combine both with a bit of finely shredded cheddar, place in rectangular pan, pour in half a dozen beaten eggs (well, I doubled the recipe… there are a lot of us living in this house), and cook for three quarters of an hour. Easy-peasy, right? Well, I figured it was simple enough that even I couldn’t muck it up too badly… but even I was surprised with how well the dish turned out. Everyone was super complimentary when I served it up. Lorelei de la Rocha… cabaret singer, monarch, med student, and domestic goddess! The Pilgrims would be proud…

    If only the conversation at breakfast had been as delightful as the fare. But no, after a hasty admonition not to spread nasty rumors about Bulgarian Teleport Zombies before the monarchs could decide upon a course of action, somehow we started talking about Mount Everest, and a horrific place high on its slopes with the deceptively cheerful name of “Rainbow Valley.” Apparently it got its name due to all of the would-be mountain climbers who have perished there, leaving behind perfectly preserved corpses in various brightly-colored parkas. Ick.

    As if that wasn’t bad enough, conversation then turned to what the Hedge might be like in such a place. I honestly don’t even want to think about that too much… but the subject has a sort of insidious lure about it. I mean, would there even be a Hedge connected to such a barren, lifeless wasteland? The places I’ve been in the Hedge have always seemed like they were positively teeming with life, and the borderlands are supposed to reflect the parts of the world they touch…

    Anyway, we split up after Sex Breakfast to pursue our own activities. Calamity and Jacob drove to L.A. to harass some Vietnamese gangsters (I thought it better if I didn’t ask). Luna did her follow-up visit to that lost hiker, and determined that he had not been adversely affected by whatever Bulgarian Teleport Zombie had temporarily left the underground facility the other day. Missy and Jane continued posing for their portrait with Julia and Marguerite, and D and I eventually joined them for an hour or two, after I’d taken care of a bit of work that needed doing to prepare for that night’s show.

    D and I left the Dream House about four o’clock to meet Dad at his place for an early dinner. He had made it back into Malibu safely, and the house was thankfully free of booby-traps by the time we got there. Dad was also dressed in his regular clothes instead of the cassock-and-body-armor ensemble he’d been sporting the last time we met. Color me at least a little relieved.

    He seemed pretty certain that the Long Night had given up on hunting Dakota, or at least that they had given up on getting her location from him. I wasn’t so certain about that, so I asked once more if he knew the names of any of his stalkers. He gave one name up. He was pretty certain that one of his pursuers was a man by the name of Alfonso Esquivel. I told him that this would be enough information for Calamity to track the hunter and make sure that he had actually pulled up stakes and abandoned the hunt.

    “Please understand,” Dad said earnestly, “that Alfonso is not an evil man. He is simply a bit… overzealous. He has a reason to hate vampires. He was unable to protect several members of his congregation from the depredations of a few particularly vile specimens. I would not like to see him killed.”

    I gave him what I hope was a reassuring smile. “We just want to make sure he and his friends have really given up the chase,” I assured him. “I’ll ask Calamity to observe and report, not engage.” I knew she probably wouldn’t be happy about that, but I promised to try anyway.

    He also mentioned that the Reverend Alfonso Esquivel has uncanny luck. Like, little things will start going wrong for anyone who openly opposes him, seemingly without explanation. And he’ll have uncommonly good luck in any such encounters as well. So there’s that to consider in the coming days.

    After that I let talk turn toward other matters for a while, and the three of us had a nice dinner. Once everyone was nice and relaxed, I brought up the other subject I’d wanted to discuss.

    “So,” I said casually, “care to tell me more about this ‘feeling’ you had yesterday? The one that somehow alerted you to the fact that I was in a dangerous situation?”

    He shrugged casually. “I don’t know that there is much more to say on the matter. I felt that you were in danger, and it worried me, so I called you. When you did not answer, I tried Maria, Marguerite, and finally Julia, who told me that you were, err… ‘hunting Russian fish-men,’ I believe.”

    I chuckled a bit at that (D laughed out loud). “Well, we didn’t find any Russian fish-men,” I admitted, “though we did talk a lot about that possibility before we left. What we did find was… you know what, the details aren’t important right now. The threat is contained for the moment, and we’re all safe and sound. But how did you know that something was going on at that particular moment?”

    He shrugged again. “If you want an explanation, Juliette, then I am afraid you will be disappointed. I cannot explain it any more than you can. I simply felt a strong urge to call and make certain that you were safe.”

    “Have you ever had this feeling before?” I asked.

    He nodded. “Yes, though not so intensely,” he admitted, “and not for a very long time. The last time I felt such powerful trepidation regarding your safety, it came to nothing… or so I believed at the time. I later discovered that you had been kidnapped by faeries and replaced by a simulacrum,” he elaborated dryly.

    “Okay, fair enough,” I replied. “And one more thing… Dakota says that when the two of you were running from the Long Night, you took her by the arm, and she felt like her vampiric powers were… muted, somehow.”

    He smirked. “I am certain she did feel that way,” he said.

    I narrowed my eyes. “Young man,” I began with an edge in my voice.

    He actually laughed at that. “Not all of the hunters of the Long Night have such gifts, but it is not unheard of. While in physical contact with a vampire, I can make them believe that their powers are suppressed to a degree. It does not always work, depending upon the vampire in question.”

    “And are their powers actually suppressed, or is it all in their heads?” I asked, curious.

    “Does it matter?” he replied mysteriously.

    “I guess not,” I replied, resigned to learning no more. “So, these powers are… what, exactly? A natural ability of yours? Something you learned from your days as a hunter? A gift from God?”

    He smiled gently. “I like to believe they are the latter,” he admitted.

    Talk turned to less important things for a bit after that, and D and I said goodbye after a while so that we could get back to the Dream House in time to prep for the Friday night show. When we got back home, we found that Julia had been playing dress-up again. Marguerite was wearing a suit, which looked quite sharp on her, and seemed pretty pleased with herself. Meanwhile, Missy was strutting about the place in a gorgeous Regency gown, looking as happy and bubbly as I’ve seen her since we got back from Arcadia. Jane was following her around, looking like she was half in a daze, but mostly just happy to see the girl she loves in such an ecstatic state.

    I’m really glad those two are settling in and making a life for themselves here. I’d really hate to have to go back into Arcadia to rescue them again. I mean, I’d do it, but I’d really hate to have to.

    We had a few noteworthy guests at the show last night, including Halley, Mari Lwyd, and Joan and Andromeda. I’m sure my face lit up when I saw that last pair, and of course I spent most of my free time that evening with the two of them. Calamity – who arrived just after the opening curtain from her excursion to L.A. – entertained Mari Lwyd, and I later learned that the two of them ended up spending the night together as well. Jacob tended the bar and kept Halley and Molly entertained, as usual.

    Missy and Jane clearly had eyes only for each other that evening. Marguerite and Julia were disappointed by that – they’ve really taken a shine to those two since we got back from Arcadia. D saw how things were developing between Joan and Andromeda and me, and offered to console our brides for their loss that evening. That seemed like a peachy keen idea to me.

    Conversation wandered this way and that throughout the evening, most of it flirtatious. Andromeda spoke at length about her latest invention, which she described as being a cross between a massage chair and a sybian. I was intrigued by this erotic innovation, and asked if she needed quality control testers in a deliberately casual voice. But according to Andromeda, the prototype isn’t ready for human testing yet.

    Joan has never actually slept over at the Dream House before, and she was hinting that she would like to change that, so I offered to give the two of them a private tour of the house with a wink. Of course they accepted, I led them up to my bedroom, and the usual frivolity ensued.

    I think my guests enjoyed the novelty of staying in my room for once. Joan in particular seemed fond of the back balcony and the early morning sunshine. And I confess, seeing my white knight smiling and stretching in the sun, with hardly a care in the world, her soft smile reserved solely for me and her fiancée… well, I think it was good for my soul.

    I made breakfast again, though nothing as fancy as the casserole, just some eggs and toast this time. Conversation was more pleasant this time around, and after our guests had vacated the premises, I shared what I’d learned from Dad with the rest of the motley. I think Calamity is planning to track down the Reverend Esquivel later today or tomorrow, and she’s agreed to at least attempt to confine her activities to surveillance and report back, so we can decide how to proceed as a group. I’m personally hoping the Long Night has pulled up stakes and left town, but then I’m an incurable optimist.

    As for me, well… John has called a meeting of the monarchs, just like he said he would. We’re going to discuss how we should deal with the threat posed by the subterranean Bulgarian facility in the woods. I… wish I knew what the right thing to do was. I mean, I know that we have to do something; the chance that someone else might stumble into it and release what’s trapped within is too much of a risk. But sending people – my people – into that place to try to effect a solution… well, I’d hate to be responsible if something tragic happened to one of them.

    And still, with all of this heavy shit going down… I find myself obsessing over what I’m going to wear to my first official meeting with all three of the other monarchs. Is that sick or what?

    Lorelei de la Rocha

I made coffee and helped Lori tend one pan while she dealt with another. Once everyone had come down for breakfast we started talking, as per usual. We started off with the serious stuff first, the bunker. We all agreed to keep the story about the bunker under wraps until the monarchs could have their talk about what to do about the facility in question. Then somehow we got on to the topic of climbing Mt. Everest. I think someone had said something about “it was there,” as in doing something because the challenge is there. I said that was all fine, but said that Rainbow Valley is still depressing. Then we started talking about Rainbow Valley and the frozen bodies that litter the place in multicolored jackets and gear. Their bodies will never be retrieved because to do so would likely kill the person trying. The Valley is high enough up that oxygen starts to get really thin and if you don’t make it to base camp, you don’t make it at all. Like I said, depressing. Then someone asked if there was likely to be a version of Everest in the Hedge and I think we agreed that there probably is and that it would be a bad place. The Hedge can be beautiful in places don’t get me wrong, but it is also in some ways a twisted version of ‘the world.’ Arcadia can be beautiful in places too, but I don’t recommend visiting there either. The Lady’s realm is a frozen wonderland at times and an icy hellhole at others. In some ways I still miss it.

She had to go alone, since it would have looked strange to have a civilian there with her on official Ranger business. So, I went with Calamity to find one of the Vietnamese gangsters that had been threatening Luther’s neighborhood. She used oath and punishment to follow him and I let her drive my jeep. I thought that we’d stand out less if we drove the jeep than if we took her motorcycle. Plus it would be a more comfortable ride for me. Holding onto Calamity would have been tricky with all those glass shards of hers. Anyway, we tracked the guy we had a picture of to a hotel in L.A. They were well hidden, but I should have seen them before that. I used to be a lot more cautious. I think Rook was right. I have gone soft. That explains the welcome we got if they’ve had arsonists coming to town trying to blame us for it. Hollywood is getting desperate and it’s starting to piss me off. I understand the subterfuge. Trying to sow discord between L. A. and The Lady by the Sea makes sense tactically. Get us fighting on two fronts. It just pisses me off because my home is one of those targets.

I don’t know what she said to them, but I did see her put her hand “through” the window’s glass as if it weren’t there. That must have freaked those guys out something good. She said that she had delivered her message and they understood that Luther’s neighborhood was off limits. All that and she didn’t draw any blood.

        Lorelei’s Diary

        So I met with all three of the other monarchs for the first time yesterday. I expected to be nervous, and I was. I did not expect the immediate aftermath of that meeting to be much more stressful than the meeting itself. Nor did I expect the uncomfortable revelations of the next day to be nearly as troubling.

        I swear, one of these days I’m going to staple Calamity’s lips together.

        So after Sex Breakfast yesterday, I threw on a sober grey skirt, a white button-up shirt, and some sensible heels, and drove out to the modest hotel where the meeting was to be held. Dark Kimberly met me in the parking lot. Since she had been to a few of these meetings in the past, she gave me a crash course on the proper etiquette and a few opinions on the relationships between the various courts.

        “Don’t interrupt anyone when they’re talking,” she began. “If you absolutely need to communicate with one of the other monarchs when someone is talking, whisper the message to me, I’ll tell the other monarch’s second, and they’ll pass it along. If Charlotte feels strongly about something and you have no opinion on the subject, vote with her. The Spring and Autumn Courts have always had a good, supportive relationship, and it’s best to foster that if you can do it without compromising yourself. Augustine and Lowjack don’t really like each other, but that’s not likely to be a big issue today.”

        Then she gave me a once-over with a lint roller and tucked a single strand of hair back into place. It was a little humiliating, but I’m glad I had her in my corner.

        In the end it wasn’t as bad as all that. John had reserved a meeting room in the hotel, and business was conducted in a manner more reminiscent of a corporate retreat than the emergency meeting of a local government. Each monarch had a table to themselves; Charlotte and I faced each other across the central table while John and Augustine managed to keep their glowering to a minimum.

        Each of us had our seconds present as well. Tamara was John’s second, to nobody’s surprise. Moon was there with Charlotte, which was apparently a little unusual… Kimberly told me that she usually brings Madame Spidertoes along. I guess she was busy today. Augustine had Old Billy with him. It was nice to see him again; he doesn’t tend to frequent the Dream House and it’s been a while since we last saw each other.

        John called the meeting to order and let me take center stage for a while. I told my story without embellishment, including every detail I could remember. Well… almost. I didn’t mention Maria’s temporary regression, but I didn’t really see that as anyone else’s business; it wasn’t related to the threat posed by the lab. The other three paused to take in everything I was saying (even John, even though he already knew it all), and then we started to discuss our options.

        Augustine, to nobody’s surprise, wanted to send a well-armed strike force into the facility to clear it out. We all agreed that this was a necessary first step, but the question was what to do with the facility itself and the machine it contained after this initial clean-up operation concluded. Several options were bandied about. We all agreed that we’d rather not alert the government or military to the facility’s existence; none of us wanted whatever arcane technology the Bulgarians uncovered being picked apart in some top secret Area 51-type place. I half-heartedly suggested that we should fill the place with concrete and bury it deep… but the facility is so large that it would be prohibitively expensive to do so.

        I think it was John who suggested a small force of engineers and demolitions experts to follow the strike force in and make sure that the machine was shut down and decommissioned permanently. Of course the obvious candidates for this team were Andromeda, who has the keenest technological mind I’ve ever encountered, and Halley, whose Gremlin powers would be invaluable.

        We just had to figure out how to keep them from killing each other long enough to get the job done.

        “Andromeda doesn’t have anything against Halley,” I said firmly. “It’s Halley’s grudge – understandable though it might be – that’ll be the problem.”

        “We could bind her with an oath not to harm Andromeda,” Augustine asserted.

        “And we won’t send the two of them in alone,” John added. “We can send in one representative from each of our courts to keep an eye on them.”

        “Calamity is fond of Andromeda,” Charlotte opined, “and she has first-hand experience with the facility.”

        “Leaving only the Winter Court,” John mused. “I could send Twitch in… he’d be able to incapacitate Halley safely at a moment’s notice if she tries anything.”

        “Anton’s a good choice,” I agreed, “but if any of your people can speak or read Russian, that might be more helpful. Calamity could allow them to communicate with the ghosts in there…”

                                                        Julia’s parents had viewed the footage again, and come up with a few more clues for us. For example, most of the scenes aside from those where the actual ritual was taking place included at least one Theban rune, which I gather is a sort of secret alphabet employed by magicians. This rune might be scratched into a wall, or scorched into the floor, or reflected in a mirror, or what have you. They’d worked on translating the runes, but thus far they’d had no luck in teasing any sense out of them. Maybe if the footage was placed in the proper order…

                                                        But having said that, Julia’s dad is pretty convinced that the order of the footage itself is in some way important, like it’s been arranged in that order deliberately as some sort of code. Who knows?

                                                        We called a few other local experts to see if they had any input. First, Calamity contacted Viktor in L.A. to ask what he knew about kindred affairs in Santa Barbara. I think he was a little annoyed at her for waking him up in the early afternoon, but he still passed along a lot of interesting (and troubling) new information. He doesn’t know what happened in Santa Barbara in the 70s, but whatever happened, it was bad for the area’s kindred. Apparently there’s a sort of cult among vampires that operates as a hive mind. No one can read their thoughts; all you get is a Roman numeral if you try. And pretty much every vampire in the area became a part of that cult when this went down. The area’s kindred don’t go there anymore.

                                                        Jacob called John to get his thoughts on the subject, too. Lowjack didn’t have a lot of new info for us, but he shared his own experiences in Santa Barbara. He looked into the disappearances around the time that they happened, but didn’t turn much up. While he was there, he had the uncanny feeling that he was being watched… like, the whole time.

                                                        I called my dad, figuring that he might have heard something about the incident during his years with the Long Night, as the incident so profoundly affected the vampires that were their prey. He sort of wryly informed me that he was not currently fighting hopping vampires as he greeted me, and I had to chuckle at that. Once I’d explained the situation, he hummed in thought for a moment.

                                                        “I do recall something of that situation, though I was not in the area at that time,” he said. “It was either late 1978 or early 1979, I seem to recall. There was an outbreak of madness in the Santa Barbara area… a large number of people were committed to a local mental institution. The Long Night investigated at the time, but did not uncover anything useful. I believe the madhouse was shut down in the 80s, but the structure is still standing.”

                                                        I thanked him for the information and we said our goodbyes. I took this information to the others, and a quick search on the internet proved that dad was right: the old asylum is still standing, but has been sitting empty for some time. Apparently it’s a favorite site for urban exploring, though the websites warned that the place had a bad reputation. It’s supposed to be haunted. With everything we’ve seen over the past few years, we’re taking the rumors seriously. Plus… the websites also mentioned that at least two urban explorers had disappeared while investigating that place.

                                                        The good news is that the paper files and records are still in the hospital itself according to multiple reports. The bad news is that they’re in the basement, which is pitch black and supposedly haunted by something known as “the Crawler,” a human-sized creature which skitters along the ceilings and preys upon the unwary. Joy. The site is also protected by more mundane measures; there’s a high fence around the property and a security guard on duty 24/7.

                                                        We conferred briefly about how to proceed, and quickly decided that there was no time like the present to investigate a spooky abandoned mental institution. It’s Thursday, so tonight’s show should be able to cope without us. We don’t have college or therapy in the morning, so if we get held up out of town we won’t miss too much. And I think we all want to at least begin our exploration while it’s still light outside… even the darklings agreed to that stipulation. I did see Becky sort of clench up when I started talking about how “we” should probably head out… I know it’s not common for a monarch to take such a direct role in things like this, but I can’t let my friends head into danger alone. I’m just not wired that way.

                                                        I confess that I couldn’t resist having a little fun with her, and I mock-ordered her to stay behind and guard the Dream House while the rest of us went to Santa Barbara. She must have seen the twinkle in my eye, because she just sort of crossed her arms, squared her shoulders, and said “no way.” Of course I was joking; I’d have begged her to come along if she hadn’t clearly been inclined to do so anyway.

                                                        So now we’re getting dressed and gathering up our weapons and generally getting ready to enter the fray. Jacob has been overseeing the packing, making recommendations about what we should take with us and how we should arrange our packs. He strongly advised us all to bring breathing masks, as the place might be chock-full of asbestos insulation… but I put my foot down when it came to Maria. I don’t want her wandering around an abandoned hospital with a face mask on. I’d rather risk the respiratory damage in her case.

                                                        I sincerely hope that this place is just what it appears to be… a creepy old abandoned asylum. But who am I kidding? We’re never that lucky.

                                                        Lorelei de la Rocha


                                                        Jacob’s Journal

                                                        Tonight was not what I was expecting it to be. The show went really well. I mean, more so than usual. It went REALLY well, at least according to the audience it did. I will admit that it went better than most shows, but I’m not a great critic on what is good and bad according to the masses. I thought it was good anyway. Anyhoo, I talked to Lowjack while part of the show was going on. He said that he had dealings with folks in Santa Barbara during the time I was asking about. He said that he had to clean up a lot of messes with teamsters that had suddenly left the area. He knew that the local Changelings had left, but no evidence pointed towards a mass disappearance. At least there were no reported mass disappearances of people from that area during that time. I suppose that is good. The people in question didn’t go missing, they just legitimately left the area or were otherwise accounted for.

                                                        So, at the show the ladies did really well. Lori and her friends from Iowa did a song called “Single Ladies.” It was really well received, as I said earlier. Not long after their show there was a disturbance in the audience area. Julia threw a drink at Stasia and overturned a table in front of the other woman. Apparently she had been upset by something that Stasia had said about her religion. It seems that Stasia had called Julia’s religion LARP’ing and had implied that it was nothing more than roleplaying. We all gathered at the table in question and decided to take the conversation behind closed doors. We talked about differences in religion and that Wiccan was not LARP’ing. We eventually came to the point of the conversation which was the video tape. Calamity talked to Stasia privately for a moment or two and then we all got back together in the kitchen. We discussed options for viewing the tape. I suggested that the coven come to where Stasia’s equipment was to view the tape if it was not possible to bring Stasia’s equipment to the coven. We agreed that it was important that the tape in question not be damaged in any way since it was still important to the coven. We have the cloud share version, but apparently it’s not the same as viewing the actual tape.

                                                        Once the conversation was over, I went out to talk to Elena. She said that she understood where Stasia was coming from. As did I. Stasia had given up her religion when her parents kicked her out of the house for the crime of being gay. They had cited their religion as the reason that her lifestyle was wrong in the first place. Elena didn’t quite understand why Stasia would go so far. She had not denounced her religion when mom and dad had decided to reject her based on her personal choices. She knew that they were likely citing their religion as the reason why they rejected her relationship with Stasia, but she had not given up her faith in response. I guess it’s just a matter of personal choice. I’m glad that Elena didn’t decide to turn her back on the faith. I know it means a lot to her. It meant a lot to me when I was younger. I wouldn’t want her to have to deal with the loss of our family as well as the loss of her religion. I know it’s something that she turns to when things get tough.

                                                        **** **** ****

                                                        Today was the big day. We all went to therapy. Me, Calamity, Missy, Jane and De la Rocha. That part went fine. Well, to be honest it all went fine, but I was still nervous about the whole thing. After therapy it was my turn to have surgery to remove the foreign particles from my brain. As it turns out there were fewer in my brain than in Calamity or Insincere Marguerite but still some there that needed to be taken care of. Obviously I came through the surgery okay, otherwise I would not be writing this journal. I am loathe to admit that Twitch did an okay job on my brain surgery. Damn, just saying it like that. I can’t believe that I let him into my head. It all turned out okay though, so I’m not too upset. I still don’t trust him as far as I can throw him. I’m sure it was all made better by the fact that Andromeda was there.

                                                        After all that was done we went back to the house to discuss things. The main thing that we needed to talk about was the video of the ritual. Julia’s parents were able to get a copy of the VHS tape so we could view it. It is still out of order, but we discussed borrowing it so we could run it through Stasia’s machine and get a more refined copy of the video. The order on the VHS tape is still off though. We are unsure why, but figure it is probably to cover their intention or it is set up as a riddle of sorts. Maybe putting the ritual in order will actually make something magical happen if it is replayed. We don’t know. I brought up the inhuman woman in the video. Perhaps she was influencing things in some way? She seems to be in most of the shots in any case. Hopefully we’ll find something useful by viewing the whole video. I’m sure that it must be more informative than the footage Stasia has accrued so far, but we still don’t know for sure. Here’s hoping.

                                                        After we had that discussion Lori said that she would talk to her father about what happened in Santa Barbara in the late 70’s/early 80’s. She got back to us rather fast after talking to her dad and he said that a whole bunch of people were committed to a mental hospital around that time. The hospital itself still stands but has since been abandoned. It is said that the paper records of that time still remain on the hospital grounds, but that they are in cabinets in the basement. The problem is that the basement and other dark areas of the hospital are home to “the Crawler.” Apparently s/he crawls along the ceiling of the hospital basement and touches the head or shoulders of those that come that far down. That obviously piqued everyone’s interest, in a good or bad way. The place is also said to be haunted. It might be the work of this “Crawler,” but I don’t know for sure. There might be some legitimate ghosts in that place. Lucky for us Calamity is good with dealing with ghosts. I’m fine with the real-life physical threats. I’ll leave the incorporeal ones to her.

                                                        Lucky me, we’re going there this afternoon. I don’t know if I’m looking forward to this or not. I’ll make sure to pack several flashlights and glow sticks. I also suggested based on the age of the place that we pack face masks in case of asbestos. Lori took me aside and asked if I was seriously asking De la Rocha to go into an abandoned hospital wearing a face mask. I allowed that she had a point and that asking De la Rocha to wear a mask might be a triggering event for her. I still think that face masks would be a good idea though, but De la Rocha doesn’t need to wear one. We just need to be careful of kicking up any white dust. Here goes nothing. Wish me luck.
