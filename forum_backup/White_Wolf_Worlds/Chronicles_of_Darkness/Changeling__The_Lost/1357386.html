    Bonuses for Dance Of The Toys

    12-28-2019, 03:50 PM
    As the title implies, I’ve been making Seeming bonuses for Dance Of The Toys, as we only have the two per normal. However, I’m a little iffy on a couple of them, so I figured I’d post them here to get some feedback.

    Darkling: Shadows are a Wisp’s best friend, and act where her hands will not. She may evoke the contract on any object she can touch the shadow of, or spend a point of Willpower to do so with her own shadow touching, the device.
    Notes: This is a pretty situational bonus, admittedly, and I’m not sure how well it would work for a Fae Touched given that their effective range for controlling an object with Dance Of The Toys is 0 (meaning they’d have to be constantly touching it). However, I have always liked the thought of a Darkling being able to use the contract from a range. Not entirely sure if this should have an additional Glamour cost to it instead of a Willpower cost.


    Elemental: The Sprite invites the object into his domain, granting it the same comfort he has in his own element. While surrounded by an instance of his associated element, the object slowly rejuvenates from damaged sustained. Damage caused by Bashing or Lethal sources is repaired at one point per scene, but damage from Aggravated sources require a full day of exposure per point without being used and is only practical for the permanently enchanted.
    Notes: This is by far the trickiest bonus I have had to make, and even now I’m not entirely sure this shouldn’t be swapped with the Ogre bonus instead. It never felt right to me to make Dance Of The Toys work on an element instead of an object, or grant an object additional movement it couldn’t make (working on statues and such) since that takes it out of its niche and makes it far too abusable by bad actors. I want to try making an option that doesn’t use elements directly but alludes to them, but I can’t figure a good benefit beyond toughness or regeneration. Open to suggestions for this one.


    Fairest: The Muse infuses a part of her own personality into the mechanisms, inspiring them to bring out all their effort and become more competent. After a meaningful interaction with a permanently enchanted object, the player may purchase an additional dot of Retainer for it. This, along with the initial dot(s), is subject to Sanctity of Merits should the object be destroyed or the enchantment undone.
    Notes: This was the first bonus I had settled on, and was inspired by someone pointing out on the forums that normally it’s not entirely reasonable to be allowed to increase an enchanted object’s Retainer value since, well, it’s an object. However, I wanted to avoid making it an easy means of getting an object that could do a task with 10 dice, hence requiring a meaningful interaction with said object. Loving the Love Bug movies that I do, I am tempted to throw in a note about how Fairest often find their Toys to act on their own without a command when they get to a high Retainer level, but I feel that’s a bit overboard.


    Ogre: A Gargoyle is meant to stand the test of time, and always weather what comes of them. Objects affected by this contract gain 1 Durability, and inflict a -2 to those who would attempt to stop them doing their tasks.
    Notes: This is a bonus that I’m reasonably satisfied with, but I’m still not sure if I’ve gotten a good balance for it. Someone suggested I make this bonus instead grant an object additional Structure equal to the user’s Wyrd, but that would be a waste for a Fae Touched so I’m not too fond of it.


    Edit 1:

    * Reworded Fairest blessing to be more explicitly about buying additional dots of Retainer and not upgrading a supernatural power emulating one.
    * Added a Willpower cost to part of the Darkling blessing simply to avoid players from doing free tomfoolery such as standing on a bridge, extending their hand and commanding cars to crash into one another. I have played with those who have done far worse for LOLs with a free power; I will not enable it.

              I'm past the halfway point of coming up with 4 Seeming bonuses for every Contract, below are the ones I have for Dance of the Toys. I like my Darkling & Elemental ones, so you could check them out if you're looking to replace yours (and a note for the Elemental one you have: objects don't normally track aggravated damage separate from any other damage, so doing that may lead to extra bookkeeping, which I try to avoid in these benefits for elegance).

              The Fairest one you have would not make sense to the way I play it: since the device uses the number of successes rolled on the activation for attack rolls, I think it is fair to say it uses those successes for any rolls it needs to make. I also think it is fair to say that when the Contract says that by buying an object as a Retainer "it stays permanently animated", that we take that to mean the Contract's duration changes to "permanent". So when you get an Exceptional Success, the object's dice pool is 5, which trumps a 2-Dot Retainer's dicepool of 4 for actions they are good at. Specific beats general, so the specific rules for how the object is animated should beat the general merit rules here. That means that upgrading the Retainer merit from 1 to 2 dots essentially does nothing. If you were allowed to upgrade it to 5 there could be a benefit to it. I'd allow my players to upgrade the merit by default: it might be an item, but they could be "investing" more of themselves/their power into it. A 5-dot floating gun Retainer will hardly be overpowered, considering that it will be much more limited than a normal 5-dot Retainer. Simply by virtue of not having any hands, for one...

              I like your Ogre one though, it's very similar to mine but I think yours is slightly better.
              • Darkling: The Darkling may whisper the command to the device along with a simple triggering event described in no more than a few words. When doing so, the duration of the Contract becomes 1 chapter, and the device lays dormant until the specified trigger occurs, then executes the command. The Contract ends once the command has been executed, or at the end of the scene during which the trigger occurred.
                • Example: Used on a car with the command “When driving next to a cliff, drive off of it.”
              • Elemental: By spending an additional willpower point, the toys are infused with the boundless energy that drives the Unbound forward. When making an attack (or when otherwise called upon to make a roll) the object uses twice the Contract’s activation successes instead of the normal amount.
              • Fairest: The Fairest gains two free merit dots that may only be used to buy devices animated by this Contract as Retainers. They may buy any device they animate as a one-dot Retainer with these merit dots, rather than needing to roll an exceptional success to do so. If they want more than 2 devices as Retainers at a time, they must purchase them as normal.
              • Ogre: The Ogre’s toughness infuses any device he animates, increasing their durability by two, to a maximum of five. (If the object’s durability is already equal to or higher than five, it doesn’t change.)

                  This is where our design sensibilities differ. Theoretically a Changeling could have a device with a die pool of 1 while having an exceptional success simply by having 1 success on the roll, 1 dot of Mantle and Wyrd while their Mask has been dropped. This is to say nothing about what happens when you achieve an exceptional success by rolling more than 5 successes, which theoretically should result in the permanent enchantment having a larger die pool than 5 successes.

                  Simply put, this reads to me as requiring a lot of bookkeeping unless you make it a ubiquitous die pool of 5 no matter how you got the permanent enchantment, which makes scouring the Mask the ideal method of acquisition.

                  However, I do have an answer from Meghan that sheds some light on why I went the route I did;

                  Originally posted by Meghan Fitzgerald View Post
                  Correct--it no longer requires an invocation roll (or Glamour/Loophole), and the animated device can go wherever. It functions just like any other Retainer, except it's not a person. You still retain the Seeming benefits. The main benefit to spending the Experience to buy it as a Merit is that it won't cost you any Glamour and it will stay animated even if it gets out of range; it'll just keep doing the last thing you told it to do until it can't anymore or you give it another command. And Changeling players are always coming up with creative ways to use what they've got, so I'm sure there are other ones I'm not thinking of right now.
                  The point in question is about how the device functions “just like any other Retainer, except it’s not a person”. Since a Retainer’s die pool is twice their dot rating for their field of expertise and just their dot rating for anything outside it, this makes me think that one doing commands for their Changeling falls into that range. If this results in the die pool going lower than it was initially activating the contract, it is offset by the fact that the enchantment works outside the contract’s normal range. However, by default it seems odd to be able to increase the power of the enchantment when the device only does what it’s told - even with the Beast’s seeming benefit, it still only changes how it interprets and executes the orders. How would the Changeling go about increasing the magic and the competence of the device when the closest comparable magical objects, Tokens, don’t (currently) have a similar means to increase their power beyond possibly Hedgespun Items?

                  Granted, one could work by making an exceptional success on the contract on the same object over and over again to do this, but this feels like a lackadaisical method of forcibly gaining a high dot value Retainer.

This is more my sense of logic in why only the Fairest and those taught by them have the ability to increase their enchanted object Retainer’s competence when it otherwise has no mind about it.

Darkling I fear runs exceptionally close to the Beast’s benefit as it stands, what with both allowing commands to trigger on a particular circumstance. Still, it’s not a bad concept to hide a command deep within something to be triggered later.

                  The Elemental bonus is nice and focused for combat, which Elementals seem to trend towards. Effective language for what it does and clear in how to use it. It’s not what I’d want for my bonus, though, since if anything it’s a bit too effective. However, with this bonus it means that even 3 successes to enchant an object turns it into an above average if somewhat limited fighter, and a permanent enchantment under your own interpretation makes it a 10 dice pool attacker, normally well above what a Changeling has on their own without willpower and contracts to fund their attacks. Unless commanded otherwise, it will do this for anyone who has matched the Muse’s own gentle strokes or fear-instilling strikes.                           Concerning the Exceptional Success Effect

                          The big charge for my interpretation is the context of the answer, for which we must go to the question I posited, that being below:

                          Originally posted by Taidragon View Post
                          I am having some difficulty appreciating the change that occurs in an object when it becomes permanently animated as a one dot Retainer via Dance Of The Toys. Does it no longer require an activation roll for accepting commands? Will it follow commands (or a single command) if it leaves the normal control area? Would it still benefit from any Seeming bonuses such as the ability to float at walking speed if a Wizened animated it?

                          Basically I want to appreciate the benefit of spending an experience on something as opposed to evoking the contract over and over again to almost certainly get a better benefit than a Retainer’s 2 dice to fulfill a task.
                          For a question regarding what the benefit is for taking a one dot Retainer is instead of evoking the contract to almost inevitably get a larger bonus, Meghan didn't seem to be particularly forthright in correcting my misapprehension about having to choose between a large Success based die pool or the Retainer's benefit of "has a die pool twice the Retainer's dot rating for something within their wheelhouse". While it's not impossible that she simply missed that aspect of the question and thus wasn't answering it, it's not as though I buried it what with it being at the very bottom of the question quote in its own sentence.

                          Still, I agree that this is probably something best left to a difference of opinion lest the thread burns from an argument over a particular facet of a single power.


                          On your Darkling Benefit

                          Sad to say, but I still don't think it's separate enough from an exceptionally succeeding Beast benefit for my liking. While I agree that complementary benefits are very nice (Tatterdemalion's Workshop and Shared Burden are fine with one benefit or the other, but are king with both) your Darkling benefit feels like it's simply allowing for the exceptional success to be done easier with less benefits for doing so.

                          I will say that it's fine; it's functional, clearly written out and unlikely to break the bank like mine potentially could (without the activation cost) for a Darkling in a dim space with a flashlight. However, the capacity for what a Darkling could do is quite potent with enchanting a device, such as perhaps working on objects without moving parts but a definite function, like a smartphone, or allowing the Changeling to possess the object instead to use it as a temporary body. It's why I opted to go for range for a touch based power, much like for a Wizened with Touch Of Wrath. However, I won't say your idea for this power is bad, just a bit limited.

                          On your Elemental Bonus

                          As I noted before, your benefit works alright if it has an activation cost to prevent the enchantment from making a player obsolete in using the device themselves. Limiting the benefit to simply a +2 or +3 to the device's die rolls works well since it means even a Fae Touched could make use of the benefit. Even without the ability to permanently increase a permanently enchanted device's Retainer rating and its die pool normally being twice the Retainer's rating, this gives a nice boost to the device's capabilities. Works well, just not enough to make me replace my own with it.


                          Concerning your Fairest Bonus

                          You make a fair point there (no pun intended), but I'm still not fond of it simply because it gives free merits as opposed to... well, something that plays around with the contract itself. It gives permanence without cost; whenever the other contracts and their bonuses give access to merits, it's inevitably temporary or requires an experience to keep it around (and often has a requirement to use them even then). As with the Darkling benefit above, it's functional and aside from that one niggle it's written out well enough not to break the game by having it in one, but not what I feel really encapsulates what a Fairest can do with this kind of power. Difference of opinion reigns here.


                          Concerning my Fairest Bonus

                          The main benefit of the bonus is that it allows the object to not just help the user but benefit others who know how to ask the device to help them during the scene, that being kind/terrible and asking for their assistance. It goes beyond the Beast benefit too, since "Help/work anyone with a white shirt" is likely outside of the capabilities of an animal style intelligence when "help" is highly context sensitive. An enchanted car can "help" someone numerous ways, be it by getting them in out of the cold and warmed up, driving them down to the nearest hospital or simply playing a pleasant tune via the radio while they cry their heart out from a particularly bad revelation, but it seems unlikely that "animal-like intelligence, allowing [the original contract user] to give it multiple or more complex commands to follow" would cover the ability to allow someone else to get the car to help them outside of a very particular instance of use.

                          The benefit is meant to convey that if the device could help with something and it's not being commanded by the contract's power itself, it is actively trying to do so. This can range from a wheelchair moving its wheels to assist the user around in a library while the person sitting in it maneuvers and holds up the books for their research attempt, a gun helping correct for any inaccuracies while sniping genocidal Hedge ferrets from a long distance off, or a pair of scissors ensuring that the five hundred and two stars representing the entire Freehold are all cut exactly as the Autumn Queen specified and not cutting over the lines; these are all things that could be done with a direct command with the Beast benefit, sure, but this grants the ability to do more without it being told to. Combined with the Beast benefit, it gives the device a sense of real life and mind, able to act for others beyond just doing as told specific actions by the original enchanter, like how a Fairest grants others a boost of Willpower without the other needing to spend their own.

                          Granted, now that I am out of the madness place, I can see this being a bit fiddly with making actions with lots of dice rolls getting even more dice rolls, which, as learned by playing in a game with another using Download Knowledge for every plausible benefit on most dice rolls, can really slow a scene down. This goes in particular if someone animates a device (requiring a die roll for activation), asks it for help in an action (which is another die roll to get the bonus) and then more die rolls for the action requiring this help. I might make this only for permanent/chapter long enchantments to avoid the initial roll, in that case, though that does make the initial Retainer bonus without any effects to buy more Retainer dots rather minimal... maybe I'll just make it a +2 or +3 when it's a secondary actor and its full bonus rolled when it's the primary actor.

                          Though not explicitly written (which I should have noted), the Fairest bonus is also meant to work with the equipment bonus of the device itself, but that might also be a bit much when it's already acting alongside you... I may simply be too obsessed with recreating a particular famous racing car for my own good.
