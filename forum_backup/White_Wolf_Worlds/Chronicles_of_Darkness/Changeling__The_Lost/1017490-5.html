    Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
    Clash of Wills happens when the "normal systems for the powers fail to resolve it".
    Yes. You may notice that this is also how Dispellation works.

    Sever Oaths can negate contracts (lasting) it with the normal systems for the powers (assuming contracts remain counting as supernatural agreements).
    Spoiler: They don't. A Contract is not a supernatural agreement in the same sense as an oath-as-relevant-to-Mage's-systems. A pledge is a supernatural agreement, as is the pactwork of the Unchained, but a Contract is an "agreement" that is part of a changeling's metaphysical makeup, not an agreement between actual parties — Contracts are struck with nonspecific entities through a shifting and mercurial being of primordial story-logic to make it possible for the party of the first part to make an exchange of catch-or-Glamour for supernatural effect, not to bind a changeling to a course of action or inaction.

    Some of their powers. Like, Gifts are part of the werewolf, but rites (outside of the sacred hunt rite) aren't if that makes sense.
    Rites are part of the Shadow's metaphysical framework and can be used by non-werewolves. Gifts are symbolic filters on werewolf nature torn into their bodies and souls as surely as the marks of their Renown.

    Contracts in 1e changeling didn't really seem part of the lost's nature, contracts were flavoured as external things they can access because of their fey nature giving them the privelage, but in 2e contracts you gain change the lost in some manner.
    Yup. Go have a look at how learning out-of-clan Disciplines works in 2e Requiem and recall how Gifts are acquired in 2e Forsaken. Attainments operate under the same logic of "gaining internal supernatural powers from external sources changes you."

    Also, even in 1e Contracts were sufficiently internal that forging one personally marked you forever.

    But the contract itself is a supernatural agreement reinforced by fate, so it should be applicable. Or is there text somewhere that I'm missing?
    The Contract is not its invocation. Being able to cut down the ephemeral goon squad I can summon through my bargain with the darkness of the shady warehouse does not remove my ability to summon an ephemeral goon squad.

    Sever Oaths is dangerous because it can wreak havoc on your mystical support net, not because a misreading allows it to do archmagic at Gnosis 3.

            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
            The Wyrd is more than content to heap adversity on its constituents, and in fact the strongest defense changelings have against outside supernatural tampering is based on the stat that operates in near-direct opposition to Wyrd.
            I'd like to toss two cents into this argument based on one thing. I wouldn't say that Clarity and the Wyrd are opposites. In fact, I would strongly argue that Kenning is a Wyrd-based ability that relies on Clarity as well.

            Clarity isn't the rejection of the Wyrd, or an embrace of reality. Its the ability to tell the difference between the Wyrd and everything else. Its the ability to tell "fact" from "fiction," where fiction is alive and powerful, admittedly, but still. Low Clarity does not allow you to embrace the Wyrd more. In fact, it actually hampers your ability to operate in the Hedge and Faerie as well as reality. Only someone with high Wyrd (alone anyways) can survive at this point, and that's mostly because they can shape the world around them to fit their mad visions, forcing the Hedge and Faerie to conform to their fantasies. But that's more of an issue of already having embraced the Wyrd, not embracing it through Clarity loss.

            As for what Brookshaw said? *Shrugs* Contracts, once made, aren't just bargains, they're literally part of who the changeling is now. "A Contract isn't just
            something the changeling uses; it's part of what she is." (Ch3 pdf). Contracts physically change a changeling's entire being, and that's far beyond just Fate. A mage can counter or dispell changeling magic, but they can't just pick out the parts of the Contract from a changeling's body and soul. That would require far more time and Arcana at mimimum than just Fate, let alone what kind of dice pool would be necessary. And if its just dealing with counters / dispells, that doesn't make it close to onesided. If it was? Then a changeling with Contracts of Abjuration is an utterly onesided match against everything and anything, including mages, rendering the Awakened powerless.
