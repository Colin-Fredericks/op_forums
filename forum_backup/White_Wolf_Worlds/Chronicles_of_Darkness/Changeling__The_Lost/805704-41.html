Instead of "you can't accept a Token of Reprieve" or "you have to notify if you have a Token of Reprieve", it should be more like "If you accept a Token of Reprieve, you are Oathbound to try and learn as much as possible about the Huntsman you're helping, so that you can later discreetly pass on your wisdom to the Freehold archives" or something along those lines. Something that furthers a story, rather than shuts it down.

                I think it's the same problem with a lot of utopian "there ought to be a law" thoughts. The problem is that human language prevents you from coming up with the perfect pledge. Imagine all those D&D players who think they're being clever with pages of text for a Wish. As I see it, the tighter you try to lock down things with pledges, the more creative people are going to get to subvert it or turn it against pledge-abiding people, and the cost of knowing that is heightened vigilance against clever loopholes and false positives, and from there it's a vicious cycle of greater and greater legalese.

                Instead, having simple and varied pledges among Motleys means that there are comparatively simple loopholes that they can see coming and work against.

                                                I'm used to thinking in terms of D&D/Pathfinder, where less obvious issues can often be untangled by pointing the party's paladin at it and asking who's evil. Much of the time, however, you don't need to worry about the DM being insidious enough to subvert it because it's going to be a dungeon crawl with the big bad wearing Darth Vader armor. It can be fun in its own way, but having black and white morality has been straining my suspension of disbelief for a while.

                                                I realize this is entirely about taste, but I'm interested in Changeling precisely because you need to pay attention to things like courtly politics and the messy issues that come with keeping a fearful group of people together in a world with no easy "detect evil" ability, clearly identifiable evil races to be slaughtered without a pang of conscience, or pure shining paragons of heroism. I want to play out that internal conflict about safety versus heroism, rather than brush off danger with the confidence that the party cleric can just revive me.
