    Questions and Theories on the fae Workings of Iron

    01-15-2014, 04:36 PM
    So first are my questions because they are starting to become very relevant in a game that I am running.

    Things I know based on reading the books:
    Cold Iron counts as: Any amounts of Iron ore that has not been touched by human heat (enough heat that changes to properties at least). This means: Pure Iron ingots (well pure enough to be called Iron instead of steel or otherwise), Meteoric Iron, or Telluric Iron. Any of these will cause aggravated damage to the True Fae and cut through fae enchantments.

    Pure Iron counts as: Iron that has been touched by human heat (significant enough to change it's properties) and is pure enough to be called iron instead of say steel. This means: Iron pokers, Cast Iron Skillets, or Iron fence posts. Any of these will not cause aggravated damage to the True Fae but will cut through fae enchantments.


    My questions are:
    1. Will Cold Iron do aggravated damage if it simply touches the Fae or does it at least have to do 1 bashing damage?
    2. When it says "cut through all fae enchantments" does that mean all contracts and fae magic? Like if someone is using a contract to be invisible, being cut by a Pure Iron knife will make them visible? If they are growing thorny vines via a contract will cutting the vines be both very easy and cancel the contract? Will cutting an object granted by a vow make the vow invalid? I'm worried that if I allow Iron to be found, that it will be basically anti-changeling. That anything anyone does can be negated by a piece of Iron.


    Frequent Story Teller for the Circle of Five gaming group.

          NOTE: The following is how I interpret the use of iron in the games that I organize and run. It is not, by any means, "canon."

          I interpret the subject of your second question a bit more liberally than The Kings Raven, although his implementation of the rule is not incorrect. I take it to mean that iron, when used in the appropriate context, is an effective bane to all fairy enchantments. For Example, if a changeling is bound in iron shackles, her Mask will fall away (it is an illusion crafted of Glamour, after all) and she will be unable to utilize her Kith and Seeming blessings as well as her Contracts. This is not to say that Iron is some sort of "anti" magic sorta allergy thing. It isn't like if a changeling tripped and stumbled into an iron fence his Mask would shatter and he would lose all of his magical protections. Rather, it is about the *intent* of the Iron in question that nullifies the specific enchantments.

          An iron ring, when looked through, would nullify fairy magic that aided in Stealth and would reveal the Mask. An iron capped cudgel would shatter fairy armor and toughness. An iron cross, when worn against the skin, would protect the wearer from ensorcellment and clauses that befuddled the senses. An iron bell, when rung would rouse someone locked in an enchanted slumber. etc etc

                          Again, I am afraid that my collection of words did not impart the information that I wished to convey. Iron in my games, is not "Dispel Magic" against fairies. As I had stated, a changeling who trips and falls into an iron gate does not lose any of their glamorous protections. Iron is effective against fairy magic because at some point in the distant past, some sort of agreement or other such fairy nonsense decided that it would be the case. It is not really about the physical or chemical composition of the Iron that acts as a ward against glamour, it is about the *intent* of the iron that acts as a bane.

                          So, a police officer's steel handcuffs bind the changeling as effectively as any other manner in which a person is restrained. A set of "cold" heavy iron shackles that some old woman sold you from the back of her van, which she claims once bound a lamia for a thousand years? Well, that will strip a changeling of their fairy magic and Mask. See the difference? The handcuffs don't have the weight of narrative or myth backing it. "Cold" Iron (even though it really isn't even a thing) is a product of fairy tale logic ... which really is the only sort of logic that fairies have.

                          Have I made myself clear? Throwing an iron nail at a fairy is not going to kill it. However, if you sleep in a bed that has that Iron nail pounded into the headboard ... it will keep a fairy from manipulating your dreams while you sleep.

                              I very much like the author Kevin hearn's interpritation. In the Iron Druid series of movels, Mr. Hearn claims that to be truly "cold iron" the metal had to come from a meteorite (i.e. not of earth).

                              As I imagine most, if not all, contracts are somehow ingerently tied to the natural world, having something basically alien to that world being able to thwart those contracts just makes sense to me. Plus, from a more mechanical POV, meteorite iron is super rare, so it limits the amount players should reasonably expect to come across. As an added bonus from a player perspective, the PCs can still intersect with surgical tool, cutlery, fences, etc. without having to constantly check if the magic is working and it gets around having to reseach hott forge bs cold forge tempuraters and the like.

                                      Ah...who was it that wrote how faeries could only be hurt by cold iron, in the same way that you have to warm up your bullets first, because gangsters were vulnerable to hot lead?

                                      anyways...

                                      I don't have the books on me to quote specifics, but I generally consider Iron to be proof against fae magic and crafts if it is the object's nature to do so in that situation.

                                      An iron sword cuts fae armor easily. Iron shackles won't unlock at their command, nor can they wriggle out of them. iron armor turns fae blades aside with ease.

                                      In a straight match between troll and Iron door, the door wins - because it's job is to keep things out.

                                      The fae are expert rules-lawyers, and will instead work around the obstacle. The fae lures the opponent into a trap, instead of attacking the knight covered head-to-toe in armor. The fae disguises himself and tricks someone to unlock the shackles, etc.

                                      And having an Iron door doesn't mean the troll can't break through the window, or that you can make fae-made things explode just by poking them with a piece of iron.

                                          Strange that no one has brought this up yet:

                                          In the story in Winter Masques a Moon Court changeling is about to be tortured by "true iron, not steel, and cold forged", His torturer has to wear gloves, because "he fared no better than Veles under the touch of cold iron." Veles panics, because he can't "afford to let the iron touch his flesh - would cut through false seemings and goblin magics as easily as skin."

                                          So at least according to that story, cold iron goes through everything you can define as fae magic defenses. Armor, defense upgrades, stealth and illusions made to protect your true self.
