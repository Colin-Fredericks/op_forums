                        Jack of the Lantern called Stingy Jack or Drunk Jack in his mortal life was an Irish black smith in Ireland several centuries ago, known for his deceitfulness and cunning. One day, one of the Gentry tried to steal Jack away to his realm in Arcadia. No one is sure how he did it, but Jack convinced the True Fae to take him to a local pub and pay for his drink. When the Gentry tried to pay by turning himself into a gold coin, Jack stuck True Fae in his pocket which contained a bar of cold iron. Unable to move, Jack forced him to never bother him again for eternity. Word of one of their own being deceived by a mortal spread like wild fire throughout all the realms in Arcadia and Jack found himself challenged again and again, until one of the True Fae was able to defeat him and whisk him away to Arcadia. One hundred years later, Jack emerged from Arcadia, but with a unique twist. He was barred from never stepping foot in the lands of the Gentry again. He eventually joined the Autumn Court, but was banished from Freehold to Freehold, because of all the chaos and mischief he created. This meshes well with a minor frailty he gained at wyrd 7 which forbids him from entering foot in place of civilization like a city of freehold. Jack now wanders though the wilderness and hedge of Western Europe and North America. He has joined the Magi of the Guilded Thorn and is one of its most experienced members. Unfortunately, he is also known to intermingle with Racketeers and is justly as likely to sell out a Changeling or Fae-Touched as well as help them.

                        Although the Darkling seeming would better suit Jack, for some unknown reason he has the Wizened seeming. He has the Barrister/Farwalker dual kith.

                            Honestly, of all the 1e books that I wouldn't mind a literal rehash/update directly for, the tier 4 books (equinox road, imperial mysteries) take the cake. I loved how Seeming and Kitsch evolved with Wyrd, but because of some changes in 2e, I think advanced changelings are a good topic to cover. More immortals isn't necessarily a bad thing. I don't know if I'd dig a big list of famous immortal changeling superfolk from legend (the true fae will break the fourth wall, find that list, and hunt them down for sport like dragons), but I don't think an example (like the stated out Inheritors, in Beast) is a bad idea.

                            However, I think as writers, Arc and the boss would not likely use someone like Peter Pan or Dorothy because they wouldn't want to cement exactly who or what such legends are. Stating out Peter as a wyrd 10 changeling or Baba Yaga as a True Fae will affect how some people use those beings in game. Which is bad, because in my last game Pan was changeling and Baba was true fae, but in my current game, Pan is true fae and Baba is a Primordial begotten with strong family ties to mages, fae, and one shuanksen that shares an ex lover with her. Now, knowing the creative geniuses behind the changeling game line, they would be able to pull off a pseudo-legendary character that MIGHT be the source of the legend, or might be just one of the near extinct Grimms stealing the plot, or a changeling made by a true fae in its own image. Either way, unless that changeling was explicitly a Grimm, or meant to become a solid character fixture like the npcs of Mummy, if they DO make example wyrd 10 changeling, Id bet they will be original characters.

                                        The Snow Queen: Thousands of years ago, One of the True Fae made a mirror that would show only the ugliness in the world. He and several other members of the Gentry flew high into the mortal sky, hoping to use the mirror to warp the God-machine and maybe even destroy it. However, the higher they took it, the heavier the mirror became until it fell from their hands and shattered into grains of sand entering the eyes of humanity, freezing their hearts and causing them to see only the ugliness and evil in the world. Many years later, they flew into the eyes of a young boy name Kai, who became mean and cruel. He began to bully and torment his childhood friend, Gerda. The only beauty he could see was in the snowflakes. Seeing his admiration for the cold, a True Fae known as the Snow Queen, spirited him away to her realm of ice and snow in Arcadia. She made Kai forget about his mortal family and his town believe that he had drowned in the river. Grieving, Gerda goes to the River and offers her new red shoes. The River does not take the shoes and tells Gerda that Kai was spirited into Arcadia, opening a path into the Hedge. While traveling along one of the Trods, she encounters one of the Gentry who removes Gerda’s memory of Kai and her life on Earth. She spends many years in the Gentry’s Garden of Eternal Summer makes her remember Kai, again. She escapes her keepers realm and continues her quest for Kai. Her keeper tries to entice Gerda back to her realm using a magical castle with a Kai look alike and beautiful clothes. Finally, she sends a group of racketeers to recapture Gerda, but she comes a reindeer to escape them. Finally, Gerda reaches the Snow Queen’s realm. The Snow Queen tries to stop her with her snow bees, whose stings leave a man frozen and dead. However, using a token stolen from her Keepers realm, she summons and angel or fire and sunlight thet kills the snow bees. Gerda enters the Snow Queens castle and finds Kai frozen deep in the Snow Queens mirror of reason. He seems dead whole trying to solve the puzzle that the Snow Queen gave him to solve, forgotten as she searches for new toys. Gerda kisses Kai on the lips to warm him and free him from the lake. Gerda and Kai, then work together to finish Kai’s task so they can leave her realm. When they spell the word eternity, the doorway out of the Snow Queen’s castle opens and they begin the long journey back to town in Denmark. However, when they returned to their town

                                        They meet Hans Christian Anderson, the Changeling Monarch of the Court of Ash and Snow. He informed them that the year was 1839, and over two hundred years had passed. He takes them back to the Freehold in Copenhagen to help them adjust to their new lives. He also wrote about their adventures in First Volume of New Fairy Tales.

                                        This fairy tale has a happy ending in that Gerda and Kai were able to reform their strong bond of friendship. Sadly, Kai was never able to remove the glass from his eyes and the only good he can see is Gerda.

                                        Note: Both Gerda and Kai have the Lost Boy merit/flaw (They appear younger than twelve, but are over a hundred years old). Gerda is a fairest Truefriend, While Kai has his own unique kith, but no seeming since he was saved rather than saved himself.

                                                            Here are some idea for Epic Changeling merits.
                                                            Pure-Heart(3 dots)
                                                            Prerequisite: wyrd 7
                                                            Effect: The Player must decide if their character is an epic hero or villain. If the character is a hero, they completely refreshes his willpower pool when fulfilling his virtue and a villain completely refreshes his willpower when indulging his vice.
                                                            Drawback: Trying to refill your dice pool through the opposite channel if greatly increased. Ignore two successes for your vice if a hero or your virtue if you are a villain. Also, such roles don’t gain the benefits of the 10 again rule.

                                                            Potent Seeming(3 dots)
                                                            Prerequisite: seeming, wyrd 6
                                                            Effect: By your actions and the power of your wyrd, you seeming begins to bleed into your mask and give your mien new abilities and qualities.
                                                            examples:
                                                            Beasts gain functioning characteristics of their animal(fur/pelt, scales, gills, prehensile tail, extra arms and legs) including characteristics of legendary monsters like the toxic skin of a Nuckelavee or the deadly gaze of a Basilisk. They may even gain powers associated with their animal like the nine lives of a cat or the hypnotic gaze of a serpent.
                                                            Darklings gain the ability to see in the dark and other scenes that usually suffer a -1 to perception. They may also lose features that allow people to track them like fingerprints, foot prints, and scent. They may become fluid as a shadow.
                                                            Elementals gain immunity to the effects of their element and even some of its features (bark skin, the fluidity of water, or being light enough to balance on a leaf). And their presence really CAN influence mortals.
                                                            Drawback: Their curse strengthens as well. Beasts might gain a derangement associated with their animal(cats an incredible fear or water, monkeys become unable to ignore their curiosity), Darklings with a high enough wyrd suffer aggravated damage from sunlight, ect.

                                                            Note: I can see many players arguing that the function of this merit is met with potent seeming and dual kith. This merit makes sense to me, because your seeming represents how you took your life back, how you regained your right to make your own decisions. As such, along with access to in seeming contracts, I can see some changelings continuing to follow the path that allowed them to escape from their keepers gaining access to more powers/abilities from their seeming.
