        Reviving this thread with a new kith. I like my own fluff for it but am still workshopping the seeming descriptions and the mechanical bits

        Hecatoncheir
        No, I don't need a spotter. Trust me, all hands are on deck here.

        They say many hands make short work, but that means little when the boss never stops giving you work. Just as on Earth, sometimes you find impoverished Gentry. As powerful as they are to their human and hob victims, they are a mid-sized fish in a sea full of sharks. Sharks who find it diverting and healthy to mess with them, raid their lands and poach their best slaves. So these "minor" gentry need to make the most use out of whatever slaves they could keep around and hide. Thus, one changeling is built to do the work of an entire team of servants.

        Those who survive without dying from exhaustion are often those who were already used to this life. Perhaps she was the overworked homemaker who had to balance all the needs of a toddler, a teenager, a college student and an immature husband. Maybe he was a janitor at a run-down school, acting as clean-up crew, electrician, heating technician or whoever else the school didn't have the money to hire. Or maybe they were a multimedia artist, painting landscapes over the abstract surfaces of their own free-form sculptures.

        When they escape, they often do so by using that same adaptability to overcome the obstacles put in their. Sometimes they break free in a whirling dervish of chaos, smashing through a wall with a sledgehammer before swapping to a shield to block the dragon’s fire. Sometimes, they just realize one day that they have taken up every function in the entire realm, leaving no other servants or guards to stop them from just running off.

        Elemental: She is a swirling mass of mud that defaults to a vaguely humanoid shape, but when pressed, she shapes out new appendages as needed.
        Darkling: compound eyes dart around as he uses one set of arms to navigate the book while another transcribes it simultaneously

        Blessing: when using Dexterity to perform an extended action, three successes count as an exceptional success.

        Multitasker: By spending 1 glamour and passing a Wits + Wyrd roll, a Hecatonchier can hold Wyrd/2 (rounded up) different two-item sets of (physically present and hand-held) equipment or weapons in reserve for the rest of the scene. They can swap out which set is active with an unrolled reflexive action, exchanging one set of benefits for another.

                Since it's revived, here's a conversion that I came up with a while back:

                Woodwalker
                Lost? Can't say the term's ever been my cup of tea.

                Arcadia is land of sweeping, majestic beauty; it is also a realm of hidden dangers lurking in trees, caves, and mires. The landscape shifts according the whims of its Fae owner, or based on a set of Byzantine, arcane rules. Sometimes not even the Gentry can manage to keep up, or they'd rather just have one of their changeling captives take care of guiding the others through blasted wastelands or windswept deserts. That's where the Woodwalkers come in.

                Most changelings who wind up as members of this kith were already intimately familiar with wilderness environments. They may have been forest rangers, deep-sea fishers, hermits, or members of a nomadic tribe. Others still come from those who romanticized natural and wild places, such as a land-locked soul who longed for a life at sea, or an office worker in the big city who saved up both vacation and sick leave for a long sojourn in the canyons.

                Despite the name, there are Woodwalkers connected to every type of environment imaginable. Any place where a traveler risks getting lost is a place to which members of this kith might attune themselves. There are Woodwalkers of steaming jungles, river deltas, deep grottoes, and even a few who master labyrinthine urban environments, weaving through glass, concrete, and neighborhood watches with ease.

                Escape more often than not comes from outmaneuvering the Gentry in their own environs, learning the land better and more intimately than its master ever would dare. Some find their ways to other domains with radically different landscapes, where interlopers lacking the Woodwalker's gift are at a steep disadvantage. Some even find that by treating the trees and brooks with kindness causes these features to return the favor, guiding changelings along hidden paths back to the Hedge and beyond.

                Elemental: A creature of snow and lichen who sometimes acts as though the tundra where they make their home is an extension of their own being. Trespassers in their domain take their lives into their own hands.

                Wizened: A stooped figure with a gnarled walking stick and roughspun cloak. They know every nook and cranny of this holler, and they'll get you where you need to go, so long as you follow close behind and don't ask too many questions.

                Blessing: Choose a general type of environment (such as forests, islands, plains, mountains, or swamps); when making Survival rolls in the chosen environment, three successes counts as an exceptional success.

                Lay of the Land: While within their environment of expertise, the Woodwalker can spend a point of Glamour as an instant action and roll Wits + Survival. Each success allows the changeling the ask the Storyteller one question from the list below:
                • What is the safest path forward?
                • Where is the nearest source of danger?
                • What is something hidden here?
                • What here is unnatural for the environment?
                • What is something that has changed since the last time I have been here?
                Answers received pertain to anything within (Wyrd x 10) yards/meters of the changeling. The Storyteller must answer the player honestly, but does not have to provide context. Successes can be allocated to the same question multiple times for additional information of the same type.

                                            I don't remember exactly what mechanics the Nix used, but if it could be any aquatic changeling, I'll offer this update to the Swimmerskin/Waterborn/Waterdweller kiths that I posted in this thread a couple years ago:

                                            Originally posted by gscrap View Post
                                            Swimmerskin

                                            Oh yeah? Why don't you jump in here and say that?

                                            The sea is many things: beautiful, mysterious, dangerous, unfathomably vast. It should come as no surprise that many of the Gentry feel an attraction to the water. Changelings with the Swimmerskin kith may have served their durances as pearl divers on the sea floor of a Fae sea, as the creatures that lurk below the waves, or as the waves themselves.

                                            A majority of Swimmerskins are at their most comfortable in the water, but many tend to avoid the depths except in case of dire emergency.

                                            Beast: He might almost be attractive, with his lean body and scintillating green scales, but his webbed fingers and faint fishy smell have made it hard to find romance in the freehold. Not for lack of trying, though.

                                            Elemental: She constantly looks like she just stepped out of a rainstorm, her hair bedraggled and her soaked clothes leaving wet footprints in her wake. When she smiles, which is rarely, her teeth are tiny chips of beach glass.

                                            Kith Blessing: When the Swimmerskin uses Athletics to swim, achieving three successes counts as an exceptional success.

                                            Born to the Water: A Swimmerskin is capable of remaining underwater for far longer than an average mortal. When submerged in water, the Swimmerskin can hold her breath for thirty minutes before making any rolls to avoid drowning. Furthermore, she can spend one Glamour to remain submerged indefinitely; however, this effect ends as soon as her head breaks the surface.
                                            I'm also working on an update to the Palewraith for one of the PCs in a game I'm about to run. I'll skip the fluff for the moment and just ask (humbly) for feedback on the mechanics-- if folks are digging it, I'll post a proper version later on.

                                            When the Palewraith attempts to navigate or to locate something in total darkness (or otherwise without the benefit of sight), achieving three successes on the roll counts as an exceptional success. A Palewraith may spend one Glamour to add +1 to their Defense rating against physical attacks for one scene. This benefit only applies against attacks made by opponents suffering at least a -1 penalty to vision-related rolls due to darkness or other impairment.
