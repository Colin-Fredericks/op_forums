                                            I am embarrassed it took me so long to recommend this book. Despite being a YA read, The School for Good and Evil is one of the best sources of inspiration for a Changeling: The Lost game.

                                            With her glass slippers and devotion to good deeds, Sophie knows she'll earn top marks at the School for Good and join the ranks of past students like Cinderella, Rapunzel, and Snow White. Meanwhile, Agatha, with her shapeless black frocks and wicked black cat, seems a natural fit for the villains in the School for Evil.
                                            The two girls soon find their fortunes reversed—Sophie's dumped in the School for Evil to take Uglification, Death Curses, and Henchmen Training, while Agatha finds herself in the School for Good, thrust among handsome princes and fair maidens for classes in Princess Etiquette and Animal Communication.
                                            But what if the mistake is actually the first clue to discovering who Sophie and Agatha really are?
                                            I have read a lot of books over the years. I mean a lot of books. I can honestly say I've never come across anything like this before. It's as if the author read a fairy tale, looked at the way we have to go to school to learn our jobs in life, and thought, "What if the princesses, princes, and villains in fairy tales also had to attend school to learn their roles?"

                                            Villains learn how to monologue, princesses are taught how to talk to animals and how to beautify themselves, while princes are taught how to be valiant and sword fight. The book, of course, mocks some of these classes. In one part, the main prince couldn't come up with a rejoinder to a princess and instantly started a sword fight with one of his classmates instead.

                                            Sophie and Agatha are well-written and it's fun to read what they will decide to do next to get to what they believed were their proper schools. And since I have never even seen a book like this before--the closest probably being Harry Potter--each attempt was a complete surprise. I was even surprised at the ending, but it was fitting as well.
                                            The world-building and concept of Soman's realm is amazing. He has a natural ability to craft a story that flows together splendidly. Although I loved reading Agatha and Sophie's story, I wish Soman had shined the spotlight on the other characters throughout the series. Both of the characters are quirky in their own right.

                                            **SPOILERS BELOW**
                                            -
                                            -
                                            -

                                            Agatha's "goodness" is cleverly hidden from the rest of the students. However, constant hints are being thrown into the readers' faces that both Agatha and Sophie are in their correct goals. From the beginning, it is clear that Agatha only desired Sophie's friendship and to go home. Meanwhile, Sophie's selfishness was evident from the beginning. She was fine with sacrificing Agatha in order to receive a happy ending. Sophie's character is downright insufferable, yet there are enough parts to her personality to make her tolerable at times. I did not like how Sophie's devotions spiraled between Aggie, Tedros, and revenge. I also found it frustrating that Agatha kept clinging onto Sophie despite countless betrayals. However, I cannot blame them because those behaviors are expected within their characters. Additionally, the boys are portrayed quite badly in this series. The first book of the series has the best concept (although the third's characters might stand up for themselves) and an amazing ending. While the rest of the series is beautifully written, the first book can stand by itself.

                                            All in all, I would definitely recommend this YA fairy tale to anyone who enjoys these kinds of things. It's a fairy tale with a definite twist, some of it dark, but all of it great fun. I will reread this book in the future and I know I will enjoy it again as much as I did this time.

                                            You can order the book here:

                                            Amazon.com: Customer reviews: The School for Good and Evil (School for Good and Evil, 1)
