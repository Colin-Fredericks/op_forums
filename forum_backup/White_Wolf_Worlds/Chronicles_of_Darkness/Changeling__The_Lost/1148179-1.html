                    The fact that you cannot grant any kind of magical boons to anyone without a permanent, life-long, unending Oath is the iron nail in the coffin for me. Sure, you can get bonuses for yourself with Goblin Pledges, but you can't actually grant them to other people. No Sealing someone's promise in such a way that they get bonuses to complete their tasks instead of just penalties for failure. No granting mortals boons as long as they follow whatever fae requirements you set forth for them (and quite the opposite in the Bargains category, really, where you do mundane drudgery for mortals in exchange for similarly trivial benefits).

                    That's the real loss of utility in the Pledge system. There are plenty of sticks, but vanishingly few carrots to incentivize using them in the first place. Being able to apply Goblin Pledge boons to ordinary Sealings should go a long way towards fixing that. And so, too, would being able to swear Oaths with a fixed duration instead of them being ways to join a Motley or a Court for literally the entire rest of your life.

                    You know, make them an actual tool, instead of just an onus placed on anyone foolish enough to commit verbally to something, or anyone who wants to join a magical social club.

                            So... one nail = final and only nail. Got it.

                            Let me show you just how easy it is to fix that.

                            New Goblin Contract: Middle-Man
                            Activation: Instant
                            Dice Pool: Wits + Politics + Wyrd
                            Cost: 1 glamour, 1 willpower
                            Dramatic Failure: The changeling has to bear the cost of the Vow, while target gets all the benefit
                            Failure: No agreement reached
                            Success: The changeling successfully serves as a middle man between a Goblin Vow they possess, and a chosen target / second party. The target gains all the benefits of the Vow, but also must fulfill any cost associated with the Vow.
                            Exceptional Success: The changeling gets the benefit as well as the target at no extra cost.


                            Or make a house rule to let you seal the goblin vow as part of a regular sealing. Or bargain. Tada. Done.


                            This isn't hard to do. Its trivial. You don't like it, house rule it. There's at least ten ways I can think of to do what you want, depending on how you want it done.

                            A changeling swearing to obey his Court's Crown and getting benefits? All changelings of Mantle 2 get a goblin vow related to their court, and swearing to fulfill a command can very well be done by swearing on the Court's goblin vow aspect. Tada, there's your benefit, its and entirely temporary.

                            Honestly, at this point, I really have to ask if you've actually used any of these in play, or you're just reacting based on the pledges not being as OMG broken as they were in 1e. Change happens. Have you tried them out in play to see how well they work for you in actual experience? I don't get that feeling - most people that do tend to start talking about the actual experience they have with it being difficult, rather than how things are theoretically difficult.

                            I'm playing in a changeling game right now. I personally haven't Sealed anyone (mostly because I don't have the need to, but that's always been true of me, even when I played CtL 1e), but my motley-mates have. I've done bargains and oaths plenty - freehold, motley, court, and two personal, and I'm happy with the results. I'm the witch of the group, so I've invested a lot in Goblin Vows, and can usually arrange for one of the five I have to do something for almost any given situation with some bargaining. Based on my experiences, the pledge system is currently very easy to use, user friendly, and doesn't make the game stop as people figure out if everything in a pledge balances out.

                            Speaking of, that need to balance things in the old 1e pledges actually made sealings all but impossible, imho. That time our Ifrit sealed someone with their Contract? That ease would have been impossible - we'd have had to stop the game, think of the right way to phrase things, and that just DRAGGED the game. And then, we'd have had other fae, who are in the know, be just as careful with the words back, and it would all amount to nothing anyways. Pledges, more often than not, ended up as nothing more than a circle jerk of people buffing themselves up. And 2e pledges don't work that way, no. But they do have some interesting uses that are fun and easy to do.

                                        When Sealings are avoidable with a single point of Glamour, your GM is, at best, patronizing you when they have enemies submit to an inconvenient Sealing.

                                        And requiring a homebrewed Contract in order to bestow a boon on a Pledge means that the base system as written is more than a little anemic. Needing homebrew just to give it some functional punch is the key sign of a system with problems.

                                        Oaths, as they stand - being permanent magical bindings - serve almost exclusively as a hoop to jump through while joining a Freehold, Court, or Motley. Their benefits are almost nonexistent - the boons they grant are, at best, minor conveniences - and instead serve primarily as a magical onus keeping you from ever betraying - or even quitting - the group to which you've permanently committed the rest of your life.

                                        Goblin Vows are the one real exception - something that has actual utility in use.

                                        "Mundane drudgery" like "Lend them your magic furniture" or "Keep their dreams clear of incubi" or "Send your buddy over to have a talk with their landlord"?
                                        Mundane drudgery like:
                                        The bargain is usually an ongoing task — a changeling might offer to clean the human’s house once a week, or sew her a dress once a month, or grant her pleasing dreams three nights a week.
                                        Obnoxious time sinks that, in terms of narrative and flow of story, only eat up your character's down time and make them less-available for Motley activities. In exchange for performing ongoing, menial work for a mortal, Huntsmen take a tiny penalty to actively track you down.

                                        The thing that especially ruins Bargains, though, is this little fact:
                                        No matter the nature of the other party, the changeling must appear to him without her Mask, and propose the bargain.
                                        I don't know about you, but blowing your identity as a faerie monster to a mortal is, alone, not worth the benefit offered by a Bargain, let alone adding grunt work performed for them on top of that.

                                        The problem with the Pledge system remains that Sealings have almost no reason to actually be used when they're so easily ignored, and Oaths/Bargains have a whole lot of risk/stick for the changeling enacting them, but a dreadful lack of reward/carrot for that same changeling, meaning that there's no motivation to interact with them outside of what's absolutely necessary. Goblin Vows are the exception - because they offer an actual carrot to the equation - but all of the other types should have similarly baked-in motivation for employing them. They don't - and 'homebrew it until it isn't a problem anymore' doesn't change that the base system is a problem.
