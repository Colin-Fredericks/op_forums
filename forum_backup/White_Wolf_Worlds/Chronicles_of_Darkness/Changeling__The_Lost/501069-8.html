    So you are saying, like I mentioned as a possibility few posts ago, that unlike with other so far revealed Seemings Fairest chose a method of leadership as their escape and acts of leadership actually enforce and root them, like with the other Seemings ie the Darklings chose to escape by doing a hard choice and are empowered by doing the same in the future, but unlike the otehr Seemings these same acts of leadership drive them to low Clarity, that is unlike with others with whom enforcing and living again their choice of escape (that is their Seemings archetype) also acts as a foil to their Curse but with the Fairest it actually drives forward their Curse.

    What I mean is that when a Darkling makes a hard choice and gains Clarity he will not be subject to his Curse. When a Beast breaks Taboo at a personal cost to gain Clarity he is again not automatically within the purview of his Curse, actually such acts of freedom are the exact opposite of their Curse, even if breaking such a Taboo may cause them to get undue official scrutiny that could perhaps lead to incarceration thus triggering the Curse. But when a Fairest takes up the task of leadership and regains Clarity he is almost certainly, really in an RPG when does a PC's plan ever go so perfectly as to not cause any collateral damage, visited with his Curse for the exact same action causing him to suffer a Clarity Break.

    See the difference? All that I am asking is consistency. Either change the Darklings and the Beast Curses so that they too trigger at the same acts, ie the hard choice only counts if you do it in blind trust of someone else, and the Taboo breaking only counts if the great cost to yourself is being captured or tied with Oaths or, in my mind a better option, just change the Fairest Curse to go along with the rest so that it doesn't trigger from the same act that triggers the Blessing. Like said I would much more prefer something like what I suggested, poorly thought out as it was, and refine it so that it touches more of the question who's the fairest of them all if the answer is you your golden if not then the Curse hits. And personally to me even the Blessing enforced leadership role is just a throwback to the Sidhe, whom I admit to loving, so perhaps both should be revisited.

    And since Filamena has told us the prize, if that option is still on the table, I can easily pay the 3-10$ that it would cost to rewrite one or both of them.

    Personally I think the Fairest should be about beauty and madness instead of leadership.

    Perhaps their method of escape should be that they had to accept the madness of the Faerie, to think like a Gentry so to speak, to succeed and so their have as a Blessing the Clarity of a Broken Mirror. The Fairest find surprising Clarity from seeming madness when the character gives in and seeks to think like the Gentry to resolve an issue he feels revived by his incursion into insanity so familiar to his former Keeper. Their Curse could then be something like the Land of the Blind. The Fairest are the one eyed man seeing the world as it is, a mad place governed by rules and Oaths most are completely unaware of, and it acts as a hard reminder of how different they are of from the blind that cling to them for company, leadership and attention. Fairest naturally gather people around them yet are acutely aware of how different, how separated, they are from them. Their are subject to Clarity Break when they forget this difference and seek to abandon the madness of the Faerie to be like them. Thus the Fairest must stay aloof of others so as not to close their eyes and join their blind ranks, when they allow others to truly see them as they are and they open themselves to another they suffer from the effects of this Curse such openness was the surest way to allow the Gentry to dig their claws in after all.

    Or perhaps their Blessing should simply be Thou art fairer than all who are here. The Fairest gain a free dot of Clarity when they use their awe inspiring looks to steal away the scene from someone else, for example by stealing the leadership role despite having no claim to it simply through the fact that you are clearly the Fairest of them all or usurping the spotlight from and stealing the credit for the work of your sage etc. Where as the Curse should then be But more beautiful still is... The Fairest suffer from their Curse when they find themselves usurped of their status and spotlight or when they are proven to be nothing more than a pretty face, should another Fairest usurp them as befits their Blessing or should they make themselves seen as a laughing stock and loose credibility they suffer a Clarity Break for it.

    I'll freely admit that I only put a few minutes of thought into these and that they could be much deeper and more refined but as examples I think they do an adequate job of presenting alternative ways to see the Fairest that differ from the leadership role given here even though both, especially the latter, also encompasses the leadership concept but neither is limited by it.

    Just throwing ideas as that is I think the idea of open development. I just feel that since the responses here suggest that most of those who liked the Fairest in 1st ed seem discouraged by their 2nd ed incarnation then perhaps it should be re-thought a bit even if that re-thinking only result in you deciding to keep the change from beauty and madness into leadership in place. So please do not get defensive about it, I know how easy that is when concerning your own material, but try to look at it from a more aloof place.

                Finally I would like to add that stealing spotlight can be done in a non-offensive manner without disrupting a game if such disruptions are common no amount of game mechanics are going to prevent it and it's a problem player and the solution is usually to speak about it with them outside the game. For example in our current, long running, Aberrant game my character is the groups leader, or rather it's face, and so I pretty much take all the credit when we succeed but on the flip side I also take all the blame when we mess up and we only play the blame game within the group never in public. I wouldn't do this if it wasn't okay for the whole group tho, that is the players as their characters of course sometimes have other opinions about my actions. In other games I have been more than happy to let other players take the credit while I have stayed in the background, it all depends on the character one is playing.

                                There are millions of things no-one, except a movie villain who has the script, can account for that can cause collateral damage and get people hurt. You give a simple order that slightly changes things and someone get's hurt and you are struck by the Curse. Let's say you order the capture of a criminal and unbeknowst to anyone that criminal has given a certain percentage of his ill begotten gains to an orphanage he grew in and that small contribution has helped keep the place afloat. Now by your order the criminal is capture and punished as he should but the orphanage goes under causing much grief for a lot of people, and emotional hurt is part of the Curse, and you are struck by the Curse. There was literally no way to know this would happen especially if even the orphanage didn't know who their mystery benefactor was. Still it was your leadership that lead there and therefore you are in for a Clarity Break and this is even true despite the fact that you are an Evil Queen who could have ordered the damn orphans to be burned alive without batting an eyelid. The point here being that even hurting people you care absolutely nothing about, since you know evil tyrant and all, triggers the Curse. It makes any Fairest taking up a leadership position bleed Clarity at an unprecedented rate, especially PC Fairest who have no plot protection, like the scripted villains on TV, or ST shenanigans, like NPC villains. You know how every ST guide always tells ST's to cheat when the players seem to crack their master minds plots, cheat in a way that the ST behind the scenes and unseen by players changes how the NPC has prepared for the encounter taking into account the idea players came up with, with ease to protect the plot. Well such behaviour now automatically tends to result in Fairest being hit with their Curse if they are in a leadership position even if it would be the best for the game as a whole. Tell me how often have you seen a PC plan to work exactly as planned? With no collateral damage whatsoever? In over two decades I have never seen that to be the case, not once

                                EDIT:
                                For a little additional food for thought how often do you think real life leadership roles tend to work without accidentally hurting anyone? Even in a benevolent environment of a workplace, no supernatural problems and no wartime leadership is needed for this to be true, every time the boss has to put his foot down someone is likely to get hurt somewhere and I'd like to think these things weren't calculated evils perpetrated by said bosses but rather unintentional collateral damage. For example at my work place two people wanted to trade shifts to accommodate their out of work schedules the boss agreed to this and gave her permission but also added that due to the shift the other worker had to do a very specific job on her new shift, the boss didn't think much about it as it was just common sense decision to her but the worker was devastated she stressed over it loosing sleep and messing up her off-weekend. She got hurt by that act of leadership even though it bore no ill-intent and the boss had no idea she would become so stressed over that. See? Most Common sense acts of leadership may cause someone to get hurt emotionally if not physically and the Curse encompasses both.
