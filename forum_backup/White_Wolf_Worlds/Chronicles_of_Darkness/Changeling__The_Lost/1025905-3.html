                    Originally posted by qendi View Post

                    We all wish you as large and as fast paychecks as possible, I think but the community wants more stuff at the same time. Would putting new stuff through KS as independent small sourcebooks be (from developer and writers point of veiw) better than adding content directly to a sourcebook?
                    I find it better both for the writers' payment sake, and because it lets us review the final core content and adapt it to suit.

                    A couple of times, I've seen content built for Kickstarter expansions that was based on pre-final content (usually the "almost final" Kickstarter content) that didn't quite match the final core book content. Whereas supplement material built for separate books works on a slightly different urgency, so it fits better. This is generally pretty minor stuff, but I find it irksome as a developer. I prefer to let the core book "cook" a little in its final form before I have writers working on material. That way I'm 100% sure they're working off final material, and they're not as rushed.

                    If you want a really good example of this phenomenon, 1E Requiem's supplement "The Blood" was mostly written before the final version of Requiem. And it shows. Even though some of the writers had access to the final, sometimes it's hard to let that final material sink in, reconciling the differences between the pre-release versions and the print version. For another example, I still have some misconceptions about what is in the final version of Beast, despite reading the final version a couple of times. I spent over a year on the original material, but only those two readthroughs of the final. If I were asked to do supplement material for Beast within a month or so of the final's release, I'd probably botch that in a couple of places.

                    David A Hill Jr
                    Freelance Writer
                    Independent Game Designer
