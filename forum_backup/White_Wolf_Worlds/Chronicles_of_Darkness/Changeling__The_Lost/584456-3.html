                                Good point, but I don't think enduring a durance has to be an inherently passive endeavor. Consider a prisoner of war who suffers physical and psychological torture at the hands of their captors, but never reveals anything beyond name, rank, and service number. Calling that passive would certainly be disrespectful as well. Simply resisting the torture and not giving in to their jailers' demands is an active struggle and an exercise of agency. They may never have an opportunity to break free in the sense you're thinking, but might go through the same psychological and metaphysical changes that grant Changelings a Seeming. And bear in mind that the word escape has multiple definitions, including simply avoiding something (as in "luckily, they escaped serious injury" or to fail to be noticed or recollected by someone. A Changeling who isn't fun to play with anymore because they've learned to ignore the pain and humiliation escapes notice and her Keepers attentions. It may not necessarily have to be in the Shawshank Redemption sense of the word. Again, I'm just musing. Would be nice to get Machine IV's take on this angle.

                                    07-10-2015, 05:09 AM
                                    Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                                    What I meant was that all three showed patience, understanding that their goals could not be achieved by acting rashly.
                                    Ok... but this has never really been under contention has it? Who's saying that Seemings can't come out of patience and planning?

                                    The complaint was against the idea that the time spent on enduring a Durance should shape a Seeming rather than the nature of the action that the changeling took to regain agency; not against the idea that some changelings need to wait.

                                    Yes, she changed seats, my point was the act of sitting was her defiance.
                                    And we're really just being a bit nitpicky about trying to be clever.

                                    My counter though, would be the change in words there. The act of sitting (and verbal resistance, and purposefully getting arrested as part of civil disobedience activist movement) is important. Not that she didn't do anything. But that she did something.

                                    If rescue arrives before opportunity, does that mean having made your plan is invalidated?
                                    Probably? A changeling's plans aren't what determines their Seeming. It's the actions they take. A Darkling might not have planned to betray someone to escape, and the situation went bad leading them to this course of action because they opt to betray and escape rather than risk recapture on the chance they all might escape. If things hadn't gone wrong, they might have come out a Fairest or Wizened as examples.

                                    My interpretation of the descriptions was that it's what you change about yourself in order to escape, not whether you actually succeed by that change.
                                    As you don't get a Seeming until you escape, I'm not sure how well that interpretation holds. The descriptions are written from the authorial perspective of telling a compelling narrative example. The details of being taken, the Durance, and the escape as a Seeming all flowing into each other in a logical narrative flow. But claiming a Seeming happens at the end of the process, and it can change based on how that process ends.

                                    If a would-be Ogre "Hulks-out", but fails to break free of that last chain, does that mean he's not an Ogre because 10 minutes later someone else comes along and unlocks it for him?
                                    He's not an Ogre until he completely escapes. He wasn't an Ogre just for trying to break free in an Ogre-type fashion, because he hasn't achieving "Seeming-hood" yet. His Seeming isn't changing back and forth during all this. It's the end result of the escape. He might retain being an Ogre after fully escaping, become Seemingless as someone supplants his agency by freeing him before he can fully assert it, or he might end up a different Seeming despite being on a path to be an Ogre as the situation changes beyond his control and he needs to find a new method of reclaiming that control.

                                    And playing possum, or going for a "Rope-a-Dope" strategy are passive methods, but still take courage to see them through.
                                    Possibly. Though again, I'm not seeing where this comes in. Biding time doesn't seem to be an issue unless it's all you do.
