            I feel like we may have a difference of opinion when it comes to when, exactly, a person starts being transformed into a Changeling. The creation of a Fetch is the second major act of malice that The Others perform on a person (the first act, or course, being the decision to kidnap them in the first place) and the first act that involves actual violation of them on a spiritual level. Fetches don't happen by accident (unless I decide to use that ex nihilo fetch idea someone pitched earlier in the thread). If, perhaps, a person was to escape the clutches of their would-be Keeper somehow and make their way through the Thorns back Ironsides without ever seeing Arcadia, the Fetch would still be there (unless they disintegrate like you mention 1e Fetches did...what book is that in?).

            And even if the Fetch does disintegrate...what happens to that piece of shadow/soul? Does it just automatically return to the lucky escapee? Does it also disintegrate, leaving the person it was taken from with a void for the rest of their lives? Or is it still there, fluttering in the wreckage, waiting to be picked up by someone? That, I feel, is the importance of an Icon, not the fact that the rest of them are created in the Hedge, nor when the rest of them are created (coming or going).

            I feel like 1e presented plenty of alternatives that made destroying your fetch a desirable outcome, most prominently specialized Merits to represent particular sorts of boons.
            None of those Merits especially motivated the Fetch themselves to fight back, though. Keeping the one piece of soul they have does.

            The whole idea of making PC-playable Fetch is to explore what it means to be something that thought they were human but learned they weren't. It's playing with the Cloning Blues, Tomato in the Mirror, and Androids Are People Too/Clones Are People Too tropes (fair warning, all TVTropes links). Every piece of C:tL fiction I've seen where Fetch are involved treat them as disposable, of lesser importance than the Changeling they were made from, or imply that the Changeling is morally justified destroying them to get their life back. That's kind of fucked up when you think about it hard enough. Even in the Anthology stories, all the ones with Fetch in them end with the discovery that one is a Fetch...when that's really just the beginning. The Fetch now has to try to live with and cope with the knowledge of what they are -- all the while dealing with a being who doesn't believe they have the right to exist.

            I've heard Promethean described as being an allegory for the queer experience (trying to exist in a world where you know you're different from almost everyone else, and where those difference make people hate you just for existing), and Changeling itself is very much a survivor allegory (a person who has suffered unimaginable trauma now has to pick up the pieces of their life and move on somehow). Well, what I think this particular rabbit hole I've tumbled into is a neurodivergence allegory -- coming to the realization that you aren't like everyone else, there is a reason why, it's tied into the very fabric of your being, and now you have to cope with that knowledge, all while others don't understand or they think they do, and think of you as an inferior version of a person (if they think of you as a person at all, and not just an object to be dealt with).

            Right. That was a tangent and a half. Let me tie it back to the Shadow-as-Icon idea. The Shadow is a metaphor for a lot of things, and to the Gentry, a metaphor is as good as truth. How I'm seeing this is that when they take a piece of the abductee's shadow, it's coming from the Jungian Archetype just as much as the physical darkness or metaphysical soul. They're taking a piece of that bit of us we all hide, that least desirable bit of someone (a guilt, a shame, an anxiety, an insecurity, etc.) to use as the seed that anchors their construct's personality in place, that makes it able to impersonate the person they're abducting. Calling it an Icon means that, as per the description, this Shadow is a memory, probably one with a specific emotion attached to it, in physical form. The Changeling has reason to want it back (it improves their Clarity, the way that confronting your Shadow self and embodying it supposedly helps you become a more rounded person), other Changelings might want it (to use as leverage or vengeance, not caring about all this soul/Id crap), but it's also forming the very basis of the Fetch's entire existence. The Fetch literally cannot exist without it. So it creates a conflict, which is what drama (and thus, Storytelling) is all about. And it does this in a way that a Changeling gaining a Merit from breaking their Fetch doesn't. Sure, it's an Icon that's not in the Hedge. It's an Icon that was created even before the Changeling was altered enough to be considered a changeling. It's an Icon that's easily accessible -- all it requires is the murder of another sentient being, one that quite literally is a part of yourself.

            Perhaps it's not literally an Icon. But having it have all the characteristics of one opens up story opportunities, I feel.

                                        Hmmm...

                                        As for what the Shadow is doing for the Fetch themselves, this is what I've come up with so far:

                                        "When The Gentry take someone, they bind a piece of that person's shadow to the Fetch in order to give them the necessary memories and emotional responses they'll need to believably impersonate the original. This shadow is not just a metaphor for the soul -- it's a portion of the original's archetypal Jungian Shadow, that part of their subconscious which contains those darker emotions they might wish to repress. Thus, a Fetch's Shadow is the emotion that feels most real to them, and acting on it renews the pledge that allows them to act in their original's stead, helps relieve stress, and reinforces their sense of self -- even if it's not healthy behavior in the long run. Whenever a Fetch acts in a manner that takes the easy way out at the expense of their goals or relationships, causing themselves a minor setback in the process, they regain a point of Glamour, and whenever they harvest Glamour from someone experiencing the same emotion they gain an additional point on a successful roll."

                                        Essentially, it acts like a Vice, but regains Glamour instead of Willpower and allows the Fetch to regain additional glamour, similar to how a Changeling's Court Mantle provides extra Glamour when harvesting but also has an act they can perform to regain Glamour without a harvest.

                                        I do like the idea of the Fetch gaining Beats for acting as their own person, rather than a copy of their changeling, however. The Shadow is a Gentry-imposed compulsion; breaking free of that might be something a Fetch would aspire to, especially after discovering the truth about what they are.

                                        New Persistent Condition: Shadow's Defiance

You cannot regain Glamour from your Shadow, and you do not gain extra Glamour when you harvest your Shadow's emotion from others. You gain no benefits from Allies or Contacts that represent the friends and family from your Changeling's life.
                                        Possible Sources: Losing Integrity from meeting your Changeling counterpart or discovering you are an artificial being.
                                        Resolution: Regain a dot of Integrity, lose another point of Integrity, take meaningful steps to make amends and repair your previous relationships, act in such a manner that your previous friends and family actually prefer you as you are now.
                                        Beat: Your character take actions to deliberately differentiate themselves from the person their changeling counterpart used to be, especially if such actions alienate their friends and family.
