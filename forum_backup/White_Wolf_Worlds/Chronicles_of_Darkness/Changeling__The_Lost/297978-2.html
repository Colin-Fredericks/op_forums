                I almost think that Clarity would do better as a set of Conditions instead of a Scale.

                So, for example, a starting Changeling might have the Shocked condition (holy crap, I MADE IT HOME.), which could progress into other "Clarity Conditions" like Amnesia (I don't remember anything about my Durance, and I don't want to remember), Stockholm Syndrome (At least in Arcadia I had a purpose, you know?                 Originally posted by amechra View Post
                (Assuming the 'Hell yes' is directed towards the first sentence rather than the second.)

                Why would having it as a number be more useful or thematic than having it be a non-numeric stat?

                I'm not trying to be combative, I'm legitimately curious.

                After all, reducing it down to a numerical score forces a simplification on the whole affair.

                Is it so we have something to interact with for stuff that interacts with Integrity systems?
                So, first of all: Not to speak for Arc, but I don't think he was saying that the stat had to be a number. I think he was just saying that there needed to be an Integrity analogue for Changeling, and missed the previous few posts ahead of your question.

                But to actually respond personally, yes, it should be a number. I am intrigued by the idea of Conditions as a replacement for the stat, but I think that misses out on some of the GMC's system strengths: unless you get an exceptional success on your Breaking Point roll, you pick up Conditions already. It seems reasonable (and Blood and Smoke does this) for Changeling Breaking Points to inflict a different set of Conditions. By itself, this captures most of the value of changing Clarity from a scale to a set of Conditions. It also allows for having multiple Conditions as a consequence of different Breaking Points. (B&S also grants the Inspired Condition for getting an exceptional success, so a Breaking Point always leads to a new Condition anyways.)

                That doesn't address why it should be a number, though; it only really says why it shouldn't not be a number. The number lets you make stronger judgements: this Changeling is better adjusted than that Changeling. Why? His Clarity stat is higher. You can't say that if one Changeling is Shocked and another is Abjectly Terrified. This is not dissimilar from saying one Vampire is more human than another, or even that one Werewolf is more Spirit than another. Furthermore, there's value in a player being able to say, "And with these Experiences, I'm going to buy a dot in Clarity." It's a fairly distinct form of character growth to do so. Thirdly, a number allows you to split the stat into zones. Clarity 7-10 might enable certain things, whereas 4-7 might enable different things. That's doable with Conditions, but you'll also pretty quickly wander into a need for Shocked (Low Clarity) and Shocked (High Clarity).

                Honestly, adapting Clarity for 2e should be as simple as coming up with a useful set of Breaking Points and alternate Conditions to inflict on different roll results.

                I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".

                    Except the Lost are also defined by their desire to join one community or another - to seek out the people who understand what they've been through, and to return to their life and family - and what they really need is both (somehow).

                    They are, in fact, Lost; trying to find their Home. That seems a lot like being Forsaken.
                    Had another thought while re-reading this.

                    Unless I've misinterpreted the themes very, very badly, I don't see Forsaken as being about finding your home whatsoever. Forsaken is about choosing your home, not searching for it. Picking a territory isn't about being accepted; it's about staking a claim. There are similarities: Changelings and Werewolves both defend their turf with all their might, of course: but a Werewolf starts with a home, whereas a Changeling ends with a home.

                    For a Werewolf, the important thing is Duty; Survival is the means. A Werewolf joins a Pack and holds territory. They have responsibilities because of who and what they are, and part of those responsibilities is their home. Staying alive and keeping their people alive is just part of that job. A pack-less, home-less Werewolf makes little sense and catches odd glances for a good reason: the presumption is that they've failed somehow, or there's something wrong with them.

                    For a Changeling, the important thing is Survival; Duty is the means. A Changeling joins a Motley, joins a Freehold, not because that's home, but because that helps them stay alive and sane. If they could manage on their own, they probably would. If a Changeling could go without Pledges, they probably would. But they can't, so they form motleys and freeholds in order to shore up that vulnerability. A community of Lost might find it suspicious for some Changeling to be without motley or freehold, but not moreso than anything else. Everything is suspicious; the motley and the freehold structures are just less so.
