    Let's take a little hike with the Margrave of the Brim.

    The Margrave is both a doomsday prepper and a wilderness guide. She helps freeholds defend themselves against threats from the Hedge and also aids changelings make their way through the more dangerous areas.

    The First Edition version of this entitlement (as noted above) was sworn not to join the courts. We removed that restriction because we felt it was one element too many for the concept, but we suggest that the March Lady is a bit above it all. I could see a Summer or Autumn Court Margrave very easily. We also added a little connection to the Magus of the Gilded Thorns through a sidebar; we figured these two blazons would absolutely despise each other, though there's a little Easter Egg about that relationship if you look carefully.

    Here's the Margrave. Tomorrow, since I brought them up, let's do the Magus of the Gilded Thorns.

    Margrave of the Brim

    Briarwolves don’t care who the king is.

    The Hedge is integral to changeling existence yet apart from it. It’s almost possible to ignore it entirely — most of the time. The Margrave of the Brim allows other Lost the privilege of doing so, for she’s taken on the duty of overseeing the Hedge itself. Part guard, part explorer, and part chaperone, the March Lady (or Lord) seeks out freeholds that need help and sets up shop.

    Privileges and Duties

    The Margrave often appears at the establishment of a new freehold, a dangerous time when securing the Hedge is invaluable. She may also arrive to help if there’s been a recent upheaval. She doesn’t involve herself in politics, and if any of the courts put their agendas above the safety of the freehold at large, she’s been known to give them a sharp lesson. The Beasts who take up this title are those who don’t enjoy society or cities but who understand the necessity of freeholds, and they can let their natural impulses free in a way that helps rather than hinders.

    Only the foolhardiest courts spurn the Margrave. The entitlement often has acerbic words for a freehold’s handling (or lack thereof) of Hedge matters, but it backs it up with action. If a problem is serious enough to draw the Margrave, it won’t have gone unnoticed among the Lost.

    The reigning monarch must facilitate anything the Margrave needs to perform her duties, such as information, additional supplies, or a safe place to stay. If the monarch and courts are accommodating, this is a relatively symbiotic relationship, as the Margrave has no interest in usurping any authority when it comes to politics.

    If the monarch balks, the March Lady always finds a way to do her job anyway. Whether this means working with those freehold denizens who aren’t fans of their leadership or making certain the threats from the Hedge are personallyfelt, courts that resist always end up worse off than before.

    Hobgoblins treat the March Lady with a mix of respect and caution (to her face, at least). Hobs know she’ll bargain fairly with them and that she has the skills to protect herself if any of their “pranks” go too far.

    Mask and Mien

    All who are granted the title of March Lord or Lady have an air of competence. The more the years pass, the more powerful this becomes. This is true in the Mask as well as the mien, and there’s always something no-nonsense and trustworthy about how the March Lady carries herself.

    To those who can see past the Mask, the Beast bearing this entitlement is marked by her duties. Each injury taken in the Hedge heals, but a scar remains in the shape of the Thorns she faced. Her nails or claws sharpen to points, all the better to slash her way through the vines. Furthermore, her clothes have a moderate camouflage effect. She smells not unpleasantly of the forest — pine and earth. Perhaps the most striking change of all is that the March Lady’s eyes change to an intense gold, with shifting green flecks.

    Heraldry Token: The Petrified Blade (• to •••••)

    To mortal eyes, this token looks like a plain wooden sword, like one used for training. In its true form, the blade is petrified, with green veins running through the hilt. The scabbard is dark green leather with the title’s crest pressed into it, and the Margrave claims it’s sourced from the hide of a fierce Hedgebeast.

    Effects: When activated, the sword grants knowledge of the nearest Hedge gate and how to awaken it. This works outside or inside the Hedge and lasts for a scene.

    Furthermore, whoever holds the sword gains the Direction Sense Merit while in the Hedge. (Conditional)

    Catch: The user must announce herself loudly and hold the sword high in the air for at least a minute, regardless of the circumstances.

    Drawback: The user gains the Reckless Condition, becoming convinced of her ability to overcome any challenge or take on any foe.

        Let's take a look at the Magus of the Gilded Thorns (if we can find her).

        This entitlement is a bit different from the original version. The Magi of the Gilded Thorn were more about understanding the Hedge and then spreading fear of it among the Lost. This felt a bit redundant with what both the Margrave of the Brim and the Scarecrow Minister do. So, we thought it would be interesting if the Magus of the Gilded Thorns (the plural is deliberate) was the Hedge's voice among the Lost, the keeper of its secrets and its herald. That fit well with the "magus" title and was more in keeping with the way 2E entitlements work. It also provided us with an interesting bit of antagonism with the Margrave that you can play with if you want.

        Here's the Magus. Tomorrow, let's do some more kiths!

        Magus of the Gilded Thorns

        I wait at the center of the Thorns, my wisdom granted only to those who can find me.

        All Lost must pass through the Hedge, and every soul it gouges leaves a little of itself behind. The Magus of the Gilded Thorns is the custodian of all these secrets. She gathers the scraps, carving out trods and communing with the Wyrd. She is in her element and has passed beyond harm. The Hedge protects its Magus, almost jealously, but none can say who’s the master and who’s the servant. Others seek her for protection and guidance, but no matter the problem, she always knows how to help… if you can find her.

        Privileges and Duties

        The Hedge is a boundary between the real world and Faerie, between Clarity and Wyrd, between sense and nonsense, and many changelings treat it as an enemy or a force to be overcome. All creatures who make their homes in the Hedge — hobgoblins, Goblin Queens, Lost in their Hollows — know it’s a place of both whimsy and danger. It doesn’t let travelers pass easily, and it loathes letting anyone leave without paying a price.

        The Magus of the Gilded Thorns speaks directly with the Hedge and knows its motives, hates, and joys. When the Hedge’s mood turns, and it shakes off its shape to try on a new form, the Magus feels it happen. When others try to impress their feelings upon the Hedge, the Magus hears the muffled argument of wills that decide what alterations to accept. When Icons are torn away in the thorns, the Magus knows their secret locations so that nothing is ever irretrievable.

        For this reason, many seek the Magus out, especially those desperate to escape. However, while often willing to help, she’s also the companion of a force that’s both realm and entity, teeming with residents but ultimately unknowable. In return for the Hedge’s knowledge, the Magus must serve as its spokesperson and guardian. She’s allowed to pass unmolested wherever she wishes, but she must aid it in hampering other changelings when the Hedge doesn’t approve of their aims. Likewise, she’s entrusted with keeping its secrets, revealing tidbits only to those who’ve won its favor. While she’s still a changeling, she’s perhaps the only one who’s no longer Lost: She’s pledged her allegiance to the Hedge and come home.

        Mask and Mien

        The Magus’s knowledge is sharp enough to sprout from her face in vicious thorns. Hidden by her Mask, the jagged protrusions may appear as tattoos, birthmarks, or oddly shaped freckles, but their nature is always revealed in the shape of her shadow. As each Magus was already aligned with all things green and growing, at first glance, she could be mistaken for just a prickly Elemental. The Masks of Magi might disguise them as eccentric gardeners, gruff gamekeepers, or just filthy vagrants.

        In truth, part of the Hedge fuses with its Magus, causing Thorns to grow throughout her body. Her features waver and shift — especially when not directly perceived — in the same way the Hedge itself shifts. The Magus’s skin appears to be made of wood, vines, or crumbling soil, and all her garments take on a freshly unearthed quality, covered over in moss, sap, or peeling bark.

        Heraldry Token: The Thorned Crown (• to •••••)

        To humans, this object looks like a tangle of dead branches, but when the Magus places this object on her brow, it manifests as a glorious thorny crown of verdant green beneath, allowing her to tap into the power of the Hedge no matter where she is.

        Effects: When traveling into or out of the Hedge, the user can choose her entry or exit point from anywhere in the local area she’s been before, though she still requires a standing gate to leave. Anyone who follows her will enter the normal location the portal typically connects to. (Conditional)

        [REDACTED]

        Catch: The user willingly cuts herself on the Hedge, taking a lethal damage.

        Drawback: When she takes off the Crown, the user takes the Confused Condition, as it takes time to adjust to cold reality after being attuned to the Hedge.

            Alright, a little late today, but let's have another excerpt. I was going to do a kiths, but I changed my mind and decided to do something a little different: the Grimms.

            For those who don't know, the Grimms are/were a seventh seeming first presented in Dark Eras. For various reasons, the system there isn't compatible with what ultimately ended up in Changeling 2E. I always thought the Grimms deserved a second chance. They're an interesting concept, and seemings are nebulously definable enough that it made sense to me that there might be a seventh one out there (or maybe more).

            Converting the Grimms was wholly a "me" initiative, as I don't think anyone else on the team remembered/knew they existed. I probably would've given them a more complete write-up in the book had I recalled them earlier, so they got put into a slightly glorified sidebar, along with a special entitlement for them (also from Dark Eras).

            The Grimms were an interesting conversion because there was very little I could use from the original. They didn't even have a nickname. So, I tried to do my best to give them a blessing that made sense, while sort of riffing on their original curse. I'm a sucker for shapeshifting powers, and I think the one I gave them has some interesting constraints that will help inform how you can play these guys. My only real regret (apart from them having a fairly short write-up) is that we didn't have the time or space to give them a Regalia of their own. Still, Mirror felt like a natural choice considering their niche, and I did manage to come up with a few seeming benefits for them in Chapter Four. I thought about giving them one of the additional Arcadian Regalia from Kith and Kin, but I also don't like asking people to have books other than the core to make things playable (within reason), and none of them really fit anyway.

            I'm going to go slightly against protocol and show you their entire write-up here; mostly, I just want to give the Grimms a new lease on life, so here they are in toto (though you'll have to buy the book to see their entitlement). Tomorrow, I'll be back with kiths, this time for real. Let's look at our two Darkling kiths: the Echoveils and the Palewraiths.

            Grim: A Tale of Conformity

            I am what you make of me, and that's what sets me free.

            Once upon a time, a seventh seeming emerged from the Hedge in the dark forests of Germany. As the 19th century dawned, mortals began to take a more concerted interest in the fairytales that bound their various cultures together, whether for scholarly or nationalistic reasons. Children’s and Household Tales by the Brothers Grimm was seminal among these efforts, and the True Fae found the stories in this book particularly “flattering,” assuming its tales must be about them. And a few sought to understand this hagiography better.

            Once

            Before Arcadia, a Grimm’s life is almost always dull and dolorous; some were taken by Gentry who wanted to add a little magic to these mortals’ gloomy worlds, but many just wandered into the Hedge, ignoring all the obvious warning signs because “fairytales aren’t real.”

            In Arcadia, this seeming bears flesh marked by myth, embodying all the characters in fairytale fantasies. She is the knight in shining armor and the serpent to be slain. He is the goblin demanding a firstborn child in payment and the mother who reneges. The Others turn them into perfect Mimics to playact fables in agonizing detail, yet learning the ins and outs of these stories saves them in the end. They gain strength from following all the beats, so well that even the Fae can’t prevent their happy endings.

            Now

            The Lost seldom encounter Grimms, as boring people are rarely targets for the Others, who also seem to have lost interest in whatever fancy took them two centuries ago. But seldom doesn’t mean never. Mimics serve the Lost as actors and envoys, and some rival the Darklings as spies, albeit with a far more social approach to espionage. Some work to help humans understand the odd tropes of fairytales so they’ll be more wary of the True Fae, but many just seek out old souls who like the classics best. Wherever mortal hearts ache for a simpler world, where all the heroes are brave, the villains dastardly, and the morals easy, you shall find the Grimms.

            Nicknames: Mimics, Fabulists, the Compliant

            Blessing: Gain an additional dot of one Finesse Attribute at character creation. Your character’s physical form shifts to fit her many personae. Once per day, she can take on any humanoid appearance that would fit an archetypal role (a damsel in distress, a brash hero, etc.) as long as it doesn’t resemble a real person she knows of and wouldn’t significantly alter her size. She gains the 9-again quality on any Social actions that help maintain this role. Reverting to her true form takes only an instant action. This costume lasts as long as your character behaves appropriately (even alone), but changelings can still see her mien unless she strengthens her Mask. It costs a Glamour per scene to maintain this form if anyone questions her motives or mocks her.

            Curse: In addition to your character’s other breaking points, she risks Clarity damage with a dice pool equal to half her Wyrd (rounded up) when she takes an action that benefits herself but adds nothing to the role she’s embodying.

            Regalia: Mirror

            Tales

            She runs one hell of a table. She knows all the systems by heart, but it’s the character work where she shines. Her players don’t realize the lessons she’s imparting whenever a favorite NPC meets a gruesome fate, but they always come away valuing their loved ones just a little more…

            They’re the homeless guy begging for smokes one day and the rich bitch cutting you off in her Jag the next. They say they just like to make people’s lives more interesting, but in their heart, they want to know if someone — anyone — can see who they are behind the acts.

            She’s in deep. The Huntsman thinks she’s its sniveling little privateer. The monarchs think she’s their double agent. The vampires? They think she’s one of theirs. The side she’s really on isn’t important: It’s what she’s planning to do when she gets everyone in one place that’ll be really explosive.

                    As promised, let's look at a couple of Darkling kiths: The Echoveils and the Palewraiths.

                    The Palewraiths make their return from Winter Masques, one of the supplements that was most influential for this book. They're not too different from their original incarnation, such as it was, but they have a much more robust blessing. As for the Echoveils, you're probably wondering what obscure book we dug them out of. Technically, they're a 2E version of the Nightsingers. "But Yossarian," I hear you say, "aren't the Nightsingers right there in the core?" They are indeed, hypothetical interlocutor. Right there. In the core.

                    Look, it pays to double-check these things.

                    Joking aside, we goofed, but it turned out not to really matter. The Nightsingers in CTL2E are very different from the original version, and our take was much closer to that of Winter Masques. Rather than scrap a completed draft, we just swapped out the name, and that was that, without any real overlap with the kith in the core. Echoveil is a really cool name for a kith, incidentally, and I'm eternally grateful to the author for coming up with it on short notice.

                    (We also doubled up the Chimera, who are in Dark Eras 2, but that was genuinely intentional. As far as you know.)

                    Tomorrow, let's have another entitlement: The Duke of the Icebound Heart.

                    Echoveil (Darkling)

                    Sure, I’ll play for your little party, but the music stops if anyone sees me.

                    Every horror movie needs a soundtrack. The Echoveils represent the haunting beauty and odd melancholy of music that floats through the darkness and sounds of the night. These Darklings weren’t taken to perform publicly but instead to provide ambiance or atmosphere. Whether behind a curtain at the twisted fae ball or in the trees to torment the hunted, when people hear the Echoveil’s music, it’s already over. Not all selected for this task are sad goths or depressed contraltos, but anyone taken as an Echoveil understands solitude all too well.

                    An Echoveil is kept in the background, providing an eerie descant to the torments their Keeper inflicts. Perhaps she’s a violinist playing higher and faster with each chunk of flesh the Fae extracts. Or he might hide in the woods, playing predator to the prey that can’t stop running. Echoveils who escape often do so by covering their tracks with music and waiting until they know their Keepers are “distracted” with their latest victims.

                    Because Echoveils were safest when they were heard and not seen, they may struggle with one-on-one conversations, though many enjoy being around other people as long as they aren’t the center of attention. They’re still incredibly talented musicians, but when they play, the memories return of when they could be nothing more than a passive witness to suffering and death.

                    Kith Blessing

                    When an Echoveil uses Expression to affect others with sad or frightening music, achieving three successes counts as an exceptional success.

                    Eerie Nocturne: [REDACTED]

                    Palewraith (Darkling)

                    Do you have to die to be a ghost?

                    Whether an underground mine or a windowless library, the Palewraith was kept in darkness so long that the light shies away. Those stolen already lived their lives half in the dark, and in previous centuries, that meant miners or factory workers. Today, it’s just as likely to be an IT professional or an underground technician. Some Palewraiths are created intentionally as spies or specters, but for others, it’s a side effect, their task keeping them in a lightless tunnel or dark cellar. Still others had the light leached from them for cruel experiments.

                    The longer the changeling spends in darkness, the more the light forgets her. As she labors underground, her fellow workers see her less and less, and her physical form seems to fade away. But it’s an illusion. Her mind is as sharp as ever; she waits until she’s forgotten and then simply floats away.

                    Many Lost feel unreal upon their return, but for the Palewraith, it’s more than that. Some go to extremes to prove they aren’t invisible, wearing garish colors and making mischief because even negative attention is an acknowledgment. These Palewraiths often end up in the Spring Court, but those who use their new forms rather than fight them make excellent Autumn Court spies and Winter Court assassins. Yet a fear lurks inside every Palewraith, a voice that says they’re nothing more than shadows, and that one day, those around them will stop seeing them altogether.

                    Kith Blessing

                    When a Palewraith uses Stealth to avoid detection in an area with little or no natural light, achieving three successes counts as an exceptional success.

                            Alright, late post today because I had to spend way too much time on hold today, so just a quick one: The Duke of the Icebound Heart.

                            This is a really interesting entitlement to me because he's kind of just in it for himself. That's absolutely a thing the Wyrd would encourage, but I kind of love that this title's ethos is to be a scumbag. There's a thorniness to what the Duke (or Duchess) does that brings some really amazing drama (assuming everyone at the table is good with it), and despite being a fairly straightforward concept, he's one of the standouts of the book.

                            The Duke was the first character we got illustrated for the entitlements. We wanted to do something a little different with them, because A) we needed to simplify things for budget purposes and B) we really wanted to emphasize that these were noble titles. The artist did an amazing job with all the portraits, but the Duke is one of the ones I keep returning too. The Byronic headdress is both cool and perfectly douchey.

                            So, here's the Duke. I think we're starting to quiet down a bit with these threads, so my current plan is to preview the rest of the entitlements, and then we'll call it a close (maybe one more Forgotten seeming too). This book is SO big, and I could spend the rest of the month previewing stuff. Tomorrow, let's check in on the Eternal Echo.

                            Duke of the Icebound Heart

                            I thought I could never love again. That is, until I met you.

                            It’s common enough to forswear love after heartbreak, but the Icebound Duke (or Duchess) takes it several steps further: To never be hurt, he must become the heartbreaker. This could be as obvious as a pickup artist looking for lonely women at closing time, but most holders of this entitlement take pride in being subtle, finding ever different ways to capture hearts. When she looks at you with those shy brown eyes, you know you’re the sole focus of her attention — she’s just being polite to all those other guys, but it’s you she really cares about. You think you’re imagining his eyes on you; he’s said he’s straight, and you’ve seen no evidence to the contrary, but something in the timbre of his voice says otherwise…

                            Privileges and Duties

                            The Duke is a rake and a cad, shattering hearts wherever he goes. The Wyrd demands he be callous — if not cruel — to his lovers, always leaving them wanting more or cursing his name. It’s his duty, though the reasons are unclear. Some Dukes believe they’re meant to harden mortal hearts against the Gentry’s seductions, but one would be hard-pressed to call the entitlement’s hedonism altruistic. Others have claimed they’re honor-bound to bring more pleasure into the world, and that true love would be a restraint on that purpose. Yet others shrug and say they have no idea what they’re good for, but it’s a sweet gig if you can get it.

                            Monarchs have mixed feelings about allowing the Duke in their freeholds. He always causes some sort of chaos, no matter how much he claims it’s mortal hearts he’s meant to break. More than one Icebound Duke has been run out of town after causing problems, anything from breaking up a marriage to nearly starting a blood feud with the local vampires. However, he’s also a coveted figure; the name “Duke of the Icebound Heart” alone carries weight, and while the monarchs may not be happy with his presence, the rest of the freehold often is.

                            He’s exciting, after all, hosting parties that skimp on nothing and bringing a certain cachet to any freehold that hosts him. Furthermore, those parties bring in money. Lost from other freeholds jockey for invitations, and it’s tradition for them to bring gifts. Historically, these were supposed to be tokens or jewelry, and while it’s still considered bad form to write a check or send a money transfer, few freeholds actually reject them.

                            The Duke is also an expert at playing social games, and if a monarch allies with him, he provides invaluable assistance in politicking and intrigue. Any Duke worth his salt (and who wants to stick around for more than a few weeks) knows whose good side he needs to stay on.

                            Mask and Mien

                            Whether in Mask or mien, the Duke is the ideal representation of whatever type he’s chosen to be. It doesn’t matter if he dresses in finery as the haughty noble or if he wears thrift store rags to better seem the haunted rogue. His clothing always shows off his form to his best advantage, even if it seems accidental.

                            The titleholder is typically attractive, but it’s his mix of sadness and longing that draws looks. His mien doesn’t gain beauty so much as a quality of remoteness, but one that comes with the possibility of breaking through that’s hard to resist. His hair, lips, and fingernails become lightly rimed with frost, and his eyes focus on whomever he’s talking to as though she were the only one in the world.

                            Heraldry Token: Winter's Heart (• to •••••)

                            This token’s Mask appears as a large diamond, imperfect but still beautiful, held in a crimson velvet bag embroidered with the Duke’s sigil. In its true form, it looks to be carved from solid ice and is cold to the touch. On activation, the heart warms just a bit, as if on the edge of melting, though it never does.

                            Effects: By speaking a subject’s name when the Heart is activated, the Duke becomes the focus of their thoughts for a moment, no matter where in the world they are. This must be someone known to the Duke, though they needn’t have had any romantic interest in him. (Conditional)

                            Drawback: The user temporarily develops the Wanton Condition. If this isn’t resolved by the next time he sleeps, he will have particularly hedonistic dreams but wake with the Condition faded away.

                                Let's sit down with the Eternal Echo.

                                One of the fun things about doing these entitlements (or at least reading them, since I didn't write any of them) is seeing how the old concepts work in the new paradigm. The Eternal Echo is a really great example of that, I think. The new version is recognizable, but it operates very differently, and we had to give it a narrative tool that justified it being one changeling instead of a bunch of them. We could've made it a legion entitlement, but I love the way the author threaded the needle on the title's goal of preserving Lost history while also being just an individual. Their token is basically a plot hook machine, and I really love the idea of them having a little entourage/cult (por qué no los dos?) that aids in their task.

                                This is another very straightforward entitlement, but I think it's endless usable as both a player concept and an ST device.

                                Oh, one minor post-development change was the benefit of their entitlement Merit. Originally, it gave them longer life, but I decided that worked better as a narrative effect in the write-up. That's always the problem with immortality/life-lengthening powers: they're something you'd want in real life, but in a game, they rarely matter.

                                Tomorrow, let's look at the Noble Sages of the Unknown Reaches.

                                The Eternal Echo

                                I’m listening.

                                Too often, a changeling claws her way back from Arcadia’s impossible realms only to find the world has forgotten her. Perhaps a fetch took over her life; perhaps she was declared dead; or perhaps she simply stepped out of the Hedge decades (or centuries) after she was taken. The life she struggled so hard to return to is gone, and her story no longer has a place. It happens all the time.

                                However, no matter how alienated or overlooked a changeling might feel, the Eternal Echo always wants to hear their tale. The Echo remembers the story of every Lost, whether it’s a tale of epic heroism, a bloody tragedy, or just a humble life lived well. She roams the world ensuring that even those changelings who return as no one will still be remembered by someone.

                                Privileges and Duties

                                The Eternal Echo travels from freehold to freehold, meeting with citizens to record their stories. Some freeholds even have rituals based around the Echo’s arrival, holding celebrations or games in her honor. The Echo’s arrival in Naha is treated as a public holiday, and a festival is held so the Lost may gather to hear her recite stories of changelings long passed on.

                                The Echo may be a solitary title, but she rarely travels alone. Each Echo inevitably gathers an entourage of friends, devotees, and guardians — some who aren’t even Lost. Hobgoblins, fae-touched, witches, shapeshifters, and more have all joined the Echo in one incarnation or another. An Echo active in the late 1800s was known to travel with a powerful ghostly advisor for decades, the specter agreeing to serve as both council and bodyguard in exchange for his story being preserved and spread.

                                To serve an Echo is a great honor for most Lost, as well as a way to ensure being remembered for ages to come. Accordingly, much is expected of an Eternal Echo. She is sworn to be truthful and impartial, with grave retribution delivered by the title itself should a holder abuse her position.

                                Mask and Mien

                                After taking up the mantle, the Eternal Echo’s mien changes in small but distinct ways. The first is that her pupils expand to cover her eyes, becoming pools of shining black nothing can escape. Her ears change too, becoming larger and more sensitive, twitching and swiveling in response to sound. Finally, a susurration of whispers can be faintly heard in the Echo’s presence, especially when she’s recalling a stored memory.

                                In contrast, her Mask hardly shifts at all, but it does take on a certain aura of attentiveness, which can sometimes be unnerving to those unused to scrutiny.

                                Heraldry Token: The Record of the Lost (• to •••••)

                                The current form of this ancient token appears in its Mask as a battered tape recorder with the title’s heraldry as a logo. The deck is jammed shut, so there’s no way to remove the unlabeled cassette, which emits only haunting static when played. Beneath this disguise, the Record is a beautiful device of polished wood and brass gears that plays crisp, clear sound and has exceptional recording fidelity.

                                Effects: When activated, the Record can be made to play back any piece of audio it’s ever recorded. This represents a staggering amount of content, as the Record is ancient, and it doesn’t have an index. A user must ask the token to play a particular piece of audio they know it contains, or her player must make a successful Wits + Occult roll to get it to play what she wants; the Echo (and only the Echo) enjoys a bonus to this roll equal to the token’s rating. A failure means the Record will play a random piece of audio for a few minutes before shutting itself off.

                                Catch: The user tells the Record a truth she’s never told anyone else.

                                Drawback: The user forgets something minor of the Storyteller’s choice. If she used the catch, it’s the truth she used to activate the Record.

                                    And now for something completely (read: somewhat) different: The Noble Sages of the Unknown Reaches.

                                    This entitlement was very much cribbing some notes from the Dauphines of the Wayward Children in Oat, Ash, and Thorn, providing individual roles for each member of its trio: The Archivist, the Diplomat, and the Bane. We felt that just having them be occultists was a bit too simple, so adding a physical and social character to the default mental one was a nice way of expanding their concept. It also made sure that Darklings of many varieties could join up, not just the more intellectual types.

                                    I'm also a fan of legion entitlements where the members are slightly at odds, and I believe each of their Legends is about what happens when one of them decides they don't need the other two.

                                    I believe this is the only entitlement where we didn't use their original logo from First Edition. The old one is a big collage of a bunch of random symbols from other gamelines. I honestly didn't really care for it, and it looked bad no matter what I did when I tried to make a vector version, so we whipped up a simple new one that I think communicates their concept a bit more clearly.

                                    So, enjoy the Sages! Tomorrow, let's look at the Tolltaker Knight.

                                    Noble Sages of the Unknown Reaches

                                    Most “monsters” just want to be heard… But yes, some of them will try to eat you.

                                    Every Lost is aware they aren’t the only strange beings wandering the world; after all, one only has to peer into the Hedge to encounter a menagerie of goblins and ghouls. However, plenty of monsters stalk the “mundane” side of the Thorns. Scheming corpses stealing blood, fallen beings from other realms, patchwork people leaving rot and anger in their wake, and even stranger things.

                                    Most Lost are vaguely aware these exotic beings exist, and the Autumn Court even studies them. Mere study isn’t enough for some Lost, however. The Noble Sages of the Unknown Reaches (the “Lord” Sages in more patriarchal eras) are a trio who dedicate themselves to not only studying and conversing with other denizens of the night but to truly knowing them, in every possible sense of the word.

                                    Privileges and Duties

                                    The titles of the Noble Sages languished in obscurity for centuries, but they’ve gained fame and notoriety in recent years with the exponential growth of communication technology, allowing them to both share and collect information over vast distances. While the acquisition and consolidation of monstrous knowledge is their overarching goal, the Sages’ membership varies in both methodology and purpose.

                                    The Archivist is the most stereotypical of the three, concerned with keeping records, verifying or disproving myths, and organizing the title’s vast repository of lore. In some ways, they have the hardest job, as most supernatural beings take pains to stay hidden and don’t typically leave written records lying around.

                                    Enter the Diplomat. The most social of the group, the Diplomat is equal part investigator and entertainer, as his role is to make face-to-face contact with things that may not even have faces to begin with. Finding esoteric and eldritch beings is difficult, but that’s often the easy part compared with convincing them not to devour him for disturbing them. Sly and determined, the Diplomat uses whatever methods he can to maintain cordial relations, and it’s from this facet that the group’s most lurid rumors spring.

                                    Finally, for monsters that can’t be bribed, persuaded, or seduced, there’s the Bane. She takes the information gained from the other two and applies a lethal edge, learning from both folklore and firsthand accounts how to slay, banish, or bind anything that goes bump in the night.

                                    Differences in approach often lead to bickering and resentment between the three Sages, but they’re united in their mission to understand their fellow monsters. Their collective knowledge is available to anyone who can meet their price. Their preferred currency is information they can add to their trove, though they’re known to accept more mundane payment, on occasion.

                                    Mask and Mien

                                    The Archivist’s mien becomes scholarly and bookish: Their eyes grow larger and darker, and their fingertips appear permanently ink-stained. Their Mask always includes reading glasses; even if their vision is perfect, a pair will appear tucked away in a pocket or perched on their head.

                                    The Diplomat’s mien glimmers with starlight: He becomes captivating, and people will turn their heads to look because he’s just so interesting. Elaborate tattoos bloom on his Mask, always in places half-hidden by clothing, as if inviting an observer to ask what they mean or to wonder what they look like when he’s undressed.

                                    The Bane’s mien shifts in a subtly dangerous direction: Her teeth get sharper, and her eyes become rounder and more piercing, like a hawk’s. She always appears to be on high alert, assessing everyone in the room (and the room itself) for threats. The Bane’s Mask gifts her with a powerful scowl, and it takes effort for her not to appear to be moments away from decking someone.

                                    Heraldry Token: The Arcane Rings (• to •••••)

                                    To mortal eyes, these items look like cheap pieces of costume jewelry, but beneath the Mask, each is gorgeously crafted from a single piece of metal: brass for the Archivist, silver for the Diplomat, and steel for the Bane. Each Arcane Ring corresponds to a symbol from the entitlement’s heraldry: a quill for the Archivist, a crown for the Diplomat, and a sword for the Bane.

                                    Effects: Upon activation, the Sage can speak the name of a type of non-fae supernatural being (zombies, werewolves, witches, angels, etc.) and gain the effects of the Encyclopedic Knowledge Merit in reference to it.

                                    Drawback: The user gains the Obsession Condition focused on learning something new about the named being.

                                        Not much time to post today, so here's a quick one on the Tolltaker Knight.

                                        This is definitely one of the more action- and purpose-driven entitlements in the book. Fae bounty hunters aren't that uncommon in Changeling (that's what the Huntsmen are, in the end), but I really like the form that concept takes here. The original Tolltaker Knighthood was about targeting only those who provably deserved it, which is a bit subjective depending on the Knights. We've shifted that slightly to be that the Knight can only target characters who have escaped punishment or broken a vow. It's a fine distinction, but I think it adds a little more nuance to the character.

                                        So, here's the Tolltaker Knight. Tomorrow, I'm gonna give myself a day off, but sound off if you'd like to see another entitlement or one of the Forgotten seemings we haven't got to!

                                        The Tolltaker Knight

                                        I know the cost of broken vows. I’ll make sure he pays the price.

                                        The Tolltaker Knight never stays anywhere for very long. The Round Table’s not for him. Depending on who you ask, he’s a mercenary, an assassin, a vigilante, or a hero. He doesn’t deny he takes payment to hurt people, but several caveats set him apart from a common goon.

                                        First, he’s precise; if it’s required that one finger be broken, that’s exactly what he does. Second, he never takes payment in money: instead, he takes tokens, Glamour, and other oddities. Third, and most significantly, he only accepts specific contracts — his targets must have broken a promise or escaped a punishment they were due. They may have escaped the Wyrd’s wrath by keeping the wording nonspecific (or by not being a changeling at all), but they won’t escape the Knight. The transgression can be anything from divulging a friend’s secret to violating an oath as an officer of the law.

                                        The Knight also requires proof. An angry ex claiming they were cheated on isn’t enough without evidence of the promise made and of it then being broken. He doesn’t care who ghosts you. And in cases where someone tries to trick the Knight into unfairly punishing an innocent, his wrath is turned sevenfold on the deceiver.

                                        Privileges and Duties

                                        Finding an Ogre to beat up someone who wronged you isn’t hard, and some people continue to see the Tolltaker Knight as just that: a hooligan for hire. Those Lost who understand the title hold it in high esteem, and every Knight who manages to keep his job does his best to improve its reputation. He sees it as his solemn duty to punish those the Wyrd can’t. He doesn’t deny he’s using his own judgment to decide who deserves his attention, but he views the fact that he has the title at all as approval from the Wyrd. Indeed, any Knight who strays from the path — going after those who don’t fit the parameters or where no proof exists — will find his Knighthood taken and be left bleeding where it was torn out.

                                        The payment the Tolltaker Knight receives varies, and depending on the assignment’s difficulty, it could be anything from a smidgen of Glamour to a priceless artifact, or even the heraldry token of another blazon. The Knights have always said they require this to seal the bargain on the Wyrd’s behalf, but some say it’s just an affectation, a way to seem more than hired muscle.

                                        In most cases, a contract on a nonhuman entity is more expensive than on a mortal, but there are exceptions. When Miss Lockhart, Chief Steward of the Boise Winter Court, hired the Tolltaker Knight to mess up a corrupt official’s face before his latest publicity stunt, the Knight had her sign over her entire Hollow — an assault on a public figure is both complicated and risky.

                                        It’s not unheard of for a Knight to accept a contract to kill, but the bar is high. He’ll only take such a contract on a target who swore a serious oath, violated it in a way that caused death or severe harm, and then escaped justice. In the Mexican city of Puebla, Judge Arturo Suárez took bribes to jail locals on trumped-up charges. Suárez didn’t know the court stenographer was a member of the Autumn Court, and she’d been paying attention. When she discovered he’d taken money from a banking executive to have a community leader sent to a deadly prison on false drug charges, she called in the Tolltaker Knight. Suárez was soon found dead in his courtroom, beaten to a pulp with his own gavel.

                                        Mask and Mien

                                        The Tolltaker Knight always looks like he can handle himself in a fight. While he dresses in practical clothing, it’s usually tidy and respectable. His voice takes on a resonant quality, carrying further than before even in his Mask. No matter his size or strength, his footsteps are almost soundless. His Mask makes him look like someone who knows exactly what you did: incisive eyes and a serious expression. He always seems to loom just a bit, regardless of his height.

                                        In his mien, those incisive eyes grow sharper, shining with righteous judgment. Anything he wears fits him like a bespoke suit of armor and the air around him darkens enough to make his lantern light stand out even more.

                                        Heraldry Token: The Luminous Lodestar (• to •••••)

                                        In its Mask, the Lodestar looks like any other lantern; it could have been purchased at a department store for holiday decorations or a summer camping trip. Upon activation, intricate filigrees appear, and the glass panes become flawlessly clear. The light that glows within has a silvery quality unlike any store-bought lantern.

                                        Effects: When activated, the Lodestar’s light changes color based on the nearness of the miscreant the Knight’s tracking. When the target is within two kilometers, the silver light turns to gold, becoming a deeper, more burnished hue the closer the Knight gets to his quarry. By the time the Knight is within 30 meters, the lantern glows red.

                                        Catch: The user must make a promise to the Wyrd. This counts as a sealing but has no Glamour cost.

                                        Drawback: The user suffers a –5 on Stealth and Subterfuge for the rest of the scene.

                                            Hello again! Happy Wednesday! Let's look at another entitlement: the Castellan of the Broken Cage.

                                            Originally known as the Bodhisattvas of the Broken Cage, this is probably the most different entitlement in the book (perhaps aside from the Magistrates of the Wax Mask). For one, we felt the Bodhisattva title was inaccurate and a tad appropriative, and we liked the idea of this character being a "Castellan" for a metaphorically breached object. We also gave the title a little more terminology, likening what it does to helping butterflies escape the chrysalis. The basic idea of this entitlement is more or less the same as in First Edition, but it's a little more focused, for lack of a better term, and more overtly altruistic in a kind of horrifying way.

                                            Tomorrow, let's look at the Legate of the Black Apple, which, sadly, is our last entitlement. After that, we'll finish off the Forgotten seemings, and then I think we'll call it a day! After that, I'll be updating the pdf with errata, but I'll make sure to keep everyone posted.

                                            Castellan of the Broken Cage

                                            Oh, come on — just try it. What’s the worst that could happen?

                                            Sometimes the caterpillar needs encouragement to break out of its cocoon. The Castellan of the Broken Cage pushes, corrupts, and emboldens others to break free of society’s chains. Each Castellan approaches this differently: Some are straightforward about their intent, while others will set their target challenges without letting these subjects know they’ve been chosen.

                                            The Castellan believes convention, hidebound institutions, and rule-following are anathema to personal growth. As a Beast, she thinks it would be a far better world if people gave in to their instincts a little more. Her goal is always transformation, but the scale of it can be anything from helping a shy Lost sing in public to shifting the way an entire Autumn Court operates if it’s too “stagnant.”

                                            The targets of these changes are referred to as chrysalids, and they aren’t consulted about whether they want to be liberated from social conformity. The Castellan targets anyone who strikes her as needing her help, be they human, Lost, or something stranger.

                                            Privileges and Duties

                                            The Castellan asks little from others; her primary privilege is seeing her goals achieved, and she derives satisfaction from knowing she’s affected the future of another. Furthermore, those who take up this entitlement believe that holding fast to tradition and convention makes it easier for the Gentry to seek targets, whereas rapid change and dynamic growth confuse them.

                                            Unlike those from more flamboyant entitlements, a Castellan doesn’t announce herself to the freehold’s monarchs upon arrival. She trusts that those who need to find her will do so. Many Lost respect the Castellan, and she never lacks for changelings approaching her to suggest chrysalid candidates. Even so, some avoid her like the plague. They see her as arrogant, believing she knows better than everyone else, and indeed, the Castellan’s Beast mien often has an element of the trickster about it: foxes who convince others to take a wrong turn leading somewhere magnificent, or magpies who revel in knocking over perfectly placed decorations.

                                            Whether it’s pushing an angry would-be activist to stop posting online and run for office or enticing a religious ascetic into a world of hedonism, the Castellan genuinely believes she’s doing what’s best for her chrysalid. This is her duty, and if she acts too often out of selfishness, she may well lose her title. Of course, unintended consequences are common for the chrysalid — an all-consuming new hobby leading to the breakdown of a marriage, or even addiction or arrest, depending on what being “released from rules and conventions” means to her.

                                            Not only do the ramifications vary, but so do the reactions. A Castellan may gently help a timid nerd out of his shell only for him to grow angry because the girl he likes still won’t date him. Another might induce a student to drop out and take up drinking and partying only for her to realize she’s much happier without the pressure of expectations.

                                            Mask and Mien

                                            The mien attached to this title represents the need for change and transformation: A Castellan’s eyes are whirling pools of shifting color, without pupils or sclera. This bleeds into the Mask, with her irises growing larger and brightly hued. And no matter her Beast features, she also develops new insectoid ones: antennae emerging from her hair, small wings on her back, etc. If her mien already involved wings, they become gossamer and ethereal. The Castellan also becomes taller and thinner than before, surrounded by an iridescent, rainbow aura.

                                            Heraldry Token: The Lepidopteran Locket (• to •••••)

                                            In its mask, this token is a simple closed locket on a gold chain with the entitlement’s heraldry engraved on the front, easily hidden beneath clothing. All it contains is a faded picture of a chrysalis. When the locket is activated, it pops open if it was previously shut, and the picture transforms. Color floods the photograph, and the chrysalis shifts into, of course, a butterfly. The locket glows with a soft light, shining through whatever the Castellan is wearing, whether convenient or not.

                                            Effects: Upon activation, the locket provides the Castellan with awareness of her current chrysalid, as well as what they’re doing. If her current target is a group, she becomes aware of the largest gathering of them.

                                            Catch: The wielder must change something in their surroundings: destroying a tree, painting a wall, or even cutting her own hair. The change must be large enough to be noticeable and not easily reversed.

                                            Drawback: The user finds it difficult to repeat the same action in the same scene. If she takes an action requiring a roll and succeeds, any subsequent action involving the same dice pool suffers a cumulative –1, increasing every time she repeats the action.
