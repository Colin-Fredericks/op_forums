    Originally posted by Gaius View Post

    Summarizing a few comments from this thread, including some from developer and writer:
    1) "Seriously hurt" is not the same as "hurt to any degree whatsoever."
    2) The current thinking is to cover unintended/collateral serious hurt. If a Fairest makes a call that involves the injury of Jimmy the Intern, no harm no foul if that happens (at least where the Curse is concerned.
    3) Since we don't know how Clarity operates, we can't actually say how bad this is. But we do know that it's a roll: there's a guaranteed free Clarity for stepping up, and only a chance of losing it if something goes awry in the manner described. The Fairest might come out ahead, with a chance of just being no worse off than at the start.
    Also, it's worth noting that every single Second Edition 'morality' analogue has offered Beats when you hit a breaking point, even if you succeed. So these things are also blessings in disguise.

    David A Hill Jr
    Freelance Writer
    Independent Game Designer

                        Originally posted by Ceycil View Post
                        Thats a dramtic change of direction for the fairest.
                        No longer just a pretty face right enough.
                        Never been too keen on having a fifth of the characters loosely classed as "Leaders" though.
                        Tends to result in players feeling that they Have to take charge and buy up social abilities or that most of the seasonal monarchs should be fairest in the setting.
                        Perhaps something more along the lines of the fairest being shining paragons excelling at whatever they focus on?
                        The fairest leader is the most convincing on public speakers who turns hearts with honeyed words.
                        The fairest fighter is a elegent duelest who wields his blade with elegence and grace.
                        The fairest thief is the cat burglar who steals the most heavily guarded item in the musuem and leaves a calling card too boot.
                        I agree very much. This is like the ventrue of old world of darkness. I'm afraid it doesn't make them interesting as much as it makes them a bit one-dimensional.
                        I also do not enjoy the idea at all that not only are you lore-wise connected to be a leader but that your whole blessing depends on it. It limits rather than opens the door. If we compare to the former version changeling, you often found in the books that rather than excluding character concepts or ideas the core-book relished in telling you just how different and awesome you could make each and every single character you ever made. I do not feel this with the fairest at all in this draft.

                        "A Fairest may not be the leader, but fate makes them a leader time and time again. Whether they embrace that, or run from it."
                        This is exactly how you shouldn't do it because it pushes the player (and one fifth of you freehold) into a pre-set position. And not only that, a pre-set position of being a nice and good character, because it is also mentioned in the draft: "                        To the Changeling eye, he can never remove the sign of leadership, often in the form of a small crown or military decoration. Whatever she does, the Fairest duty and responsibility peek through, and so when someone is in trouble. Without consideration, it’s the Fairest’s feet they will throw themselves down on." and "The Fairest struggles, every day and night, to live up to their own myth, to be the great hero that they expected to be."

                        This is not cool! Reading the seemings and kiths so far has been a delight, but I had to stop at the fairest and re-read them again because this is the one area where it didn't go as planned. Rather than opening the horizons to endless variations the description here makes it seem as if the fairest are the leaders, no matter if they like it or not, and the fairest are more often than not the good guys. This doesn't feel very world of darkness, to put it bluntly. The leader caste, if there must be one, should be full of selfish, cruel, distant people. That's world of darkness. Things are meant to be scary, horrible and bad.

                        So what would I see different? First of all, a fairest should be someone who excells at what they do, not the leader of the freehold. We don't have to repeat vampire. Anyone can lead and anyone can fail while doing so. As said before me by Cecyl,                         trying to make themselves shining paragons at whatever they do fits their situation better to. Excell, stand out and be above others. Recover clarity when you outshine someone who you consider to be of equal skill than yourself and lose clarity when you attempted to do something important but failed in front of others who will judge you for your failure (for example).

                        Secondly, change the description/idea for their personalities. There should definitely not be a "good guy" group in a world of darkness game. I am aware that I am comparing a lot to the former version of changeling here, but let's take a piece of the old fairest description.
                        "This is the way of the Fae, and it’s the way of the Fairest: they take what and whom they will take, and they will have their fun first. It is their prerogative to be loved and admired, and their right to treat that love any way they will. Sure, they’ll try to rise above it, but there’s always the fact that they really are the fairest of them all. They won their beauty fairly. They deserve to be beautiful."

                        And here is their curse description:
                        "Curse: The Fairest, similar to the creatures who stole them, can be callous and unfeeling, vicious and prone to toy with others, even people who love them."
                        We should absolutely not turn what was kind of the darkest and the cruelest of the seemings into the 'good' characters of this game. Maybe it was wording that I didn't really understand, English it not my native language, or maybe it was a design choice. In either case, it doesn't feel world of darkness at all. It feels like reading the description of a good elven race in D&D.

                        Sorry for long post here is a
                        TLDR.
                        Remove leadership and hero position.
                        Introduce paragons and villain position.

                        And once again I'd like to add, I have loved all the stuff I have read so far, especially the kith blessing look -really- freaking cool. I enjoy almost everything that you guys have put out so far but not this draft. It needs to be reworked/reworded!

                            Might also want to point out that the other main splats have an archetype who specializes in leadership roles. Vampires have Ventrue, Werewolves have Storm Lords, Don't know about Mages, but I'm going to assume the Obrimos or Mastigos, based on what I've heard about them.


                            Also, I think you're being unfair by saying the Fairest being shoe horned into being leaders. Remember that this time around, Kiths are not restricted by Seeming. You can be anything you want, but your Seeming is how you apply it. If you don't want to be a leader, you don't have to be, you can pick another Seeming and still choose the same Kith. If anything it offers more customization than the previous version.

                            As for the Fairest being 1/5th of the population, that's a bit of a stretch. You're saying the number of Changelings is evenly spread among everybody, which is very unlikely to be the case. How can you call yourself "fariest of them all" if you've got 20 super models walking around the place stealing your fans? The way I imagine it, Fairest would actually be a rare Seeming and those who possess it would be among the top ranking positions of a Free Hold, rather than being in every position of government. They're not the supervisor or the lawyers, or the board of directors, they're the CEO's and company presidents. They don't work for the company, they own the company!


                            You also are forgetting that Changelings NEED to stick together. That is the one thing they have that the True Fae don't. They share power rather than hoarding it all for themselves. The True Fae can not comprehend what it means to feel true selflessness or charity for others. If the leaders of the courts are stealing from the city's coffers and sending the Sheriff of Nottingham to extort people, they might as well put a giant, gleaming sign right in front of the Hedge saying "here we are!"


                            Changeling is a game about psychological horror. You were taken to a strange, alien world, you were forced into slavery, warped physically and mentally, and you somehow managed to escape. Now you've got Huntsmen tracking you day and night, waiting for the chance to drag you back into slavery, where a very pissed off Keeper is likely waiting to punish you. And here you have the Fairest, who the rest of the court is looking to for support and leadership. They place their lives in your hands... and then you fuck up and get them killed or worse.


                            How is that for a horror story?
