    Originally posted by Eliseo ap Liam View Post
    Ok, let's see. If your Seeming is the way you escaped this could be:

    Beasts: You return to your most primal self, to your animal parts. That's not just wild, it's pure instict. Only impulses not reasoning.
    Darklings: You embrace the darkest parts of yourself. You flee harming others, or hurting yourself, physically or mentally. It can be betrayal, sacrifice, some kind of fae-death or much darker things.
    Elementals: You forget to be human, you become something else because you can't escape Feerie if you don't understand it. And to understand Feerie you have to be a part of it.
    Fairest: You lie, seduce, intimidate, manipulate... You're the Beauty, the Grace. And (like the old tales tell) Beauty means you can do, you can get anything you want, by any mean you need.
    Ogres: Violence. The ogres has always been creatures of violence, not just brute force, but abuse and torture, both mental and physical.
    Wizened: You keep withering, until you where nothing but your work. And your work, and through your slavery you free yourself.

    I think the most important thing is to get something that can makes that any kind of flight work for every Seeming. For example, if you betray your Keeper: a Beast could embrace his primal being until become the best dog of his Keeper until one day he bites him and eat him; a Darkling could sell his own shadow to get a poison that will kill everyone with a shadow in the realm where he is captive; an Elemental could be a lover made of fire from the hearth of the house who keeps warm hier Keeper everynight, until one night she burns him, and all his palace; a Fairest could seduce him, make all kind of promises to him, until he begins to make promises to her, and she can use them to tie him and free herself; and Ogre could cook for his Keeper and add a poison to his food to make him sleep, and then suffocate him ith her bare hands; a Wizened could be the best smith in the realm, there are no armours like te ones he creates, until the time is right and he creates an armour that reveals agains his own wielder.

    I think that every Seeming should reflect something wide enough so any kind of escape can work will every Seeming. Just saying "Darklings is betrayal" sounds too easy. Perhaps, sometimes some escape could work for two Seeming, but that's better than made it too narrow.
    The issue with some of these is they don't quite embrace the Agency part enough, which to me means the Seeming as being Empowering. Hence, the focus on the more positive parts of each Seeming earlier in the thread. Thoughts?

        (Note, I'm playing Devil's advocate here as much as I can, sorry if that comes off badly at any point.)

        Originally posted by SoulGambit View Post
        Off the top for Seemings as Agency. Will probably circle back and expand on these later.
        Fair.

        Beasts
        The Ones Who Bought Freedom
        - Billy Goats Gruff style "Oh you don't want me."
        - Ten years a captain for a hundred years of service. Your Keeper's come due for payment but you'll be damned if that's going to happen.
        - He just wanted your heart, so you left it with him. Sometimes you can still feel cold fingers grip your chest.
        - Beasts are flat out more desperate and willing to give up more than other Changelings. They know more than anyone else the price of freedom.
        This... doesn't really match Beasts in 1.0. I mean, for one thing, "bought," doesn't really seem like something Beasts would do. Does an animal buy anything? And that's the main thing that has defined Beasts before this, being primal.

        Now, yes, Beasts will change, but why in this direction?

        Darklings
        The Ones Who Slipped Away
        - Run. Other people played the Fae's games. Most of them didn't make it. You have your bag of tricks but you use them to do the smart thing: run.
        - Darklings are more cunning than other Changelings. They observed and learned their keeper's habits, and then worked in the places their keeper didn't see. While all Changelings "escaped" in some fashion, Darklings escaped.
        These both seem like traits almost every Changeling would have to use at some point anyway. I will grant you cunning may fit Darklings more but running? At some point, that's what every Changeling had to do, even those who played the game, why should Darklings get to be especially good at it, especially when part of their theme was being scary themselves?

        Elementals
        The Ones Who Played Along
        - Shawshank Redemption style playing along with the system and then using it as leverage to escape.
        - Some elementals are just let go. They don't know why. Maybe their Keeper got bored. Maybe they didn't. Not sure which is more terrifying.
        - Above all, Elementals endure the torments other Changelings shy away from. They look their keeper in the eyes as she carves poetry into their lungs.
        With the 2nd, wouldn't their Keeper just forget they exist, and therefore, not let them go? The leverage idea also seems odd given that implies a social ability most Elementals aren't really supposed to possess, considering their alienation themes.

        Now, I actually like the idea of enduring torments others can't, because that could fit taking being a material to your own advantage.

        Fairest
        The Ones Enticed The Guard
        - Not "No." Instead "Yes, but..." Fairest engaged in a delicate dance with their Keeper by holding what they wanted just out of reach until the opportune moment.
        - Maybe you still have the thing the Keeper wanted (assuming it wasn't you), maybe you put it forever out of their reach, or maybe you gave it to them. All good angles for story.
        - Pick a princess or historical figure that made the hero do really stupid and dangerous stuff in exchange for um... Love. Lets call it Love. Napolean's Wife is actually pretty hilarious as inspiration for this. If you haven't read her letters I highly recommend it.
        - Pitting one Keeper against another or enticing someone else to fight for you should probably fall under here as well. Fairest and Ogre/Wizened/Darkling pairs should be a thing.
        - Fairest know what everyone else wants and knows just how to not give it to them. They are more willing to play the "game" and far more prone to winning. More than any other Changelings, Fairest will do anything to win.
        No issues here, all emphasizes the social aspects of being a Fairest that we all know and love, along with their darker bits.

        Ogres
        The Ones Who Broke their Chains
        - They pried their freedom from their Keeper's cold, dead hands. If only Keepers stayed dead.
        - Pick a prison break scene where like three people escape at the end.
        - Pick any action horror movie ever.
        - More than any other Changelings, Ogres understand they have to fight. While other Changelings plan or coerce Ogres act. They don't win by knowing the rules, they win by just being good enough.
        Again, no issues, seeing as Ogres are the ones said to be crafted from violence in the original, and that theme still feels strong here.

        Wizened
        The Ones Who Found the Key
        - Caroline with the snowglobe scene.
        - Sphnx's riddles.
        - You figured out the rules to the game and with lateral thinking figured a way out.
        - Wizened understand Magic and the Wyrd more than any other Changeling. Others look and see a malestrom of chaos where the Wizened observes a sea of opportunity. They tricked the Fae at their own game.
        On one hand, I do agree mental would be important to them, but on the other hand, what about their expertise? To be fair, yes, that's taking a Kith centric approach where it doesn't work anymore, but its worth noting Wizened were defined a lot by their profession, and by extension, a particular expertise they had. Shouldn't that be the case here as well?

            This is a -tiny- bit off topic from how Kiths relate to Seemings and going into how Seemings relate to powers.

            Just want to take a moment to comment on the powers, contracts, and how that relates to Seemings. I'm not certain if "Contracts will be flavored by your Seeming" is something that is set in stone but I hope to God it isn't. That design decision will cause massive headaches for all future supplements and all fan creators. I think what you are trying to do is have Seemings create an undercurrent to how the players solve conflict within the story. The Ogre who beat the worst thing to ever happen in her life by punching it in the face is likely to punch all of her problems in the face until she runs into someone without a face. Or that can't be punched. Whatever. The Fairest who was entrusted with the Fae's True Heart (a giant ruby) and collected the things he needed for his escape by claiming he just needed them to tend to or enhance the greatness of the heart is likely to solve all of his problems like that until he just can't get the upper hand.

            There are a lot of ways to accomplish that without creating a ton of strenuous work for your team. I like bucket-of-powers ala Mummy and Demon. You can seperate those into categories and give an EXP reduction for your Seeming's category like Demon does with Embeds. You can also have Seeming-Specific Merits like B&S does that adds special bonuses. You can even have Mantle (Seeming) if you want to copy that style wholecloth. Now you have some unique but minor bennies that say "This Seeming is just -better- at this sort of thing" and a set of powers it favors.

            Not enough? Have merits that modify Contracts. This lets you do exactly the same thing you are thinking of doing, but makes sure that you only have to do it when it makes sense instead of doing 5 options for each Contract.

            Not enough? Well, the powers are Contracts and you have Catches, which jive well with players. You can make it so that to learn an Out of Seeming contract, you must fulfill the Catch. Contracts in your Seeming are things you inherit or can just do. Contracts out of your Seeming are things you have to earn. If you go through the trouble of fulfilling an In-Seeming Contract's catch then you can use the power for free or get a + to it or something. As an aside, this makes Goblin Contracts easy--just make them Out of Seeming for everyone.

            You guys are great, and I'm sure you can think of options I haven't. Please don't take this as confrontational, but I think you should really explore your options and compare workload to benefit before committing yourself to that model.

                            So, I got a spin on the Darklings.

                            Agency through exploitation, power from hiding.

                            Darklings have a bad rep. Of course they do. the Darklings are the ones who got, well, dark with their escape methods. All changelings are opportunists by necessity, but the Darklings are definitely opportunists by choice. Whether they tripped their companion while running or talked another changeling into doing something that would upset the Strangers before running away, whether they spread whispers of the Stranger's banes being known, or, hell, even threatening them with nothing but an empty bag and some dark words, the Darklings would lie, cheat, extort, threaten, and take advantage of anything-or anyone-to get out. It requires killing the soul a little bit, snuffing out some of the humanity in their soul, and that light never really comes back. More so than anyone else, the Darklings are the ones who cheat the game-or perhaps, more accurately, they cheat the players of the game, both willing and unwilling.

                            How's that?

                            I have two variations for the Elementals on the entire game line, I'm still pondering them as a whole. "Those who become the game/Those who change the game".
