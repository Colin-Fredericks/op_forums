            At this point in the development of the White Wolf properties, I’d like to see the developers hold off on a Changeling Translation Guide until after the latest editions of Dreaming and Lost are both published. I absolutely think that such a guide is doable, if you focus primarily on things like “how to run Dreaming, but with Contracts instead of Cantrips” or “how to run Lost, but with the Mists instead of the Mask”; but especially with some of the changes that are occurring between Lost 1e and Lost 2e, a Translation Guide is going to be very different depending on which edition of Lost it’s translating for: compare 1e’s Seemings-and-Kiths hierarchal model with 2e’s Seemings-and-Kiths dual-axis model. Likewise with Dreaming 2e vs. C20: though it’s a 20th Anniversary Edition and thus is less likely to make the sorts of drastic changes we’re going to be seeing in Lost 2e, I fully expect that C20 will be the most radically changed of the 20th Anniversary games. And until we know how both games are going to turn out, it’s going to be hard to decide how aanslation guide will turn out.

            All that said, I did a proof-of-concept Changeling Translation Guide a while back, which unfortunately was lost with the old wikis; so I’ve got some practical experience on how a Translation Guide could work. Break each game down into its component pas, and do a compare-and-contrast of each component one by one; then provide suggestions for how each component might be incorporated into the other game, assuming everything else remains the same.

            For instance, what might CtL look like if it had Childlings, Wilders, and Grumps instead of Beasts, Darklings, Elementals, Fairest, Ogres, and Wizened? What might C20 look like if it had Beasts, Darklings, Elementals, Fairest, Ogres, and Wizened instead of Childlings, Wilders, and Grumps? How do CtL2e’s Kiths compare to C20’s Kiths?

            Another example: how does C20’s concept of Banality compare to CtL2e’s concept of Clarity? How might you hack C20 so that it uses Clarity instead of Banality? How might you hack CtL2e so that it uses Banality instead of Clarity? How does C20’s take on Glamour compare to CtL2e’s Wyrd-and-Glamour?

            Another example: C20’s Dreaming vs. CtL2e’s Hedge and Skein. Again, compare and contrast, and picture what each game might look like if it used the other game’s cosmology, but was otherwise unchanged.

            Seelie/Unseelie Courts vs. Seasonal Courts. Houses vs. Entitlements. Cantrips vs. Contracts, pledges, oneiromancy, and talecrafting (assuming those are all still things in CtL2e). There are lots of parallels to work with.

            I also noted that, settingwise, each game already has in it a very rough analog of the other game’s protagonists: in Lost, we’ve got Charlatans; in Dreaming, we’ve got that bit of backstory where the Resurgence resulted in mortals being taken to Arcadia as their lives were co-opted by the returning Sidhe. If you wanted to port either game’s protagonists whole cloth (more or less) into the other game in a manner where they coexist with the existing protagonists instead of supplanting them, you could do so — though the relations between the two games’ protagonists would tend to be contentious in both cases.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
