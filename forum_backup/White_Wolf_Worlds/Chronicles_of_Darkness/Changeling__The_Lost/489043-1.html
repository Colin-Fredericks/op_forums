    Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
    This is just complete speculation here, and I am sorry if it sounds stupid.

    What if the 7th Seeming represents Agency in two ways?

    In the first sub-Seeming, you took Agency by embracing Imperfection. More so than a human, you do not let exacerbated Vices, naive Virtues and unrealistic Aspirations rule you. Unlike the Gentry, you do not wear a mask of an angel or demon. The Blessing is "Human, All Too Human": once per story, you may automatically get an Exceptional Success on a roll to convince someone to find balance. Whenever you reach an Aspiration related to your flaws, take two Beats instead of one. The Curse is, believe it or not, "Do As You Will": at least once per story, your character will be forced to make decisions with two extreme outcomes, you stubbornly try to find a middle ground that is simply not possible. Assume that whether your character is forced to make a choice or does it of his own volition, the final result is much less impressive than it could be otherwise (Exceptional Success becomes Success, Success becomes Failure and Failure becomes Dramatic Failure).

    In the second sub-Seeming, you took Agency by striving towards Perfection. More so than a normal changeling, you focus your Vices, Virtues and Aspirations into sources of great power. Unlike the Fae, you truly take others into account as more than extensions of your whims: they fire forged friends and respectable rivals. The Blessing is "Fae, Wondrously Fae": once per story, when you fulfill your Virtue, you regain all glamour points. Alternatively you may fulfill your vice to regain 50% of your maximum glamour, round up. When you reach an Aspiration related approaching your idea of perfection, take two Beats instead of one. The Curse is "Larger Than Life": at once per story, the Wyrd offers an opportunity to find the perfect middle ground for a conflict. Whether your character is forced into finding the middle ground or goes after it himself, the final result is less grandiose than he would like (same as the Curse above).

    This Seeming could be called Seeker. Those who belong to this Seeming can change what they Seek, but this is best done through play instead of rolls. Some seek balance, some seek greatness in either direction. Both have flaws the other does not have. I know it's kind of cheating to have a double Seeming, but I still think it would be cool and I would use it.

    Example: Draconic kith the Seeker Seeming could be a wise mentor like a Chinese Dragon, helping settle disputes and advising rulers. A Draconic kith with the same Seeming could an ambitious destroyer, like an European Dragon, conquering most foes and desires, but showing true respect for Knights that challenge him (nobly or begrudgingly on either side).
    That's a really cool foundation for a Seeming.

    Well then.

    I actually have an idea for an eighth apocryphal Seeming. But I may or may not have room for it down the line. It can't fit in the core book, or REDACTED.

                                                Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                                Well it says they rejected the civilized world and embraced the beast within. They also gain back clarity from doing something that is socially unacceptable, like say pissing on your front door to make sure everybody knows that's your territory! I think it mentioned clawing and biting during the escape attempt, which seems pretty in touch with instincts to me. I don't think being uncultured is going to really help you escape your cage, but lashing out with claws and fangs might.
                                                Like Errol said, instinct is a valid expression of it, but what's been described really is broader than that.

                                                Originally posted by The Escape
                                                She already knows what it feels like to be without choices, to submit, to lean on humanity and civility. After all, that way failed her as her situation went from bad to worse in the hands of her Keeper. So she makes a choice, she chooses to do something wild, something socially unacceptable, something animalistic. She bites through flesh, claws through soil, smears herself with shit and blood to escape the Huntsman on her heels. She chooses to break a taboo, destroys her civility, and in making that choice, she escapes.
                                                Note all that taboo stuff she's doing. The emphasis is on how it's something she chooses to do, not something instinct compels her to do. She's very much in control. She's just made the decision that if behaving in a socially acceptable, civilized fashion is going to deny her options that might actually get her out, then fuck that noise.

                                                That attitude carries through to the other side of her Durance. The emphasis is always on freedom,* especially freedom from societal standards of propriety. She doesn't let those standards get in the way of doing what she wants to do how it seems best to her to do them. This in no way means (by default) that what she wants to do is indulge in instinctual animalistic behavior. Animalistic behavior is simply deemed an acceptable means to an end, where applicable -- it got her out, after all -- but not the end itself.

                                                * The idea of pushing the boundaries on what she can do skill-wise was a nice touch, I thought.

                                                This is why the Beast's Blessing involves flaunting cultural taboos: it's so self-affirming to remind herself how little hold the ever-present chains of societal pressure have on what she does that she's willing to risk a high price to prove it. Like, the first comment on the blog post includes an example of a fox girl taking a selfie in front of a bunch of meth. I mean... Daaaaamn, would she have earned that Clarity. No animalistic instinct in play, there. ... Now, it's kind of an unfortunate see-saw that getting caught means imprisonment, and the resulting breaking point might take that Clarity right back, but hey! XD

                                                Again, "acting on animal instinct" isn't a bad way to play a Beast, but it's important to recognize that it's a circle within a larger circle labeled "violating cultural taboos" in the Venn diagram.
