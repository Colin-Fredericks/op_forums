    Of Strangers and Shoggoths (On Chimeras and Fae Whimsy vs Utility)

    12-12-2021, 11:00 PM
    Was advised to move this discussion from the Simple Question thread to avoid clutter; conversation can be picked up later, but my weekend is almost over, so I figured I should get this typed up while it was fresh in my mind.

    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
    While I like our current version of Chimera, I wonder why it was changed from this version?
    Probably because getting arguably the best dice trick in the game on every form of rolled supernatural evasion for one of the most easily-procured supernatural fuel sources in the setting is a bit much for an exclusive power that's already adding a global bonus to Social rolls with everything from the gameline's primary otherworld.

    I guess this is a disconnect then, because I was under the impression that being mutable was a definite and defining quality in and of itself. Are you saying that’s not the case?
    If the only guaranteed thing about it is that nothing is guaranteed about it, it's not much of a quality.

    Look at myth and legend. Tam Lin is shapeshifted between a dozen different forms in his captors' bid to make his savior let him go. Sun Wukong can transform into anything that exists, animate or inanimate. Proteus, the god from which we get the word "protean," turns into other things to keep from having to answer people's questions. Notice that most of these things are the result of directed divine or divine-adjacent power akin to Royal Contracts that turn you into specific things.

    On the other end of things, look at Demon, where the God-Machine's outlook is much closer to the bluntly practical concerns of "this one worker needs to be able to handle multiple different problems." There's a demonic form ability that lets you change your other form abilities on the fly, but it's the highest-rated type of form ability, it can't be used outside of a full transformation, it takes Aether and an action for each swap, and you still have defaults that you need to reset to rather than swapping a form ability from one thing to another.

    I mean this is fair, but I guess I don’t understand the Others mind set at all. I chose that background because of Shoggoths being a vaguely similar creature to what I was doing, and they were created and enslaved to basically serve a role. That role being whatever the Elder Things wanted at the time. (They are basically robots as I understand) I figured that worked well enough to port over to Changeling. (I don’t read Lovecraft, really but I browse wikis sometimes)
    Gonna dip into Mage for a momentary comparison and highlight the difference between The Abyss and (Greater) Abyssal Beings, because those operate at about the same cosmological level and you can replace "The Abyss" with "Arcadia" and still hit the same points of that I'm talking about:

    The Abyss is the magic-adjacent part of reality where anything can happen; the laws of the universe change unpredictably, and it is very difficult to survive for any length of subjective time without a more powerful patron to support you. Fire might not warm you but instead cause you to turn transparent. Writing the word "delicate" might turn your bones to jelly. Reality is out to lunch.

    Abyssal Beings are the main powerful patrons that can offer stability in the greater Abyss, world-beings with consistent-enough personalities and metaphysics that you can get the hang of how they operate after enough time to acclimate much like you would anywhere on Earth. The Prince of a Hundred Thousand Leaves is alien to our reality, but it is reliably a cannibal timeline with its own bits of awful history that it tries to paste over ours when given the chance.

    The True Fae are not Arcadia. They are parts of Arcadia, and they express their desires within the limits they bargained their power from. They have Titles and Regalia and oaths that mean pursuing their Aspirations has to work around the limits they agreed to, which includes not having everything be anything all of the time. Their reality-warping is handled using the systems for Hedgespinning and oneiromancy, which can't trivially make substantial changes to important characters, and while that's obviously not the system they use to create changelings, it's still applied through the lens of the Fae's identity as a being with a Title based on an emotion, sensory experience, or object.

    The Name behind the Marchioness of Death In The Freezing Mountains may have a passing fancy for the warmth of a sunset across a desert plain, but she will need to use existing agreements to actualize that desire, and her Title may come with obligations that work directly against it. She may have to make overtures to the Princess of Burning Footsteps Pursued By Scorpions or make war on the Duke of the Scarlet Horizon for his lands and deeds, or satisfy her craving with bottled memories from other domains, but she's not going to turn her whole chunk of Faerie into the temperate and topological opposite of itself on a narrative timescale that can't be observed by anyone who's not plugged into the dream-machine that fools her servants into thinking she's omnipotent.

    A shoggoth is basically a giant genetically-engineered super-amoeba that accidentally attained sentience. The Elder Things made them through genetic engineering and mind control, because the Elder Things are not gods operating practically outside the constrains of material reality through conceptual bargains struck with mythic symbolism. If the True Fae were to have shoggoth-equivalents, they would more than likely be a type of hobgoblin, because turning humans into shapeless blobs of flesh is a little more Deviant (or Vampire or Demon) than Changeling, as sensibilities go.

    On a more general note, "can survive in any environment and have some fun body horror with benefits" is, now that I remember, reasonably close to describing certain aspects of the Elemental seeming — Primal Glory can cover a fair amount of ground for survivability, and the seeming's blessing is flavored such that the right choice of element can be proper spooky.

    Employing Hobgoblins would make much more sense, but I get the impression asking one of the Kindly Ones to swallow their pride just won’t happen unless they are forced to.
    They're less prestigious to use for the same purposes as changelings, but if your goal is just to have somebody to handle the job then it doesn't really matter if they don't come from the other side of the Hedge.

    On the subject of contacts, this Kith really isn’t that different from Talon and Wing. Talon and Wing doesn’t explicitly say you can’t get a creatures ability to survive in certain environments.
    Talon and Wing doesn't explicitly say that because it instead explicitly says that the Contract can grant three different effects and describes what those effects are, with the sole example effect outside of that list being a seeming bonus, and there's a Common Contract for ignoring damage from particular bits of nature in another Regalia.

    (The only way I can kinda approach the Others mindset is assuming my desires are the only thing that matters, and nothing else is really…real.)
    Yeah. That's how they operate, generally. The Strangers' closest mundane analog is the hyper-rich, who include among their number the sort of person who buys rugs you would expect to find in a museum as regular replacements for something they could absolutely just use regular carpeting for (flooring a room in their yacht, if I'm remembering this specific example correctly).

    Later 2e supplements may shift exactly how accurate the technicalities of this description are, but there's a line in Mortal Remains that summarizes the True Fae as their own unfiltered self-images. They're bastards. They don't care. They set up a hundred-dozen agreements with the universe to specifically allow them to continue not to care. Some of those agreements rely on their never caring. That explanation I gave earlier about hobgoblin deals being designed to squeeze the non-goblin parties for everything they're worth? The Gentry play with that concept's meaner, nastier older sibling. You will serve until they're done with you.

    (And to expose my own "might just be a 'me' thing" here: I hate having to make changes to a thing that require me to first undo the work of the initial setup. It feels like going backwards and if you fuck it up you've added even more steps to the process. And this is for something less complicated than re-rigging a human being you took for one purpose to serve a completely different purpose that doesn't overlap at all. It's less trouble by far to start over from as close to the changeover point as you can get pre-made, especially given that you'll have to do that anyway if you bungle the undoing process.)

    The merit to modify dropping the mask is interesting. I don’t think we have a merit for modifies the Mien when dropping the mask for benefits. It actually really suits a number of character archetypes like a berserker Beast or Ogre who want to drop the mask for a physical power boost even if they don’t really use contracts. Do you think I should drop the kith in exchange for the merit? The kith could just be something like a Broadback or Cleverquick that really invests in that merit.
    It would certainly get around the problem of figuring out what Skill the blessing benefited, if nothing else, and I'd been thinking of making a similar Merit for different purposes anyway.

    I’d like to say I do like the pool of merits. I edited one of my previous posts, but I don’t think you saw that. To reiterate, I had few extra merits I thought could be added, and asked about Beast’s advanced merits. I looked to see if any conversation had emerged about using the Advanced Merits, and I saw a previous conversation on this very thread about them. Now I’d like to ask if there is any reason not to port the Advanced Merits that don’t use Beast specific mechanics over if I thought it could fit?
    Looking at the edit, I should point out that Hardy is already half-covered by the benefits of Wyrd, Freediving modifies a system that didn't exist in 2e until Deviant updated it, and Double-Jointed functionally removes the cost of Portaling out of a grapple in a way that doesn't feel like the kind of thing that comes as a side benefit.

    Double-Jointed (Advanced) is quite close to the 1e Tunnelgrub blessing, Direction Sense (Advanced) cuts into one of the printed example benefits of a personal oath and also a Royal Contract, and I feel like Iron Skin's general deal doesn't play nice with Changeling's standard methods of damage-avoidance at above-basic levels. Striking Looks (Advanced) feels conceptually iffy for changelings and rote quality is, as I mentioned up top, arguably the best dice trick in the game. Fast Reflexes (Advanced) should work fine.

              I think part of the problem is similar to what Aquatosic faced with the multi-limbered kith: you're focusing on form, not function. It is better since you decided to narrow it to survival adaptation, but you're still trying to squeeze too much from the form instead of saying what this shapechanging does effectively for the story.

              Tam Lin is changed to make holding him as undesirable or cumbersome as possible, it is the crux of the challenge his savior must overcome. Sun Wukong uses it to hide his identity, it is his signature ability as a liar. Shoggoths are the greatest creation of the Elder Things, and destroyed their creators with it, it makes them apex monsters. Some legendary creatures that live in dreams change to grant desire or cause fear, it is a tool of emotion. Gods and their servants in many myths change to interact with mortals, it serves communication.

              Observe that no one does everything with such power. The more complex, nuanced or simply powerful a creature, the more tangential a power to change shape actually is. They all change for real, but the new forms aren't deemed all-purpose tools, instead they are very specialized for each one, in and out of character.

              Use this. Instead of trying to figure out how to make a character that can assume any shape, start to think what your kith gets out from its ability to assume any shape. Make this specific and let go other functions.
