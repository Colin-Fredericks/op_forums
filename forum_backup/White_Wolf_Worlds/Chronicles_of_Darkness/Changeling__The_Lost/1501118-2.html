    As a general rule, the Mask is the second most potent passive concealment power amongst all the major templates after Cover, and is in some ways superior to it*. How much of a bother it should be to pierce the Mask is dependent on the context of the game primarily, but it's not something you should idly just breach. Changelings, after all, might be liars, unless they're willing to vulnerable.

    *Cover's main weakness that the Mask doesn't have is that Cover theoretically applies to all aspects of the Cover's life, and investigation into incongruities of the Cover can cause it to wear away-even if strictly speaking, it's not investigating the Demon in cover at the moment. The Mask does not wear away, even after death, and can only be removed by the changeling at will or at the subjection to otherwise hella fucking powerful magic, perfectly conceals a changelings Mien at low levels (barring their shadow), and only really leaks without prodding when a changeling starts to become exceptionally powerful (Wyrd 6+). It's a tough bit of passive concealment to punch through, and while active concealment powers are always more powerful and and active perception powers can always be argued to have leeway to pierce it, anything lesser will pretty usually fail.
    Originally posted by orathaic View Post
    Is there a collective term for all the stuff beyond a hedgegate?

    And is it more than Arcadia and the Hedge?
    While Faerie is usually reserved for the domain of the True Fae on the opposite side of the Hedge, it has been applied as a collective term for the Hedge and Arcadia by certain parties (usually non-fairies). That said, most people refer to what's beyond the gates as The Hedge and Arcadia, unsurprisingly.

    So far, the only demarcations of Faerie (in the sense of everything beyond the Hedge Gates) are the Hedge and Arcadia. The night woods of the Huntsmen may become particularly demarcated in their own way but by and large are just a part of the Hedge, in the same way the Dreaming and Wishing roads are technically their own distinct partitions in the Hedge but are still the Hedge. Otherwise, there are no distinct worlds beyond the Hedge Gates, unshockingly.

    Hollow are not subdimensions anymore than rooms in a house are subdimensions. Wards might create the illusion of such, though.

        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post

        For the first question, it depends on the courts and the agreements between them for emergency responses, and what particular emergencies are planned for. Changelings on the whole are more prone to flight than than fight or defend as a reaction, but that's mostly something to keep in the back of your mind while establishing what that looks like. A unified freehold will react differently than a freehold in conflict with itself, the response to a hurricane is different from the response to a True Fae, and the way the Seasonal Courts handle a scenario would be different from how the Rose Courts would challenge it.

        For the other supernaturals, a lot of it depends on the ability to access such, but as a broad rule, the Hedge is a pretty reliable safe space against anything on the whole given it's over all general subversive hostility, and the world of fortifying and defending a Hollow multiplies that. A demon might still want a Bolthole in that hollow, but pretty much anyone else with a pursuing antagonist can hole up pretty well in ther, so long as the particulars of that antagonist don't facilitate access. The downside is that unless they have a friend in a changeling willing to compromise their Hollow a little bit of that ficilitation, they don't have any more of a guarentee to keeping a Hollow than anyone else, and the Hedge is just as hostile to them as anyone else.

        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
        On the issue of the Mask, this is something that can kind of get variable across books. The general rule is that the Mask is a potent enough kitch of magic that it's not idly broken through, but within the purview of what they can do and more specifically specialize in, it can be apparent enough. As a general run down (and a caveat, assume a lot of these carry a rider of "particularly if a changeling's Wyrd is high):
        • Vampires can probably tell there's something off about a changeling's blood and heartbeat by the scent and sound and will definitely realize something's up when they try to drink the blood (subject to rules on Unnatural Affinity and The Player's Guide to the Contagion as applied), and Auspex 2 is usually enough to pierce the Mask.
        • Werewolves can usually smell through the Mask, can usually taste something off in their blood, and if they're in an environment that let's them take advantage of their hearing may be able to hear oddities from a changeling's Mien. Gift Facets like Scent the Unnatural and Know the Prey are pretty reasonable excuses to just pierce the Mask.
        • Active Mage Sight can usually pierce parts of the Mask based on the relevant Arcane to the changes, and Fate or Mind AMS can pierce it outright.
        • Prometheans have no particular innate feature that lets them note changelings (though as a spot of fun, you might rule a Promethean sleeping next to a changeling whose kith is elementally aligned with their Lineage might restore Pyros, leading to questions and shipping), but Distillitions of the Bestiae Alembic in Metamorphosis might pick up on details leaking through, and the Vitreous Alembic of Sensorium and the Veritas Alembic of Spiritus are viable for piercing the Mask.
        • Hunter's entire schtick is investigating and poking at incongruities, which often entails long-term observation, so as a general rule they don't, though obviously some Conspiracy Hunters have particulars-off hand without going to the book, the Malleus Maleificarum, Lucifuge, and Ascending Ones all probably have ways of piercing the Mask.
        • Sin-Eaters and other Bound may be able see parts of the mien of changelings with death/ghost associated Kiths, may be able to pick up something off with certain Caul transformations, and could properly reveal the changeling unto themselves with Oracle.
        • The Arisen don't have any particular ability to sense incongruities or pierce the mask on their own, but may perceive the changeling's lacerated soul with Soulsight, pierce the Mask with Godsight, and might see some bleed through of death/ghost associated Kiths with Deathsight. Reasonably, the third tier of Secrets Ripped from the Sky could pierce the Mask. I also wouldn't consider it too wild if someone evoking Tier 3 of Blessed is the God-King also got to pierce through the Mask, but that's a me thing.
        • Demons have no particular gift for breaching the Mask on their own, but there are so many tools for doing getting the hint or otherwise piercing the Mask. Inquisitorsrs may reasonably be able to use their Prepared for Anything Condition to get that something's off with the changeling. In terms of Demonic Forms, Electrical Sight, Sonic Acuity, and Blind Sense migt pick up on elements of the Mien bleeding through, and Aura Sight can pierce the Mask. A demon who knows, broadly, that they're looking for a changeling can use Special Someone to point one out in a crowd, though that won't pierce the Mask.
        • Family Resemblance may allow a Beast to know a changeling is a changeling, but doesn't pierce the Mask. The Eye of Heaven may be able to discern the changeling's deep ties to the Wyrd, and Low Satiety Needs Must can pierce the Mask on a Clash of Wills. It's not inconceivable that a Nightmare might able to affect the Mask in some kind of way, but no such Nightmare is currently textual.
        • Deviant has too many variations for me go through, so no inante ability, but probably have something that can do the job.
        As general aids on the subjects go, Kith and Kin, Rites of Spring, and Players Guide to the Contagion are probably the most useful.

            On that note, because everyone should know this: if you get one book after the Changeling Core Book, Get Rites of Spring. Doesn't matter if you're playing Second Edition (though if you are, the next big suggestion is Kith and Kin), Rites of Spring covers so much ground work of how Changeling works that it still generally works in Second Edition, or at least certainly answer questions that probably won't be answered for some time (though when The Hedge comes out, *drools*). Doesn't matter what you are specifically doing with your game (Yes, even if you are doing an intensive antagonistically driven game [Autumn Nightmares, Night Horrors: Grim Fears], deeply self-exploratory stories or far ranging and maybe even global stories[Winter Masques], deeply politically intriguing games [Lords of Summer, Goblin Market, Oak, Ash, and Thorn], high fantasy, grand powered, and end-gamey stories [The Equinox Road], or highly variable stories [Dancers in the Dusk, Swords at Dawn, and Victorian Lost]), you want Rites of Spring.

                                Straightforward answer would be to treat it like the Influence Dread Power on p. 254 of the corebook.

                                Werewolf has Facets that give specific Influences with dice pools composed of set Attribute + Skill + Renown combinations. Especially if requiring a roll even unopposed, you might possibly consider picking specific Power and Finesse Attributes or a specific Attribute and Skill thematically appropriate to the Influence and the being it was stolen from, and then adding Mantle if the Influence is suitably near the purview of your Court.

                                                Originally posted by Cauthon View Post
                                                Changelings are the only ones who can aquire a Mantle, correct? Mantles are "Changeling" merits, and as such wouldn't be valid to aquire via Borrowed Favor + Court Goodwill?
                                                Mantle is an alteration of a changeling's mien, which things that aren't fae don't have in the strict sense, hence why fetches, fae-touched, and non-changelings with Borrowed Favor use Court Goodwill instead of Mantle.

                                                Court Goodwill being more than a purely social construct means the court's patron still extends some mystical benefits to particularly dedicated characters, but until the level of Goodwill at which a changeling could learn outside Royal Contracts those benefits tend to amount to mundane dice bonuses.
