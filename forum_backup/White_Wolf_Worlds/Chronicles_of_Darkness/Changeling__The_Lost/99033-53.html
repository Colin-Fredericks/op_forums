    Lorelei’s Diary

    Mmmmm… I woke up this morning as part of an achy, uncomfortable, sweaty pile of body parts, with three people (and two slightly crushed wings) jammed into two zipped-together sleeping bags. The sleeping bag and tent were designed to help stave off the chilly wilderness nights… which meant we were stewing in our own juices in the ever-present 76 degree heat of Southern California.

    Like I said… Mmmmm. A girl could get used to this!

    So Christmas morning dawned somewhat earlier than most of us are used to in the Dream House, as we were all serenaded by the dulcet tones of Christmas carols sung with a French accent drifting out of the vents. Yes, Marguerite had decided that we had slept long enough, and was eager to get Christmas Day started. The lot of us headed down to Sex Breakfast with varying degrees of enthusiasm… but even the grumpiest and sleepiest among us had a noticeable undercurrent of good cheer. A few applications of Spring contracts later, and we were quite a merry bunch.

    Calamity got breakfast started as soon as we were all assembled. I moved to help her, but Missy and Jane headed me off and asked if they could speak with me privately for a few moments. Since they were both obviously nervous about something, I followed them into the theater.

    “Umm, so…” Missy began, noticeably uncomfortable.

    Jane took over from that point… but oddly she seemed just as uneasy. “So, did you know there’s some sort of altar in the attic?” she asked after a moment.

    I chuckled and breathed a sigh of relief. “Yeah,” I said, “that’s Julia’s. She’s Wiccan… it’s a Neo-Pagan religion based on venerating the Earth, sort of a druidic tradition that…” I trailed off at their blank looks.

    “So,” Missy tried again, “it’s not a Satanic thing?”

    “No!” I said, a little shocked myself. “It’s… it’s just a different religion. She’s not evil, I swear.”

    “We saw some of her paintings, too,” Jane admitted, “they’re beautiful, but kind of disturbing That one with her and Marguerite holding pistols to each other’s’ heads…”

    I sighed. “At least you didn’t see the one with the double hanging,” I said with some resignation. The Goth movement was just getting started when we were Taken, but it hadn’t made much headway in Defiance. “Look, Julia’s artistic sensibilities are a little morbid… it comes of being a darkling. But she’s a good person at heart, just a little different. I swear.”

    “Kay,” Missy said with a watery grin. “And… we also checked out the basement while we were looking around, and…” she didn’t seem able to continue that thought.

    I sighed again, I couldn’t help it. “So you found the playroom,” I said. “It’s not my thing, but some of the other girls really enjoy it.”

    Jane’s eyes widened. “So it’s like a sex thing?” she asked. A moment later she had a follow-up question. “A voluntary sex thing?”

    “Of course,” I assured them. “Nobody uses those toys unless they want to be there. There are hidden release buttons I can show you if you want… that way nobody can ever use them against you in the future. No changeling would ever lock another up against their will… that’s one of our most important rules.”

    Once we had that straightened out, the three of us returned to the kitchen. I helped Calamity as much as I could with the cooking, and the two of us started planning for that evening’s Christmas feast as well. There was a lot to do, and though we are lucky enough to live in a house with a fully-stocked larder and a commercial-grade kitchen, getting the timing down so that everything would be ready at the proper time took some serious scheduling. In the background I could hear Missy and Jane interrogating Julia about her religion (“I don’t believe in Satan, and even if I did, why would I want to summon him in my house?”).

    D and Trinh entered the kitchen a short time later. My wife was showing off by firing small laser blasts from her fingers. Yes, that’s right… I am married to a Latina woman who can shoot lasers from her fingers (I admonished her to be extra careful with this newfound skill, as those fingers frequently… well, you get the picture). Apparently it’s some sort of Summer Court thing; I found out later that Gloria can do it too (although she is less likely than D to make cute little “pew-pew” sounds when she does it).

    Sex Breakfast conversation hit all sorts of topics, as usual. Marguerite’s disdain for French Canadians came up again, and Trinh asked if this attitude extended to Cajuns (she’s from Louisiana). Apparently it does not, as Marguerite feels that the Cajun people at least have the decency not to call themselves French. This got us talking about Cajun food, about which Trinh knows quite a lot. She’s offered to help us prepare a Cajun meal one of these days, and to teach those of us who wish to learn how to prepare such meals for ourselves in the future. As a diligent Pilgrim I saw it as my duty to pick up this skill if I can.

    Plus… red beans and rice, Andouille sausage, jambalaya, gumbo… yum!

    The question of frailties came up at some point as well, and had to be explained to Missy, Jane, and Trinh. They took the information in stride… I think they’re all really starting to get a handle on this changeling thing. I wish I’d had someone to guide me through the growing pains in those early days. We discussed a few of our own minor frailties, and warned them that knowingly exploiting another changeling’s frailty is considered almost unspeakably rude. They all solemnly promised not to ask me to sing anything, knowingly break glass in front of Calamity, give Jacob anything hot to drink, etc.

    I… didn’t talk about my other frailty. I don’t even like to think about it, honestly. And hey… D still claims that she has no frailties of which she’s aware, which I know deep down is a crock. But I suppose I can’t blame her… frailties tend to be very personal and somewhat disturbing. I don’t like talking about mine either. And hell, D still hasn’t told any of the others when her birthday is, for crying out loud… I only know because it came up when we were doing to paperwork to put our marriage through.

    So in the course of our discussion about frailties, our little jaunt to Disneyland came up. The girls are now fully aware of the reasons for my eternal feud with Goofy, which the rest of the motley can’t seem to help bringing up from time to time. I laughed it off with everyone else, telling Calamity that she should take a cue from Jacob and learn how to “Let It Go.” That got a laugh from most of those assembled, but had to be explained to the newcomers.

    We whiled away about twenty minutes outside after breakfast. I flew up to the roof and tossed a few clay targets in the air so that D could shoot them down with her finger lasers. Yes, I just wrote that sentence in my diary. Aside from the question of finger lasers, I thought to wonder aloud why we have a supply of clay targets at the Dream House, but nobody could satisfactorily answer that question, so I let it pass after a while.

    Most of the rest of the afternoon was devoted to preparing a Christmas feast which will not be soon forgotten. We had a turkey, we had a ham, we had roast beef (I took up my old habit of referring to the latter as “roast beast,” which had Calamity calling me Cindy Lou all afternoon, but whatever), and that’s just the main courses. Sweet potato casserole, mashed potatoes, cranberry sauce, mixed vegetables… we really covered all the bases this time. And for a wonder we actually seemed to schedule the cooking so that everything was ready at approximately the right time!

    We had a few other people over for dinner, aside from those currently living in the house. Becky stuck around, of course, and Stasia and Elena stopped by to spend the evening with us as well. Gloria came by too, since she hasn’t yet been able to reconnect with her mortal family. Oleander dropped by for dinner, but she was still mildly toxic, so we had to content ourselves with chaste Christmas hugs and air-kisses.

    Conversation was lively, and ranged all over the place, from cyber-security to Goth Day at Disneyland to the torture chamber in our basement. Turns out Stasia and Elena know about that… Calamity gave Stasia a key before we left for Arcadia, and it was strongly implied that the two of them had… “enjoyed” the accoutrements of that room on more than one occasion. You could color me shocked at the time (no offense, but Elena seems kind of vanilla about such things at first glance), but after certain other facts came to light the next day… but I’m getting ahead of myself.

    Dinner came out well, and was enjoyed by all. Afterward most of us gathered in the theater for a little karaoke (Oleander had to leave, unfortunately). D asked if there was anything I’d like to hear her sing, and I asked her to sing me anything by the Beatles. She got this look in her eye…

    “Anything with a melody,” I specified. “No ‘Revolution 9’ or ‘Wild Honey Pie’ or anything like that, okay?”

    She grinned and bore it, and when her turn came up she gave a fair rendition of “Come Together.” I know it’s not her kind of music, so I made sure to show my appreciation once she’d finished with a big hug and kiss. “Anything you’d like to hear me sing?” I asked, figuring fair was fair. “Anything at all?”

    She settled on a song I’d never heard of called “Punk Rock Girl” by the Dead Milkmen, and carefully phrased the suggestion in such a way that I wouldn’t be compelled to sing it or anything unpleasant like that. It’s… well, it’s not really to my taste at all, but like I said, turnabout is fair play.

    There were a few other standout performances. Jacob did a nice tenor rendition of a Russian Christmas song for us, which proves that he can get into the spirit of the holiday when backed into a corner. I performed “Let It Go” for Missy, Jane, and Trinh’s benefit, since they had expressed an interest after my earlier reference to the Disney song. I got Missy and Jane up on stage with me to show off our harmonic chops with Three Dog Night’s “Old Fashioned Love Song,” and it went over like a house on fire. Trinh strutted her stuff with Paula Abdul’s “Straight Up,” and Marguerite sang her own take on the Brenda Lee holiday classic “Rockin’ Around the Christmas Tree.” Finally, Gloria belted out a power ballad from the 80s called “Every Rose Has Its Thorn.”

    Oh, I should also mention that Calamity surprised us all with a soulful rendition of “We’re All Alone,” a song I’ve performed for audiences at the Dream House before. The reason I single it out as noteworthy is that she sang it at Becky’s request, and the song seemed to have some significance to our dear claviger. Though I’d never point it out aloud to either of them, I found this very sweet. What can I say? I’m a romantic at heart.

    And so the evening wore on, with music and food and wine and pleasant conversation. As it got later, people started drifting off to bed, realizing that we’d need to get up early the next morning to head out for our camping trip. I glanced over at D to see what her intentions were, and she threw me a smile, and then turned to Gloria. “So,” she asked her girlfriend with suppressed mirth, “is tonight the night?”

    Gloria looked at me, swallowed, smiled, and nodded. “I think it might be,” she said hesitantly.

    D headed to the kitchen for a bottle of chardonnay. Gloria couldn’t seem to meet my eyes in the meantime. “Is this… I mean, do you really want this, Lori?” she asked me after a moment.

    I nodded. “If you’re sure this won’t come between you and D... and won’t adversely affect our friendship, then there’s nothing I’d like more. Hell, I’ve thought you were hot ever since I saw you pinned to the wall on your first night here…” Her smile grew a little more genuine at that, but I could tell that she was still a little tense.

    D got back a moment later, and the three of us headed up to the boudoir. Our lovemaking fell into a pattern wherein two of us concentrated on bringing pleasure to the third. Maria knows me well enough that she put me in the hot-seat first, and… wow. Those two are both really strong. Gloria’s not as strong as Maria is, but she’s still really strong. They were gentle with me, and always made sure that I was where I needed to be, if you know what I mean, but I could feel the strength in their muscles the whole time, like steel hiding under silk.

    Gloria got to be the center of attention next. D and I have a lot of experience working together in that way, and we make a great team if I do say so myself. Plus, as much as I like being the center of attention, I must also admit that I enjoy showing off my skills. I think Gloria appreciated my efforts, based upon her reactions. And D knows her well enough by now to give me a few pointers about the things she particularly likes, so we had that going for us too.

    Finally, Gloria and I saw to D’s needs. We didn’t try anything fancy; we just let her know in no uncertain terms that she was surrounded by girls who loved her. The evening ended with all three of us sweating and breathless, Gloria and I looking fondly at each other across D’s upper chest as she wrapped her strong arms around the two of us.

    “Thank you,” I whispered once things had calmed down. “Thank you both so much for sharing this with me. You’ve made me feel so special… you’ve made me so happy.” They both expressed similar thoughts, and we started to drift off. I closed my eyes as I said the last two words I wanted to say that evening: “Merry Christmas.”

    We woke up earlier than usual (again) the next morning, and headed down to breakfast. Things were a little less rambunctious than usual, as we were busy preparing for our trip into the mountains, but a few barbs and double entendres still slipped in here and there. Most notably, Trinh – who is currently using the basement guest room – made a few pointed comments indicating that Stasia and Elena might have made use of the “torture dungeon” last night, and that she might have overheard a bit of their play. Elena was so embarrassed by this talk that she slipped under the table to avoid the stares.

    Sudden inspiration struck as we were talking excitedly about that day’s camping trip. “Hey, Gloria,” I said with animation, “are you doing anything today? You want to come with us?”

    She looked surprised to be asked, but pleased. “It sounds like fun, if you’re sure it won’t be too much trouble.”

    I glanced at D. “No trouble at all,” I said, “except that we won’t have enough tents… unless you’d like to share with me and D. It’ll be cramped, but I’m sure we could squeeze you in.”

    D nodded enthusiastically. “We can work it out,” she agreed.

    Gloria chuckled. “Okay then, I’m sold. We can even take my car if you want, since there’ll be like fourteen of us.”

    It turned out to be sort of a stroke of luck, actually, as Gloria’s SUV proved really useful in transporting not only the fourteen of us, but all of our luggage and equipment. Gloria, Maria, Missy, Jane, and I ended up in the SUV. Calamity, Becky, Trinh, Marguerite, and Julia took the van. Jacob, Luna, Elena and Stasia piled into Jacob’s jeep. Twenty minutes after breakfast was done, we were on the road.

    The trip was largely uneventful; I found myself singing along with Gloria’s 80s radio station more often than not, chatting with Missy and Jane, and looking at the beautiful Southern California scenery out of the windows. At one point I got a text from Julia. Apparently Trinh’s continued gentle teasing of Stasia and Elena now indicated that – contrary to preconceived notions – it was actually Stasia who was bound to the St. Andrew’s Cross while Elena held the crop. It’s always the quiet ones…

    So a couple of hours later we pulled into the park and started unloading the vehicles. Backpacks have been… tricky for me since my return from Arcadia; they’re never comfortable and I have to be very careful to avoid pulling out feathers when I’m wearing them. I commented on this, and D offered to carry my gear. I tried to refuse her (we had three other girls with wings in the same boat after all), but she wouldn’t hear of it once the idea entered her mind.

    The hike itself was fun; nothing strenuous, just a pleasant nature walk. I spoke with several of the local birds, who seemed happy enough. Jacob told me to warn them that he was going to make it snow later that night, and I tried, but… y’know, they’re birds. I doubt they remembered the warning for more than a few minutes. Marguerite slipped on and off the path, walking nimbly on top of the soft carpet of fallen leaves and brush without sinking into it, and looking inordinately pleased with herself. She didn’t even object when I called her Legolas.

    We got to the campsite with a few hours of daylight left and set about pitching our tents. D, Gloria and I managed to get ours set up pretty quickly (not as quickly as Jacob & Luna or Elena & Stasia, of course, but they’re the real experts at that sort of thing in the party), but some of the others had a bit of trouble. Missy and Jane fumbled with their tent for a while, and I eventually went over to help them, explaining a few of the minor innovations which have arisen in camping gear over the past forty years or so.

    Trinh was having trouble of a different sort. She kept getting frustrated when little things went wrong, which of course just led to more minor mistakes. Eventually it came out that Trinh was feeling guilty about the trouble her presence in the world was causing her Fetch. “Here I am having fun with good friends, and she’s holed up in some seedy little crack-house afraid for her life… because of me!”

    We tried to assure her that none of this was actually her fault, but she was inconsolable for a while. In the end it was Elena who got through to her, talking quietly with her about her identity issues and the things she can and can’t control. She also made sure that Trinh’s tent was set up properly so that she could sleep securely that night. After a while, Trinh gave Elena a tearful hug, and apologized for teasing her about what she and Stasia had gotten up to the previous evening.

    We set up a campfire, sang traditional songs, told scary stories, and generally had a fine old time. I think Calamity was a little disappointed that she didn’t get to fight a bear while we were out there – she’d been talking about the possibility for the last few days, despite protests from our resident park ranger – but other than that the experience was fully enjoyed by all.

    Jacob did make it snow for a little while, which provided a nice bit of ambiance for our annual winter camping trip. It made things a little chilly, which encouraged people to cuddle up close to each other. I think Maria was probably in hog heaven, with me snuggled up to one side of her and Gloria on the other. I remember how good it felt the first time I got to be with D and Oleander at the same time, and I’m glad I could help her feel that way as well. I mean, we’ve both been with Marguerite and Julia plenty of times, but this is different… this is bringing together two loves who have always had a bit of distance between them. It’s creating a new bond.

    Once the talk started to die down, I yawned and stretched theatrically and indicated that I’d be heading to bed soon. “At least this time I know I double-checked,” I said as I rummaged around in my pack. “I’ll be able to sleep in my nice warm paja… dammit D!” Once again, my flannel PJs had disappeared from my pack. I caught her exchanging a wink with Marguerite, so I’m guessing there was some collusion from the French faction in this conspiracy.

    So the three of us retired to our tent, got undressed, and somehow managed to squeeze into two sleeping bags which had been zipped together. We played a bit, and there were plenty of kisses (and other things) exchanged, but due to the tight confines things were really more tender than erotic. Once again we fell asleep with D between us, but I made sure that my hand reached across my wife’s stomach to rest on Gloria’s waist, and after a while I felt her hand settle onto my hip. I cooed contentedly as the three of us drifted off.

    I woke up about an hour ago, and extricated myself from the sleeping bag as quietly as I could. The other two stirred, but didn’t rise with me. I’ve been writing in this diary and watching a gorgeous sunrise over the eastern ridge of the mountains. The birds are singing… and I cannot tell you how utterly beautiful that song is when you can understand it. Unfortunately, there aren’t human words for it, so I won’t even try to convey its meaning here.

    Once the others are awake, we’ll have a light breakfast, break camp, and hike back to the cars and head back to Malibu and back to our lives. We have Trinh’s Fetch to deal with, and the New Year’s Eve show to plan, and a thousand other things to worry about… but those are concerns for later. For now, I’m just going to listen to the birds and revel in their joy as it mirrors my own.

    Lorelei de la Rocha


    Jacob’s Journal

    Sometimes my life feels surreal in some ways. Today was the second time I’ve been woken up on Christmas day by someone singing Christmas carols in the vents. Insincere Marguerite loves this time of year and decided that we had all slept long enough and she wanted us up so we could all enjoy the holiday. So we all got up and made our way downstairs. Calamity made it to the kitchen before Lori this time and started cooking breakfast. Pancakes, bacon, eggs, toast, it was all good. I of course made the coffee. Iced for myself and hot for everyone else. Conversation turned to the camping trip tomorrow and about whether or not we had all the gear we needed. I assured folks that we had what we needed to go camping for one night. It was kind of amusing actually. Before I entered the Lady’s service I technically camped all the time. There are no cabins in the Lady’s realm. There are no shelters, except the occasional cave here and there. It is a harsh place. And I lived there for years. In the snow. With no tent. No sleeping bag. And this group here is concerned about camping for one night in the hills of California where the temperature doesn’t know how to go below 65 degrees Fahrenheit. Though to be fair my plan was to lower the temperature a bit so it would more easily snow tomorrow night. Still, it won’t get close to freezing. I know Lori is familiar with the snow, she’s from Iowa, and my sister knows about camping in the winter, but I’m not so sure about the rest. The motley at least has experienced camping with me once before, so they know what to expect. It’s going to be fun, even if Calamity does keep going on about knife fighting a bear.

    Later in the afternoon folks started coming over to help prepare Christmas dinner. Elena and Stasia came over to help and just be here. My sister apologized to me for being clingy. I told her that it was fine. I understood why she needed to be near family right now and told her that I would always be there for her. I feel bad that I wasn’t there at Thanksgiving when our parents rejected her in the first place. She couldn’t come to me because I was gone in Arcadia. I wouldn’t trade the trip to Arcadia for anything. We did a lot of good there. We freed three innocent people, but I do regret the fact that I wasn’t here when my sister needed me the most. Thankfully she had Stasia to lean on.

    Conversation took an interesting turn. Apparently Missy and Jane went exploring the house this morning and came across Julia’s Wiccan altar in the attic and the sex toys in the basement. They thought that the altar was some sort of Satanic thing I guess. Lori assured them that it was okay. No devil worship in this house. It was interesting though, when the sex toys came up, my sister and to some extent Stasia got sheepish for lack of a better word. I guess that they had made use of the toys in the basement while we were gone. I’m not going to ask. It’s none of my business, but it was kind of funny to watch my sister’s face turn red and see her sink below the level of the table top.

    After all that De la Rocha wanted to continue showing off her new ability. She can now shoot lasers from her fingertips, and she was targeting little bits of paper that were set up around the room for her to target. They decided to take it further and have Lori go up on the roof and throw some clay pigeons off the side so De la Rocha can target them with her lasers. Julia joined her up on the roof. It was fun to watch and all, but I thought it might be good to do something fun for Elena. So, I asked her if she’d like to go ice skating in the backyard. I told them that I could freeze water on the stage so we could go skating. Elena and Stasia both agreed that sounded like fun so I did just that. It was good to see Elena smile. She’s been doing it so much less than usual since Thanksgiving, and I just wanted to make her happy. To take her mind off of what our parents did. Of what the Little Shit did. I think it worked for a little while, and that’s all I can really hope for isn’t it? Reality always has a way of reinserting itself into your perceptions no matter how much we try and avoid it.

It’s second nature to me, but when we started talking about it and explaining it to those two I realized just how much information they need to know. Hell, they need to learn about the internet and email before they can learn about network security. I take my hat off to De la Rocha for tackling that burden.

    Then there was preparation for dinner. Trinh, Lori and Calamity were the main cooks in the kitchen, but the rest of us pitched in here and there. There was a lot of food, and it all needed to be done around the same time. Don’t want the potatoes getting cold while we’re waiting on the turkey. Talk about first world problems. I just reread that last sentence and started laughing. In the Lady’s realm I was lucky to have a fire to cook food over. A lot of times I just ate the meat raw. I didn’t have much choice in the matter. Cooked is always better, but sometimes you have to do what you have to do. Not so anymore. I think I’ve gotten used to having regular meals, which still feels a bit surreal if you ask me, but I expect it now. I’ve stopped hiding snacks in my go bag, though I still have a go bag. I can leave at a moment’s notice if needs be. And through all of the cooking Missy, Jane and Julia were having a cordial argument about whether or not Satan exists. Julia says he doesn’t, but the other two disagree. I don’t know if Satan exists, but I do know there are evil beings in ‘the world’ and beyond. The Gentry are not all pure bitch evil, but a lot of them are in my opinion. They have too much power and it goes to their heads. What’s the saying? Absolute power corrupts absolutely. That’s the Gentry.

    Dinner was good. The food all came out at the right time and was enjoyed by all. We even had a few extra guests. Gloria and Oleander came to celebrate the holiday with us. Oleander was still a bit toxic from her trip to the Hedge, so she didn’t stay too close to anyone else, but I know that Lori was especially happy to have her there. Elena got a bit teary-eyed a couple of times but Stasia and I were there for her. Despite being a little bit melancholy during the meal I think my sister had fun. Conversation ran the gamut from reminiscing about De la Rocha hitting Augustine with a church pew to Changeling frailties. Some of us admitted what our frailties were. I said I couldn’t drink hot drinks anymore. Hence me always making myself iced coffee. Lori has to sing if she’s asked to, Insincere Marguerite can only walk barefoot on the bare earth, and Calamity has to repair any glass broken in her presence. Some, like De la Rocha said they hadn’t noticed any frailties, or they didn’t tell what theirs were. They tend to be rather personal things. My thing with the hot drinks is annoying, but not overly personal. Calamity on the other hand is hurt if innocent blood touches her. That was something that she did not want to explain to the newbies, and I don’t blame her. I’m sure that I have another frailty, but I’m not sure what it is. I guess it just hasn’t come up.

Some people just sang what they wanted to. I got pressured into singing, so I decided to do a traditional Russian Christmas carol. Insincere Marguerite managed to find the music for it and I sang to the best of my ability. I wasn’t very good, but that wasn’t the point. I wanted my sister to have a bit of home this holiday season. I think the best was when Lori, Missy and Jane all went up to sing in harmony. They really are very good together.

So, we had French toast for breakfast. Calamity cooked. It was good. Then everyone finished packing their bags and we loaded up the vehicles.                                                 Devotees

                                                Kellie Ann Flowers approached the church with some trepidation. It wasn’t in the best part of town, and when you live in Los Angeles, that can be a very serious situation. On the other hand, it was a church… and her life to date had conditioned her to consider such places safe havens. She hemmed and hawed on the sidewalk for a moment – long enough to attract the attention of a few rough-looking young men on the corner – before making up her mind and ascending the building’s front steps.

                                                I have to know, she thought to herself with resolution. I have to find out more about what really happened.

                                                It had only been a day since the hospital had discharged her, and only two days since she had nearly died. Of course, the E.R. surgeons who had admitted her didn’t think the incident had been that serious. By the time they’d put her in the ambulance, she’d only had a few minor burns and some reddening of her skin… painful but not dangerous. No one had believed her when she told them that she had actually been inside the burning car for nearly twenty seconds… that she had felt her skin blackening and flaking off.

                                                “I know,” the sympathetic nurse had said while taking her blood pressure, “when you go through something like that, it can feel like it lasts forever. You’re just lucky it didn’t really last that long, sweetie.”

                                                She didn’t understand. None of them understood, and she desperately needed someone to understand… which is what had brought her to the church in the first place.

                                                The sanctuary was locked up tight, but the Fellowship Hall was open, and the lights were on. Long tables filled the room, and a counter separated a small kitchen at one end. At the far end of the hall, a set of stairs led down to the basement. The room appeared to be empty.

                                                “Hello?” Kellie Ann called out hesitantly.

                                                “Downstairs,” a gruff, masculine voice responded.

                                                Kellie Ann walked across the empty room, the click of her high heels echoing eerily off the bare walls. The stairs were bordered by a rusty metal railing covered in layers of peeling blue paint. She descended carefully, keeping one hand firmly on the rail. The heels were not made for stairs.

                                                The room at the foot of the stairs was some sort of youth fellowship headquarters. The floor was covered by a hideous multicolored threadbare carpet, and a number of sofas and armchairs in various stages of disrepair were arranged in a rough circle around the center of the room. The cinderblock walls were painted an institutional shade of mint green, and sported many inspirational posters or advertisements for local Christian rock concerts.

                                                An older man dressed in a cheap suit sat in the armchair opposite the stairs, while a younger man in a tight black t-shirt and camouflage pants paced restlessly around the center of the room. The older gentleman had a severe lined face and flyaway salt-and-pepper hair that descended into a somewhat unkempt beard. The younger man was heavily muscled, with neatly trimmed short black hair and a tidy goatee. Both turned to regard her as she descended, and both looked oddly familiar to her. The room was otherwise empty.

                                                “Greetings, my dear,” the older man said, rising and approaching her. “Have you come because you have accepted the inevitability of the End Times?” he asked, his intonation making the capital letters clear.

                                                “Umm,” Kellie Ann mumbled uncomfortably, “I don’t know about that. I saw your ad in the Times classified section… about the… y’know, the Angel?”

                                                “Indeed,” the older man said with a smile. “I thought I recognized you… you were there, Miss, weren’t you?”

                                                “The burning car,” the younger man said, snapping his fingers, “you were the driver!”

                                                She nodded. “I was out with a… a co-worker, when all that weird stuff started happening. I… I don’t remember it very clearly, but I know someone pulled me out of the car after it started to burn, and I know that she… she healed me. Somehow.”

                                                The older man regarded her shrewdly. “There are hundreds of people discussing what the press are referring to as the ‘Lost Angel Incident,’ Miss… why join our little group?”

                                                She shrugged. “I need to find out more… more about her. I was searching the Internet when I came across your classified ad. You’re right, there are lots of groups forming out there… but your ad was the only one I saw that mentioned the colors.”

                                                He grinned slightly. “The colors, Miss?”

                                                “Her wings,” Kellie Ann said in reply. “They weren’t white like an angel’s would be on television… they were a bunch of different colors, like some sort of tropical bird. Red and blue and green and gold. Anyway… nobody else is talking about the colors, and none of the pictures or videos on the Internet are clear enough to make them out… so I figured you guys must have been there too.”

                                                “Yes!” the older man said with fervor. “We were so privileged. I confess that at first I believed the end times had come at last, and began preparing myself for the Blessed Ascension… much to the chagrin of my wife, I am afraid.”

                                                The younger man snorted. “Started tearing his clothes off right in the middle of the street,” he commented, still staring at Kellie Ann as though he was trying to place her face. “Look, no offense, but we should make sure you are who you say you are, too, so… what color was the Angel’s halo? Can you tell me that?”

                                                Kellie Ann smiled. “No offense taken. It was green… a beautiful green like sunlight on summer grass. And kind of… pointy.”

                                                “She checks out,” the man said with a wry grin. “Anyway, I’m Stan. They call me ‘Bad Stan’ most of the time,” he added. He extended a hand and shook hers firmly.

                                                “Robert,” the older man said reproachfully.

                                                “I like Stan,” the younger man replied with an edge to his voice. For the first time she noticed that he had a pistol strapped to his waist.

                                                “You ought to embrace your Christian name,” the older man insisted. “There is nothing wrong with the name Robert Palmer.”

                                                Kellie Ann chuckled. “Robert Palmer?” she asked. “You mean like the…”

                                                “Yes,” Stan said through gritted teeth, “like the pop star from the eighties. Please call me Stan, Miss.”

                                                “Okay,” Kellie Ann said with a nod, before turning back to the older man.

                                                “Charles Nelson Grissom,” he said, extending his hand in turn.

                                                “Grissom like the astronaut,” Kellie Ann acknowledged, “one of the Mercury Seven.”

                                                The old man smiled. “My third cousin once removed,” he said proudly. “And you are…?”

                                                “Oh, sorry,” Kellie Ann said with a nervous laugh. “My name is…”

                                                “Roxy?” Stan asked, squinting at her. “Roxy Stiletto?”

                                                She closed her eyes and sighed. “That’s my stage name,” she muttered, her cheeks beginning to burn. “When I’m not working, I’m Kellie Ann Flowers.”

                                                Stan whooped and slapped his thigh. “I knew I’d seen you somewhere before!” he said with triumph. “Shit, I stuffed a twenty into your garter not three weeks ago!”

                                                “Language, Robert,” Grissom chided. He turned toward Kellie Ann with what he probably thought was a kindly expression. “Regardless of your occupation, my dear, you are welcome here. After all, the Good Book tells us that Jesus Himself ministered to the prostitutes…”

                                                “Hey!” Kellie Ann said indignantly. “I’m a dancer, not a hooker, okay? I just… I moved here from Spokane a year or so ago and things didn’t turn out like I planned, and everything is so expensive in the city, and I’m trying to get into an online program to get a degree in something, but I haven’t gotten any nibbles yet…”

                                                Grissom held out his hands to placate her. “I apologize, Miss Flowers,” he said softly, “I spoke out of turn.”

                                                “So,” Stan said, leaning against one of the sofas and stroking the handle of his pistol idly, “are we gonna talk about the Angel or what?”

                                                Grissom nodded. “A harbinger of the End Times, sent by God to warn his people that the Age of Judgment is nigh,” he said, as though stating the obvious.

                                                “I don’t know about that,” Stan rebutted. “The Angel appeared during rush hour on the gridlocked streets of L.A., and gave us the shock we needed to get moving again. I think it’s a sign that we need to break free from our own complacency and throw off the shackles of unearned authority. No man has the right to rule another, and no matter how entrenched the establishment might be all it takes is a few right-minded freedom-loving individuals in the right place to…”

                                                “Whoa,” Kellie Ann broke in. “You think the Angel wants you to… what? Start a revolution?”

                                                Stan blinked. “What else could it possibly mean?” he asked, clearly confused.

                                                “The first thing she did after she rose into the air was to stop all the violence,” Kellie Ann reminded him. “She shouted, and everyone stopped fighting and either fell to the ground or ran away.”

                                                Stan smiled smugly. “She shouted ‘enough!’” he replied. “And after that the cars started moving again. See? Enough stagnation. Enough gridlock. Enough of the rigid hierarchies of laws imposed by those who think they’re above the common…”

                                                “Or enough of this world, perhaps?” Grissom opined. “Enough sin. Enough suffering. Better to call the faithful to their eternal reward in heaven than to allow them to languish in this Hell on Earth.”

                                                “You’re dreaming, old man,” Stan said, his voice rising in annoyance. “End of the world? That’s science-fiction bullshit. It’s an escapist fantasy propagated by those who’d rather make excuses than do something to improve the world they live in. When I was in the Army…”

                                                Kellie Ann raised an eyebrow. “You were in the Army?” she asked.

                                                He shrugged. “For a little while. Corps of Engineers. Got drummed out for insubordination… totally trumped-up charge.”

                                                “Color me shocked,” Kellie Ann muttered.

                                                “In any case,” Grissom said, clearing his throat, “whatever this visitation might signify, I think we can all agree that we have witnessed something truly special, truly… unearthly. Yes?”

                                                Stan nodded. “Can’t argue with that,” he said.

                                                Kellie Ann agreed. “She healed me,” she elaborated. “I was on fire for fifteen or twenty seconds, and look at me now… a couple of fading shiny spots and some red patches like a bad sunburn. I’m not a doctor, but… shouldn’t I be lying in a burn ward talking to some specialist about skin grafts right about now?”

                                                “Yes!” Grissom said. “You weren’t the only one, either. The woman with the knives attacked two men in a nearby car, and injured the one brave fellow who tried to stop her. All three should have died from what I saw, but she blessed them with her touch, and their wounds closed.”

                                                Kellie Ann thought about telling them more… about how the woman calling herself Sabrina Watson had muttered darkly about how ‘they’ were after her just before the insanity began. Sabrina had always been odd… Kellie Ann had never really liked working with her, but somehow she always ended up doing whatever Sabrina told her to anyway. Like two days ago, she had found herself driving Sabrina back to her apartment, where the two of them intended to indulge in a little Ecstasy. Kellie Ann had never done drugs before; she was proud of the fact that she had remained clean since she arrived in L.A… but Sabrina had made it sound like so much fun…

                                                But just before the flames had erupted within the car, Sabrina had glared out of her window at the redheaded woman with the knives, and had hissed out two words: “kill them.” The woman had spun around, breaking off her attack immediately and turning her attention on the occupants of a nearby BMW. At the time, Kellie Ann had thought the woman was just a random crazy… but she had seemed so intent on harming Sabrina prior to that point. And then, as the heat haze rippled and the flames distorted her vision, Kellie Ann had been almost sure that she had seen a pair of horns poking through Sabrina’s hair…

                                                The signs seemed clear enough to Kellie Ann… her current lifestyle was putting her on the road to Hell. The Angel represented a chance at redemption.

                                                “So, a sign from God then, whether of the End Times or not,” Grissom said magnanimously, breaking her reverie. “What are we to do about it, if we cannot even agree upon what it means?”

                                                “Wish we could ask her,” Stan said after a moment. “If she’s really divine, she should know what’s best. And we should do whatever we can to help her with whatever mission the Big Guy’s sent her here to accomplish.”

                                                “We three should continue to meet, if nothing else. We must discuss this further… and as no one else – my own dear wife Ethel included – seems to have retained a clear memory of the incident, I think it is logical to assume that we three have been chosen for some greater purpose. We should keep our eyes open for further signs… further appearances of the Angel. If we can find her again, I am certain that all will be made clear.”

                                                Kellie Ann raised an eyebrow. “Oh, I know where we can find her,” she said simply.

                                                The two men goggled at her. “What?” Grissom asked, astounded.

                                                She pulled a small, weathered piece of paper out of her purse and unfolded it, revealing a printed advertising flier. The woman in the picture was unquestionably the Lost Angel. “Yeah, I recognized her as soon as I saw her. She sings and dances at a club out in Malibu… she’s kind of a local legend. She’s supposed to be really good. She disappeared for about half a year after she got married, but I hear she’s back now. Goes by the name of Lorelei Grace.”
