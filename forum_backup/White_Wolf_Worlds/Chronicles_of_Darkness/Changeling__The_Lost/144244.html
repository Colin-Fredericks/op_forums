    Alchymists of Most Noble Good

    05-21-2014, 03:59 AM
    Evil into good, that is my vow.

    Isn’t it a wonderful thing that the Devil exists? Now if you’re confused by that statement you’re in the good company. Most Changelings would say that it is utterly unfair that both the Gentry and the Infernal are allowed to exist, one would be enough for any world.

    The alcheymists see it differently. The chemical saints believe that the Inferno is cosmologically significant in a way that the Gentry are not, an absolute origin point and frame of reference for evil. More importantly, demons are an absolute origin point that can be punched in the face. The alchemyists believe that demons are the weak point in evil, defeat the demons and good will triumph over the world. Naturally they dedicate themselves to this task. Killing a demon is good, but the Entitlement’s true mission - the reason they are named Alchymists - is to transform demons into something good; they do this using a power only the fae have, the power of Pledges.

    Despite what the name would imply, the chemical saints’ closest analogue would be a monastic military order. The Entitlement is devoutly Abrahamic, filled with traditions, behavioural vows, and codes of conducts. The abrahamic faith is a divided one and these divisions are reflected upon the Noble Alchymists, Chemical Saints of different denominations often like each other as people, but are usually unable to live together due to conflicting vows and codes. The Entitelment’s typical response is to just ignore the whole thing and act as though their chapter is the entirety of the Entitlement.

    Member Titles: Noble Alchymist, Noble Alchemist, Chemical Saint.

    Picture: A gnome wearing an open brown labcoat, A long parchment is stitched to one breast of the coat, hanging loose. He wields a rangun-style chemical spray weapon and a grappling hook gun. Both are covered in alchemical and catholic decorations.

    Requirements: Wyrd 5, Clarity 6+, Willpower 6, Academics 3, one of Brawl, Firearms or Weapons at three.

    Joining: Membership of the Chemical Saints is a three step process: First the initiate must travel to one of the monasteries (there’s only a few in the world, but they’re in the Hedge so you can reach them by walking from just about anywhere) and convince the master that you’re worthy of service.

    The second stage is monastic life. The initiate must live and train as one of the Chemical Saints, proving that they can live up to the ideals and vows expected of all Alchymists. This typically lasts for a month to a year.

    The final stage is your first quest. The initiate must find and deal with a demon, an initiate may go alone or in a group of initiates though the Wyrd typically provides a greater challenge for a group and if it doesn’t the initiates may be sent out again to find a worthier challenge.

    Mein: A Chemical Saint’s Mein becomes bolder, dashing, inspiring. They are radiant, with not a hair out of place. No scar or dirt from their battles remains for long.

    Background: You might be surprised to learn that more Chemical Saints hail from Autumn than Summer. The Iron Spear are better fighters, that much is undeniable, but they are motivated by Wrath, righteous anger against their former captors. The Autumn Court seek a new beginning in the faerie world, to use what the Gentry gave them and create a life for themselves. In their exploration they may learn occult lore about the Inferno and a way to find meaning in the faerie abilities they never asked for.

    Further afield you can find alchymists hailing from both the Dawn and Sun courts. The hope enshrined by Dawn fits well with the ideal of vanquishing evil itself. As for the White Hill, the elders of the Sun Court might gripe that youngsters who believe you can vanquish evil are missing the point, but the Durandal Courts have been filed with gripping elders and stubborn youngsters since the beginning. Travel further east and you’ll find that both the Stupa and the Sword fit well with the lifestyle of the Entitlement. However the abrahamic faiths have not dominated the Directional Courts’ heartlands as they have elsewhere and so fewer members hail from those courts than you might expect.
    Whatever court they came from, the chemical saints tend to be disciplined people before they joined. Many also felt a sense of dissatisfaction with the glamour and chaos of faerie society. They didn’t want to hide, they wanted to stand tall, to stand for something.

    Organisation: The chemical saints divide themselves into chapters, each centered around a fortress-monastery in the Hedge. Each chapter lives by it’s own code and follows a different branch of the Abrahamic faith, there’s nothing to prevent two Catholic chapters existing but the Entitlement just isn’t that big.

    For most alchemists their monastery is the closest thing they’ll have to a home. They live on the road, tracking down demon after demon. Others live in a freehold, particularly if it’s in a large urban area where there’s plenty of demons to hunt. These Chemical Saints see themselves as protectors of the freehold from infernal threats, many also moonlight as Pledge lawyers. The Entitlement’s skill at pledges is renowned as the fate of any who suffers a curse of vulnerability against them.

    Privileges:

    Laws from Heaven: There are two types of being truly renowned for their still at bargains and contracts. The Fae and the Infernal. To weave contracts and trickery on helpless mortals, any fae is capable of that. To do the same to a demon, that takes the best of faerie kind. The Chemical Saints train themselves to be that best, and all have three dots in the Pledgesmith merit.

    Reliquary of Arms: Pledgebinding a demon is the ideal but not always practical, so the Chemical Saints travel armed. When a Chemical Saint performs a notable act of valor, such as their initiation rite, they are given the right to wield one of the Entitlement’s relics. A weapon, normally blinged to the nines with religious and alchemical iconography, that has been passed down the Entitlement for generations since some famous battle. The character gains a two dot Hedgespun Weapon or Token weapon which, in addition to it’s normal damage type, deals holy damage. Players may spend merit dots to buy a better or additional weapons, either earning a more venerated relic through another act of valor or creating one out of whichever non-relic they were using at the time - you have to decorate new relics yourself.

    Unbreakable Faith: Clarity can be lost through immoral fae-like behaviour on the Changeling’s part, but also from damage to a changeling’s mind. The sort of damage a demon might inflict. The Chemical Saints are trained to protect themselves, by praying for one hour in an Abrahamic temple, (including the ones in the Entitlement’s Hollows) the changeling automatically succeeds on their next roll to prevent Clarity loss, unless the Breaking Point was caused by their own immorality. In that case they suffer a one die penalty to the roll.

    Lead into Gold: The final Privilage of the Alchymists is to turn lead into gold, an evil demon into something good. They are taught a Pledge to bind demons, a pledge to perform no act unless it is good. The fact demons seem to be able to honour this pledge (no matter how much they hate it) is taken as proof by the Chemical Saints that demons truly are an absolute cosmological axis of morality. Normally the pledge is a one sided one with the Alchymist just witnessing the demons careless words before the Wyrd; if it is one sided then it will not count towards the Changeling’s maximum Pledges or cost extra duration or sanction.
    This is the typical form of the Pledge:

    Task: Greater Forbiddance (-3): Any act which is evil.
    Boon: Greater Blessing (-3): A three dot merit to provide Essence, since otherwise the demon will starve from a lack of evil long before redeeming itself.
    Sanction: Death (+3)
    Duration: Lifetime (-3)

    Rumours:
    So where does a group who spend all their time hunting demons get the cash to build a whole set of fortresses in the Hedge? And those military grade weapons underneath all the decorations, stealing isn’t their style is it? They’ve got support, someone or something is funding the Alchemists.

    You’ve read the stories right, and for us stories are practically rulebooks. You know that the Lord of Sunday doesn’t like faeries. And now we’ve got this group going around with all that holier than you and praying to you-know-who before fighting demons. Trust me, no matter how noble they seem you do not want to get too close

      On another thread I described these guys as Demon hunting faerie space marine lawyers.

      Much like 40ks Space Marines, these guys combine the attitude of a military monastic order with very modern weapons, or their hedge spun equivalents. Visually I'd say they're a mixture of heroes like the Rocketeer or Dan Dare, mixed with the Space Marines tendency to cover everything with decorations (and hand down weapons as relics), and a touch of the Tyblalt from Baz Luhrmann's version of Romeo and Juliet.


      The first Rumour is an allusion to the Lucifgue's reconciliation having a similar idea in Dream Catchers.

      The idea of pledgebinding demons like this obviously came from Planescape: Torment.
