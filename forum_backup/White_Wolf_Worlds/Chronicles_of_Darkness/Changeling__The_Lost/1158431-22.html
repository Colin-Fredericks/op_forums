                Originally posted by Poseur View Post
                Im pretty aware that we can't really say that much about the kickstarted 2ed yet, but more to come. But could someone explain the differences between 1ed and Mr. Underhills version, eg. vision, of the game as it was.
                Broadly speaking, the sorting of primary non-changeling antagonists in the Underhill version of the game was that your fetch was your main issue at low Wyrd, the Huntsman your Keeper sent after you at middling Wyrd, and your Keeper or other related Fae at high Wyrd.

                Huntsmen were generally not dispatched to retrieve a changeling until they had made a name for themselves, which made them a strong motivator for changelings trying to build greater sources of security to pursue higher ranks among the Courts and specialized jobs within the freehold, since the additional Approaches of a freehold's structural apparatus made them lower-priority targets when the Huntsmen came calling; the appeal of a Token of Reprieve was that, generally speaking, you were more likely to be offered one if you were on the lower rungs of the ladder or outright Courtless, since the Huntsmen could come after you to build up points of Yearning more easily without specialized Approaches and if you managed to hold onto one long enough you had some insurance.

                The above sorting structure also meant you were more likely to see powerful characters retreat to potent Hollows, act through proxies, and be drawn out of hermitage through the abduction or theft of characters and resources personally significant to them through history and/or pledgecraft, as well as the occasional disappearance of their Icons to elsewhere in the Hedge and the need to harvest Glamour through dreams or bounty or mortal emotion.

                The trajectory then went that at low Wyrd you could more readily spend time in the mortal world and deal with the complications of rebuilding your life in place of or separate from your fetch (who could meddle with you personally without regard for specific narrative/stylistic beats), then increasing involvement in Courtly politics eventually attracted enough accolades and infamy to warrant sending a Huntsman after you (at which point a Court offered more defense but not total defense), and after that you had the defense of your life and your holdings and your Clarity as imperiled by direct intercession from the True Fae (who were at their most constrained within the territory of a freehold acting directly against its entitled nobility) while managing emergent aspects of your Lost condition that the Hedge and Arcadia can best sustain.

                There's also the fact of how the Clarity system worked, which made the biggest difficulty high-Wyrd character had in maintaining Clarity a function of having so many things that could prompt a roll and having the consequences of failure on those rolls cascade so readily — at Wyrd 7, losing Clarity meant a full week of being more susceptible to losing Clarity and having a harder time interacting with anything that wasn't born from the Hedge, while successfully retaining your Clarity meant you got a little bit better at putting things together (regardless of where on the scale you were) but could completely lose that benefit for a scene and get saddled with a substantial penalty on all your actions from exposure to something as simple as a stroke of lightning.

                    On the kiths thing:
                    Part of it is that we had a whole bunch of them in 1E, and the reduction in number means that a lot of players with only the 1E books will crack open the 2E core as soon as they get it and find that their favorites aren't in there. Assuming that they're doing this prior to getting ahold of Book of Kiths, they'd need to homebrew them personally using the kith creation rules. While this might not be difficult for some players, others might have trouble with it or have a Storyteller that only allows published kiths in their games. This could prove especially shocking to people who hadn't been following the previews closely and were still expecting the 100 kiths promised in the Underhill previews. The fact that the new kith descriptions are so long means that some people are wishing that there had been more kiths in the same space. I don't think anyone has proposed that any of the other important information shouldn't have been included, but rather that they would have preferred 24 kiths at half the length each over 12 full length ones. I actually like the new details given, even if I would have thoroughly enjoyed having a few more. As someone who enjoys writing kiths, I've been having fun trying to convert my own (as well as some of my favorite canon kiths) into the new format, so I'm not as bothered by it as some. Granted, some are trickier than others.

                        Originally posted by Poseur View Post
                        So for us that just recently got into Changeling the Lost and aren't very well versed in the setting etc etc. I look forward to the 2ed coming out and david hills version really sold me into changeling.
                        But it seems to be more or less a flamewar going on about the 1st ed, "Underhill ed" and the new Rose and Meghan "2nd ed".
                        Im pretty aware that we can't really say that much about the kickstarted 2ed yet, but more to come. But could someone explain the differences between 1ed and Mr. Underhills version, eg. vision, of the game as it was.
                        Note, his surname is just Hill. The “Underhill” moniker refers to the fact that it was developed “Under Hill”, and it sounds very fairy tale.

                        So, one difference between 1st Edition and Underhill is the role of Kiths and Seemings. In 1e, Seemings were fairytale archetypes, and Kiths were specific expressions of those archetypes. In Underhill, Kith was how your Keeper changed you, and Seeming was how you changed yourself. Specifically, each Seeming, though it still resembled a fairytale archetype, was largely defined by its means of reclaiming agency, and were heavily inspired by common psychological responses to trauma.

                        Another big change was Courts. In 1e, the four Seasonal Courts were pretty much the assumed default, and their role in Changeling society was pretty analogous to Covenants in Requiem. In Underhill, the custom Court creation rules were the assumed default, and a few examples were given of Courts created using those rules, including the Seasonal Courts. Courts roles changed to primarily be about maintaining a Bullwark - a ritual that keeps the Gentry and their minions away by forcing them to abide by a narrative the Courts controlled in order to invade the Freehold.

                        Another big change was the addition of the Huntsmen, which didn’t exist at all in 1e but took over one of the many roles previously played by the Gentry. Instead of there being an ever-present threat that your Keeper might come for you to bring you back, the fear is that your Keeper might send a Huntsman after you to do the same on their behalf. In so doing, they allowed the Gentry to remain the distant threat, while making the immediate threat even more immediate. They also serve as something of a dark mirror to Changelings.

                        The big thing that sold me on Underhill, and was closely tied into the Seeming rules, was Clarity, which shifted from being about sanity in 1e to being about perception in Underhill, and brought the post-traumatic stress metaphor much more to the forefront.

                        There were some other, more mechanical changes as well, like how Contracts worked, how Pledges worked, etc. but I think I covered most of the bigger narrative differences.
