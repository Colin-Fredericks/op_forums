          Question: Do they need to be on par?

          One of the cool things about stories like this is finding out how the normal humans keep up. They often become the more interesting characters because their circumstances breed the greatest amount of conflict, and the way they deal with that tend to be very clever or bad ass because they push more to keep up.

          Balance is a matter of who can do the most with what they got, not some arbitrary balance of numbers. I'd actually say let your players have a run without extras and see what they do to scrape their wins. If it's too much, you can always retroactively hand them 5 or 10 EXP to give them a boost. But I think you'd be surprised how well they can keep up on their own.

                  One way to go, is to have humans focus on the supernatural merits which they even could have gotten from oaths with various changelings. Doing so also has the added benefit of integrating the mortals into Changeling society and to have them enscorcelled from the start. Alternatively you could look at the endowments of Hunter.

                  As for balance, in a perfect world where the Storyteller manages it perfectly, game mechanical power differences can be compensated for through the story. I have rarely played in such a perfect place though. The Storyteller has to be very aware of each characters' strength and weaknesses and make sure they each get equal attention. It is easy for the player of a weak character to feel useless, and such is never good for the game. Furthermore, you also need to make sure your players are up to it and that they also grasp the consequences of their choices. Story attention is not enough for everybody

                                      Originally posted by Madfox11 View Post
                                      As for the rest, I am not sure I will buy and use them in my current campaign, but nobody is forcing us to do so
                                      Of course not! Onyx Path's team of edition ninjas is still in training. They won't be able to compel compliance at swordpoint until at least November.

                                      But yeah, seriously, do not worry about it. As with every edition change in every game, there will be some wholesale switching to the new version, some sticking with the old, and a lot of tables combining elements they like from both. Of the 2e changes I've heard, I think I regard all/most of them as making a stronger game. Even so, for various reasons, I may prefer to actually play with a couple of things closer to their 1e forms.* It's a personal thing for each individual to feel out.

                                      * Or, if I'm honest, their 1e forms as heavily filtered/refined by the passage of time, incomplete memory, and unobserved thought. ^_^

                                      If it helps, many people have said they felt 1e was pretty much perfect. You certainly won't have a bad game on your hands by any stretch; it's built up a healthy following for a reason. It also has the unquestionable advantage that all of its supplements are already out, whereas there'll be a bit of a wait on 2e. When and if you decide to pick up 2e, you can make decisions about how many and which elements to use from each edition.

                                      If you're curious about any changes, you can find the open development blog posts up here and the 2e outline is available here. Plenty of people will be happy to answer any questions you have about other tidbits that may have been dropped on the forums. Same story if you'd prefer to keep on point with questions about the edition that's actually out.

                                      --

                                      Enough of that tangent. Madfox11 is also right that mortals can travel to Arcadia; they sometimes chance on openings to the Hedge. Not everyone who goes there comes back a Changeling. An alternative includes the mortal PCs being brought in by a motley, going on a rescue mission for one or more mutual recently taken (Changeling PC) friends.* Or the mortals may have also been taken, but weren't in Arcadia long enough to become one of the Lost. You've got options.
