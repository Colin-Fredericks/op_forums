                        So overall I like it.

                        My primary concern might sound a little odd, but I felt it was a little too easy to become Fae Touched. What I mean by that is that I can't see any way for; E.G. a mother to try and rescue her stolen child without becoming Fae-Tocuhed.

                        I suppose it comes down to a the exact definition of a promise - but I would imagine that mothers who promise to always be there for their children is the default state of motherhood. This means that it's actually hard to have regular mortals dedicated to rescuing Changelings without them becoming Fae-Touched.

                        I suppose the answer could be "is the game you're playing called Hunter or Changeling?" but I do think that a mortal trying to rescue a Changeling would make a good Lost NPC because Changelings would relate to the differently than to Fae Touched. It would also support the thematic point that mortals are awesome - and it was a bad thing that a Changeling's mortalness was stolen.

                        Now for various smaller points:

                        Is there enough spare wordcount for a Promise of Vengeance merit? Now he's the beloved Spring King, but once upon a time he was the junkie who knifed your son and you haven't forgiven him.

                        A possible effect would be that the Wyrd knows it's personal. Other people tend to stay out of your conflicts. If you can explain why someone's wronged you, or you have a sealed oath to avenge a wrong on someone else's behalf then any attempt to talk yourself out of trouble requires two fewer doors. If that Spring King does the sensible thing and tells the police he has a stalker, reduce the Doors by two to convince them to stay out of it. In addition you've got bonus dice on Integrity Breaking Points in pursuit of vengeance.

                        One thing I wasn't clear on is what percentage of Fae Touched have completed their quest to rescue someone? The impression I got is that most Fae Touched upon entering the Hedge end up dead or enslaved, with only a small minority returning to Earth without completing their quest and different small minority returning victorious. After all, upon entering the Hedge they have a direct line to their Changeling.

                        I just wanted to check if that's true?

                        The Drawbacks didn't feel right to me. Shortened lifespan is fine, though mostly a non-issue since games typically don't last that long. However the other three didn't feel right.

                        Arcadian Dreams: A good concept but dicepool penalties are a bit bland. Perhaps something like the Spooked Condition which explicitly encourages the player to go chasing the missing Changeling. Memories of their dreams give them fresh clues or red herrings to peruse. Alternatively give them a new Condition that's identical to Spooked, except it gives you -2 to all unrelated actions unless you spend a Willpower to suppress it for a scene. The bit about sensing the direction to their Changeling in the Hedge should probably be moved from Drawbacks to the powers section.

                        Hedge Addiction: As written this will just get worse and worse over time until you're at -5 penalties. Some way to reset the counter is required. More to the point, I couldn't find anything on what they actually do in the Hedge. Do they just have a picnic near the gate then go home? I feel that the core Fae-Tocuhed concept doesn't have enough ties to the Hedge in of itself to provide answers. I imagine they're probably not mounting a rescue attempt because that's mostly a succeed or die thing. Key Smiths make it seem like a long term lifestyle.

                        Susceptible to Madness - I understand the logic but the Madness Condition is just penalties to random dicepools. Crucially it didn't help either players or Storytellers portray madness. I wasn't too fond of it in GMC and I think it has the same problem here. Advising the storyteller to inflict Conditions penalties on Breaking Point failures or writing a new more focused Condition (perhaps one of the ones for Changelings will be suitable) could fix it.

                        When this happens, her only hope is that a changeling comes across her, as eventually her presence is noticed by one Gentry or another and she is taken.
                        There's probably some cool plot hooks that could happen if you say turning into a hobgoblin is another possibility.

                                                        Okay, first, the Fae-Touched are awesome. And fit perfectly with a C:tL character concept I've been trying to get working for, like, months. I'm going to be referring to first draft a lot over the next few weeks.
