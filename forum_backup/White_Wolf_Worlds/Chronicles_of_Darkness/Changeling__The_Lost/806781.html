    Goblin Markets: How Can I Capture That Magical Mos Eisley Moment?

    01-12-2016, 01:13 AM
    Thanks to The Force Awakens being in theaters, I've had one more instance of my mother stressing how jaw-droppingly bizarre the Mos Eisley Cantina scene in the original movie was when she saw it in theaters. The amount of talent put into the makeup, puppetry, special effects and so forth to provide such a variety of aliens was a spectacle unlike anything my parents had ever seen at that point in their lives. Cheerful music and curiosity pull you in even as strange, grotesque creatures and anxiety from the unknown repulse you.

    I'd love any advice on making a Goblin Market feel like that.
    • Strange wares: Post ideas for absurd baubles and dinguses, improbable gadgets and gizmos, forbidden fruits, bottled nonsense, and colorless red dreams!
    • Strange prices: Post impossible exchanges, in-character haggling!
    • Strange merchants and customers: Make me feel like I'm the alien who's been living a sheltered life under a rock. Because I pretty much am.
    • Unfamiliar dangers: What hazards might a naive tourist face beyond cursed merchandise?
    • Sinister delights: Make that thing I've always wanted scary! Make that thing I've always been afraid of enticing!
    On a more direct level, I'm thinking of a Goblin Market that's decided to "modernize" in a twisted attempt to mimic a sci-fi convention and/or a tech expo, which might leave a mortal thinking he stumbled into a real life version of Mos Eisley full of "aliens" if he saw it.

          If you have the option to do so, there is a lot of creepy carnival music (horror style) on youtube which I used for the single goblin market scene that I've had the pleasure of STing so far. Sadly, the motley crew of characters had the decency to critically fail when discussing sensible matters with a neurotic goblin dealing in ancient fae explosives which ended up blowing the market to pieces. So, no market anymore

          Other than that, I really focus on the ambivalency of dealing with goblins. They might tell you that all their ware is extremely expensive and well-crafted, just to turn around and offer the same wares at the price of a piece of junk. Also, I usually depict goblins as very alternating in emotional states, going from happy to hateful in an instance.

          In terms of wares: Anything. Pieces of junk, fae artifacts, standard electronical devices, ice cream. For me, the fun of creating a goblin market is partly that there are absolutely no limits to what is offered, and at what price.

          Dangers really depend on the power level of your group, I'd say. Typically, goblins are the most obvious danger, albeit in a more social and mental than in an actual physical way. There also is the possibility of other changelings, maybe shopping for some sinister move they are trying to make and would likely not leave witnesses of. Finally, the gentry themselves, usually in disguise, present very interesting ways of weaving dangerous social interactions into the scene with a chance of erupting into actual combat if the characters aren't careful (or roll those critical failures we all love. sigh). As a general rule, I want to focus on social perils when planning a goblin market and leave the more combat-oriented dangers to the thorns and other places.

              Strange merchants and customers - A merchant haggles with thin air - gradually you realise there's an invisible patron (but all the merchants can perceive them just fine). You experience a burning/formication whenever you get too near to an ordinary-looking person. One of the merchants exists in two, or more than three, dimensions and it hurts your eyes just to look at them (which really helps them get a better deal out of you). One of the stalls is apparently sentient, or controlled remotely, with clockwork-limbs manipulating the merchandise and no face to look at (or even a source for the voice). One merchant/stall appears differently to everyone. Another is on puppet strings that stretch up into the clouds.

              Sinister delights - A creepy,possibly morally-compromising provenance can taint almost anything. 'This confection was taken from the dream of a child!'. 'You want mortal wealth? Of course. You'll need a drop of blood from the person you want to ruin, of course. Money has to come from somewhere!'. Making something enticing can work like this in reverse - it's the effect you want, but the container/method of use is horrible. 'Just put this beetle under your skin and you'll find yourself blessed for a day!'. 'Eat this rotten heart and it'll cure your heart condition (or your money back).' Another touch is that the market seems to have a rather impressive personalised marketing campaign - signs advertise things very specific to you, such as a cure for your embarrassing illness or reference, obliquely, the events of your life or even what you've come for, Merchants accept payment only in very specific memories other people are unlikely to possess.

              Unfamiliar dangers - Forget cursed items, or items with nasty drawbacks you're not told about... How about goods which require a level of... installation? Your magic sword needs to taste your blood to attune itself to you. Your token-limb requires the hob-merchant hack off your old one (with a rusty saw). The opposite issue - giving away the colour of your eyes or some of your dreams hurts and might impose Conditions. How about the danger of comign to get one thing but getting something else instead? Tempt players with awesome things they don't need - and can't afford in addition to what they for - so that they either don't get what they wanted or leave with an appropriate sense of ennui 'Next time I'm buying the damned unicorn.'

              Strange wares - Revenge. No matter what you ask, it's unclear if this achieved through magic/fate (and if this will affect you at all), the hiring of a hobgoblin to beat up someone or something else. But the merchant promises you your vengeance at bargain rates! One merchant is offering a perfect death and he's popular - people are queuing up to die well and you can;t help but notice that a few of them don't seem to leave his curtained tent.

                      That sounds like a pretty good picture of how a goblin market should feel from time to time.

                      In terms of items: I had one of the merchants sell a fae dagger dripping with poisonous liquid as long as the wielder spends 1 glamour each to keep the poison around. He only asked for the tear of a true fae or dominion over the world as a price. The characters, especially the one who has a specialty with daggers, were furious. Other interesting items included:

                      A piece of junk metal that serves as a storing place for glamour (allowing a changeling to keep a glamour reserve, so to speak) but being made of cold iron, it actually would damage any changeling trying to use it in the process. They never found this though, so I didn't have to think about a price. It likely would've been very low, as the merchant didn't really know it's worth. I usually improvize prices, as a sidenote.

                      A drop of Vitae in crystallized form, created by a true fae craftsman. No direct purpose, although any vampire would likely pay a high price for such an artifact. They didn't find this either because they were to busy blowing up the market.

                      Odd tokens, like a watch that stores time, making the wearer age faster but by turning back the watch it would rejuvenate the wearer (same or other person) by the amount of time stored by the watched. Could also be used for partial rejuvenation, if not all stored time was used. I plan on giving this to the players on another occasion as they didn't think of buying it and they might actually need it some day.

                              "Freshly squeezed wisdom, straight from the tooth! For only the price of your shoes!"

                              The wart-covered flabby-skinned vendor with wild bulging eyes waves a cork stoppered test tube filled with a chalky white fluid as he tries to entice you to buy his wares. Rows of similar test tubes line racks in his stall and the flimsy battered card table in front of the stall has a small open wooden box filled with what appears to be human molars.

                              Do you Purchase, Decline, or Haggle?

                              Purchase
                              "What the heck, they're only shoes, why not try something even if it turns out to be a scam." you think to yourself. You say "Sure, I'll take one." The merchant doesn't even haggle with you, which you find odd, but you accept the test tube you feel a breeze on your feet, but then are surprised to notice that you aren't barefoot! You look down and confirm what your skin told you, you're wearing sandals. Puzzled, you wonder why you would be wearing anything on your feet because you traded your shoes but then you remember you've never owned shoes, like for your whole life. That's a strange memory and you have a moment of panic as you realized what you really traded!! Your shoes means ALL YOUR SHOES, EVER!

                              Gain the Condition:

                              Never wearing proper shoes. Always have a -1 equipment penalty when checking contested foot chases/races rolls against those wearing more athletic footwear or naturally barefoot runners. Also may be subject to taking damage if with adequate protection for feet would be needed. Also will not be able to meet certain dress codes.

                              Gain a Story Beat when lack of proper shoes causes a hardship like being refused service/admittance due to dress codes or lose an important foot chase or foot race.

                              Do you save for later, discard, drink the test tube?



                              Decline
                              You think to yourself, "Silly hob, no way I'm going to trust that deal!" After smugly scoffing, you wander further into the market to be tempted by other outrageous bargains.


                              Haggle
                              You glibly say "These shoes?" The hob clarifies with emphasis "Your shoes." You feel a little wary by his wording.

                              Choose from the above options or clarify again.


                              Clarify Again
                              You say with emphasis as you point down "These shoes on my feet?" and the hob replies smugly pointing straight at your chest "Your shoes." as if that is his final answer and that he isn't going to clarify further. Choose Purchase or Decline above.


                              Save for Later
                              Feeling foolish you tuck away your purchase to investigate later. You wander further into the goblin market, and think to yourself that even if it is a scam you bought at least a little wisdom this day.

                              Choose from below to Discard Test Tube Later or Drink the Test Tube


                              Discard the Test Tube
                              Angrily you smash the test tube on the cobbles stones and the vendor just shrugs and mumbles "You bought, you can break it if you want." You are so angry. You fume as you think to yourself that at least you got a little piece of wisdom today even though it was a scam.


                              Drink the Test Tube
                              "You think to yourself "Might as well, I already paid for it" As you swig the chalky and salty brine you have a flash of realization that you should invest in the future. You even get some ideas on what may help. Gain a Story Experience or Sane Experience that you may spend now.


                              Discard Test Tube Later.
                              You throw the test tube in the trash regretting your folly.

                                  Pale_Crusader Nicely written.

                                  Expanding on my idea of a sci-fi flavored Goblin Market:

                                  Location: Fictional Texas city on the Gulf Coast, derelict theme park "Port Atlantis"

                                  "Port Atlantis" was an ill-conceived business venture, derided as a "wannabe EPCOT Center" that didn't know if it wanted to be a theme park, tech expo, or aquarium. As many pundits observed, Atlantis sank like its namesake, only financially instead of literally. To mortal eyes, the run down park appears to be a popular spot for science fiction LARPers to gather for massive games. The resulting "used future" aesthetic makes the place look like a seedy space/sea port in the armpit of the galaxy. Those who can see through the mask, however, see a Goblin Market that takes the usual anachronism in a different direction: In an attempt to "modernize," the merchants have gone retro-futuristic. Here, magic is woven to mimic the expectations of Hollywood science, including strange radiations, explosive malfunctions, AIs both naive and sinister, and "aliens" who appear mostly human, but with a dash of the exotic. Any mortal or changeling who visits is inevitably bombarded with the unfulfilled promises of the space age: Flying cars. Hover boards. Robot servants. Plots of land on the moon. Trips to Mars. Genetic modifications in a bottle. Cybernetic enhancements. As expected of a Goblin Market, these purchases are unreliable or dangerous at best.

                                      Hmm... this gives me an idea for a very different sort of "high-tech" Goblin Market:

                                      The Market is tricky to get to, not only because it's discreetly tucked away in the hedge, but also because the trod that takes one there can only be accessed from inside the closed campus of a major Silicon Valley tech company. Upon arriving at the market itself, visitors are greeted by a crude banner that looks like it came out of an old dot matrix printer and reads "Goblin Disrupt." Passing under the banner, one enters a large clearing on the hedge, populated by a motley assortment of colorful and unlikely creatures decked out in cast-off clothing ranging from tattered polo shirts to slick (if slightly threadbare) business suits.

                                      Shoppers at this peculiar Market find themselves bombarded from all sides by enthusiastic goblin salesmen giving presentations on the amazing and (allegedly) unique products and services they can provide. Most use visual aids, ranging from surprisingly professional-looking PowerPoint presentations to trifold display boards that would look at home in an elementary school science fair. The sellers make grandiose statements about how groundbreaking and life-changing their offerings are, and pepper their pitches liberally with marketing catchphrases, corporate buzzwords, and tech industry jargon, showing little or no regard for the actual meaning of the words.

                                      Most of the items on sale are somehow related to electronics or computer software, or are at least dressed up to look like they are. Merchandise can resemble artifacts belonging to any time period since the beginning of modern computing, so one seller might offer a USB drive containing all the secrets of a once-famous stage magician, while another might offer a thick stack of punch cards that somehow encode the memory of a true love's first kiss. Buyers lacking the necessary hardware to access their purchase can usually request "technical support" from the seller - for a nominal fee, of course.

                                      Perhaps more unusually, some of the items on sale appear to be entirely digital in nature. A Changeling might be granted access to a Goblin Contract by clicking on "I Agree" at the end of what looks for all the world like a particularly oddly-worded EULA, while a vice peddler might offer its wares in the form of special QR Codes which enable a mobile device to access otherwise nonexistent web content (even though the Market lacks both cellular reception and wireless internet access). Other items are only superficially, even lazily dressed up as any sort of modern technology. One seller offers an assortment of goblin fruits bearing old "Intel Inside" stickers, while another offers cutting edge "personal digital assistants" who are plainly just hobgoblin servants in fanciful robot costumes. The latter merchant does warn that his products have a tendency to run off to the hedge once their "warranty" expires, which, he adds, is a great opportunity to upgrade to a newer model.

                                          Continuing on Port Atlantis:

                                          Despite the distaste the local Seasonal Courts have for the market and the slave trade that usually comes with it, too many Changelings find it useful. They managed to reach a compromise: As long as the freehold liaison or his chosen representative remains on market grounds, there will be no slave trading in the market. The goblin head of the market has taken the name of Ubuntu Serif, and the appearance of an elderly guru from India with deer/antelope features, including fractal antlers that make your eyes itch if you look at them too long. Ubuntu enjoys humanity in a way fitting of a goblin merchant: He likes people who are willing to take risks and/or suffer simply to sate their curiosity. One thing that makes him stand out from most goblin merchants is his "negative pitch," where he honestly emphasizes how dangerous his wares (or their prices) are. He usually avoids any specificity in those dangers, and sometimes even avoids speaking about the benefits. He's largely successful because he's adept at spotting anyone who fosters that sort of self-destructive curiosity. When the market is closed, he runs a well-hidden rare book shop in the mortal world.

                                          Ubuntu Serif quotes:
                                          • "Suffering brings enlightenment. I'll take that over blissful ignorance."
                                          • "That Large Hadron Collider was a huge disappointment. They promised us black holes! Who cares about bosons?"
                                          • (When a half-insane customer tries to return a cursed book) "Did you not see the yellow sign?" (He points to the sign at the front of the store: "No Returns")
                                          • (Amused laughter as he reads a mortal textbook on economics.)
                                          Rumors:
                                          • The merchants have been getting very adept at luring the liaison away to ... "frolic" ... long enough to get some quick slave trafficking in. The liaison has been looking for strong willed individuals to take his place as a representative. Or to keep his vice under control.
                                          • Every once in a while, Ubuntu offers cold hard cash from his bookstore's sales at Port Atlantis specifically to demonstrate that you can put a dollar value on various abstractions.
                                          • The Autumn Court is working with the market because they expect Ubuntu to put a valuable magical tome up for sale, either at Port Atlantis or his bookshop. Either way, they're keeping eyes on his wares.
