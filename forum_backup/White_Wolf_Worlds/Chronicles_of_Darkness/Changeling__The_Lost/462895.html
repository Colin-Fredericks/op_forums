If true, what are the implications of this? What's the metaphysical relationship between Contracts, Embeds and/or Exploits?

              Fuck no. A thousand times fuck no.

              There's a similarity in that there is the establishment of rules, but the Machine works on scientific principle pushed ahead of schedule-several embeds draw from psychological phenomenon as well as edge questions about quantum physics(Or hell, even quirks of normal physics). When it makes things like the movies, it's relying on the law of averages to produce an outcome that looks like the movies and then precludes all other options.pacts are a matter of re-programming the reality after the fact, which is why you have the odd tansferences of memories and the somewhat abrupt transfer of blessings.

              A Stranger or a changeling doesn't care about statistical probability and physics. To a fae, a one in a million shot is really a one in two-either it narratively happens or it doesn't*. This is because the Wyrd is a force of connection and context, metaphor and simile. When things happens like the movies around a goblin, it's because the fae are exploiting the Wyrd connecting these things together. THe contracts of the fae are the matter of establishing a connection with the Wyrd, and that's why it takes some time for those blessings to appear.

              *SPOILERS FOR MADOKA MAGICA!
              In the show, they mention the odds of recovering a magical girl from their fate as a witch is one in a million. When Kyoko goes to save Sayaka, she fails. Later on, in the movie, without having her god-powers, it's up to Madoka to save Homura from herself as Homulily. Statistically speaking, she should fail, because the odds are still just as bad as before-in fact, they're worse. But we've already seen the NO answer come up, so the only answer left to explore narratively is YES-so that's what happens. It works, because a YES answer here opens up the door for more conflict(Essentially making it a YES BUT answer). In the end though, as far as saving witches and turning them back to magical girls, it should always more or less be impossible to do, but in reality, it's fifty fifty, because it's Either a NO or a YES.

                                                          Alright. I kinda hate cross-over, but in the name of trying to be helpful, let's make an attempt.

                                                          What if it were possible to actually storm Arcadia? It wouldn't be easy, but the first thing you'd have to do is shift your perspective. Understand that Arcadia is basically a massive network of ancient Infrastructures cut loose from the G-M, running on a perverted form of Essence called Glamour. At first, maybe you'd recruit a Demon or two to help you figure things out. They get some Aether out of the deal; Aether that you can't use anyways. The trick is to find the places where the Keepers can't see, but are still Arcadia. The real Arcadia. The clockwork Gears. The mechanical Turk invisibly playing chess. Some Demons know how to find those; they know what to look for. You smash those and entire Realms cease to exist. Some of the True Fae flat-out dissolve on the spot; the fetches they made crumbling and the Changelings who escaped finding themselves still Mantled and Pledged, but without Contracts or Glamour. The consequences of even an incomplete, if massive, victory.

                                                          But then you realize that the G-M is interested in helping you. It doesn't want Arcadia around anymore. Making Changelings is a problem for it: sometimes the abducted were necessary pieces for a matrix. Sometimes it fails to notice that its matrix has been disrupted by a replacement fetch and everything comes crashing down. A Messenger Angel gives advice to one of your friends on how to re-insert himself into his old life. A Guardian Angel shows up when you're cornered by your Keeper. This G-M: it's being helpful. It's being trustworthy. Maybe you should do this. Break Arcadia itself. Sacrifice a Demon's trust to gain the G-M's. It offers to crack you open, unlock the sixth dot of a Contract: an Exploit. No fairy catch to it: merely that your mask shudders from its use. Do you take that gift?

                                                          Of course, what you don't know is that the G-M is ready to betray you. It perceives the Keepers as creating half-angel, half-human hybrids. It's smart. It knows what the root of the problem is. But once the root is destroyed, there's still the matter of clean-up. If you find out, is saving your skin worth allowing Arcadia, all the suffering Changelings, all the Changelings-to-be, to keep going? Is saving all the Changelings who have already escaped once worth the inevitable creation of more? And what do the Demon rings think of all this? Is a Saboteur going to decide that Changelings need to therefore die? It doesn't see that you're trying to break a piece of the G-M; it sees that you're a product of the G-M, indirect and much-removed, but a product and thus inevitably part of its ineffable plan. Is an Integrator going to try to re-connect Arcadia to the G-M, re-assert its control, rather than see it destroyed? What if some of the Changelings are helping it? They were promised that their Contracts would be restored by such an act.

                                                          I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".
