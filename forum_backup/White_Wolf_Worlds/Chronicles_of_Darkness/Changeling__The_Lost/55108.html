    Courting New Ideas

    01-21-2014, 09:51 PM
    I have run Changeling with three different gaming groups and every time I introduce the courts, it really confuses them. On one hand, your "court" is tied to your mantle and preferred glamor-feeding (you are what you eat, I suppose). But your court is also a political entity for a region, headed by the most powerful of the Lost. But that political group shifts every three months, handing over the crown as the seasons change, so it's really a set of four "power couples" who pass around the responsibility of being CEO, but all of them are on the board of directors (so to speak)? The system seems extremely unstable with the constant switching of who is at the reigns-- how do they get anything done? And that brings up the main question-- what does the court do anyway? If this was an old fashioned aristocracy, they would be training and arming an group so those knights & soldiers could beat up people for their lunch money, or in other words, taxes. If the Lost Courts are a more modern form government, they would be funding public works with taxes-- building roads, collecting trash, keeping up a police force and law courts, etc. So I'm curious, what you do think the Lost Courts do? Do your courts switch heads of state every three months?

      They're a support group for a bunch of people who've had their lives thrown out of whack in a way that most of human society can't really understand and the structure exists to try to capture some of the feel of Arcadia without the constant gaslighting and horrifyingly unchecked power imbalance. "Getting things done" is tertiary. (Blending in with mortal society and keeping everybody mostly functional is secondary. "Finding everyone something to do" is part and parcel of that primary point, given that just about everybody had to break out of a role to get back to Earth and being stuck in that in-between phase is not great for Clarity even in a place where the world isn't likely to visibly change in the blink of an eye because a mad god-beast thought it would be funny.)

      The cycling leadership is insurance against this relatively safe space becoming yet another dreamlogical domain and also gives the Lost hope for the next season if this season's Monarch is particularly unpleasant but a revolt isn't in the stars.

          I'm very new to the system and CtL, but I think there is a lot of potential 'deep narrative' in the higher purposes of the Courts. While it could be that Courts would turn with the seasons that are mundane, my sense of story would be that this is not what actually happens.

          As the fae clock seems to run slower, and the powers represented by the Courts are also a philosophy, and/or if you are using some of the 'foreign courts' of other continents........... I'd not expect to rotate rulers every three months. I'd expect magic to be turning those wheels, not the calendar, and it would be hard for the PCs to put their finger on the pulse of those changes. Further, it might involve secrets that someone with High Wyrd, or very specific Social skills might learn about.

          It could also be entirely like Courts of old, where a large part of the Court's duties is preparing to resist incursions of the Fair Folk. Who is to say that there are not constant tricks built/moved/added to the Hedge, or some spying on deep Fair Domains, to see what abuses are being done?

          I could see one campaign where the Courts are like the NSA or magic rumors.... another where live combat is going in deep in the Hedge with terrible Dream Monsters.... another where Goblins and various low power races are actually allies with the Lost Courts, in some hope that centuries of abuse by the Fair Folk might some day end.

              One purpose of the Courts is to befuddle the Gentry. Keepers are inherently static and greedy creatures. The act og voluntarily turning over power without a fight is completely alien tot eh Keepers’ mindset. It makes the changelings less Arcadian, and thus harder for the Gentry to home in on. When Courts stop power-sharing, they become more like the Gentry, and thus easier to ferret out.

              Also, they are not really governments, but more like clubs or mutual-aid societies. Yes, there are rules, and you pay dues if you are able, but those can be in favors or aid, not necessarily in money. Courts definitely aren’t going to open hospitals. If a changeling needs a job, and you own a business, maybe he becomes a night watchman or a clerk in some essentially made-up department with a generic name and a murky place in the corporate org-chart. Remember, changelings are profoundly broken people with enough idiosyncrasies to fill the Pacific; herding cats doesn’t even begin to describe it. Cajoling and just being there to listen is going to work much better than penal codes and tax breaks.

              Changelings also know the schedule for turning over power. I imagine non-time-critical decisions get put off until the appropriate Court is in power. Unless someone needs to be taken out now, leg-breaking would be dealt out mostly in the summer months. On the other hand, Courts don’t just dissolve in the off months, so if some person just needed to be put in the hospital, the Winter Queen might ask the Summer King to pretty please send some of his courtiers with anger-management issues to pay said person a visit.

              And always remember that the other Courts are not the enemy. Courts may give each other grief, and try to one-up each other to a certain extent. But the enemy is the Gentry. No one goes so far as to hamstring their allies enough to let the Gentry profit. It’s like a big clannish family. We may slap each other around every once and awhile, but we will beat you senseless if you slap one of us.

                  Winter is probably perfectly capable of handling that themselves, as are the other courts. It would be a rare Court that wouldn't have someone capable of being menacing or taking someone out, and Winter are famed for their assassins. The four courts aren't just party-goers, scholars, sneaks, and fighters. I prefer to view them in terms of how they choose to define their relationship with the Gentry. Winter hides, Summer fights, Autumn studies, and Spring prefers to throw itself deep into the human side of the world, but none of this means that you couldn't find a Spring Court thug (he just wouldn't be as interested in fighting the Gentry, just other humans) or a Summer scholar (he'd just be more interested in the practical applications than an Autumn colleague might be). Events have to be a bit more on the large scale side before I see them working in the way you describe (Winter is entirely capable of putting someone in hospital, but if a war against the Gentry is looming...).

                  As to the seasonal rotations, every three months is exactly how it works in my Freehold. The one possible exception is that the other three courts have little respect for Winter (which has fewer than half the members of the others), where it's become accepted that Freehold issues are voted on by all four Kings and Queens, with Winter generously being allowed to break ties.

                  This all sort of ties into the OP's question. The principle role of the Court Leaders and the Freehold is to take care of its members. Each Court has a different philosophy, but the voluntary giving up of power helps to keep them hidden from their old captors. Most of the time, the main job of the Freehold is not to have Keepers or mortal authorities noticing and mucking with their business. I quite like Satchel's response as well.

                      I think the way in which the Courts are presented in the rulebooks are confusing as well. As presented, the Seasons are metaphysically philosophical support groups as well as political organizations that govern matters of supernatural and secular importance. They speak of the unifying nature of freeholds and then speak of the Courts as quarrelsome clans bent on the subjection of their peers. What the hell? In Rites of Spring, it is written that changeling freehold organization varies greatly depending on culture and region ... and then Lords of Summer goes and contradicts it. Ugh.

                      For me, the Seasonal Courts are not really a political entity. It is more like a philosophy/religion that just happens to have the benefit of being backed by one of the fundamental forces of Nature. One's Mantle is not representative of that changeling's status or relevance in freehold culture, but rather it is a sign of that changeling's particular devotion to the precepts and ideals of their Season. The benefit of high Mantle, besides being able to shoot lasers out of your eyes and what not, is that one's greater connection to the earthly Seasons strengthens their connection to the real world, making it difficult for fairies to spy on or manipulate them. Areas that have high concentrations of Seasonal courtiers (freeholds) tend to have lower instances of fairy mischief and malice due to the power of so many Mantles gathered in close proximity.

                      Of course, since changelings are people, they'll want to organize in some way. This is especially true given the nature of the threat that all changelings are running from. Since birds of a feather flock together and all that jazz, changelings group themselves with other folks that share their beliefs and opinions. However, the manner in which the Seasonal courtiers organize depends on a lot of factors. But the manner in which a Freehold is run, ultimately, has more to do with the ambitions and motivations of the changelings who are a part of it and less with their Seasonal alliances. Some freeholds symbolically give power to courtiers when the Season of their affiliation rolls around, and everyone tries to adopt some of the traditions of that belief system until the weather starts to change. But not everyone feels so compelled by faith, even magic fairy faith, and some will act against the ruling Courts interests. However, it is usually best not to mess with courtiers when their Season is in power, as strange weather phenomena and strange destinies become more common when courtiers of the ruling Season get messed with.

                      So what do the Lost Courts do? Well, they do whatever you want them to do. Some freeholds may be organized like a mafia syndicate, another may be centered around a popular downtown nightclub scene. Changeling courtiers color their lives with their Seasonal alliances, they are not ruled by them

                          I'd like to thank everyone for participating so far-- everyone has brought something different to the table and that is really helping me think through my own ideas and assumptions more fully as well as adding to the mix. I keep rolling around Satchel's and Phalamir's ideas of the Court as support group-- I admit that I never quite thought of it that way because I was imagining that the motley was a social/support group and the courts were something else-- a more political something. If the Courts worked in strong support capacity, it would fundamentally change how I introduce them to the characters-- they would be ready to show up very early to help though the transition to the mortal world.

                          Currently, my version of Changeling courts are running on a few key ideas-- I understand that my version is not really that close to the rule book version.

                          Power in any given court rests on four high Wyrd ranking pairs (they are not necessarily couples, but they are allies). Each couple is important because others of their season respect them and feel they are affiliated with them in a basic way. One couple is ascendant, or Regent and will remain so, like a British Prime Minister, until there is a lack of confidence and it goes to a vote. This means that an area might have a set of rulers for fifty years, or maybe for just one. It also means that if a region attracts more of a certain type of season, because the climate (both meteorological and social), then one seasonal group will have an advantage. For example, there are a lot of Summer Lost in Las Vegas because of the hot climate, but the vice centered economy also means that Spring Lost are found there aplenty. Those two groups tend to hand power back and forth. There are some Autumnal and Winter Lost there, but their voting block tends to be smaller.

                          I think my most non-canon idea is that Courts and Freeholds are two different things. I have a map of North America that is broken into territories defended by Changeling courts. There are places left out of the map-- Detroit for example is not held by any Changeling court because Vampires are so dominant there that the turf is not claimed. The Courts are devoted to holding territory, squabbling occasionally with other Changelings for territory, fending off Keepers, ferreting out puppets, and enacting some ritual magics for the seasons. Freeholds are city-states with that are neutral politically and only hold a tiny territory. They offer places for courts to meet and confer, people from different courts to do business, and a place to live that is less dominated by seasonal politics and wyrd power. Freeholds provide community to those on the outs with Lost in power at a court, but also give someone with high Wyrd but no desire to rule a place to live in peace.

                                  Firefly Night has very good questions-- really they are part of the heart of why I asked about everyone's take on the system in the first place. I have some answers that I've come up with in my game, but I continue to be interested in other people's take on why courts are what they are and do what they do.

                                  Essentially, Firefly has hit on my answer: my version of the court structure exists because of the Seasons. In order to anchor themselves to the mortal realm and prevent the Keepers from taking them back or overrunning the world, the Changeling Courts perform rituals that enrich the Seasons, generating power by creating the right emotions in the mortal populace.

                                  Every court needs representatives of all four seasons (which also represent the most primal emotions) to enact the Solstice and Equinox rituals properly, which create barrier that force the Gentry to follow rules in this world that they do not have to in their own. The highest mantled people of any given area are both needed and to some extent driven by their pacts with their Season to enact these rituals. Others of their season are attracted to these events to both Harvest but also because they affirm their connections to the mortal realm.

                                  The people that are called to work the rituals are always one male and one female because the Seasons and the mortal realm in general favor the complimentary pair. We see this in dualities of death & birth, night & day, and in the realm of contracts the “eternal” and “fleeting” pairs. If the Seasonal magics are left undone, then that region becomes very vulnerable to Keeper invasion, and the mortals and Lost suffer.

                                  The Courts are not affiliated with one another in any top down system—they fight and squabble for territory like feudal Europe (and marry and ally in the feudal way as well). All of my stuff is pretty big and abstract, how a particular Court actually runs on a day to day basis could differ quite a lot from other Courts as long as the basic purpose is carried out by the top people.

                                  As for the crowns, while top couple in each season are designated King and Queen, only the Regent couple are in power. The Regents have the Crown; if there is a successful take over, the new couple's Crown will manifest as a result of the combined will of the Lost and the blessing of the Season.

                                      Like phalamir said, the main thing is (even if the changelings don't know it) that Gentry don't understand court system. It doesn't matter what Courts rule, just that the rule changes. (If it has to be the four courts, then Dawn/Dusk and Sun/Mon pairs would be impossible). In Lords of Summer Winnipeg freehold has Winter and Summer court, Summer ruling during the three summer months and Winter during the rest, but the Winter changelings doing certain rituals before and after Summer's rule to emulate the other courts.

                                      Imagine being in a hobby club, where the ruling party changes every year and the new ruling group decides things without asking from other people. And then imagine that it's not just the hobby, but they also have a word to say about where you live, where you work and so on. That's how it is. You are part of the Court, they are part of your life.

                                                  My game is set in Brisbane, Australia, so the city has a mortal population of about two million. There are a total of 30-odd Changelings in the city. The Spring Court is close to half the population on its own, Autumn and Summer are roughly even, and Winter has just 5 members. The entire Freehold is clustered in around the CBD, having retreated to an area that was traditionally Spring territory following a dust storm (cover for a Fae attack) a few years ago. Spring was the only Court to come through relatively intact, thanks to the powers of their high Wyrd Oracle Child-Queen. New Changelings don't emerge very often (the PCs were the first arrivals in the four years since the Fae attack, but there have been a few more arrivals in the six months since then), and there is a general siege mentality. The Summer Court are pretty much all hunkered down in a fortified Hollow, the Autumn Court owns a few businesses in the local China Town and has strong roots in both criminal gangs and the police force, and the Spring Court mostly revolves around a small hotel. Oh, and one member owns a brothel.

                                                  For me, Changeling populations are much more tightly dictated by the availability of trods than the amount of Glamour available (Glamour doesn't take anything from the person supplying it, so it's hardly a limited resource). In my game, the population is kept on the low side by a few nasty Fae that have been locking down the local trods, but when their alliance crumbled Changelings started to trickle through again. I could easily see a massive Changeling population in a small town of a few thousand people, so long as there were plenty of relatively safe trods nearby.
