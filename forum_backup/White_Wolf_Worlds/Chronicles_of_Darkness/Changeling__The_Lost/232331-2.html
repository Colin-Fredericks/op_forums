    This is probably way too early for this at this point in dev, but one of the things I want addressed is Cold Iron and what it is. Partway through the line, there was a retcon that it was iron that had never been heated by human agency, which kept it appropriately rare, I suppose, but was weird on way too many levels. Just for starters, how do you actually make anything but a crude club out of that?

    For another, it was weird internally in the line, too -- the corebook had a vignette where a (later enchanted) human managed to grab a bit of wrought-iron fence that, from its effects, was implied to, through a stroke of luck have been Cold Iron. It's weirdly lucky even if Cold Iron is something more sensible, but when you can only get it from meteorites (Because you have to heat ore to extract usable iron) and any way humans actually work iron makes it no longer "Cold", it's... yeah.

    I'll give my books a (re)read and see if I can think of anything else, but separating Kith and Seeming seems good, and fits my long-planned C:tL character (a clockwork dancing doll who, shaped by her Durance but also trying to reject the role her Keeper put on her, has gone heavily into mechanical engineering, clockwork, and kinetic sculpture) a bit better, since in everything except that Doll = Elemental, Fairest (possibly transferring to something appropriate under Wizened if she can manage it) seemed more appropriate for her.

        This would definitely address the concern I voiced earlier. On a conceptual level, I heartily approve! The next question is what that would mean in terms of mechanics: I’m assuming that your choice of Seeming still provides a strength and a weakness as well as favored and disfavored Contracts; if you merge Beast and Elemental into Primal, what would its strength and weakness look like? The contracts favored by Beasts and Elementals would be favored by Primal; what sorts of Contracts would be “disfavored”?

        BTW, I’m not too hot on the notion of shutting the door on Contracts due to your choice of Seeming; I’d rather have something like Mage’s Ruling/Common/Inferior setup, where Contracts that somehow conflict with your Seeming are more expensive or otherwise discouraged but not flat-out forbidden.

        Back to Beasts and Elementals: if for some reason you decide that reducing the number of Seemings from six to five is a bad idea, you might consider splitting the concept of Primal along some other dividing line besides Beast and Elemental — though I can’t think of what that might be off the top of my head.

                        Originally posted by MachineIV View Post

                        Beast and Elemental are actually one of my big challenges conceptually right now. As I keep considering more and more content, I'm finding that the two overlap in a lot of ways. It's a case of something where I could merge them, I could change them, or I could just leave them the same. But just leaving them the same doesn't address the cognitive issues I'm hitting as I play at them.
                        (A brainspew of my own half-formed idea.)

                        Perhaps rather than Elemental/Beast the divide could be Inanimate/Animate. (I recall DA: Fae had something similar with the Inanimae. I need to go grab that book out of storage).

                        The Inanimate embody the unliving concepts in Arcadia - the metal and stone of building blocks and gateways, the fire and light illuminating the cities, the clouds and wind gusts and the rains and flowing rivers.

                        The Animate were formed the populations of living things - all the flowers, trees, birds and animals in the forests. The flesh-eating plants and the rabid attack-hounds guarding the Fae castles. The decorative birds in the cages. The thick-furred companions sleeping at their master's feet.

                        Just a thought.

                        Edit: Changelings making up those animate/inanimate objects that aren't just Gentry-constructs, in case that wasn't clear.

                                                        I'm interested in this concept. Unlike some of the other posters, I have actually created characters far more defined by their Seeming than their Kith. That could be because I'm an Ogre fan, however. Ogres, whether they be Gristlegrinders, Bloodbrutes, Gargantuans, even a Witchtooth or what have you, all have a pretty strong Seeming theme to fall back on. They feel more united in their Seeming than Elementals, Beasts, or Wizened certainly. An Airtouched and a Fireheart or a Soldier and a Chatelaine might not share much common ground at first glance, but Ogres almost certainly do.

                                                        I'm probably feeling a bit more wary towards the idea than many of those posting as a result, but I will try to push past that initial "I don't like change" feeling. The concept isn't inherently bad. There's no need for there to be a difference between my Ogre character being an Ogre Gristlegrinder or a Gristlegrinder Ogre. We'll see how the execution goes. Like some of the others, I'm a bit wary of the message being sent by certain Contracts being locked out by Seeming choice, given what Changeling generally stands for.

                                                        What I'm really hoping for is that Contracts actually feel like agreements, pacts, and, well, contracts. I never agreed that the 1-5 dot rating system didn't suit Contracts. I felt perfectly normal for a list of clauses to be unlocked for a participant in the Contract. So I'll be keeping an eye on how that unfolds with great interest.

Iron did cause a lot of confusion among our group when we started, and the names of the two different kinds were the issue. The big problem is that pure iron is referred to as "cold" iron. It goes through faerie defences, and it doesn't matter how exposed it's been to heat or magic. Hand-forged iron is the one you're talking about, and confusingly it is the type that actually has to be left cold and untouched by flame. It is consequently far more rare, and does aggravated to the True Fae.

                                                        So yeah, Cold Iron can be heated. Hand-forged needs to be kept cold. One of the worst and most confusing choices of terminology, but I don't feel it needs much work as a concept beyond a new set of names.
