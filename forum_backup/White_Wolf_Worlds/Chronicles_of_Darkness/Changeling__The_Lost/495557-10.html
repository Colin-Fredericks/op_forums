                    Originally posted by John Snead View Post
                    [] I'm the author of the kiths David posted here, and I was initially a little worried about Artist being too weak, but the point of the kith is basically being an inhumanly good artist, and the bonus for spending Glamour, + needing only 3 successes for an Exceptional Success + creating Conditions accomplish this pretty darn well.
                    First off, awesome work on these kiths! Love 'em.

                    I see where you guys are coming from with the Artist Kith now that I'm taking into account how Exceptional Successes generate Conditions/Beats in the new edition. However, I kind of wish that the Kith was more about creating inhumanly good art, instead of being an inhumanly good artist. As is, they are exceptional artists who can produce work much faster than usual. Presumably, they would also make really good art more consistently, though I don't think the rules explicitly state that. The art they create, however, is basically the same as that created by human artists. I think it would be awesome if, instead or in addition, they could make supernatural art - a play that drives people insane like the King in Yellow, hyper realistic paintings that instill deep horror in the viewer like in Pickman's Model, etc. I've been re-reading the Conditions section in GMC, and I'm thinking that they could possibly do this, to some extent, under the current rules. It says "complex behaviors" can cause Conditions; the example given is a well planned heist causing a police chief to be overwhelmed. So, conceivably, a well-executed work of art could instill Shaken (or something else appropriate) in viewers even though it's not explicitly stated in the rules. Would that be a correct reading of the rules? (I guess this would mean that even mortal artist's could do this; it would just be harder for them.)

                    One final argument for adding a little something extra to the Artist's blessing:

                    Now that I'm looking at the Artist a little differently, I compared it to other Kiths that are geared toward Exceptional Successes. The two that stood out were the Illes and the Palewraith.

                    The Palewraith also needs only 3 for an Exceptional Success. It's true that they only gain that benefit under certain conditions, but those conditions are pretty easy to achieve. The Artist can spend Glamour for +3, yes, but Stealth is of much greater utility than making art in most games and is often rolled several times a scene when it's called for, whereas making art is an extended roll that could take minutes or even hours per roll and is normally relegated to pauses in the action. So, the Palewraith is arguably going to be getting a similar number of exceptional successes (and Conditions/Beats), or possibly more, give the greater frequency of their affected rolls. And, in addition to this, the Palewraith also gets a very useful combat ability (anything that gives you Defense against Firearms is pure gold).

                    The Illes is actually even better at getting Exceptional Successes AND is focused in an area that will come up much more than creating art. Think about how often characters fast talk, seduce, entertain, and butter up NPCs in an average game. Nearly every game session will have a social scene (or several). Sure, the bonus is conditional, but the conditions aren't that hard to meet, and it can apply to a broad range of skills and applications. And when benefiting form the +4 and spending Glamour so that they only need 3 for an Exceptional Success, the Illes is MORE likely to gain an Exceptional Success than an Artist (assuming the same unmodified dice pool) because of the higher bonus to the rolls.

                    So there are two Kiths with similar or even better potential for Exceptional Successes who also have abilities that are arguably more useful in an average story. And one of them also gets a sweet combat ability.

                                                    This was from a while ago, but hey, touching back on it.
                                                    Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
                                                    As of now, we don't have anything like [an extra specialty/dot]. If people are interested in seeing it, I'm not against the idea. I don't think I'd do a Specialty, though. I've always found adding Specialties to be really superficial. It's typically a Specialty the character concept should have anyway, so why not just add a general Specialty? Like with Hunter, you get a Specialty in an Asset Skill at creation. But like, I don't think I've ever seen a character NOT get a Specialty in those Skills without the designation of Asset Skills, so it's functionally identical to just adding an additional Specialty.

                                                    I considered a Resistance or Finesse Attribute. Not sure though. Not given it much thought.
                                                    Oh, I'm basically in agreement that adding an extra Specialty really is pretty superficial, it's just a little something I've kinda liked for flavor reasons. I find the behavior you've seen interesting, as I tended to use it for little incidental changes the character experienced. Say, a Changeling gets out of the Hedge with hooves or horns for a Brawl specialty involving kicks or headbutts. I just find it fun to give those sorts of things some kind of mechanical reflection, should the character's Kith not cover them, even if they're not critical to the character concept. They don't really need anything, of course, but sometimes a Specialty is fun for a minor dash of flavor. By contrast, welcome as a Resistance/Finesse Attribute might be, it feels like it goes too large to scratch this (incredibly minor) itch. It's not like regular Specialties are exactly pricy, so it's hardly a big deal one way or the other.

                                                    That said, you're right that there do have to be more interesting and substantive ways to reflect that sort of thing. At the least, taking a Merit or two and refluffing to suit the Mien would be more substantive and satisfying than a Specialty. Not necessarily arguing in favor of handing out a free Merit instead or anything, just musing to myself. That said, this does make me wonder. Are there plans or drafts for Changeling-focused Merits in the book that could be useful for reflecting those relatively incidental bits and pieces?
