              I'd recommend refining the concept of the patrons further to actually get a symbolset going, much like the Directional Courts draw symbolism from the Four Auspicious Beasts or the Leafless Tree's Bargain with Retaliation manifests in ways appropriate to the frontier justice it's built on. Colors are incredibly broad as symbolism to the point where most of the time you see it in cultural symbolism it's just the symbol for a different referent like Death or Marriage or The Sea.

              Depending on what scale of involvement "takes place on a newly settled planet" entails (i.e. whether the whole story takes place in different parts of the same planet for a novel setting or is instead one locale among many in a spacefaring travelogue), it may be prudent to consider how you want the distribution of courts to go — a setup where almost every freehold on the planet has all seven courts is going to be very different from one where each court is more or less geographically associated and the courts in a given freehold are determined by territorial overlap and diplomacy, which in turn is different from a unified body of changelings whose courtier are assigned to locations based on theme/mood/need/whim.

              It's also worth asking what "sci fi campaign involving Changeling" means for the larger context of adapting the base material — is this still a situation where changelings are hidden monsters coming together to share strategies for coping with the fallout of their durances, is it a more broadly applied use of Changeling's material to make weird alien cultures, or something else?
