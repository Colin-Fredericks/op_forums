                        2.0 may introduce the idea of Seemings being how one literally changed themselves to get out of Arcadia. This could allow them to control dreams, along with them probably having similar possible magical links to humans. Perhaps they require Loyalists or Privateers to get the necessary links?

                            You know, one thing that I really loved from Dancers in the Dusk were the Goblin Miners-because they emphasized and at the same time completely crashed the cosmology of Changeling. For those that don't have that, the Goblin Miners were Hobgoblins that began digging in the Hedge and dug their way into dreams within the Skein, where they would harvest dreamstones, take them back, and sell them.

                            Keep this in mind.

                            The biggest reason presented in the first corebook for traipsing the dreamscape was because that was where the True Fae worked first, conventionally. I don't think it was ever squared away if they were Skein-walking or doing the same sort of stuff the Lost were doing, but either way, it was the frontlines, the borders where the True Fae were first held off. They would go and seed the dreams, poisoning them, haunting them, and manipulating them-to the point of controlling them in their sleep.

                            See a connection?

                            You want me to functionally connect them all together(Hedge, Arcadia, Dreams?), can't help you, but I like the idea that the presence of the fae warps dreams. If you don't watch that stuff, the fae starts leaking out of dreams into reality.

                            If you run along with the idea I presented in the Inspiration from Demon thread(Which David might not be), this also creates a terrifying point-if a changeling doesn't master their own dreams, handle the fear in their head, then it will manifest and become real. Changelings in the old edition were masters of their own dreams-I like them being adroit with it, but perfect control? Nah-some of the first dream adventures should be in motley mate's heads.

                            Compounding the problem in the last one, If changelings warp the dreams of people around them, it seems fair that it can go both ways. A changeling is connected to the dreamscape in ways others aren't, so a changeling is prone to having other dreams mix with theirs. The thing that makes them adroit with it also leaves them vulnerable. It's a thing you have to watch-constantly.

                            Feeling the paranoia build? See the shadows grows? Worry about the glint of white you see in them? How big was it? How many were there? Were you only looking at that shadow? You're so sure something just moved behind you, but you see the smiles there.

                            When does paranoia become a real threat? When do real threats become just paranoia?

                            Hope you have the Clarity to see the answers. It's a long way home.

                                        Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                        So: in 1e, you’ve got this dynamic where a human got abducted by a Keeper and dragged through the Hedge into Arcadia, where he spent some time being toyed with by his Keeper before escaping back to the mortal world… where he discovers he can enter peoples dreams??? How the heck does that fit in?

                                        So, a bit of brainstorming: how would you change things in 2.0 so that oneiromancy is less of an “out of left field” thing, and feels more like an integral part of what the game is about?
                                        It is an unavoidable and also tantalizing theme in the WOD that the universe gets processed into a different story by thinking slivers of the known world. So you have Mages, Changelings, Vampires, that all can tell you exactly how the world works (and how everyone else has really messed up the story.) Even then, you can split the story again between all Vampires into the ways "things work best".

                                        Gamers then have a heck of a time trying to resolve all the inherent conflicts. It really cannot be resolved. I think that's the point.

                                        All that said, the Dreamscapes offer plenty of mystery and not much to hang a method upon. Likewise the Keepers are mysterious and most of those who play Changeling seem to like it that way.

                                        I think, if you want to retain the 'Lost' theme as the central focus of Changeling, you are doing well to retain the Mystery of the abduction/durance/nightmare of what has happened to your character. Which is not to say that "out of left field" is there for its own sake. However, part of horror and trials and really being Lost is that what has happened to you does not make sense. It is not fair. It is madness.

                                        Wasn't the world supposed to be fair? Was science and civilization telling us life was fair?

                                        Dreams/Nightmares are some central Mystery to the Keepers themselves.

                                        I suggest that you take Dreams/Nightmares and imagine the earliest being we would call "person". Before civilization may we assume there were Dreams.

                                        When mortals were hunted by beasts and lightning and hunger, there were Dreams.
                                        And when miracles saved 'persons' from the Threats, there were miracles and guardians.
                                        And when 'persons' slept in huddles or caves, they dreamed again of Threats and Guardians.

                                        So I think Dreams are primary to the human condition, yet something about Dreams has changed enough that Keepers are Fiddling with the mix. Looking to 'engineer a better Dreamscape' perhaps (like science is trying to engineer better food, better medicine? only use the analogy if you like it otherwise, meh.)

                                        The Lost become attuned to Dreams as they are tampered with. And now can see Dreams like an additional sense. Actually being canny at Dreamwork is another matter.



                                        Arref

                                                I feel late to the party, and I'm not going to let that stop me.


                                                Lets say you're an abuse survivor. Your tormenter is behind bars and you've moved to another country. You've escaped, you're safe but you can't shake this little nagging feeling.

                                                The danger isn't real, but you feel afraid anyway.

                                                In short, you're having trouble telling the what's real.

                                                In Changeling: the Lost, the inability to tell what's real is called Clarity. I don't think I need a citation to claim that it's an important part of the game.


                                                So: A Changeling can't tell what's real and what's not real. Dreams aren't real, but Changeling's can't tell that. To the lost Dreams are real, and that is why it's a universal power for all Changelings.

                                                But where as the mortal abuse survivor is just factually wrong to feel threatened, it's not so simple for faeries with actual supernatural powers. Dreams are real to them; if you prick them with a dream thorn do they not bleed? If you feed them with dream food they are nourished.
