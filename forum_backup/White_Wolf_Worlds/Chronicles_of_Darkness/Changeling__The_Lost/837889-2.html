                    This had me laughing for quite a while, since I could see this being a genuine Domain from a particularly strange Keeper.


                    Originally posted by MCN View Post
                    My personal restriction is that it can be anything that's present in reality and somehow observable with the naked eye. So no invisible forces like sound and magnetism, sorry. That said, I've allowed elementalists to control sound through Element (wind), including the point of creating full symphonies with the right powers. Magnetism I flat out banned, due to the fact that only iron-derived substances are magnetic in any way, and I don't like the idea of any Contract working on iron. Same reason I don't allow Element (blood). There's actually a bit of an odd relationship with vampires in my setting because of the iron element within blood having an unpredictable effect on fae magic.

                    Some interesting elements I've used - Food (edible carbon-based non-living material), bone, silk (spiders!), feathers, polution and poison, radioactive isotopes.
                    The reason I pondered on Sound was because I'd thought of a Minstrel/Metalskin Changeling, who would sing/scream into her hands to produce an appropriate source for her contract to work from, and also giving her a thematic element to work with (it'd also make for a killer Black Canary cosplay - complete with Canary Cry). Still, I appreciate where you're coming from, particularly with magnetism...although I have to wonder how radioactive isotopes, being something that's far too small to see, could be a viable and wieldable thing (not to mention balanced, given how radiation tends to cause things to die). Wind being used to make sound is a nice alternative, however - perhaps it could be the power of Breath?

                    Edit: I should note I don't know the particulars of radioactive isotopes, so it may not be that unfeasible. Just throwing in my two cents.

                            Ah, now I understand. I thought you meant radiation in its minutia, the atoms and neutrons and so forth, rather than radioactive objects. That makes complete sense now, and though I probably wouldn't be too fond of running radiation as a mere poison, it works well enough that I could get behind it (if nothing else, it makes walking through nuclear fallout more bearable). Heck, with that kind of reasoning you could probably run a game of Fallout: Changeling edition and come away with some interesting elements.

                            I never got to play Changeling 1E, since the GM found his muse was leaning far more towards Geist and we transitioned to that instead. However, given what I've read of 2E (and admittedly 1E) my understanding of "Element" is a force in the world that exists or could exist naturally but doesn't have a great deal of life behind it. They are forces that are about the world and though they can be called upon for assistance, that same wielding is used as a defense as well. Ergo, while I wouldn't consider Animals wouldn't to be an element, things like Dust, Water, Sound, Light, Stone and even something as insubstantial as Shadow would be viable, as they are primal forces to be wielded and protected against; some of them would likely come up more than others (I can't think of a mundane source of "darkness" that a Cloak could provide immunity to) but in this crazy World of Darkness, there's likely some magical source that any of these would be handy against.

                            I see what you mean regarding the powers, as you don't want to make players investing in Cloak of Elements (Wind), only to find that they are faced with a tornado which goes above a mere "wind". However, by that same logic I feel that some restraint should be given to what these powers would affect. If a Changeling had Cloak of Elements (Wind) and they stood in a gale, they would probably come out of it without any damage to their hearing, even if a normal person may have a slight ringing in their ears; however, if that same Changeling were to use that Cloak of Elements (Wind) behind a jetliner while it was taking off, they wouldn't be knocked down by the air currents the engines generate, but the sound and heat coming from them wouldn't be protected against, as it's not the wind ripping by them that is causing the damage but something made by the engines themselves. Likewise, having a Cloak of Elements (Sound) would protect the Changeling from going deaf in a similar situation or being thrown around by a sonic wave, but they'd be thrown quite a ways by air currents.

                            As of now, I've not seen a contract that allows a Changeling to become an element, which may become problematic with certain elements such as, well, Sound, but in that case I'd apply comic book logic to it; if it doesn't make the user invisible, it should make them visible but patterned after their element. In the case of sound, lots of ripples, transparent, echoey when speaking, all that jazz (if you'll pardon the pun). Communion I can see working, given Elements called upon - after all, a spirit of Sound would know what someone was saying, and how loud or quiet their steps were, but if it were to be asked what color something was or a design on something they carried, they'd be out of luck, since such things don't leave anything to sound, not even to a stretch like echolocaton. I do appreciate why you put the restriction in place though, and I applaud your way of working around the issue. It hope you guys had a good time in that session.

                            Apologies for the rambliness of this post. :-p

In 1e, the Cloak had a specific catch that changelings could still be hurt by the elements if it was used as a weapon. A jet engine isn't a weapon, but a concussive grenade is. 2e works differently - the Cloak of the Elements flat out would protect you against any instances of "wind" damage so long as its not magical. Of course, we get into some question about Task Force Valkyrie and their pseudo-scientific equipment being potentially magical, but that's another matter.

                                Heat would still be a problem, unless we start talking Element (fire).

                                Actually, they hid it as the Elemental subset of Phantom Glory - this power is a combination of Elements 5 and Separation 5 from the old game. Not to mention that you don't have to "call" the element, or shape it. Sound does fit nicer in the new power set than the old one.

                                Where Sound in the new one runs into issues is using Control Elements (ooo). You inflict one of five Environmental Tilts - Blizzard, Earthquake, Flooded, Heavy Winds or Inferno (and Blinded with the Darkling option for Shadow / Light used alongside other elements). I'm personally looking at Heavy Winds and wondering if its appropriate? To quote from Beast pdf on Heavy Winds-

                                "Heavy winds are loud, so characters suffer a −3 modifier to aural Perception rolls. Also the wind inflicts a penalty to all Physical rolls when out in the winds — including Drive rolls. Grade the wind from one to five — one is tropical storm level (around 40 MPH), three is hurricane level (around 80 MPH), and five is tornado level (150+ MPH). This is the penalty applied to Physical dice rolls. Characters outside in the maelstrom take damage from flying debris, taking bashing damage each turn equal to the wind’s rating. Characters can make a reflexive Dexterity + Athletics roll to avoid damage."

                                The Perception roll check makes sense, but I'd almost want to go for Deafen instead. The damage and penalty also makes sense, kind of. Its not how I'd like to see it modeled, but its workable, I suppose. Though, I think I'd rather use Murkblur - that Blinds, Deafens, and can Stun and send someone Insensate.

                                EDIT - the full list of Environmental Tilts that I see across all games are Ice, Heavy Winds, Heavy Rains, Flooded, Extreme Heat, Extreme Cold, Earthquake, Drowning, Blizard, and Avalanch. I'll add in Blind, Deafen, and Poisoned/Sick since they're the form of some fairly popular elements.

                                Oddly enough, there's no option for poison in Contracts, though Brewer and Venombite kiths have some. Ice shows up in Flickering Hours, and we talked about Murkblur already, which is possibly appropriate for sound deafening/disorienting people. Extreme Heat/Cold is part of Night's Subtle Distractions.

                                I see nothing for Avalanch, Drowning or Heavy Rains, which is understandable. Avalanches are hard to pull off in most cities, Drowning really shoudl be personal as the result of Flooded, and Heavy Rains mostly served to create the Flooded, Heavy Winds, and Extreme Cold Tilts.

                                Most other Elemental themed abilities in the list tended to be plant based or artifice based (woodblood/manikin). Even making a fetch-like being!
