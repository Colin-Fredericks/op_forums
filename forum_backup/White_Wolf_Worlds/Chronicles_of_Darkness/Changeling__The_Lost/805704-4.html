    Originally posted by Wildfire
    A Huntsman is always going to track down the player if the ST wants it. Kill the Loyalist, or Kill the Huntsman? They just keep coming back if the ST wants it. It's just not terribly different than what already exists.
    Wildfire, you have my deepest sympathies. I, too have had really bad storytellers that ran every villain they threw at us as an unstoppable juggernauts devoid of any humanizing weaknesses. I know what that feels like and I want to let you know that isn't actually how the game should be run. See game mechanics and defining how things work in a setting builds a consistent frame work for a story that is more than just the pre- magna carta serfdom under a tyrannical Storyteller. See role playing isn't just a game of imaginative pretend and storytelling we play with our friends, but it has rules and mechanics which define which characters should be able to do. Just because storytellers you've been exposed to feel free to play changeling antagonists like a preternatural terminator doesn't mean that's what loyalists actually are. Loyalists are fundamentally human characters, with human weaknesses and should if they are portrayed correctly as the fully fleshed out characters they deserve to be, will have these weaknesses put on display, for a variety of reason ranging from making them sympathetic to make them even more contemptible for their pettiness.

                As someone who, for better or worse, never played or ST'd CtL, but has read the Core and parts of the supplements, I like these guys. The problem I saw, from my admittedly inexperienced perspective, with using True-Fae-Outside-Of-Arcadia was that to keep them legendary threats you had to make them serious monsters and if a Changeling actually managed to kill her Keeper in "meat space" then... that was it! You are free now! Huntsmen give the possibility of an eternal chase while allowing even the average Changeling a serious chance at fighting back. At the same time, the Gentry are kept as ominous threats of immense power behind the curtain. After all, you only kill the Evil Queen at the very end of the tale, right?

                As for the Loyalist vs. Huntsmen discussion, I am with Pale_Crusader. Even the most deranged Loyalist is still partly human and does things out of human motivations, even if they are not admirable. That flows into how you (can) interact and deal with them, as well as the moral weight of killing one in cold blood. A Huntsmen is entirely a creature of tales and has the abilities to match their moniker.

                To be entirely honest, Huntsmen is one of the first things (except Talecrafting back in 1e) that really makes me want to play CtL.

                The only thing that turned me off slightly is the need to kill. It just seems a bit overly contrived and unnecessarily limiting. Sure, it fits a "horror story monster", but I think I would prefer if the actions they have to do earn that essential epiphany were broadened a bit to include other "enlightening" acts that still attract attention (and therefore can warn an astute Changeling that a Huntsman is around).

                    The difference between loyalists and Huntsmen is where the alien factor comes in-these aren't humans turned fae after you, these are fae turned human after you, and that makes a subtle but telling difference.

                    While both loyalists and Huntsmen play a sort of spy game, the Huntsmen plays from the outside, always, while a loyalist can get inside. Loyalists can be persuaded and changed, but Huntsman can't. A Huntsman can't pretend to be a courtier the way a loyalist can. On the other hand, the loyalist can't offer miracles the way a Huntsman does. And so on and so forth.

                    But the biggest difference is intimacy, deranged closeness to you. Thanks to the summoning ritual of the Huntsmen(which you'll see later), Huntsmen are bound to one changeling-they can grab multiple, but rather like fetches, their have particular power over one changeling-but more importantly, they have connection to one changeling over others. Switching over to a more IC presentation, they are made for you. If a Huntsman prefers winning by seduction, then that Huntsman is sure to be alluring to you, carrying the touches of everyone you've ever found attractive.

                    They've got you on their mind, and if they understand anyone in particular, it is you.

                    If a fetch is a You Minus, the Huntsmen is a Anti-You, or a You Plus*.

                    And that's something no loyalist can pull off so neatly. Keep in mind, this is a game of psychological horror.

                    *Though I tend to reserve You Pluses for the True Fae.

                    EDIT: A few things that I like about "kill for mortal form":

                    1) It adds stakes. If you only temporarily banish the Huntsmen, that's another corpse because of you. If it keeps up, how many people will die because of your failures? Maybe you should turn yourself to them, to stop the killing. Maybe that matters to you. Maybe it doesn't, since that means staying here, safe. Maybe you should worry about that.

                    2)Call me a sucker for "face of the victims" type antagonists, you'd be right. Gives variety and flavor.
