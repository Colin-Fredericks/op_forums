                    Lots of games and GMs have their strengths. I have had GMs that run incredibly compelling narratives. Stories good enough to be a book. I've played under GM's who could run a fight scene with the best pacing and juuuuust the right amount of tension. I've had a GM who could run an awesome dungeon crawl you looked forward to planning, and a GM who put you though the wringer and you did not care because it was so awesome. My strength is scenarios, characters, relationships. My stories are not terrible, but nothing to write home about. What makes them work, I think, are the NPCs, their complexity, their and both where they are unpredictable and predictable. I love it when players start putting together stuff about an NPC's past, or what their behavior may mean. Honestly, if I could get togather with someone who does plot well, I could do a lot with characters or relationships and we could get a hell of a book out.

                    Of course, it is not just me. My players are great. They play all sorts of characters. They communicate what they want. They react to a complex tapestry of NPCs and relationships by giving their characters those themselves. No matter how good a GM I was my stories would not be so good without them. My players are awesome.

                            Session XLII

                            Gawain’s Memories

                            June 17th, morning

                            The Spring Court’s wake up power is the best, really. It’s time for Highsummer festivities and despite not sleeping any last night, I’m still, you know, raring to go. It helps that I think we really did some good last night. I mean, even just holding vigil with Joan would have been doing good, but I think we really helped.

                            See, De la Roche caught us all before the show yesterday, to tell us that Andromeda had, you know, withdrawn again. Except that it wasn’t really again, cause it wasn’t like it was before, she was super emotional, just not, you know, interacting with people. Unless Joan started crying, then Andromeda would get upset.

                            Moon checked her out and said it was trauma from what happened the night before at the Dream House. Spring courtiers were keeping an eye on her to make sure her desire to run away to her Keeper (the last place where no one betrayed her) didn’t, you know, overwhelm her. Joan was standing vigil to make sure she didn’t make a break for it or try to hurt herself and Monica sat up with her. Calamity went to check on her during the first part of the show too. Apparently Andromeda wanted to talk to her, but not more than she wanted to stay hidden in her own mind, same as she did with Joan. So Calamity decided to try and craft a dream for her, with my help.

                            We, me and Calamity and Jacob for moral support, went back after the show. She wasn’t asleep yet, but I came prepared with a deck of cards. I managed to cajole Joan into playing by overruling Mary Helene’s suggestion of strip poker. I hope it helped her relax some, a t least a little. She certainly wasn’t gonna get actual, you know, rest.

                            The dream weaving went awesomely. We were going for subtle, you know, taking the already existing elements and making them more here than Mothershippy, and making Joan more like she was than what she seemed to be to Andromeda at the moment. She didn’t kick us out which we were afraid of - she did Moon and Chalcedony. But Calamity was point and apparently was welcome enough.

                            And it worked! When she woke, Joan was right there and Andromeda actually talked to her! Mary Helene scanned her and found she really did, you know, seem to be better, and Calamity got to talk with her some and everything. Joan said they repledged their love and support for each other and might even show up at Highsummer! I really hope they do.

I almost forgot to - I was just talking to Desiree to make sure she was doing alright and she mentioned Becky not liking her, so I got reminded. She’d wanted to talk with Princess Joli that day cause she was feeling insecure and upset, with Felicity being kinda a rival now, and Queen Charlotte being jealous of her and all, you know? I told her that maybe those two problems canceled each other out a little - if Queen Charlotte is only jealous of Desiree and not Felicity, maybe she knows Felicity isn’t really a rival, right? I also promised to ask Calamity to talk to Queen Charlotte about it. I dunno if it helped, but I think trying to help out with the investigation did, at least as a distraction.

Becky even said earlier, according to Desiree, that she’d kill Desiree if she gave her any excuse to do so, you know, legally. She’s got a serious thing for Princess Joli, I guess.

So that kinda opened the field then closed it again, since, you know, you have to be really powerful to do that. The Warlock, Madame Spidertoes, Annika, Tommy and maybe Sidhe and Darke were the only ones we could think of. Chalcedony might, and might be hiding it, since all her blue might be a Wyrd frailty. Or maybe just an eccentricity, you know? But it did get into her contract with the Dream House even. And if it was a pre setup thing, it could have been easier to know that Becky was gonna be near Desiree than Dark Kimberly. So Chalcedony’s dislike of Desiree might be the motive...

I promised I’d hang out with Desiree some, since she was bummed that Felicity would be Princess Joli’s official date. And Joan and Andromeda might show up. And my “official date” is rocking just enough leather and a whole lot of Calamity. It’s gonna be a good day, I know it.


                            Lorelei’s Diary

                            This is necessarily going to be one of my shorter entries; I only have about half an hour before we have to head out to the Highsummer Festival. I’m already laced into my tight corset – thanks for that, Julia – and ready to head out, but some of the others are still getting prepared.

                            Okay, so a few things have happened since I last wrote in this journal. First, when D got back from work yesterday, my honey informed us all that Andromeda’s fragile mental state had not improved. In fact, she seemed to have dropped into a sort of fugue state, and refused to communicate with anyone except Joan. The Spring Court had people watching her and checking her desires every half hour or so to make sure that she wasn’t about to bolt back into the Hedge to rejoin the Mothership. And yes, according to Marie-Hélène and Chalcedony, that was a real danger. She felt betrayed and rejected by the Changelings of the Freehold, and subconsciously longed for the security and clarity of purpose she felt with her Keeper, and only her love of Joan was so far keeping her from bolting.

                            Apparently restraining her, cutting her, and slapping her the other day only added to the sense of betrayal she was already feeling. Yeah, I already feel like shit about that, so no need to dwell on it now.

                            Calamity and Gawain offered to head over to Joan’s place to see if they could help, and Jacob and Marguerite went with them for moral support. Gawain wanted to try a little dream therapy, and Calamity felt that the bond she shares with Andromeda might help the poor girl to open up a bit. According to their reports the next morning, there are hopeful signs. Calamity has almost convinced Joan to let the Winter Court’s therapists speak with Andromeda… and considering how well they seemed to have worked their magic on Calamity herself, I can only see that as a good thing. Gawain crafted a dream for Andromeda that should help her to see things a little more clearly. He didn’t go into details about the dream itself, but I think he cast Joan as a protector rather than a restrictive element.

                            God, I hope she feels better soon. If nothing else, I feel horrible about that little slap the other night. I lost control for a second, and made things worse for the poor girl. I’d really like to apologize to her, when she’s ready to hear it. That’s the main reason I didn’t offer to go with Calamity and Gawain last night, of course… I was afraid that the sight of me would just make things worse after my outburst.

                            Of course, we didn’t exactly have a quiet night here at the Dream House, either. The show went well enough, though it wasn’t one of our better nights. Many of our regulars were taking it easy due to tonight’s big production, and I felt that my attention would be better served working on the play than planning and rehearsing new numbers for the Dream House. The gate was still pretty good, we sold a fair number of drinks, and people seemed to enjoy themselves… which I suppose is the only yardstick that matters when measuring our club’s success.

                            White Maria showed up to watch the show. She’s been coming around a lot more lately – not that she’s ever been a stranger to our club – and I can only assume that it has something to do with Insincere Marguerite’s continued attempts to seduce her. Well, those attempts bore fruit tonight after Marguerite got back from Joan’s place. D was… not pleased, to say the least, and while we were socializing with Moon and Marie-Hélène (who was not on Andromeda Watch at the time), she turned her back pointedly on our French friend and tried her best to pretend that she couldn’t see what was happening across the room.

                            I tried to take her mind off our paramour with idle conversation and copious booze, but in the end I just took her hand and led her up to our boudoir for a little lovin’. Before we got to anything physical, however, she turned very serious and said she wanted to talk to me. I admit to a little trepidation, but asked what was on her mind.

                            “Lori,” she began softly, “I’ve seen what’s going on around the Freehold. Relationships are breaking down all over the place. Joan and Andromeda are having trouble, Annika and Kimberly are fighting, Monica and Haley split. It’s like a goddamn epidemic.”

                            I smiled in what I hoped was a reassuring way. “Nothing like that is going to happen to us, sweetie,” I said softly.

                            She smiled weakly. “I know. I’m not really worried about it. Hell, it’s probably just the turn of the seasons. Everyone hooks up in Spring, when Desire is heavy in the air. And then Summer comes, and Anger takes its place… it’s the natural cycle. But… you mean so much to me, Lori. I don’t want to risk anything like that happening to us. So this is it. I’m giving you carte blanche. I know my previous relationship with Spidertoes has been a… touchy subject, but I want you to know that you can ask me anything, and I’ll answer it as truthfully as I can.”

                            I was blown away. D is usually such a private person. I know she’s opened up to me more than she does to pretty much anyone else, but this was on a whole new level. I confess that for a few minutes my mind was blank. I couldn’t think of anything I wanted to know. I’ve always accepted that her relationship with Madame Spidertoes was a huge part of D’s past. It was very intense. And anything that means that much to you is bound to leave its mark.

                            “Just answer me one thing,” I said softly. “Did she ever hurt you?”

                            D smiled, reached out, and stroked my hair gently. “Not intentionally,” she said, and I was shocked to see a sheen of moisture in her eyes. “Not like you mean, anyway. I think we hurt each other plenty without meaning to, though.”

                            I waved that off, and told her I was satisfied; that I trust her. She nodded gratefully and took my hand as she went on. “It’s just… I want you to understand why I react the way I do when people talk about her.” She took a deep breath, and then took the plunge. “She’s old, Lori. I mean, everybody knows that, but most people don’t seem to get it. I know you understand what it feels like to be left behind by the world, but she suffered through her Durance before cars were invented. You’ve seen how hard it can be for older people to adapt new technology like cell phones, right? Well she had to go through that with light bulbs. She’s incredibly intelligent, and wise, and powerful, and she puts everything she has into this Freehold, and all she asks in return is that we put up with a few quirks and go to the occasional tea party…”

                            “…and people talk about her like she’s some sort of freak,” I finished for her softly. “I’m sorry, D. I never really thought about it like that before.” We were both quiet for a minute or two. I cracked first. “When you and I… when we first got involved, she asked to speak with me,” I said hesitantly.

                            D nodded. “I remember,” she said.

                            “It was a little scary,” I admitted. “She’s so powerful, and she still cares for you so fiercely. I think she wanted to make sure that I would treat you well. But she also offered me some advice on how to make you happy. That was very important to her. And then, later that evening, she sent me a letter to apologize for confronting me like that. It was delivered by…”

                            “Spiders,” D finished, the hint of a grin touching the corners of her mouth. “It’s kind of her thing.”

                            We both chuckled at that, and things were okay for a while. We… actually, we mostly just slept last night. It was nice; and I think it made us both feel comfortable and loved. At least I hope so.

                            Unfortunately, whatever sitcom gods watch over my life applied their sense of awkward comic timing acutely the next morning. D and I were just about to head down to sex breakfast when she stopped dead in the doorway in front of me. I leaned around her and spied the reason immediately: White Maria was emerging from Marguerite’s bedroom, looking disheveled and decidedly uncomfortable.

                            “What the fuck? ” D growled dangerously.

                            Marguerite peered out of her bedroom looking very pleased with herself indeed. “Is there a problem?” she asked innocently.

                            “No,” I said, edging my way around D and into the hall.

                            “Yes,” D said at almost the exact same instant. Anger and disbelief slipped across her face, and I thought I caught a glimpse of genuine hurt there as well. “I told you not to fuck her, Insincere Marguerite.”

                            “I don’t want to cause trouble between you,” White Maria said softly, “I’ll just go.”

                            “No,” I said firmly, placing a hand on D’s shoulder. “You’re a guest in our house, White Maria, and you’re welcome here.” I turned back to face D. “Sweetie, Marguerite is a grown woman; she can make her own decisions. We don’t have any right to tell her who she can sleep with.” D gritted her teeth, and I could tell that she planned to argue with me, so I headed her off. “Remember what you were talking about last night, hon? About how so many relationships seem to be breaking down for no good reason lately? We don’t want that to happen here, do we?”

                            She still looked inclined to argue, but I saw something click into place behind her eyes. She nodded slightly, and turned to face White Maria. “I’ve been rude,” she said grudgingly, “you can stay for breakfast if you want to.”

                            White Maria sighed. “I… I don’t want to cause problems for you,” she said to the hall at large. I’ll just go.”

                            “Stay for breakfast,” Marguerite said imperiously.

                            I decided to take a more diplomatic tack. “Please, White Maria,” I said, “we’re going to have to get past this at some point. Stick around; Gawain is an incredible cook.”

                            She looked uncertain, but nodded. “Well, if you’re sure,” she said after a moment.

                            So breakfast was tense, and D and White Maria spent most of it not looking at each other, but things could have been worse. No blood was spilt, no insults were hurled… all in all it was chilly but civil. I’ll take it.

                            After White Maria left, Gawain, Calamity, and Jacob filled us in on how things had gone with Andromeda. Gawain also mentioned a brief conversation he’d had with Desiree about Becky’s attack on Flores the other day. Desiree mentioned that she knew Becky had strong feelings of dislike for her, and she had thought at the time that Becky’s target hadn’t been Dark Kimberly, but herself. It’s no secret that Becky is jealous of the time Jolie has spent with Desiree, but she’s always been pretty good about keeping a lid on those feelings before.

                            Desiree apparently said that Becky could have been suffering the effects of ‘Bedlam.’ Something clicked from one of the textbooks I’ve been studying lately, and I blinked at Gawain. “Bethlehem Hospital in London?” I asked plaintively. “The old madhouse?”

                            The others looked at each other briefly, and I was again reminded of how little I still know about the strange world of the Lost. “It is something particularly old and powerful changelings can do,” Marguerite supplied. “They can take very strong emotions and force you to act upon them, regardless of the consequences.”

                            “But no one at the scene was powerful enough to be able to do that, as far as we know,” Gawain said.

                            “They would not have to be present,” Marguerite added. “If they saw Becky, and they knew where she was going, and they knew that Dark Kimberly or Desiree would be there, they might have simply used Bedlam to intensify her anger. It might not have shown until she saw something to be angry about, non?”

                            “So… who in the area is powerful enough to use this ‘Bedlam,’” I asked.

                            It’s quite a list. Madame Spidertoes. Tommy. Annika. Darke. Old Billy. Boss Lowjack. Captain Jack. The Warlock of Night. A few others might have the potential, but it’s hard to know for certain. Flores is probably on the cusp of gaining that power, but out of everyone there, she had the least to gain from using it. In fact, she got hurt by trying to stop the chaos from spreading.

                            One further theory was mentioned: Chalcedony. She doesn’t seem to be that powerful, but she might be acting weaker than she really is. There’s her thing about wearing blue clothes, for instance. Everything she wears is blue. I’ve seen her in the dressing room, and I do mean everything. Calamity informed us that there’s even a rider in her contract with the Dream House stipulating that she not be required to wear anything that isn’t some shade of blue. It could be what’s called a ‘frailty;’ a little personality quirk that plagues older Changelings, like Madame Spidertoes’ thing about washing blood out of clothes. If that’s the case, then there’s clearly more to our azure acquaintance than meets the eye.

                            It was at this point that someone reminded me what time it was. “Maybe we can figure something out by watching how she acts at the Highsummer Festival,” someone said, and…

                            “Shit!” I shouted. “I have to get ready!” Cliché, I know, but if I’m going to be the Dream House’s social butterfly, then I might as well take my role seriously, right?

                            I ran upstairs and started getting ready. I decided against wearing the Hedgespun dress this time. I mean, I’ve worn it for several important occasions already, and I don’t want to get the reputation of being a one-outfit girl. Plus, its deep red color contrasts with my wings and gives me a decidedly ‘Christmasy’ look… not what I want to go for at Highsummer. So I stripped down and started getting dolled up in my newest medieval finery. Unlike the peasant dress I wore last time, this was a real noblewoman’s gown, shimmery green and soft and altogether like something out of a fairy tale (if you’ll forgive the expression).

                            Unfortunately, it wasn’t the easiest outfit in the world to assemble. At some point I leaned out of my door and bellowed downstairs “can I get someone up here to help me lace up my corset, please?”

                            “Thought you’d never ask,” Julia whispered in my ear after an indecently short interval. There was a lot of inhaling, some small sadistic noises from Julia, and a few moments later I was firmly entrenched within my outfit (not sure yet how I’m going to get out of it, but I’m sure someone will volunteer to help when the time comes). I put a few finishing touches on my hair and makeup, and was ready to go.

                            I can’t wait. The Highsummer Festival sounds like a lot of fun, even if I’m not participating in any of the martial events like some of the others. D is escorting me, in a purple leather highwayman’s outfit (she’s doing the purple thing on purpose now, I just know it). And the two of us are going to meet Oleander there. That’s right, the three of us are going to spend the whole day together. I am both nervous and exhilarated at the thought.

                            And then there’s the play tonight. My directorial debut! I couldn’t be more excited! I’ll write more later to record how it all turns out.

                            Lorelei Grace

                                It hurts like a bitch when I break a shard of my glass. It's not like a damn fingernail, it's more like a tooth. But I know, no matter how many times it happens it will come back. It's fucking part of me. The glass rooted in my fucking bones... in my fucking soul.

She was lost somewhere between rage and panic. How was I supposed to know Joan hadn't told her what fucking happened while she was recovering? How was I supposed to know she was going to fly off the handle because the monarchs agreed the Table was a piece of Mother Fucking Dangerous Gentry shit that needed to be destroyed?

                                I should have seen it coming. The way she asked me about it should have warned me to be careful with my answer. I should have been cagey and deceitful until I could judge her sanity and her mood better. But no, Calamity had to go and drop the truth on her without a second fucking thought. I assumed her lady love had been straight with her. I assumed that because she seemed to be her old self that she was going to react like a gorram adult. In short, I was more fucking wrong than a Peanuts and Family Circus slash fanfic.

She started screaming like a child at a supermarket and rushed for the door. Joan was stunned and I was pretty sure from how completely batshit she'd gone that she wasn't in any condition to be loose and alone. I tried to grab her but fuck... I am MADE of fucking sharp edges and knives. I skipped Grecro Roman foreplay class. And damn that girl was nothing but a ball of thrashing and screaming fury. Hell, the gash didn't even slow her down. I'm not sure she even noticed as worked up as she was.

Jacob managed to catch her without doing more damage and Gawain just picked her up like a kid having a meltdown and carried her off to the den.
