                It seems like people recalling what the Darkling says when they quote it in this thread is wondering away from the actual wording in the Darkling write-up again:

                Originally posted by Clarity of Abandon
                There are just times when the best choice is the worst choice. Any time a Darkling is forced to step in and make a bad choice for other characters, make the choice no one else could, she gains a a sense of herself. She regain a point of Clarity for free.
                This includes but is not limited to:
                • Kidnapping a Beast to hand over to imprisonment to a Huntsmen for a Token of Reprieve to save someone else(besides yourself). (ie The Beast's worst fear)
                • Killing the serial killer instead of turning him into the police where he has a chance to escape and hurt people again.
                • Bring serious harm to someone a Fairest ordered to partner with you for the greater good of the freehold. (ie a Fairest's breaking point)
                • Tell the Ogre the ugly truth he never acknowledged about those he hurts so he can stop. (ie an Ogre's breaking point)
                • Cause a Wizened to fail a project she is working on because it'll bring ruin to her family life. (ie a Wizened's breaking point)
                • Clandestinely murder anyone's friend who is truly a burden and whom they'd be better off without. (a pretty terrifying thought)
                • Require another Darkling to trust her because she needs to be trusted to help even though the Darkling knows exactly how traumatic that is for the other Darkling. (ie a Darkling's breaking point)
                • Tell the Elemental the ugly truth about those hurt in its chaotic wake to get the Elemental to realize they have to be more considerate of others. (ie an Elemental's breaking point)
                • Originally posted by ShadowKnight1224
                  destroying a Token of Reprieve to prevent a Motley from tearing itself apart over it.


                There is a whole lot of room to hurt/upset others in the name of helping others (who may be the person you are upsetting/hurting, and sometimes it is someone else). This can be used as an antihero, an antagonist motivated by the welfare of those other than himself, or a dark protagonist. All are valid expressions of these mechanics.

                'Bad' can mean a slue of things from politically inconvenient, emotionally unsettling to others, morally wrong, tactically incorrect, or many other options. It seems important that the choice must be something that would be difficult for someone else who didn't have the Darkling's seeming would resist for some reason they feel is "right", "good", or "correct" and the darkling doesn't need to hesitate because they've already accepted in their soul that they are not "right", "good" or "correct" because their actions during their escape from their Durance.

                While other seeming can be scary withing their own context, Darklings can get away with anything (which respect towards Clarity) as long as it is for someone else. The worse they'll feel is a very very fleeting guilt or being "broken" for very brief time since gaining the clarity resolves those feelings, or even more scary they can be even more self-actualized if they didn't fail their break roll. People who don't feel bad for doing things that hurt others can be extremely frightening to those that recognize them for what they are.

                I will say characters that represent different sections of the moral spectrum will probably clump together because people of differing moral disposition tend to (but not always) dislike the company of those radically different than themselves either because are disgusted by those significantly worse than themselves, or feel judged by those significantly better..

                                            As for what I like about them, the biggest thing for me is that simply by existing they create dramatic tension. Even if I as an ST don't plan to use Huntsmen in a story, my players will always need to be prepared for the threat.                                             I don't think I would actually use Huntsman terribly often, because they're such a game-changer when they show up. But the same can be said of Strix and Idigam. The titular antagonists of the game lines' signature Chronicles are enormous plot catalysts, so you only use them when you want the story to be heavily focused on dealing with them.
