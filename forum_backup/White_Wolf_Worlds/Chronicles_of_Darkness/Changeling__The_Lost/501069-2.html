    Thank you for all your comments. I'm getting some thoughts here. I will remind you this is a very first draft draft, and clearly, I need to tighten up some of the language. So here's some thoughts and comments and questions answered. Ready? Here we go!

    1)Someone asked if the Clarity check is for when you make hard choices and people get hurt or also when you're 'job' makes you punch a dude, and they get hurt. I can clarify that. It is in fact when the damage is splash-over or accidentally. It's meant to be about collateral damage, not about 'I need to punch a guy because that guy needs to be punched.' It's meant to be, "I punched a guy because he needed to be punched, and then he died, and now his widow can't support her family any more." This plays into the idea that ever if you're a good leader (which not all Fairest are) the choices you make, even perfectly legit choices, are going to have consequence. And for the tragic Fairest, that eats at you. See, the Fairest didn't ask to be a leader, didn't ask for people to look up to them, but they kinda have to do it. And even though they generally don't 'sign up for it' so to speak, it still eats at them and slowly kills them that they fuck up even when they don't fuck up. That's the tragedy of the Fairest. And the horror. No matter what you do, even as you do your best, you can never be as perfect as everyone thinks you are, and that could just break you into a million pieces one day. If you know the play, ask yourself, what did Lear look like before he was old and established? King Lear is a place where Fairest can end up, easily, and plenty of them KNOW that, and maybe, can't do shit to stop it.

    2)I see some comments about 'horror' and 'this isn't dark enough.' Well. Horror comes in many shades and genre. Changeling the Lost is, at it's core, psychological horror. Horror in your head and your gut, not horror that jumps out of the corner and screams at you. So. Sometimes the scary is more cerebral. See above. And below probably.

    3)I see some discussion about Disney Princesses. First, full disclosure,I have STRONG opinions about Princesses. They are highly problimatic and yet incredibly important, and that they can do as much good as they do harm, culturally speaking. And actually, that's kind of the Fairest too, isn't it? They CAN do a lot good, but will they? I also saw someone saying basically 'Disney Princesses can't be Fairest because they had everything just handed to them." Actually, that's exactly why they would be Fairest. Each Fairest made a choice to step up, to stand up, to help someone, and that let them escape. But they didn't sign up for the long-haul, you understand. It's thrust on them. Imagine if you saved a guy from being hit by a car one day. Nice feelings, yay! You made a really good self sacrificing choice! Cool! Now the dude is living on your front doorstep, waiting for you to come out, asking you what to do with his life, begging you to take care of him, advise him, and worse, his friends are joining in. If you're not careful, there's going to a cult around you in no time. And just imagine how that cult is going to react when they find out that you only saved the guy because you were trying to steal his wallet. Whoopse. Higher the pedestal, the harder the fall. (You see this in the treatment of famous people ALL THE TIME!) To bring it back to the Princesses, because I can't resist them... Imagine Cinderella. She's spent most of her life now, abused by her stepmother, all of her skills involve house cleaning and management. She can mend clothes. That was good enough. Now (because she saved some mice, basically) she's a queen of an entire kingdom and married to a man she doesn't really know. A pure heart, or at least, a moment of purity put her in this position. What if she can't hack it? What if she turns around and abuses her staff the way she was abused, because she has no idea how to communicate with employees except the abuse she learned? What if the rush passes and she realizes her Prince Charming is a jerk for XYZ reason, and or she simply can't connect with him? What will the people of her fair kingdom do when she's caught in an affair with a stable hand because, frankly, she can emotionally connect with him better? What if she's asked to make an important domestic choice for the kingdom, and the result hurts everyone? Mice-workers right SOUNDED good, but it's crushed the local economy because she doesn't have the know-how to transition the kingdom from a domestic-slavery culture to something more fair?

    4)On a smaller, more intimate level, the big brother who takes the abuse so that drunk dad can't murder mom and little sister? Who ever tagged this idea? Yeah. Hard, but, yeah, that's actually the deeper undertone I was going for. If you want to take it to this level, which is smaller and harder, you look at the Fairest like this. You take the beatings so dad doesn't murder mom. Every day you ask yourself, though, would mom have left if you weren't taking the heat? Would mom and everyone leave if little sister got popped the way you do, even once? (How unfair would that be, huh?) Little sister is kind of turning into a monster, she's hurting small animals, is that your fault too? Why doesn't anyone ever fucking THANK you for the beatings you take? Would everyone be better off if you just murdered dad? Who would you be if you killed him? Maybe you should just leave, let them sort their own shit out! But. You won't do that. You can't do any of that, because they need you, they don't thank you, but they need you, and know it.

    5)Darklings and Fairest are opposite, but not enemies. In fact, they should work in a fucked up sort of harmony most of the time. In the example in number 4? Yeah. The Darkling there might be little sister. Big brother can't do the deed, the deed that needs to be done, and so, one day, Darkling little sister will creep into dad's room when he's out cold, and put something in his drink. She knows big brother may have to hate her for it, but she does it anyway. Big brother will do the right thing, probably, though, and take credit for the killing, going to prison, but the end result is actually the right one. Mom is free of dad, and little sister slips off through the cracks, and big brother gets to be the big hero. The fairytale ending, right?

    6} Someone wanted Elementals next. I can nudge that to happen next. No problem. For a taste? Have you seen the movie Party Monster? Or know the story of Michael Alig? Look it up if not. That's a place to start.

                                                        Conceptually, I feel like the Fairest write-up as-is is a little narrow - in that there aren't many provisions for a Fairest concept that doesn't involve being in a position of authority of some kind.

                                                        Would "being tested" be compelling to anyone as the thematic idea for Fairest? In fairy tales, the prince is crowned and the princess succeeds because, when they are presented with tests of their morals - for example, "did you drop this golden axe or this silver one or was it this normal one" - or their wits or their humility, they give the right answer, invariably, even where the Gentry would answer wrong. The Fairest treat their Durances, sooner or later, as a test. They prove that they're just as good as their Keepers, if not better, and thus escape by asserting that they are not beneath the Gentry, who come and go as they please, and thus should be afforded the exact same right. They inherit the crown and win the princess's hand, and thus become nobles. In the trauma-and-asserting-agency analogy, they're the ones who say "You think I'm useless? I'll show you. I won't just meet the insane expectations you set, and I won't just exceed them - I'll be better than you are, and I'll do it without borrowing a single page from your playbook." And it feels so good to be proven, but from then on, every time there's a test, you need to prove you're better than your Keeper all over again...

                                                        (Might give Clarity for pulling out all the stops to win, for the sake of proving something or accomplishing something on behalf of others or out of principle, and cause Clarity issues when you're being tested and you think of yourself, or other people think of you, as having failed.)
