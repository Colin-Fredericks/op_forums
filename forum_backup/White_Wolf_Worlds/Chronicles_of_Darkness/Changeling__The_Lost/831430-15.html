                        Okay, so let me start on the subject of Reticent itse-Actually, no, let me explain something about myself first, because I'm sure a question has cropped up in people's mind, and I want people to understand why somethings works the way I do.

                        Namely, why is it I sometimes seem like I have no idea what's going on in the new texts.

                        There's a couple of factors into this, but there are too many for me to talk about in a short deviance, so we'll hit the big one. Namely, I remember stuff better when I make the point of reading it in big chunks. If I read something small bit by small bit over time, I tend to forget each bit and it's connection to everything else, and thus it fades away. If it's all one cohesive block in my head, it sits better.

                        This is a problem when you're following stuff in an RPG project, because stuff by necessity has to come in small bit by small bit, and there's not always time to just have big sit down reads for this stuff. For relevance, I came in around the time the writers were wondering where their redlines were, which means most of the first drafts were done. I read and understood the first draft version of the game. As redlines and second drafts started making their way in, though, while I've been reading them, it's been piecemeal, so it hasn't been sitting well. The same thing happened with Beast as a playtester, which is why when we got the PDF for that, I made damn sure to read it all the way through again.

                        So, in case you were wondering, that's why.

                        NOW let's talk about Reticent.

                        First thing I want to say about Reticent is that i actually know this feeling very well, and I know the effect. After an emotional high, you basically do become effectively depressed, it sticks with you for a while. I even know some of the biological and psychological ideas and theories behind this, but that's not the point here. And to be honest, I think it's a fine consequence in and of itself. it keeps you from drinking from the same well over and over again(which is one problem I have had in games), and it also creates good consequences that's true to life without being horrific-even casually making someone feel good about themselves might ruin their week because they just aren't feeling it in bed with their spouse or some such.

                        Now, enough people have brought up the ALL WILLPOWER GONE thing, and upon reflection, yeah, sure, that makes sense. Still, I would like someone having been nommed on to have some of the effects of listlessness and depression after it comes on, like a penalty to all actions based on how much of chimp you took. But Dave has already said he's gonna look at it, so there's that. For me, it's a good baseline. Do I think there's room in supplements for exploration on consequences? Hells yes, but that's for when I get me a big ol' book of sorcery to play with. Rites of Spring 2, I want it.

                        As for the Scaling Meal sort of thing....Eh? I dunno.

                        The thing of it as it applies to Satiety is to help encourage a certain level of predacity, or rather a different kind of predacity from lower levels. Well, okay, no, it's not just that, it's the mythic scale of Beast at work, the need for an epic figure to go onto bigger and bigger challenges. Changeling doesn't quite have that sense of need of escalation-folklore, as fairy tales often are, don't quite scale up the same way a myth does. I mean, it does, but consider that while stories like the Gallant Tailor, that inestimable bastard, might have his stakes increase, but his capabilities don't range up in similar fashion, at least not in the same image as the sort of stories Beast draws from.

                        I get the desire to incentivize fae behavior from one angle, and on that I would honestly just add added success the more and more your harvest relates to your own personal myth(or put more simply ,that harvesting should idrectly relate to you and how permanent their emotional memory of you is like), or something like that, but the catch to that is I wouldn't want to mandate fae behavior for your magical gummi drop ammo, because Changeling has this weird tug of war going on with it's relationship to the fae that largely ends with someone deciding on their comfort level with the fae. Wyrd rising does enough of that as is. As is, creating the incentive should have rewards, but there should be rewards for leaving it the fuck alone too(Because Changeling can be a game about putting that thing back right where it came from or so help me, so help me, so help me, and cut, it's a musicial).

                        So, finally, i just want to reiterate that there's this pervasive sense of changelings not being all that bad as a monster as the sets go, and I get that and the appeal of it, but it's always been something that bothered me. There's plenty of horror without, but sometimes I feel like we try to sugarcoat the effects of trauma with Changeling by only paying half as much attention to the horror within. In a lot of ways, Changeling has been too clear cut and squeaky clean for being the game that it's about, and that's something that needs to change. Hell, it's in the name. As changelings move away from their trauma, they should have legitimate reason to worry about what they are changing into. I think creating a consequence for harvesting emotions is not a bad way to go as one of several starts.

                                    A large part of this has to do with the fact of my experience with the world. Like, I've mentioned the closehand second experience with the consequence of emotional manipulation, and I'll say here and now I'm not comfortable with my own personal experiences with that aspect, but I want to kind of sum up my experience with a little bit of real talk.

                                    In many ways, I have been Lost. I'm not going to claim I have a landlock on this subject, because I know for a fact many other writers actually have it way worse than I've had, but I can say I've been there. I think the most pertinent one though, is that I have been both the abused and the abuser.

                                    I'll spare you the details, just, you need to know that I have done a few heinous things in my life. And for me, the biggest horror now was not that I did it, but the fact that I felt justified in my position because of what abuse I had received. Because of shit I had gone through, because woe was me, I had no qualms wrecking other people, physically, emotionally, mentally.

                                    Lost did a lot of things for me when I finally got it, so much so that I'm not even sure I can properly grasp it, but one of the biggest things it did was consolidate a lot of feelings and experiences for me into a clear understanding of a disdain for justification and unawareness of self. I don't truck well with things like nostalgia, short-sighted self assurance, and other such things. It's ironic that I stumble around with Clarity a little, because the big thing I got was the sense of rightness in always questioning whether you're in the right place or not, and if you're not, how to fix that, or at least turn it to good use, or even beyond that, just owning it. Either don't go beating up people for kicks, or own up to the fact that you beat up people for kicks, don't justify it me, and particularly not to yourself. The one thing I dislike most is when a person loses awareness of what they're doing and who they are. Know Where And Who You Are is a big ass commandment for me, shortly followed by Don't Rely On Your Own Judgment.*

                                    And this where this conversation comes in. Yes, Changeling is a dark game. But lots of people don't treat it that way. "Bleakest game, my ass" is something I've said so many times I wouldn't be surprised if that ended up the quote they put on my future TvTropes page, so it's not like I'm not guilty of it-but the responses for Lost Second Edition have largely seemed to lean towards this crazy ass notion of Heroic Changelings fight nobly against Evil Arcadian Forces while dealing with tragic elements. We talk about the horrors of the Lost, sure, but it feels so lightly brushed that it's infuriating. I don't need a "EVERYONE IS AN EVIL MONSTER" sort of thing, but I want some teeth to this.

                                    On a semi-related tangent, I want it on record that while I like the Becoming Nightmare mechanic, I also kind of hate it, particularly in relation to abused-abuser dialogue, because that has become our main way of talking about that part of the game....and I don't think that's actually what it's talking about, but nevermind that, the bigger problem is that by lumping all our thoughts of that dialogue onto that mechanic, we ignore the small, messy ways that changelings run the risk, the ones that actually matter. The broad picture of a changeling becoming a True Fae is nowhere near as terrifying to me as the moment when a changeling decides to make a person fall into lust with someone else to fill their battery without considering the consequence of how that makes them, in some ways, as bad as their Keeper. The fact that we're having this conversation shows how people sort of brush aside the notion.

                                    I'm all for subtlety. I really, really like subtlety. But if something is so subtle that I feel like it misses the point, as even my own experiences with Changeling First's mechanics have yielded, then I don't object to making it more overt. And following the fan community from it's inception, I have no problem saying that we lose sight of a lot of important shit(Hell, even I did for a while), and in the course of updating this game, it pays to bring it to light.

                                    Games are art. Art is about communication. If the message isn't strongly getting through, it's time to consider how you sell your message.

                                    *Overtly Rely, I should say

                                    PS: Inappropriate to this entire conversation, would you believe me if I told you my second favorite gameline after the Chronicles of Darkness is Golden Sky Stories?
                                    Edit: Semi-related to the inappropriate conversation as well as this, would you also believe me if I told you slice of life was my favorite mode of play?

                                        As an aside from the tone discussion that's going on above, I think I'll ask what's potentially another aspect : Do people actually enjoy playing through "feeding" scenes at their tables?                                             It's hard for me to talk about this due to personal reasons, but part of the reason I was really against the idea of changelings as predators or having really obvious dangerous and monstrous facets, was because I find the idea a bit dehumanising towards victims. In my Changeling stories, I don't want to constantly deal with the way in which the changelings are similar to their Keepers. I don't want my themes of hope and growth and healing to be repeatedly derailed or made null by that sort of reminder. I feel that should be an optional issue to explore. For me, Changeling is about hope prevailing through despair and the guiding light in the darkness, same reason I love Geist and Promethean.

                                            The fact that I feel like I have to apologise for liking things that way IMO speaks volumes of the way I perceive the general community to be inclined towards.

                                            I want to see changelings overcome their flaws, conquer their fears, stand up to their Keepers, face the darkness and emerge scarred but victorious. I don't want those victories to be made hollow or Pyrrhic.

                                            Consequences are fine. But IMO the game should approach that aspect from a different angle. Instead of making it inescapable, intrinsic to the changeling condition, it should be a temptation. When you are a victim that lashes out or abuses others, you do it for a reason. It's a way for you, the disempowered, to empower yourself. It's a way for you to stop feeling helpless and scared. If I were to be charged with implementing that aspect into CtL 2e, I'd do it in the form of optional actions that give out rewards. Maybe have a mechanic where you can invoke some aspect of the Wyrd when performing an action (maybe it's doing an "extra-strong harvesting", maybe it's invoking the power of Tales to set up a Wyrd-driven form of narrative mind-control to get them to act the way you want them to) against someone you feel strongly towards. Someone you truly hate, or are terrified of. Maybe someone you're madly in love with but doesn't want you. That way, the changeling is tempted to empower herself by doing something awful to another (not just mortals, but other changelings as well). It's not mandatory, but it's tempting. It's empowering. It gets you what you want... at a price. It sets you up perfectly for a moment of realisation about your own capability for victimisation.

                                                            My thoughts on reticent, in brief: it makes sense that if a changeling feeds on someone's emotions, that person would be left emotionally drained, or outright emotionless. Just like when a vampire feeds on someone's blood, that person is left drained of blood or exsanguinated. Just like when a human feeds on animal or plant matter, that animal or plant is left physically diminished or completely gone. You can't really consume something without leaving an absence of that thing where it used to be. I'm ok with Reticent as it is, though I wouldn't mind if it was tweaked. But I like the idea that harvesting emotions leaves an absence of emotion in the person who was harvested, and I hope that concept remains, what ever form it may take mechanically.
