                        Originally posted by OracleOnline View Post
                        Not to derail the thread too much but I do have a character idea and was curious if you knew what the new kith "helldiver" is?
                        It's a kith from the 2e core book; basically, Helldivers are Changelings who can become invisible and intangible (known as Diving) by spending a glamour, and if they observe a gate to another realm being opened they can open it with a glamour like it was a hedgegate. However, to stop Diving they need to spend another glamour, and unless they run into another Helldiver or a Changeling using a specific contract they can't interact with anyone while Diving. Also, unless they find a way of breaking it, each Helldiver has a silver chain from their back to their Keeper that they can be pulled with. Helldiver chain is, itself, an example token.

                        Also, since you're not quite up to date on what Kiths are like now, Kiths now grant two powers; one is the Kith Blessing, which grants an exceptional success on 3 successes for a particular skill in a particular circumstance (Artists get it when using a Crafts specialty; Helldivers get it by using Larceny in the Hedge), and if you make your own Kith then the circumstance should be more specific depending on how often a die pool gets used (Brawl or Firearms, for example, are skills that tend to be used a lot when they come up). The second part is Trickery, which is (usually) an ability that is activated by spending a Glamour and rolling a particular die pool; Trickery has a unique name unto itself, but is officially known as, well, Trickery.

                        This change in Kiths has likely been why Dual Kith has kind of vanished from the core book as of now. The game is a lot more friendly for custom content, and given how Kiths have two powers to them, a player can now make a custom Kith that has what they want much easier than if they were using two pre-made Kiths.

                                Originally posted by OracleOnline View Post
                                So can Helldivers enter the Shadow or Twilight/ the Underworld? Does being invisible and intangible mean nothing from these realms can harm them? With this chain attached to them do they run the risk of their keeper dragging them back at any time?

                                If duel kithing has disappeared from the core rulebook, why do so many character concepts take the duel kith merit still...do you gain two blessing/trickeries?

                                Anywho, thanks for the informative response.
                                In their durance, yes, they could be pulled back at any time. Once the chain is broken though, they are as free as any Changeling. And no, Diving does not make them any safer than other beings who can do similar tricks by going into Twilight; they see, are seen and interact with other beings that are ephemeral, and if the realm has effectively no Twilight (such as the Underworld), then they are material as well. They also need to eat and sleep like normal, Diving or not.

                                Dual Kith is so prominent here because most of the characters here were from before the Kickstarter materials were revealed, and not everyone has access to them. The kickstarter materials, which have been said as being around 90% (or more) ready, do not have the merit, so if you wanted to take it you'd need to effectively balance a character having two Trickeries and two Kith Blessings yourself. That's why I brought up the idea that the benefit of the current system is that everyone has two qualities, without the need to take two Kiths. If you want your character to be able to do something, discuss with the GM and create what you both agree works.

                                        So can Helldivers enter the Shadow or Twilight/ the Underworld?
                                        With access to a suitable entrance, yes. They have to be Diving to do it, but it otherwise works the same as opening a Hedgeway.

                                        Does being invisible and intangible mean nothing from these realms can harm them?
                                        Hard to say, but probably not, looking at the combination of references to how Dive works in the text of the blessing itself and in the Royal Shield Contract Whispers of Morning. A Helldiver can see and interact with pretty much any invisible and intangible entity or object and is specifically able to choose whether changelings using Whispers of Morning can see him, but being in a realm in which ephemeral beings are normally material subjects them to the same limitation.

                                        With this chain attached to them do they run the risk of their keeper dragging them back at any time?
                                        They broke the thread as part of their escape; the pieces of such a cord are one of the sample tokens.

                                            Helldiver
                                            Occasionally, the True Fae need to get places that even they can't reach. Sometimes they need a spy or a thief in their byzantine intrigues, someone who would go unnoticed and unaffected. When they feel like engaging in espionage, the Others create Helldivers.

                                            Helldivers are unusual among changelings in that they are not only expected to leave Arcadia, they are encouraged to do so. However, this does not free them from their Keepers. Even in their otherworld forms, members of the Helldiver kith still in their durance have a silver thread attached to their person leading back to their Keepers. All Helldivers know the excruciating pain of being yanked out of another realm unexpectedly to face the wrath of their Keeper. Once the thread is broken, the Helldiver is free. Helldivers break their threads in different ways. Some find a goblin smith willing to destroy it (for a price). Some have them broken by the people they were sent to infiltrate. Some simply go through the agony of breaking it themselves.

                                            Whether in their durance or freed, Helldivers are never in one place for long. They are always chasing some new rumor of a rare token, exploring alien worlds, or listening in on conversations they really shouldn't. Helldivers are hungry for knowledge. After all, knowledge is power.

                                            Beast: This changeling has bright, inquisitive eyes and vestigial wings. They move with startling speed and are usually content to listen — that is, until you get them talking.
                                            Darkling: Even when not Diving, she doesn't seem completely solid. Her eyes are constantly roaming the room, checking for threats. She speaks in a hazy, distracted sort of way, as though not all there.

                                            Kith Blessing: When the Helldiver uses Larceny in the Hedge, Arcadia, or another unearthly realm, achieving three success counts as an exceptional success.

                                            Dive: Spend a Glamour point to make a Dexterity + Occult roll. On a success, the Helldiver begins to fade into an incorporeal, invisible form. It takes a number of turns equal to (10 - her current Clarity), to a minimum of one full turn, to completely fade. While fading, the Helldiver cannot take any non-reflexive actions or interact with objects or people, and attacks with a non-magical component pass harmlessly through her. Once she completely fades, she acts like a
                                            dematerialized Hedge ghost (p. XX), unable to physically interact with anything except other immaterial beings and objects, such as Hedge ghosts, other Helldivers, changelings using the Whispers of Morning Contract (p. XX), the unquiet dead, and spirits of all stripes. She can see and interact with all of these, no matter their current state. If she can find a gate to another realm, such as the deathly Underworld or mysterious Shadow, she may slip through with a point of Glamour as though it were a Hedgeway (p. XX). If ephemeral beings are normally material in another realm, she becomes so as well. She can spend as much time as she likes Diving, but she still requires basic necessities, such as food and sleep.
                                            To end this effect, spend another Glamour point and make another Dexterity + Occult roll. If successful, the changeling fades back into the world at the same rate she faded out. Should a Helldiver gain the Comatose Condition (p. XX) while Diving, she immediately vanishes from wherever she is to reappear inside her Bastion, as though she had passed through the Gate of Horn to be physically present in her dreams.

                                                I'll put down the concept for a changeling character evolution for a mortal PC of mine. You may critique and advise, since the book isn't out yet (but will be by the time I can apply this). Like virtually anything I do, it's long-ish.

                                                ---

                                                Panome was a knight of King Ambrosius Aurelianis, a man of honor, discipline and conviction in a Britain falling increasingly under the enchantment of Merlin. He was also quite skilled, and shared his prowess with a young lady who insisted (as was rarely the case for her gender) on becoming a knight--a feat she achieved by her own qualities, but not before fanning the flames of her competition and honor into a hatred for her teacher that set her firmly on a dark path.

                                                Merlin's great enchantment hinged on the mystical Celtic cycles of the seasons and its links to mortal rulers, among other side-effects this brought the Hedge closer to the mortal world, fraying the boundaries and permitting a bit of both worlds to leak between. Ideas of chivalry and medieval technology centuries in advance came in vogue within decades, and the Arcadian courts of the British Isles, already arranged in endlessly competing grand coalitions known as the Seelie and Unseelie, peered across with great interest at the burgeoning Glamour of "adventure." Panome's adventures left him a marked man by an Unseelie queen, and his student's hatred gave the queen agency to affect revenge. He was drawn into the Hedge, and in their final conflict his student pierced Panome's heart with a blackthorn spear and levied a fell curse on him. Though he escaped and fell out once again, the fae magic would not stop; only the fateful (as was his wont) appearance of Merlin enabled Panome to be sent to the best help and treatment the enchanter new: his contact at the Seelie court.

                                                Panome is not a name. "Panome", in the imagined tongue of the Seelie courts, means "(an object of) shaped wood." The Fae do not "help"--the Fae bargain. And Panome paid many things to avoid his fate, but first among them was his name. The Seelie queen, according to their rules, could not un-do the work of her Unseelie sister--but she could warp it and harness it in a way that pleased her. Instead of a will-less creature of black thorns, pain and violence, she could carve a figure of pleasant lines, rich wood and proper bearing. Instead of placing it among the faceless ranks of thorn-maiden garden-guards and goblin-goads, she could put it in her court of fair maidens and brave knights, of endless feasts and thrilling adventures. All beautiful and appropriate to the tales--and affected by nimble "dwarven" craftsmen and their golden tools, without anesthesia, nine times until the "right" result was found.

                                                Even a master can sometimes struggle to draw out the best from their piece of wood.

                                                Panome was placed as a fair maiden amidst the fantastic Seelie court. She waited on faerie nobles, danced, cooed at brave knights and listened to their wondrous stories. There was dressing, undressing and the making of dainty clothing. Sometimes she was placed in the forest--for that is where adventure is found, and she was, after all, wood--and there came upon the knight as a Fair Unknown. Often, there was rescuing involved. Occasionally she might be asked for a favor before a joust. Panome was a prop. An extra. And deeply confused by her desire to pick up a sword, strap on some pants and (silver) chainmail and right a wrong in a puissant and honorable fashion. None of the other maids seemed to have this problem--they seemed content to talk incessantly about changing fashion (which always did), who might marry whom (which never did), and who was or was not in favor. There was day and night, but only for the different stages of courtly life, and it was always spring--or maybe early summer, it was hard to tell.

                                                One day Panome was talking to the dragon--who was really a pretty swell fellow once you get over the fire-breathing (kinda a big deal when you're made of wood)--when she snapped. She remembered--a bit--being a knight. She KNEW how to be a knight. Why should she not be a knight? Go on adventure? Serve his.. er, her.. king? After the knight showed up and killed the dragon (a shame, really, but to be expected), she was carried back to witness his acclaim. In her bag, however, she carried armor, clothes and arms--props from the befated dragon's cave. In the morning, she dressed as a man, set a helm upon her head, and formally requested leave to adventure--the usual custom of hospitality. So granted, she rode her horse out of the Hedge, and back to the world of men.

                                                Description: Fair and shapely, Panome moves with grace and determination, as well as a full measure of recklessness to match the glint in her eyes, making use of a solid firm musculature. Her hair is long and seems to change color in the seasons, from auburn in fall, a brunette-dark in winter, to near-white blond in spring and an almost greenish cast in summer. Especially in spring she's followed by the scent of flowers, and always with an air of the wild. Under the mask she's a woman of polished burl oak wood, so finely and cunningly articulated only a thoughtful finger could find the joins; if you looked very closely, you might see the whorled grain is made from the tiniest of flowing scripts--a language meaningless to most mortals, but writing out the terms and tropes and tales of import to the Fae, and the terms of her own contract with them. Her hair is a mass of living strands, responsive to the seasons, and within the orbits of her face dance eternally a pair of brilliant fireflies.

                                                Kith: [waiting for the book]

                                                Seeming: Fairest

                                                Court and play: Remains to be seen about courts, but the obvious play hooks at work are, that despite escaping Durance she has really changed very little in her circumstances--except now there remains the opportunity to make or reclaim a life, the honor of knighthood that calls her, and fight the curses that are still at work. Her fetch is there, a few years older, muddling along in her place, doing "his" duty but wasting the potential. Can the work of faerie be undone? Can she claim a real name? Sure the land knows lady-knights, but they run right up against stubborn issues of inheritance and legal standing that push women toward traditional power circles and prop-like dynamics....

                                                --Khanwulf

                                                        Keeping in mind that I'm outlining what are very likely but not assured future character situations, the curses would work like this:

                                                        1. For breaking faerie customs on leaving the Unseelie queen's realm, namely refusing to replace her defeated riverside guardian-knight for all time, a curse that increases recklessness over time. This is independent of the Changeling affliction.

                                                        2. The Curse of the Black Thorn--struck within the Hedge by a lance of the Unseelie queen's name-wood, transforms the victim into a living thorn-maiden obedient under the queen's orders. This curse will be effectively neutered and amended through the Durance. As you can probably tell, practically speaking the character hasn't gained much in the trade, except the Unseelie Court prefers ugly things, and the Seelie Court pretty things, to human perceptions. The sticking point ultimately that being "pretty" to human perceptions is only an asset if you're not intending already to run contrary to trope!

                                                        3. The Tynged of Math fab Mathonwy - sealed with the pronouncement that he will remain cursed "until [he] knows the full measure of woman's sorrow!" This is heady, Mabinogian stuff, and ensures that unraveling the work of the Fae won't be as easy as, say, picking up every bit of torn soul from the Hedge, or excising what the Seelie queen used as filler and wishing to be a real boy.
