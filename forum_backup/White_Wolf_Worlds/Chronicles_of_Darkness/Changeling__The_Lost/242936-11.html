    Originally posted by NLight View Post
    Throwing out structure just seems like an open invite for early power creep and the "yeah, whatever, let's let them do this" like Gifts from Apocalypse that stray off to unthematic regions.
    This actually is a risk, I think. I noticed there was a certain degree of ... awkwardness in nDemon's non-tiered abilities; powers that were really too strong or too weak being shoeorned into the same box; which kind of gave a sense that their powers were more, well powerful than anything else they could buy. If an ability was to "compete" for our attention, it almost needed to be too powerful.

    I think this is nicely solved by keeping the dot rating without requiring each Contract fit into a matching set of five, though, that's a really excellent idea.

    Originally posted by NLight View Post
    Skipping levels in the progression also stunts the curve. Speaking as a player rather than a GM, knowing how to use the low level abilities effectively sets you up for applications of the higher end. Jumping right into the deep end of a contract will just leave you to drown in your own ideas, wondering "why did I pick this again?" Your mileage may vary, but I'm drawing on my experiences specifically with Reflections. It goes somewhere, and I'd be in unfamiliar waters if I jumped right into reaching through a reflective surface to grab the knife in my bathroom's toothbrush holder if I hadn't gone through the experience of grabbing my television and rewinding to find out how the hell I ended up on my couch when I was left for dead in the Hedge the night before.

    [...]

    Divorcing the progressive structure cheats the deal. You don't learn your way around using the concept creatively (which many clauses need to be utilized as more than parlor tricks). You just jump right in and say "Ha! I'm a giant fireball! FEAR ME!" without having any progression up to that point that proves to the concept that you'll even know WTF you're doing. See above about the curve. You start the promise, the Contract, and you fulfill your end of the bargain through application, and get paid with access to more and greater.

    Ok. That's my spew for now. I'll get the mop.
    I think the biggest issue with this is ... what if I don't want lower levels? What if they don't fit my character?

    If my starting concept is "I'm a giant fireball!", does it really make sense to force me to buy "pyrokinesis" or "see infrared" before "they can't catch you if you're on fire!"? I might decide I want those later, but then again, I might decide I want to fly instead. Why not at least extend the option?

    Originally posted by Enokh View Post
    Minor point of contention, game balance between lines isn't a thing at all. On purpose. Not because they want unbalance, but because it isn't a concern. As....DaveB, I think, said: They aren't going to make Spirit Arcana weaker so that Werewolves can be the best at Spiriting. They're going to make Spirit as strong as the other Arcana.

    No balance between lines, simply interaction.
    It is, in fact, a thing.

    The designers don't worry about interfering with the themes and mechanics of other games, yes, because those are separate tools in the toolbox you can pick up or put down as you wish. But they all share the same core rules for Merits, Attributes, Skills, etc. etc. Abilities are (in theory) balanced with those options, and thus remain compatible with any other content for those same core rules.

    No interaction between lines, simply balance.

    Originally posted by Yiodan View Post
    I may have completely misunderstood the concept, but that was how I read the core book so I never could understand how you suddenly came to know how to make yourself eligible and why it should cost XP to state out loud "because I'm made of leaves, I should be able to do X". Or for that matter why allies couldn't just tell each other "BTW, you're made of leaves. So, you're eligible." and then contracts get spread like cookies.
    As I understand it, that would be the remaining niche of Goblin Contracts, now you can buy regular Contracts out of order:

    A regular Contract is, essentially, a tried-and-tested Goblin Contract; it's reliable, so you have to pay XP. Much like you have to buy the Resources Merit to, say, own a shop.

    But yes, you can just make one up on the fly. If you have the right skillset. It will be hard, and costly, and most importantly unreliable - but you can do it, in a pinch. That would be, metaphorically, selling some junk to get enough money to pay the rent - you have to find a buyer, and you can't do this every month, but you get a pile of cash with the right Availability and you can spend it, regardless of where it came from.

                                I like the idea of being able to connect Contracts in some way. Hmm... This might be too subtle, problematic, exploitable, or produce some sort of perverse incentive that's not yet occurred to me, but perhaps some allowance for a Changeling to use multiple Contracts at once, only if simultaneously fulfilling all of their Catches. For hard mode, only if the means of fulfilling one is the same as fulfilling the rest.*

                                Easy ones might just have identical Catches,** combinations that would be overly powerful can have mutually exclusive Catches or ones that depend on incredibly creative lawyering.*** To prevent potential abuse, one caveat might be that it works just fine the first time a particular method is used to creatively combine Contracts. Trying to repeat the exact same thing no longer has that inventive spark to it: the Wyrd won't sanction it and may visit some misfortune on one who tries.

                                Again, not sure how well this would actually work out; might be a lot of work for not a lot of payout. And it might place demands too stringent on what Catches can be, instead of just letting them be something that suits the Contract.

                                * e.g. If the Catch for one contract is wearing something meant to keep one warm and another is wearing something red, simultaneously using both would require something red that's meant to keep one warm
                                ** I'm admittedly not crazy about this notion. I like having lots of diversity in Catches. Aside from that, I like the Catch to be tailored to the Clause in 1e. I'd like that to be even more true, if Contracts are becoming more robust.
                                *** I'm thinking of the sorts of incredibly dubious requirements a lot of faerie curses/blessings seem to carry that nonetheless get dramatically fulfilled through creative interpretation. Like Stardust's 'only when the moon loses her daughter in a week when two Mondays come together' deal.

                                Ah, unrelated to this current line of thought. For Seeming Contracts, will each "Clause" have its own Catch, or will they all be linked by a common Catch? Especially if the idea is that the different "Clauses" will help to characterize the different Seemings, different Catches would help to add even more personality, so that'd certainly be my preference. On a related note, might Contracts potentially have multiple Catches? Or is the idea that Catches will have a brainspew of their own?

                                                            First, the “why fix what isn’t broken?” thing has already been addressed: the developers have stated that they consider the five-tier system to be broken, as well as why. The borrowing of Promethean’s “categories” approach is an attempt by a fan to reintroduce some of the strengths of the five-tier system without reintroducng its drawbacks. Second, Promethean is apparently going to be moving away from the way its Transmutations were set up in 2e; though I don't know what will be replacing it yet.
