    Character concepts for a chronicle

    03-26-2015, 07:19 PM
    One of my favorite horrors for C:TL I've come across is the Marquise Tistresse, a faerie that began as a mere hobgoblin and has now evolved into something that fits into human society better than most True Fae can and which will literally eat them for breakfast. In fact, her whole purpose for coming to the mortal world is to build up strength so she can storm Arcadia and start gorging herself on the True Fae with impunity.

    I blame this idea on the fact one of the potential plot points (via the "Rumors" entry in her chapter) is that she may actually be becoming human enough to sincerely love, something no faerie is capable of. As the title says, would you allow this duo in a Changeling: The Lost game as NPCs, or even potential PCs? I'm not really thinking to bend the rules for them or give them any unique powers/stats, but the origins are what's making me nervous.

    The basic idea is that a man is pulled into the Hedge - perhaps by a less than discerning Keeper (like maybe The Hook), perhaps he stumbles in by mistake. Whatever the cause, no Keeper pulls him through to Arcadia proper, to an actual True Fae demesne, but he still wanders into danger - right into the web-tangled lair of a hobgoblin in the form of a giant, intelligent spider. Hearing this creature taunt him in English, mind racing, he tries something most would never think of: he talks back to it. In fact, he starts flirting with it, pouring on the charm in an effort to make it change its mind. Through charm, audacity, whim and a hell of a lot of luck, it decides it doesn't want to eat him. Instead, it keeps him as a pet.

    For a meaningless age, he remains in the faerie-spider's lair, eating and drinking what it gives him and paying for its "hospitality" with songs and stories and grace and general humanity. As the weeks (months? years? who can say in Faerie) go past, he stays alive, though his "fee" graduates to physically tending to his host - flattering it on its beauty and polishing its carapace, sharpening its fangs, etcetera. But as they spend so much time together, so much talking, the hobgoblin learns more and more about humanity. But with knowledge can come understanding, and that is the fate that befalls it.

    As we all know, even in the True Fae, coming to understand the way that humans think is to forsake much of one's Faerie nature. Even the mightiest of the Gentry, faerie gods, can be reduced to weak creatures exiled from Arcadia - I believe the term for them is "Imposters". If that can happen to the True Fae, then what can happen to a little hobgoblin?

    The spider, having begun to think of itself as herself long by this point, one day demands her pet repeat to her some of the stories that have been ringing in her mind, stories of faerie brides and bestial wives. As he winds to a conclusion, she shakes her alien head and offers him a deal; thoughts of this thing humans called "love" have been haunting her, and while she doesn't quite understand what it means, she knows that she wants it. Only imperfectly understanding what the term means, she declares that if he will take her under oath as his wife, she will lead him through the Hedge and back to the mortal world.

    And he makes that pledge, and she makes that pledge, as strange a union as even Faerie ever did see, binding themselves together through ignorance and desire and the raw power of the Wyrd. And she leads him through the thorns, sneaking past the horrors of the Hedge, and they both cross through to the mortal world. And there she stops and shudders and her body splits open and something that looks like a woman and yet subtly like a spider steps from the gore. And arm in arm they go off together, neither quite knowing what they are now or what they're going to do.

    In game terms, both of them are Changelings, perfectly ordinary and by the rules. I'm uncertain what to make of the male - Romancer, perhaps. For the ex-hobgoblin, I was thinking of making use of the optional "blending" rules from later splats. That is, she's a Mixed Breed Dual Kith, with the Fairest seeming and the Beast kiths of Venomheart and Riddleseeker. The kiths represent her fundamental spidery nature, whilst the seeming reflects both her desire to be a "fairy bride" archetype and the fact that she just doesn't GET humans as well as most other changelings would. The spider has lowered Clarity (4, would be my initial answer) but increased Wyrd, due to starting out as a fae rather than a mortal.

    The Pledge the two have is unique, and the basic "bonus" is that it's what makes her function identically to any human-origin Changeling. It demands the two treat each other as husband & wife and honor the vows they swore — most importantly, to never be unfaithful to each other. If one of them cheats, they die; if he commits "adultery" on her, then he will be paralyzed, unable to even muster the will to move as she reverts to her giant spider form and devours him, but if she starts dallying with other men, then her intelligence and power will be stripped from her and she will become an ordinary spider, scuttling off into a crack in the rock.

      There was a similar horror story to this in which a man encounters a monster and in exchange for his life, he swears to never tell anybody that he saw it. A little while afterward, he encounters a woman and ends up falling in love with her. The two get married and raise a pair of kids, but the woman finds out he's been drawing pictures of the monster and even has a figurine made of it. When he learns she's been going through his stuff, he explains to her about the monster, at which point she begins to transform into the monster, screaming that he broke his vow. Just before she kills him, the man sees that his children are also transforming, both terrified of what's happening to them. The story ends with the monster and her children perching on a roof top.

          *Shrugs* It's a pretty common tale, all told. Asiatic fox-wives (from Japan's kitsune, who are loving spouses, to the more murderous/vampirish gumiho of Korea), Sedna marrying an auk in Inuit mythology, the woman who married a bear in Native American lore, Irish selkies, Japanese star-maidens who come to earth disguised as birds, the aswangs of the Phillipines, the tikabalang...

          Really, my big worry is not that there's not a mythological basis for it, it's that it breaks the rules of how a changeling can come to be, and what a changeling is. Perhaps not so much under 2e rules, from what I've heard whispered in passing, but under 1e, well...

              I feel like, if it's the story you and your players want to tell, then it can be a beautiful and terrifying story. However, I also feel like the execution is going to be tricky.                   They're actually intended to both be NPCs, at this current stage of thought. As you've said, there's a lot of possibility for abuse if only one of them is a PC, after all. They're perhaps better suited for movers and shakers in the local area, if not the local Freehold, in that in that manner, a party can either choose to avoid them and leave them as just "color", or get involved in their tale as it generates various plothooks.

                  For example, what happens when women start turning up dead - perhaps poisoned, or (partially) eaten - and the Spider Duchess is blamed, whilst her husband insists upon her innocence? What if the Courts start trying to manipulate them, based on dogma? What if someone tries to "re-educate" other Hobs? What happens if other supernals get involved - a vampire trying to blood-bond the husband, a mage abducting the wife to examine her? What happens when they decide they want to have a family and offer a reward to anyone willing to fetch them the rare ingredients (pericle velvet from the Hedge-Tundras, or a dose of water from the River of Dead Seed in the Underworld) they need to make one - and how do the local changelings react to the potential to steal something so rare and occult, or to the idea of, let's face it, a *faerie* having a half-human child?

                  But then, that's just me, I guess.
