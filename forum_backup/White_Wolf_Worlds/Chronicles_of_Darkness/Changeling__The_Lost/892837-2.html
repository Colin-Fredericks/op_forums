    Originally posted by falco1029 View Post

    That's similarly a false equivalency. I'm not saying the powers should work exactly the same. The main issue I'm getting at here is that a Mage and a Changeling treat a MORTAL differently in dreams. I don't care that Changelings go there physically; that's cool and thematic, and should use a different system.

    However, it should be COMPATIBLE, and rely on the same base system for how a third party without a power is treated in dreams; mortals specifically. If a mage and a changeling are both in a mortal's dreams, you have a mess because they both say something different happens to the mortal when damage is filled up. And for the mortal's base stats, for that matter. THAT'S what my issue is.

    I'm all for a Changelings specifically taking real damage that gets downgraded, and Mages getting a boost in their Dream Form's statistics. But both should have the dream form rules for a mortal or other third party with no special system. Just like a werewolf's Gift and a Mage's spell should both do damage to a health track in a similar manner; the effects are different, but damage is damage. It'd be weird if one said the mortal gets the 'bleeding out' condition if they take Stamina + Size levels, and the other used actual health, you know? And even weirder if Werewolf had an entirely different system for a mortal's combat stats than mage did.
    On one hand, yeah, it would make sense if what happened to mortals when getting dream-punched by a Mage/Beast/Changeling was the same. On the other, you do have differences in the combat subsystem like that: a Vampire's bite grants the Drained Condition, but other damage sources don't, for example. The source of the attack can cause the outcome to differ, which can get confusing when trying to use both systems, but allows thematic and mechanical freedom. It's a hassle on the crossover-ST side, but simpler on the Dev side, and the mono-splat-STs are unaffected either way. I agree that it would be nice for it to be normalized, but I can understand the decision not to, and I don't think that kind of "compatibility" should be required.

                                It's honestly not hard to make them compatible. Changeling differentiates three "classes" of Dreamer: Normal, Lucid, and Changeling(-like). Sleepers project a Dream Form. Normally, if someone's Dream Form is reduced to 0 HLs then they suffer the Soul Shocked condition. If they are a Lucid Dreamer then they suffer downgraded damage upon awakening and if they "die" in the dream they still suffer the Soul Shocked condition. In exchange they can use environmental attacks, half their supernatural advantage as a damage bonus, dream riding, dreamscaping (if they are somehow in someone else's dreams), dream scouring, dream bolstering, and remember their dreams perfectly. I'd also lean towards letting Lucid Dreamers take relevant dream merits.

                                If they are physically present in the dream then they use their normal attributes, add +(Wyrd) to their Derived Traits (Health Levels, Defense, Initiative, Speed, Willpower), "real" damage is applied instantly and it is not downgraded. Too Solid Flesh turns everyone into a Lucid Dreamer and ensures damage is not downgraded when they are damage. Include a sidebar that says supernaturals with their own rules regarding being in dreams use their rules to determine traits instead and use a Beast's Horror as an example and decide whether they count as lucid dreamers or not. I lean no, even if it's illogical, unless they take the lucid dreamer merit. This is just because it'd make the Changeling book override other books. The 10-again thing is weirdly the most difficult thing to fit in.
