    Huh, I am genuinely surprised. I really was thinking of courts on a very different scale. When thinking about Changelings and what kinds of stories to tell, I thought about groups that suffer from t PTSD and alienation: people in the military and victims of human trafficking, as well as oppressed, outside groups like transgender people.
    It's totes a YMMV sorta deal. However, there are certain implications that need to be taken into account if you decide to have a large population of magical people in any given city.

    1) since they harvest emotions and don't cause damage (or much damage) to the human population, they are not limited to being an apex predator like a vampire
    As is the main conceit in the hidden world subgenre of fantasy, the World of Darkness setting has a certain expectation of supernatural secrecy as its modus operandi. Humanity is kept "in the dark" as it were when it concerns the existence of the fairy world, and since there is no equivalent of the Mists in this edition of changeling, it is assumed that this ignorance is either due to a deliberate, multi-tiered conspiracy on the part of the Courts (which would require a vast level of influence and resources) or that there aren't enough changelings to cause more than a blip on the "Weird Shit is Going Down" radar.

    I'd personally go with the latter choice because, to quote Benjamin Franklin, "Three men can keep a secret if two are dead."

    Even with the Mask, each changeling leaves a pretty big magical footprint on their landscape. A population of over two million magical creatures (fetches included), possessed of some pretty blatant magical powers, wouldn't go unnoticed for long, as not all of them will have access to Vainglory 5 or be ruthless/canny enough to silence every mortal who witnesses fairy magic. I'd imagine in such a setting, the existence of fairies and their antics would be an accepted (and perhaps unproven) part of metaphysical belief, instead of being considered make-believe.

    A simple, mechanical, fix would be to give every changeling the Hidden Life merit found in Rites of Spring. This would explain how changelings manage to have such an entrenched infrastructure and remain hidden from the perceptions of mortals. Honestly, I was a bit sad to see that there was no "Mists" equivalent in this rendition of Changeling, as I always found it to be a poignant and bittersweet mechanic.

                        My campaign is set in a city of about 260,000 and I’ve established the local freehold to have approximately 86 members; though I’ve only detailed around a third that number. And yes, I do have it established that the courts amicably hand off power each solstice and equinox (though spring is now half way through and the Summer Court was almost completely annihilated in a recent botched attack, so there are some concerns as to whether or not they’ll be able to take power come June 21st).

                        The books pretty clearly state that there is no higher authority above the level of the freehold. I always assumed that the similarities in the court structure from one freehold to the next were the result of traditions passed down (and spread) from the courts’ four founders. It’s been some time since I gave Lords of Summer a thorough reading, but I don’t recall anything contradicting the variance indicated in the core Changeling book. I recall it stating several times that the information it contained was just representative of a TYPICAL freehold.

                        Speaking of Lords of Summer, it has information on several titles often held in certain courts and the useful services that they provide to their court in particular and to the freehold in general. However, it doesn’t have as much to say about what the actual monarchs do; though in my experience, any time you have a group of more than a dozen people for ANY reason, it’s a good idea to have someone in charge or there’s gonna to be bedlam.

                        So that’s my perspective on some of the questions that have been raised in this thread, now I have of my own. Can someone direct me to where it discusses courts being ruled by pairs of people? I can’t recall reading this anywhere.

                            In terms of higher authority, I can think of a few references. One of the Morpheans in Dancers in the Dusk can grant a lot of power to someone, but it won't last.                             In Equinox Road there's also the Entitlement called... something about the Gilded Torc. Whatever it was, it was only eligible for Court Leaders, and those that joined found that the Entitlement rules as a democratic council that make decisions for all of the Freeholds of their members (or something along those lines).
