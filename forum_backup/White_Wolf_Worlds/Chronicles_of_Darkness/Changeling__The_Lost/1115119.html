    [Idea help] The Once and King in Future

    07-12-2017, 12:46 PM
    Ok folks, I have a setting arc idea I'm going to use with my players that I'd like to toss as red-meat the forum. Please critique and advise. Obviously since this is going onto the Changeling forum I see some potential to interpret what's happening from that perspective. In particular the recent True-Fae material got me thinking... "what if this is actually the domain of a Fae?" --My original plan involved time manipulation, which could still be a thing. I have quite a bit of material developed, but will start with a summary. Items in brackets are placeholders and replaceable with personal campaign elements (NPCs, missing characters, rumors and passing mentions).

    So, please, help me properly Changling-ize this.

    ---

    The PCs wake (being struck by crowbar on the head is optional) to discover they are residents of a massive, fantastic city: a towering metropolis of steel, wood and stone, populated by all manner of people and stranger things--fantastic beasts and animalistic fairy things slouching on corners, hawking wares beneath blinking neon and running errands to and fro. All about are crowds and conveyances, the most remarkable of which hover and fly, or are pulled by mechanical horses and such beasts.
    The world has shifted under them and things are... different: it is England, but the world is ruled by Arthur--the King in Night, from his great mega-city of Camelot and it's ferocious high-technology.

    Arthur's knights ride forth on their mechanical steeds, armed with swords of fire and lances of lightning to tilt in tourney, adventure, quest and ruthlessly subjugate his domain--the continents of the world and such far-flung possessions as his ships have reached among the Sea of Stars. Chief among his knights stands [Krum], the Graal Knight, and at Arthur's right hand sits his ravishing Red Queen, Lilith. To his Round Chamber, the great senate of his knights, flock the greatest, most powerful and idealistic knights from across the world, be they Day Court or Night Court, all bow to Arthur and he generously dispenses gifts and justice... but little mercy.

    All, however, is not well and hidden treason lurks at the gleaming heart of Camelot's 3 billion souls. There is gossip of Krum and Lilith and improper romance, and within the Day Court Regent Travis, adopted son of Arthur, plots to seize the Eternal Throne. And, of course, rumors that Merlin, absent for more than a millenia, or even older gods, have returned. Arthur ignores all of these, as he has for centuries, subsisting on the blood of the Graal and suspended within the levitating ideals of high chivalry. One wonders what occurs when the anti-gravity is cut off....

    ---
    WTF is Going On Points:
    * Merlin was killed (or worse) by Lilith at the height of his great magic work (say, 515 AD), which underpinned the unnatural elevation of Camelot and the development of culture and technology centuries ahead of its time. Without Merlin to bend the arc of the seasons back into Winter (as planned for his own ascension play), Arthur and Camelot have persisted and arguably achieved escape velocity....

    * Arthur is a vampire, and supremely powerful by virtue of the combination of blood and true magic (see Merlin, above). He's not God, and knows it, so still exists within a recognizable framework of chivalric duty, obligations and respect for the Creator. This constitutes a weakness in the same fashion that the quest for the Holy Grail pulled down his court in the stories and laid the foundation for his unhinged responses to Guinevere's betrayal with Lancelot. (Arguably he was responding MORE strongly to the deaths of his knights, than to her infidelity. Understandable, since he did some sleeping around, himself!) Arthur wear armor of plates of gold, laced with technology and magic to defend against certain things. His robe and cape made of tiny gold linked strands... in other words, he's a King in Yellow.

    * [Krum] is the Blood Graal, a mortal with supernatural abilities [perhaps derived from self-generated vitae ala Masquerade revenants]. His blood is devoted to the High King and he serves as a living chalice to sustain Arthur without forcing him to eat a feast's worth of commoners nightly. He's essentially Lancelot in all other respects, including Arthur's trust and regard. [Krum] also has been given by the narrative Lancelot's relative martial skills, which means he's effectively one of the most dangerous creatures on the planet, even if he needs that power-armor to match a vampire's full speed and strength. [Krum] is also therefore a client king, having been rewarded with a very substantial domain someplace by Arthur.

    * Lilith is Lilith. She's ridden the tail of Merlin's magic to become the Red Queen astride the world. It--with all its power and souls--is hers to command and administer, so long as Arthur remains content to fritter the nights away in pursuit of court and chivalric ideals. She encourages that, and has for centuries since becoming queen and showing him how to live forever. But she is equally a creature of unreasoned passion, and for all intents and purposes is Guinevere; she's been carrying on with [Krum] for some time.

    * Merlin is gone. The PC effectively taken his place. They can pursue being knights and magic, if not nearly as good as Merlin, and trying to figure out what's happening, and how to "fix" things. They can be given a companion, a talking, brown cat, who everyone ignores but seems to know much if the right questions can be asked--but the closer questions get to the Round Chamber the less she knows.

    * [Lareth] plays the part of Sir Mordred, existing in a kind of feud with Lancelot ([Krum]) but ultimately loyal to Arthur because he represents the world [Lareth] wants to build. He's infatuated by Lilith, but what else is new--everyone loves the Red Queen!

    * Other characters may or may not exist based on need.

    So what do the PCs do?
    The best approaches are to A) work to uncover and then steal secrets underpinning Arthur's power--for which they can find an ally in Morgan (le Fey), and/or B) play the part of uncovering the Lancelot-Guinevere treason to Arthur. The latter will cause Arthur to turn on Lilith--not something she wants, and something she'll discover is a real challenge since he's accumulated the benefits of Merlin's powerful Fate magic over the past millennia. He may even succeed in burning her alive, which would slow her down some and further strip her of power.

    [Krum] would then flee to his land, Arthur follow in a war, while "back home" [Lareth] is left to run the country and rebel against his sire, destroying Camelot and ultimately immolating the knights and Arthur in the Battle of Camlann.

    Other options include C) swearing fealty to the inevitable Future King, Arthur, and becoming one of his knights, trapped in reality-warping legend. That... is less optimal.

    If this scenario springs from time manipulation the best result is for Lilith to recognize all this as it is shaping up, and adopt a time ritual to prevent herself from acting in the past. That would correct the time-stream and return the PCs to their rightful place. If it's the interpretation of the setting that this is a True-Fae's domain, that of either the Queen in Red or King in Yellow, replaying endlessly the high drama of Arthurian legend with bizarre twists and the utter conquest of many other worlds/realms, then the best result is to not play the game at all--escape, probably after Arthur "dies" and is carried to Avalon; there, for a moment, the hedge will be weakest.

    ---

    So ideas? How can I align this more with Changeling, at least to create ambiguity as to what is *really* happening?

    Thanks,

    --Khanwulf

      From his throne the High King rises and steps down the seven ivory steps and past each flanking, carved lion. Today he is shod entirely in the royal armor, and angular plates of gold slide across the body as he moves as if each competes to find the best combination of protection and balance. The tiny lines of force and runes that edge the plates casts a golden light and the armor seems to shimmer and ripple in a disrupting haze. Behind him a tattered cloth-of-gold cape falls and lingers over the steps, hovering a few inches above them as its billions of tiny links flow together. Arthur, High King of Britain and Emperor of Rome and Its Colonies, Immortal Protector of All Under Heaven, raises his hand; tiny plates clink, seamlessly following, rearranging his gauntlet.

      "My Knights of the Round Chamber, assembled Kings and worthy guests among the court!"

      Arthur's voice booms out effortlessly past pale lips, and where it goes knees bend like a breeze through grass. You feel a rush of voices almost unheard, whispering of his majesty, feel a thousand tiny hands plucking at your garments, bidding your obeisance. It would be easy to comply. To honor the High King.

      It would be... wise.

      “We are pleased that the heroes of the Grail Quest have returned to their homes once again. Their efforts, and especially those of good Sirs Galahad,
      Bors, and Percivale, have returned the realm once again to normal.

      This quest has once again banished the Grail and healed the wasteland that plagued Our people!

      But.... So very many brave and noble knights have fallen, never to return to Us. So many immortal knights, even--an irreparable loss. Many seats lay open within the Round Chamber....

      We have ordered the greatest of tournaments to celebrate this victory! Many of you have already joined this worthy contest, but We welcome others! Let your valor, skill and nobility shine forth and be rewarded by Us!"
      ---

      The tattered golden cape represents a focus that transforms Arthur into a kind of avatar for the King in Yellow. He literally flays a bit from every soul under his rule, making his Majesty unassailable (the lying meme of the King in Yellow being that his right to rule cannot be denied) and ensuring he has effectively limitless energy for regeneration and powers. Arthur quite simply cannot be wounded while he has the cape, in other words, and it serves as an analogy to the scabbard of Excalibur, which in the legend Morgan ensured was stolen--permitting Arthur to be mortally wounded at Camlann.

      But in this case Lilith gave him the cape, and has benefited from her trade with the King in Yellow... only Arthur has proven both more obstinate than she expected and at once easy. She cannot rule openly and in power as she wishes, and that has grated on her for centuries. Lilith is nothing if not rebellious, and that character... flaw... is the magnetic attraction to Merlin's great magic, and may yet bend the course of history back to earth as he intended.

      With the PC's help, that is. And the fact Lilith has enabled Arthur to become even stronger and more dangerous than herself, fueled as he is by a millennium of mighty magic and the will of a planet's worth of humanity.

      The players will need to steal Arthur's cape. Fortunately he takes both it and his armor off. He really doesn't pay such heed to his toys as he should. If he understood everything he'd be much scarier, but he's focused still on chivalry and courtly love and the role of the high king, oaths and honor in his society. He's faced rumors of his queen's infidelity for some time, but refuses to entertain them or that [Krum], the Greatest Knight in the World, champion of courtly love and his trusted Blood Graal, could betray him to high treason with the queen.

                  A few thoughts that sprang to mind looking over this:
                  • Dancers in the Dusk has several dream-characters that have resonance with what's happening here, including Mordred himself and the primal archetype of all Cats. In fact, consider: what if this all is a dream? A dream there is no waking from? A dream with its own stable logic, and it's hard for the PCs to tell who are just actors, who are real people trapped in their roles, and who might be the dream's architect. (Perhaps Lilith, the True Fae who wove this dream as a spider's web to catch her prey. Perhaps the King in Yellow, who is filled with wicked joy as he makes everyone conform to his ideas of them. Perhaps [Lareth], who watches and manipulates from the sidelines, letting his prisoners think they are in charge. Maybe some strange Hob who constructs dreams to serve as traps for its prey, like the dromes from Pratchett's The Wee Free Men.)
                  • Consider taking cues from King Haggard from The Last Unicorn for the King in Yellow. It is dangerous to attract his attention, it is even moreso to be forgotten by him, for anyone he forgets will cease to exist. An eternal ennui, a maniacal pursuit of the last things that make him feel alive. Your best Christopher Lee impression. Okay, maybe not that last, but still.
                  • Would your protagonists be Mortals or Changelings? Changelings would probably have some more tools to manipulate the environment, particularly if it is a dream, which they are naturally good at shaping. On the other hand, imagine how terrifying it would be to know that you're stuck in a dream, and it's too strong for you to shape, like you can with any ordinary dream- the question becomes how, then, might the dream be weakened? On the other hand, Mortals don't have any magic, any tricks up their sleeve, and just have to work out the dream-logic and play the Triumvirate against each other.
                  • I keep returning to the dream idea, but it's the natural fit - more natural, I think, than having it happen in Arcadia itself. Strange things happen in the dream-bastions hidden within the Thorns, after all. Since you want ambiguity, however, it could be possible to seed hints that it might be happening in some strange Hedge-city, and all these people are either Hobs (the goblin-creatures of the Hedge) or Changelings who very long ago went mad and tried to make their own kingdom in the Hedge.
                  • (Imagine the hubris! Thinking you can make King Arthur's Court in the Thorns without things being twisted, as through a mirror darkly, and your best intentions turned to beautiful nightmares, your memories washed and torn away as your flaws seep into the world around you, just as much as if not more than your virtues. In this interpretation, perhaps it was a motley of Arthur, Krum, and Lilith who tried to create a utopia together which now exists as their shared delusion. The question then becomes: do you leave them to their self-inflicted hell, or do you try to save them, too? Will their Kingdom allow them to leave? Why did they run away from the real world in the first place? Bonus points if you can draw parallels between the Motley and the PCs in their motivations.)
                  • If the Triumvirate are Changelings, either trapped in a dream or in their own Hedge-kingdom, I'd peg the King in Yellow as a very sanguine Fairest almost convinced in the truth of his own legitimacy, Krum as a Ogre resigned to his suffering at the hands and teeth of his king, and Lilith as a Darkling who delved too deep for certain secrets, or even being lost in secrets she's forgotten that she once knew (assuming that none of them are something stranger and fouler in disguise).
                  • Strange loops, strange inconsistencies, history repeating itself, scenes changing without actual, you know, transition, and only the PCs noticing, everyone is stuck in their roles until an outside force (like the PCs) start coming into their orbit and pointing out uncomfortable truths and tearing at the structures of this false utopia. And the more the PCs draw attention to the strangeness, the more they start coming to the attention of the- well, let's call them the Stagehands.
                  • The Stagehands might take the form of Arthur's knights. Or they might be "Elves". Or they might be dragons. Whatever they are, they are safeguarding the stability of Camelot, and intend to silence dissent and disruption decisively. Even fatally. The Triumvirate, of course, don't know they exist: the Stagehands are, after all, doing all this for them. So they never have to wake up. So they never have to face the truth.

                      Thanks Raz_Fox! Very good points and ideas--much appreciated! And, resonating as well: my intended use includes one PC for whom it literally is a dream he can't escape from (he ends it by dying), while the other PCs fall into it and either don't think it's a dream or it isn't--to them!

                      It's the origin and duality and other strangeness that I want to emphasize. To go overboard with. The setting is supposed to be intentionally way too much. And in the end? If the PCs help unravel future-evilish-Camelot? They can wonder if they saved mankind from a fate worse than death... or burnt man's best hope for a truly just world.

                      Stagehands: excellent thought. Agitating about what is "wrong" about Camelot is a quick way to be challenged by knights and scorned by ladies--all in the trope, of course. Such things can be handled.

If the PCs, or their players, are sensitive to such nuance it should unnerve them further, along with the King in Yellow imagery.)
