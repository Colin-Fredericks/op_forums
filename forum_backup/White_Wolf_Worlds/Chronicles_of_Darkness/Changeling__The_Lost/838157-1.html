            Seconding Charlaquin's note that treating Mage cosmology as "correct" is engaging in the Sharpshooter Fallacy writ large.

            Call it tautological, but as far as I consider the question to particularly matter, Faerie is the place/"place" nearest to the Wyrd in the same sense that facilities are part of the God-Machine and the qashmallim are made of the Divine Fire. It's not like with the Arisen and Sekhem, where their entire model is built on taking a particular type of magic out of the world and putting it into another dimension — goblin markets notwithstanding, fae cosmology is much more concerned with tit-for-tat and meddling in worldly affairs than harvesting agency and sentiment from the universe.

            Treating Faerie as a Lower Depth would mean assuming it's one that's somehow closer to Earth than the Inferno, which is about as close as the Depths normally get.

                    Actually, I've thought of a better analogy. Pluto. Pluto is a roughly spherical celestial object that orbits the sun. Prior to 2006, astronomers called roughly spherical celestial objects that orbit stars "planets". Then, in 2006, a committee of the world's leading astronomers got together and decided that the definition of "Planet" was too broad, and from then on they were only going to call roughly spherical celestial objects that orbit stars and don't have any other similar-sized celestial objects within a certain radius of their orbital path planets. If you were not an astronomer or a follower of astronomical news at the time, you were likely confused why the astronomers were suddenly saying Pluto wasn't a planet any more. After all, nothing changed about Pluto. It's still a roughly spherical celestial object that orbits the sun. It still fits the pre-2006 definition of a planet. And if you're not an astronomer or astronomy enthusiast, you might not even be aware that when astronomers say "planet" now they mean something different when what they meant when they said "planet" then. It also probably makes no practical difference in your life if you're not an astronomer.

                    Similarly, when Mages say "Lower Depth" they mean a place that lacks a supernal truth and consumes the truth it lacks from the fallen world. Does Arcadia match that definition? I honestly don't know, I was never that into Mage in 1e and my Mage knowledge is pretty much limited to the 2e spoilers and some things I've heard from discussions on these forums. But it also probably makes no practical difference in your character's lives unless your characters are Mages.
