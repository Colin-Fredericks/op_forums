    Originally posted by K9ine View Post
    So there seems to be a lot more character customization than in vampire. Do your Kiths decide your discipline equiviant?
    In 1e, Contracts (the Discipline equivalent) were pretty much Disciplines by any other name. They were rated from 1-5 dots, each dot was an individual ability, you got a list of Affinity Contracts based on your Seeming that you could buy at a discounted experience cost (Kith didn't normally come into it, but there were some Kiths that gave a small discount on a specific Contract that wasn't Affinity for its Seeming). Then there were Court-specific Contracts that required you to be a member of the appropriate Court or have at least one dot of the Court Good Will Merit in the appropriate Court to learn. Considered Affinity if you were a full member of the Court, non-Affinity if you learned it through Good Will.

    In 2e, Contracts are being totally restructured to differentiate them from Disciplines. Now they work much more like Merits, with each power being an individual Contract instead of lumping them into groups of five escalating powers, and each Contract has a dot rating, with splashier effects costing more dots, and Contracts cost 1 experience per dot. Any character can learn any Contract, but some Contracts have bonus effects tied to certain Seemings. If you have the right Seeming you get the bonus effect for free, and if you're a different Seeming you can learn the bonus effect for one experience if you have a teacher of the right Seeming. Court Contracts are a little different, basically they work like normal Contracts, but some of their effects are variable and depend on your Court.

        I know this is getting further off topic, but while we're on the subject, what are x splats, and y splats? Regular splats are just vampires, or changelings right?
        Yeah, I usually say "templates" instead of "splats" for the type of monster (vampire, werewolf, mage, etc.) to avoid getting the terminology up with what I call X-, Y-, and Z-splats. But, what I refer to as an X-splat is the inborn subsplat for a Template (Clan, Auspice, Path, etc.) What I refer to as a Y-splat is the chosen subsplat for a Template (Covenant, Tribe, Order, etc.) What I refer to as a Z-splat is a further refinement of an X- or Y-splat (Bloodline, Lodge, Legacy, etc.)

                        Its alittle more complicated in Changeling, including Changeling 2e.

                        In Changeling 2e the X splat is split in 2. You have your Kith which is what your keeper turned you into. And your Seeming, which is how your escape transformed you. Both are innate.

                        There are over 100 Kiths and 7 Seemings (Beasts, Darklings, Elementals, Fairest, Orgers, Loyalists, and Wizened, although Loyalist tend to be anatogonists). That's over 700 possible combinations for an X splat right from core.

                        The Y splat are Courts when vary depending on Freehold. Miami's Freehold uses a traditional Seasonal Court system, with Autumn, Spring, Winter, and Summer courts. The Toronto Freehold uses a very multicultural verison of the 1001 nights system, with the Brass, Jade, Ivory, and Silver Courts (and possibly Ruby Court). A freehold usually can have 2 to six courts with 2 being the minium). They're will be a variety of example freeholds with different court systems including Toronto, Miami, Budapest, Cape something (Africa), Tokyo, Paris, possibly some others.

                        The Z splat (Zed splat I'm Canadian), is Noble/Eldrich Entitlements, which think has been mentioned somewhere as being in the core.

                        There is some Changeling 2e material in Dark Eras, but the machanics are mostly in iffy condition, but I'm hoping when Changeling 2e is published they post errata fixing them.

                                            Since others have covered the general changes, I thought I'd mention why I'm going squee over some of them:

                                            1) Seeming being about how you escaped: Having it affect your Clarity really ties it in with your character's method of coping with their trauma. It also gives a feeling that you got something out of Arcadia because of your choices, instead of your Keeper's. I can imagine Changelings taking pride in having a Seeming that runs counter to their Keeper's favorite themes.

                                            2) We now have a clear explanation for how the Courts prevent the Fae from just running up and grabbing Changelings, and it seems very appropriate. I think it also illustrates the mindset of the Fae and the Wyrd: Fae are the incarnation of story logic, and are instinctively obligated to keep things interesting. They have to use certain Approaches and beat you at what your Court is good at. Before, the Court system seemed to boil down to confusing the Fae with the process of passing power between Courts, something the Gentry find illogical. That's still an aspect of the Court system, but now we know how the system also protects individual Changelings, rather than just the freehold as a whole.

                                            3) The new Clarity system: It reinforces the theme of coping with trauma, now with personalized triggers. Aside from your Seeming's free Clarity, you regain Clarity by facing your triggers and keeping your self-control.
