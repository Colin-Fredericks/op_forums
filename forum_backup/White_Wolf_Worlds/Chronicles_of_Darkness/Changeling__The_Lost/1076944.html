    Fae vs Spirits: making a sale pitch

    04-12-2017, 09:59 AM
    Not sure if this is the correct thread but here is my question : how diferent is to cut a deal with a fae entity (changeling, goblin, fae lord, aspect of the wyrd ) from doing the same thing with Spirits ?
    I was thinking about making a campaign with mortal shamans (not awakened ) gaining power through bargains with entities from the Hisil.

      I would say the main difference is that spirits are far more predictable, but harder to bind.

      On the one hand, while spirits in the same choir do have variants and differences, once you know it's a "spirit of dogs", say, you've already learnt quite a lot about it. Maybe it will be more specific- "dogs as predator" or "dogs as guardians" or "dogs as companions"- but you know everything about it will be in some way related to "dog".
      You can get some unpleasant surprises but overall, once you know what the spirits embodies ,you know what it wants and what it does. There's not much "personality" with a spirit- they don't really have preferences or quirks or beliefs beside what their resonance implies.

      A True Fae, on the other hand, is far more unpredictable. Learning a title doesn't help in the same way as learning a spirit's resonance does. It's other titles don't necessarily need to have anything to do with each other, and even then they're more themes then anything else. The Bearer of Fire can appear as a brutish flame-beast, a sneaky arsonist or a wise bringer of enlightenment depending on its current story, and even that's lost if it switches to its other title of "the Queen of Locusts". Each fae is an individual, and you need to be prepared for anything.
      The same for other Fae creatures- changelings are ex-humans and have all the variety of humans, and goblins encompass dozens of sentient species. Each one is an individual that you need to get to know to bargain with

      On the other hand, unless you have a specific power, your bargain with a spirit is just a standard bargain. If the spirit decides the deal isn't in its best interest any more, it's perfectly capable of taking back its power there and then. Once you've made a bargain, you need to ensure that the spirit keeps a good reason to work with you. (on the flip side, you can also betray the spirit if that helps you).

      The Fae can't. If you've made a full Pact with them, they can't break their word, not without something terrible happening. There can be loophole abuse and technicalities, but if a Fae creature promises not to attack you it won't attack you regardless of how convenient it is. The flip side is that you are also bound if the Fae is even remotely competent.

      TL;DR- spirits are easier to deal with but potentially treacherous. The Fae are unpredictable but once a bargain is struck are more reliable.

              A changeling is most likely to give you very small, but possibly supernatural benefits, which make your life convenient for little more than a symbolic cost. What they offer pretty much has to be what they're capable of, in their free time, with minimal cost. The main things changelings want is connections to other humans which make the huntsmen overlook them.

              Goblins are like back alley thieves trying to sell you wonder on the cheap. Their contracts aren't usually that strong, but they're bloody useful and straightforward ways to get what you want... but have a tendency to explode - their drawbacks suck, and the Goblins don't have to warn you about it. All they ask for, though, are abstract qualities you probably won't miss: last night's dream, a memory from infancy, your first crush, your last regret.

              The True Fae beget terror. What they want is captives (escaped changelings or new ones ) and glamour. They may ask you to place an innocuous item under someones pillow (to poison their dreams), or to "scare" some one (removing a changelings layer of defense), or may just harvest one of your emotions to leave your soul empty. No matter how innocent it seems, though, they'll use it in a way you'll probably find evil: kidnapping people, driving them insane, maybe making fey monsters from your emotions.... You might not see the results. Maybe. But they do love toying with people. The plus side? Magic is cheap for them they can do almost anything and think nothing of it. And if your request requires they enter the mortal world, why then you have to sustain them while they're here, and they get all kinds of access that wasn't part of the deal...

              You have to be fae to make a contract with the Wyrd and it requires a truly epic task to make a new one. If you can get in on that, though, its super straightforward to use. "Goblin vows" are a lot like bargaining with spirits, actually: do something which enhances the Aspect, it does an equivalent power for you. Contracts are even mores structured: pay glamour and/or this minor cost, get this specific thing.

              Spirits are usually more straightforward than any fae. They want essence, protection from other spirits, or access to earth to shape their influences. If you set it up right, you can do minor favors in their purview (feed stray cats, never hide from the rain, always keep a flame in your house) to maintain a relationship, then do a ritual that generates essence to ask them to use their power. Unfortunately, you have to find the right spirit, and you may have to talk down a vastly more powerful being fron the insane things it demands (stop a forest from being developed, destroy the most popular business in town, give them a human to possess). You'll always be weaker than a spirit, so need to really know your stuff to negotiate, and that knowledge isn't very easy to get.
              (And that's if werewolves aren't part of the setting. If they are, you're always going to be at risk for pissing off one of them accidentally)

                  The biggest difference is that while both spirits and fae are oath-happy otherworldly beings with a tenuous-at-best grasp of the human mindset outside of established grifts, the fae operate at a greater remove from material reality by default and have a much more scattershot distribution of power and intelligence compared to spirits. A spirit acting out-of-sorts with its portfolio is evidence of a weird exception of its circumstances or a hidden facet of its nature, and the creatures whose Rank affords them the treatment of nobility tend to be more approachable than the beasts-and-children demographic they treat as resources, but a substantial number of fae are dissembling and/or treacherous just by virtue of being both closer to and further from humanity.

                  Spiritual resources are almost exclusively additive, which is part of why more powerful spirits tend to feed by proxy through their lessers, but pretty much every pixie, nixie, goblin and djinn can give or take goods and services of wildly varying levels of abstraction in based on criteria that only make sense to them and the Wyrd. Meanwhile, if a spirit is intelligent enough to ask you for something that's not within its immediate purviews and has no obvious relation to feeding it, the first explanation (besides the above bit about hidden variables) is that Politics is happening and it needs to blight a rival or curry favor from an ally.

                  There's also the matter of active lifespan to consider; most spirits tend to be persistent consequences of significant objects and events, but almost all fae hail from a place where dreams populate the wilderness and the rules making immediate sense is lower on the world's priorities than prices being paid and aesthetics being consistent — self-preservation is paramount in the Shadow and hit-or-miss in the Hedge.

                      Also, a spirit wants essence. That's all a spirit wants, ever- no matter how complex its plans are, they all boil down to "get essence". This makes spirits comparatively simple to deal with and approach- can you get it essence? Then it's listening. If not it's not. Likewise, if the spirit doesn't think it can gain essence by attacking you, it won't attack. Spirits have simple motivations that you can easily exploit if you know what you're doing.

                      A True Fae doesn't have that. Arguably, it wants "a story" but that is far vaguer and harder to exploit then wanting "essence", and it seems they do have other motivations and goals anyway. Changelings and goblins, of course, have all the variety of any human in what they want, fear and need. With a Fae creature it's a lot harder to come up with a "sales pitch", at least in the same generic way you can with spirits. You can't just offer glamour and call it a day.
