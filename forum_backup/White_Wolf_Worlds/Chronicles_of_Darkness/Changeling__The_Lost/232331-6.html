                    The fox doesn't care if it's this hunt or the last or the next, or which huntsman is pursuing him. He just wants to go home. Likewise, a hunter doesn't care what fox she's chasing. She just wants to get together with her friends and have a merry time. Hunting parties aren't really about the hunt so much as they are another way for humans to socialize. Well, then again, I don't hunt.

                    I dunno, I still like the prideful lightning bolt assassin who defines himself by one-shotting targets that physically more powerful comrades would need a proper brawl to lay down. He's great at work, but when he comes home, he doesn't know how to relate to the rest of his motley, or other courtiers, because he doesn't exist outside of that job. Maybe he was one of Zeus's lightning bolts in his durance.

                    As for "things being acted on", that would be the default state of an Elemental; that's what they were in Faerie. Now that they're back in the human world, they're going to figure out how to become more proactive. If you're playing a Truefriend or Romancer, you're probably going to have more social goals for your character anyway -- and heart wrenching arcs of people trying to figure out how to love and be loved are, again, a staple of stories everywhere.

                    The icy maiden keeps seeking out fiery companions, hoping for some warmth, but can never really bring herself to get close enough to melt -- and if she did, would it destroy everything she ever was? The teddy bear is overshadowed by his closest friend -- but when his friend disappears, what is he supposed to do alone? And how is he supposed to find them again? How does the guiding star feel about her friend constantly straying from her advice?

                    It's a slow journey to go from completely impersonal object to seeing oneself as human again, and getting back from one's durance is only the first step.

                                    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                    My one qualm with Elementals as “things being acted on” is that while it’s a neat Storyteller Character idea, I could never see myself playing someone who is defined by passivity. Perhaps I’m just misunderstanding the concept, though?
                                    Maybe passivity is the wrong way to go about it. It's not that they're passive, they're just not active toward the same things, and the effect they have on others just flows out from who and what they are. Take the Elemental Muse as shooting star example from earlier (because I love that one). That Elemental inspires other characters, but not from actively trying to inspire them, just from going about whatever she goes about in a manner that is very natural to her. It's just what she does. Perhaps an Elemental Seeming would tend to give a spin on a Kith related to just observing or being near the Changeling, without needing direct action on her part...? I dunno. May not be wise to generalize to that degree.

                                    For the record, I want to keep the Beast / Elemental split at present -- well, if I'm honest, I mostly want to keep some representation particular to Beasts. I'm still rolling things around, though, and I can see some persuasive bits to it. I'm hopeful I'll find a justification or rationale that satisfies me, whichever way that is. For now, they just feel too distinct to me to be happy about one category for both, even if I find trouble articulating the why of it.

                                        So, a thing to consider.

                                        We're discussing Seemings as coming out of your escape. The method of escape stains what you were, what role you played in your Durance. That's mostly just to add a little more flavor on the splat intersection, since we're going to front-load the Durance a little bit.

                                        Also, regarding the two Kiths, here's a weird thing that comes up in a lot of games. "Naturalist" character archetypes don't occur much in my games, because we tend toward heavily urban games. They certainly exist, but they're not a large percentage of the character base. When I'm in a Vampire game, I see few Gangrel, and even then, heavily urban concepts. Ventrue almost never take Animalism. When I play Werewolf, I see a disproportionate number of Iron Masters. Not that Elementals or Beasts are either anti-urban, per se. But their Kiths trend heavily toward character types who are. With both Elementals and Beasts, when I see them, their concepts both swing heavily toward rejection of the highly civilized.

                                        Again, my personal experiences here.
