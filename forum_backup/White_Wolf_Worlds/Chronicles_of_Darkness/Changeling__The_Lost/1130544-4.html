                    Not to continue the thread derail, but I think this is relevant in the sense of talking about what has changed post-Underhill:

                    A) In the Underhill version at least, you couldn't look at someone and tell what Seeming they were. That was explicitly stated. You looked at a changeling and saw a large brutish giant with fangs.

                    B) In the Underhill version (and I'm thinking this will still be around in a slightly different form) Ogre Seeming =/= large brutish giant with fangs, it equaled 'someone who used violence to escape' (The new version seems to be 'someone who was turned into a violent thing, someone who uses violence now that they have escaped, or someone who used violence to escape').

                    Between these two points, the idea that you can't play an 'Ogre' that isn't violent is flawed because an Ogre Seeming is a game mechanics concept and not an in universe one. You can be a Concept!ogre without being a Seeming!Ogre.

                                                            Originally posted by White Oak Dragon View Post
                                                            Which Seeming do you think would go best for one that just makes a desperate break for it and is lucky enough to not get caught?
                                                            The Underhill Seemings leaned on the narrative trends of ex-captives in 1e, those being that for the most part you either dramatically changed something about yourself to escape or you were let go, each of which fed into a different point of concern after your return to Earth. Something as significant to a changeling's story as their Durance doesn't end in a narratively compelling fashion with "I just made a desperate break for it and got lucky" when you're talking about escaping from a fortune-favored god-monster, at least not when looked at comprehensively.

                                                            Changeling is one of the lines that least benefits from the shallow reading of Vampire's answer to "how did you become this kind of monster?" because the process is substantially more obviously gradual and involved on account of requiring physical transportation to another world. "Second Edition's generally increased focus on how acquiring a template works and happens in-story means I can't as easily disregard backstory and treat my character as a normal human with magic powers thrown on" is not ever going to be a compelling argument for looser standards of character creation.

                                                            For that matter, could the sole changeling of a Keeper ever become a Fairest in an escape?
                                                            Changelings and Fae have never been the entire population of Arcadia by a long shot. The Underhill Fairest got out by using others, whether those others were changelings, Strangers, hobgoblins, friendly birds, or what have you.

                                                            Someone who lived a peaceful life, had a comparatively peaceful Durance, and tries to live a peaceful life afterwards is just as much an Ogre as someone who used violence for everything before, during, and after their Durance, provided that the first used violence that one time to escape.
                                                            "That one time" is a formative and character-defining moment regardless of whether it mechanically determines your seeming, because "that one time" led to you becoming a different person. It also follows from the person that you used to be most of the time.

                                                            If your character had a peaceful life and a peaceful Durance, where does it follow that they should aesthetically be calloused and lacking in outward vulnerability? What part of their Durance and their personality led them to determine that hardening their heart and mien was the best way for them to escape? Where does the need for physical insensitivity and emotional fragility come into play for such a character?

                                                            It's technically possible to make an Ogre whose background and Durance supplied no real explanation for their decision to escape through the story-beats of an Ogre's rebirth, but putting the onus on you to figure out how that works was not unique to the Underhill model of Changeling.
