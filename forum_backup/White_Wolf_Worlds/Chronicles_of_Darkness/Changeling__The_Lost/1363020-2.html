    Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post
    As a curious cat speaking; Am I right in interpreting part of the above ruminations as implying that Emanations are Titles unclaimed by a Name / Titles are Emanations claimed by a Name?
    More that any given stretch of Arcadia could, at any given point in time, be an Emanation or a Lustrum or an interface therebetween; this particular interpretation of what I refer to above as "the Arcadian map-smear" is more of a personal read than anything with especially strong support by the text.

    There's a bit from Dave on the old forums that I'm sure is quoted somewhere around here about how the "it's complicated" answer to the Arcadia Question circa Imperial Mysteries shakes out ("Fucking faeries" is the main end-punctuation I remember from it, but that's apparently not enough for Google), chiefly that the Exarchs totally cast down the Old Gods of Thistle in the time of the Celestial Ladder and that, as symbols of narrative time in a part of the cosmology where time and space are imposed by gods for their guests, this has utterly failed to stop them from carrying on as though they never left, because it happened a few dozen kalpa ago, or hasn't happened yet, or happened in a dream, or doesn't concern them because they never agreed to be invaded, et cetera.

    So sometimes actions in Faerie have consequences like you'd expect from poking around the Supernal Realms, and sometimes they don't, because Faerie is what decides (through the Fae and fundamental reciprocity) what the consequences of actions are. Sometimes a Name is a higher being that's only dragged down to Phenomenal activity when it has nothing else to support it, and sometimes a Title gets to act like a little slice of Heaven, and sometimes a Faerie's territory is part of the god-lands and sometimes it's more like an archmaster's Chantry. Everything is an extension of something else (however unfaithful it may be), and the hierarchy of those elements is in flux based on laws you need divinity-class insight to parse through on a human timescale.

    What I hope has been clear from this thread, mainly, is that "the 1e Arcadias are the same place" does not imply "the 1e Arcadias work the same way at all levels."

            So it turns out "Bloody Faeries" were the actual magic words:

            Originally posted by DaveB
            Okay, so. Bear with me here.

            I'm going to explain how emanations and supernal symbols work. I'm going to use Arcadia as an entirely hypothetical example. The example is not canon. But you'll get why.

            So.

            Here's how reality forms.

            first we have raw, undifferentiated potential. This might be the supernal without a filter, or it might bete theoretical Empyrean.

            Then we have the Supernal, which is made up of every possible Truth, expressed as symbols.

            Then we have Emanations, which are more solid, and based around particular symbols.

            (Then the Abyss cracks reality in half, introducing Lies to mis it up a little)

            Then the Phenomenal world.


            When Archmages enter the Supernal, they become symbols. When they interact with the other symbols, they can change them, often without meaning to. Everything below the Supernal is defined by it.

            Gods are just symbols of self-aware things; they think because they should think.

            So, in our example. We have a group of Supernal symbols that we'll call "The Old Gods of Thistle". Because they exist, an Emanation Realm we'll call "Faerie" also exists "below" them. Whenever they're redefined, Faerie will change to suit.

            So our Exarch-symbols stomp in and kill them all. Faerie ceases to exist. But wait! We clap our hands, and remember that these Old Gods of Thistle symbols have an optional at best relationship to time. So the Exarchs have/will/will have been/whenfore kill/ing/ed them. So what? They keep coming back, and they've never been away.

            And Faerie keeps coming back, and has never been away.

            Our bullying Exarch-symbols exclaim "fuck it", and head back/will head back/have headed back to the symbols of things they understand, like "important people drive history" and "everything falls apart". You know. Symbols humans can understand. The four-fifths of the Supernal that has the good grace to be conquered after they've conquered it, rather than before or sideways, is good enough. There are even an infinite number of time-symbols that place themselves after the event. Good solid Time- and Fate- symbols that a person can get their head around. Not these stories.

            When someone becomes an archmage, they gather up the symbols of the things that were attracted to their Mystery Play and turn them into a Cintamani, which is Archmage-speak for "the symbol of 'I'm an archmage'". This means that everything made by those symbols gets altered, which makes new archmages cry until they become numbed to the transhuman gnostic horror of their existence (tm). But it's okay, because they're now an archmage. They're a archmage because the Supernal says they're an archmage.

            The Golden Road Ascension is when an Archmage then goes "so... If I were to Be an Archmage back down below, and, say, convince the two Wings that they're same order and that magic is alive, then return to the supernal, I'll have attracted the symbols of the two orders and magic's not-aliveness toward the symbol of "I'm totally an archmaster". I can then use the same trick as in my Threshold to hammer them into shape. Back down below will conform, I'll always have been right and if I do it enough times, maybe I can make a symbol of "The Mysterium ( ps I'm Ascended)" out of them all.

            But I digress.

            Whenever the Exarchs attack the Old Gods (and again, because of Time they're always and never attacking them now, tommorow and yesterday). The Old Gods get changed in the struggle, and Faerie gets changed as the Supernal symbols of Faerie alter. To make matters worse, when they sometimes/always/sometime/never win, te Exarchs kick Gods out down into the horrid realms of solidity and such. Sometimes, the Old Gods land in Faerie and go native, accepting a bit more reality. And because Time, they're constantly doing so.

            Is Changeling Arcadia...

            1) The "Faerie" Emanation, occupied by continously exiled story-beings, with the "Wyld" being the Supernal?
            2) The conglomerate of "Faerie" symbols in the Supernal, with the Wyld being the Empyrean?
            3) A combination of the two - it IS possible to get from an Emanation to the supernal, after all. Arcadia is the border between them, the Emanation meeting the symbol-beings?
            4) Lots of Emanations, one for each story-creature, with easy routes between them?

            Because Time, it's all four at once. And never. Whenever you walk the Hedge, be careful, for you don't know which Hedge you're walking. And none of them lead anywhere good for a human, with their quaint notions of cause and effect, should go. And because the story-beings find notions like "we should stick with one version of ourselves" incomprehensible, they're interacting with one another! Option 1 above lives quite happily overlaid over Options 2to 3, and multiple versions of the same story-being exist in the same metaphysical space.

            And this is why Arcadia is unknowable. It's a great big smudge on your map, thanks to Time not really existing in that "part" of the Supernal. The titanomachy, the Ascension War and the end of the world are all happening "at once". Everything that's ever happened, or ever will, to Arcadia already exists and has ended in Arcadia. The Old Gods of Thistle are incapable of interacting with linear time properly, because they ARE narrative time. If they could be MADE to, if someone managed to Ascend redefining the entire mess as being in the "past" and have that mean something, then they'd instantly have been killed. Good luck Sealing an Omen that says all stories have already ended, though.

            What a Changeling experiences is well-described. What that means, though, from an Archmaster's point of view is "system error. Does not parse".

            There is no answer to the Arcadia question.
            The answer to the Arcadia question is "yes/no/fnord/it was a dark and stormy night".

            Bloody Faeries.

                I feel as though a lot of that stuff Dave is saying comes from a good deal of time before the Second Edition Awakening corebook, with its whole line about the incredible complexity and weirdness of the cosmology and the notion that the Fallen World can consist of things other than humans that rise up to insinuate themselves in the Supernal.

                And doesn't Lost Second Edition have allusions to the idea that the True Fae are invaders of the space called Arcadia?

                Actually, on the note of the naming thing, it's kind of funny how To The Strongest portrays Greek mages as applying the name Arcadia to refer to the domain of their mythic prehistory and not to what is perceived by those Awakened to the Acanthos Path (I believe that book presented the idea that at this point mages don't quite regard things in terms of separate Supernal Worlds yet?). Like, Awakening Second Edition on the whole seems to present things in terms of names being something that the academically inclined willworkers came up with, rather than innate and essential. They call one of the Realms Arcadia for cultural and etymological reasons based in origins in people who used that name to refer to something else, without even proposing that those were the same thing. Changelings and the True Fae in that context can easily have their own reason to call their particular corner of the cosmos Arcadia (and presumably not even for every culture, I probably want to check out the Dark Eras Changeling stuff).

                Ultimately, I kind of feel like positing the True Fae as Supernal Gods demeans them both. That the Others are more interesting as something else, and the Gods are more interesting if they don't need to directly account for every thing in the cosmos. Plus an idea that Anachronisms and Moirae and the Gods above them (not to mention Medreut and Glorianna) possibly adopting those forms largely to be able to approach mages on levels that will mean something to them (which is still operating on a separate principle to what I understand of Fae titles).

                Since Pangaens were brought into this conversation at some point, my take on them is that they and the Border Marches are the more natural intermediary beings and world of flesh and spirit, from a time when those qualities were a spectrum rather than a dichotomy. They were like Harmony 5 werewolves writ large, complete with how the Gauntlet between forces the survivors to shift to the extremes of one direction or the other. In that perspective, I'd propose that their capacity to use Arcana is less about having some direct relation to Supernal Gods and more about how the flesh side of them gets to have an awareness of the depths of reality on a similar level to how humans that could forge Cintamani could. If Pangeans could theoretically have still existed into the modern day, I could think that the growing sophistication of the Lie over eons more greatly excludes anything but humans from perception of the Supernal.

                EDIT: Also, doesn't Second Edition Changeling move away from the notion of Fae as beings of narrative logic and more into Wyrd as a force of reciprocity? I feel that's another point of growing separation from the Acanthus Path, which more strongly than ever emphasizes a notion of being driven by a narrative logic, of the sequence of events being rich with meaning, where the Fae engage in terms of what can be offered and acquired in exchange (which I'd say has implications if their homeland is somewhere in the cosmology on a kind of tangent from the Astral).

                                            Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post

                                            It is a long-standing in-joke that the modern incarnation of the Aeon of the Abyss uses a walking stick made of a child's spinal column. Nothing more Paradoxical than the Saint of Paradox taking a shape from the wrong World of Darkness.


                                            In any case…

                                            The neck of the cosmological woods that Mage, Changeling, and Mummy place the gods and/or the seat of destiny in is territory where identity gets weird. Changelings and the Arisen get different tastes of the knock-on effects of that through the difficulties of Clarity and Memory, while the Awakened largely encounter it as a thing that makes building a coherent model more difficult, but the point is that at the level where a being is using world-altering magic on a regular basis, the interconnected nature of all things gets really obvious.

                                            The True Fae are immortal. Period. They vastly prefer some forms of immortality to others, because those forms come with the ability to have preferences, but a Name is an expression of a piece of Faerie, expressed further through Titles and re-expressed in memory and Legend. The Wyrded frame is robust, because it is built on everything being tied together in ways that catch the bits that break off or die for long enough that they're revitalized or claimed anew.

                                            Supernal gods like the Exarchs are immortal. Period. The General isn't just the manifestation of rule by force, or the concept of it, or the spirit of it, she is Rule By Force, and all other expressions of that fact — even the ones that aren't a direct extension of his will like an ochema — are extensions of the thing that cannot die. Tyranny has its price, as far as keeping count of Ministers over time goes, but even at the level below "dwells in Heaven on a permanent basis and wields massive deific power over legions of servitors" the Awakened view of the cosmology gives us archmasters making subsouls that work like familiars work for Travelers. The fungibility of the division between souls and soul-adjacent phenomena like ephemeral beings is well-documented even before getting into the part where the non-Supernal realm with the best view of the descriptive heart of the Tapestry and the gulf of unreality that cuts it off is the one where everything you can interact with is explicitly a smaller part of a larger world-mind.

                                            Sutek, the Judges, and their parents… look, you get the idea. It's hard to be more explicit about "everything is an extension of something else" than a cosmogony that begins with the forces of Coherent Existence, Creation of Meaning, and Destruction of Form building Purposes #1-42 and Free Will, who then make the world and mangle the body of Free Will into suffusing all of existence (and all will aspire and rebel) while Purpose tries to bribe Destruction away from the world after Free Will got the final say-so from Creation on what they can and can't do. Dreams of Avarice is in many ways an extended reading session of "You are four thousand different gods, some of whom are more directly each other than others, but most importantly, by having free will you are Free Will, Sutek the Unjudged, and the Judges of Life and Death cannot take away your self-determination even though they made Azar and the rest of the gods out of his mutilated body." Getting free from the service of the Judges is a process that includes wresting control of your Name from them, which lets you reclaim the bits of your memory that were otherwise gated behind the event of journeying to another world between lives (sound familiar?). Theoretically someday the world will end for good and Ammut will bite down on the rock of Creation (and all will be still and silent once more), but until then, the fundamental forces of the universe will keep on trucking and the forty-three great gods who made humanity and its place therein will be there shoving things along by their existence and diverse manifestations.

                                            Bonus round: the God-Machine, while largely a Phenomenal entity, is theoretically immortal. It has no heart and lives in all its work, be that the literal gears under the city oiled by the blood of innocents or the images that burns themselves into your mind so deeply you grow circuitboard tattoos on the inside of your wrists. Light of the Sun pegs the Awakened magic for interacting with it and its creations as Prime and Fate, the Subtle Arcana corresponding to Mummy's Pillars of the soul called the Essence and the Name. (For contrast, because Faerie's difficulties with essential nature are well documented, note that the Arcana for dealing with fae stuff are Fate with a side of Mind — the Arcanum corresponding to the Pillar of the Spirit [confusing, I know, given that the Spirit Arcanum is a different thing] that governs perception and drive, which combines some sort of way with the Name's application to knowledge and identity.) The Saboteurs' perennial work of dismantling the Machine is complicated in no small part by its getting into the systems that keep the world intact and coherent and actively confounding attempts to separate the two cleanly.

                                            What I'm getting at is that things get messy when god-magic is involved, particularly when it comes to who represents what and where the consequences lie — oftentimes what you're dealing with isn't even the god so much as the line of thought the god was entertaining while it was in an especially sapient mood on a geographic/conceptual timescale. Passions stir the divine pantheons to action; not for nothing are some of the most powerful beings in an individual soul the drive for self-improvement and the myriad personifications of flaws and weakness, and that echoes up the Astral in the constant ideological warfare of the Temenos, the vicious turning of the wheel of consumption by the Ecstatic Wind, and the petty diversions of the Old Gods.

                                            There's a bit from Malcolm Sheppard that hits on this (talking about the Aeons here, but again, the Aeons occupy a cosmological niche extremely close to Heaven) that's worth quoting especially as it relates to the Fae's limitations:

                                            Medraut is the Aeon of Fate in the same way that you're a forum poster. You're probably not just a forum poster, since you're a person with other interests and a body and such, but as far as I see you, when I visit this forum, that's what you are. This is true for all of the Aeons as I conceived of them when I invented them. They are ineffable and probably cannot be understood as beings of history or even discrete identity, but if they did not simulate these things, they would be incomprehensible or comprehensible in a way that made your soul explode. But this comprehensibility requirement is a constraint. Just as you, a forum user, cannot make grape jelly ooze out of my screen with your post, and you can be responded to by other users and even constrained by moderation, Medraut existence as far as the people he allows to see him is constrained by various narrative and metaphysical ideas that can be somewhat grasped.

                                            I got a bit rambly here, but hopefully that helps clarify things at least somewhat, or at minimum gives some more stuff to chew over.
