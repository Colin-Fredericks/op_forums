    It has always seemed to me that if there are enough supernaturals in a given place, they will inevitably run into one another. They might not understand much about each other, but glimpsing each other's otherworldly aspects becomes more likely with every meeting. Some groups could choose to stay away from each other, others might have truces, and yet others could try to kill each other on sight. Part of that will have to do with the history and rumors of the area.

    Depending on their current standing, supernatural groups do not necessarily have to be automatically antagonistic to each other. It depends on threats, resources, leadership, and the like. The Lord Sages of the Unknown Reaches are already in the books to act as a possible bridge between splats, but any cross-splat group would have would likely confer mostly social and knowledge-based benefits.

    Since you want this to happen, I suggest you go by splat and determine a few things:
    Population: Who's left of that type in the area?
    Recent threats: What's hurt them lately?
    Which other supernatural types they know of: Do they already know of other splats?
    Attitude toward other supernaturals: For whichever splat they know well enough to form an opinion about, give them a default attitude as per Damnation City pg. 156. Future meetings/deals/conflicts will modify that rating up or down the scale, depending on how they work out.

    This will set up the default way that the supernaturals talk about and treat each other, which will influence the player characters - but it won't (and shouldn't) define how the PCs ultimately feel toward other types. Their own experiences will determine that, and if they decide they hate the way the freehold bullies everyone into joining and decide to work against it, that is their prerogative.

        My thoughts for general trends:

        Vampires: Changelings and vampires are not likely to get along.

        This is just a flat statement of fact. Vampires are controlling, inhuman and dangerous in a way that is likely to evoke memories of the Gentry, but without the world-shaping powers that keeping changelings from killing the Gentry. On the flip side, changelings have hallucinogenic blood and lethal powers. If a vampire makes a pact with a local freehold, it will be entirely on an individual level, and there's no real "this is how it's likely to shake out" that can be drawn up.

        Werewolves: Changelings and the Forsaken are much more likely to get along, from what I know of Werewolf. Both have similar goals - protecting themselves and their people from dangerous creatures that live outside the world - and both tend to place strong cultural emphasis on honor and loyalty. I can see information-sharing pacts between urban changelings and rural werewolves going a long way, with both sides calling the other in when particularly large problems start to form, and a werewolf pack could attach itself to just about any Court (except maybe Winter) without too much trouble.

        Mages: Other people covered this pretty well, I think!

        Prometheans: As I understand it, changelings have no resistance to the Wasteland, and Prometheans are uncomfortably reminiscent of fetches. I can't see it ending well.

        Hunters: In theory, a moral freehold would be strong allies to a hunter cell; both sides are alert for dangerous monsters, and changelings know better than most people how dangerous the Wyrd can be. Plus, a changeling without magic isn't generally in trouble inherently (there's no real penalty for being out of Glamour), and they don't hurt the people that they draw Glamour from, so they are a "safe" supernatural group.

        In practice, it's going to depend heavily on the conspiracy and the freehold. If the local changelings are basically benign, they could make common cause with TFV, or the Loyalists of Thule, or the Union. They probably get along pretty well with the Lucifuge, who understand what it is to be both mortal and supernatural at once. Maybe they could work with the Malleus Maleficarnum, if they can prove they are victims of the supernatural, rather than agents, or the Agnis Kai Doru could make common cause with a more reactionary, Bridge-Burning freehold.

        If the freehold is actively meddling and doing damage, that's less likely. If the Hunters are determined to drag them out into the light (a la Network Zero), it's equally unlikely. If it's the Cheiron Group, not a chance.

                                    the basic premis could work but there would be a lot of "if"'s involved.

                                    if the setting is a major city there would be a sizable vampire population to contend with. or if not something really really bad happened to them and the survivors have no choice but to swear fealty to the freehold. Perhaps a True Fae came in a mopped the floor with the blood suckers, (considereing the True Fae stats and basic Invulnerablity even in the material world thats not entirely unfeasable). if the setting is a small town, there would be a substantially smaller vampire population to begin with and making nice with the local fae courts is just eaiser than dealing with a city full of competition for blood.

                                    werewolves are a bit trickier. despite apperances, the Lost and the Uratha are not friends. Changelings are otherworldly weirdos who eat emotions and Uratha are tempremental furry death machines. Both sides are relentless hunted and thus prone to paranoia. furthermore the Uratha deal primarily with spirits, not True Fae, both are very alien and almost unknowable to the other. if the changeling courts had something to offer the Uratha such as tactical advantage over the pure by using guides throw the hedge to set up ambushes, supply of goblin fruits, and other such in exchange for having that furry death rage pointed toward the gentry on occasion, even better. it probly would cover more along the lines of alliance between sovereign states rather than fealty of vassal to liege.

                                    Mages are the easiest to incorporate, just have one survivor of going into the hedge and seeing what a horrible idea it really really is be in charge of his lessers and you solved the "what you've been to Arcadia can you take me?!" dilemma. haveing a a free hold at your back when the servants of the exachs come calling is going to come in handy.

                                    Prometheans, i'd say are probobly better off not being included in this grand expirement on the account of the disquiet which i don't know if its turned off in the hedge or not (if not definiitly don't use them that place is scary enough and as hollows are by nature apart of the hedge...) furthermore the promethan pilgrimage is designed to make the character human, and chagnelings for all their sad stories and memories are most certainly not. let the poor guy stash his stuff there as he moves on in exchange for comeing to defend the freehold when called upon. keeps them mobile and solves any and all problems with the disquiet.

                                    fetches are not created by changelings and are not by nature friendly to changelings. they are artifical simulacrum that are designed to steal a changeling's identity. the only soul they have is a maybe a little bit of the changeling's that their keeper scrapped off the thorns and stuffed into a scarecrow made of dried leaves and rabbit organs. if you would like the other critters to be a part of this that's cool, but Fetches are way too dangerous by nature for a freehold to keep around and a really, really painful insult to changelings that they are unlikely to forgive.

                                    likewise don't try to throw hunters into the mix, this place is already a powder keg just to due to being a motley crux of the various wod critters put together under the same roof and answering to the maddest of all their hatters. throw in a person who's sole purpose in life is to kill these things and it will just explode in your face.

                                    a giest should be fine here and there, especially when paired up with gravewrights. there shouldn't be too much to get involved so long as both parties agree as to what's what and how things should be done.

                                    just remember if your going to use these things in your chronicle don't gloss over what they are just for the novelty of having them there, a vampire is fundamentally a vampire and werewolf is a nine foot tall hulking engine of furry Apocalypse (pun totally intended). a powerful free hold is could make it work but they are going to have to step EXTRA lively if they want to keep the whole thing from blowing up in everyboies faces. have fun.

                                        Well, a few thoughts -

                                        1) One, weaken the Veil between supernaturals. A vampire can recognize a changeling on sight, as he would another vampire. Changelings can see Vampire, etc writ all over him. Same with mages, prommies, demons, whatever. This makes interaction between various races easier. Strengthening the Mask, Obfuscate 2, aura manipulation, etc would hide the supernatural in question, but its a deliberate act on the part of the supernatural (except demons, but they specialize in looking like humans).

                                        2) Vampires tend to be looked down upon by most everyone, and not because they're weak, but because I find that they're the most evil of splats - they're cannibalistic by nature, something that most other groups have rules against - killing/eating people for mana/essence is bad. Enslaving people is bad. Blood magics to live forever is bad. Vampires keep herds, brainwash people, and often inflict pain and addiction as the Beast rides their emotion. To a changeling's perspective, this very Gentry-like behavior. There should be a good set up to keep vampires in check. Maybe they're a guilty pleasure for some 'lings, or similar. Maybe there's corrupt 'lings in power who trade with the vampires.

                                        Mummies are probably the only splat that can match Kindred for being a highly evil splat. You're a servant to an evil necromancer who feeds off suffering and wants to take over the world, and pushes you in that direction. Your magic involves working with the dead and pain, and you, by your very nature and presence, brainwash cultists. A lot of the same problem with vampires, but coupled with religious zealotry. If vampires would be a racial equivalent to privateers, then the mummies would be loyalists.

                                        3) Werewolves, Sin-Eaters, and Unchained tend to be focused on their spiritual antagonists. As long as they can do their job, and don't ruin territories, I suspect that there's no reason for them to not get along, and more than a few reasons to do get along (pledges for power not-with-standing). There's a bit of an issue with Werewolves and spirits, and how it interacts with some Contracts (notably, things like Contracts of Communion) and goblin vows, which could also be interesting. Darklings and some autumn get along with ghosts, so there's already precedent for that.

                                        4) Demons... demons probably just hide from 'lings as it is, or make deals. Really, so could angels, so its problematic for demons in any case. Demons, due to how the war against their angelic opponents run, tend to need to have Covers that involve illegal and criminal activities, including gun running and human trafficking. Idealistic demons don't last, and they all do some very nasty things in the name of their war against God. That said, its not so different from the Changeling's wars against the Others, so there is a lot to hold in common there, once you get past different ideas of what hell means to each other, and that the big difference between God and Gentry is that one is emotionless, and the other is highly emotional. Both are still psychopathic powerful entities that can't empathize with humans and see them as toys/parts.

                                        5) Mages and hunters are unique of races, in that they actively want to seek out other supernaturals. Mages to study so they can gain Arcane Experience from mysteries, hunters because they have a whole competition with keeping supernaturals from preying on humans - hunters and summers would get along, I suspect, while the more extreme hunters would more likely work with bridge burners.

                                        6) Prometheans are rare as it is, encourage being abused, and run away. I can't see any of them wanting to stick around for long in any case, given the whole Pilgrimage, so discussing them, without first talking about rule changes for crossover, is an issue.

                                        7) The Chicago has a small paragraph or three about what would happen if one supernatural ruled over others (two others, point in case). A vampire court dominating werewolves and mages, for instance, would have spirits chained in Elysium as guardians, and mage spells protecting the prince and his favorites. Think about what each supernatural could bring or benefit the Fae with. Spirits, magic relics/spells, trade in the taboo, communing with lost relatives, gadgets, etc. Giving each a subservient role (at least on the surface) in the city can help flesh your city out, and why the races in question just haven't been entirely driven out, or whatever.

                                        8) Have fun! Sounds like a blast!

                                            The first thought that comes to mind is that these changelings would probably do extremely well at goblin markets, and may even have an ongoing or regularly recurring goblin market in the freehold. Vampire blood, werewolf fangs and mage tears would all probably be very valuable at the market, to say nothing of more esoteric energies like raw plasm or bottled pyros. Of course, this wouldn't all be given to changelings for free but it would definitely give the town a lively magical economy. as for specifics:

                                            I don't know vampire too well, but I suspect that there would be a bit of friction with the more ambitious vampires who wish they could have more power and the changelings who know vampires can sometimes get a little… fae-like. that said, I think the ordo dracul would get on pretty well with the autumn court, and possibly the circle of the crone. the carthians would probably also appreciate the novelty of the changeling power structure, though honestly I'm not sure the carthians and invictus would really feature too prominently in the game since they're very much political entities and the changelings are the ones who hold the power.

                                            Werewolves I see falling into a sort of symbiotic relationship with the changelings. the emotion influencing powers of changelings and the way they feed can probably mess with the spiritual side of things sometimes but I imagine if they consult the werewolves they could turn those same habits toward making a healthier spiritual environment. in return, we have werewolves patrolling the freehold. having werewolves as backup when a bunch of privateers or even a fairy appears is in no way a bad thing.

                                            Mages, once they get past the "can I go to arcadia" stage and changelings get over any lingering suspicions they may have toward mages (depending on how mages act and are viewed the way you run them), would have a blast. The hedge, in small doses and with a changeling guide, is like a theme park for mages, particularly if they can go to some of the more mysterious places there. I cross mage and changeling plots a lot in my games (even had a mage who was taken by a keeper and awakened in arcadia) and once a mutual trust is established their strengths compliment each other extremely well and on a thematic level they benefit from being two of the most overtly fantastic things in the world of darkness.

                                            Prometheans would probably need to live on the fringes of society, though that's nothing new to them. They'd be able to work with the freehold and they may even be afforded some hollows in return where they can take up permanent residence without causing a wasteland, which would probably be a very helpful step toward humanity for them. I also like to imagine the wyrd and the principal as being great cosmological entities pressing against each other, a little bit like the triat from old world of darkness, so that can possibly be worth a couple plot hooks.

                                            Sin eaters would probably get on just fine with the changelings, they'd probably have a very similar deal as with werewolves, only with ghosts. gravewrights and the contracts of shade and spirit would probably synergize really well with the sin eaters. also, I don't recall for sure whether the hedge has a twilight side and if that forces ghosts and spirits that enter it to manifest, but the geists might appreciate having the option to interact with at least that part of the world directly rather than vicariously through the sin eater.

                                            Mummies I can't think of too much for, save for the entertaining confusion that would spring from them trying to figure out tokens. The local mummy would probably be the one the changelings give any props they recover from arcadia, seeing as he's sitting on a bunch of powerful magical artifacts to guard anyway and has a cult devoted to keeping them safe. you might also be able to explore some interesting interactions between the changeling's understanding of the Wyrd and a mummy's understanding of Fate.

                                            Demons would probably take a little while to get used to the idea that changelings can bind an agreement without writing it down. changelings would probably take a while to get used to demons being able to have as many contracts as they want (and, if they find out, the idea that demons can obliterate a soul with one). Probably the thing demons would find most interesting about changelings is the idea of a freehold. They have free agencies of their own, which actually seem very similar to freeholds thematically, but the interesting thing about how changelings do it is that they have an ordered system that, by the nature of its organization, achieves a specific effect (making it harder for fairies to enter and stay in your domain). This would probably look a lot like infrastructure to a demon, and more to the point it's an infrastructure that pushes back at the godly entities that were once the changeling's masters. I figure a lot of demons would take interest in that idea, perhaps exploring the possibility of adapting the system for use against the god machine and creating a comfortable little slice of hell for themselves. Also, I tend to see the god machine and the wyrd coming into conflict with each other (and the principal) like I mentioned above.

                                            as for the Z-splat thing, the differing flavor texts behind all the different Z-splats makes it hard to imagine all of them being part of one organization but I could easily see them being formed in concert with one another and being tied closely enough together that they essentially function as the same organization. Actually that would probably be pretty awesome, and any hunter conspiracy that forms from that union would probably get some really interesting options considering how many sources it would have to draw from.
