    Originally posted by Anteros View Post
    What is exactly the distinction between Mien and Mask? Does the Mask merely hides the supernatural parts of the changeling's appearance, or is it a completely different visage? For example, if a Changeling with Many Masks should change their facial features or sex, would their Mien be affected by those changes as well? or would the changeling's Mask and Mien have completely different features or sexes?
    Per the description of Mask and Mien in the Chapter Two and the description of Manymask, your Mask conforms more or less to the height and build of your mien, which starts out mostly human with vestiges of your durance and adjustments based on court affiliation, Contract knowledge, and other history at low Wyrd; below Wyrd 5, a changeling with Manymask may gradually shift cosmetic details of their Mask (which, aside from facial structure, are mostly matters of color or pattern).

    The prominence of supernatural features in a changeling's mien grow with their Wyrd, and accordingly their Mask grows further apart from it or harder to be covered up by it.

        Originally posted by Anteros View Post
        What is exactly the distinction between Mien and Mask? Does the Mask merely hides the supernatural parts of the changeling's appearance, or is it a completely different visage? For example, if a Changeling with Many Masks should change their facial features or sex, would their Mien be affected by those changes as well? or would the changeling's Mask and Mien have completely different features or sexes?
        Look at it from the other direction. The Mask is how you used to look as a human, with some minor influences of your Mien.
        The Mien, on the other hand, is how you look like now, which can be completely independent from your old human form. I.e. it can be really similar to the Mask (maybe it's basically the same but with antlers and hooves), or it could be so altered that it's impossible to determine what the Mask would look like (a stylised mannekin with a blank face, for example, or a completely new face). Aside from having roughly the same height and build, it's up to the player's preferences how much the Mask and the Mien intersects.
        Manymasks is an exception to how the Mask usually works since it allows you to take on completely new features instead of being permanently roughly how you looked like before your Durance. If planning a character with the Manymasks Merit I'd simply not have the Mien be similar to the Mask.

            I tend to be pretty direct when it comes to the relationship between Mask and Mein. Outside of Contracts or Merits, even big and small changelings are just Giant or Small Framed--bigger or smaller is a Contract, or an easy way to tell Hobgoblins from the Lost. I am part of the camp that says that the mask merely hides the supernatural elements of the Mein. For example, in my original time period of 1999, weirdly colored hair or eyes persisted in the Mask--("wow, you must spend a lot of money on hair dye", "doesn't it hurt to wear contact lenses all the time like that?"). Meanwhile, in the 1912 portion of the same universe, oddly colored hair or eyes are entirely mundanized. The Mask might allow for blue hair in modern times, but not back then. Similarly, in 1912, one of the characters has azure skin. In 1999, I might've had him look African, or perhaps even have that chemical imbalance to make him blue. But in 1912, he just looks tan.

            Aside from features from contracts or merits, I tend to go with the idea of describing it loosely, because the mask doesn't work too hard. "Her youthful/ancient eyes stared upon you" fits for both mask and Mein, and is thus appropriate. "He ran his finger through his shaggy hair and scratched his furry chest". It's all about metaphors and similes. Most of what you see is what you get, even if the Mein makes it more Wyrd and can be extrapolated to supernatural levels. "He pulled his fiery hair from his face" works for both, even if the humans see bright red and the fae see actual fire.

            It also serves as a good character development trait when it comes to the features that CANT be translated, like a satyr always being barefoot (because shoes are uncomfortable on hooves, especially with such thick toenails if you don't file them down) or wings or extra arms. Having to explain to your lover why your bangs are always pointed upwards, even after sleeping, because you have antlers is a good story point. So is the weirdness you feel if your mask just makes your antlers Invisible and your lover can't even feel them. Having vestigal arms coming out of your back is scary and weird, but do is the fact that things move around you that humans couldn't see you touch, both because you have four arms.

            A big note, however, is that the Gentry don't much care about your face. They may want you to be prettier or uglier, they may have a certain look or theme in mind for their mutant offspring, but rarely would a Faerie even notice what you specifically look like to a human degree. That's why the mask and Mein rarely diverge too much. The facial structure you had to start with may be morphed, but the Gentry don't usually pay close enough attention to the majority of the details about you. Some will ("I don't like that mole", "your eyes need to be bigger to see with"). But everything outside of those whims will be something the Gentry literally don't even see.

                                Common-sense reading based on how the game works.

                                If an average professional in a field has two to three dots in each of the Attributes and Skills necessary to do the thing they do, it is unlikely for anything but the absolute worst-case scenario to bring them down to a situation where a dramatic failure will happen without the player upgrading a normal failure.

                                If a completely talentless novice in a field has no Skill dots and minimal Attribute ratings for the task to which those things apply, no amount of good gear and lucky happenstance is going to let them have better odds of success than the aforementioned average professional working under normal circumstances, because past a certain level of bonus it's stopped being the character doing the thing.

                                On a practical level, it also stops the number of dice on a single roll from reaching "bring a bucket" without a lot of luck and a specific physical approach to the X-Again, rote, and Advanced Action qualities. Most characters will not roll more than ten to fifteen dice on the thing they're good at.

                                        In 1e, there were explicit modifier caps. In CofD, each modifier "usually" ranges from -3 to +3 and is capped at -5 to +5. However, there is nothing about total modifiers being capped. Indeed, there are examples of situational modifiers being at 5 and equipment modifiers being at 5 and said example makes no mention that you are limited to +5 total between them.

                                        RAW, I see no support for a total modifier cap, only a per-modifier cap.

                                        The Changeling book makes this a bit fuzzier by rewording the standard text as it exists in core and the other books, but looking at the other books makes it pretty clear to me.

                                                            12-27-2020, 06:05 AM
                                                            Originally posted by moonwhisper View Post
                                                            I have a question about the Comatose Condition. From what I understand:
                                                            - If your Clarity track is filled and you take mild damage, you gain the Comatose Condition.
                                                            - If your Clarity track is filled and you take severe damage, you get the Persistent version instead.

                                                            Does this imply that the non-Persistent version is temporary? Usually, non-Persistent Conditions are meant to be resolved within the session, and may be removed if no longer relevant (and the ST may choose do grant the beat or not). Does this apply to the mild Comatose as well? Can the Condition "fade away" after some time, without being resolved? What happens to the Clarity damage then?

                                                            To put it in another way: why isn't the mild version Persistent as well?
                                                            While Persistent Conditions are supposed to be lasting, in terms of rules they only differ from regular Conditions in two ways: They take more effort to resolve, and they grant one beat per session if they impact the character's life.
                                                            The regular version of Comatose can be resolved the very same scene with no external input. The Persistent version requires external help, thus requiring more effort than the regular version.

                                                            Conditions can fade because of two reasons: They have a timer, or they stop being relevant to the character. Technically both regular and Persistent Conditions can fade, but Persistent Conditions will rarely, if ever, do so because they're supposed to represent lasting issues or circumstances.
                                                            In practice, however, neither version of Comatose will ever fade because being comatose should never stop being relevant.
