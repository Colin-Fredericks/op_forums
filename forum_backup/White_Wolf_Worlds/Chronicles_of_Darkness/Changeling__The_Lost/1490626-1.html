            Originally posted by Madhatter View Post
            I am very curious as to what the books explores, besides kiths. As the title includes Kin, does it introduce fae blooded or explore the fae touched in greater detail?
            It's the Changeling 2e Player's Guide, much as Flowers of Hell was Demon: the Descent's Player's Guide.

            There's a bunch of new Contracts, a bunch of new types of pledge (including a specifically-magically-oriented modification of bargains that lets you allow someone to see through the Mask, a means of anteing one of your possessions to make pursuing an Aspiration more rewarding, establishing mystically-enforced rules for an event, and straight-up cursing people), discussions of some new notable subsections of the Hedge (the BriarNet gets a proper address here, and the Wishing Roads are introduced as a set of trods that connect both space and abandoned hopes/dreams/wishes), a deeper look at Regalia (including a system for manifesting one of your Regalia onto a powerful token or stranger things), and a discussion of the aforementioned dramaturgy system (which interacts with Loopholes, temporary mystical identity theft, and basically the entire framework in which hobgoblins do their work).

            There's also a fairly in-depth discussion of changeling psychology (including variant Touchstone mechanics and a couple of alternatives to Comatose for characters who hit Clarity 0), a lengthy discussion of motleys, and a number of new safety tools.

            On the "Kith" end of things, there's also mechanics for being kithless (three different ways, the more unusual pair of which come with their own interesting drawbacks, while the standard means offers what I would consider a reasonable tradeoff for kithlessness in the form of a kind of broad pseudo-kith-blessing) and acquiring a kith (also three different ways, the least involved of which is "getting a secondhand kith from a hobgoblin who has one available, which might come with leftover Goblin Debt whose creditor you'll need to figure out," while the other two involve incorporating another changeling's Icon or undertaking an ordeal with a Condition not unlike the Founder's Mark one takes upon trying to forge a court).

            It's a hundred and forty-odd pages of New Material, a good chunk of which is new kiths because the Kickstarter had multiple stretch goals dedicated to adding more kiths to the book. It's sufficiently full of Content that all I'm really still waiting for to be confident I can finish getting setup ready for transitioning my Changeling playtest game to 2e proper (besides player schedule alignment) is to see what material The Hedge will contain for consideration.
