                        Apologies for the late arrival, some journals were delayed. It’s time to cover our big downtime session. This session was a shorter run, as Gawain and Ace’s players could not make due to holiday plans. We only had Calamity as we moved the game (Gawain and Ace would have missed regardless).                         Session XX

                        Lorelei’s Diary

                        Wow, I have really let the journaling slide lately, haven’t I? Checking the last entry, it looks like it’s been close to three weeks since I last wrote anything in this diary. And I don’t even have the excuse that nothing much has been happening. Okay, we haven’t had any more deadly explosive firefights with insane fetches – I haven’t even had to use my healing powers at all – but that doesn’t mean things haven’t been eventful nonetheless.

                        In the wake of our brief dalliance, Princess Jolie seems to be playing the field. Okay, I don’t know that she’s been sleeping around, exactly, but she’s definitely stepped up her dating schedule. She’s been out with any number of the Freehold’s eligible singles, both male and female. In fact, Insincere Marguerite approached me the other day to ask if it would be okay with me if she went out with the Princess. I know that I don’t have any room to complain about Jolie’s dating habits considering that I turned her down when she flat-out asked me to be her girlfriend a few weeks ago, but I confess that our fling feels less special now that she’s going out with so many other people.

                        Of course I told Marguerite to go for it. Who am I to stand in the way of true lust?

                        Speaking of the L-word (well, sorta), things are still going just swimmingly with D. We had one of those important talks the other day, ushering in a new phase in our relationship. I took her out to dinner and brought her back to the Dream House as per usual, but as we were sitting on my bed – our bed – before things got too intense, I handed her a small box tied up with a red ribbon.

                        She got this sort of deer-in-headlights look on her face and I had to laugh. “Jesus, D,” I said with a grin, “I love you, girl, but it’s only been about a month. It’s not a ring, so you can stop looking so terrified.”

                        She chuckled and unwrapped the box. Inside was a small key ring with four keys on it. “I talked it over with the others,” I said softly, taking her hand in mine, “and they agreed that it would be okay for you to have your own set of keys. That’s the front door, that’s the back door, and that’s the garage,” I said pointing to each one in turn.

                        “And what’s this one,” she asked, holding up the fourth.

                        I sighed and decided to take the plunge. “That’s a key to my ‘vette,” I said after a moment’s hesitation.

                        She just kind of stared for a minute.

                        “I hope you know what this means to me,” I said, “and how much I trust you. Nobody else has a key to my car…”

                        “Shut up,” she said breathlessly, and then she leaned in and kissed me and… things progressed from there. A few days later she gave me a key to her apartment. I’ve broached the subject of her moving in here officially a few times since then, but she’s determined to keep her own place despite the obvious advantages of cohabitation. She can be as stubborn as a mule sometimes.

                        I’m really in love with this girl, and sometimes I even forget to be scared by that fact. I’ve attempted to subtly clue my father in on my newfound bisexuality, but the good reverend is determined not to take my hints, and thus remains blissfully ignorant of his young parishioner’s romantic proclivities. Sigh. I’m going to have to come right out and say it at some point. I hope he can deal with it.

                        And speaking of things that are nominally good for me but probably wouldn’t make my father particularly happy, my star seems to be on the rise! A package was delivered to me the other day (I had to sign for it and everything). When I opened it, I found to my surprise that it contained ten copies of next month’s issue of Playboy. There was a Post-it note stuck to the top copy: “Check out page 34, Jimmy.” Curious, I flipped past pages of reader mail, editorials, and breasts, and found the page in question.

                        I ran into the kitchen, but only Calamity was there. I slammed one of the magazines down on the table. “You have to see this,” I said breathlessly.

                        Calamity picked up the magazine and flipped to the table of contents. “Cheerleaders of the Atlantic Coast Conference?” she asked flippantly.

                        “It’s not what you think,” I said in a rush, “I’m in this one!” She gave me a look, and I realized how that must have sounded. “No… I’m not naked or anything… well, actually, okay, I am naked, but it’s… it’s not what you think!” I said again.

                        She arched an eyebrow. I sighed. “Look, just turn to page 34, okay?” She did.

                        Jimmy Liles is a very promising young man studying photography in Santa Monica. He photographed me a few weeks ago, ran the results through some filters and computer doohickies, plopped some text on the finished product, and submitted it to the advertising department of the Jack Daniels Brewing Company as part of a nation-wide contest. He won.

                        His ad is going to be in several magazines with national distribution. My picture is going to be in several magazines with national distribution!

                        The picture itself is a side-on shot of me seated at a table, mostly naked but with all of my bits covered by the angle of the shot (it’s what they call a “tasteful nude”). I’m looking sultrily at the camera with a bottle of Jack raised halfway to my parted lips, and a lit cigarette smoldering in an ashtray in front of me. Ironically, Jimmy used the computer to give me wings… white angelic wings rather than the colorful ones he couldn’t see.

                        For some reason, looking at that picture made me think of one of the other birds – one of the other girls, I mean – who briefly shared our cage in Arcadia. What was her name? Annabeth? Annabelle? Something like that. She was lovely, but she didn’t last long. None of the girls lasted long, aside from Missy, Jane, and me. Weird that I should think about her now, after all this time, though. She was no angel, as I remember her, and her hair and wings were both bright red, so I don’t know why a picture of li’l old me wearing angel wings would bring her to mind.

                        So anyway, Calamity said she’d use the Dream House’s contacts in L.A. to get me meetings with a few agents. Real agents, not lowlife scum like Gary. With any luck, I should be doing some much bigger shoots in the not too distant future. And once they hear me sing and see me act… who knows?

                        In other news, it looks like my production of “A Midsummer Night’s Dream” is coming together nicely. I’ve stuck to my guns, and I’m casting changelings only as mortals and mortals only in faerie roles. I’m playing Hermia myself, which is maybe a little bit on the nose, but it seemed like the best choice. I think we’re on target for our “one night only” show at the Solstice, and we should give the Freehold – and the local mortals – a night to remember.

                        I’ve been practicing on the piano for the past few weeks, and things are really starting to come back to me… I think I might actually be able to perform in front of an audience soon. It’ll be an interesting change letting one of the other performers take center stage while I provide musical backup, and I doubt I’ll do it very often, but as they say, variety is the spice of life.

                        Oh, Jacob took me out roller skating again, and we brought the rest of the motley with us this time. I had a blast! I bought my own skates on a whim a few weeks ago, and they fit perfectly. It was so much fun hanging out with my friends and watching them enjoy each other’s company in that strangely familiar setting; I hope we can do it again soon, and maybe even make it a regular thing.

                        One last bit of fluff before I move on to more serious matters… by and large, movie night has been a rollicking success. We’ve been cycling through the motley members based on Courts – Dawn, Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter, Night – and my place in the rotation has come up twice now.

                        So the first week, Gawain picked a film called Ladyhawke; a fantasy drama about a pair of lovers who were cursed so that they turn into animals at different times of day… they could only see each other in human form for a few fleeting moments at dawn and dusk. It was silly but romantic in its way, and I ended up really enjoying it.

                        For my part, I had been told that I needed to see a movie called Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom. I sort of apologized as I suggested it, since the title sounded so silly, but most of the others were more than happy to watch it with me. Calamity suggested starting with another film called Raiders of the Lost Ark, since it was technically the first film in the series, and I agreed readily enough. It was… much better than I was expecting! A great period action film, with a bit of romance and supernatural horror thrown in. Plus monkeys. And Nazis. Monkeys and Nazis are guaranteed to make any film better, I’m told.

                        For the next week, Ace inflicted a zombie film called Dead Alive on us. It wasn’t really to my taste, but it was fun cuddling up to D during the scary or revolting parts. I did a little research, and apparently the film was originally named Braindead, and the director went on to create some pretty popular adaptations of the Lord of the Rings books. As that was one of the few fantasy novels of which Dad actually approved when I was growing up, I read it more than once. Maybe I should check those films out… though if they came from this Peter Jackson fellow, I’m not sure how good they can possibly be.

                        Calamity’s selection was more to my taste… a little fantasy romantic comedy called The Princess Bride. It was written by William Goldman, the same guy who wrote Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid. It was amazing. Exciting, romantic, funny, moving… it seemed to have everything, all wrapped up in the touching framework of Columbo reading a story to his sick grandson. It might be my favorite film that I’ve seen since I escaped Arcadia. And when I mentioned at the end of the evening that it was time to go to bed, the way D said “as you wish” sent shivers down my spine…

                        The next week was… well, the films were good, but… I dunno. Donnie Darko was – as its name might imply – quite dark, and sort of mind-bending… I never quite knew where it was going, sort of like Marguerite herself (who suggested the film). Jacob’s selection was a heart-breaking three-hour portrayal of the Holocaust called Schindler’s List. I… don’t think I can talk about that film yet in any meaningful way. It was emotionally devastating, and the way the director used occasional flashes of color in the otherwise black-and-white film was a thing of terrible beauty. I almost couldn’t believe it when Jacob told me that it was directed by the same man who gave us Raiders of the Lost Ark.

                        So when it came ‘round to the next movie night, both Gawain and Ace were unavailable, so the selection fell to me and Calamity. I’d been looking into The Princess Bride online, and found a persistent rumor that it was tangentially connected to a film called This Is Spinal Tap, a “mockumentary” about a fake heavy metal band. It seemed interesting, so I suggested it, and the others went along with it. It was fun, and really funny, though not as good as The Princess Bride. It went all the way to eleven, to quote what I am informed is a highly overused line from the film.

                        Calamity brought a French film called Le Pacte des Loups, or The Brotherhood of the Wolf. It was an exciting period piece set in 1700s France, supposedly based on the true story of a monstrous wolf that terrorized the countryside for months. I’m not sure how much of the story is actually true – I never knew Canadian Indians knew such powerful kung fu, for example – but it was certainly action-packed, and an enjoyable way to spend an evening.

                        Okay, so I’ve dicked around enough with this entry, talking about how hunky-dory life is and how happy I’ve been. Now it’s time to get down to business. A few important things have happened lately, and it looks like life is about to get interesting again.

                        First, Jacob approached us the other day with word from Master Darke, who has had people looking into the attack on Dark Kimberly at the Rose & Thorn. According to his sources, a group called the Phantom Tong is probably involved. From what I’ve been told, they’re an organization of changelings who try to spread chaos through the world for reasons which frankly escape me. The very inexplicable nature of the crime does seem to point to one of their terrorist acts, but it doesn’t quite fit in my opinion. The Tong always leaves some sort of calling card at the scene of their mayhem; they want people to know that they’re behind these senseless acts. Nothing like that has been uncovered in Kimberly’s case as of yet.

                        There’s also the fact that Darke seems to be working really hard to tie the attack to Lucien Torrez, who has had some circumstantial connections to Tong members in the past. Now, I don’t like to point fingers at powerful members of the community or anything, but it’s well known that Darke and Torrez hate each other, and it seems to me that Darke is just using this as a way to sling a bit of mud in Torrez’s direction.

                        (Oh, that reminds me… Mr. Torrez got me my gun! It’s a cute little pistol by American Arms called a Black Widow. Calamity’s been teaching me how to shoot along with lots of gun safety rules so that I can avoid, you know, killing myself and shit.)

                        The other recent development is possibly even more disturbing, and certainly calls for more immediate action. Andromeda dropped by the other day and asked to speak with Calamity about a matter of some delicacy. It turns out that things have gotten quite steamy between her and Joan lately, and they’ve been ‘experimenting’ with some of Andromeda’s more… exotic inventions. During the course of their exploration, Joan officially – physically – lost her virginity.

                        Here’s where it gets interesting. Joan is apparently suffering the effects of a curse; something she’s lived with since her durance. She becomes completely and utterly subservient to whoever takes her cherry. It’s kind of scary, actually… to hear Andromeda talk, you wouldn’t even recognize her as the same feisty, grumpy warrior we all know and love these days. She’s like a puppy, eager to please its mistress.

                        Andromeda is understandably upset about this. Aside from the fact that Joan is no longer the girl she fell for, there are practical considerations in place as well. For example, her reputation in the Freehold really can’t take another hit right now, and if people were to start gossiping that she caused these changes in Joan’s personality intentionally, it could spell the end for her in Malibu and Santa Monica. So she’s asked us for our help.

                        Calamity went by the Autumn Court’s library to look into ways to reverse the curse. Unfortunately, Gentry magic is pretty unique, and there are no catch-all ways to remove such a powerful enchantment. However, most sources seem to agree that if we can somehow restore Joan’s virginity then the detrimental effects will pass, and as long as she and Andromeda are more careful in the future there should be nothing to worry about.

                        We came up with two options. First, Joan could restore both her physical and spiritual virginity by completing some sort of mystical quest in the Hedge. Seems like a no-brainer, right? I mean, Joan is almost as much of a knight in shining armor as Gawain is. You’d think this sort of thing would be right up her alley. And ordinarily, you’d be right. But such a quest requires clarity of purpose and steely determination, and Joan is running short of both of those at the moment. She’d probably do the quest if Andromeda asked her to, but her heart wouldn’t really be in it, and that could be dangerous for her… maybe even deadly.

                        So we looked into option two. There is a kind of goblin fruit that can restore someone’s virginity. It’s known to grow in the Hedge not too far away, and we thought there was at least a decent chance that we would be able to find some in the Freehold. Alas, such was not to be. I spoke with Flores about it, but she didn’t have any on hand. Of course, I didn’t bring Joan’s name into it… I’m actually rather proud of my subtle misdirection. I told her that I was looking for the fruit for “a friend,” which of course made her think that I wanted it for myself (or maybe for D).

                        I won’t deny that the thought has crossed my mind. There’s a part of me that sort of wishes I could give that part of myself to someone that I really love, and D would certainly fit the bill. I guess there’s a part of me that wishes I hadn’t lost my cherry to Tommy Salinger when I was… I’ll just leave it at ‘far too young,’ shall I? I mean, I really only did it because I knew it would infuriate Dad if he ever found out. It hurt, and Tommy was a real jerk about it later. I’ve always wondered if it would have been different if I’d been with someone who really cared about me that first time…

                        No. I’ve made my choices, and some of them were mistakes, but I’m going to live with them regardless. D knows that I’m damaged… hell, I’m a changeling, right? It sort of goes with the territory. Anyway, she loves me all the same. It doesn’t pay to start second-guessing myself at this point.

                        So the others didn’t have any more luck with their own courts. This goblin fruit is either really rare or not worth the trouble in most cases, because no one seems to keep it handy around here. So we’re going to head into the Hedge in the next few days to try to track it down. We have to hurry, because sooner or later someone is going to notice the changes in Joan’s personality, and that’ll be the end of that. The good news is that we know approximately where the fruit is located in the Hedge, and have a pretty good idea of how to get there. The bad news is that it’s located in a swamp. Sigh. Why don’t we ever get a quest that leads us to beautiful tropical beaches?

                        Lorelei Grace
