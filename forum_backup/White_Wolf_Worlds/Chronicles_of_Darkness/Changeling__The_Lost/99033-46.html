            Lorelei’s Diary

            Christmas Day is just around the corner, and the excitement is almost palpable in the air… particularly whenever Marguerite is present, but I think we all feel it to some degree. I think I’m just about done putting the finishing touches on my presents for everyone. It’s difficult to work on them without any of my friends or lovers finding out what I’m up to, but I’ve been able to grab a spare moment here and there. I hope they’ll take the gifts in the spirit in which they’re intended… as something that I’m putting my heart and soul into, rather than something I’m spending a lot of money on. I think the motley will get it… I’ve never been particularly stingy with my money in the first place, and I wanted to do something truly special for my closest friends instead of just dropping cash on a designer handbag or something.

            Though I admit, I saw an adorable handbag that would look perfect on Gawain’s shoulder the other day…

            Ahem. In any case, Oleander was a good sport about getting up early on Sunday to join me at church. It was the Christmas service, of course, and Dad had asked me weeks ago to do a solo befitting the season. I decided to go with “O Holy Night,” as it’s always been one of my favorite holiday songs. It practically screams Christmas, but plays up the religious significance of the holiday rather than the secular aspects. Plus… I admit that it doesn’t hurt that it gave me the chance to show off a little bit, as it requires a vocal range (and a set of pipes) not often displayed in your average church choir. So that was a blast.

            I got a standing ovation for my rendition, which was a little unusual for our sleepy little Methodist church, but I took it with a smile and a nod that wasn’t quite a bow (it didn’t seem appropriate given the venue). I also got a very pleasant surprise when I looked out to locate Oleander among the congregation… Marguerite had slipped in at some point. She was standing a few rows back from Oleander and applauding with the rest. I threw her a toothy smile to let her know that I’d noticed her and appreciated her presence.

            I looked for her after the conclusion of the service, but she was nowhere to be found. I guess she must have slipped out quietly with the rest of the congregation as I was changing out of my choir robes. Dad commented on her presence (she had shaken his hand on her way out), and I made plans to join him for at least one meal later this week… possibly dragging Maria along with me, depending on her mood. Christmas with my Dad! I never would have thought it possible a few months ago.

            Oleander joined us briefly for sex breakfast after driving me back to the Dream House, but she couldn’t stay for long, as she had a few things to take care of before the big coronation later that evening. I made sure to thank Marguerite for coming out to support me at the church that morning, and she said “you are welcome” with a shy little smile. D had spent the evening in Gloria’s company, and the two of them seemed to have enjoyed themselves, which… my God, I think that fact is actually starting to make me happy now. I guess I’ve come a long way over the past few weeks. Getting over the thing with Spidertoes probably helped a lot.

            I spent most of the rest of the afternoon hanging out with my girls and getting ready for the party that evening. D was opting for a casual approach, but I figured that since I’d be performing it would only be right to dress up a little. I put on a sparkly sky blue evening gown that showed off my curves. Someone at the coronation even wryly referred to me as “Elsa” at one point, so I guess the reference wasn’t lost on people.

He’d asked me to put my own spin on Simon and Garfunkel’s “A Hazy Shade of Winter,” which seemed appropriate given the circumstances. When he made the request, he specifically mentioned a remake from the 80s with which I was unfamiliar, so I looked it up. The Bangles sort of jazzed up the song a bit, adding electric guitar to the instrumental portions and upping the tempo, but the beautiful core melody remained the same. I’ve always liked that song; personally I think it’s one of Paul Simon’s better efforts.

            I began to regret my decision to wear a tight evening dress after a while. For one thing, it was pretty cold at the coronation, and I hadn’t brought a coat. Of course, D came to my rescue pretty quickly, tossing her leather jacket around my shoulders (I had to fold up my wings, of course, but it was a nice gesture). It was warm, and it smelled like her, which was nice.

            One of the reasons it was so cold at the coronation is that the event was held at an ice skating rink. Now, there was no way in hell that I was going to get out there and make a fool of myself in that dress. Though I’m not a bad ice skater, I was most certainly not attired appropriately for the activity. Most of the others got out there at one point or another, though. Jacob and Luna just looked like they were having a good time, but Marguerite and Calamity couldn’t resist strutting their stuff and showing off for the crowd.

            D and Julia kept me company on the sidelines; neither of them wanted to get in the way of whatever private competition Marguerite and Calamity had going. We spent most of the evening people-watching, honestly. Most of the Freehold was there, and I passed a few pleasant words with just about everyone. I got to see Joan and Andromeda, which was nice… they seemed just as happy as ever. Oh, and Flores came by to say hello too, which is always fun.

So while many of my friends were gliding and twirling around on the ice, I lilted my way through “Winter Wonderland.” So I guess I got a secular Christmas classic in on Sunday as well. What can I say? I love this season! I mean, maybe not as much as Marguerite does, but still.

After a while, I noticed Calamity subtly pointing Stasia in our direction as well. As her bestie seemed intrigued by the notion of forming a sextet with us, we allowed as how that would be fine. At the time I wondered why Calamity would do that… but in hindsight I’m guessing that she planned to spend the evening with Becky and didn’t want Stasia to have to ride home with Jacob (who was still sore about the whole seducing his sister thing).

I mean, it’s hard not to look smug when you’re walking downstairs in the company of five beautiful women in various states of dishabille. I don’t know what was said in that little private conference, and to be perfectly honest, I don’t want to know. Stasia seemed okay after they both returned, in any case. I personally think Jacob’s taking this overprotective brother thing a little further than he should… but it’s none of my business, so I’ve kept my mouth shut on the subject. Y’know, mostly.

            The rest of the day was sort of a lazy stretch for me. It was Monday, so we had movie night to look forward to, and got to put aside the hustle and bustle of our usual pre-show routines. D had to go to work, of course, and I once again brought up the possibility that she could quit her job at the hospital and come to work for us at the Dream House full time.

            She sighed. “I’d like that, babe, I really would,” she said earnestly, “and I swear I’m thinking about it. But the problem is, if I quit my job as an X-ray tech, and then ever wanted to go back to that sort of work, it would be a real pain in the ass to get recertified.”

            I nodded sympathetically. “I get it,” I said, “I hadn’t thought about that side of things. I’m just, well…”

            She quirked an eyebrow at me. “What?” she asked gently.

            I looked away, sure I was blushing twelve shades of crimson. “I’m just imagining a time when I don’t have to say goodbye to you three days out of every week,” I mumbled, probably nearly incoherent.

            She chuckled. “I’ll give it some more thought,” she promised as I walked her to the door. “Or, you know, you could get your ass into a local college and start working on that medical degree you’re always talking about. Then one of these days I could work at your office.”

            That gave me pause for thought, even if it was obviously intended as a joke. I mean, even assuming I could get into a pre-Med program at a local college, I’d have at least six or seven years of school before I could even get a residency anywhere, let alone set up my own practice. And that’s assuming I could go to school full-time, which is… not incredibly likely considering my commitment to the Dream House, my plans to rescue Missy and Jane, and the possibility (however small) that I might need to take over the position of Spring Queen sometime within the next few years.

            Still… medicine does call to me as a profession, and most of those obligations aren’t going anywhere, so… isn’t it time I got my ass in gear and started taking the first steps?

            In any case, D left me on the doorstep with a kiss and a promise that she would seriously consider the possibility of coming to work for the Dream House full-time as security. I wandered upstairs, where I found Julia painting a portrait of Flores, with Marguerite lazing languidly off to one side. It seemed like a peaceful environment, so I grabbed a textbook from my room, returned to the attic, and sprawled out on a sofa to read.

            It seemed like no time at all had passed before D was back and it was time for movie night to begin. As Calamity had missed the previous week’s selection of holiday comedy classics, she selected a couple of her own favorite Christmas movies for us to view this week. And, well… I mean, the movies weren’t bad or anything… and they both took place at Christmas-time, so I guess they qualified, but… let’s just say that Marguerite was not well pleased by the selections.

            The first film was called Gremlins, and was a sort of horror-comedy mishmash from the 80s about these tiny, mischievous creatures who are prone to transforming into real monsters based on nonsensical dietary restrictions. Like, they can’t eat anything after midnight. I tried to raise a few logical questions about how ludicrous that particular aspect of the film was, but apparently that nit has been well and truly picked on the Internet over the past twenty years or so, and Julia had a quick response to each of my hypothetical queries on the subject.

            Honestly, the film wasn’t bad once you just accepted certain concepts as a given. There were one or two really effective jump-scares late in the film that had me clinging to my honey and squealing in alarm, though by and large the film seemed to be a comedy. There was one truly horrific scene, in which the main character’s love interest describes the awful reason why she doesn’t like the Christmas season. I saw Marguerite discreetly remove herself from the media room as that scene came up. She returned a short while later, and seemed to be okay again.

            The second film was a straight-out action movie (also from the 80s) called Die Hard. It’s about a New York cop who travels to LA on Christmas Eve in the hopes of reconciling with his estranged wife. He gets caught up in a hostage situation, and is forced to prove what a badass he is by taking down all of the bad guys pretty much single-handedly. The main villain was played by Alan Rickman, whom I remembered from that Robin Hood movie we watched a few months ago (heh… buttcakes!). And the hero was played by Bruce Willis, whom I have seen in a few other things over the past year, as he’s fairly prolific.

            So we talked about quite a few things as the final credits rolled. I’d never heard of taking off your shoes and making “fists” with your feet in pile carpet as a cure for jetlag, so I posited that this was just something the writers dreamed up so that the hero would have to pick broken glass out of his bare feet later in the film. Marguerite wanted to make the (perfectly valid) point that just because a film is set during the month of December, that does not make it a “Christmas film” per se. Julia, on the other hand, seemed personally outraged by the fact that Bruce Willis was seen as something of a sex symbol when the film was originally released.

            So we talked about that for a while. I didn’t get the big deal, honestly. I mean, he wasn’t like an Adonis or anything, but he had a hard body and a certain rough charisma that was very appealing. I wouldn’t kick him out of bed… not that this is setting the bar particularly high, I’ll admit. But most of the others seemed to agree with me on that score. Calamity thought his badass-ness made up for his lack of conventional good looks. D came down on my side, stating that while Mr. Willis might not have been a Chippendales dancer or anything, there is a certain appeal to the strength the character exhibited. And Luna just shrugged and admitted that she used to have something of a crush on the man in any case.

            Julia eventually gave up the argument, which was probably wise on her part, as I think the group as a whole was about ten seconds away from using the discussion to tease her about being one of the only true lesbians in the House. I hope she realizes that we only tease her because we care about her. God, I love her so much, I’d hate to think that I was actually making her feel bad over something like that. My relationship with Julia tends to get overlooked sometimes compared to the deep eternal feelings I share with my soul mate and the drama that often surrounds my relationship with my French lover. But that doesn’t mean my feelings for Julia are any less profound, or any less real. I may be marrying Maria, but I fully intend to share the rest of my life with all three of them, and Oleander too if she wants it.

            We also talked for a bit about shaking things up for our last show before Christmas (which is… technically later today, as I write this). Marguerite and I are going to do a duet, and Marguerite and Calamity are going to share a number as well… but I’ll write about the specifics of those acts in my next entry, so I can write about how they went over. I think Calamity is also going to reprise her “Mistress for Christmas” number, and I’ll probably roll out “Santa Baby” again too. I think I’m looking forward to the show almost as much as Marguerite is, though of course she’s grumbling about “last minute substitutions” and “needing the practice.”

            I swear, at this time of year she is absolutely adorable!

            So the four of us retired to our chambers after that, by which I mean Marguerite and Julia’s room… for some reason I can never convince the Darklings to join me in my own bedroom, what with the big picture window opening up on the balcony and all. After a relatively sedate round of lovemaking, the three of them have finally drifted off, and as per usual, I’ve been reading medical journals, writing in this diary, and musing ‘pon the mysteries of life while I listen to them breathe.

            Christmas is coming… my one and only Christmas as Lorelei Grace, assuming I go ahead with my plans to take D’s last name. I guess I’d better make the most of it.
