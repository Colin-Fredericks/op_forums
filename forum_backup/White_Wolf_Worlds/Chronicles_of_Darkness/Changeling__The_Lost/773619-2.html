            This whole debate about what scientific property of iron is truly responsible for dealing aggravated damage to the Gentry strongly reminds me of another eldritch entity: SCP-2845 (AKA The Stag). The ways that the Stag controls living and inert matter is pointed out in the article as impossible, according to our understanding of chemistry and biology. They had to consult a shaman of sorts to explain how the hell this Thing worked, and he essentially described how many supernatural phenomena in the NWOD function, including the Gentry to a fair extent:

            Rituals do not work because of some underlying laws, such as those that science operates on. Rituals work because they are rituals. They work because an arbitrary set of criteria has been met with exacting care. Belief that meeting these arbitrary criteria achieves a certain end assigns power to the ritual. The actions that were once meaningless now have been assigned Meaning through their repetition and application.

            This is how one seals away a god, and this is a god. I know that the Foundation does not approve of using the term, but it is still the case. The Stag is a god, and it is not a petty local god. It is not one of the mild gods of Earth, or some weaker spirit that is bound by the strength of man’s belief.

            We have, for the time being, fooled it into thinking that we have overpowered it. It does not understand that we do so falsely: it is a god. For gods, words have Power. Ritual and belief hold greater power over a god than all the laws of science.
            I find the idea of beings so powerful and bigger in scale than all of humanity that our laws of nature mean nothing to them a lot scarier than "there is some principle behind it that I can understand in my own terms and use against it". But to each his own. Trying to figure out the one weak spot of an eldritch monster with the combined efforts of the Mysterium and the Null Mysteriis sounds like a fun idea. Even if they figure out how to stop the monster, they probably won't know exactly the why or how. And curiosity is right up there with hubris in the top 10 reasons that Mages and other investigative types get killed or worse.
