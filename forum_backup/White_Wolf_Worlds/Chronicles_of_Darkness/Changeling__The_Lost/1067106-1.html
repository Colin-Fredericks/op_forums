I mean, even on Earth magic gets harder depending on how obvious and how many witnesses there are. The Hedge cares nothing for who sees you. But it has RULES, sir! One does not just LEAVE before their part is played! Turning back at the threshold is fine, of course, plenty of sisters and brothers of the poor hero have done that. No harm, no foul. But for those who choose to go further in its not proper at all. They've become COMMITTED. Now there's a tale to tell, and no tale simply STOPS until it has a proper ENDING. Does a prince show himself and fail to remove the curse with a kiss? What sort of wolf would learn of a tasty creature living in a certain house and fail to enact a plot to invade the home? No no no. There are traditions and patterns and rules and if you disagree you will surely be PUNISHED.

            Which is a long winded way of saying that a Mage deep in the Hedge should, by my reading, have at best a hefty set of penalties on the dice for getting back out. If he no longer has a good grasp on where the mortal world is relative to himself (which of course happens all the damn time in the Hedge) it gets worse. The easy way to mitigate the problem would be to find your way back (and probably suffer through some strange encounter arranged by the Wyrd along the way). Another way might to fulfill some barely understandable purpose between you and the Wyrd ("Why yes, Mr. Hobgoblin, I am a learned sorcerer and can answer your obscure question!"...and hopefully Mr. Hobgoblin doesn't use the knowledge you gave him to sneak into the mortal world and devour your sister's son...the Wyrd is a bitch that way) and then invoke your magic. But as a general rule, the deeper you go the harder you will find escape, but there might be ways to get around that problem if you fulfill the demands of Fate and the Wyrd (which is really just Fate when it gets bored and a little drunk).

            Also, if you do manage to push your way past Fate's designs and create your door, might want to be careful anyway. Yeah, maybe it'll lead to your inner sanctum as planned, but Fate might have worked its way into the enchantment more than you knew and you could end up falling 100 stories to your death or find yourself staring at a pack of Abyssal dogs on the hunt for mana to suck from a mage's bones. Or maybe Fate is cowed by your mastery but whispers to its good freind Time and you end up a few centuries beyond the mark.

            Before we get completely off topic for my whimsy, I'll throw in a few more questions I kind of allude to here. How much control does a Mage generally have over his destination with these Irises? Are there any factors that can fuck with that control, ala how lost they are, what forces in the universe might be opposing them, etc. How long does the portal stay open, and how many things can come through besides the mage (hell, could a swiftly moving hobgoblin or desperate changeling get the drop on a Mage and skip on through his portal before he can, perhaps with the damn gate shutting in the Mage's face?). Given the rules the Hedge normally plays by, does the gate eventually vanish as the Avernian Gate does in the referenced spell....or does it stay in place forever waiting to be reopened?

                                This seems like a REALLY heated topic so I dunno if I can pick it apart in a short span of time, but maybe I can offer a compromise to the central topic---a topic I think is also in the wrong forum to begin with but whatever!

                                A Master of Fate can make a Hedge Gate. But doing so will still violate a Contract. The Mage is under Contract, knowingly or not, to exit the Hedge legally. If they break that Contract, their life is going to get more interesting very quickly. Maybe not everyone will enjoy this sort of "fix" I'm proposing, but I think it is a fair one considering that 1E mage had realms that Masters of Space cannot escape from either.
                                Namely the Crossways.

                                So anyone who wants Mages to be able to make irises in and out of the Hedge without Archmastery are going to be breaking the Contract that they implicitly agree to when they enter the Hedge. Consent is not a part of this contract at any time.

                                The other thing I wanna mention is something I think Satchel is trying to say too. Mages are arrogant bastards that trample all over the cosmos. Yes, that's their sthick. If you don't like it, deal with it. And by deal with it, have an ST who looooves hubris. What I'm actually saying is, you don't need paradox to bring about a Mage's hubris. Any mage player who is really good can probably evade any of the punishments they have coming their way but it will come at the cost of some of their freedom and peace-of-mind. Logically this is how hermit-mages come to be. They've pissed off so many forces-of-nature that they can't go anywhere without it being open season. If a Mage can do anything, it will have consequences. It doesn't have to be easy.

                                With that said, I do prefer the idea that the Hedge can fight back against a Mage. Fate 5 Gateway out of the Hedge? Sure, but here's a clash of wills you gotta pass against a being you cannot see, possibly the Hedge itself. Prooobably won't pass it, but maybe you'll get lucky. That's what Fate's for right?

                                Maybe Mage magic can deal with the thorns, but maybe the Thorns have a Withstand you're just not going to get through without an exceptional success. If they do succeed, maybe some other form of ire will follow them.

                                Any punishment or vengeful response the forces of Faerie might throw at the mage might also be something the Mage has a counter for, but in the end they've created an endless chess-game of consequences for themselves all because they broke the Contracts of the Hedge.

                                There'd be no easy fix for that short of Archmastery and a whole lot of deal-making.

                                And all that would have been prevented if the arrogant Mage hadn't broken the Contract, which they'll know better if they're listening to their mentors or listening to changeling's for half a second.

                                And while all of that could just seem annoying to a mage player, or as a half-assed "fix" to changeling players, from a Storyteller perspective I think it sounds interesting.

                                Kind of like a Breaking Bad thing where its just one problem after another because of 1 really dumb life-choice. You could build an entire story off of this single choice to break the Hedge's rules. It could be especially interesting if it ends up being that the only ones with the social clout to get the Mage out of this endless chess-game is swallowing their pride and asking changelings for help or guidance.

                                                Namely the Crossways.
                                                Here's the problem, expressed in terms of its separate elements:

                                                The Crossways is an Abyssal incursion specifically built around victims getting stuck deeper and deeper inside a maddening spiral of unreality, as a thing specifically and primarily extant to bedevil and/or entrap mages, sometimes as a direct result of their carelessness with magic. There's one way in and out per manifestation and Space needs the support of an extra Arcanum to reach outside of its bounds for communication. The Abyss has business directly negating easy shortcuts past core problems it presents to mages, because it's as omnipresent as the Supernal is and serves as one of the central pillars of how Phenomenal reality feels oppressive to the practice of Awakened magic.

                                                The Hedge is an ever-shifting combination of elven thoroughfare and fateful borderland nestled around the physical/metaphorical space where dreams happen, and its entrances and exits — of which there are many — regularly possess keys of no more substantial difficulty to use than those of any of the other run-of-the-mill Irises the Awakened encounter. People can and do get out of the Hedge on purpose or by luck, and the former category includes people who are fairies who can brute-force the creation of an exit with a suitable (and, in 2e's presentation, visually unique rather than archetypal) door/archway/mirror/threshold; in other words, if there is an agreement, it's one that's flexible enough that a legal gateway is relatively easy to finagle for anyone with the right "in" with fate even if you don't have the capacity to trick a gate into opening by faking its key.

                                                The Arcana of Awakened magic are increasingly capable of things that just plain shouldn't work according to the common sense of their respective portions of reality — Matter can make steel a liquid at room temperature, Prime can make tass that doesn't have any Mana in it, Spirit can transform you into a Twilight state even in realms where Twilight isn't a state that exists, Death can make a ghost possess its body for a lot longer than the scene/hour it would normally be able to maintain that Manifestation, and so on and so forth. Of particular note here is that at the level of proficiency in Fate immediately below the one needed to make a Hedge gate, you literally have the ability to alter the terms of a supernatural agreement enforced by the dictates of fate, and at that level, you can accomplish things like a spontaneous solar eclipse or a normal human going skydiving without a parachute and surviving.

                                                Generally speaking, the setting's attitude toward crossover is such that the various cosmic-level forces that concern themselves mostly with singular gamelines aren't too worried about individual attempts at skirting their territory — the God-Machine stops wholesale subversion of Its angels because It is a precision-oriented process and an angel breaking its defining orders of "do not betray your mission" has severe consequences beyond the immediate scope of whatever it is made to do instead, and a Fae Prop created to perform a specific task can only be stopped from performing that specific task by another act of god, but Luna's hands-off approach to Her children and the Divine Fire's questionable presence of mind in general tend to be the norm — things like the Judges and the Exarchs are where you get the kind of direct possessive behavior for things on a smaller scale than violating the sanctity of a phenomenon's metaphysical makeup/affiliation; everybody else, especially the older and more jaded among the Fae, doesn't really care.

                                                All of which is to say that the pettiest circumstances the Wyrd gets involved with concern the keeping to the letter of one's word, and even in 1e Changeling included elements that dealt with getting around the spirit of a vow's original context; an unspoken agreement like the one implied to be behind the forbiddance in 1e is no agreement at all, particularly when creating an exit is still using an exit and even in 1e the capacity existed to fake a key to open a gate. There's basically no coherent means of outright blocking the ability that wouldn't arbitrarily disregard a lot of things about both gamelines.

                                                If you absolutely must add more difficulty to the process, I wouldn't go with blanket immunity. No, take cues from Goetic Summons and the intervals for finding an active gateway — add one Reach to open a gateway from a region of the Hedge corresponding to a less populated area, or two Reach from mostly uninhabited wilderness, with denser spaces being accessible with the default parameters of the spell.
