    New to Changeling: the Lost

    12-01-2013, 06:42 PM
    So, I'm familiar with Vampire, Werewolf, and Mage, mostly the CWoD versions. Changeling the Lost looks pretty snazzy, but I'm not sure what sort of campaigns work for them.

    I mean, Vampire lends itself to building an empire and crushing rivals, Werewolf lends itself to monster hunting and epic quests, and Mage lends itself to weird reality bending games that play around with conceptual themes.

    I'm not sure that sort of games Changeling lends itself to.

    My default campaign structure (from D&D) is usually a five step hero's journey. 1) The bewildered bystanders, in over their heads. The goal is to survive. 2) The seasoned veterans, who have survived and strengthened, seek wealth and fortune. The goal is to prosper. 3) Having earned a reputation for wealth and accomplishment, people look up to the heroes as leaders, and bring all their problems to the hero's feet. The goal is to lead. 4) A great threat looms, that could destroy the entire community/kingdom/empire, and it must be defeated. The goal is to protect. 5) The heroes are long retired, having retreated to the mountaintop, sometimes in solitude and contemplation, sometimes in mentorship to younger heroes. Rather than die quietly, however, the old heroes gather once more to go shake the pillars of heaven. The goal is to change.

    I don't know if that structure would work for Changeling. I mean, part 1 could be "escape the hedge" and part 5 could be "seek vengeance on the Fae that created you"
    But I'm not sure.

      Changeling, like the other splats, lends itself to many modes of play but I believe if you wanted to sum it up simply it would be 'coming to terms with yourself.' Whether that involves embracing your fae-ness and rushing to fight the Keepers or slipping back into society and balancing your Mask with your Mien is up to the individual.

      With the arc you've stated:
      1: Changelings have escaped their Keepers, made it back to the real world. There is something keeping them from just slipping back into the place they were taken from, be it a fetch, time, or anything else. As an added storytelling benefit, they have powers and now, new enemies. They find a freehold, it's the only safe place, but how safe is it?
      2: They find a bit of equilibrium. The first hurdles have been overcome, maybe a couple fetches or another servant sent by their Keeper. Whether or not they've re-joined their former lives, they now have a place in the real world, and within the freehold. But they're players, so they have a bit more ambition, a bit more potential than NPCs, so they don't have to settle, they can become kings of the freehold, leaders of their courts, tops of their field.
      3: They're not just extras anymore. Whether they're in positions of power and influence, or they've made a name for themselves in other ways (bringing good things to the freehold? slaying local threats? uncovering hidden dangers?), as you said, in some way or another, they lead.
      4: Just as you say. In my own game it is an army base full of hunters. Maybe it's a rival freehold, an organized bunch of privateers, an army of hedge beasts, a single high wyrd/low clarity changeling, a mage, a vampire, or one of the true fae themselves.
      5: Some changelings stick to being ruler, some decide to leave it behind and make something for themselves in the Hedge, some make a niche for themselves in the real world. They did the hero thing (or perhaps not) and now they are not just names, they're legends. They can be called upon to destroy that Fae they never truly destroyed, whose loyalists prey upon the freehold. Or perhaps that Other they dealt with, to learn the secrets of their enemies, has become a Keeper, or something else. Maybe the Keeper of the motley's leader takes their child, their girlfriend, their husband, so the band gets back together.


      It's pretty simple. The big difference from other splats is that there is a personalized threat, in the form of the Keeper, and rather than separate yourself from the rest of humanity (except for midnight snacks) you have some incentive to put yourself back into it. Which isn't to say you have to, there are changelings who want to become the monster, who refuse to deal with humanity and never set foot out of their freehold or out of the hedge. But the story arc you described easily fits in to changeling.

          This structure would work pretty excellently. In part because, what with Talecrafting and the way the Wyrd works, the characters might be deliberately trying to create this narrative and use it as a source of power… But that’s all stuff from a later supplement (Swirds at Dawn) and presumably you've not got your paws on that yet.

          That structure, to me, would start off with them as hedge-fresh, terrified, confused by what they’ve become and what they can do, struggling to take back their lives and deal with the fetches (or to establish new ones from nothing) and other weirdness and finding a way into insular changeling society.
          Then they’d have some stability and would seek to find a niche for themselves in the Freehold, possibly even gravitating to leadership within its Courts. They achieve this and naturally learn that such positions have a lot of responsibility and work that must be done. Then it’s time for a True Fae/goblin incursion they have to deal with lest the Freehokd be destroyed and those unlucky enough to survive be dragged back to Arcadia…

          Then, finally, the old changelings (possibly in emeritus positions at Court, or still holding some leadership or other key role, or living quiet lives as unusually spry old people) have to reunite in response to a new threat – a True Fae they fought before but knew would return, IT-style, perhaps (maybe they defeated the True Fae before and it agreed to leave for fifty years… and forty-nine years later they’ve got some work to do). A former friend/ally/PC who raised their Wyrd, haemorrhaged Clairty and became a True Fae that knows where they all live… The Wyrd itself has plans for them and they decide to go against the Freehold and become bridgeburners that are targeting the Wyrd itself. The possibilities are pretty damn great.

          So here are a few of the things you can go for in Changeling – epic tales that reference or follow fairytales, narrative tropes and suchlike. Paranoid, spy-style thrillers based on distrust and fear of Pivateers/Loyalists fronting for True Fae. Inwardly focused narratives based on the changeling’s family and fetch. Political manoeuvring in the Freehold – it can be as corrupt as any vampire’s court, or a relatively harmonious organisation or anywhere inbetween. Character can be ambitious, unconcerned with politics or more interested in patching things up. Exploration of the Hedge, encountering its many dangers and wonders. Entering dreams for fun and profit, Inception style, or as just another battleground against the True Fae.

                      Originally posted by trudge View Post
                      I'll check out Swords at Dawn for info on tale-crafting, for building the arc I'm looking for. Equinox Road also looks good. Really, there aren't that many books, and they all look pretty useful. The line doesn't seem to suffer from much bloat.
                      Rites of Spring is great for character creation and alternate/extra rules. Almost a must-buy. The Equinox Road I adore, and it's geared towards high-level play mainly, Winter Masques has more character creation options and alternate Courts, Lords of Summar delves into the Courts in more detail and Autumn Nightmares has lots of sample antagonists and some neat rules for Fetches and True Fae,... But on the latter point, Equinox Road has some too. Those three aren't too high priority for a newcomer, IMO.

                      Swords at dawn adds Talecrafting, which is a huge gamechanger, and some new Contracts and lots of random things. Dancers in the Dusk is more of an antagonist book, filled with sample Hobs and finally gives more detail on going into dreams and also adds creatures involved in Fate. Hard to rank their importance unless aspects of them really appeal. There's also Goblin Markets, which is pretty self-explanatory in what it's about, and Victorian Lost, which I doubt you'll be too interested in.

                      So.... Get Rites of Spring and Equinox Road. Others are much more optional. Autumn Nightmares should probably come last in importance.

                              When running stories for changelings, remember that you're running stories for characters who're fugitives, and, in some way, still hunted by the Others. Their relationship to the True Fae is what defines the Lost and separates them from humanity. Whether you're looking for ways to fight your Keeper, trying your best to keep away from her, secretly still working with your Keeper because otherwise he'll eat your family or trying to take his place, in some way, all changelings have to deal with the subject matter of being fugitives and survivors of abuse.
