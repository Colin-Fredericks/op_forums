    (Setting Advice) How many kids for my setting?

    12-11-2013, 09:17 AM
    So, I'm working on a freehold right now, and I'm planning to put it online once I've got enough of it together to be a functional fan supplement, but I'm hitting a minor roadblock and I've decided to source fan input before I go too much further.

    Theoretically, if there were a freehold which was modeled after a boarding school for students aged 10-14, and the student classes were made of a mix of changelings and mortals (with the changelings drawn from other freeholds that couldn't take care of the traumatized, magic-slinging kids safely and who had made treaties with this freehold to look after them)...

    How many changeling children would you think there could be before your suspension of disbelief got strained?

    I realize that this question is pretty individual; it's not as though there's a "right answer". I've done up drafts with anywhere from twenty to sixty changeling kids right now, and I'm trying to decide what to settle on.

    "Remember - popcorn can feel pain."

          As hilarious as that would be, the mortal children are mortal children! Without real human contact, the changeling children would rapidly go insane, and the purpose of the school is to help them re-integrate into the world by giving them a stable environment to study in, and a controlled environment to interact with mortals until they're ready for the wider world.

          More specifically, they are children that the changeling teachers at the school convince parents to send to boarding school for special training - probably gifted, but often a little unstable and in need of special attention, many of them fairly well-off but some arriving on grants or scholarships. Quite a few of them end up embroiled in changeling affairs, and then forget that aspect of things when they go home. It's a constant mess, but that is the price of putting together a school like this.

          *edit* Which is not to say that a story about one of the Gentry discovering the school and kidnapping a couple of kids, replacing them with fetches as a weapon, wouldn't be an interesting story.

          Or a story about the Headmaster deliberately bringing some child fetches into the school to see if they could be fixed, or even so that if the children came back they could be slipped into place...

          ooh... there's a risky idea that might be fun.
