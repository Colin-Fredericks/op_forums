                    A lot of heartfelt conversations. How well they went, and how everyone felt, depends on who you ask…

                    Also: Random Disney Attack

                    Session LIX

                    Gawain’s Memories

                    Nov 15, early morning

                    So, Molly, right? We need to help her. Darke wanted a pair of tools to carry out his will, not real people, so she doesn’t have any memories, or anything from more than a year back. A little like me, kinda, but at least I get, you know, glimpses of a past. She doesn’t even remember Arcadia. I mean, that might feel like a blessing, but it’s kinda, you know, a touchstone, I guess.

                    So Jacob and I, we tried to come up with what we could do to help. Calamity and Lori stopped by to help some too. Calamity figured a kinda montage, like 80s movie style, would be best - you know, we’d take her around to all sorts of places and see what it turned out that she likes to do. Lori approved, with some mixed metaphors, something about wide nets and seeing what’d stick.

                    I spent most of the show last night talking with Molly - and Jacob of course. The first thing we’re gonna do is Disneyland. She got really excited about the idea. She’d found a pamphlet once and was nervous at first cause it sounded like what people said about Arcadia. But she’d read a lot about it since then, and was just, you know, super thrilled.

                    We also talked about other things and hobbies and stuff and we learned she’d never read anything but the Encyclopedia Britannica - they were the only books, the only, you know, anything in her house. So I decided to get everybody to loan her their favorites. I haven’t decided what mine’ll be. I’m thinking Redwall though.

                    There were other folks at the show of course. Most of the usuals, plus TJ. I didn’t like the way he was looking at Rowan, or, you know, the way he was so pointed about turning over his arsenal, so I checked out his motivations. He had orders to intimidate Rowan and find out what she was up to, with explicit instructions not to hurt her. That made me feel a little better, though I still didn’t like it.

                    Anyways, he didn’t try anything I could call him on, and we figured out Lori hadn’t seen pretty much any Disney movies. Plus, she was super nervous, you know, about talking to her dad today so... Disney marathon. We watched The Princess and the Frog, Beauty and the Beast, Little Mermaid, Mulan and The Lion King. Julia even took a break from Insincere Marguerite and Desiree to watch Beauty in the Beast with us.

                    Lori’s off to her dad’s church now, with De la Rocha. We volunteered to go with. She had trouble saying no, but even I could see it on her face. Hopefully it all goes OK - but now it’s time for a nap.


                    Nov 16, evening

                    Lori’s coming out went well! It went long, but I was just starting to get worried when De la Rocha let us know things were good. To be fair, I was still a little worried, but Calamity pointed out they wouldn’t be sticking around unless things were going good. Well, not with De la Rocha saying it’s ok at least. It did mean that Lori wasn’t around to, you know, ‘poof’ Insincere Marguerite, or Julia, or Desiree, who had a long night. Desiree was all tapped out of Glamour, but she was able to ‘poof’ Insincere Marguerite and Julia, after they gave her some food.

                    I made sure to save Lori and De la Rocha some, and they eventually got back. Lori told us a little about what had happened, and that her dad was gonna come by for sex breakfast on Tuesday! Of course, we probably shouldn’t call it that, while he’s here. Insincere Marguerite was a little hurt by that kinda idea - Lori didn’t want to spring the whole, you know, poly thing on him right after the Changeling thing and the daughter thing and the bi thing. I think Insincere Marguerite’ll be ok though. She came back after just a little bit anyway.

                    I got to talk to everybody about adding Desiree to the motley. They were all for it, which is great! Things have been going better for her all around - I think she’s really making a place for herself here and not thinking about the Director so much. Insincere Marguerite says so too. I know she was worried about repercussions for us, political stuff, but I was thinking, if we had a, you know, united front about how we didn’t care about politics and all, if it wasn’t just me saying it, then we could offer her a family.

                    That’ll be for later though. For now, we have a show. Lori’s taking a nap, since she hasn’t slept in like three days, but the rest of it all goes on. After the show, we have a few more Disney movies to watch. Aladdin, Pirates of the Caribbean and Frozen. Oh, that reminds me, we got a big stack of books for Molly: I did decide on Redwall, Insincere Marguerite recommended Fingersmith, Jacob Good Omens, Julia a book of Romantic poetry, Lori Eyes of the Dragon and Calamity... Cocklords of Mars. With all that, I also tossed my Halo: the Fall of the Reach on the stack, to get some more variety, you know? De la Rocha didn’t have anything, you know, fiction/fun reading kinda books in English, and I don’t think Molly can speak Spanish. I should maybe ask. Anyways, hopefully Molly’ll find something she likes.


                    Lorelei’s Diary

                    I feel like singing!

                    Okay, maybe in my case that isn’t the best indication of my mood. I sing when I’m happy, I sing when I’m sad, I sing when I’m angry… let’s face it, I do a lot of singing. The moods just sort of come and go in the background. So let me make this a little clearer: I’m so happy that I can’t seem to stop crying.

                    So the last time I wrote in this journal I was obsessing over my coming meeting with my father, just knowing that I was going to be a nervous wreck until it was over. I was trying to take my mind off of it up on the roof with Marguerite and Julia, but it was sort of a lazy setting while Julia was engrossed in her painting, and I couldn’t keep my mind from wandering back to my cares. I decided that I needed something else to focus on, and went down to the living room with Desiree’s DVD.

                    Into the Woods is a fine musical in the best tradition of Sondheim classics like West Side Story and Gypsy. His talent for writing clever, intricate rhymes is more evident here than in any of his other works (at least, any that I’ve seen… I’ve heard good things about Sweeney Todd). The story is a mish-mash of a bunch of popular fairy tales tied together with an original story about a baker and his wife who are trying to undo a decades-old curse. The performances were amazing. Bernadette Peters, who was an up-and-coming actress in my day, steals the show as the witch, but the entire cast was top-notch.

                    I confess I got a little teary-eyed near the end. Though the musical by-and-large is very comedic, the second act – which deals with the consequences of “happily ever after” – turns a bit dark, and even the story’s narrator is drawn into the action and killed at one point. Infidelity, treachery, and recriminations abound… and one of the sub-plots dealt with a son becoming reacquainted with his estranged father, which of course struck a particularly poignant chord with me.

                    So I imagine Desiree probably thought it was pretty strange that I had obviously been crying when I returned the DVD to her. Then again, maybe not… I found her reclining up in the catwalks with Marguerite as she went through some pre-show technical checks. Desiree was naked, but seemed relatively comfortable in that state. I’m still not sure exactly what’s going on between those two, but Desiree seems to be a willing participant, so I guess it’s really none of my business.

                    I joined the others in the kitchen, where Gawain immediately asked me if girls still liked ponies after they grew up. After a few of my trademarked confused blinks, I replied in a serious tone. “I grew up in rural Iowa during the gas crisis. We had rodeos and riding contests at the county and state fairs. And I never liked horses of any size. Give me a tricked out Boss Mustang any day; that’s the only pony for me.”

                    Gawain’s shoulders kind of slumped, and he muttered something about “back to square one.” He eventually explained that he and Jacob had been trying to come up with some potential activities to share with Molly in their attempt to find out what sort of things she might like. I suggested casting that net as widely as possible, to see what sticks. This derailed the conversation entirely for a few minutes, as Gawain accused me of mixing metaphors, but I stand by my florid speech… there’s nothing saying a net can’t be sticky, is there? I mean, look at spider webs…

                    Gawain had also hit upon the idea of having each of us loan Molly a favorite book, since she apparently reads the encyclopedia if left to her own devices. It seemed like a good idea, though my own favorite authors (like Tolkien and Dumas) might seem a little archaic or heavy-handed to someone who’s not familiar with their literary styles. Instead I offered up a paperback I bought at a used book store a few months back: The Eyes of the Dragon by Stephen King. I bought it because I recognized his name from decent horror novels like Carrie and Salem’s Lot, but much to my surprise, this turned out to be a pretty good high fantasy tale about the somewhat strained relationship between two brothers.

                    Anyway, eventually the group had the idea of taking Molly to Disneyland out in Anaheim. I confess to getting a little excited about this; aside from that one day trip to Adventureland in Des Moines, I’ve never been to an amusement park before… and Disneyland is sort of the holy grail of that particular bucket list item. We’re planning to head out there on Monday, and everybody started chattering at once about what they wanted to do first, and which attractions they were most excited about.

                    Of course this only drew attention to the fact that I’m still woefully behind on my pop culture references. We haven’t taken the time to see many cartoons during movie nights, so when someone said that they wanted to get their picture taken with “Belle,” I had no idea what they were talking about, and said so. I should have realized that they meant the protagonist of the Beauty and the Beast film, of course, but I wasn’t thinking about the translation from the French at that time.

                    This gave Gawain an idea. After the show that night, he insisted that the rest of the motley catch me up on my Disney films. I protested weakly for a bit that I was too nervous about church the following morning to really enjoy the films, but Gawain got this serious look on his face and told me flat out that he wouldn’t allow me to pace around in my room all night making myself sick with worry, and that he thought giving me something else to focus on might help me to relax for a bit. D backed him up on that, and in the end I think it was probably a good idea.

                    Oh, and speaking of the show, I think it went pretty well (considering I was working on no sleep and a case of nerves the size of a Buick). I tried a new number, a ballad from the 80s called “Broken Wings” by a band called Mr. Mister. It’s about attempting to repair a damaged relationship. When I sing it, of course, the lyrics take on a somewhat more literal meaning. “Take these broken wings and learn to fly again…”

                    I noticed that the selkie Day Courtier who calls herself Rowan was in the audience once more. We’ve spoken briefly a few times, and I stopped by her table for a while to chat again that night. She seems like a nice enough girl, though she’s obviously nervous about the tension she senses in the Freehold. She kept glancing across the audience to a table where TJ was sitting, and occasionally he would throw a glare in her direction. Before I left, I told her a little bit about why the Freehold currently seems to be so unfriendly toward her Court, and advised caution on her part. To soften my words, I assured her once more that the Dream House is a haven for all changelings, and that we would never let anything happen to her while she was under our protection. She took my hand briefly and sort of nervously thanked me for my words.

                    Damn, she’s cute, and she seems to be interested in getting to know me better. She also seems like a genuinely good person. She mentioned that another reason she was nervous was due to the presence of Salk and Marguerite, both of whom are prominent Night Court members. She said that she joined the Day Court because she genuinely wanted to help people and that she doesn’t care at all about the rivalry with the Night Court… but she’s not convinced that they feel the same way about her. Once again I did my best to assure her that she was safe with us, and I think I put her mind at ease, at least a little bit. If the interest I saw in her eyes is real, then maybe the two of us could get together some time, or at least strike up a platonic friendship. I think I’d like that.

                    After the crowds dispersed, we settled down in the TV room and got started on the Disney movies. We watched five films last night, and most of them were quite good. “The Princess and the Frog” was probably the weakest of the entries we watched, but it was still exciting and funny enough to keep me interested. The villain – a voodoo conjure man named Dr. Facilier – was quite creepy, and ably voiced by a veteran voice actor named Keith David. Seems like he’s a name to watch for.

                    Our second feature was probably the best of the evening… maybe the best animated film I’ve ever seen, if you come to that. Even Julia came down to watch the show with us, as she was an admitted fan of the film. “Beauty and the Beast” is apparently the only animated film which was ever nominated for the Best Picture Oscar, and it’s richly deserved. The story was tight and engaging, the characters were likeable (or despicable, in the case of the villain), and the music was good enough that Disney eventually decided to adapt it into a stage musical. I’m glad I’ve seen it, and I’m sure I’ll see it again before too much time has passed.

                    The third film was a loose adaptation of the Hans Christian Anderson story “The Little Mermaid.” It was also quite good, and I understand that it’s often credited with ushering in the ‘second golden age’ of Disney films in the early 90s. Again the music was top-notch; most of it had a Caribbean flair that set it apart from more traditional musical theater. The comic relief was side-splittingly funny, but it didn’t detract at all from the love story – or the family drama – at the heart of the film. On any other night, I probably would have said it was the best film we saw that day. As it is, it came in third.

                    Next up was “Mulan,” a film based on a Chinese folk tale about a young woman who disguises herself as a man so that the government won’t press her ailing father into military service. It was good, though not as top-notch as many of the other films we watched. The cross-dressing angle was explored repeatedly in a family-friendly way, which led to a lot of light-hearted moments. The story sort of gently explored the nature of gender roles, and though the moral of the story seemed to be something along the lines of “don’t underestimate girls; they can do anything boys can do,” I can’t help but feel like some of the humor ended up reinforcing certain long-standing sexual stereotypes. Also, the creators of the film seemed a bit confused about who the barbarian invaders were. They kept calling them Huns, but they were portrayed as Mongols. The latter would have made a lot more sense from a historical standpoint… not that I claim to be an authority on that subject or anything.

                    Our final film of the evening was “The Lion King,” which I had never heard of before. Several people hastened to inform me that while Disney had originally advertised the film as their first which was not based upon a previously existing story, they had actually lifted most of the characters and large portions of the plot from a Japanese cartoon about an albino lion cub.

                    That brought me up short, and I held up my hand for attention. “Wait,” I said, “are you trying to tell me that this is a big budget movie version of ‘Kimba the White Lion?’”

                    For once it was everyone else who blinked at me. What can I say? They used to show that cartoon on TV all the time when I was a kid, and I loved it. I can still remember watching it on the sofa in our living room with Mom on Saturday mornings. She used to laugh…

                    Anyway, if any of the films we watched last night truly rivaled “Beauty and the Beast,” it was that one. The animation was gorgeous. The songs used traditional African instruments and rhythm patterns, and the story was an epic tale of a young man coming of age and finding his destiny. The comic relief characters were never too distracting (and those hyenas could be absolutely chilling in some scenes), and the villains were incredibly vile. Whether Disney owned up to its source material or no, I don’t think anyone can argue against the fact that they produced something truly remarkable.

                    So yes, five films intended to take my mind off my troubles. One about a girl who loses her father. One about a girl who has to rescue her father, and how he sacrifices everything in an attempt to save her from her prison in a fairy tale castle. One about a girl who acts out and rebels against her father. One about a girl who dresses as a man to save her father’s life. And one about a lion who has to avenge his father’s death. Whooooooole lotta fathers goin’ on.

                    So by the time the final credits rolled on that film, the sun was up and a fifty-pound lead weight had settled back into the pit of my belly. I sort of shakily asked D if she was ready to get dressed for church, and then hastily zapped us both with some Spring Court energy.

                    The rest of the motley asked if I wanted them to come to the church with us, as a show of support. I thought about it for a moment, but in the end it didn’t seem like a good idea. “I want you all to know that I appreciate what you’re offering to do,” I said softly, “but I think I have to do this on my own.”

                    D and I showered and dressed, drove across town, and kissed chastely on the front steps. Then she headed into the sanctuary, I went to the choir loft, and the two of us settled in for the longest sermon of my life.

                    No, I couldn’t tell you what the sermon was about. I was too nervous; too busy thinking about the conversation to come. Honestly, when the choir sang today, I’m surprised I could produce anything louder than a croak.

                    So after the benediction I changed out of my robes and joined D in the sanctuary while dad said goodbye to his parishioners. When the last car had pulled out of the parking lot, he turned to us and motioned for us to join him in his study with a kindly smile.

                    I sat in a comfortable chair while he settled into a similar chair across the mahogany expanse of his desk. D settled into a smaller chair behind me after giving my shoulders a squeeze of support. I looked around the office, taking in the various and sundry religious texts and sedate framed photographs. I think I was subconsciously trying to put off the conversation, but dad was having none of it.

                    He cleared his throat gently. “Now… what can I do for you today, my dear?” he asked kindly.

                    I sighed. “Reverend, I… I need to tell you a few things. This isn’t going to be easy for me to say, and it’s not going to be easy for you to hear, either. I…” I broke off after a moment. “Wow, this is hard,” I muttered to myself. How do you even start to explain the circumstances of a changeling’s existence to someone who’s not in the know?

                    He looked concerned. “You know that you can tell me anything, Lorelei,” he said after a moment. “I will not judge you, and will do what I can to help you.”

                    I nodded. “I know, Reverend Wells,” I said, “it’s just that you’re probably going to leave this room thinking that I’m crazy. I know I would.” I let that hang in the air for a moment, and then forged ahead. “What are your feelings about the supernatural?” I asked in a rush.

                    That brought him up short. “You will have to be more specific,” he replied carefully. It wasn’t the answer I was expecting. I should probably ask him about what exactly he meant by that at some point.

                    I thought about it for a moment. “I want to know if you believe in things… phenomena which can’t be explained by science, but aren’t necessarily religious in nature.”

                    He smiled warmly. “Everything is religious in nature,” he opined, “as everything is a part of God’s ordered creation.”

                    I smiled back at that; nothing can stop dad when he gets invested in a religious debate. “Okay, granted,” I said, “but there are some things people tell stories about which aren’t mundane, but aren’t necessarily related to the scripture. Ghosts, vampires, and the like. Are you willing to accept that some of these things might be real?”

                    His brows knit together in thought. “I am not prepared to discount any possibility,” he admitted, “though I confess that I would need some evidence before lending credence to such tales.”

                    I sighed. “If I could show you proof of the existence of the supernatural, would you be willing to listen to my story?”

                    He nodded. “I will listen to what you have to say regardless of proof, Lorelei. Have you encountered one of these creatures? A ghost or a vampire?”

                    I shook my head. “Are you familiar with old European fairy stories, Reverend? About how the fairies would abduct people and take them off to their homeland, only to leave duplicates in their place?”

                    He nodded again, and then said something which sort of surprised me. “The changeling stories, yes,” he said in acknowledgement. “Do you fear that someone you know has been replaced in such a way?”

                    “Yes,” I answered softly. “Me.”

                    He had nothing to say to that.

                    “They took me and two of my friends from the side of a road near my childhood home,” I said, lowering my eyes so that he couldn’t easily see my tears. “They replaced me with a duplicate so that nobody would look for me. They changed me in awful, fundamental ways. And then I escaped, and made my way back to the mortal world.” I looked up, wiping the tears from my cheeks. “Would you like to see what they did to me, Reverend?” I asked plaintively.

                    He nodded, obviously sensing that this was something I needed to do. I crafted a quick pledge; nothing too binding, just ensuring that he would hear me out and withhold judgment until I’d had my say. Oh, and I tried to impress upon him how very important it is to keep the existence of changelings a secret, too. A few minutes later it was done, and he was sitting back in his chair with wide eyes.

                    “It’s… remarkable,” he said, his gaze staying on my wings. “I’ve never seen anything like it. It’s almost angelic…”

                    I shook my head. “There’s nothing divine about me, Reverend,” I said, “I’m just trying to be the best person I can, like everybody else.”

                    I gave him a few minutes to get used to the sight. I saw him darting quick glances at D as well, so I confirmed his suspicion that she had also escaped from Arcadia, though her alterations weren’t as visibly extreme as my own. I told him that he would probably start to see others like us on the streets now that I’d let him in on the secret, but that it would be safest if he paid them no mind.

                    Once he’d adjusted to the new world in which he found himself, he posed me the most difficult question of all. “I’m honored by the trust you’ve shown in me, my dear,” he said, “but may I ask why you have chosen to reveal yourself to me in this way?”

                    I sighed. “Because there’s still one thing I haven’t told you. Maybe the most important thing of all.”

                    He quirked an eyebrow. “I’m listening,” he said gently.

                    I looked down again, unable to do this face to face. “I told you that I was Taken from the side of a highway near my home. What I didn’t tell you was that when I found my way back, I learned that three and a half decades had passed while I was in Arcadia. I was Taken in 1978. To me, it seemed like… well, time is funny over there, so I’m not really sure, but I would have sworn that it was no more than a year.”

                    He shifted uncomfortably in his seat, but didn’t interrupt me. I pushed on.

                    “When I got back, I looked for my father. I had grown up in the Midwest, but I soon learned that he had moved to the West Coast, and I followed him out here…” And all of a sudden I couldn’t say any more. The words just stuck in my throat. Luckily, I didn’t have to.

                    “Juliette?” he asked in a very small, quiet voice.

                    I found my voice again, sort of. “Hi, Daddy,” I whispered. I don’t know why I used that particular term; I haven’t called him that since before Mom died. But it felt right, maybe because I felt small and frightened and completely at his mercy in that moment.

                    I looked up at him. He looked stricken, flabbergasted, completely at a loss for words. After about a minute, he rose and walked unsteadily toward the door. “I… I need some water. Will the two of you please excuse me for a moment?”

                    He was gone for a few minutes which seemed to stretch into hours. D got up and put her hands on my shoulders, rubbing gently while I sat there and trembled. It was such a comfort to have her there when I was at my most vulnerable. I’ll never be able to thank her enough for the strength her presence gave me. I could never have done what I did without her.

                                                        The last two days have been… well, it would be clichéd to say “magical,” but that’s honestly how I feel. Things are going very well for me right now, and I do not intend to look this particular gift horse in the mouth. With only a single minor moment of existential terror to interrupt my winning streak, I feel like things are finally going my way again.

                                                        So I was sitting in the kitchen waiting for everybody else to get up so that we could get on the road yesterday morning, bouncing up and down on the balls of my feet and basically acting like a kid on Christmas morning. The others were probably pretty amused by how excited I got, but what can I say? Maybe having a dad again is making me want to regain my squandered childhood. D – God bless her – seemed to find it cute and charming.

                                                        But business has to come first, of course. Gawain told the lot of us that he had made contact with the incubus which had been dogging Leanne. It seemed to be a reasonable being, describing itself as an information broker. It had been gathering information on Leanne’s activities for the Hollywood Courts, but mostly because it saw a chance for gain rather than out of any political ideals or loyalty. In exchange for its freedom and safety, it offered to give some of its information to us instead of Hollywood. Gawain crafted a simple pledge to bind it to its word, and made plans to meet with it in his own dreams the following night. Calamity is going to go into the dream with him. I had to applaud my motley for going with negotiation first instead of resorting to violence!

                                                        So in any case, by the time we had exhausted the possibilities of this subject, Molly had arrived and we were ready to hit the road. As there were eleven of us, we couldn’t all fit in Gawain’s van, so we took Jacob’s jeep, too. I volunteered to ride in the jeep so that I could feel the wind on my wings, and of course D opted to ride with me. Luna rounded out our group, naturally.

                                                        I put on a suitably pretty ensemble that made me look sexy but wouldn’t get us thrown out of the park. It wasn’t anything fancy, just a dark top with spaghetti straps and a mid-thigh length skirt. D was decked out in her leathers, of course. Marguerite put on a t-shirt and a pair of short-shorts (like me, she was careful not to be too daring today), and even consented to bring a pair of sandals along as a concession to the park’s rules. Julia went all-out, wearing a dress similar to that worn by Belle in Beauty and the Beast, but the skirt was dark-blue-fading-to-purple, which looked fantastic on her. The others just threw on jeans, shorts and t-shirts, of course.

                                                        About an hour later we pulled into the parking lot and made our way toward the front gates. We quickly found our way to Tomorrowland, where Gawain pointed us toward Astro Blasters, which was a sort of combination of ride and shooting gallery. Guests were expected to shoot Laser Tag style guns at glowing alien targets as they’re pulled slowly through the ride environment. The ride tallies up a score for each participant, and at the end of the day the top 100 shooters are displayed at the park’s exit and given a commemorative postcard with their score.

                                                        It didn’t really surprise me that Molly took the top score for the entire day. Though I’d never actually seen her shoot before, I’d heard stories of her legendary prowess with guns of all sorts. Calamity was in the top ten as well, which again was not too surprising, considering that she taught me everything I know about marksmanship. What did surprise me was that Jacob and I were neck and neck, and both of our scores were in the top fifty for the day! When she saw that, Calamity looked at each of us proudly and gave us a You-Have-Done-Well-My-Pupils nod. And I must admit, it’s nice to be recognized for my prowess with a (sorta) combat skill… especially in the company of my motley.

                                                        After that we stood in line for the legendary Space Mountain. Now at this point, I must confess, the childlike glee I took in my surroundings was replaced by an all-too-adult sense of mischief. I leaned over to D as we were about to board the ride and whispered “so… we’re riding a roller coaster together, and it’s dark and loud…”

                                                        She smirked back at me. “You’re not going to let this go, are you?” she asked.

                                                        I thought about it. “Well, considering how often you bring up the fact that you’ve had sex on a roller coaster – and I haven’t – I can only assume that it’s something I need to experience for myself,” I returned with my sexiest grin.

                                                        She sighed and rolled her eyes in mock exasperation. “Okay,” she relented after a moment, “I’ll take care of you.”

                                                        The attendants seated us, and D’s hand crept under the waistband of my skirt. It wasn’t very comfortable, and the ride only lasts a few minutes… but D knows my body really well, and with the two of us working together we managed to get the job done. In fact, I was giving a pretty good O-face when we were captured on camera near the end of the ride. Of course I bought a copy of the photo (from a smirking girl at the exit kiosk).

                                                        I leaned in and kissed D tenderly as we walked back out into the sunshine. “Thanks sweetie,” I whispered, “that was a ride I’ll never forget.”

                                                        She raised an eyebrow in that dead-sexy way she has. “You know there are like five more coasters in this park,” she husked.

                                                        I chuckled. “Yeah, but they’re mostly outdoors and pretty well-lit,” I replied. “I’m as exhibitionist as anyone, but… well, there are a lot of kids here, D. Think you can handle us just being PG girlfriends for the rest of the day?”

                                                        She looked around surreptitiously to make sure nobody else was listening in, and then whispered “No day spent with you is wasted, Jules.” She’s so sweet when she doesn’t have to worry about her tough-girl image!

                                                        The lot of us wandered into the Magic Kingdom area next, where Gawain geeked out over the Cinderella castle, which is apparently based on some real medieval structure in Germany. I swear, that guy knows more about the middle-ages than anyone else I’ve ever met. It was interesting at first, but one can only hear so much about crenellations and flying buttresses and voussoirs before you just start tuning it out.

                                                        As we moved toward the next attraction, I spotted someone I’d been looking for. I grabbed Jacob’s arm and dragged him over to stand next to the woman dressed as Princess Elsa. “Can I take a picture of my friend with you?” I asked her. She agreed, and posed next to him. He looked pretty grumpy about the whole situation, and refused to smile no matter what I did. “Come on, Jacob,” I said sweetly, “there’s no use holding a grudge about something like this. Let it go!” Then I snapped the picture as quickly as I could. So now I have a picture of Princess Elsa laughing uproariously while Jacob flips me the bird.

                                                        And then it happened… the one smudge on an otherwise perfect day. As I was walking around, taking in the sights and half-listening to Gawain drone on about the castle (again), Goofy came strutting around the corner… you know, in that way cartoon dogs do. He was surrounded by a gaggle of admiring kids and their parents, and he was talking about the “It’s a Small World” ride. And then he said it.

                                                        “Gawrsh, I’m sure y’all know the words! Sing it with me, boys and girls!”

                                                        And just like that I couldn’t stop myself. The familiar words of the song seemed to well up in my chest, pushing their way up my throat and out of my mouth. It was like trying to hold back a yawn, or a sneeze, or an orgasm… I could only fight it for so long. I went through one verse and one rendition of the chorus while all of my friends were staring at me like some sort of psychopath.

                                                        “…it’s a small, small, wooooorld what the fuck?” I finished a minute later. I caught a glimpse of Jacob filming me with his phone, but I suppose that’s only fair.

                                                        Everybody exchanged nervous glances. “Do you think that’s…?” Gawain asked tentatively.

                                                        Calamity nodded. “We should probably test it though,” she added. Then she turned toward me. “Sorry about this, Lori… but could you please sing ‘For He’s a Jolly Good Fellow’ for me?”

                                                        And there it was again. It was like a compulsion; I just couldn’t say no to her request. “…which nobody can denyyyyyy you bitch,” I finished, glaring at her.

                                                        “Sorry,” she said again, “but we had to test it. I think we’ve found your frailty, Lori.” Her sheepish grin turned wry after a minute. “At least now we know you don’t have to take orders from dog-headed men, though,” she said with a chuckle.

                                                        I apologized for calling her a bitch, then held myself aloof from the rest of the group for a while. D walked with me, her hand firmly around my waist. “It’s not so bad, as frailties go, babe,” she soothed me.

                                                        “I know,” I replied glumly, “and I’m grateful it’s not something really debilitating. It’s just… I felt like I’d come so far since Arcadia, and this is just another reminder that I’ll never really escape from that damn cage. I’ll always be what She made me.”

                                                        “Yeah, well,” she said, subdued, “Calamity’s right, in any case. Now that we know what it is, we can avoid your trigger in the future. C’mon, babe… don’t let it ruin your day. You’ve been having so much fun so far, and your dad’s coming over to meet everybody tomorrow…”

                                                        I sniffed once, and then nodded. “You’re right,” I said after a moment. “One dark cloud doesn’t spoil a picnic. What do you want to do now?”

                                                        She shrugged. “Ride another ride? See one of the shows? Maybe play one of these games?”

                                                        My eyes fell on the High Striker booths, and I felt a naughty grin coming on. I leaned in and batted my eyes. “Win me a stuffed animal?” I asked in a sultry tone. “Maybe… a bigger stuffed animal than that bear Gawain won for Calamity?”

She turned to Gawain and said “you’re not going to let some trash talking little girl outshine you, are you?”

                                                        I looked into D’s eyes as we approached the games. “Remember sweetie, you’re not just doing this for me… you’re doing it for the entire Latino community.”

They hefted and tested their mallets (each of them had taken the heaviest available). Then the glamour started to flow. I couldn’t tell you all of the contracts and tricks they tried, but I know that their mantles were flaring like nobody’s business.

The bells clanged… and then both platforms groaned as the springs and fulcrums folded in on themselves and collapsed. The hawkers gawped at my friends for a few seconds before dully telling them that they could take any prize they liked and hanging “Out of Order” signs on the attractions.

D marched straight to a larger-than-life-sized version of Olaf the snowman from Frozen, marched it over to me, and held its stick arms out while dutifully reciting “My name is Olaf and I like warm hugs.”

I’ve been writing in this diary ever since, but I really should start getting ready for tonight’s show soon. Plus, I need to talk to Julia and figure out how I’m going to implement my surprise for Marguerite.

                                                            We took two cars to the park, my jeep and Gawain’s van. We had to fit the whole motley plus Desiree, Gloria, Molly and Luna. Once we arrived at the park we pretty much headed straight for the Astro Blasters ride. It was like a really slow roller coaster with laser guns. Needless to say Molly won for the day, and I do mean that for the whole day, across the whole park. She was damned good. Calamity wasn’t far behind her and ranked up there for the day as well. Lori and I ranked up there too. I was a bit surprised by Lori scoring so high, but she has been taking the same lessons from Calamity that I have, so maybe it shouldn’t have been a surprise. We were not quite as high as the others, but still up there. Luna, Insincere Marguerite and De La Rocha came in just under us. I think the whole lot of us afforded a pretty good showing for the Dream House and Freehold in general if you ask me.

                                                            After that I think the majority of the group went on Space Mountain. Luna and I stayed behind and just watched as the others rode the ride. Luna could not go on the really fast coasters because of her wings so I decided to stay behind and keep her company. It was just as fun to watch the other people in the park scurry around and their reactions to the ride. After that we headed towards Fantasy Land. On the way there I heard Lori exclaim “Ah ha!” and ran over and snagged a poor woman dressed as Elsa from “Frozen,” and she proceeded to try and get a picture of the two of us together. She maintained that I should just “let it go.” At which point Elsa laughed and I flipped her off. She got that image immortalized on film. I hope that she is happy with that picture. Not that I begrudge her the opportunity to get it. It’s just not something that I would have agreed to if she had asked. My thoughts last night ran too close to the Lady to find the connection appealing. That said, the poor girl that they had playing Elsa was cute and she looked like she was used to being used for willing and unwilling pictures alike.

                                                            And as if karma isn’t a bitch we discovered shortly after that just what Lori’s frailty is. Some guy dressed as Goofy asked folks around him to sing along with “It’s a Small World,” and Lori was compelled to do it. I couldn’t resist. I took a video of it on my phone. I know Lori is kind of freaking out about it right now, but we’ll be laughing about it later. We tested our theory that she had to sing if someone asked by requesting that she sing “For He’s a Jolly Good Fellow.” It worked. She sang it and promptly called Calamity a bitch for making her do it. There was no real heat in her words so I don’t think she actually meant it, and Calamity didn’t take it seriously.

                                                            After that we went to go visit Sleeping Beauty’s castle and Gawain kind of geeked out. Apparently it’s a recreation of the Neuschwanstein Castle in Bavaria, Germany. Mad King Ludwig’s castle. Gawain was keen on explaining the history of the actual castle and all about the architecture. He really is quite knowledgeable about it and seemed to have fun educating the rest of us. Soon after the castle we went to go play some games. Some folks played the strength game. Gloria won a stuffed Lady from “Lady and the Tramp.” Insincere Marguerite also won a prize for Julia. It was a stuffed Mrs. Potts from “Beauty and the Beast.” They both did really well. Then Gawain and De La Rocha had their turn and needless to say they won the biggest prizes for their lovely ladies. Gawain won a giant stuffed Iago from “Aladdin,” and De La Rocha won a giant stuffed Olaf from “Frozen.” Not surprisingly to us they both broke the games. The employees had to put up an “Out of order” sign after we left.

                                                            While they were breaking the strength game some of us played a throwing game and did pretty well. I afforded myself well and won a stuffed Robin Hood for Luna and Insincere Marguerite won Julia a Lumiere to go with her Mrs. Potts. Around this time Gawain also got a Knight’s helmet along with a plastic sword and shield. It was kind of funny to see a suit of armor wearing a plastic helmet, but the mortals can’t see through the mask, so it worked. Also throughout the day Marguerite has been wandering off and coming back minutes later with her purse getting fuller after each trip. I found out later that she had been pickpocketing money, drugs, and anything else she could get her hands on from mostly College students. There was a surprising amount of hard drugs in the growing stash. I think Insincere Marguerite threw those out and kept the less dangerous drugs for use later on.

                                                            One of the last rides that we went on was the log flume ride. Luna and Julia did not ride it, stating that they did not want to get wet. I did ride it. It was warm out and I wanted to cool down. At least as much as I could in this ever sunny city. After we’d dried off some we went to dinner at the castle from “Beauty and the Beast.” It was passable French food, at least according to Insincere Marguerite. I thought it was pretty good, but I don’t have the most experience with French cooking. I took Marguerite’s word on it. I think everyone enjoyed the meal though.

                                                            After dinner we waited around until they set off the fireworks. It doesn’t matter how old you are, they are pretty. It reminded me of living in Portland. Every July we would go and watch the fireworks. We always did stuff like that as a family. It wasn’t really a holiday that we celebrated, but we liked the fireworks all the same. Sometimes I miss those carefree days.

                                                            When we got back to the Dream House we decided to do our normal Monday night activities and watch movies. Since we had company we decided on watching “Amelie” and “Princess Bride.” Molly seemed to enjoy herself, which was kind of the point. We had all watched both of those movies in the past, but we re-watched them because “Amelie” is about a girl on her own discovering the world around her and the “Princess Bride” is about love. In a very simplistic way, but it is about love. So, we watched them again and Molly watched them for the first time. I think a good time was had by all.

                                                            The next morning me and Calamity went to our bi-weekly therapy session. We discussed some things about my family since it came up. The fireworks reminded me of them and my therapist thought that it was worth spending half of our session just talking about my parents. I didn’t really want to keep going since I’ll never see them again, but he thought it was worth exploring. For me it was just a reminder of what I can never have again. I tried to steer the conversation away, but the doctor kept coming back to it. Maybe this therapy idea wasn’t such a good idea after all.

                                                            When we got back there was a couple of important things disclosed to the group. The first was the meeting between Calamity, Gawain and the Incubus. The meeting seemed to go well and they found out what the Incubus was up to. It turns out that the Hollywood courts are interested in the growing Diurnal courts in our area and how we’re handling it. They are also interested in and a little afraid of some of the more powerful members of the Freehold like Madame Spidertoes and Tommy Rawhead. It didn’t know much about the brainwashing of the Hollywood Courtiers and said as much. It said that Hollywood was also interested in how much, if any, information Leanne was giving up about them and their activities. She was fairly highly placed among the Hollywood courts and was the lover of one of the Monarchs. The Incubus has not yet reported back to the Hollywood courts and may not if we can keep its interest here. I don’t know if there was any further plans regarding the Incubus, but Gawain did seem to leave things open ended there.

                                                            Before we all got together and talked me and Calamity went out back and sparred. I think it was a good thing. We have sparred together long enough that we know each other’s moves, but that wasn’t the point. The point was the release, and that is what we got. We needed to let go of extraneous emotion and thoughts and what better way to do that then to focus entirely on fighting? I think it worked for what we needed it for and that was the point. I may be familiar with pretty much all of Calamity’s moves by now, but each day is different. There is also the point that it is damn good exercise.

                                                            After we had showered and gotten ready for the meeting with Lori’s dad De la Rocha called us all together to have a quick talk before he arrived. What she had to say was not what I was expecting. She wanted to explain to the rest of us before the Reverend arrived is that her first name is Maria. He knows her as such and it would seem strange if her friends did not know her by the same name. It was Calamity that put the pieces together. She asked if she was the same Maria that Twitch was so afraid of. Turns out that she is. I was so very tempted to tell her flat out that she was my new favorite person in the world, but she didn’t seem overly happy about the fact that she was the person that Twitch was most afraid of. I suppose I can understand that to some extent. I am not proud of the things that I did while in the Lady’s service, but can’t say that I’ve been faced with those actions face to face. She has. She knows that she is the person that Twitch is afraid of, and that might be something to be concerned about except the fact that Twitch has been a horrible person. He has done horrific things to people of his own free will. He might be afraid that this nebulous “Maria” might find him, but that didn’t make him into a good person. He could have just tried to hide and I wouldn’t have such a problem with him. But no, he had to decide to experiment with people’s brains. With their emotions and fears. He drugged the ever living shit out of a friend of mine to see what he could make him believe. Twitch is not a good person and if he lives in fear of the mythical “Maria” then good. He deserves no less.

                                                            Maria was a little defensive about her past and I added that it was Twitch that made his own choices. No one made him do the things that he did, that was all him. She seemed to think that it was her that drove him to Darke’s side and to follow his orders, but I don’t agree. He could have just hidden. He didn’t need to follow Darke’s twisted orders and do things to people. He didn’t have to remake people into complete strangers. Who knows what he did to Molly and Vik before Grey turned t hem into completely different people. There is still such a thing as free will. Twitch acted of his own free will and for that he will have my enmity. In the end I think we agreed to disagree. De la Rocha has her idea and I have mine. I don’t think she’ll appreciate it but I think she is one of my new favorite people.

                                                            So after all of that we got dressed and ready for Lori’s father to arrive. Calamity was dressed business casual, Lori and De La Rocha were dressed nice, but not quite “church nice.” The rest of us wore jeans and a t-shirt. I didn’t feel the overwhelming need to impress Lori’s dad, but I did make sure that my attire was neat. So, Robert, Lori’s father, showed up on time. I think he was a little taken aback by the appearance of a few of us, Calamity, Gawain and Luna in particular. I can see that. Their appearance is the furthest from normal human out of our group. I am just paler than normal and have frost going down my face and arms. Insincere Marguerite is lanky, but no more so than usual. The rest are all fairly normal in appearance if you ask me. Gawain though is a big suit of armor while Luna has beautiful moth wings and Calamity has glass shards coming out of her shoulders and arms.

                                                            Overall I think the meeting went well. There was conversation about what Changelings do and Calamity mentioned the bit about gathering glamour. Gawain explained things in terms of trees and oxygen. He pretty much said that what we do does not harm humans in anyway. We just gather what they give off normally. Calamity also mentioned how much of a rarity that it is that Lorelei got to reconnect with her family. Most of us do not have good relationships or any relationships with our families. Robert offered to be the go between to help us bridge the gap between us and our family. At least one of us already has a relationship with their family, others like me, do not. This is where the whole conversation about fetches started. We explained to Robert what a fetch was. That it was a creation by the Gentry to replace the human that had been taken. We explained that they were made of odds and ends and not perfect copies of the originals. There were always mistakes. Robert seemed to feel for us and wanted to know what he or the church could do to reconnect us with our families. I think most of us turned down his kind offer. I think most of us think that it is too late to try and do that. I for one don’t think that my family would have me back even if they knew about me. I have not been a good person since I’ve been gone and they do not need the complications that I would bring to their lives. It is better that they do not know the truth about who/what they call their son.

                                                            Robert also commented on our mantles. We explained about the Courts and he surmised, accurately, that if we have different Courts then there must be a fair number of Changelings in the area. Gawain confirmed that there are about one hundred of us between Malibu, Santa Monica and Venice Beach. Sometimes I forget just how many of us there are here, and how many there are in other places. How many people have the Gentry taken? How many people have found their way back? Anyway, Robert also asked what the church could do to help us. I think he meant Changelings in general. I don’t know that we really had an answer for him.

                                                            After our talk Gawain took Robert on a tour of the house. They stayed away from our bedrooms, but they went through the stage, backstage and costume areas. Gawain was especially proud of showing off the Hedgespun clothes that we have. I think he also told Robert about dream crafting. I only caught bits and pieces of the conversation but Gawain seemed to be thoroughly enjoying himself, as did Robert. Once the tour was over Lori escorted her father to the door and they said their good-byes.

                                                            Annika showed up about that time for her lessons with Calamity. And Gawain, as per usual, took up broom and dustpan and began to sweep near the stage. I’ve seen them practice, and Gawain sweeping, enough times, so I decided to do something different. I was reminded of glass sculpting when I sat down at the bar to tidy up a bit, which then led to thoughts on ice sculpting. Julia had asked me if I could do an ice sculpture for their wedding and I figured that it wouldn’t be bad to practice. So, I got some ice out of the freezer and began shaping it into different things. At first I was just messing around making different shapes, but then Luna came by and inspiration hit. I started carefully sculpting the ice to look like Robin Hood from the Disney movie. Luna figured out what I was doing pretty early on and stayed to watch me work. I haven’t just let loose and played with ice for a really long time. Since before I came back to ‘the world.’ I used to be more playful, back before my memories and my conscience came back. I used to create all kinds of figures out of ice, from pieces as small as a chess pawn to grand sculptures that were as big as I was. I tended toward natural shapes, like animals or nature in general, trees, flowers and such. I made several things for the Lady in my time. Depending on her mood she either praised my work or smashed it without a thought. Her moods were mercurial, like the winds, and I never knew how she would receive my little gifts, but I kept making them. When I served her my the Lady was everything. But right now I just want to make pretty things for Luna and my friends. Maybe I’ll start making little ice cubes shaped like flowers. I can put them in the drinks for motley mates or friends. Might be an interesting thing to do for the regular guests as well. It would definitely make the club more unique. I guess it all depends on how long it takes me to make them. Something to look into though.

                                                            Care and Feeding

                                                            Insincere Marguerite DeLisle followed Julia Oldacre into the large attic room Julia had turned into her studio. Julia had changed into a long white dress that was very much covered in paint, while Insincere Marguerite still had on her shorts and French flag t-shirt. Julia turned and smiled at her fiancée as the more petite girl sat down cross-legged on the floor.

                                                            “So you like Pirates, do you?”

                                                            The French girl held up a finger. “No, I like the romantic fiction of pirates. The sort of…,” she made a rotating gesture with her hands, “The magic islands and the pirate women and the things. Not real pirates.”

                                                            “Of course,” the Artist sighed. “But...”

                                                            “Real pirates are stinky.”

                                                            “Um, Bon-Bon?”

                                                            “Stinky.” She said the word emphatically, with a tone of finality.

                                                            Julia sighed good naturedly, looking down as her fiancée looked up. “So you like the ride and the movies, neither of which are stinky?”

                                                            “Oui!”

                                                            Julia smiled. Insincere Marguerite usually only spoke French for her. Occasionally Lori. The Artist tried not to frown, to push any jealous thoughts out of her mind…

                                                            “Ma petite chou?” The grub cocked her head, trying to read Julia’s expression.

                                                            “Sorry Bon-Bon…,” Julia sighed. She considered lying a moment, but decided against it. Her fiancée could read her too well. She decided on a dodge instead.

                                                            “I appreciate the Beauty and the Beast toys. I knew you would do well with the ball toss, but I was surprised by the strength test.”

                                                            Insincere Marguerite blinked at the change of subject. “Well, I mean, the other girls got toys…I couldn’t just, you know, leave you bereft. I am sorry I could not give you something as big as they got.”

                                                            Julia giggled. “You really worry about that, don’t you?” She knelt to look her lover in the face. Insincere Marguerite’s eyes were searching. “Bon-Bon. I am serious. I am happy with you. You are not failing me. But I worry.”

                                                            She took the grub’s hands into hers.

                                                            “I don’t want you to think I love you less. You’ve become more empathic, more loving. I love that. I mean, the sex is great, and I love it when you take control, but that is not something we can build a relationship, a marriage, on.”

                                                            This time it was Julia who took a hand to Marguerite’s face. The Darkling’s deep blue eyes were still searching, as if they were looking for something more as Julia looked in them. What could she possibly be looking for but love and admiration?

                                                            The disdain she believed lurked there. Julia had been afraid of that.

                                                            “Ma mimi.” This time the Artist spoke French. Insincere Marguerite froze at being called a pussycat. “There is a scared girl behind your eyes. I see her. Lori is not the only one that can help her. That can help you. Don’t turn away from me. “

                                                            “Miaou?”

                                                            Julia sensed the cat noise indicated uncertainly. Anxiety. She placed her other hand on her lover’s other cheek and kissed her. Hard. The Darkling almost lost her balance, but her lover held her up. She held her face and looked in her eyes.

                                                            “Is that what you needed? Is this what you need? Because damnit that and a whole lot more I will do. I’m serious. I still want to marry you. Do you know what that means? Please stop and think for a moment what that means about how I feel about you, and whether it means I think you have become a lesser person. This is not inertia. This is not me failing to see how you have changed. This is love goddamnit Marguerite. Insincere Marguerite. Whatever you want to be called.”

                                                            Normally, the Darkling would protest at her lack of honorific. At the moment she could not find her voice.

                                                            “I was willing to move out with you when you were broken. I was willing to leave Lori and Maria for you. If I knew I would be with them, I would have never thought I would do that for anyone a year ago….”

                                                            The taller woman let go, the smaller collapsed briefly into a pile of limbs, before pulling into something approaching a human shape. She looked up at her lover.

                                                            Julia looked down. “That…that looks painful.” She laughed, nervously. That made Insincere Marguerite smile. She shrugged slightly. “You get used to it.”

                                                            Julia sat down cross-legged to face her. The grub tilted her head. “What do you want to hear? Of course I love you. I believe you love me. That part, that was not in doubt. It is the…,” She clicked once, trying to think, “It is my abilities, it is…more intrinsic.”

                                                            Julia shrugged. “I saw you get the courage to propose, get Desiree to abandon her former master, bounce back from inadvertent but major emotional pain from one of the dearest people to you, recover from a betrayal from your best friend, and steal enough from a single trip to Disneyland to make a down payment on a boat and a car to pull it. That’s outside the weed and cards. Frankly, that is more impressive than anything you did before this month.”

                                                            “But I don’t….,” The Darkling paused, considering. Her reverie was interrupted by her fiancée putting her hands on her own.

                                                            “Mimi I don’t expect you to be okay tomorrow. Calamity and Lorelei hurt you. But…you can’t argue that you are now greater then before, that,” The Artist imitated her love’s cadence, “That the great Insincere Marguerite has not shown herself to be as great as before, that she is still pulling off the…the…heists impressionnants? That she is a…a voleur formidable?”

                                                            The Lurker simply stared. “I…will try.”

                                                            “Bon.” Julia rose and walked over to get her sketchbook, bringing it back. “I need to get the portrait of Desiree ready before she performs. And I would like a very still ball of warm in my lap.” She smiled at her love.

                                                            Insincere Marguerite smiled back. When the Artist was seated, she crawled bonelessly into her lap and curled up into a ball. Julia scratched her head gently while she considered her composition. And just like that, she was fast asleep.
