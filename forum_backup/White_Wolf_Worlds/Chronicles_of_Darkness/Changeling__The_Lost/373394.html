    Losts population explosion

    01-17-2015, 04:20 AM
    As most of you know, I run an episodic game for our RPG club about creation of changelings Freehold in small ( about 2500 citizens ), town in New England. As there are surprisingly high population of Lost in there ( now it's something like about 10 changelings returning in few weeks, and still counting ) it's one of main "meta-mysteries" of the game and glue of the game. Second is sudden disappearance of previous, first colonization Freehold a 200-300 years ago.

    But here is a thing - I got the problem ( there are many people missing and returning in this place ) but still do not have answer on it, or even ideas on possible answers. Question of topic is simple: What could be reason for this high ( almost tidal ) return of Lost to this very much location, in such high numbers? Suddenly, they will be like 1% of population in general citizens in short notice.

    On first though I got idea it's some kind of Gentry play, where Lords and Ladies are letting changelings free for they own amusement - but I still do not have why would they done this. Why would couple of Keepers steal many humans in region for years, and suddenly now let them free in almost the same time?

    Or maybe you have a better reason than this?

      1. Someone recently recreate a landmark that had been demolished years ago. It becomes an anchor in the minds of many of the refugees and helps guide them home.

      2. A Pledge was struck here a long time ago with a Lady. Wrestled from her from an iron spear. All Lost that encountered her in the hedge would be herded out the Hedge to the town's Trods. To the Freehold's dismay, she thoroughly enjoys the challenge of the badly worded Pledge, both nipping at the heels of recently escaped changelings to the town and keeping the town's changelings out of the Hedge.

      4. All of the above.

              Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
              Why would couple of Keepers steal many humans in region for years, and suddenly now let them free in almost the same time?
              For fun.

              To imagine the mentality of solipsistic God-monsters with little understanding of human emotion, the easy go-to perspective is that everything is a game. A game with horrifying consequences for everybody else involved.

              So the reason for this "catch and release" policy could be the setup for a competitive hunt for the Gentry. Think of how most fox hunts work: the fox has already been captured, days in advance. The fox is released purely so that the spectacle and thrill of horns and hounds baying can have a target. The Gentry care about the Hunt more than the Prey.

              A group of True Fae start dribbling changelings into a local area. Arcane rules are set in place. Once the freehold population reached a saturated point, the trigger fires. It's all-you-can-abduct, kill-what-you-want Fae Freehold-for-all. If they drag back more souls than they previously released, good for them: they possibly won the game.

              That can be what happened oh so many years ago, with the previous population. In one frantic evening, everyone Lost or near them was thrown into a sack or torn apart by ice-hounds. And it's going to happen again.

              If the motley discover this, how can they stop it? Maybe spreading the new arrivals out into distant freeholds will dissipate the focus of the hunters. Perhaps the nature of the game requires some very specific conditions which can be spoiled before it even starts. Maybe the only thing to do is try to shore up the trods, load up with iron arms and pray.

                          That's pretty much exactly what I had in my head. It's a good idea to have it worded in the way of a contract between the Fae, too. I'll get to that in a bit...

                          Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                          Secondary questions: How this kind of deal coud be broken? How could PCs get to know this plan? Would True Fae Send Loyalists?
                          Loyalists? Absolutely. The Gentry are just like real English gentry: they employ a mob of workers to ensure the hunt goes the way they wish. Beaters, houndsmen, groundskeepers... all they do is ensure that the stage is set for the Master's slaughter.

                          They could find out about it in a few different ways. Suspicions could start with small observations: dreams of portent, an eerie silence in the Hedge, any number of strange resonances in the Wyrd. Final confirmation could come from a captured Loyalist or a local prophet/seer. Maybe a rare written record from the last time this happened is found. I love the idea of Banished Fae: you don't even need to give them too much of a sensible motive. They tend to be absolutely insane, even for the Gentry, so you can have one babbling about "what's coming" as he/she paces around a derelict building or muttering about having "lost" while slumped in an alleyway.

                          Breaking the deal is where the exact wording of the contract can come into play. Throw ideas around, the more arcane the better. Perhaps the whole thing kicks off "When the First Summer Sunrise graces the Haert of the Townstead." Turns out, all you need to do is cover up the statue of a stag in the middle of town with some heavy blankets, holding the whole thing off indefinitely. Any number of events could be essential precursors to the Hunt. True Fae are literally incapable of deviating from such clauses. If the players can find out the exact words of the agreement, they're halfway to saving everyone.
