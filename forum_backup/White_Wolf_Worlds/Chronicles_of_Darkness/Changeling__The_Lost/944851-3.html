                        Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                        Something I don't understand about the "setting-in-a-can" thing, why don't 2e games count as having those? Each one has a selection of "settings-in-a-can" that you can easily grab and use rather than having to make one up yourself. Or am I misunderstanding that aspect of the issue?
                        Depends on the gamelines for starters, i didnt say Setting-in-a-can was something of 1st edition, i said 1st edition changeling.

                        1st edition Werewolf and mage had pretty big holes in their setting that expected Dm to fill in. How the concilium and uratha politics works was horribly explained from werewolf tendency of treating each pack as its own island but them throwing elements that could only exist on an organized scheme to mage attempt that boiled down to just throw options after options without explaining how said options interact with each other.

                        A perfect example of setting in a can is Werewolf the apocalypse whose setting was clearly define. This is the tribes, this is how they interact, this is how they resolve disputes, this is how they and why they get more rank. This is the positions of their septs, etc etc. Very few elements were not given a "canon" answer, as a dm i could always change whatever i didnt like but i had that canon answer to fall back or as a base to work from.

                        Same with changeling
                        This are the courts, this is the party line for resolving conflicts, this the positions, etc etc. They even manage, though very very clumsily, stablish the political importance of the motley. The courts have a non-local history to them, making each court localize and its history start and finish in its own location feels mundane to me, i know personal taste but i like when the organizations surpass the locations and i preffer having to adapt a group to a court and thus show a establish court under a new light than than making up a court that perfectly fits them.

                            Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post

                            Im curious what was the problem with bloodlines the choosen?
                            Just logistically very, very challenging. While being a very small book, it was a ton of work. And it was hard to find enough serviceable drafts that weren't just overlapping copies of the same bloodlines, or covering the same conceptual space.

                            It's always hard to work with a big team. It's even harder when they're not seasoned writers. It's even harder when they're not involved intimately with development, and given strong direction from the outset.

                                        Originally posted by MachineIV View Post

                                        The final version is going to have:

                                        Toronto
                                        Paris
                                        Miami
                                        Tokyo

                                        We planned to have a couple more, but writing fell through on those, and I decided the words would be better spent fleshing out a couple of other game concepts, like the Hedge.

                                        THAT SAID, I have plans for more settings upcoming. And hopefully on a relatively short timeline. I have some plans for the Directional Courts, but I have to make a few moving pieces fall together right.
                                        I find it interesting that the Settings are bigger then the settings in 2e Requiem, 2e Mage, 2e Promeathan.

                                        Toronto, Miami, and Toyko are all really big (I've only seen Toyko and Toronto, but Miami was the core setting in 1e, so it seems obvious).

                                        I figure even Paris will be reasonable big, if the others are any indication.

                                        You have 5 courts in Roserie Courts, 4-5 Courts in 1001 Nights Toronto Freehold (plus two smaller Freeholds, seasonal and seelie), Toyko has 3 Moon Princess Courts, and 4 Seasonal Courts in Miami.

                                        That 17 individual courts to be detailed, including mechanics court contracts, mantle, various merits, bulwark, approaches, court story, court symbols/colours/ranks, court fluff, ect....

                                        Then each setting has important locations like the CN Tower for example.

                                        It has a History, plothooks, local weirdness, Huntsmen, and other assorted elements.

                                        So I think they're bigger word count wise, and that doesn't include more universal Hedge elements in the Hedge section.

                                        One place I'd love to see done later in another book is Khajuraho in India.
