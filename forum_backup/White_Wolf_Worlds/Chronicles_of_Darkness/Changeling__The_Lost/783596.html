    The Displaced, fan made Seeming

    12-10-2015, 03:58 PM
    After reading on the 2nd Edition changes to the Seemings, Kiths, and the relationship between the two, not to mention hearing of the addition of at least two more Seemings, I started thinking of a new Seeming to fit with the old style fairy tale vibe I was getting.

    Technically, the Displaced have two 'sub-seemings', one focused on time, the other on space, both function the same, just a different emphasis on either side of space-time.

    “Not all those who wander are lost.” - J.R.R. Tolkien

    The Displaced doesn't seem to fit right in, or maybe they're just a bit out of place or out of sync with everyone else. Perhaps they follow foreign customs or speak in an archaic tongue. They are the ones looking for a home, a place to fit in, a time that makes sense to them. But they're all wanderers, and when the whims of fate hits, they'll wander once again.

    Appearance: You will know a Displaced by the way their accent, actions, clothes, maybe even their stance. You can feel that they're not suppose to be there, or that it's not their place. But they try, they really do. But their attempts at fitting in makes them, at best, look like bumbling fools, or, at worst, look like an insulting satire.

    Background: They were once a pillar of the community, some person of renown or someone who helped everyone in their time. But things change. Perhaps they wanted to see the world, or break out of a boring routine. To see the sights and go to times and places exotic. The desire to leave grows within them, bit by bit. Until they run to the Keeper for just one adventure, or were tricked by a seeming benign offer.

    Escape: They ran away from reality and into a fantasy. In their Durance, they witnessed wonders and horrors, excited and suffered by their Keeper. But, sooner or later, they lose interest and look back to their homes in yearning. Some wander Arcadia, knowing the destination but not the path, others lose their sense of time, not knowing if hours or centuries had passed. In losing their sense of direction or time as they focus on home, they found a way to escape.

    Character Creation: Displaced are wanderers but highly perceptive, if at least knowing the difference between their surroundings to their old home makes them as such. Ironically, they make excellent guides, scouts, and sentries. They have an easier understanding of actual social relations for they see things in the eyes of an outsider.

    Blessing: Clarity of Difference. Things change, even if the same events happen again or happen in a separate place, they'll be different that what happened before. They notice this and learn more of their new surroundings or perhaps try to introduce new ideas to the new place or revive ideas from an old time.

    Curse: Yearning for Home. They'll try to fit in, to once again be a part of their new community. But their difference would only make them stand out. Any time they try to fit in, only to be excluded from the group, is a Clarity Break.

    Concepts: They say his head is in the clouds. Thinking about spaceships and rayguns, he acts as if he lived centuries from now. But, from time to time, one can notice the shine of a chrome raygun in his pocket.
    She stands in front of the old style microphone. Swaying left and right as she sings a jazz cover of some pop song. But one can't help but look at her, listen to her voice, and imagine themselves backwards in time to some lost black and white film.
    Some look at the man as if he's some bumbling tourist or some exotic traveler in his yacht. But to him, he looks at the island in the distance, his old home, so close yet so far away. Obstacles are placed before him but he'll find a way home.

    The ideas that inspired the Displaced are Rip Van Wrinkle and other fairy tales where the character returns home from fairyland centuries later, for the time Displaced, and the Odyssey, for the space Displaced. In my opinion, the Displaced are probably the closest in human appearance but with the sense of difference that makes them hard to fit in,

    Kith examples:

    Antiquarian - He appears to be an actual medieval scribe, or perhaps act like one.

    Artist - She appears to be an artist of light, using strange tools to create holographic masterpieces.

    Manikin - He appears to be imitating an old fashion idea of what a robot looks like.

    Whisperwisp - She appears to be a ninja, with silent steps and sharp hearing.

    Skogsra - He appears to be a throwback to the Stone Age, or perhaps a dirty image of a barbarian.

      It's an interesting idea, but I don't know if it really encapsulates what Seemings are about. Seemings (to my understanding) are supposed to embody or reflect how the character regains Agency and escapes Arcadia. This Seeming just doesn't feel empowering or a source of agency to me. They just wandered away. It doesn't have the philosophical "punch" of the Elementals' "letting go of their sense of self" or the Beasts' abandonment of their humanity. It just feels like it misses the mark on what a Seeming is supposed to be, sorry.

                                      Wanting something is indeed not agency. The two points above aside here is my take:

                                      From what I can tell about Seemings, they involve deep cognitive self-transformation that removes part of their psyche, ability to trust for darklings, sense of self for elementals, inhibition towards violent for ogres, the separation of their identity from their craft for wizen, inhibition towards breaking social norms for beasts, indifference towards the suffering of others for the fairest. All these losses are important coping mechanisms for getting along in society and produce severely unbalanced people when they are absent. Changeling is a game of Madness, beautiful or grotesque as it may be and these core game mechanics ensures that it is.

                                      If you are making a seeming that could be counted as "well-adjusted" you've missed the mark of what Seemings are, and their purpose for enforcing game themes. Seeming are the spiritual/psychological/metaphysical scars needed to escape Arcadia , acquired like a fox chewing off its foot to escape a trap, and their purpose is to enforce the themes of the struggle out of madness, chasing after broken coping mechanisms, and relapse caused emotionally devastating psychological triggers. I mean look at the blessing and curse of all the existing seeming, that is exactly what they represent and seeming only mechanical purpose seems to enforce these themes in a variety of ways that are interesting for the story.

                                      Every Seeming needs a well written blessing and curse to fulfill these goals. Also it should be easy to identify what the changeling cut out of their psyche to escape. Your curse is really well written and I think exactly what you were shooting for. Well done. Your blessing and what part of their being they cut away from themselves is a little muddled. Part of the problem is that the existing seemings cover the cutting out very broad pieces of the Lost's psyche, and many ideas that could fit this potential seeming are mere small subsections of existing seemings. Also the themes of wanting a home and not being able to find one should be worked right into the mechanics of the seeming. Here is a possible revision:

                                      The Displaced doesn't seem to fit right in, or maybe they're just a bit out of place or out of sync with everyone else. They are the ones looking for a home, a place to fit in, a time that makes sense to them. But they're all wanderers, and when the whims of fate hits, they'll wander once again.

                                      Blessing: Clarity of New Beginnings. When a Departed abandons his long term associations, belongings and home to make a new beginning unfettered to their past they regain a point of Clarity.

                                      Curse: Can't find Home. They'll try to fit in, to once again be a part of their new community. But their difference would only make them stand out. Any time they try to fit in, only to be excluded from the group, or deal with abandoned associations or return to a previous home, it is a Clarity Break.

                                      I will not tell you what I intended for this to remove from the changeling's psyche, because I hope it is clear enough I don't have to. I will proudly say it enforces the themes you had for the Departed.
