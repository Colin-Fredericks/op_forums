                Originally posted by Claire Redfield View Post
                If it helps, here are the working notes for my own setting. Note that it's a work in progress, and I haven't given the Court writeups that polished shine just yet. I'm trying to get everything worked out, then I can write up the history and everything. It's light on detail, but that might help you other players come up with ideas for your Freeholds.


                Court Contracts
                Spring:
                Emotion (Desire)
                Fang and Talon •-•••
                Probably going to change to Vainglory, but my initial reasoning was that the children of Spring were more closely tied with the birds and beasts, the living things growing upon the earth, especially in a place where nature is so prevalent. So they learn how to exult in Spring's glory the way the living creatures of Crescent Valley do. The valley is nestled right into the Cascade Mountains.

                Summer:
                Emotion (Wrath)
                Might of the Terrible Brute ••

                Autumn:
                Emotion (Fear)
                Night's Subtle Distractions ••

                Winter:
                Emotion (Sorrow)
                Touch of the Workman's Wrath ••

                Mantles
                Each Court gains the following benefits at each level of Mantle.
                Mantle (•)
                Spring: Courtiers gain a keener awareness of the world and its delights. +2 to perception rolls.
                Summer: The anger of the Lost always simmers just beneath the surface. +2 to wrathful Intimidation rolls based on show of force.
                Autumn: Mysteries abound in Charleston's history. +2 when making research rolls pertaining to the occult.
                Winter: Gain +2 on Expression rolls made with members of the Freehold.

                Mantle (••)
                All: Gain a dot of the Goblin Vow Merit related to the Court.

                Mantle (•••)
                Spring: Gain the Striking Looks (••) Merit.
                Summer: Gain the Hardy (••) Merit.
                Autumn: Gain the Eye for the Strange (••) Merit.
                Winter: The cold, patient chill of Winter is a good time to stay indoors and study. You gain the Library (••) Merit, choosing a Skill appropriate for the area.

                Mantle (••••)
                All: Refine and prioritize a Court Approach. Huntsmen gain no benefit at all if they do not use this Approach first.

                Mantle (•••••)
                All Courtiers may choose Dual Kith (•••) at this level of Mantle instead of the below benefits.
                Spring: Your ability to immerse yourself in the healing energies of life grant you the Faerie Healing (•••) Merit.
                Summer: The mightiest of Summer are a powerful presence indeed. You gain the Enchanting Performance (•••) Merit.
                Autumn: You have tamed a powerful familiar to serve as ally and guardian. Gain the Hedgebeast Companion (•••) Merit.
                Winter: Your journeys into the snow-covered Hedge have given you command of a fae beast. You gain the Fae Mount (•••) Merit.

                Court Approaches
                Spring
                Beware the Roseflame: An extremely rare flower, the Blue Roseflame, grows in the Hedge only during Spring. The flower is said to have intense aphrodisiacal qualities, and it never wilts as long as it is &quot;fed&quot; with Glamour. Huntsmen gain a point of Yearning when they use the allure of the rare flower to bait changelings into a trap.
                Dream Girl: Despite its name, the Huntsman can appear in any form: male, female, androgynous, transgender, bearing whatever qualities a Spring courtier desires most. The Huntsman gains a point of Yearning if it seduces an otherwise wary changeling in exchange for another changeling's stolen desires.

                Summer
                Fire Pit Fight: Summer courtiers in Charleston often wage ritual battles by firelight on hot summer nights out in Crescent Valley. If a Huntsman issues an earnest challenge to a changeling by the fire for its safety or something else precious, it gains a point of Yearning.
                Quenching the Fire: Summer never yields its anger. If a Huntsman can erode at the Summer courtier's sense of anger, whether by instilling joy or sorrow, it gains a point of Yearning.

                Autumn
                Theater of Dreams: The Huntsmen gain a point of Yearning when they manipulate the dreams of a changeling's mortal allies in order to draw out the changeling.
                Solemn Vow: Huntsmen excel at twisting the bonds between the Lost. The Huntsman gains a point of Yearning if it can manipulate the changeling or one of her allies into breaking a pledge.

                Winter
                Not All Who Wander are Lost: The Huntsmen are masters of manipulation. When one uses a token of something a changeling lost to break her confidence, it gains a point of Yearning.
                Frozen Heart: Winter courtiers are often wreathed in sorrow as the land is wreathed in snow and ice. If a Huntsman can prey upon a changeling's sorrow and convince her to retreat into mournful solitude, it gains a point of Yearning.
                Thank you so much, Claire! Do you have any insight on what the Goblin Vows should target?

                Also, what does it mean when a universal contract, like Might of the Terrible Brute, is a court contract? If no one can speak to the latter, I understand.

                                If you want to call a seasonal alliance or Day and Night courts the Seelie and Unseelie you can, but I think that Day and Night is too caught up in the literal 'It's daytime, we're in power for the next twelve hours' to be a good representative of the myths. Emotionally I could see an argument for Disgust fitting the Unseelie but Shame isn't what comes to mind when thinking of Seelie emotional possibilities. Court of the West's Honor would be a good seelie emotion, maybe. There's more than a literal brightness and light and happy sunshine to the seelie and there's more to darkness, nighttime, and evil to the unseelie. I think a proper write up would have to get into more of the subtle details.

                                Mythology changes depending on the source, of course, but the Unseelie consider chaos as a natural state. Being changeable and adaptable help them survive, power unused is power wasted, and instinct and desire are things that should be acted on. In the seasonal alliance model Spring gets tossed in with the Seelie, but 'Desire' in the hands on powerful fae creatures who don't hesitate to use their power where their whims take them can be a very dark thing that produces the classical Unseelie disregard for humanity. On the Seelie side, we often see a strict code of honor, chivalry, and the exaltation of beautiful things. The Seelie are just as likely to snatch a human as the Unseelie- they're just more likely to set that person in a gilded cage to 'keep them safe' and fete them with parties, dancing, and gorgeous clothes while they're trapped. For their own good, of course. In game terms, the bias towards pretty could wind up with the Seelie being rather Seemingist though a hideous dwarf type changeling who can craft beautiful instruments or produce beauty in some other way also fits the ideal. The Unseelie take everyone though, and I could some changelings who might normally be Seelie by nature wind up going Unseelie simply because they don't like how Seemingist and shallow the Seelie can sometimes.

                                Anyways, just spitballing.
