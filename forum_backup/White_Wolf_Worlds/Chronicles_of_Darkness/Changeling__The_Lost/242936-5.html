    I'm probably going to get flak for this, but I do not like this fundamental change to how Contracts function.

    A Contract should feel like a Contract. Contracts don't change their wording based on who signs them. In 1e, a Contract is literally that. It's a contract with the fundamental forces of the world, signed by one's inherent nature as a Changeling and the Arcadia they escaped from.

    Having five different versions of each contract detracts from that theme. I would rather the idea that these are contracts be reinforced, rather than relegating them to just another power set.

            I actually have to agree, I'm not sure I'm super-comfortable with the entirety of Changeling contracts being unordered and assorted in this way. Feels a bit like it's invoking an edge of Demon or Vampire Bloodlines here (which, while awesome are entirely different games). Barring the problems with disparities in contract power levels, I would say it is convincingly the most mechanically solid set of powers (barring a few whiffs) in NWOD1.0, and the tiered progression coupled with the catch system helped it immensely. I can't say I've ever genuinely been stumped for how I'd want to build a changeling with a set of contracts that do something I want outside of Archmage-level worldbending.

            It was also a comprehensively simple system to explain to players new to the game, with each series having a general theme and each clause of the series being able to be easily digested as a one-sentence function of how they work (trust me! I've been working on cheat-sheets of contracts for a Changeling LARP.) There's a bit of duplication that could probably be cut down, especially with the Seasonal contracts in mind, but overall it works really well.

            While I could be convinced otherwise, I don't think it is one factor necessarily worth changing just for the sake of change.

            At this point, I think if you want more grab-baggy type powers, a revamp of Goblin Contracts would be worthwhile. And revamped in a way that they're no longer the fastest way to throw out 50XP outside of Character Death.

                IDK, I'm having trouble thinking of Contacts that would work better split up that way, but if there are some good examples ... MachineIV, do you have even some vague outlines of Seeming Contracts you could show us that really wouldn't work as Universal Contracts?

                Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
                That would functionally have the same end result. If they have a higher Strength, they'll buy Contracts that take advantage of it.
                Yes, that's the idea, IIUC.

                It's not the same result, though, because as Data pointed out above the "favoured Kith" idea has some unintended results.



                OK, let's use the sample Contract, just to make it clear we're spitballing:

                Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
                Contract of Strength (2 dots)
                Makes you strong!
                Ogre: You get a direct Strength boost. Bam. Favored Seeming for this one.
                Elemental: You can draw on an example of your element to use its Structure instead of your dice pool for feats of strength.
                Fairest: When someone makes you look bad, your next strength action gets their successes added to yours.
                Darkling: When taking an action when nobody sees you, add your Stealth to your Strength.
                Beast: Grow a Size. Gain a Strength. Gain a Stamina.
                Wizened: Too bad, so sad. If you want Contract of Strength, pay an extra Experience and take another Seeming's manifestation.
                Now, imagine I'm a Fairest Draconic. A beautiful sparkly dragon. Clearly, I need to go out-of-Kith for this one, since the Fairest version doesn't fit my concept.

                Now, being a Dragonic, clearly the best fit for my character is the Beast version. Dragons are big and strong. But the Ogre version is explicitly better than the others - if I don't buy it, I'll be significantly less strong. I have to choose between effectiveness and what fits my character.



                OK, scrap that, and imagine for a second that it works like this instead:

                Contract of Strength (2 dots)

                Strength + Wyrd

                Makes you strong!
                Ogre: You get a small Strength boost.
                Elemental: You can draw on an example of your element to use its Structure instead of your dice pool for feats of strength.
                Fairest: When someone makes you look bad, your next strength action gets their successes added to yours.
                Darkling: When taking an action when nobody sees you, add your Stealth to your Strength.
                Beast: Grow a Size. Gain a Strength. Gain a Stamina.
                Wizened: You get a Strength boost when using Crafts or Weaponry.
                Now, as a big strong dragon, clearly I want to buy up Strength anyway. So this Contract will work well for me - which makes sense, since it's a good fit for my character! Now, I could buy the Ogre version, but since they're all equally powerful, I go with the one that fits my concept best - the Beast version.

                Now, it's still inherently easier for a Ogre to use this Contract than for most characters; but that's because they're the type of Changeling that would, not because they bought the most effective version. Any Changeling can be that sort of character, and be about as effective, although without the Ogre Boon it'll be harder; and they can use the right version of the Contract for them to do it.

                                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                            Just for the sake of clarity: I wasn’t referring to the Strength attribute anywhere in my last post. Let’s use a different example: a hypothetical Contract of Violence. Nearly every Seeming has its own invocation of this Contract, except perhaps the Fairest (who are more inclined to destroy reputations than to hurt people); for instance, the Bestial invocation of Violence includes claws and fangs; the Elemental invocation imbues the attack with elemental power (flaming swords, fists of stone, etc.) But Violence is what Ogres are all about; and when an Ogre invokes the Contract of Violence, mayhem ensues.

                                            The challenge is why, say, a Fairest who invests in the Contract of Violence wouldn’t always go with the Ogre’s version. Three possibilities: one is that the Ogre’s version of Violence is specifically written to leverage whatever Blessing the Ogre receives; another is that the Ogre’s version comes in two stages: the version that you get if you’re an Ogre (which is designed to be superior to the other four), and a version that other Seemings get if they spend an Experience to access the Ogre version (which is a watered-down version of what Ogres get, designed to be on par with the other four); finally, it may be that the Ogre’s version of Violence is exclusive to the Ogre, and that nobody gets to spend an Experience to get access to it: in order to revel in Violence the way an Ogre does, you need to be an Ogre.

                                            [...]

                                            The second option is what I originally proposed. Its downside is the added complexity: Every Seeming Contract writeup would need, say, an extra paragraph for its favored Seeming, describing what additional benefits you get if you actually are that Seeming; or conversely, you might need a paragraph to describe which of the benefits are toned down or removed if you’re not that Seeming.

                                            [...]

                                            This would be also my favored option - some little add on for Ogre only while activating the favored contract.
                                            However i am worried about the wordcount - you are "still" actually writing 6 versions of the contract (all 4 other kiths, Ogre for Ogres, Ogre for non-Ogres.)

                                            Some simple alternatives which do not add too much to the wordcount:

                                            Ogres which use the contract of Violence (the Ogre facet, or also all the facets)...

                                            1) Are already satisfying the catch, so they need not to spend glamour or willpower.

                                            DOWNSIDE: you don't have to use catches, and using catches is fun.
                                            Moreover, a character has an incentive in buying "seeming" contract which are really expensive, since the advantage is enhanced.

                                            2) May roll the contract activation with the Rote quality, or with 8-again.

                                            DOWNSIDE: it may be actually too powerful - 8 again or rote with a good amount of dices is quite difficult to be resisted.
