    For the fun of it, how about a Kith that's usually never thought of with anything BUT darklings; Gravewights?

    A Beast would be those who take the superstitions about animals to the extreme; a black cat is a harbringer of death, the crows and ravens act as a messenger between worlds, and an owl is able to see the lingering souls in the world, and perhaps sense when new ones are to form. Acting as psychopomps and instinctive guides, these companions do their best to move the dead on. Some may then attempt to keep the living from interacting with the dead, while others might take their duty as harbringer of death seriously, choosing who among the living should be moved on with little more than a whim as guidance.

    A Darkling has severed their proper ties to the living in their mind, instead taking a grim comfort in consorting with the dead. Slowly losing touch with the living, the comfortably predictable patterns of earthly spectres seems to comfort them in a way, a reminder of how they've managed to escape the world of the ever-changing Fae. This separation, of course, has a cost, making it difficult for them amongst humankind, and they all too often find themselves mirroring the haunting games of spectres and shades in order to keep their solitude.

    An Elemental is as much flesh as ephemera, a being half formed of the ectoplasmic essence that makes up the lingering dead. While a Darkling consorts with the dead, an elemental becomes them, not purposely withdrawing so much as simply finding their minds forming an entirely different sort of thought process than any human would. They find it difficult to do more than follow and watch their loved ones at a distance, unsure how to connect firsthand.

    A Fairest commands an otherworldly allure, pale skin and blackened hair somehow enhancing them instead of detracting. The shades flock to them like moths to the swamp light, and their reach extends beyond the realm of the living to networks of undead informants and allies, working to spread word of their beauty and worth. That's not to say that the deceased are their only followers; their eerie grace and exotic form of beauty seems to captivate the living just as easily, putting a shiver down mortal spines while they long for the courage to get closer.

    An Ogre finds themselves as brutal protectors of the sanctity of death, stalking graveyards to punish those that might vandalize graves or uproot bodies. The spectres act as their eyes and ears, guiding the fury of one who knows all too well what it's like to have one's body taken and mutilated with no care for proper respect. No small number of tales of creatures guarding graveyards are actually about the Ogre guarding them, rather than the dead themselves.

    A Wizened is a necromancer and wisdom keeper, performing small favors for the deceased to learn of their secrets, or perhaps to get the chance to study one of these ghosts directly. Blunt and to the point, they rarely see the shades they study as deserving much more respect than an odd bit of flora or fauna, a little cold to them even when they work to assist them. Rumors spread of those that even go so far as to warp the still-aware spectres into odd shapes and forms, creating otherworldly artifacts fueled by the essence of the dead.
    Ooh! Cool! Definitely some interesting ideas here, to say the least.

            Okay, so I'm just going to go on a long brain barf about Elementals and see what we can get out of it.

            Elementals in the old version were the products of being made part of the environment, of being made one with the world. This implies a direct connection to the Stranger in question more so than most, because the world is their Domains, sometimes their Realms. No wonder the bastards have humanity issues-,they've been twice infected, once from that perspective of the breathing world(as opposed to the bleeding world, ala Beasts) and once by sheer Fae effect. In a sense, this means that when a changeling uproots itself from the Domain and walks out, or about, the Elemental is more of a direct thief than any other-they literally became part of the Stranger and them ripped themselves off and out. It's like if your elbow skin just said "Welp, I'm out", and tore itself brutally from your arm and preceded to run for the nearest bar.

            Elementals seem like kind of rude bastards that way. That's not relevant.

            It might seem like I'm suggesting "Agency through theft", but that's not it, it doesn't parse, not with the examples handed out by Dave-burn the manor down, bury the dogs in a landslide, hold your breath and count to ten minutes(Guybrush). Still, it informs it a little.

            One thing that strikes me is that in the varied aspects of many Domains, it seems like Elementals are consistently baser and rawer. We have places where there place is a playpen, where an Elemental might be born from the surging mass of teddy bears and thus be a teddy bear themselves, one cotton and cloth drop in a plush friend ocean. Instead we get examples of Sand and Metal and Water and Fire and Earth and Wood and Lightning and Wind-we get those because they are archetypal, they're what we expect, but it also feels rawer. In fact, along with that rawness comes the feeling of power, of might, of something barely contained-each Domain that was marked as being Elementally favored had that sort of feeling, of action and energy just waiting to leap. You never had limpid pools, you had surging oceans. You had castles of glass and wire in the middle of the storm, and the lightning running through them was leaping, struggling,seething through. It feels ready to rip everything apart, even at it's most controlled-or at least that's how I remember it.

            And with that comes the sense of overwhelming power-not in the same way as a Ogre. Elementals were often marked as tough and power-partly because of their Blessing, sure, also largely in part of the simple and brutal elegance of the Contract of Elements(and even Communion and Wyld). But I think a larger part of it has to do with the fact that they are used to being part of the environment, used to having it be them. Admit it-when you see an Elemental in your head going nuts, the element is filling the room, the element consumes the subject matter. It floods, it snarls and grows, it eats with it's many flickering tongues, the wind pierces all, the lightning is in our roofs, our steeds, our blood, the Earth quakes, the sand and snow twists and blinds and binds. It doesn't even matter if the natural element is there, the Elementals bleed it. They become powerful because they are walking Domains. They control the place.

            It occurs to me that, of the aspects of a Fae, it really is the Domain that makes the Elementals. You can make a (poor) argument that the aspects of a Fae all tie to the Seemings in ways-Props to Wizened, Actors to Fairest, other things where the metaphor falls apart. But Domains are very Elemental, a part of the world that is not more directly part of the fae-Elementals could but probably don't come from Realms. The main thing that marks the two apart is that the Stranger is in control of a Realm, it is not in control of a Domain, even if it is a part of them. It's a place where power like that can come unchecked, where the Stranger is most likely on the wrong foot.

            Okay. I think I got a simple sell for the Elementals.

            Agency through chaos, power from unity and control.

            Elementals take the raw, unbridled power of the Fae, and in an Autumn-like twist, throw it back at them(Hi Granfather Thunder) They create chaos and destruction and problems on the large scale. They burn the house down, the cause the landslide. They bury Strangers in a sea of teddy bears and run like hell. Chaos is a great equalizer-when no one knows what's going on and no one has the power to handle it, everyone is equally unable to act. Elementals are the earthquakes the Strangers never expected, and they get the power to express that agency by dominating the world they are in-and continue to do so. THey become one with the world they are in and seek to control it's every aspect, and gain the upper hand on people by throwing their expectations out the windows. Elementals are the ones who change the game. They learn to control the board, and deny others(Others) the chance to do so.

            How's that for a thought?

                            I'm of the opinion a Seeming is a scar you give yourself too-but let's acknowledge that you are giving yourself a scar.

                            The Seeming is the Moment. It is a moment in time where you did something to take control of your life.

                            No one said it was a moment you should be proud of, regardless of what Summer says. No one said it was a moment you don't regret.

                            That might have been the moment you found out just how much of a monster you can be.

                            Anyways, that's not wholly related.

                            Still, I must say this again: I don't like the the idea of pushing through regardless. People don't get out of bad situations just pushing through regardless. People don't grow, don't control themselves and their lives by just pushing through regardless. Nothing changes when you just push through regardless.

                            You have to do something, change the situation, before pushing through regardless becomes meaningful.

                            You have to let the cops arrest your husband before you can stick to your guns and never deviate from the path that will put him in prison. You have to decide to leave him before you can stick to your guns and never go back. You can't stick to your guns when sticking to your guns means you're still getting hit every night.

                            No one who improves their lot in life does it by just enduring.

                            EDIT: In fact, I'll just say it outright-endurance, by itself, has nothing to do with agency.

                                                        So coming back to this.

                                                        I know some people says some of my suggestions have been an all changeling thing, and this is that for me.

                                                        I think some parts of the issue is hueing too closely to the old version. I get that, I like the issues with their own personal humanity that the Elementals had. But under the new context I find myself saying "Shouldn't this be something all changelings can suffer or grow from?"

                                                        It may be more direct with Elementals, but the idea of betraying one's self for agency is across the board. A Fairest sleeps with a Stranger(...), a Darkling hamstrings their buddy to ensure they're slower, an Ogre breaks the Stranger's arm with the same cruelty and malice that it broke his own, the Wizened causes trouble for his mates elsewhere to disrupt the system, a Beast eats a goblin's heart.

                                                        Aren't all of these betrayal of self? It depends on the person, but for most people, doing this betrays the average persons's sense of right and wrong on several fronts. Do the circumstances justify the actions? Maybe, but the psyche isn't always prepared to accept that.

                                                        In fact, compared to some of these, a simple "Abandon humanity for power of the world" seems small, almost laughable. I can almost hear someone say "Oh, you had to become a tree? Oh babe, I'm so sorry. I had to shoot someone. They didn't die on the first shot. They didn't die on the second one either."

                                                        I want the door open for all of them. Sometimes, the moment you took agency is a proud, shining moment. It shows your composure, your cunning, your knowledge, your boldness, maybe even your kindness, if you're lucky. It might be your most human moment ever. Sometimes, the moment you took agency is a terrible thing you hope you'll never have to do again. It shows your selfishness, your capriciousness, your cruelty, your deceitfulness, maybe even your misanthropy, if you're unlucky. It might be your most alien(monstrous) moment ever.

                                                        Embrace it or run from it, the Seeming is a Moment, and that Moment is Endless. You will be marked forever by your answer to the question of what you felt you were worth, and how you define yourself from then on is going to come back to how you feel about that Moment.
