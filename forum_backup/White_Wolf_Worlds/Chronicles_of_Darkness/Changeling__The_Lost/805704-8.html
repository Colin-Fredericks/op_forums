            First, awesome concept. Huntsmen are not what I'd thought they'd be, they're better. They also somehow manage to make the Immortal Assassin type of antagonist somewhat sympathetic (they're also victims of the Keepers as well, when you think about it-the difference is that's all they really can be, with even their victory only meaning a longer age before their next Durance) while still keeping them utterly terrifying, and in a way that I don't think Immortal Assassins have been before (charming masterminds rather than voiceless brutes).

            Second: I must add my voice to the "why always murder?" brigade. I get why it's there (to force the quarry to confront the fact that banishing the Huntsman is becoming complicit in serial murder), but that just seems...rather droll for what the Huntsmen are.

            I'd modify that into killing a victim, or driving them to a mental breakdown (Integrity 0) in nightmares and real-life gaslighting. That way, it keeps the horror of becoming incidentally complicit in a terrible crime as the Huntsman regains its bearings, but it also opens up a new set of stories, as well as increasing the paranoia of searching for Huntsman manifestation (people tend to hush up mental problems to begin with, and people go insane for reasons other than a psychotic fae mercenary rummaging around their psyches). One could even tie this into the "Practicing skills for the Hunt" theme, since Huntsmen psychologically torment the Lost by normal tactics; either they work on their physical skills or their cunning in preparation for their real target.

                                                        Originally posted by Pale_Crusader View Post

I, too have had really bad storytellers that ran every villain they threw at us as an unstoppable juggernauts devoid of any humanizing weaknesses. I know what that feels like and I want to let you know that isn't actually how the game should be run. See game mechanics and defining how things work in a setting builds a consistent frame work for a story that is more than just the pre- magna carta serfdom under a tyrannical Storyteller. See role playing isn't just a game of imaginative pretend and storytelling we play with our friends, but it has rules and mechanics which define which characters should be able to do. Just because storytellers you've been exposed to feel free to play changeling antagonists like a preternatural terminator doesn't mean that's what loyalists actually are. Loyalists are fundamentally human characters, with human weaknesses and should if they are portrayed correctly as the fully fleshed out characters they deserve to be, will have these weaknesses put on display, for a variety of reason ranging from making them sympathetic to make them even more contemptible for their pettiness.
