        Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
        So I have been reading Dark Eras 2, and I discovered a neat section on making Fetches playable. Unfortunately, while the section has good information about contract use, and roleplaying tips, it seems rather bare bones in other areas. So I’ve got a few questions about it.

        1) Does the Fetch use Wyrd or Azoth for it’s power stat during the 7 wonders era? Or both?
        2) Do fetches get the seeming bonus dot? It says their stats are equal to the Changelings, but is that before or after they are taken?
        3) Can fetches use the gain the mantle of a court through court goodwill?
        4) Can a playable Fetch buy Wyrd on their own, or does their Changeling have to do it?
        5) If I wanted Echos to be purchasable, what should the cost be?
        6) Should Fetches have Fetch only merits? Everything else playable seems to have merits only they can purchase?
        6.5) Should some Changeling only merits be available to Fetches? Like Acute Senses?
        7) Can a Changeling or their Fetch buy Parallel Lives before the Changeling has escaped?
        8) Can a Fetch hedgespin?
        8.5)Can a Fetch enact Paradigm Shifts?
        9) Can a Fetch Portal, or Incite Bedlam?
        10) Does a Fetch get the Seeming benefit if using Mimic Contract?
        11) Can a Fetch participate in a Oaths if another Changeling actually does the magic?
        12) Can a Fetch use Oneirmancy?

        Oooo, I can dig in to this a bit, since I'm running a game where the players are Fetches.

        1) Fetch should use Wyrd. They are beings of Glamour, after all, and while there's a LOT of cool stuff you can add to a Fetch game from Promethean, Fetch are ultimately different beings. You might consider some crossover, though -- I have some Pandorans running around in my game that react to both Wyrd and Azoth, because they were created by one of the Gentry trying his hand at wielding The Divine Fire.

        2) When I worked up my Fetch rules, I created six splats to parallel Seemings. The splats dealt with what the Fetch was constructed out of, and how that influenced their lives. Each of these "Bundles," as I called them, gave a Fetch a different bonus (though I used the whole grid of Power/Finesse/Resistance and Mental/Physical/Social to devide up the bonuses). The Fetch were not required to take a Bundle that had anything to do with their Changeling Counterpart's Seeming.

        3) I'd allow it, since otherwise they're gimped if they have Mimic Contract but their changeling counterpart uses a lot of Court Contracts over Arcadian ones.

        4) I'd allow the players to make such an expenditure, sure. Whether or not it represents the Changeling increasing their Wyrd and the Fetch gaining it by proxy or whether Fetch themselves can raise it is immaterial -- I view advancement as a Player-level choice, not a Character-level one. But that's just how I handle it.

        5) I use one Experience per dot of Wyrd requirement -- a Wyrd 1 Echo costs 1 Experience, while a Wyrd 2 Echo costs 2, etc. I also packaged a Favored Echo into the Bundles I created, allowing each Bundle to have signature strengths. I imported a lot of Echoes from Autumn Nightmares (cleaned up for 2e) and made a few of my own, and my players also submitted ideas for Echoes that I worked the mechanics out for. This, of course, if just how I do things...my recommendation is to keep the price in line with other supernatural abilities, which tend to cost between 2 and 4 Experiences in most of the splats.

        6) I think so. I swiped a bunch of Promethean Merits for my game, allowed Fetch to take some Supernatural Merits normally reserved strictly for mortals (Aura Sense, Omen Sensitivity, and Object Reading), as well as came up with a series of Merits based on the idea of "Fetch Catches" from Autumn Nightmares.

        6.5) Again, I think so, and allow Merits with some caveats (some have prerequisites of requiring certain Echoes, most notably anything related to entering or navigating The Hedge).

        7) I see nothing but interesting story developments from that, so I'd allow it as an ST. It will fuck up the Fetch's life no end to be that tied to their Changeling even before their escape.

        8) I allow it, assuming the Fetch can get into the Hedge in the first place. But I bundled the ability to Hedgespin into the Enter The Hedge Echo, so it's not a universal ability among Fetch.

        9) Enter The Hedge is already an Echo that Fetch can take if they want. So some Fetch can Portal. I don't allow them to have the "slip free of any binding" ability, though; that's strictly a Changeling ability. Likewise, I have an Echo that allows Fetch to incite Bedlam. I've also swiped some Promethean Bestowments to use as Echoes (most notably Ephemeral Flesh, Inscription, The Soul Is In The Software, and Spare Parts).

        10) Yes. They gain the Contract exactly as their Counterpart knows it. I view buying new Seeming Abilities the same way I do buying Wyrd -- that's a Player-Level choice, and you can justify it in the narrative however you like.

        11) Not so much. I have an Echo that allows Fetch to use Sealing, but not Oaths. I use the 1e version of Pledgecrafting in my game as a supplement, but I haven't given the Fetches the ability to do more than Sealing thus far.

        12) Again, I bundled this ability into an Echo that allowed Fetch to enter dreams if they can get into the Hedge. I didn't make the ability universal for all Fetches.

        If you are curious, I wrote out a lot of my Fetch Player Creation rules here. This is the main Request To Join thread for my game, which includes all the player options and rules I'm using that aren't in the base Changeling book.

                    As a brief overview:

                    Ephemeral Flesh allows the character to see into and interact with beings in Twilight.

                    Inscription is a secret word of phrase that's been written on the character that the character can invoke to renew their sense of self or purpose, allowing them to use their magic fuel (Pyros in the Promethean's case, and Glamour in the Fetch's) to be spent like a point of Willpower on one of three skills. They can only do this once per skill per Chapter, but it's still really handy as it's easier to regain magic fuel than Willpower in a lot of cases.

                    The Soul Is In The Software allows the character to separate parts of themselves into smaller bits to run around and do things. It's mostly useful for separating a piece of yourself to do recon or to save yourself from immanent death (the separated body part can grow into a new body if the original one is killed).

                    Spare Parts allows a character to replace their body parts with fresh ones, allowing them to switch around their stats or even heal damage. I allow Fetches to use things other than flesh parts for this -- each Fetch has a "primary element" they're composed of (wood, plastic, mud, bones, non-iron metal, etc.) that they can swallow down to heal damage (this gives them a way of speeding up recovery between fights, and is mostly a gameplay consideration) -- Spare Parts for them works with their primary element, and is basically meant to be an extension of the ability taken to its logical conclusion.
