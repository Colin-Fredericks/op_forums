                                The Yearning approach is...not something I would do without Huntsmen. The beauty of the system is that you have a synergy between the common antagonists-True Fae are uber powerful, but have to obey every approach, Huntsmen are fairly powerful but need to rely on the Approaches sometimes to keep up their power/life, and the Loyalists aren't bound to the Approaches at all but aren't anything more or less than changelings.                                             For the Loyalists/Privateers, I assume. That might be doable, as it avoids the "One Point or All Points" dilemma. The problem is that it's not terribly crossover-friendly. It doesn't make much sense for some supernaturals to refuel when they abide by an Approach (vampires getting Vitae out of beating someone at their own game feels... strange), and while it can work as Willpower for mortals and Hunters, it doesn't do much for Horrors and the like.

                                            Maybe instead of a Yearning, an Approach grants a Twist of Fate? Whoever abides by it and beats the changeling at their own game, gains some sort of token, evocative of the Approach itself, that they can later use to bring misfortune to either the changeling themselves (assume they're not dead or captured already), or to the rest of the Freehold. Mechanically, they could use it to downgrade a roll one step (from ES to S, from S to F, or from F to DF), or they could use it "organically" in a diceless scene to take control of the narrative and implement a dramatic twist.

                                            I think I like that idea. It's very Changeling in nature, and it encourages long-term antagonists of the entire Freehold to prey on the weak and accumulate Twists of Fate so that they can take out the big fish later on, and personal antagonists of the PCs to do some "prep work" before confronting them, which also gives time for the PCs to prepare countermeasures.

                                            My homebrew hub.
