        After the most recent Court preview, the whole direction of Second Edition has really clicked for me - Second Edition is putting the spotlight on seizing the narrative of your life back from the Gentry, hence how each Court system reflects the narrative of that community, rather than a universal model applying. At first I was worried about taking the focus off the 4 Seasonal Courts would decrease the "surviving trauma" themes of the game (given that those 4 courts correspond intentionally to Denial (Spring), Anger (Summer), Bargaining (Autumn), and Depression (Winter)) but now I'm really seeing how they're taking it in a more intentionally empowering direction.

        Seeming reflecting the escape you seized for yourself, rather than what the Gentry turned you into (which is still there with Kith), is in the same model of the game increasing focus on that empowering theme.

                    You need to be more specific to have a point.

                    When the nWOD came out, people were saying that the baby was being thrown out, and the nWOD shouldn't be called a "WOD" and the game lines all got the same sort of treatment. Requiem isn't Masquerade. It threw out the baby and doesn't deserve to be called Vampire any more. Discussions of the WOD have seen this about the nWOD, and every nWOD game line that shares a name with a cWOD one. It's happened in all of the nWOD 1e to 2e transitions.

                    Pretty much every WOD splat ever is partially defined by archetypes, because archetypes are the basis of splats. Ventrue are the noble vampires, Gangrel are beastial ones. Full Moon werewolves are warriors and Crescent Moon ones are spirit workers.

                    1e Changelings at Seemings, Kiths, and Courts, that define a character through archetypes. 2e does too.
                    I completely agree here. With 2E each Seeming is presenting an Archetype that helps contextualize what the Character HAS done and what they are PRONE to do in the future. My critiques were made without much context as what the intention was behind the sample write-ups in the context of the whole Book. I'll explain that short sightedness latter here.

                    I have battled many a player over al problem that stems from the existence of Archetypes. Now I'll repeat what I just said above to keep from being confused. I fully support Archetypes as building blocks for Character design.
                    I have told a couple players to check themselves when they are too stuck in a mindset of Stereotypes. (You’re playing a Winter, fellow player. Act sadder. You’re not playing that right.) That bullshit pisses me off.
                    For further personal context. I hate the DnD class system. Even in a Fantasy Setting that could potentially promote a Caste System for Adventurers (Pathfinder). The Class System is little more than short-hand for what a Character is training to do in the effort to reach their goals. Most DnD players seem to take this system as a holy gospel. (You are playing a Cleric. Get behind the party.)

                    I guess another way to contextualize MY original fear of the new Seeming Archetypes is that that the first three revealed read like Classes. I'll now explain my apparent confusion (ignorance?) of the intent behind the write-ups below.
                    For example: We do have Orders, Tribes, and Covenants that seem to assume their own superiority. But they do so from a position of that being their stated goal as an organization. Seemings are instead personal representations of an internal choice. The Fairest Changeling made a choice to assume authority by taking responsibility. Leaders generally are responsible. But not every responsible person is a leader. Is this their Soul trying to force the Fairest into being a Leader after they exulted personal responsibility? Where does Seeming come from? Is it Wyrd enforcing a Narrative ON the Changeling?
                    I thought it was the Changeling’s Soul redefining their place in a Narrative being spun upon them.

                    Originally posted by Merinoc View Post
                    As has been stated already, most of these complaints are for things that do not seem to be present in the teasers. Changeling has always been a game that, for me at least, inspired a great deal of creative lateral thinking and after reading these forums for some time I do not think I am alone. The people complaining appear to have taken the opposite view though and have boxed the game in with either a very narrow perception of what the game is and can be or seek (strangely) a reduction of options, as seen with those who are complaining about the plethora of options being offered through the greatly expanded kith list. To take a specific example that someone mentioned regarding the unsuitability of the Beast Seeming for their player's Fox/Trickster character, then don't use Beast with that character. We are going to have not only a massive kith list (which it baffles me why this is seen as a negative) but additional Seemings as well. It is easier than ever now to find combinations that suit a character's personality and ambitions. The information we have so far makes it look like we will have more freedom to create in the new edition which seems pretty appropriate for the Changeling setting.
                    I forget where I saw it, but it makes sense. The write-ups are intended to represent the most fanatically ‘X’ of a particular Seeming. With this understanding I can go to my players whining about the Darkling and say. "Sure, that is indeed what a fanatically ‘Darkling’ character would be. But your character is more than her Seeming. She has a Kith and in the future will ponder an association with a Court. She will have "Vice" and "Virtue" that will pin down her world view. And all of this is affects your Character's whole in whatever balance you like"

                    I still don’t like that the Fairest revolve Around LEADERSHIP. I personally read from the first paragraphs that they seem more empowered by PERSONAL POTENTIAL. Or lack thereof. That might be a leadership trait, but so is fearlessness against insurmountable odds (Ogre) The willingness to take dangerous risks (Darkling) and the abandoning of traditional expectations (Beast) I like the direction of the Seeming Archetypes. I don't feel the execution of those Archetypes is perfect mechanically and fluff-wise. Accept Ogres. Ogres are almost on the nose as far as perfection goes.
