    Originally posted by Tessie View Post

    While Persistent Conditions are supposed to be lasting, in terms of rules they only differ from regular Conditions in two ways: They take more effort to resolve, and they grant one beat per session if they impact the character's life.
    The regular version of Comatose can be resolved the very same scene with no external input. The Persistent version requires external help, thus requiring more effort than the regular version.

    Conditions can fade because of two reasons: They have a timer, or they stop being relevant to the character. Technically both regular and Persistent Conditions can fade, but Persistent Conditions will rarely, if ever, do so because they're supposed to represent lasting issues or circumstances.
    In practice, however, neither version of Comatose will ever fade because being comatose should never stop being relevant.
    Thanks. I guess the name Persistent Condition is a bit misleading, at least to me. As you put it, the difference with regular Conditions is less about fading or lasting, and more about the effort required to resolve it. The two concepts usually coincide most of the time, but not always.

                                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                            Does the destruction of a Comatose changeling's Bastion or the depletion of their Dream Health cause them to realize they are dreaming and resolve the Condition?
                                                            It's not that you destroy their Bastion to wake them up; it's that you wake them and the Bastion is destroyed as a result. And yes, if you successfully wake them (in accordance to the specifics of the Comatose Condition) then they wake up. I'm personally of the mind that jarring paradigm shifts and being killed automatically makes the dreamer realise it's a dream; the former because of the natural dissonance between the Bastion as it was and the effects of the shift, and the latter because that's also quite jarring and it gets rid of the situation where the dreamer (potentially a PC) no longer has any agency at all.
