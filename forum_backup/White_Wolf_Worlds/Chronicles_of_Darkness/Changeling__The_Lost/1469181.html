    [Wayward and Weird] Supernal Contracts

    11-29-2021, 01:07 PM
    The “Mysterious Frontiers” chapter of Dark Eras 2 contains special rules for the Lost sealing bargains with the enigmatic sentient symbol-creatures of a more rarefied plane of existence. Here is a selection of the sorts of powers the dwellers of the Supernal might grant a changeling in exchange for drawing the fairy one step closer to the brilliant, horrible truth of creation.

    Sometimes mages accidentally (or otherwise) forge a connection between the Wyrd and the Abyss. Sometimes changelings mistakenly (or otherwise) swear bargains with intruders from anti-reality. Each Supernal Contract notes additional effects it entails when acquired from an Abyssal entity.


    Enlightened Sight

    Cost:     1 Glamour

    Action: Reflexive

    Dice Pool: None

    The changeling aligns his second sight with one of the Supernal Realms. Each instance of this Contract is attached to one of the ten Arcana, and the Contract may be purchased multiple times. When the changeling makes a Kenning roll, he may activate this Contract; on a success, the changeling is alerted if any detected supernatural phenomena fall under the Arcanum’s purview. If the Lost rolls an exceptional success on the Kenning action, he gains the effects of Active Mage Sight for the Arcanum for the rest of the scene, or until he dismisses the visions with a reflexive action.

    When used with the Fate Arcanum, the changeling’s Kenning roll scores an exceptional success with three successes instead of five.

    Loophole: The changeling employs a symbolically appropriate divination tool when Kenning, such as peering through a witch-stone.

    Abyssal: Glimpsing reality through a Dur-Abzu lends truth to madness. The Lost can make a Kenning roll with this Contract regardless of his current Clarity rating. Any Perception penalties from Clarity damage apply as a bonus to the roll instead.


    Elsewise Power

    Cost:     1 - 3 Glamour

    Action: Instant

    Dice Pool: Attribute + Skill + Wyrd - Withstand

    This Contract allows the Lost to use a single spell that she bargained for with a Supernal Entity (who must be capable of casting the spell with its own Arcana). The spell in question must be rated at three dots or less, and require only a single Arcanum to cast. The Glamour cost of the spell is equal to its Arcanum rating, and its dice pool is determined when the Contract is purchased, using traits symbolically appropriate to both the spell in question and the Entity that provided it. The changeling gains no Reach to spend on the spell (or put another way, the single free Reach is automatically allocated to Instant casting time).

    This Contract may be purchased multiple times, each instance representing a different spell. Not every Mage spell is appropriate for this Contract. The Storyteller may disallow the Lost from gaining certain spells if their usage would be nonsensical or disruptive, or alter a spell’s effect to something comparable but more suitable for a changeling.

    Success: The changeling casts the spell, affecting a single valid target the user touches. Set the primary spell factor to the number of successes rolled (ie, if the Lost rolls four successes, then the spell has either a Potency of 4 or a Duration of five turns). The changeling cannot use this Contract again until the cast spell expires (although she can still cast other spells she knows as Supernal Contracts), and can dismiss a spell with an instant action.

    Although drawing on the Supernal as part of the bargain of this Contract’s effect, the magic itself is filtered through and levied by the Wyrd, and thus is not subject to Quiescence, Dissonance, or Paradox. Mortals still suffer a Breaking Point for witnessing overt supernatural effects, however. Although lacking a Wisdom trait, comparable Acts of Hubris may result in Clarity attack rolls at Storyteller discretion based on the spell and its consequences.

    Exceptional Success: The changeling gains one free Reach to spend on the spell as she pleases. If a given Reach effect costs Mana, the Lost spends Glamour instead. She may also divide up successes among all spell factors, so long as at least one success is invested in the Primary Factor.

    Failure: The spell fizzles out.

    Dramatic Failure: The changeling gains another point of Supernal Debt for the price of her hubris.

    Loophole: The Lost employs a spellcasting implement or performs a symbolic action that would act as an appropriate Yantra with a dice bonus equal to or greater than the Contract’s Glamour cost. Treat the Realm of the patron entity as the changeling’s Path for Path Tools. For Order Tools, the Lost may use objects that resonate with the themes of her court.

    Abyssal: Every time the changeling casts the spell, roll a Paradox dice pool equal to the spell’s dot rating. Apply the dice modifier chart on page 115 for relevant conditions. In addition, add any Perception penalty the Lost is suffering due to Clarity damage as a bonus to the roll. The Storyteller can also spend Abyssal Debt to add dice; each point spent adds two dice. Successes scored on this roll can be applied as Paradox Anomalies. The changeling can take control of the chaos if she’s willing to further the Abyss’s grasp on her soul: For each additional point of Abyssal Debt accepted, the changeling may instead dictate the allocation of a single Paradox success.


    Apprentice’s Boon

    Cost:     1 Glamour

    Action: Reflexive

    Dice Pool: None

    The changeling takes on the role of sorcerous assistant to one of the Awakened. By activating this Contract, the user allows the mage to store one spell in her as if she were a Sleepwalker, though no preexisting Sympathetic connection is required. A changeling with this Contract may hold a total of (Wyrd/2, rounded up) spells at a single time. The Lost treats her Impression level with any mage whose spell she’s currently holding as one step higher, and can seal their statements without spending Glamour.

    Loophole: The mage who cast the spell currently shares a bargain with the Lost.

    Abyssal: By spending a point of Willpower at any time, the changeling can consume a held spell, ending its effect immediately and gaining Glamour equal to the spell’s Arcanum rating. This also generates the Lost’s Wyrd rating in Paradox dice, which are automatically applied to the next spell she witnesses a mage cast. Stored Paradox dice stack, but fade at the end of the story.


    Elf’s Forge

    Cost: 3 Glamour + 1 Willpower

    Action: Instant

    Duration: One story/month

    Dice Pool: Intelligence + Crafts + Wyrd - Size

    In ancient tales, cunning folk who live under mountains and among the roots of enormous trees forge mythic blades and unbreakable armor for heroes and gods alike. Drawing upon the underlying symbols of these narratives, the Supernal entity grants the changeling the power to perfect Fallen materials into their idealized form.

    Although the activation roll for Elf’s Forge is an instant action, the Lost must spend at least an hour working the material in a way appropriate to its form, passing gold through fire, hammering tin, stripping and soaking leather. Furthermore, this Contract can only be enacted in the Hedge, and the user cannot perfect iron in this way.

    Success: The changeling’s magic transmutes the worked material into its perfected form. It possesses all the inherent mystical properties of its type (cf Signs of Sorcery for details), can be used to form amalgams, and so on.

    There are flaws to perfecting materials using this method, borne of those bothersome old Arcadian oaths: In addition to the Contract’s duration, every material perfected this way bears a specific condition, dictated by the Wyrd, that will cause the item’s magic to fade. Example conditions include using the material to kill a mortal, spilling a particular type of liquid on the material, breaking a promise sworn on the object, exposing the material to sunlight, telling the truth while holding the item, or provoking a Paradox Anomaly while using the material as a Yantra. The changeling automatically intuits the determined condition; whether he chooses to pass on this information is up to him. In addition, attacks against the material with iron ignore Durability, contact with cold iron reduces the object’s Structure by one point per turn, and contact with Siderite causes it to shatter, immediately and irrevocably.

    Exceptional Success: The perfection extends to the object’s more mundane properties as well. The player chooses from one among the material’s Durability, Structure, equipment bonus, weapon damage, or general or ballistic armor rating to increase by one. This extends to any future shape the material takes.

    Failure: The Hedge’s transformative powers fail to affect the object. The changeling may keep trying.

    Dramatic Failure: The changeling’s careless handling of the material damages both. The user suffers a point of lethal damage, and the object gains either the Fragile or Volatile Condition.

    Loophole: The changeling works the chosen material in a Hollow Workshop with symbolically appropriate accoutrements.

    Abyssal: Objects perfected with this Contract become magic leeches. The first time each scene a character uses a supernatural power in the presence of the material, the character loses an additional point of relevant occult currency.

    Perfected Tokens: Sometimes a changeling gets the idea into his head to try out this Contract on a token. It more or less works as normal, though the token must be composed of a single type of material in order to be perfected, and the roll is penalized by the greater of the object’s size or dot rating. The token is then treated as an appropriate perfected material for the Contract’s duration. In certain cases where the material is of particular symbolic importance (such as a silver cross that repels the undead), the user may find the token’s effect enhanced as if it were one dot higher.


    Sage’s Raiment

    Cost:     2 Glamour

    Action: Instant

    Dice Pool: None

    The Supernal Entity grants the bargaining changeling the use of a single two-dot Attainment, either a Utility or one form of Mage Armor. Once activated, the Attainment’s effects last for the rest of the scene. The following changes apply to Attainments granted with this Contract:

    Eyes of the Dead/Mind’s Eye/Spirit Eyes: The changeling is automatically alerted to the unmanifested presence of a relevant class of ephemeral entity as if she had Peripheral Mage Sight, and such entities can be detected with a successful Kenning roll. By spending an additional point of Glamour, the changeling can interact with the class of entity for the rest of the scene.

    Conditional Duration/Permanence/Sympathetic Range/Temporal Sympathy: These Attainments work on any spells the changeling casts using Elsewise Power. The cost of activating the Attainment is covered by the Glamour cost of this Contract, but all other prerequisites must be met (only a Matter spell can benefit from Permanence, etc). The two Contracts are woven together in the casting, and the total cost in all resources for both must be paid. Example: A changeling who wants to cast Temporal Summoning using the Temporal Sympathy Attainment must spend a total of five Glamour, take two instant actions, and accrue two points of Supernal Debt, as well as utilize an appropriate Sympathetic Yantra.

    Improved Pattern Restoration: When active, the Lost can heal her own wounds with fresh Glamour. After a successful harvesting roll from any source, the changeling can choose to heal bashing damage for one Glamour each, or lethal for two. Only immediately harvested Glamour may be spent this way. When used with Reaping, the Lost heals all bashing and lethal damage, but gains no additional Glamour from the action.

    Universal Counterspell: The changeling may counter other Contracts she perceives at the moment of their activation. Roll a Clash of Wills for the two parties; if the user wins, the opposing party’s magic fails to take effect, and any resources spent wasted. If the changeling spends an additional point of Glamour, her opponent is forced to use their lowest Finesse Attribute in their dice pool rather than the most appropriate. The Contract has no authority over the Dread Powers of hobgoblins or Huntsmen, but may be used against the Influences and Numina of Hedge ghosts if the Lost is aware of their effects.

    Mage Armor: Each Arcanum of Mage Armor must be purchased as a separate Contract, and a changeling with multiple versions cannot switch between them the same way a mage can. Where an effect is calculated using Arcanum dots, substitute half the changeling’s Wyrd rating, rounded up. If an effect within a given Armor calls for Mana, the Lost spends Glamour instead.

    Loophole: The changeling first clears her thoughts of all distractions, taking a minute (or a turn in action scenes) to meditate and succeeding on a Resolve + Composure roll. The Contract does not have to be activated immediately after meditating, so long as it is within the same scene.

    Abyssal: No further effect. Some Abyssal entities offer bargainers corrupted takes on extant Attainments or powers similar to those wielded by the Scelesti, but these are unique and dangerous circumstances. Cross-disciplinary occultists are pretty certain a changeling can’t receive the Elder Diadem, but no one has ever found any historical record of any Lost making a bargain with the Old Man, and so far no one in modern days has been brave or crazy enough to try.
