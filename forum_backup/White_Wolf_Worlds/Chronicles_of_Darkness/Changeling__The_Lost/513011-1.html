                                                To be absolutely clear, when I said that any place can fit that narrative function, I meant that strictly in the Vladimir-Propp-style-narratology sense, since I assumed that's what Arcane meant. Narrative functions in that context are extremely general, such as "hero leaves home" or "villain is defeated", so virtually any location can serve as the setting for any narrative function.

                                                Insomuch as Mordor or the Lonely Mountain can be the equivalent of Arcadia, because of the characters' actions or development there and how that moves the narrative forward, then any place can be made into the equivalent of Arcadia. That location doesn't need to have the same metaphysical characteristics or symbolic meanings to fulfill the same basic narrative function. Sure it won't do it in nearly the same manner as Arcadia, but neither does Mordor. And not every Changeling game will use Arcadia for that purpose. It's narrative function is going to be different in each Changeling's individual story (I'm talking post Durance here, it obviously fills the same role for each Changeling's backstory), and that function is separate from (though it could be influenced by) Arcadia's place in the setting's metaphysics and its metaphorical significance.

                                                I think the disagreement people are having is rooted in looking at the question "How important is Arcadia?" two different ways:

                                                1. How important is Arcadia to the canon? - I think everyone can agree that Arcadia is a major part of the lore of the setting as written. Apart from the difference of its absence, removing it will cause ripples that will affect many aspects of the game. Lots of questions that will have to be answered for the setting to make sense, or that will become mysteries (see my previous posts). Additionally, it will remove or alter certain thematic elements of the game that some people consider core to the games identity. (Personally, I think that other things could be used in its place to restore most of those thematic elements, but that's still more change.)

                                                As opposed to -

                                                2. How important is Arcadia to running the game? - This is going to be different from ST to ST and game group to game group. There are a lot of ideas and themes presented in CtL, and even more that suggest themselves; not every gaming group is going to use all of them. Some groups will focus on certain themes and let others drift into the background, some groups will largely ignore the setting's more prominent themes and replace them with themes of their own, and others won't worry about themes or metaphors or any of that at all and just enjoy the setting without analyzing it - it is a game, after all. So, how important it is to running a game depends on how heavily you tend to focus on the elements that will be affected the most by Arcadia's absence. I think this is where MCN is coming from. Not using every theme or idea that the material provides - or avoiding the "central" themes and focusing on "lesser" ones or ignoring the stated themes altogether to tell a story about themes that you find more interesting or important - doesn't make a game bad.

                                                Personally, I think a game with no Arcadia, or an unreachable Arcadia, could be an interesting way to explore new themes or to use different metaphors and elements to explore old themes. I think it would take a good bit of work to do it well, and it would definitely be a different experience, but it could be very rewarding. That said, I'll probably never get around to it...

Not very. The most important question here is "Why should the characters care?" If you have an awesome world built up, its not that important if you don't actually use it in play.
