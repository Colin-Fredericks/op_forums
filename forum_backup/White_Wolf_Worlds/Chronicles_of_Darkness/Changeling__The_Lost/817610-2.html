    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
    Uh, how isn't being a feral child psychology?
    You seem to be overly focused on the aesthetic idea of a feral child, since you are associating the Beast Seeming with it, when the only thing they both have in common is the appeal to wildness. The psychology of a Beast does not match the feral children. Like I said before, try Ogre or Elemental instead.

    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
    The thing is that I guess I don't follow how someone goes from being screwed over by the Gentry to social justice warrior. The two tones seem to conflict quite heavily. Beast seems to be trying to pander to "primal reversion" and "angsty rebel" simultaneously and I don't really think it fits. Maybe it's not really trying to be the formal at all, but it certainly tries to pretend it is. I don't even know why they're called Beasts anyway, doesn't seem to really fit what they're supposed to be.
    I think the problem is that you're overly limiting and restricting character concepts to rigid stereotypes, and don't allow sufficient leeway for things to actually make sense. If your Keeper was a stereotypical patriarchal great white hunter, then you might see shades of him whenever a rich older white man speaks on TV. It makes quite a bit of sense if you are willing to imagine the situation and allow it to unfold to its logical conclusion instead of just fixating on a single specific thing and expecting everything to revolve around it. Not all Beasts are overtly feral or predatory. Some of them are skittish and fast, some are big and majestic, some are sleek and elegant, some are sharp and ornery, some are gentle until roused, and so on. The thing they all have in common is that they regain Clarity when they defy societal conventions, because it evokes how they escaped.

    Even the current text doesn't always reference predators or feral changelings. One of the examples is a changeling that digs his way out of his kennel. That's very non-confrontative, and yet it's also a defiance of social norms. You're not supposed to dig your way out of a cage. You're supposed to stay in there like the good tamed pet you are.

    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
    I guess the problem with the tone is there's a difference between ignoring cultural norms, and actively spitting in the face of cultural norms. Beasts, in their current state, seem to do the latter. It's almost some mix of preachy and rebelling without a cause, and it really rubs me the wrong way.
    I don't think it's fair or reasonable of you to expect all Beast changelings to behave the same way and have the same opinions regarding societal mores. Some may be happy merely ignoring them (some may do so rarely, others constantly), some may want to actively spit in the face of them. It's character diversity, and it's a good thing.

    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
    That's why I'm bringing this up, I think maybe it should be reevaluated.
    I would tone down the predator references (done to death in Vampire, Werewolf and Beast), but other than that, I think the core concept of Beast is absolutely perfect and I think it should be merely diversified rather than reevaluated.

            Figure I might as well bring up my character concept for Ziggy Zapf for critique, since it's appropriate to this thread.

            Abduction and Durance: He was a game developer stuck in development hell caused by numerous conflicts. The game he wanted to design was a survival experience where the player controls a pack of magical beasts resembling those in collectible monster games. His Keeper offered him a way to make his game "come alive." He didn't realize how literal the deal was. He winds up as a disembodied game warden, allegedly impartial referee, managing a version of his game twisted to bloodsport.

            Escape: His Keeper often threatened to punish him by turning him into one of the monsters and forcing him to fight for his survival. After preparations, he manages to set up circumstances so that he can essentially customize his monster body, and contrary to his Keeper's expectations, he focused entirely on running, seeing no dignity in going down in "honorable" combat. He used his knowledge of the game, stealth, and so forth to reach an exit he put in a briar patch. Suddenly having a body again was exhilarating, especially one that he modified himself, and its athletic ability served him well in the realm's ever-shifting terrain.

            The inner conflict: Ziggy was always an intellectual type, but his Durance and escape gave him a deep appreciation for his physical side. When not attending to formal matters, he often wears as little clothing as possible, to the point that he writes his best computer code in the buff. Additionally, while he still likes being connected with the human world, he has little attachment to the "pure" human form: He enjoys self-modification, both in mundane forms like his tattoos, and magical transformations. He routinely visits the local Goblin Market in hopes of finding such means of self-improvement.

                                    I think part of the problem is that you're thinking in terms of archetype, and that's not what Seemings are about. Seemings are about how you took agency. They're about what you did to escape. They're about action. There is no "feral child" Seeming because feral child isn't an action, it's an archetype. There is no "following your base instincts" Seeming because following your instincts isn't an action, it's a motivation. Any Seeming can be motivated to escape due to instinct. Any Seeming can become a feral child when they return to the mortal world. What defines this feral child character's Seeming is what his instincts drove him to do.

                                                One thing I like about seemings is that while the escape strongly defines your bond with the faery, it doesn't necessarily directly affect your personality. That it's more subtle than "Okay you escaped through breaking a convention and expectation so now your constantly rebelling", instead encouraging and discouraging behaviours through clarity.

                                                One of the chains that binds humanity is that you can't just do what your instincts tell you to, but knowing that "Wait, if I do act on my instincts I'll be more grounded." means there is more reason to act on your instincts so you just end up doing it more. But you'll also want to be tied down less, not because "Fey magic says so" but because such experiences remind you of your durance and increase the grip the faery has on your perceptions.

                                                Just thought I should say that I greatly enjoy the subtly and finesse in getting the mechanics to influence the players rather than ordering the players.
