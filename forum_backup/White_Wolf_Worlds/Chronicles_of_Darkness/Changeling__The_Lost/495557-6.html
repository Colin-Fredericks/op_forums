        Originally posted by Phaolan View Post
        Question (and sorry if it's been addressed somewhere that I didn't see): How do you expect a new player to wade through 100 Kiths and decide what she wants her character to be? I mean, in terms of starting player choice, this sounds like a daunting task…

        I guess that’s my biggest worry about this. In First Edition, the process of Kith-after-Seeming made those choices linear, and the fact that one of the Lost didn’t NEED a Kith within his Seeming left a lot of options open in the character development process.

        My troupe adopted notions that Kith can ’emerge’ later, that a ‘fresh’ changeling need time and effort to understand what all happened to her. The loss of pieces of her soul to the thorns (on the way into Arcadia and on the way back out) left bits and bobs that were replaced by the stuff of Faerie. The journey of discovering just WHAT that means, both in terms of game mechanics and in the character’s mind, was a huge part of our enjoyment of Changeling: the Lost.

        This version appears to mandate (and please correct me if I’m wrong) that a specific Kith be chosen for a new character like how a Vampire: the Requiem player chooses a Clan. Vampire has five Clans, but now Changeling has 100 Kiths, which is a hell of a big difference. Starting with a Seeming (one of six) wasn’t a huge conceptual hurdle, and once Seeming’s chosen, a Kith grows out of that.

        …This is a concern, mind you, not a gripe. My troupe has evolved a very fluid notion of how Kith and Seeming interact, not unlike the one you’re adding to Second Edition. The difference is that we’ve studied and played and worked on the game for years. A new player won’t have that advantage, will she? I just detest the idea of saying, ‘Welcome to the game! Here’s your homework.’ :\
        There are three strategies I can recommend:

        1) Have an expert. One person who's reviewed all 100 in detail and is able to hear a character concept and say, "Ah! Here are two kiths that sound similar." Now you only have to deal with 2 choices.

        2) Skip the fluff and read the blessings. Your goal is to get a sense of power level. Then, when you have a character concept, just skip all the ones in the book and come up with one whole cloth.

        3) Skip the kith, as you've been doing, and just have a character concept. Gain your blessing at some point later when you've got a handle on things. Or never, if it doesn't bother you to not have access to the extra bonus.

        I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".

            Originally posted by Hexmeister Vic View Post
            I like this new approach to building powers that started in Werewolf and continues here, what with using existing mechanics rather than having to build native mechanics for each splat to do the same things.
            ...I don't know that I agree. Oh, I DO recognise the expediency of not 'doubling up' on powers and mechanics that already exist, but I think that using an off-the-shelf approach really weakens the feeling of every 'splat' being unique. This could especially become an issue with any kind of cross-over game. I mean, if a changeling's kith is defined by her access to a Merit and a quick imposition of a Tilt, can't another creature meet that same set of criteria? Beyond that, I can see 'metagame' issues of players getting into the habit of saying, 'That dude's just given me a Condition, ain't no thang.'

            Please know that *I* know it's not exactly as I state above, but it's something that rubbed me wrong when I first saw this. I believe that I'll get over it (David's comment about the references to God-Machine Chronicle and other sources being eventually 'integrated' into the Changeling book means a LOT to me), and no one needs to take my comments too seriously. I'm just processing...

                    Yeah I'd generally recommend a mixture between 1 and 3. At the very least have someone read through and at least somewhat understand the kith list (most likely the storyteller since he can focus on helping all the players during character creation.) After you have someone understanding it, they can help people build by starting with character concept a move on from there. Generally the way I approached my players when building changelings is, "This is a modern fairy tale setting, what kind of character do you want to play? I'm almost certain there's at least one way to make that work."
