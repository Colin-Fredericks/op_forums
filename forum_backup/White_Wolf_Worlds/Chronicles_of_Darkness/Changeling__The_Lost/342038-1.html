    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    Well, I want to incorporate PCs motivations, only players still do not want to send me them. But I think that curiosity is enough for young changelings to try to see what is it in there...
    It really shouldn't be, with what Changelings in general have gone through. I don't think it's a good enough motivation unless they're taking it upon themselves to investigate everything related to the Fae in the area or are planning to protect everyone from possibly supernatural influence. Or they're heavily Summer Court leaning with no sense of self-preservation. Quoting myself:

    Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
    If I was a Changeling, I wouldn't even attend something this suspicious. It screams "faerie plot". Give them reasons to want to go to this thing.
    Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
    You need to have some sort of motivation for your player characters to attend this. If there's just a random ball reeking of faerie business and they're such a small group that doesn't even have a Freehold to turn to, it would be ridiculous for them to go along with it.
    It don't think it's good form to plan something like this without considering the interests of the players and their characters, especially if you're just planning to leave them hanging in the case they can't find a good justification to pursue this story thread. There's always a certain degree of contrivance involved when you plan events in the game together with the players, but I entirely agree with BaronSamedi's points and HobbitGuy's observation. You really should involve your players and take into account their expectations, even if you can't satisfy everyone; because if you build railroads that only lead towards a certain direction and your players aren't interested in the destination or find the train fee unsatisfactory so to speak, most of your work ends up being wasted and no one's happy because there aren't alternatives.

                        Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post

                        As such, I think that idea about Banished Old God that want to get back on on rest of Feud by giving the local Lost clues about the Game itself - is totally in order.
                        In that case I have something I ran near the end of my last Changeling chronicle that you might mine for ideas.

                        Our story starts with a Gentry known as The Scarecrow King. 200 years ago he was a powerful and power hungry Autumn king. He did many terrible things to obtain strength in magic, in the freehold, and even in the mortal world but he justified himself to himself with the thought that all this personal power was for the greater good as it let him protect others. It was this lie that let him sleep at night.

                        When his freehold wound up besieged by one of the Others the Scarecrow King managed to best the Fae and take a Title. In doing so he destroyed his freehold and ascended to become one of the Fair Folk himself. Still, he held tight to his lie - “This is all to help others.” For the sake of this lie even as a True Fae he helps the Lost in his way. He plays games with freeholds arranging for them to win boons if they are strong enough and in exchange he gets the conflict, the story, he needs to survive.

                        In the present day The Scarecrow King challenged the PC’s to a game. He would take 13 mortals connected to the freehold, friends, lovers, even children, and place both them and their fetches in the manner in the woods on All Hallows Eve. If the changelings could keep the majority of mortals safe until dawn then each freehold monarch would get the truthful answer to one question and all the mortals would be returned safely. If the majority of the mortals succumb to the dangers of the house then all the mortals would be forfeit and the changelings would get only the fetches as consolation.

                        The house was decorated as a Halloween party and everyone that entered (including a local Werewolf pack) was magically costumed so they could not be recognized. Nobody could recognize one another nor the mortals and fetches nor the phantasmal monsters of the house on sight nor scent. The first challenge was simply telling who was who. Luckily the changelings had prepared by knowing one another’s desires, fears, and sorrows as well as those of their mortals.

                        All the side rooms of the mansion have various puzzles and challenges where they could free this mortal/fetch or with threats that might “kill”* the guests if they weren’t stopped. These ranged from riddles to Professor Layton puzzles to combat. In addition to the mitigation of threat solving puzzles also rewarded the changelings with various objects* including 2 keys. The artifacts didn’t do much in the way of magic but tended to be helpful elsewhere. A thesaurus that always opens to the last word spoken isn’t that great unless you need help solving a riddle before the timer runs out.

                        As morning approached I threw one last curveball - the house started collapsing. Anyone trapped inside would be “killed.” In the last few minutes (and this was being run in real time!) the PC’s realized that they needed a key to get out of the locked house. There was no front door nor any other exit so they just needed to be touching the key. They looked at their two keys and realized one has a sun printed on it and the other was labeled “There’s no place like home.” After a quick argument they arrived at the correct conclusion. They needed the key to survive dawn not a key to get home.

                        At this point they had 10 or 11 mortals left and nobody could quite decide which of these were real humans and which were fetches. They ran the clock down to 20 seconds arguing about which mortals they could take with them before the Sumer King remembers they only needed to save 7+ mortals, saving fetches had no penalty. Again, this was run in real time. I was sitting there with a timer watching them. It was pretty amazing and was some awesome catharsis as I announced the house melting back into forest and all 13 friends and family safely around them.

                        * The whole manner was a dream so deaths were not permanent, everyone would be fine in the morning so long as the PC’s won and got the mortals back. Going to Arcadia isn’t a great option even if your new keeper is “sympathetic.” All the bric-a-brac of the house and, indeed, the house itself was also phantasmal and vanished in dawns first rays.

                        This all probably isn't exactly what you want to do but I hope it can provide some ideas for your masquerade XD

                            Okay, here are my thoughts:

                            The core problem you're trying to tackle here is "How do I get my players interested in the main plot line?" This is a very common challenge, especially in modern horror settings.

                            The issue is that you're trying to force this interest (via a villain/employer hybrid) in the course of one session. Believe me, from experience: it often takes multiple sessions to get a motley to realise 'Huh, maybe we should look into this?' Drip feed the clues into what the players want to do, rather than force feeding them. The satisfaction they get from facing a challenge they uncovered themselves will be greater than if they're told 'Here's your main quest: complete it or else."

                            Let's look at the possible options your players may take when faced with an exiled Fae that's strong-arming them into doing what it wants:

                            1) They run away. These are fresh out of the hedge changelings with no backup: no Courts, no Freehold, no powerful contacts. They've been manipulated into a trap. If I was an Ogre in that situation, I'd make a door through the nearest wall. If I was Fairest, I'd blast Vainglory to buy enough time for everyone else to escape. The threat of killing hostages works only if the players care about mortal deaths: plenty of players are calous enough to let them die to save their own skin. Never assume heroic intentions in a World of Darkness game.

                            2) They attack the host. This is the reverse of running away: they take the heroic incentive to the logical conclusion. This is a villain, it's threatening to kill everyone... Combat time. If this is an exiled Fae, it should be diminished enough in power that a lucky or clever motley has a chance of hurting it. If it dies or runs away, you can take it from there easily: the players have saved the day and can take their own course of action in working out what's going on.

                            Otherwise, they die. Or are captured. Nothing that incentivises them to suddenly start working for the thing, anyway.

                            3) They plain refuse to accept the terms, standing there doing nothing. See above concerning calous players. The Fae butchers a room full of mortals then probably attacks the motley. Then you're up at 2).

                            4) They go along with your plan. This actually the riskiest and most damaging course of action from a Changeling perspective. What the above posters are saying about overexposure of Gentry is true for multiple reasons. Just spending time with a True Fae long enough to have a conversation is a major Clarity breaking point. This alone should dissuade your players from sticking around to chat. Nowhere in any of the books (from what I remember) does it say anything about Exiled or Charlatan Fae being exempt from this effect.

                            If the group then decides to start taking orders from the thing, regardless of the motivations, every other changeling that discovers this will consider them Loyalists. The fact that it's to save some lives will be irrelevant: many Loyalists work for their masters with similar threats or incentives looming over them. "Bring me pretty things or your family spends a season on the Hurting-Go-Round..."

                            From your players perspective, they will resent the task given to them. Rather than curiosity pushing them to solve the case, they will try to solve the problem of "How do we escape this servitude we've been forced into?" They'll keep looking for the opportunity to enact any of the other options above, probably after freeing the hostages.

                            Okay, all of the above is hypothetical. I hope it demonstrates all the directions a group of players can go, assuming they even accept the invitation and go to the party.

                            Absolutely, plan a masquerade ball if that's what you want to do. Myself, I'd make the host an Ensorcelled mortal who moved to the area from another Freehold. He/she can use the opportunity to introduce the players to standard Freehold concepts: if the group has truly never meet any othe changelings outside of Arcadia, these are things they'll need to learn: the role of Courts, leadership, the threats that face the Lost and how to survive amongst regular people without turning into monsters.

                            And, yes, plant a seed for a greater plot. Have a murder but keep it a mystery. Have a bloody, exhausted Changeling drop through a bathroom mirror, having just escaped Faerie. Don't stick a fully grown tree in front of the players and say "This what you're going to climb."
