                            Perhaps... hmm, here's a combo.

                            Perhaps an Elemental is the core of something, a concept that has all its, "extraneous," parts taken away. Sometimes, that makes it natural, but other times... not so much. They're like a drawing with no shading, or any of the tiny details that make something unique. A Draconic Elemental is nothing but that creature, without any of the hallmarks that would make it say, European or Asian style dragons. Now, how is this an expression of Agency?

                            Well, that goes into the second bit, which is refusing to be apart of something else. This goes back to the original pile of bricks example, where from once being part of a building, you escaped. Well, separation could be refusing to have the details that the others have, instead becoming a core concept, without any of the needless shading. This would also fit the emotionally cold part of being an Elemental, by refusing to embrace the details of others, and become what someone else wants.

                            Obviously, this has issues, not the least of which that it probably doesn't work with certain Kiths, but what are the thoughts on this?

                                A thought strikes me, and I'm not sure if it's because it's a good one or because it's 11:15 at night.

                                It feels like some of the problem folks are having is trying to wrap the old Seemings up in this new paradigm of agency. Trying to work "this is what ____ means as a statement on agency" into the concepts behind the old Seemings. But why keep the old Seemings at all - at least, while in the planning phase?

                                It feels like it might be more productive to instead go "what statements do we want to make about those characters who manage to escape to become Changelings, and what concepts flow from that," and then, once those ideas are nailed down, either attach them to the old Seeming names or, if necessary, invent/combine Seeming names to suit them. Shoehorning old square concepts into new round holes will just lead to issues of setting consistency that'll cause issues down the line if and when they decide a third edition might be needed.

                                So, what sort of "agency archetypes" might people be looking for, and what sorts of statements might such archetypes make, independently of labels such as "Beast," "Wizened," or "Fairest?"

                                                    Darkling: Sacrifice. As others have said they sacrificed something, a friend, deep held beliefs, a part of thier humanity, a literal part of themselves, and darkness filled the void within.

                                                            Originally posted by shkspr1048 View Post

                                                            This gets me thinking that a Seeming could go one of two ways: Either it's the same as in 1e, as the beginning shape a keeper molds a Changeling into and that later clarifies into a Kith; or as it is currently suggested, a vague shaping of the Wyrd in response to how the Changeling takes back control of his life, a sort of instinctual, reflexive version of what the Keeper did to ingrain the Changeling's Kith.

                                                            ...hmmm. Would it be feasible for a Changeling to have two Seemings, one reflecting her enslavement along with her Kith, and a second embodying her escape?
                                                            I wasn’t really thinking "dual Seemings" so much as "keep the 'groupings of related Kiths' thing in some form". And since Kiths are "what the Gentry did to you", it seemed reasonable to me to have 2e Kiths, rather than 2e Seemings, include a Frailty. The bit about having groups of Kiths share a Frailty was an attempt at avoiding mechanical bloat: if there are essentially six Frailties, that takes up a lot less space and requires less demands on the designers than if every Kith has a unique Frailty of its own.

                                                            OTOH, Kith Frailties wouldn’t necessarily have to be limited to precisely six, each corresponding to a Seeming. That would be simplest; but there would be nothing preventing you from creating new Kiths with new Frailties if you want. That said, the proposed “shared between Kiths” nature of Kith-based Frailties ought to encourage someone creating a Kith with a new Frailty to ask “what other Kiths with this Frailty might exist?”
