    How to run a Changeling Larp?

    07-07-2015, 09:25 AM
    So, I've been pondering this idea and project in my mind for months now : I'd like to run a CtL larp. Now, there are a few things you need to know first about my case :
    • I'm french, and we never got Changeling the Lost around here. Which means that I'll have to translate a lot at first : courts, kiths, universe, etc. That also means that in a way, I can easily get rid of elements that would be too hard to implement.
    • That also means that there are no Mind's Eye Society around here as well. Well, not exactly : we have something we call the Camarilla Society, but it has nothing to do with any other WoD game. So I can't have much help, no global plot, it's all up to me.
    • I'm no stranger to LARPs, I've played more than a dozen as a player, but almost all lasted a whole weekend, and were only run once a year. I'm also currently in a VtM larp, but quite frankly it is pretty bad. I'm not opposed to working with them with my own larp, though. Finally, I did once help organize a LARP, so I have a bit of experience in this, even if I wasn't the lead GM.

    I'm asking for any of advice, really, but my first concern is about some features of the game and how to include them. For instance, how can I show the difference IC between a Mask and a Mien? Should I have my players dressed in their Mien at all time, and have my NPCs just play along and see them as their Mask?
    What about Fetches? Should I include them at all? How can I feature them in my game when they're supposed to be copies of my players? Obviously, I can't ask twins, but I don't want to have a guyin a black t-shirt and a paper slapped on his chest marked "Hi, I'm Jim's Fetch, I look just like him but human!"...
    What about Mantles, Gentry, Clarity, Oneiromancy, Contracts... I really don't know how to make them work, honestly. And what kind of system could I use?

    If anyone ever had experience with a CtL larp, I'd really welcome your advice, stories, or any kind of help, really.

          One thing that can be useful for IC description is the name badge. The players can have separate "mask" and "mien" fields to fill out. For NPCs, just remember that if it's not Fae, it sees the Mask unless they're in the Hedge or the Mask was dropped. For fetches, again, a nametag or quick OOC announcement of "This guy looks just like Steve, but as a human" will do plenty. The players will have to use their imaginations, but they'll be doing that anyway.

          As for system, you'll need to consider that for the needs of your own game. The Mind's Eye Theater book interfaces decently with the original tabletop mechanics, with only minor modification 100% necessary. Worst case, you can cannibalize the Mind's Eye Society's addendum for Changeling.

                                  >For instance, how can I show the difference IC between a Mask and a Mien?

                                  You don't really. People come dressed as they want, and give a quick (10 second intro at opening Circle) saying anything else that people should know by looking at them. Changelings can see through the mask, yes, so it's generally assumed that (unless someone hardens it), that's what they see. If someone hardens it, then they have to describe what they now look like.

                                  >Should I have my players dressed in their Mien at all time, and have my NPCs just play along and see them as their Mask?

                                  Yes, but it depends on the NPC.

                                  >What about Fetches? Should I include them at all? How can I feature them in my game when they're supposed to be copies of my players? Obviously, I can't ask twins, but I don't want to have a guy in a black t-shirt and a paper slapped on his chest marked "Hi, I'm Jim's Fetch, I look just like him but human!"...

                                  Fetches, I feel, should be more about personal interactions (If it's a character's Fetch). Good for one on one stuff with players, but not as much for big games. Of course, this can be bent if you have a particular plot you want to run. Fetches could be a PC (I wouldn't recommend it if you're starting out with a game, as they have somewhat different goals, and Freeholds hold them in high suspicion most of the time)

                                  >What about Mantles, Gentry, Clarity, Oneiromancy, Contracts... I really don't know how to make them work, honestly. And what kind of system could I use?

                                  Mantles are core book, Gentry are in Autumn Horrors, Clarity should be kept (just make sure you're clear about how it will work, but it is in the main book). Oneriomancy and Contracts are also in the main book. It's just like using abilities from other games. But, I'd recommend not doing Oneriomancy in game for a while. It is fun, but it can be confusing for new players.

                                  The Mind's Eye Society uses a 8 for a success on a pull, and each additional success is 3 above that. (8, 11, 14, 17, 20, ect). I find it works well. You just determine the dice pool, use that as a bonus to the pull, and then let them pull from a deck of 10.

                                  I recommend checking out the Mind's Eye Theatre book. It was helpful for me. I've been in a few Changeling Larps, and it's probably my favorite game to Larp.

                                      I've been playing in/running an ongoing Changeling larp for several years (one with no ties to the Camarilla Society or other MET system). We're a theater larp, rather than a boffer larp (using dice and description to settle combat, rather than boffers and birdseed packets), and it sounds like you're looking to create something similar. I can tell you what we use in our system (and what we've tried and found unsuccessful). We have a game every other week during the college school year (our gaming space is hosted by the local college), amounting to 12-13 games a year, split in two.

                                      For Mask and Mien, we encourage players to dress as much like their Mien as possible. When a new character is introduced, we take a few moments out of character for them to describe their Mask and Mien. For some things, like Striking Looks or playing a different gender, we ask players to pin a notecard to their shirt saying such. For NPC Changelings (and goblins, hedgebeasts, and Gentry), we do the same thing, though costuming is generally an accessory or two, rather than the detail players go into. For NPCs that have no Mien (humans, non-Changeling supernaturals), we dress appropriately, but don't describe Mien. We haven't dealt much with high-Mantle PCs, though the reigning Monarch always gets an appropriate crown to wear.

                                      For dice-rolling and determining successes, we use the complete character sheet of Attributes and Skills (and players carry their sheets around to refer to), but only one die. Add Attribute and Skill (or other Attribute), roll and add 1d10. Formerly, we had a 10-5 approach, because it was simple to remember (10 was a base success, every 5 after was a further success, -5 or lower was a botch). 10s and 9s exploded (add the 10 or 9, then roll again), 1s imploded (subtract 10 from the total, then roll again). However, that made successes harder than they should have been. We're piloting the 8-3 system suggested above this year (8 is a base success, every 3 after is a further success, 0 or less is a botch), with 10s exploding and 1s imploding; it's been working fairly well. We use the MET system of capping successes at 5, particularly in regards to combat (so PCs at full health will never be one-shotted by a single opponent; it makes PVP more interesting and less aggravating). For non-combat ties, we roll the pools again; the former number of successes are kept, but the tie determines who wins. (e.g. Bob is trying to hide from Ana and Claire. Bob rolls 3 successes; Ana rolls 1; Claire rolls 3. Bob and Claire roll again; Bob gets 0; Claire gets 2. Ana still doesn't see Bob, but Claire catches sight of him.) For combat ties, defender wins by default.

                                      We have done Fetches. Generally, we introduce them as human. Our view on the way Changelings see each other is that they generally pay attention to the Mien, not the Mask. They may not immediately realize some human looks like their friend's Mask. We'll give them Wits+Composure rolls to tell something is unusual, but won't spell it out without the players investigating.

                                      We have done Gentry. Their treatment has varied from team to team; we try to only use them in large plots, and they tend to only show up during end-of-semester or end-of-year events. For costuming, we try to make them a bigger deal, but can't always do so when we have to play other NPCs during the night.

                                      Clarity is an ongoing tricky issue for us. We're still working on a good one-die rolling system for Clarity breaks. We have a house rule that you start hallucinating at Clarity 4 (ongoing Madness Condition, with the penalty equal to 10 - current Clarity). We've removed some of the as-written breaking points (mostly the life-changes stuff).

                                      Oneiromancy, as said above, is really hard to do in game, and we mostly don't. Most dream-stuff is done over our forums, between games.

                                      Some Contracts are banned from use in our game (e.g. Hours, because we don't want a PC getting hit with Hours 5 and being told they need to sit in the out-of-game room for the next hour). Some haven't been banned, but don't see much use due to setting/time constraints (Dreams above 1, because we don't often have dreams in-game; Eternal Seasons 5, because no one wants to sit still for an hour to conjure a storm). Fleeting Spring 3 got a bit abused, and had to have restrictions put on of what you could make, as did Dreams 5.

                                      Also, we don't do many extended rolls in-game, because of the aformentioned sitting-and-waiting-and-doing-nothing-else. We try to keep things moving, or at least let people interact with each other while rolling.

                                      Lesse, other tips and advice... From a balance perspective, almost all of the GMC updates are a good idea (Merits, combat mechanics, Conditions). The only things we don't use are the new experience system (including Aspirations) and the Integrity system. Having players collect Beats for dramatic failures and the like is waaaaaay too much overhead, as is trying to remember every player's individual Breaking Point. (Although we did like the increased risk of Dramatic Failures, hence why 0 or less on a roll gets you one automatically.)

                                      We've done a lot of individual home-brewing (splitting the skill Crafts into Arts and Crafts; rolling up Politics into Socialize; re-balancing kiths; capping Wyrd at 5; detailing what exactly Cold Iron is) that may or may not be a good idea for your game; I'm happy to go into more detail if you want, but this post is already super long.

                                              No problem!

                                              Other house rules/anecdotes/ect: When there's a lot of players trying to do multiple things, particularly timing-sensitive things, we drop into combat rounds. It does break the immersion somewhat (although combat rounds do that regardless), but it makes things more fair for players, particularly when they're doing mutually exclusive things.

                                              We also try really hard to not have more than six players in a combat, as combat tends to be slow, particularly with new/non-com characters. If there are that many, we allow "fair escape": an uninvolved character can leave a combat scene on their turn, without further danger. They can't re-enter until the combat's over, though. Which also leads to interesting time dilation effects, where one character can fair escape, have a half-hour conversation with the people outside, then come back in less than a minute after they left (because that's how long the combat took). Haven't found a solution for that one yet.

                                              Speaking of time dilation, we've had to make navigating the Hedge easier, since we don't want to give our players an hour-long time-out when they make a poor roll. Instead of roll failures in Hedge navigation getting them stuck there longer, it ends up with them in more dangerous areas. Which either leads to combat (and spent time) or running away (which leads to us throwing something else at them). It's helped us to have a Hedge random encounter table, with about a quarter of the things on the list being plot-relevant, half being setting-relevant, and the rest being puns and allusions.
