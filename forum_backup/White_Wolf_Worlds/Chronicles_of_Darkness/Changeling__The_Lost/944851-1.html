                I'm trying to withhold judgment until the complete book comes out. That said, I do have several misgivings. For me the first edition of Changeling the Lost was about as perfect a game as WOD (or COD now, I suppose) could have made, with a myriad of ways for a clever player to exemplify the theme of beautiful madness and build the character they truly wanted to play. So it is with a heavy sense of foreboding and consternation that I'm watching all of these major changes to the rules taking place. Whereas 2nd Werewolf and Vampire can be said to mostly be the same game with minor updates and adjustments to properly fit in with 2nd edition and the introduction of a new antagonist to shake things up a little, Changeling sounds more and more foreign to me. I don't want to give up on the game, but I'm definitely prepared to just stick with 1st edition entirely if the new edition can't live up to the old setting. But like I said, I'm not going to make that call until I have the final version of the rules in my hands.

                    I don't know man, Werewolf had some significant changes both to mechanics and to the setting. The Gifts system was completely restructured, Rites got their own completely new system, Harmony was re-built from the ground up to focus on balancing between extremes, Kuruth got significantly expanded upon and the system for determining Kuruth triggers was changed as well, Lunacy was divorced from Willpower and tied into the Breaking Point system and split into three different Conditions (four with the right Gift Facet), the Tribes were changed from roles in werewolf society to organizations founded on common sacred prey, Unahir were excised from the game... The fact that it still feels quintessentially Werewolf the Forsaken, that anyone could look at the two editions side by side and say they were "mostly the same game with minor updates and adjustments" is a testament to the developers' ability to stay true to the identity of the game even when making significant changes to the specific expression. I'm pretty confident the Changeling team will be able to do the same here.

                    As to your specific concerns regarding "ways for a clever player to exemplify the theme of beautiful madness and build the character they truly wanted to play", YMMV, but from my perspective it looks like a lot of the mechanical changes are being made specifically to expand the ways for players to play the character they want to play. With Seeming being divorced from Kith, over 100 Kiths to choose from and options for creating your own unique Kith, Clarity Breaking Points being highly personalized, a system for creating custom Courts and Freeholds... 2e is looking more customizable and personalizable than 1e ever was, which is saying a lot.

                    I think the biggest thing that's going to make the difference between players who adopt 2e and the players who stick with 1e is going to be that "beautiful madness" part. 2e does seem to be shifting away from that theme towards "beautiful lies and terrible truths", and for some that may be too dramatic of a departure. To me, the latter is just a more mature expression of the same ideas the former speaks to, but I've heard from others who disagree. Hopefully the end result is satisfying to many existing fans and brings in new ones to boot, but if you find you and yours would rather stick with 1e, that's a valid option as well.
