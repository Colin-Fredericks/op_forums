    More Details On The Mask (And Other Lore Clarifications)

    08-03-2018, 01:42 AM
    I'm unclear on a few key pieces of lore in the book and I was wondering if one of the developers could clarify them.


    The Mask

    The book says unfortunately little about the Mask in comparison to the Mien. Do you look like your former self? In the past you haven't necessarily, yet one of the short stories in the book has a character recognize a Changeling by name after their escape with a simple look. This left it pretty ambiguous for me. Suppose your Mask doesn't look like your former self then. Is there any rhyme or reason to its appearance? It doesn't look like you, but is it similar? If not, are there any limitations to the random nature of the Mask? Does it have to share your age, your race, your sex?

    Does the book intend to be this vague? It feels like an oversight. Even the appearance of items with a Mask are given a more detailed description. This is odd since this is a game in which the first thing a player might do is investigate their old lives, and as ST I currently have no idea how that would actually play out.

    True (Fae) Lies

    The book contradicts itself several times over the 'truthiness' of True Fae. I'd like to drop a few examples. I hope that's okay.

    The True Fae are liars. You know that now.
    The True Fae are bound by their word — even their lies are partial truths and unspoken information, rather than outright falsehoods. A Keeper might fail to point out a loophole in a contract, or abide only by the razor-sharp fine points of a deal, but to outright break a sworn promise is anathema to them. Many cannot even understand humanity’s ability to do so with ease.
    Promises are worth less than nothing without a contract to back them up. You know, because you heard so many and every one of them was a lie. “Of course you can go home, if you build one toy for each good little moppet on Earth. ‘Tis child’s play, for one with your skill!” “I’ll set you free as soon as the war is over, my dear, but I so desperately need you to bring all my dead soldiers back to life each morning.” “Be my lady-in-waiting for a hundred and one years, just that! and soon you’ll be on your way.” You worked your fingers to the bone, hunched over your workbench or running yourself ragged in the fields, day in and day out. All that, for a glimmer of hope — surely this time your Keeper would cleave to his word. He never did.
    Could someone clarify this since it seems so important? You could argue that some of the promises on that list were half truths, but the words 'lie' and 'liar' are fairly clear so I don't think I'm reading too much into this. It's my suspicion that Fae actually do understand lying perfectly well and use it whenever they can get away with it, but lying carries huge risks for Fae like losing Titles and Regalia tokens. As such they wouldn't lie to someone that knows this. I might be way off though.

    Thank you in advance for any answers or other discussion!
