Not only because it is implying that writes don't actually know what they're writing and intending to convey (and that others know better), but that it misses other possibilities.

            Were you familiar that the writer of Fahrenheit 451 was told, to his face during a lecture he was holding, that his book was about the dangers of government censorship, despite the man himself denying it? There's lots of people who will swear, up and down, that the story is about censorship, all evidence or claims to the contrary. Now, I'm not saying this is what's going on here, but there's enough similarities for me to be bothered by it.

            Inside Out, the movie, tapped into Joy, Sadness, Anger, Disgust and Fear as the five emotions because that's actually a real thing. Science has a very strong leaning that there are five (give or take one) universal emotions that all humans have. Is it any surprise that the Seasonals tap into these major emotions as well as any number of other things, including the Stage of Grief? There is a link between the Seasonal Emotions and the emotions of SoG, but its not necessarily cause-and-effect. There could be any number of reasons. I personally like using the Seasonal Court emotions by linking them to holidays - Halloween, Christmas, Easter and July 4th (US only). Each emotion relates to the themes of each holiday, and nothing more.

            If someone wants to link the four Seasonal emotions to the Stages of Grief, more power to them. If that's the type of thing they enjoy, more power to them. But suggesting that its really what the authors intended, despite any protest? That's very iffy.

                        There is a big difference between "this is a message that exists in the work despite not being intentionally put there" and "this is what the authors really intended." Intent implies conscious will anyway, so saying a message was included unconsciously specifically does not mean it's the author's "real" intent.
                        Unfortunately, I've seen too many people insisting that interpreting the Seasonals as the Stages is the One True Way and the Only Right Way that Changeling 1e should be read as. The difference is there, and it's big like you say, but not everyone bothers to (or is able to) spot it.

                        Taking the Spring Court for example. Desire has many causes and expressions, of which Denial is part of. Denial, too, has many causes and expressions, of which Desire is part of. The two overlap in a Venn diagram. 1e Spring Court was placed inside or at least overlapping with that middle ground. But which circle should it lean to? My vote goes to Desire, because intense passions in general fit in with almost any story. The Stages of Grief work nicely for, well, grief and dealing with it, but that's a narrower narrative niche than "emotions."

Some people, often victims of real life trauma, complained that the Changeling game offered the stages of grief minus actual recovery as their society with the implication that there is no getting over tragedy or turning your life around.
