    Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
    The beast is breaking the social convention and social expectation. So personally I think instead of "He wants me to suffer. It's the unspoken rule. Do what I want and you might make it out. Entertain me and you may live. Dance for me, little monkey. So fuck that. I'm not your pet. Doppel-Ralston becomes stoic." it would be "He wants me to suffer. It's the unspoken rule. Do what I want and you might make it out. Entertain me and you may live. Dance for me, little monkey. So fuck that. I'm not your pet. Doppel-Ralston starts to enjoy the pain rather than suffer through it."

    Beast is being a noble class person, and acting like a peasant. It's using the wrong fork up to 11.
    I think there is value is making Beasts more nuanced and diverse than that. IMO refusing to play by the rules by displaying defiant stoicism is a Beast-like trait, and could be reflected in big, powerful animals like elephants, rhinos, whales and bears.

    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
    So, beasts are all about the rebel culture. Okay, guess that explains why it's all about the predator terminology (Because everyone else is sheeple, WAKE UP). Oh well, that's rather disappointing. So there's not really a seeming that references base human instincts.
    Seemings are about agency. Every Seeming makes a choice when they escape. The Fairest and the Darkling sacrifice (the Fairest themselves, the Darkling someone else). The Elemental lets go of their sense of self. The Ogre lashes out. The Wizened outsmarts. The Beast defies/subverts.

    "Base human instinct" is not really a choice, it's an impulse. There's little agency involved in that. You could spin it as an Elemental-like surrendering to their primal self, and perhaps become some sort of nature elemental, that's about as close as you're going to get to what you want.

    My homebrew hub.

                I'd say "subversion." The Beast subverts a rule that they are expected to obey. This subversion is what stuns/distracts/outrages their Keeper and allows them to escape. It's a calculated move meant to startle and wrongfoot the Gentry, and that's why the Beast regains Clarity for doing something socially unacceptable. That moment of stunned disbelief you have when you see a woman eating with her hands instead of knife and fork is what restores the Beast's Clarity, because the Keeper experienced a similar stunned reaction and that's what allowed the Beast to escape.

                                            Hey, characters don't have to be perfect.
