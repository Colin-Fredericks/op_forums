    Inspiration for Lost Boys and Lost Girls

    07-28-2021, 02:55 AM
    In a previous dialogue, I had shared my hope that changeling 2e will continue with the theme it introduced with Summertime Sam; changelings who are trapped in eternal childhood. On youtube, I found some videos I think can be a good source of inspiration for developing chronicles that focus on these characters. The first ones I want to introduce are play throughs from Fran Brow.

    Set in 1944, the game tells the story of Fran, a ten-year-old girl struggling with mental illness after witnessing the violent murder of her parents. After being found alone in the woods, Fran is admitted to Oswald Asylum and separated from her black cat and only friend, Mr. Midnight. Under the care of psychiatrist Dr. Marcel Deern, Fran is administered pills. The pills cause vivid hallucinations of a phantasmagoric parallel universe, filling the Oswald Asylum with smears of blood, mysterious messages, grisly torture, human subject research in psychology and neurology, and otherworldly shadow beings. Driven to escape her imprisonment, find her cat and return home. Fran eventually finds her cat in the house of two evil conjoined twins. Fran eventually escapes them and comes across a bridge, from which she falls, after which Fran passes into an another world. Still searching for an explanation of her parents' murder, Fran finds herself transformed into a tree in a world called Ithersta, a land where vegetables, insects, pine cones, and roots live in harmony. After departing Ithersta, she encounters a giant skeletal creature named Itward. Fran finds out that it was Itward who helped her in her search for her cat. Itward then tries to take Fran back home in his flying machine.
    Throughout the game, Fran and other characters grapple with psychological trauma, survive the abuse of parents and doctors, and learn what it means to live amongst other humans and spirits.

      Another series that can be a source of inspiration is I Hate Fairyland! I Hate Fairyland is a black comedy that follows Gertrude, a woman who was transported to a mystical world called Fairyland as a child. Thirty years later, Gertrude is now an un-aging, violent misanthrope who, alongside her reluctant guide and friend Larry, constantly tries and fails to return to the real world.
      The plot starts with Gertrude falling into Fairyland as a ten-year-old. She spends 27 years looking for the key to escape with her insect sidekick Larry, but never ages, resulting in her becoming a violent sociopath…..

                      If you've ever seen Jeff Lemire's Black Hammer comics, one of the characters is Golden Gail, a Golden Age/1940s superhero based on the original Captain Marvel. While she has aged normally, her superpowered form is perpetually 9 years old. Because the characters are trapped in a mysterious small town limbo and she's stuck in her child form, this causes a lot of issues and bitterness for her.

                      Also the 2009 film Orphan, to a degree, and certain parts of Interview with the Vampire.

                                  In a Changeling chronicle that included a Lost Boy/Lost Girl or even a motley of Lost Boys I think it would be interesting to explore how they survive. Because of child labor laws, they would have difficulty finding a source of income. Plus, people will eventually notice that they do not age. A series that could be a great source of inspiration is an old Japanese manga titled Chairudo. The series is about a race of beings with the appearance of children, but possess superpowers like mutants from Marvel's X-Men series.
