        I think some things need to be clarified about my approach and reasoning.

        Broad strokes first.

        1)I don't feel beholden to obey the standards of the first edition.

        Okay, that's not true. I always begin each seeming with what was said in the first version. I work from that groundwork. Of course I do-I love the old material to no end.

        That said, keeping strictly to the old is not useful. It helps to think outside of the box and reinvent the wheel sometimes, and sticking to the old material like it was scripture does not allow the series to grow and evolve. I look to reinvent and reinterpret to breathe new life into the framework of changeling. I'm swapping the old lens with new ones and seeing if I get a brighter color. I don't want to settle.

        2)This is immensely helpful for the new take on Seemings and Kiths. In the first version, Seeming and Kith were what happened to you. It may have traces of you in it-they can't make something from nothing, it has to be in you, but by and large you are crafted to serve Their purpose. Thus, certain things make sense within that context.

        But Huntsmen has clarified it's position on Seemings, and it's the exact opposite score. Kiths are inflicted on you by other, but your Seeming is self-inflicted, for better or for worse. This is why I think of Seemings as kinds of actions, particularly the further along I get in the writing process. I want to experiment more with that idea because it seems functional and useful.

        That's deviating. The point is that we can't keep thinking about the Seemings in the way we were taught, because those answers don't apply. The old should be incorporated and inform the new direction, sure, yeah, but it shouldn't drive it. And if you want proof that that works, allow me to refer you to Blood and Smoke(Which, by the way, is the only thing in Vampire that caused me to like Vampire, as context)

        3)This becomes really, really important when you remember the first half of the debate was kind of about whether or not Elementals and/or Beasts served any kind of purpose and had any thematic strength of their own. I know there was at least one nostalgia ridden fool who really didn't want to let go of them(that fool was me), while other people were sad at the idea of it going(that was everyone else), but there wasn't compelling arguments based simply on the old to keep them around. We've been told they are staying, and I imagine that's because Dymphna has some great ideas for them(Don't disappoint us, we're counting on you!), but without information, it seems folly to assume the old stuff is strong enough to keep going. So, we find a better, sharper angle to make a compelling argument for their standing.

            You say self-inflicted. Well... let me see if I can interpret that for more pain, which I say because I'd argue Agency has a cost, even if it is far preferable to being held by a Keeper.

            To go to the Elemental, who I insist is not solely defined by being part of nature, means to me making a conscious choice to give up part of your humanity in order to get free to your Environment.

            As I said, I don't view Elementals as purely natural, or even close, not when we had ones of Pollution, the Mankin(I admit, that was an odd one out to the point of spawning a new Seeming, but still), and in a way, ones made of Electricity I wouldn't necessarily call natural.

            Why does that matter? Well, to me, that's because one of the things holding Elementals back earlier in the discussion was that I think we were tying them too much to nature, when that wasn't inherently the case in 1.0, with all the exception Kiths for example, and for that matter, where in 1.0 it was suggested they came from in supplements, such as massive cities. Cities are hardly nature, and that was one of the big locations for Elementals to originate from.

            It also matters because I think this could define them far more in 2.0. Now, being an Elemental meant that you chose to do something with your Environment. You chose to try becoming more in tune with rocks, wind, glass, or what have you, and in the process, became partially like that. Alienation is still a theme, but now that Choice hangs over everything you do when you escape Arcadia. The same dilemma happens to every Seeming, which is that you chose to become something different from, and in some cases less than in some ways, human, in order to get free.

            Going off that last one, Fairest chose to become like their Keepers to escape them. Beasts chose to embrace animalistic parts of themselves to escape. Darklings chose to be willing to use fear, and anything else needed really, to escape. Ogres chose to become violent, to the point where that violence now defines who they are on some level. Even Wizened had to choose to embrace a craft to the point where it changed who they were in ways that are irreversible.

            And painful change is what Changeling the Lost is all about, whether its inflicted by someone else upon you, or you inflict it on yourself.

            Thoughts? Trying to figure out a different way to approach 2.0, if you will.

                    I suppose my thing is that I'm a little more divorced from the entire "Sacrifice Humanity" spliel. There's a few reasons for that.

                    Not really a substantial reason on it's own) I don't feel like going pro- or -anti-transhumanism with this sort of stuff.

                    1) On a physical=spiritual level, I've never understood nor brooked with the entire changing of physicality makes you more or less human. I'm thinking of Shadowrun here, for the record, where you literally lose your soul each time you got cybernetic implants(that translated into a diminished ability to do magic or whatever). I think the mentality of a person who would gladly swap their body for cybernetics is more likely to diminish humanity, but that's because the person is already setting a stage, which they don't nessecarily need to act on. I believe in humanity being stripped from you, and I believed in the entire "Live like a tree for so long and you lose humanity" thing the Fae did, but when you accept something to yourself, I don't see that as voluntarily giving up your humanity.

                    I really don't believe that in the context of the sort of moment Seemings seem to exist in either. I mean, granted, Seemings are probably actually things that take time to be grafted on-if anything, I will freely admit I probably have been presenting this as a snap change where it's more likely at least months worth of slowly acclimating, which is where the inhumanity comes in. But that said, I have trouble with seeing that as a deliberate slicing off of humanity.

                    2)In fact, I have some problem with the words "Sacrifice Humanity" to begin with, namely because I find myself asking a question:

                    What, exactly, does that mean?

                    Like, I get it on the prolonged exposure front. It takes time to become the Sand Dune that is also the Guy You Are Screwing Over. But let's put this in, well ,moments. Because I have trouble seeing it outside of the exposure front.

                    See, I can see shooting a guy as sacrifice humanity. I can see breaking a Keeper's leg while he's crying and begging you to stop as sacrificing humanity. I can see whispering dirty lies in the wrong circumstances for visitors as sacrificing humanity. I can see a lot of things done as sacrificing humanity.

                    But, like endurance, you just say "Sacrifice humanity" and I'm like "What does that mean?". Because by itself, it's almost meaningless.

                    And I'm not saying mentality doesn't play a part. I have been, at times, an inhuman fuck, and it's not because I did anything(although let's not pretend there weren't times), but simply because I framed my mind to think...well, to diminish people by huge scores. But you have to do something, and you have to suggest the nature of it.

                    Let's get a little frank here.Doing something to assert your agency, particularly in the way you'd need to escape Arcadia, runs the risk of psychopathic behavior, if not solipsistic. You are literally telling the world-a far more literal breathing, thinking world with purpose-that you come first. You are more important. What you want is more important than anything else there. That what you say goes. You have to! You need to be bigger than the world in order to get out! You need to take the world's lunch money! Or at least make it think you will.

                    But doesn't that mean everyone who does that risks sacrificing humanity? Particularly if there are others you aren't saving?

                    That's an extremely pessmistic view on that last note, but at the same time-you do have to admit you're making choices, and that does mean you elevate some people's worth over others, and it comes down to you asserting your priorities over everyone else. There may be nothing you can do about it the choices you have, but it's still a choice.

                    God damn, that's really pessimistic.

                    Anyways, the point of the matter is that you can't just tell me "Sacrifice humanity for power/agency"-or, rather, you can, but it doesn't satisfy me. I need to know what you did. I need it to suggest what sort of person and decisions make an Elemental, and "Sacrifice humanity" doesn't mean anything to me in that context.

                    Of course, all this needs to be framed in with the following in mind.

                    3)Of course "sacrifice humanity" is part of it.

                    I suppose I need to clarify that this is like endurance. How?

                    In that I'm not saying this isn't important or this isn't a thing.

                    Because it is. It's an important thing. This is totally a thing that is part of Elementals. It's there for a reason.

                    I don't have a problem with sacrificing humanity. I don't have a problem with endurance.

                    My problem is that those things, by themselves, only worked in the first edition. In the new one, it needs context. It cannot stand by itself, or at least I think so. I think they are natural consequences of the things that make Elementals, but that said, they are consequences. They are effects in need of a cause, branches in search of a tree trunk.

                    And of course, you may disagree with me. I felt like looking at those,I was looking inside the box. I moved out, I read everything the old books had on Elementals, I looked at what they could do, I looked at the in-depths discussions, and I reworked the material to find something that felt like a root answer from the new direction that fulfilled what was said. And again, feel free to disagree.

                    I think the idea of someone who feels they can express their choice and persona through throwing everyone else off their game(Agency through chaos), who strives to do so by being intimately in control of their surroundings and uniting with other elements/aspects(Power from unity and control/The Ones Who Change The Game) leads to someone who has the power to handle the odd twists, turns, and winds of the adversaries(Endurance) and someone who objectifies their world and understands it as their own game rig and what any element can be used for(Sacrificing humanity,specifically on the social level).

                    I can totally be wrong!

                        Perhaps the notion of “Element” needs some broadening beyond the classical elements. I was just thinking of Steve Jackson Games’ In Nomine, and particularly about its Ethereal Player’s Guide; in that book, an Element could be one of the Classical Elements (Earth, Air, Fire, or Water); but it could also be an Astronomical Concept (e.g., the Sun), an Emotion (e.g., Fear), a kind of Information (e.g., News), a type of Life (e.g., Beasts), a Sense (e.g., Light), an element of Society (e.g., Family), a type of Structure (e.g., a Home), a form of Struggle, a kind of Tech, Terrain, Wealth, or Weather.

                        In Changeling, I think that an Elemental needs to be very Kith-specific: to an Elemental, every Kith represents a concept, an idea around which the Kith is built — and the Elemental internalizes the Kith’s essential concept and starts identifying with what the Kith is more than with people: an Elemental Smith is going to be more comfortable around the things the Smith makes than around people; an Elemental Hunterheart identifies more with predatory animals than with people; an Elemental Muse identifies more with the notion of inspiring people than with the people that he tries to inspire; an Elemental Snowskin becomes a personification of ice and snow.

                        And, like a Chinese Finger Puzzle, by internalizing the core element of his Kith, the Elemental loosens the strictures that were placed on him, gaining more freedom to wander Arcadia on his own — and to leave. That paradox — gaining freedom by surrendering it — is the heart of the Elemental’s agency.

                        As for the distinction between Beasts and Elementals: Beasts are very visceral, driven by emotions and instincts; Elementals are very cerebral, driven by concepts and abstractions.

                        …which gets me thinking: maybe the Seemings exist in opposing pairs? Beasts and Elementals; Darklings and Fairest; Ogres and Wizened. Or not; I’m just spitballing here.

                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                    --Seeming as Agency--

                                    We can really break this down into three possible options. What, How, and Why.

                                    What is the simplest, it is the act itself. While it strikes me as a bit limiting, thematically, it's also surprisingly flexible as far as grey areas go. Say you seduce someone and stab them in the back when their guard is down. Would this make you a Fairest or a Darkling? You could make convincing arguments for each, really.

                                    How is a little more open to interpretation. Rather than limit specific actions to specific Seemings, it places the focus on performance and context. This is my guess as to what the final product will most likely be. It's just defined enough to be limiting and just broad enough to be accommodating to players' concepts.

                                    Why focuses on the motivations of the characters. It forgoes the actions themselves and looks to the source of the characters' agency. These are the least defined, sticking to very general emotions and concepts. Take my previous example, with actions driven by Fear, Beauty, Anger, etc. I generally view these as less of a slow build and more of a spur of the moment revelation or surge of action. They're the thing in you that kicks off the "Enough is Enough" moment.

                                    --Six Seemings--

                                    Beasts (and Elementals, who we'll get to later) have the problem of coming from a catchall seeming. In 1.0 they were mostly defined as a collection of "Beast Kiths" and even with the new rules, it would be easy for someone to just group all the beastial Kiths together for the sake of organization. So far, the major thing I'm seeing people try to carry over is the focus on Instinct. I would argue that's a solid enough concept for a foundation and in keeping with the spirit of 1.0's Beasts without focusing too much on a certain type of Beast-ness. (IE - Loyalty leaves out the nasty buggers, Ferociousness leaves out the bunnies or w/e.) The other issue I feel Beasts (and again Elementals) have is the 1.0-style physical manifestations of their seemings. With 2.0's seemings being a little more subtle and not something, as David mentioned, people will wear on their sleeves, it seems a bit odd for someone to roll around with big ol' lobster claws for hands. Granted, it could be assumed that maybe their durance was spent as some sort of lobster-carpenter or something, but I think it would probably work a bit better if the Beast seeming worked a bit more subtley than a simple kith. IE - Animal Eyes, Feral Presence, the nondescript claws/pointy teeth that like every changeling piece of art has, etc.

                                    Darklings are a simple enough concept. Sneaky, creepy, crawly, slimy, and a bunch of other not so fun things. Their most common themes are things like Fear, Moral Ambiguity, and Stealth. I see their escapes as being defined by at least one of those three things, fairly easily as it is. As for manifestation, "Dark and Shadowy" seems to be popular. To be honest, Darklings feel pretty cut and dry.

                                    Fairest are a teensy bit tougher. There seems to be a tendency to jump straight to sexy and gorgeous. Aesthetics are certainly a major part of them, and I think that should reflect in the manifestation of their seeming without a doubt, but I think its a bit more complex when you try to link it to agency. The first thing everyone jumps to is "Seduced the Guard," which I think fits, but there's definitely more to Fairest than just bonkfests. Besides aesthetics and the appreciation thereof, other major themes with Fairest seem to be artistry, majesty, brilliance, and just about anything else you could think of as beautiful. On the darker side of things you see stuff like social manipulation, addiction/desire, borderline sociopathy, and more social manipulation.

                                    Ogres, like Darklings, are pretty cut and dry. Anger, aggression, and violence. I think they might be the one seeming that nobody has any real arguments about, which can be a good or bad thing depending on how you look at it.

                                    Wizened are about the only ones I can see premeditating their escapes. If any seeming could be a slow-burn its them. Their time in Arcadia has granted them a certain set of skills... skills that make them a nightmare fo- Anyway, they seem to be defined by their crafts, their intellect, and their skill with magic. Though all changelings are good with magic, Wizened are definitely a bit more Gandalf about it. I think with the breakdown of the 1.0 Seeming/Kith system, the focus on crafts will have to broaden a bit. They might need to start bringing in some of the stranger changeling jobs, like living torch (or lobster-carpenter!) As far as how their seeming manifests itself? I honestly have no idea.

                                    And, like a Chinese Finger Puzzle, by internalizing the core element of his Kith, the Elemental loosens the strictures that were placed on him, gaining more freedom to wander Arcadia on his own — and to leave. That paradox — gaining freedom by surrendering it — is the heart of the Elemental’s agency.

                                    As for the distinction between Beasts and Elementals: Beasts are very visceral, driven by emotions and instincts; Elementals are very cerebral, driven by concepts and abstractions.

                                    …which gets me thinking: maybe the Seemings exist in opposing pairs? Beasts and Elementals; Darklings and Fairest; Ogres and Wizened. Or not; I’m just spitballing here.
                                    Thiiis. People forget the other definition of Elemental is "basic, fundamental, or elementary." With the split of Kiths and Seemings, Elementals become less about earth, wind, and fire, and more about distillation of concept (IE - Kiths).

                                    Also, I absolutely think the pairs thing works, except I would pair off Fairest and Wizened because they're basically Art vs. Craft, Heart vs Mind. Darklings and Ogres then become avoidance vs confrontation or direct vs indirect.
