    Originally posted by Khanwulf View Post
    So in Arcadia items are either ephemeral, real (and thus intelligent) or Hedge-spun, right? Am I missing a category? I mean, what about the clothing your Changeling slaves wears? Do all escapees show up either naked or with Hedge-spun stuff on?
    Either is a distinct possibility, but it's worth remembering that "intelligent" doesn't necessarily translate to "active" outside the context of fae magic —a Wizened tailor might extract whispered promises from their handiwork, but it's just as possible for a changeling to escape with stolen kit from a new arrival or piece together a rough outfit in the Hedge on their way back to Earth.

    The thematic connection clothing and identity have and the fact that the Fae regularly have the ability to make their servants immune to things one might need particular clothes for (or force them through those things regardless) generally make an escape from the Durance rough and/or some level of supernatural theft/kidnapping — your own garments may have been shredded long ago by the Thorns or your Keeper's household, but if you escape with nothing more than the clothes on your back then they either were magical or belonged to somebody (or both).

    Other personal possessions fill the same niche, but in general there's a reason the Gentry still largely stuck to Arcadia even in 1e.

                        Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post
                        So, I'm scouring all the 2E books I can grasp at for anything that looks like an Ephemeral Entity rules for reasons. How do Hedge Ghosts work now again? Or more to the point, are they divergent enough from the standard Ephemeral Entity rules enough that it wouldn't be wise to benchmark them for homebrewing a different set of ephemeral entities?
                        They're similar and different.

                        At their foundation they do use the Ephemeral Entity rules. Differences (at least from the KS manuscript, and assuming the Proofing process since then hasn't drastically changed them) include:
                        • Having a Wyrd rating instead of Rank (so same Trait point allocation).
                        • Glamour instead of Essence
                        • Can enter and influence a target's dreams for the purposes of gaining Glamour.
                        • Frailties instead of bans and banes (amount determined by Wyrd).
                        • Common abilities including: being able to navigate through the Thorns in the Hedge, being able to Reach into the mortal world while on the other side of a Hedge gate, can sense Glamour, and the ability to know where the closest Goblin Market is.

                                    What happens if a changeling invokes Contracts of Fang & Talon (Insects) in the middle of winter? Would the clause fail automatically because there are no living insects nearby to answer the call? Would dormant insects wake themselves up and crawl out of their hidey-holes, only to die of exposure within seconds? Or would the magic of the contract protect the insects from the cold until they fulfill their obligations to the Wyrd?

                                    The last option seems the most fair to the player, but it opens up avenues for abuse and manipulation. If the Wyrd protects the player's insect allies from cold until they fulfill their obligations, then why wouldn't the Wyrd also protect them from the heat and smoke of a fire, or a cloud of pesticide? And that doesn't even get into magically-created cold: what happens if that changeling summons their insect allies but then someone else invokes "Riding the Devil's Jawbone" to freeze the whole area? What if the changeling calls for his insect-allies' aid from inside a meat locker or a walk-in freezer?
