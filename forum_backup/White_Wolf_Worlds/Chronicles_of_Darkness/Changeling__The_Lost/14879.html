    Making Fae Lords, Titles, Tells, Hints, etc.

    11-20-2013, 05:22 PM
    Hi,

    I'm brainstorming ideas and I want to make a Fae Lord as an antagonist for a group of PCs. The game has the PCs as Mage Archmasters sending 'lesser' supernatural agents in to achieve any random variety if tasks to fulfill their Omens. The Archmasters are more a framing device to allow for disparate plots, crossovers and shenanigans to make 'sense'.

    So, a True Fae is likely to be an enemy they will face.

    But I was wondering if anyone had made a true fae and had suggestions, hints, experiences etc. with them and would be willing to share. I have many questions and probably more will come as I think of them but for now...

    1. how many titles should a true fae have?
    2. how many can they have?
    3. example titles?
    4. what makes good tells?
    5. how to drop hints about them?
    6. how to drop hints about weaknesses? (the whole issue of the ST thinking they've made it obvious but the players missing it entirely)
    7. what is a good number of titles to give them?
    8. can actors/props/wisps be affected by mind control/reading/manipulation powers? How about body/material/etc. alteration powers?
    9. where do titles come from?
    10. are new ones created? if so how?
    11. are there instead a fixed number number of them being fought over and captured and traded etc.?
    12. if there are a fixed number how many? ballpark?
    13. how many true fae are there? ballpark?


    Aside from questions, making pacts, deals, contracts, etc. with players...sounds really difficult...if I am talking as a true fae and want to offer a deal or suggest a different pact that has loopholes and such for the fae lord's favour...how do I do that? I don't know how to make watertight deals. Any hints of suggestions on how to get around this?

      Originally posted by Scutarii View Post
      1. how many titles should a true fae have?
      Game of Immortals weighs the starting counts toward two and four, but can conceivably run the gamut from one to five.

      2. how many can they have?
      Five before they hit Wyrd Ascension by the default count, though that number is subject to individual-game jiggering.

      9. where do titles come from?
      Description of Fae by other Fae.
      10. are new ones created? if so how?
      The consumption of a Fae's name by another Fae transforms it into a Title which a third Fae describes and consequently gets the right to fight its holder for.

      13. how many true fae are there? ballpark?
      Literally numberless.

      Aside from questions, making pacts, deals, contracts, etc. with players...sounds really difficult...if I am talking as a true fae and want to offer a deal or suggest a different pact that has loopholes and such for the fae lord's favour...how do I do that? I don't know how to make watertight deals. Any hints of suggestions on how to get around this?
      Having come up with at least two chronicle ideas whose ultimate inciting incidents amounted to "a couple of Fae had a bet going," stick to broad outlines and important rules and add in details as investigation requires.

              Example titles: The Shape in the Dark, the King of the Cracked Mirror, The Abyssal Hunger, The Caress That Brings Ruination, The Clavinger, The Argent Flame ... I prefer my titles abstract, Note these are not necessarily what the True Fae goes by day to day.

              For fun, try to create each in your head as an Actor, Prop and a Realm.

              When it comes to weaknesses, I prefer to link them intimately to the True Fae's MO somehow. The Trua Fae that often appears as a preacher? Hates church bells. The one that comes to you in mirrors? Broken mirror shards are its Bane. The one that tries to make you its perfect lover? Genuine affection hurts it and the tears of the heartbroken burn its skin.

                  I created three true fae which I posted up on my blog – titles and information on those titles included. Red Queen, Jack Frost and the Snow Princess. The Red Queen and Snow Princess both have an Actor, Prop, Wisps and an Arcadian Realm while Jack Frost has the first three but lacks an Arcadian Realm. I did this because his core concept was based around the idea of being a landless Peter Pan / Doctor Who type figure who took kids away on adventures into other people’s realms. If he gained more titles, he would simply gain more Props rather than bother with a Locale.

                  A True Fae can have a huge amount of titles, though I like to imagine that the average one had between 3 – 4. I don’t know if canon says any different, however. 3 – 4 seems good to me.

                  Since Actors / Wisps have resistance stats, I’d have them roll against mind control. It’s not that a character would be mind controlling them, per se, but it’d be part of the ‘deal’ and the ‘laws’ that the Actors / Wisps are submitting to which would allow them to be mentally coerced. They’d know the whole time, however, which means they could subvert it in interesting ways while obeying the letter of the law of the powers.

                  I imagine there are tens of thousands of titles and that each one is a separate part of some larger ‘entity’. Changelings don’t discover titles when they hit Wyrd 10. They’re summoned by the Arcadian mass of abandoned titles and then penetrated and possessed by one, becoming its tool, as without a human mind – even a badly torn apart one – a Fae would lack even its own rudimentary creativity and ability to follow or comprehend stories. But that’s my personal opinion.

                  My horror roleplaying blog with a strong focus on World of Darkness and Call of Cthulhu can be found here: http://stwildonroleplaying.blogspot.com.au/

                                  My own headcanon is that if a changeling managed to get a Title, they would start the process of transforming into a True Fae almost immediately; their Clarity would nosedive, and their Wyrd would slowly rise, and they would cast aside their mortal bodies and take up their places as Lords of Arcadia.                                   Weirdly, the plan was successful, as my players never suspected him of anything until the fateful transformation occurred. They just thought they were going to kill a Gentry...)

                                  My headcanon also doesn't have changelings automatically go Gentry at Wyrd 10, but if it did, I would assume that their mortality and nature, as it boils away, transforms into their first Title - a short description of the person that they once were, which they now wear like a fine suit over a raging maelstrom of Wyrd.

                                                          To be fair, very few changelings will ever reach Wyrd 10, it's preventable by lowering your Wyrd back down to 9 and going True Fae isn't the same as becoming a a deathlord, etc. It's not gained through age or knowledge or revelation. You've just strengthened your intimate connexion to a realm of madness too strongly.

                                                          Your point about abuser becoming abused and fecundity are valid, though. That said, I don't think True Fae intend for this. They're not trying to reproduce like a parasitic wasp. In a sense, the changeling has a choice, though - Wyrd, under normal rules, only rises with XP. You choose to raise it. At high Wyrd you're very inhuman and laden with frailties. You choose to keep going. You choose, without quite realising it, to become True fae, and that's the horror. The Wyrd never cares if you understood the choice, only that you made it.
