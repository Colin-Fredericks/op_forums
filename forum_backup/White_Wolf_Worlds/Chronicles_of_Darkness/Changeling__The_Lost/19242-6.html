                                                            These have been used before, but I couldn't help but put out my own interpretations. They're not entirely subtle though.


                                                            Your family has been murdered by something you cannot understand, and one being takes the credit. She tells you that you can get them back, indeed, that you can leave happy, if you play a game with her to stave off boredom. Simply explain to her how everyone died in a way that denies magic, and she'll even run you through how they could have died at least. However, she isn't showing you exactly what happened. After all this is just how they could have died. Everything in the gameboard she provides is magic - your grandfather was a magician, his elderly cook is possessed by your opponent's teacher, his servants are all magic themselves, and every hour sacrifices are made. Worse, even if you manage to get through one game with plausible deniability of magic, it isn't enough to end the games, and each gameboard is different. The pieces are the same, but the deaths are different and in different orders. Sometimes victims live when they otherwise died, and logic just doesn't work when your opponent is able to set down truths about what happened outside of the magic.

                                                            The only way to win is to understand how the other side works...and even if you do, by that point you're practically ready to start hosting your own games. Would you even want to leave once you've discovered how the other side feels with these endless games?


                                                            -------------------------------------------------------------

                                                            He is a man. An impossible man, charming, intelligent, able to go anywhere within his realm and defeat the monsters within. Out of everyone within, he has chosen you to come along, his companion, to help him with his adventures, be rescued by him and ask him questions he will solve. He can pull anything out of his pockets, has a trunk that is massive within and inconspicuous without, and with it he can take you anywhere; he simply can't stop traveling to new places. Even when he would be defeated, he gets back onto his feet, remaking himself in all ways to be better than before. You and he are eternally young as you travel together, in this marvelous realm with this marvelous man.

                                                            ...only, people seem to die where-ever he goes. Not you, never you, and the places you visit are always populated, but people have died in your arms. Sometimes you're changed. He makes sure you're changed back, perhaps with a few benefits left, but it's clear that there is danger. There are these strange rules, too, rules you need to remember if you don't want to annoy him and help him. Rules about time and this and that. He never explains things either...and when he gets angry, there are horrible consequences. It's rare the monsters don't make him angry. It should scare you when he is, but the joy of being rescued puts end to that...usually.

                                                            You've also found clues, in his trunk, about him having other companions with him. He never talks about them, never tells you about them when asked. You don't even know his name, just a title that acts as one. You're sure things will never end...but you have to wonder, what will happen when they do? Will you be back at home, which he assures you is still there, even when you both never seem to end up in the right place when you want to visit it? Who are his people, the ones he says he ran away from? And why is he so scared of them, with their courts and politics, when he is so much better and freer than them?

                                                            Are you entirely safe with this man?
