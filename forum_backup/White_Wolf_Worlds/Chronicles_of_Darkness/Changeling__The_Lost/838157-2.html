                                            Honestly, I don't think a cohesive cosmology could do anything but hurt CoD. Contrary to some people who have posted here, I think that Mages being broadly and catastrophically wrong about the nature of reality pretty much demolishes the themes of their gameline. At the same time, their being right about everything they believe would do the same to a lot of other gamelines. The best solution, in my mind, is that every splat is right; it's actually the everyday mortals who are wrong.

                                            Mortals being wrong is nothing new in the CoD, of course. Most of them don't believe in the supernatural at all, or else only believe in a fairly specific collection of supernatural things that are part of their religious and cultural upbringing. But if mortals are wrong about Vampires and Werewolves and Fairies and a hundred other things, why should we be surprised that some of their basic assumptions about the universe are wrong as well?

                                            Mortals, on the whole, expect the universe to make sense. They believe that there are objective truths about the universe that can be observed, quantified, and understood by the human mind, even if we haven't uncovered them all yet. To me, that seems like exactly the kind of expectation that the world of the CoD likes to defy. I think that perhaps the most basic, overarching theme of the CoD is that the world is stranger than we know, and that doesn't just mean that there are monsters hiding in every dark corner. It means that basic deductive logic sometimes breaks down, that mutually exclusive things can be simultaneously true, and that the universe laughs in the face of your attempts to chart its structure. And honestly, that's something I really like about the setting.
