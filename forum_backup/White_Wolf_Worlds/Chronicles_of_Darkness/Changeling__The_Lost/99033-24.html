                    the short version of my post is "Taidragon is right about everything."

                    Ace's Player: When Ace's player joined, they regularly played on that night, but some difficulties with job situation and their recently being a parent made it so they missed sessions frequently. This meant he made about as often as he missed. When his job situation shifted, Sunday became the only day he could really spend with his family. He is currently playing in Night Train Detective Agency for Demon which is a very late game since he does not have to work on Sunday, but that is his only game at the moment. We are all in our 30s or 40s so this is just a reality of play. Malibu Dream House is actually not in my own game room but two players' house as they have a child and this lets both them play rather than one staying home and missing.

                    Journals: I give 1XP for doing journals. if the whole group does them (who attended the session) an extra 1XP goes to the group fund. Groups normally gain 1 Group XP for the fund they can spend per session, on stuff like Hollow (and all the sub-areas) Bastian (and all the sub-areas) Harvest, Resources (for the house) etc, but nothing for a personal PC. Malibu Dream House is the only game I run which regularly has journals from everyone. I mentioned Night Train above, with normally around 2 journals per session of 5 regularly making players (though one of those journals, Speed Demon's, is written as a noir detective fiction covering all the events from the game with or without Speed, and my god it is good). Golden Eagle has 3-4 journals of 7 players. This is largely due to, as Taidragon noted, busyness or creative roadblocks. A lot of us have kids. Some have jobs where working on journals at work is just fine, others have jobs where that is impossible, dangerous, or could get them in real trouble. Some just see them as homework. Some of us are in several games and have to pick and choose which ones to journal for (There are nine games being run in our extended group right now, I am running four, one person is running three. I am not even running all the CoD games, there is another Changeling game out there).

                    I prefer 3 journals to a game. That tends to cover everything going on. Night Train I am less worried about (as noted, Speed covers everything and her journals are frequently around 20 typed pages by themselves.) but outside that 3 tends to work. More is unusual, but I have been very fortunate with Malibu Dream House, and 4 is not unusual for Golden Eagle.

                    Does that answer everything? Your questions and comments are of course appreciated.

                                                All is not well in the Dream House. The Darklings still are not on speaking terms and jealousy is beginning to rear it’s head elsewhere. While the Motley focuses inward, things continue to broil from without.

                                                Session LIV

                                                Gawain’s Memories

                                                November 9th, evening

                                                Man. If anyone took a look at my hands, they’d think I was a very clumsy... paper shuffler? I dunno, that metaphor got away from me, but I am getting a collection of paper cut looking wounds. The Contract of Potential, you know, the Martyr’s Will clause, has come in handy these past bit. Not, like, essential, but I like to think it’s helped keep spirits going strong and all. Even so, I don’t have to shed blood, it’s not like I’m Glamour starved, but it makes it feel more real, you know?

                                                But I’m going all over the place, as usual. The show last night went well, for the most part. The stage part definitely. Lori was just, you know, amazing. I think she was showing off for Annika. Insincere Marguerite’s debut of ‘Je ne regrette rein’ was awesome too. I think we all gave her a little boost, but I'm sure she'd have hit it out of the park anyway. Calamity didn’t get to see it and was a little bummed, but she was worried about stressing Insincere Marguerite out, you know? She and Annika stepped out for cigars.

                                                I guess I’ve mentioned it a few times now, but Annika was there, obviously, which was a little weird, since she’s usually only by when she is performing or teaching Calamity. But she’d heard the night before that, you know, Calamity wasn’t doing so well, so she came by to try and keep her spirits up. Becky stuck around too and even stayed the night.

                                                It was nice having backup since Calamity really wasn’t doing super still. Especially not after Moon came by with the news. I was happy to see hir - well, at first I was nervous to, since we hadn’t precisely left on, you know, the best terms last time. Ze seemed a little nervous for hir part too, but we were cool when ze left. Anyway, hir news was that Gunnar - who’d been out of contact for a month apparently - showed up at the Lighthouse and insisted that he should be there for Chalcedony’s Dreamhealing. He kept insisting even after being told no in no uncertain terms and then Molly popped out from being invisible with a gun to his head.

                                                So Gunnar shrank to about 2 feet tall and Molly shot, cause that counts of moving I guess, and then they had a whole big fight. Eventually they both turned invisible and left. Well, they probably left since Queen Charlotte came out a little later and demanded they show themselves and they, you know, didn’t. They’d been looking into it, the Autumn Court folks - Boss Lowjack just said Molly was doing her part of the Winter Court’s duties, and Lucien claimed Gunnar hadn’t been out of contact, but working for him and wouldn’t say how. So, that was great.

                                                Well, mostly it was a mess, but no one seemed to have gotten hurt, you know, yet. Still, Calamity did not take kindly to hearing that Molly had started a fight with an Autumn Courtier at the Lighthouse, or that Boss Lowjack was being cagey about it. But she fixed the glass she broke, and we managed to talk her down, the three of us. I do think it left Calamity feeling kinda, you know, fragile. She let Jacob make the rounds anyway.

                                                The next day, after the night of drinking Calamity’d had, and to celebrate Insincere Marguerite’s awesome performance, sex breakfast was blueberry pancakes and heavily loaded scrambled eggs with gouda, one of Insincere Marguerite’s favorites. It went over well, I think. All and all, everyone seemed at least a little on, you know, an evener keel. Insincere Marguerite even joined us, with Calamity there. She didn’t really, you know, engage with Calamity at all, of her own will, but she was there. De la Roche had gone with Lori to church and that seemed to have gone well also. De la Roche’s outfit certainly did. I’m not sure any of the ladies weren’t drooling over her suit and purple tie combo.

                                                We did talk some about the Day/Night crowns. Julia attempted to give an explanation for why the Monarchs feared such a thing so greatly. And they do have a great fear, that this freehold should go the way of LA, where the Seasons lost their power to the Diurnal cycle. And so the Monarchs here, in an effort to maintain their power where LA’s Seasons failed, speak of driving off or killing the Day and Night Courtiers that make this their home. It is as if they have no confidence that their subjects would follow the dogma presented to them by the Seasons, given their own truly free choice. It would seem to me that if the only way to maintain their power is to slaughter those who disagree, then perhaps they do not deserve to have any power...

                                                But. But. Focus. Julia had spoken with Queen Charlotte, who, to her credit, was interested very much in protecting Insincere Marguerite as the Dread Esquire. Which, you know, is not the biggest thing in, like, an overall view of morality, to only protect people you’re, you know, fond of, but given that it is Insincere Marguerite were talking about, I’m not gonna fuss. She’s been pushing the other Monarchs to agree to not go with the whole killing Diurnal Courtiers. Princess Joli feels kinda similar to Queen Charlotte, I guess, just mirrored, since she wants to protect Felicity and distrusts Insincere Marguerite. Boss Lowjack does wanna drive out the Diurnal folks, but with incentives, rather than violence, so, I guess that’s better? And Viscount Augustine is ‘listening to the will of his people’, so I dunno what that might mean.

                                                Anyway, it hasn’t happened yet, so I guess we’ll see. Other, more immediate things got a little, you know, tense at the end of breakfast, when Calamity pushed Insincere Marguerite a little hard about fixing things. Julia was just about to explode. I can’t really blame her, really any of the hers involved, I guess, but hopefully Calamity can be a little patient, until Insincere Marguerite gets the distance she needs.

                                                Desiree tried to help with that, suggesting a trip for ice cream for those of us left behind when Insincere Marguerite and Julia went up to the roof and Lori went to help De la Roche get her shirt off. Gloria stayed behind at the House though, probably to hang out with De la Roche. And to avoid Desiree. Jacob and Calamity had a talk first, long enough that Becky and Desiree started making up one of those fanfics Calamity talks about sometimes, about the two of them. It was really, you know, weird? But it was kinda nice, right, to see Becky forget she didn’t like Desiree.

                                                Ice cream was great - the place Desiree suggested had Rocky Road, my favorite. The news we got there was not so great - Jacob got a call from Boss Lowjack just as a story came on the TV, that a weapons cache downtown had exploded, which was perhaps related to a car explosion outside a bar called Lowjack’s. Boss Lowjack told Jacob that things had, you know, escalated after the things with Molly and Gunnar. So, that was exciting. It’s not our problem though, not right now.

                                                What was more our problem was what Lori told us when we got back. Marie-Hélène had backed out of performing for a time, until she could feel comfortable in our domain again. I can scarcely blame the lady, given her continued lack of knowledge as to what was happening and why, seeing as the raw details of the matter were truly damning. I do not understand Lady Grace’s reluctance to reveal the truth to one we know to be a close friend of Chalcedony’s. I do understand that the prejudice against the Director’s Changelings would make it inadvisable to connect the two in the public eye. However, that detail is not necessary to resolve Lady Beaumont’s confusion, or the confusion of others. The rest however - I do not care what the Monarchs wish hidden...

                                                Still. I thought, you know, since we keep having problems with communication among, you know, us, that we should hash it out. We agreed that explaining that she had a magical effect lingering on her from her Keeper, to anyone who already knew where things stood from, you know, an outside perspective, was OK with everyone. It really does explain the actions taken, without letting on the anything that might imply anything along the lines of loyalist like the words ‘the Director’ do.

                                                I went to the forge after that, to try and work out the... tension, which doesn’t seem to have, you know, worked so great. Poor Calamity came looking for me after a while, frustrated that Insincere Marguerite wasn’t letting Julia yell at or hit her. Bottling up emotions usually isn’t, you know, the best idea, but I gotta trust that Insincere Marguerite and Julia know what’s best for them. I hope I was able to convince Calamity.

                                                We got interrupted before we could relax with a nice shower, me, Calamity and Becky, by De la Roche, who’d found out Gloria had been programmed by Hollywood. De la Roche had found some notes on us, and confirmed with Desiree - I guess even her, you know, handwriting changed when acting under the compulsion kinda thing. I used one of Calamity’s shards to spill a few drops of blood to focus our wills and we got down to it.

                                                As unfortunate as it was, it did kinda feel nice to have a problem we could do something productive about. I let Calamity take the lead, since she, you know, really needed the chance to do something good, and we rocked it. The brainwashing wasn’t as extensive as it was for Desiree - they didn’t have the Director’s scripts to crib from. She was just supposed to gather what she could, then leave it in a dead drop. We managed to convince her to ‘cut the strings’ and free herself. Now we’re just waiting for her to wake and probably to do some more explaining.

                                                Here’s hoping that things keep looking up.


                                                Lorelei’s Diary

                                                I don’t really know how to characterize the last few days; it’s been an emotional roller-coaster ride, with every heartbreak followed by a triumph, every gain sliding into a terrible loss. While I’m more hopeful that our motley will survive the current storm, it’s beginning to look like the debacle with Chalcedony is going to have far-reaching consequences, and the situation with Gloria has become more… “complicated” as well.

                                                So in any case we spent the rest of yesterday afternoon getting ready for the show, which was a welcome distraction from the fact that the next morning I was planning to introduce my father/preacher to the love of my life, who happens to be a punk Chicana girl with anger management issues. Add to that the fact that Calamity seemed intent on pushing Marguerite into a conversation before she was ready, and I think I was holding up remarkably well under the circumstances.

                                                So I got dolled up and headed backstage to make sure the rest of the performers were ready to bring their A-game (Saturday night is one of our biggest shows, after all). After I was reasonably certain that our talent was in-line, I wandered out to mingle a little among the crowd, as usual. I saw D standing off to one side, glancing again and again at a table where Gloria and Halley were sitting, and trying not to be obvious about it.

                                                “What’s up?” I asked her as I approached.

                                                “I wonder what’s going on between those two,” she explained with a hint of concern in her voice. “I didn’t think they even knew each other, but they’re chatting like old friends.”

                                                I could feel the evil smirk hit my face, but there was nothing I could do about it. “Why, D?” I asked sweetly, “Are you jealous?”

                                                “Of course not,” she snapped, not meeting my eyes.

                                                “You are! ” I taunted her quietly, “D, that’s so cute! ”

                                                She sighed and gave me a weary look. “Why are you doing this to me?” she asked plaintively, blushing.

                                                I leaned in and kissed her nose. “Because I like watching you turn all those pretty colors, babe,” I said with a laugh.

                                                She glared at me, but it was a glare of love. I can tell.

                                                In any case, the two of us made our way to their table, and discovered after a few moments of conversation that Halley was there on Monica’s behalf. Our green gal-pal had been planning to introduce Gloria around to a few people this evening, but had begged off at the last minute for “personal reasons,” and had asked Halley to make her apologies and do what she could for Gloria. Halley either didn’t know or didn’t want to say what was going on with Monica. I hope everything is okay. Maybe D and I should make some time to hang out with her in the next few days. I’ve missed our workouts, anyway.

                                                I had to cut the conversation short as curtain approached, and I made my way backstage. I performed a few of my old standbys last night, and tried out one new number as well: a smoky torch-style rendition of the Elvis Presley classic “I Can’t Help Falling In Love.” There wasn’t much staging; just me in a slinky evening gown lounging on a chair, but it seemed to go over well regardless. I’ll have to think about adding it in to the regular rotation.

                                                I admit that I put a lot of effort into my number tonight, and I’m not too proud to own up to the fact that part of that was because Annika was there checking up on Calamity. I don’t know why I feel the need to assert myself in her presence. Of course I find her superior attitude off-putting, and it’s no secret that I’m jealous of her skills as a dancer, but, well… she’s honestly never been anything but civil to me. She certainly hasn’t done anything to deserve the sort of hostility I can’t help feeling around her. I should probably work on that before it leads me to do something I’ll regret.

                                                And speaking of regret, Marguerite still regrets nothing, and her number went just as well last night as it had at her debut. I did what I could to help her out using contracts… mostly because I thought her funk over the situation with Calamity might adversely affect her performance. I think Gawain might have done something similar. I’m not sure if our magic helped her performance or if what we saw was just her natural talent shining through, but either way her act was spectacular.

                                                Things wound down pretty quickly after the show; most of the motley wasn’t really in an entertaining mood, and I think the crowd picked up on that. Oh, we weren’t rude; we didn’t chivvy people out the door as soon as the bows were taken or anything like that. It just seemed to me that our patrons didn’t hang around as much as they usually do, and cleanup didn’t take very long either. In light of what happened the next day, I’m starting to wonder if there was a reason the place cleared out quickly.

                                                We were hanging out at the bar, waiting for the last of the mortal patrons to slip away when Gawain walked up with some disturbing news. Moon had told him that there had been trouble at the Lighthouse earlier. Gunnar Friday showed up and demanded to know what they were doing with Chalcedony. When they wouldn’t allow him access to her, he became very agitated, and claimed that it was vital that he know what had been done to Chalcedony, and what the Autumn Court was doing about it. Eventually Molly Nuit materialized behind him holding a gun to his head. He reacted badly to this (understandably, in my opinion), and used a power to sort of reflexively shrink to about three feet tall. Molly fired over his head, and after a few shots were exchanged in both directions, they each disappeared into thin air. The Autumn Court is pretty sure they both fled the area after that, as Queen Charlotte issued a direct command for them to show themselves, and neither one did.

                                                So things are heating up in the Freehold over the Chalcedony thing. I really hope the Autumn Court can do something to help her, and that things can get back to normal soon. As the events of the next day proved, though, this is far from over. Lucien Torrez won’t just take an attack on one of his people lying down, and Boss Lowjack is unlikely to budge from his position either.

                                                D and I checked in on Marguerite and Julia, who had retired to their bedroom as soon as Marguerite’s lighting duties were complete. They seemed a bit better, but both were obviously still shaken by what happened on Friday. They even shared an anecdote that would have been amusing if it hadn’t been so sad. Apparently Calamity approached Julia earlier in an effort to apologize for the harm she had done to Marguerite. In an effort to prevent further scenes, Marguerite had ordered Julia not to speak to Calamity at all. As she was unwilling to go against this directive, Julia was forced to respond to Calamity’s contrition via pantomime.

                                                Sometimes I worry about the amount of control Marguerite has over Julia.

                                                In any case, Marguerite wasn’t quite up to our usual bedroom shenanigans, so D and I made our way back to our room, stripped down, and climbed into bed. I snuggled against my honey’s back, feeling her heartbeat and the warmth of her skin. That’s usually enough to put me in a place of deep contentment, so I rarely have trouble sleeping (once we’ve both settled down for the evening, of course).
