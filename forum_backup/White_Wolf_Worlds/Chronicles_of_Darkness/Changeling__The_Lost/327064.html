    Let's make antagonists of fantasy series into True Fae!

    12-01-2014, 11:57 AM
    As the title goes. A fun little thought experiment that came from me writing the details for an original fae and all his Titles, which then led to a discussion with a friend over antagonists and villains of well known series who could easily be reimagined into True Fae. I'm sure we weren't the only ones to have those thoughts, so come on in and share what you thought up if you want!

    Two examples me and my friend discussed more than once:

    The Lord Of Burning Wastes, Sargeras (Warcraft franchise)

    Name: Sargeras
    Actor: The Deceiver (Kil'Jaeden)
    Actor: The Defiler (Archimonde)
    Actor: The Destructor (Mannoroth)
    Actor: The Darkener (Tichondrius)
    Wisps: The Burning Legion (The myriad demons of the Burning Legion)
    Realm: The Home Of Warlocks (Argus, the homeworld of the Eredar)
    Prop: The Dark Seether (Gorribal, Sargeras's sword)

    The Dark Lord Of Middle-Earth, Sauron (Lord Of The Rings)

    Name: Sauron
    Actor: The Lord Of Gifts (as he appeared to the elves in the form of Annatar, Lord Of Gifts)
    Actor: The Necromancer (his physical body during the events of The Hobbit and the Lord Of The Rings trilogy)
    Realm: The Land Of Shadows (Mordor, of course)
    Wisps: The Dark Wraiths (The Nazgul)
    Prop: The One To Rule Them All (the One Ring)


    Sargeras was mostly brainspew with aforementioned friend, but I did start writing the sheets for Sauron, which I won't share at the time because of their current incompleteness.

                                      The Gentleman With Thistledown Hair, from Suzanna Clarke's "Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell", recently adapted into a TV series by the BBC. He is actually one of the main inspirations for the True Fae, and the book is cited in the recommendations section of the 1ed core book.

                                      True Name: Not mentioned in the book or TV series, as far as I know. Probably something very pretentious and rather convoluted, but still comprehensible.

                                      Actor: The Aristocratic Whispers. The entity we think of as the Gentleman.

                                      Realm: Lost Hope. A very opulent and impressive mansion with Gothic and Victorian aesthetics, but with a very ghostly weather surrounding and filling it. It's illusory residents and True Fae visitors are profoundly elitist in garment and demeanor. However, they have numerous small marks of decadence and ugliness, such as an odor of roses with a hint of rotting corpses, old bloodstains and animal-like visage. They often try to hide these imperfections with make-up, masks and other accessories, but only make themselves look even more fake and arrogant. Their imitations of noble pastimes and mannerisms are pale at best, and abhorrent at worst.

                                      1ed Seeming And Kith Blessings: Fairest + Lyrical Voice (True Fae), Perfect Protocol (Chatelaine) and Shadow Beauty (Illes).

                                      1ed Contracts: Vainglory, Separation, Smoke, Mirror, Omen, Darkness, Control The Elements, Communion With The Elements.

                                      1ed Goblin Contracts: Fool's Gold, Mirror Mirror, Delayed Harm, Diviner's Madness, Fair Entrance.

                                      Frailties: Must always act in an aristocratic manner, even when in the grip of strong emotions (Major Taboo), when he makes his presence known, must make at least one scornful remark about the features and choices of others that he deems improper or undesirable (Minor Taboo), must avoid direct physical confrontation, be it through guile, magic or stealthy kills (Minor Taboo).

                                      If you want to make him more capable of defending himself when cornered, you could give him dots in the Stone Contract, the Lethal Mien and Charmed Life Merits, and make his characteristic garments a Hedgespun Raiment with Armor.

                                      Also, he has the Manifest Mien Flaw, which makes his Lyrical Voice audible through the Mask.

                                                          I was reading through the Arkham Lost seeting hack, and I wondered how exactly the Great Old Ones and Outer Gods would work as True Fae. The later lose some of their scope by being reduced to True Fae, but the former can still work.

                                                          If I had to define the Outer Gods as a single Gentry, I'd say its Titles would be like this:

                                                          Yog-Sothoth: The Key And The Gate. Most frequently takes the form of a Realm, with pulsating cosmic energies crystallizing into alien matter and fading back into quantum threads of time and fate. When in Realm form, its portals lead to and connect many places on Earth, as well as dimensions like the Astral, Underworld and Shadow. It can also become a Wisp consisting of floating spheres that are neither flesh nor light. This Title acts as a patron of sorts to sorcerers and scientists who seek the ultimate secrets of the universe.

                                                          Azathoth: The Blind Idiot. In the Cthulhu Mythos, Azathoth is not conscious of all that it creates and destroys, and should it wake up, it is said all would cease to be. As a Title, it changes its manifestation with no apparent pattern. It usually appears as a gigantic Actor whose mere presence reshapes the Hedge around it for miles, making almost all of it pitch black and completely silent, save for the cacophonous piping and drumming. It is closer to a sleepwalker than mindless, occasionally lurching out of its throne and moving numbly through the Thorns, carrying the absolute dark and accursed music with it. Sometimes, its passing through the Thorns creates and destroys both trod and gate, sending hobgoblins out into the mortal world or trapping changelings inside the Hedge. It is said entire species of hobgoblins and Hedge towns are brought into existence and wiped out because of Azathoth.

                                                          Nyarlathotep: The Crawling Chaos. The human disguise it takes in the eponymous story is the Actor manifestation, and it is the one which interacts with any Loyalists, Huntsmen and other servants. It is a master of Contracts that let it create wondrous devices, scare and awe people and assume monstrous forms. If need be, it can also become a Wisp to act in many places at once, with each instance an unique nightmare.

                                                          The Necronomicon: The Forbidden Secrets Of Utmost Darkness. It is always manifested as a Prop in the form of a tome bound in human flesh and inked in blood. Many Tokens and other magic items have been made that mimic this Prop, but none hold as much power and pose as much danger. Within its pages one can learn all manner of Contracts accessible to anyone, but they all have costs that make them analogous to Goblin Contracts. Most involve the summoning of terrible hobgoblins or even Gentry.
