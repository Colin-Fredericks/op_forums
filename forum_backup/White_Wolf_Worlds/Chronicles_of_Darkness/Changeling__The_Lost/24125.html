    Some questions

    12-05-2013, 10:28 PM
    Hello,

    I am working on a character for a game and some questions came to my mind.

    1) as my character will be a waterborn elemental, one of the things about elementals is their difficulty to interact with humanity and other changeling. I am wondering though how to just bring that accross that my character has difficulty now that she has come back from the hedge?

    2.) How would religions interacting with changelings and the courts?

    -herkles

      1) Mechanically that difficulty is represented by losing 10-agian on specific social rolls. This translates into your character not being incompetent in interactions, esp. if they've got some talent with it, but that they're detached enough that they have trouble in complex social situations (aka when you have people rolling vs. each other because their average successes are lower they'll lose such contests more often) and they rarely succeed by large margins.

      From a more role-playing perspective, there's a lot of ways of handling it which is an excellent place to add some quirks based on your character. Perhaps as a Waterborn your character tends to let words flow out without a lot of attention to syntax, pausing and other minor verbal cues, and frequently transitions to new topics in the same statement. All of this tends to make them hard to understand or follow in conversation, making people less likely to listen to them and making it harder for them to effectively communicate.

      2) Lots, and lots of ways? There's no set response here. Every individual sit going to have their own reaction. The Courts, as a whole, are not presented as being terribly concerned with religion as a default. However Courts in a highly religious area might be impacted by this and different Courts might also trend towards gathering individuals of a similar reaction to religion after being taken to Arcadia.

          Hello there~

          1) I play an elemental, and the basic component of how I present that is to simply be dense and slow with social interaction, especially 'subtle(r)' ones. But beyond that, one of the more interesting and fun exercises is to re-imagine social behavior from the perspective of the element. How would water flirt, what emotions does it commonly feel, what are idioms and phrases it would use, how does it get what it wants or avoid being manipulated? It's a silly and fun project (or rather, an endearing but insane project - this is Changeling), and it can add a lot of quirks to your character.
          One of my favorite examples was a woodblood giving someone a queen honey-bee to express romantic interest. I don't have anything specific on hand for waterborn, but I'll share if they come up.

          Also, if available, Winter Masques gives more depth about the perspective and experiences of each Seeming.

          2) There's no default reaction between changelings and religion, but any response is going to be to reconcile existing religious beliefs with what changelings experience. The themes and foundations of the game are rooted in psychological trauma, not in anything religious, so there's plenty of room for changelings and their Courts to adapt or adapt to religious beliefs as well.

          Autumn Nightmares had a lot of interesting explanations of the Keepers based on what the changelings experience - one such story was that the Fey are angels who didn't fight in the War, and were banished to a half-way realm for not supporting Heaven (a story rooted in one Catholic reconciliation with folk Irish myth, by the way). There are a lot of other aspects - the trials of Job, traitors in our midst, a war for the souls of mankind, various good and bad spirits in many religions, the half-human children of the Greek gods - where religious themes could parallel the lives of Changelings. I'd start with a good understanding of the religion(s) in mind and of the personal experiences of the changeling(s) to seek out good overlap. If their are commonalities across the freehold, that may be grounds for Court-wide foundations.

          Second Chance for

          A Beautiful Madness

                                  In regards to religion entitlements, there is of course the Lost Pantheon, whereupon a changeling believes themselves to be divine, or close enough to it. While often they become atavistic icons or gods known from the age of myth, it's entirely possible one could believe themselves to be Jesus or Buddha or a figure from any number of modern religions. They can even "distill Glamour out of the worship of mortals, though doing so necessitates the revelation of one's true form to the unensorcelled."

                                      Originally posted by herkles View Post
                                      Another question that came to my mind recently is about the other 'time' courts, dawn and dusk and sun and moon. How are politics done with them? as half a day, or less, seem like it would be difficult to do politics, even though I like courts philosophies.
                                      It's been ages since I read them (skimmed them? >.> ), so I can't remember if any advice or description was given in the write-up. Politics would still be in play, just different. There's an understanding that if you try to put forth a longer term policy, the other Court might shut that down and/or undo your work. There are a number of responses to this. To name the three that come easily to me:

                                      1) A given Court does stuff the other won't find objectionable. If it's something nobody dislikes, the other Court could put forth effort to block you (and may do so for political reasons, depending), but incentives to do so aren't as obvious. There's likely some degree of staying out of each others' way.
                                      2) A given Court persuades the other to permit/assist on a certain policy. This will usually involve non-trivial exchanges, whether of goods or knowledge or oath-sworn favors or whatever. Somewhat in line with 1), it may also mean simply permitting/assisting with a certain policy the other Court wants.
                                      3) A given Court makes a sudden, dynamic declaration/action that gives the other Court little choice but to go along with it. If, say, one Court starts what amounts to a sudden war with another Freehold, supernatural group, etc. then the other Court may not be able to escape the consequences (e.g. victory might be possible if both Courts fight for it, but the freehold's toast or unacceptably diminished for sure if they don't). I imagine these are very significant instances, but undertaken very carefully because good gog will there be a ton of blowback and ill will for it.

                                      That's my reading on it, broadly speaking. Again, I could be way off in how they actually work.
