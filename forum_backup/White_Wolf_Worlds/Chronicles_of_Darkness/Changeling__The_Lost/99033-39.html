                                        The Last Dance Motley sets an ambush for two dangerous Loyalists. They were unprepared, however, for two questions: 1) Will there be any repercussions on the psyche of a member, and 2) what happens if they succeed?

                                        Everyone has a journal, including Gawain. Gawain and Insincere Marguerite are short fictions. Gawain’s all happened at the table; it is the battle at the climax of the session. Insincere Marguerite is more of a recap as journal, and did not take place at the table. Depending on the tastes of the readers, some might want Lori’s overall recap before Gawain’s for better context, but that is up to you. Also people who don’t usually read Jacob’s may want to give it a look this time. It’s different.

                                        Enjoy.

                                        Session LXXIII

                                        Gawain’s Memories

                                        Gawain took a deep breath of the crisp forest air. He felt full of life, bolstered by the hope of an honorable resolution and the successes of the night. Hunting Hawk had been captured and Lori had brought De la Rocha back to herself before she had killed the loyalist. They’d grabbed Rook without a fight and stopped the Summer Court from summarily executing him, there in the parking lot, like a dog.

                                        He stretched, preparing for the fight to come. He was glad Lori hadn't come, that she'd stayed at the edge of the forest with Insincere Marguerite. This wasn't a place for her, for either of them. Insincere Marguerite hadn't even let Julia come to the club to grab Rook, so she was safe as well. He looked over at Jacob. This hadn’t gone down like the Ice Elemental had wanted, but it didn’t show on the Russian man’s face. Not that much ever did. He glanced at Rook. He was similarly impassive, sitting in a meditative pose in the center of the clearing. His eyes were locked on Jacob.

                                        Gawain had to respect the man’s resolve. He didn’t agree with it - loyalty was a sword that should cut both ways and the Lady didn’t seem to be holding her end of it, from Jacob’s telling of it. But it was Rook’s choice to make, his loyalty to give. And with that loyalty, he couldn’t make the only pledge the accept to let him go free. Promising he’d leave Jacob, his motley or even the entire Lady by the Sea freehold alone wasn’t enough for any of them, and promising he’d never abduct anyone was too much for him.

                                        Given that, and the fact that no one was willing to consider holding him hostage until he agreed to be, as he put it, a ‘gold-star, bake sale participating citizen of the freehold’, there weren’t many options left. Rook had asked the favor of dying on his feet, fighting for a way home. Jacob had granted it.

                                        When the loyalist stood up, the six champions took their places around him, Jacob and Luna with their knives, Calamity with her glass, Molly with her Uzi, De la Rocha with her fists and Gawain with his zweihander. Rook acknowledged each of them in turn, and then, without any formal call, the fight began.

                                        Calamity struck first, a quick jab testing his defenses. He moved fluidly around her strike, not avoiding it completely, but causing her glass blades to skitter across the swirling darkness that surrounded him. Jacob followed up quickly, taking advantage of his distraction to score a slice under his brother’s ribs. Rook hissed through clenched teeth.

                                        The taller man struck back, almost quicker than Jacob could respond. His blades went deep, but still cut into nothing but the icy flesh Jacob had conjured up. Icy water soaked his shirt, puddling on the ground beneath him. The two brothers locked eyes.

                                        After a brief moment Rook’s widened as Molly shot a quick controlled burst into his back. He staggered, regaining his balance just in time to move so that De la Rocha’s fist just barely missed his nose. His chest heaved as he leaned away from her, but he snapped back into a ready position, trying to fend off Luna’s knife. He was slowing though, the sheer weight of their numbers wearing him down. The knife cut down his arm, dispelling a swath of darkness and revealing a line of red.

                                        Gawain was the last of the Changelings to swing, his movements precise. His contracts with Summer and Dawn roared through him and he felt the weightiness of the blow even as he began his swing. Rook moved to get out of the way, to block the blow, but he moved too slowly and there was too much momentum behind the blow. The zweihander sliced completely through his wrist, then his neck.

                                        Gawain fell to his knees as the corpse did, the rush of his Contracted power leaving him all at once. He took a deep, shuddering breath as he gave the dead man a warrior’s salute, Calamity stepped over and laid a hand on his shoulder. He laid a hand over her, patting it gently but not looking at her, or at the head that rolled over near him.

                                        Jacob picked up the head, cradling it and gently closing Rook’s eyes. Gawain laid down his sword and, after looking to Jacob for permission, gathered up the body, laying the severed hand on top of it. He laid it out on the backseat of the van and Jacob clambered up to sit beside it, still holding the head. Gawain looked at him.

                                        “We’ll bury him in the Hollow?”

                                        Jacob paused. “A pyre.”

                                        Gawain nodded and took a deep breath as if to speak, but then let it out and turned back for his sword. He handed his keys to Calamity on the way.

                                        On the ride home, Gawain cleaned his sword. It was a soothing, repetitive task and he lost himself in it, trying to forget the all too familiar feeling of his sword slicing through a neck, burying his deep feelings of satisfaction at justice delivered. He couldn’t have told you how far the impromptu fighting ground had been from the Dream House, but his sword was clean when they got home.

                                        Some part of Gawain’s brain registered surprise at Madame Spidertoes’ presence at their fountain, but she wasn’t a threat and he had a duty to fulfill. He gathered up Rook’s body again and made directly for the hedge gate, not wanting the girls inside to see the body. Calamity, Jacob and Luna followed.

                                        Calamity pulled Jacob aside once the pyre was built and Rook’s body had been laid out on it. She spoke earnestly, a hand on his shoulder. “You tried. You gave him a chance.”

                                        He nodded, his expression stiff. “I still watched a good friend kill my brother.”

                                        Gawain moved away, not wanting to eavesdrop anymore. After another moment, the two parted and Jacob lit the pyre, Luna standing nearby. It was a solemn affair, and when the fire was down to embers, Jacob turned without a word and went inside the Hollow house. Gawain watched Luna follow him, until Calamity took his hand and led him out of the Hedge.

                                        Desiree was waiting for them, and took his other hand after a moment of hesitation to see if she was welcome. She smiled up at him worriedly, but he managed a weak smile for both of his girls as they walked back into the Dream House.


                                        Lorelei’s Diary

                                        Shit.

                                        Of all the ways I saw that confrontation going, of all the horrible things my imagination had dredged up from the darkest corners of my mind, I have to admit that reality has once again surprised me. Even without conscious intent, it seems that the Empress and her servants are still finding new and interesting ways to hurt me and those I care about.

                                        Calamity quoted some military aphorism before we left the Dream House to hunt Loyalists: “no plan survives contact with the enemy.” We had talked our strategy through ad nauseum in the hopes that we could come up with a contingency for anything Rook might try, but the one thing we failed to account for was our own unpredictability. Now things have gone to hell in a handbasket, and I’m forced to contemplate something I would have said was unthinkable yesterday because my love finds herself at risk unexpectedly.

                                        To start at the beginning, we drove to L.A. and set our plans in motion yesterday afternoon. Davey and Calamity (disguised as Elena and Natasha, respectively) browsed the gallery, while most of the rest of the motley and Molly Nuit hovered close by, either invisible or in disguise. D was one of the latter, she had tucked her distinctive hair up under a large hat, swathed herself in drab colors, and strengthened her Mask to make her skin appear darker. Gawain, Luna and I were stationed down the street at a trendy café… close enough that we could lend support if necessary but far enough away to avoid notice (or so we hoped).

                                        Calamity had offered to take away my fear using Autumn Court contracts before she entered the gallery. I thought about it, but in the end I decided not to take her up on the offer. God gave mankind fear for a reason; it keeps us sharp, it keeps us out of unnecessary trouble, it keeps us alive. I believed that I could overcome my fear if necessary, and that I might very well need the edge it would give me before this confrontation was over. I still believe I made the right call.

                                        We were all in contact with these teeny-tiny headsets that linked us together through our phones. I tried to be nonchalant about this technology, but it wasn’t easy. I mean, this is some Dick Tracy level shit here! Communication devices so small that a casual observer won’t even notice that they exist? And apparently this technology is common enough these days that if anyone did notice it, they’d probably just assume that we were having mundane phone conversations with other people. Wonders of the modern age, man…

                                        Anyway, it wasn’t long at all before “Elena” got a text in Russian from one of her fellow students, who was also at the gallery (luckily we had included Davey in our pledge with the girls, so he could understand and respond to the text). We were in contact with Elena back at the Dream House, and she told us that the student in question was a casual acquaintance, but not a close friend. We almost universally agreed that Rook had set his trap.

                                        Davey and Calamity responded to the text, and agreed to meet the sender at the little café inside the gallery. Once there, it was obvious to Jacob that this fellow was indeed Rook in disguise. I’m not sure how he disguised himself, but apparently he had certain tics and habits that Jacob recognized, so in the end it was all for naught. He told the “girls” that he had spotted some of the people following Natasha outside of Elena’s apartment, and that it wouldn’t be safe for them to return there. Davey let Calamity take the lead here, and she played the part brilliantly. The two of them agreed to head to a safe place he knew until the heat died down. He gave them an address, which turned out to be the location of a pretty seedy club nearby. Still, they played along and agreed to meet him there, though the club wasn’t open yet, so they had to wait for about ninety minutes before departing.

                                        Meanwhile, Gawain, Luna and I approached the gallery, thinking that we might be able to nab Rook as he left the building. As we walked into the gallery’s parking lot, I happened to glance up at the sky and noticed something odd… at least it was odd for L.A. There were four large birds circling directly overhead. They were obviously raptors of some sort… hawks or falcons, I would guess. I felt a chill go up my spine and called the others’ attention to the birds.

                                        And then it happened. The boogeyman who has haunted my dreams for the last year stepped out from behind a parked van. He wasn’t as tall as I remembered, but he still towered over me… he was nearly as tall as Gawain. He was dressed in blacks and browns, and his sharp features were drawn up in a cruel grimace of triumph. His eyes glittered yellow under his sharp brows, and he walked with a fluid grace many dancers would envy.

                                        “Stop,” he said sharply as we approached.

                                        “What do you want?” Gawain asked, moving to stand in front of me.

                                        “I just want her,” Hawk said, inclining his head in my general direction, “I have no quarrel with the rest of you or your Freehold.”

                                        “Yeah, well, that’s not gonna happen,” Gawain said simply, drawing a knife (he’d left his big-ass sword in the van, as it tends to attract attention).

                                        “So that is how it’s going to be?” he asked in response.

                                        “It doesn’t have to be this way, Hawk,” I said desperately, and then everything went to hell.

                                        I can only assume that my comment about the birds carried over the headsets and got her worried. She leaped out of the shadows between two cars and barreled into Hawk. I didn’t recognize her at first due to the drab clothing, the scarf she suddenly had wrapped around the bottom half of her face, and the vicious, feral way she attacked. But it was the love of my life… it was my D.

                                        She nearly killed him in the first few seconds. She ripped at him, tearing at his flesh like a beast… but she was utterly silent the whole time. Hawk panicked and ordered his birds to attack us while he struggled to throw her off. One of them dived straight at me. I had just enough time to realize that it was some sort of Hedge beast before its talons scraped at my right eye. My vision went red and blurry.

                                        A couple of seconds later Gawain knocked the bird away from me. I barely noticed; my entire attention was captivated by what was happening between D and Hunting Hawk. She had an insane predatory gleam in her eyes that I had never seen before, and I realized that she was going to kill him if I didn’t stop her.

                                        “D!” I shouted hysterically. She turned to regard me, pausing in her rampage for a moment. “Don’t kill him,” I said soothingly, “he’s not worth it.” She cocked her head at me. I could tell I was getting through to her. And then Hawk took advantage of her distraction to lash out against her. Her Leechfinger powers absorbed the brunt of that first blow, but it was enough to send her back into a killing rage.

                                        While Gawain and Luna took care of the birds, I kept my attention fixed on the melee between D and Hawk. I called out to her again, and I could tell that she was there, listening to me, but her instincts had a powerful hold on her. I should have known that forcing her into a position where she had to hunt from stealth would awaken that part of her, but, well… I’d never seen her that out of control before. They both took some licks over the next thirty seconds or so, but D gradually began to calm down and started turning her attacks more toward holding him and less toward dealing direct damage.

                                        I reached into my purse and pulled out my pistol, sliding the safety back as I drew. “Hawk!” I shouted, demanding his attention. “Call off the birds and stand down. I am trying [u]very[/u] hard not to kill you right now, and you are not making it easy for me!”

                                        Hawk glared at me, then sort of slumped in D’s arms. He gave a quick whistle, and the two remaining birds flew away. Gawain gently took Hawk from D’s grip as I approached.

                                        “Sweetheart?” I asked in a quavering voice. Her eyes turned to regard me from the depths of her disguise. Her pupils were dilated to such a huge degree that her eyes almost looked like mine. “Baby,” I tried again, “it’s okay now. I’m here. I’m okay.”

                                        She took a hesitant step toward me, but didn’t speak. I closed the distance between us. She didn’t shy away, but she made no move to embrace me… or even approach me.

                                        I reached up and touched her face. Her skin was pale and waxy, and felt clammy to the touch. I tugged gently at the scarf covering her lower face. She didn’t want me to do that. I’m not sure if she was ashamed or if she was just instinctively shying away from my touch. In any case, when I pulled the scarf away, I saw that her mouth was set in a ghastly rictus grin. I pulled her closer to me, and the rigid expression began to twitch.

                                        Her hat fell off as I was kissing her, and her luxurious black and purple hair spilled out, and she was my love again.

                                        “Umm,” Gawain cleared his throat from a million miles away, “I’m pretty sure this guy is gonna die in the next few minutes unless we can stabilize him.”

                                        D breathed deeply. “Go on, Lori,” she said softly. “I’ll be okay now.”

                                        I bandaged Hawk’s wounds as best I could in the parking lot, but I didn’t waste any healing magic on him. I did use Spring contracts to heal the worst of D’s wounds, and then to fix my own eye. Hawk had passed out at some point, and Gawain put his unconscious form in his van, and the four of us rode over to the club to lie in wait for our other quarry.

                                        D held on to my hand for dear life the whole way over there. I did my best to be a comforting presence, and to reassure her that this changes nothing between us… and much to my own relief, I’ve found that it doesn’t. I’ve seen her at her worst now, and I’m happy to report that I’m just as much in love with her as I was before. I haven’t been calling her Maria, though… I didn’t want to evoke her time at the Hospital any more than absolutely necessary.

                                        The confrontation with Rook was almost surreal by comparison. We just sort of surrounded and restrained him in the club’s parking lot when he arrived. For a few seconds he tried to persist in his act, as though hoping to convince us that he was really just a normal mortal student. Davey changed his appearance at that point into the most intimidating person he could think of… Lucien Torrez stood there cracking his knuckles in a threatening way, the gemstones in his cufflinks glittering in the lights of the parking lot. That’s right, it was Davey’s patented Lucien Disguise With Diamonds™. That thought might have been the only thing that made me smile all night.

                                        Then Jacob appeared out of nowhere and called him by name. Rook let the façade drop, and a tall, thin man with ashen skin, colorless eyes, and short, spiky dark hair stood before us.

                                        “Well,” he taunted, “you managed to catch me. And it only took a third of the Freehold to do it this time. Bravo.”

                                        I don’t think any of us knew quite what to say to that… a fact which seemed to amuse Rook further. We had done it; we had captured both of the Loyalists targeting our motley… and now that we had them, we didn’t know quite how to proceed. Vic had shown up to take Hawk into custody, so at least he was no longer our problem for the time being.

                                        Things almost turned deadly at that point. Gawain spotted someone on a rooftop across the street… a changeling with a strong Summer mantle which nevertheless did not illuminate his face. This particular someone had a rifle aimed at Rook. Gawain stepped into the line of fire and the sniper fled the scene. We’re all thinking of Louie Guo at this point, but I guess it doesn’t really matter considering how the rest of the evening went.

                                        Rook threw us another wry grin as things settled down. “So what happens now? Am I to be executed, or perhaps offered the chance at glorious rehabilitation? Perhaps I could become a gold-star member of the Freehold? Participate in the bake sales and so on?”

                                        Gawain – who had been acting sort of high-on-life throughout this whole encounter – made some quip about how he would get a discount on baked goods if he did, but most of us didn’t really see anything humorous in the situation. Jacob mostly stayed quiet through the conversation that followed… he knew Rook very well, and I think he already suspected how the evening would end for his erstwhile brother.

                                        We tried. We really did try. I want to make that perfectly clear. But, well… Rook is a man of honor, and refused to betray his Lady. And while there’s a part of me that truly does respect that sort of loyalty, I am really beginning to hate the sound of that damn word. “Honor” caused White Maria to demand Dark Kimberly’s death earlier this year. “Honor” nearly got Joan killed or exiled when she demanded that the monarchs refrain from asking Andromeda to use the Table again. Hell, D was defending my “honor” when she clobbered Viscount Augustine a few weeks ago (still waiting for that apology, by the way). And yesterday, Rook’s “honor” prevented him from accepting the out we tried to give him.

                                        “I cannot betray the Lady,” he said over and over. And then he said it again, with a vicious cut of his eyes in Jacob’s direction, “I am a man of honor.”

                                        We tried everything we could think of to convince him. I have to consider how my words reflect on me. If I align myself too closely with Rook then my loyalty to this Freehold and my motley comes into question. If I show too much emotion over the death of my brother then people will start to wonder if I really aren’t a loyalist myself. How do I be honest with people without being completely honest? They don’t want to know the truth. They don’t want to know what thoughts are actually going through my head right now. They don’t want to know how much I hate them all for celebrating the death of my brother.

                                            They all get to go back to their lives and pretend nothing happened. They have their little orgies and act like all is right with the world. How the fuck am I supposed to fake that? How the fuck am I supposed to act like everything is alright and that I’m right as rain? Truth is that I can’t. I don’t know what is going to happen to me because of it, but I can’t pretend that nothing happened. I can’t pretend that I didn’t just lose one of the most important people in my life. I’ll have to fake at least some of it though. They can’t know how I actually feel. I would just put too much suspicion on me and my actions. I honestly don’t mean the Freehold harm, but they would think I do if I told anyone what is really going through my head. I can’t even really tell my therapist what is going through my head. I’m sure that to some extent they report on suspicious activities or thoughts to the monarchs, and my thoughts would definitely be considered suspect.

                                            On the plus side to all of this I hear that Hunting Hawk was captured and turned over to the Winter court for holding. There was apparently a bit of a fight in the parking lot of the museum, in which De la Rocha defended her lady love from the loyalist who wanted to return her to her Keeper. Gawain and Luna were there as well helping to fend off some birds of prey that Hunting Hawk had called upon to help him. They managed to convince him to send the rest of the birds away and then handily knock him out. Something about Lori pulling a gun on him and yelling at him. I don’t know the particulars.

                                            So, after therapy Calamity and I skipped our usual morning sparring match. I was not in the mood to fight with blades so soon after Rook’s death and I think that Calamity figured that out. She said that she was not up to sparring and I just agreed with her. We went into the house where everyone else was and had our usual breakfast. I was still a bit hungover, but covered it up with more coffee and Gawain’s excellent cooking. Lori and the others discussed what they wanted to do with Hunting Hawk. It was decided that Lori would ask him some questions focused on what happened to the two girls that she left behind when she fled Arcadia. So, I called Lowjack and explained that Lori and some others wanted to speak to Hunting Hawk that morning. He said that he would need at least two hours to have the man conscious. Apparently they were keeping him sedated so he couldn’t escape. I think I’d rather be locked up than drugged into oblivion, but that is just me. That was fine by Lori, so we planned to meet at the clinic in about two hours.

                                            Shortly after that Dr. Bright called me back to say that they had a vested interest in talking to Hunting Hawk after we did. Apparently he has been dreaming of the same Maria that haunts Twitch’s dreams, or I suppose nightmares. In any case he was dreaming of her, which makes me wonder exactly what happened in that parking lot. I told Dr. Bright that his plans made sense and that I would pass along the note to the others. I came back to the kitchen and told them all what Dr. Bright had revealed to me. To say that Lori and De la Rocha were nervous was the understatement of the year. At that moment I would not have put it past Lori to have murdered the man that dared to malign her beloved. Just the look in her eye. It was revealing. Anyway, we all discussed options. Lori said that we had two options. We could admit that De la Rocha was Maria and get ahead of things or we could kill him. At least I’m fairly certain that’s what she was going to say before Gawain interrupted with his idea to contact Madame Spidertoes and get her involved in all of this. We could admit that the “Maria” that Twitch and Hunting Hawk dreamed of was a Darkling, but not exactly who it was. Madame Spidertoes is a well know advocate of all the Darklings in the Freehold. We could admit that Maria was among us but that she didn’t mean any harm and was not a threat to the Freehold. She really has changed her ways since she left Arcadia, so that’s not a lie. I was behind the idea to admit to Spidertoes who Maria really was. It is harder to keep a lie going when someone starts to poke holes in it. It’s easier to have as much of the truth be a part of your story as possible. De la Rocha was trying to completely distance herself from who she was, which I admire, but which is hard to fully achieve. The truth was going to come out at some point considering there are those living that remember who she was. It was not feasible or advisable for De la Rocha to continue lying anymore. Coming clean to Madam Spidertoes first was the best option we had to getting control of the situation. Or at least telling Madame Spidertoes what was up and getting her support. I think it was mentioned that she already knew De la Rocha’s secret. Which makes sense. They used to be lovers after all.

                                            Now I have to put my mask back on. I have to pretend that everything is okay. That I got my grief out with the pyre and the drinking. Truth is I don’t know when this feeling will end. I don’t know if I want it to. I have to remember him. I have to keep him alive in my thoughts at least. If I accept his passing then it would feel like I’m giving up on his memory and I can’t do that. Not now. Not yet. I know I won’t be able to explain this to the others. How do I explain to them that despite the pain he caused me, the things he did to me in Her service, he was still my brother and I am still his? I guess I am still the monster after all.

Juliette’s group went on ahead, while Calamity and Davey followed Andrew’s car, and I followed at a distance, my appearance changed and Jacob and Molly invisible in the back. When we got there, Davey took the initiative and changed to look like Lucian, which gave us all a start but caught Rook off guard enough that fighting was not necessary. “ The Darkling looked up, then down. “Then Gawain spotted Louis Guo on an opposite roof with a sniper rifle.”

Her whole body was moving, some in strange ways.” The Artist looked excited. “She said something was wrong with Maria. I had heard you guys were coming back, so I was able to assure her, but she insisted on waiting at the fountain.

Andromeda commented she was happy that I was happy here working, but said the really missed the…quirkiness…of the Lady By The Sea having a resident artist who painted people from rooftops. My lurking is missed, apparently.”
