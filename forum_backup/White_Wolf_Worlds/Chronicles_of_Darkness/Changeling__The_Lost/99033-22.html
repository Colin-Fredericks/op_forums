I shed my clothes as quickly and quietly as I could, slipped under the covers, and snuggled up against her back. Julia had her head pillowed on my right breast (of course… sometimes I’m honestly surprised that she hasn’t tried to write her name on my boobs in indelible marker) and D’s breath was warm and comforting on my neck, while her hand rested possessively on my belly. That woke up both of my partners, and after a brief yet intense debate, we agreed that food was a marginally more immediate necessity than sex. We threw on some clothes (I just grabbed a silk kimono-style robe out of my closet, actually), and made our way downstairs.

Felicity came over to the Dream House so that she and Marguerite could talk things out and decide how to proceed. Marguerite asked me to “chaperone” the meeting to make sure that she didn’t regress as she had in Felicity’s presence in the past. Of course I agreed to do so. I didn’t have high hopes for the meeting, though I recognized it as a necessity.

As the two of them spoke, I took a peek at their hidden desires to see if I could glean anything about their respective intentions. Each of them has a dark side they’re suppressing… but honestly, the same thing could be said of just about anyone. In Marguerite’s case, there’s a part of her that wants to possess Felicity, to keep her hoarded away from anyone else who might desire her… like she did with Yvette in Arcadia. In Felicity’s case, she’d like a little payback against the girl who humiliated her ex-boyfriend, the Day King… but she also gets a naughty little thrill from the idea of humiliating him further by being with Marguerite.

Things were remarkably polite throughout the exchange, and I began to wonder whether I really needed to be there at all… but I’m glad I was. I tried to gently steer the conversation along appropriate paths, and I think I must have succeeded on some level. Whenever either one of them seemed to be on the verge of giving in to their darker urges, I did what I could to bring out their more positive aspects, and both of them seemed a little more at ease by the time they parted.

It didn’t make much of a difference in the end, but I had to try, and I’m not sorry I did it. I know that as a member of the Spring Court it’s sort of inimical to my nature to dampen the flames of passion… but I still don’t think this relationship will be good for Marguerite, and I hated to see that look of despair on Princess Jolie’s face last night.

They’re going to take things slowly at first, with an actual date rather than an erotic rendezvous, and I think that’s a hopeful sign. I didn’t see any indications that Marguerite is playing a long game here, hoping to “collect” Felicity and claim her as a possession. But I have a wary eye on her, and I don’t intend to relax my scrutiny any time soon.

But…” and then it came pouring out, everything that’s really been bothering me about this whole situation. “Marguerite, I’ve been on the other side of this obsession before. I’ve been the most treasured possession; the prettiest pet in the cage. I’ve been someone’s property. I know what it’s like to lose my sense of self, to find everything that I am subsumed by what my Mistress wanted me to be. I can’t let that happen to anybody else.” I paused for a breath, and whispered the last thing I needed to say. “And I can’t be with anyone who would do that to another person.”

    Her grip on me tightened, and I almost didn’t hear the quaver in her voice. “You will keep the watch on me, and I will be very careful, and together we shall see that it does not come to that. Agreed?”

    I nodded. “Agreed.”

    I rejoined the rest of the motley as Marguerite said a private goodbye to Felicity. In our few moments of (almost) assured privacy from her, we hastily discussed a few plans for her birthday tomorrow (and Julia’s is next week… jeez!). Unfortunately, nothing seemed to stick. We already did the whole “somebody unlikely jumps out of her cake” thing on Calamity’s birthday, and we didn’t want to repeat ourselves and make things boring. Some mention was made of buying an erotic cake shaped like Kevin Costner’s stunt butt – since at least that was topical – but the others overruled me on that one. I confess that I was not the most effective advocate of that plan, as I couldn’t seem to stop myself from dissolving into giggles every time anyone (myself included) uttered the words “butt cake.”

In the end, however, this was vetoed by Calamity when she found out that I’d never seen another film called The Goonies. It was a cute film about a bunch of kids who get chased into a series of caves by crazy gangsters and end up working their way through a bunch of booby traps to eventually find the lost treasure trove of a pirate captain. It was pretty enjoyable, though I can’t help but wonder about the name of the pirate in question… One-Eyed Willy? Seriously?

    The other film was a mildly subversive comedy called But I’m a Cheerleader. It was about a girl whose parents send her to some sort of ultra-conservative re-education camp to “cure” her of lesbianism. Ironically, she ends up with a much clearer view of her own true sexuality due to her experiences at the camp. The film was surprisingly deep, calling into question not just the mainstream view of sex and sexuality, but some much more ingrained ideas about traditional gender roles as well.

    The film was obviously pretty meaningful to D. She played it cool, but anyone who knows her really well could see that she was affected by the story as it unfolded on the screen.

    As I lay draped across three of my lovers watching the film, I started thinking about my own sexuality. Okay… maybe “sexuality” isn’t really the right word for it. I’ve known ever since Arcadia that I’m bisexual… I enjoy the physical act of sex tremendously, regardless of the gender of my partner(s). But what about my feelings on an emotional level? I only seem to fall head over heels in love with other women. I haven’t really felt a serious romantic connection to any male since Kyle back in High School. Does that make me a lesbian? I don’t think you’re allowed to be a lesbian if you like having sex with men, though. And even speaking on a strictly emotional level I’m not sure I want to label myself like that. I mean, what if I meet a man tomorrow, or next week, or twenty years from now, and we just sort of click? Do I want to throw away that possibility because I’m misinterpreting the romantic circumstances of my current life?

    I think in the end it’s easier for me to just go with the flow. I’m going to continue to defy any label anyone wants to hang on me, and be my own person without limiting myself needlessly. After all, as soon as you use a word to describe what something is, you’re by omission also describing all the things it isn’t. And who needs that?

    In any case, after the films D asked to speak to the motley briefly. She wanted us all to promise that none of us would allow ourselves to be alone with the Handmaidens. A few people seemed a little irritated at the request (Calamity, I’m looking at you), but D made a good case: as a Leechfinger herself, she knows the cravings that those two feel… the urge to drain the life out of their partners completely. To hear her tell it, they’re so adept at giving pleasure that their victims often consent in the end, and give up their own lives voluntarily. D wanted to make sure that none of us put ourselves in that position.

    I shrugged it off. “D, I’ll admit that the idea of spending time with them is intriguing,” I said, “but I remember what happened to Tamara. I’m horny, but I’m not stupid. Well… I’m not that stupid, anyway,” I finished with a grin.

    She shook her head like she always does when I get all self-deprecating, but she seemed to accept my statement at face value. “I can’t lose you,” she said seriously after pulling me a short distance away from the others. “I know what those two do to people, and I can’t lose you, Lori.”

    “Have you ever… you know?” I asked after a moment.

    D nodded. “Not for a long time, though,” she added quickly, “and I’ll never let myself do it again.”

    I thought about it for a minute. “You could, you know,” I said softly. “With me, I mean. I’d let you. I trust you completely.”

    She shook her head adamantly. “Never,” she said in a voice that brooked no argument. “You’re too important. I’ll never look at you as food. I swear.”

    I took in the look in her eyes. “Okay,” I said, and the two of us rejoined the others for the usual post-movie shenanigans.

    Now, my presence is requested by a gaggle of giggling girlfriends, and I’ve never been very good at saying no to them. After the rest of them drop off, I’m going to hit the books for a while and try to think up something truly special to do for Marguerite’s birthday.

    Until next time… buttcakes!
