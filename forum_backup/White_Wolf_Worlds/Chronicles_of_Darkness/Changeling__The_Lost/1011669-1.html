                                Something about the World/Chronicles of Darkness, Veindark, is that immortality is really not something one can attain without a significant sacrifice, either bodily, spiritually, mentally or all three. Immortals usually require sacrifices to be made for them (such as bathing in the blood of others, stealing their organs or their entire body), and if they don't it usually makes them either a target for other, less scrupilous immortals or comes with a hideous downside. That's also not getting into the problems that comes with being immortal yet having loved ones who are not.

                                In terms of playable immortals, Prometheans, barring accidents, can live for an exceedingly long time in their path to become human, and Vampires have an equally long lifespan if they can avoid the problems of Hunters, the sun and, of course, each other. However, Prometheans can never stay in the same place without ruining the world around them, nor can Vampires just enjoy themselves with their own kin because of how cut-throat Vampire society is. However, both still need to eat, quite regularly, and both have significant weaknesses in exchange for their immortality (such as fire).

                                As for high Wyrd Changelings, as noted, it's only Changelings who are Wyrd 10 that are at risk of becoming Gentry - you do not have to be very low Wyrd to avoid it. Further, there is a merit that actually grants the life extending bonuses of higher Wyrd while being lower Wyrd, though it doesn't grant bonuses past the maximum listed amount. Even if you never go past Wyrd 5, and don't invest in the merit, Changelings can live a very long and fulfilling life. Yes, they live with the fear that the Gentry will come and steal them away...but that's why Changelings form Freeholds, Courts and Motleys. There is safety in numbers, and by being changed by the Gentry you now have a whole new world to explore and marvel at, and at least now you're equiped with abilities that make you better able to withstand the world at large (far better than mortals can anyways).

                                And if you really still want immortality as a Changeling? Isn't there an entitlement that grants something to that effect, or at least significantly prolonges one's age? I remember Baroness Nerak has one Changeling of the Autumn Court (Madam Spidertoes I believe) who has it, and she's described as being from an age before electric lights...

                                Also, Charlaquin wasn't being insulting regarding playing Changeling Luke Skywalker - mechanically you can become Luke Skywalker in the game by being a Fairest Weisseu Frau with a sword Token that has the benefit of ignoring durability when striking objects and or people. What they were getting at is that if you want something in a game, just work it out with a GM and see how you can make it work without unbalancing everything. If you want immortality, make that a character arc for your character to discover. If you want to find or even become "the one Good Gentry", then make that into the story of the chronicle - maybe the way to become a force comparable to the Gentry involves some secret deep within the Hedge and a great deal of permanent sacrifice, but in doing so you ascend to their level.

                                Though it's not anywhere near ready, a Changeling Freehold I'm making has at least two Fae Touched in it, both with high positions, one being the wife of one King (the Paint Court) and one being the actual founder of a court (the Bone Court). The Paint King's wife manages to becme a proper Changeling by making a quick, but flawed deal with a Gentry whose Huntsman had captured here (it's meant to be a quest for players), whereas the Bone Queen wishes to become a Changeling herself in order to have mre time and capabilities to save her husband (another quest the player characters can indulge in). As of now, in the books, there is no explicite system for Fae Touched to become Changelings, but that doesn't mean that you can't make something up or work with a GM in order to make something, so long as it's interesting, balanced and, most of all, fun.

                                                Originally posted by Veindark View Post

                                                As for the Former yes and as for the Latter, if the &quot;True Fae&quot; are former changelings then shouldn't they be still be sympathetic and relatable as they were Changelings before?
                                                I believe I noted this earlier, but no, not really. Changelings and the True Fae are trapped in a cycle of abuse, power and vanity - Chaneglings are changed utterly by their experience in Arcadia, and even if it's not as terrible as some durances, a Changeling is still changed and given triggers that if, well, triggered, make them feel as they were in Arcadia instead of how they were as human. This is a type of insanity, and part of the reason that Wyrd 10 Clarity 0 Changelings go into the Hedge to become True Fae is because they believe not only that they're still in Arcadia, but that they have power comparable to their previous Keepers. In a sense, this is a case of Changelings empathizing with the Gentry, who are all mad, vain and self grandizing. A True Fae may have some familiar aspects to those who knew them as a Changeling, but in this case they have simply become what changed them in the first place.

                                                Part of pretty much every splat in Chronicles of Darkness has a familiar theme: with great power comes great sacrifice, and the potential for great insanity. Attaining a level of power that puts you even a little beyond human physically and mentally (that is, where you can start getting Attributes and Skills above 6) has you starting losing your fuel stat regularly, as well as having requirements beyond what low tier characters were required. This can end up changing the character significantly. Becoming a being like a True Fae, which seems to be what you're aiming for, would require a much greater change to them.

                                                Remember, True Fae have effectively divorced themselves from how they were as humans (or whatever they may once have been); they are utterly alien to the normal rules of society and reality. They make their own rules, and are effectively gods within their own domain (to the point that some are actually unkillable within it, no matter how desicated a corpse they leave behind). If you want to become a player version of that that is powerful and empathetic and relatable, something has to change, because you've effectively removed any downside of becoming a Gentry save that the non-empathetic ones will see you as a target even moreso than a Changeling would be.

                                                That mini-game mentioned above was about how the True Fae regularly fight to obtain the titles and names of other Gentry, as that boosts their considerable power further. Vampires are cutthroat, but Gentry are downright terrifying to one another. In that sense, it's not that bad staying as a Changeling - there's always a bigger fish to fry, after all, and what's bigger or juicier to a Fae than a coveted name/title belonging to a rival?

                                                    Veindark, gotcha.

                                                    In that case option one is probably the best. And that's drawing upon the idea of becoming more than Changeling, but less than a True Fae.

                                                    One of the most brilliant move of 2nd Edition is the idea of "No Neat Boxes", as pioneered as often as possible to showcase the mystery and unknown of the setting. So if you want an immortal Changeling that completely fits within the horror core setting of CofD, then all you need to do is have them search for a means of transcending mortality without completely abandoning it. This won't be easy, in fact it'll be as difficult as following the Equinox Road back to Arcadia to confront your Keeper and gain some measure of closure for the torment your character endured. But it is possible.

                                                    To just spit ball some in game ideas: The Hedge. The Hedge is already full of faerie creatures that lie somewhere between reality and Arcadia. If you said, "my character is going to quest in the Hedge for a means of
                                                    becoming an immortal Hobgoblin" then I cannot imagine a single ST who wouldn't start salivating at the mouth (because as we know, all Story Tellers have an insatiable appetite for good stories.)

                                                    Now, the catch is that in order to stay true to the setting you have to compromise a little for the sake of Changeling's themes of "beautiful lies, painful truths." Yes, your character might not succeed and either become a monster or be recaptured. Yes, your character might succeed except in a way they weren't expecting. But that compromise allows all imagination to open up within the confines of the themes and moods of the setting.

                                                    And that's all I've got. Hope it helps.

                                                    (P.S. As for the latter, I'm going to have to agree with Taidragon again. The only way to play the Gentry is as inhuman fantasy settings that walk and talk. The only way to play a sympathetic True Fae is to play an Exile. Those banished from Arcadia and with barely the powers above a Changeling or Hobgoblin. Their disconnect from Arcadia forces upon them human concepts, and is the only time they come close to genuine human thoughts and feelings.)
