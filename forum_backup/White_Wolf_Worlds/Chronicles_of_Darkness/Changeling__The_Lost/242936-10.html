            Originally posted by Barbrarilen View Post
            There's something ironic about how the flavor behind earning powers sometimes hampers it. Does that sound like gibberish?

            If you want to broker a deal with Fire in Changeling or hunt down and strongarm a spirit into teaching you a Gift in Werewolf, that whole event, which can practically eat up a lot of (awesomely spent) time in a session kind of feels like payment itself - to then have xp costs on top of it can be so frustrating, especially since an 'earned' power isn't any mechanically stronger than one given after a time skip or level-up cutaway, or such.

            Changeling's flavor always makes it sound to me like your character would not learn any non-court contracts once they are back in the mundane world unless they are in serious danger from low clarity and tapped into that dangerous, enchanting undercurrent of an otherworld... Which is of course just silly.

            Having a load of different ways to earn contracts is important so players aren't hampered, but there is such a disconnect from the story for some as opposed to others.
            I don't see a huge need for there to be an in-game session dedicated to learning it, but I do think there should be an in-game explanation. It doesn't even have to be a great explanation, but it should reinforce the themes. "I hunted down the appropriate spirit and learned the Gift of Whatsit" can happen off-screen, by my lights, and that's fine. Or "I spent some time off-screen to learn about the Death Arcanum so now I have a dot in it." To me, it's the standard "so when did the adventurer go to the bathroom, exactly?" There isn't some dungeon-crawler pixie dust that makes them immune to the need to defecate; it's just something that happened off-screen.

            To me, XP expenditure is just "I have sealed this as part of my character." There's a difference between buying the Striking Looks merit and using a power to achieve the same result; in the former case, the ST can't touch it. In the latter case, the ST might dispel the Life spell or invalidate the Contract's effect or whatever. Similarly, if you get a bunch of Changelings who all sit in a room and learn about Smoke, but they don't spend XP to buy the Contract on their sheet, I'd find that grounds to story hook that into that having random stuff happen to make them lose access to those powers too.

                    Learning about contracts usually involves a mentor or reading. Like an attorney's office with the world, I imagine changelings have all sorts of 'legal documents' that explain the set up for a Contract.

                    Of course, the thing of the matter is that Contracts are a matter of speaking though the clauses of that contract. A lot of the Wyrd Sorcery(I'm just borrowing the term guys) stuff you see in fairy tales is actually the articulation and exploration of a Contract. Grimoires are books of laws for changelings, each spell the signing of it. But I imagine it's taught by either reading the law books or learning it from a teacher.

                    Enacting Contracts is something akin to dialing the phone number of the universe and calling it to fulfill its' dues, with the glamour or catch filling in for the change you plug into the machine.

                    No...that metaphor's not quite right. Certainly a short story (Lost 1st Edition, Chapter Two opening story) shows that a changeling thought to the wall, so sure, it's true in a sense. But Contracts are the little brothers of Titles, same as Pledges are(I'm not sure where to place Pledges and Contracts on the scales). By signing the Contract, the aspect and the Changeling are kind of one,within the terms defined, much like a Stranger is his domain. Glamour and Catches just provide the momentary wiring, affording changelings some distance and identity by having the connection not all be 'on'. Pledges are always 'on', so long as it's defined so.

                    Maybe when the other side talks back, that's all just the changeling. I dunno, somewhere between the payphone and the wiring metaphors is the truth.

                            Wyrd is....the simplest way to put it is that it is that it's connections. It is a active, if not living, force of connection between everything. THe reason the Self can have a sense of the Other is because of the Wyrd. Fire can burn you because of the Wyrd. You can command fire to dance when you invoke the Cotnract because of the Wyrd.

                                Originally posted by MachineIV View Post


                                In our experience, yes, it's the five dot structure. If, as a developer, you come up with an interesting idea for a power that you feel really warrants inclusion, the onus is then on you to craft four other powers. For example, Vampire's gone through tons of editions. None of the core Disciplines have gone without any sort of change, because some of the powers rendered each other redundant, were boring, or felt thematically out of place. But there are certainly powers we know we want vampires to have. After over 20 years, we're still feeling them out and tinkering with them in order to make that five dot progression feel right. If we're still struggling with that after 20 years and dozens of writers, the structure is probably to blame, yes.
                                I really don't mind forgetting the 5-dots system, but I really have a question: If the 5-dot system is a design problem because you have to come up with 5 powers, why change it with a system where you have to design 6 different variations of the same power? I mean, in theory you'd run into the same problems designing 6 variations of the same contract (redundancy, theme, etc) than creating 5 tiered powers. Plus, you'd have to change the testing process to accommodate the fact (testing the different contracts in different seeming combinations so no degenerate combinations occur or no Seeming gets all the boring, out-of-place contracts).
