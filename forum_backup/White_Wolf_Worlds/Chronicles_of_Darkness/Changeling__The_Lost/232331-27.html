        I kinda get the sense that they are doing it function-first for 2e. You pick the Kith you want, and then pick which of the 6 Seemings to apply to that, with the intent that all 6 can fit it just fine. I have trouble seeing how they could make that work without making the Kiths relatively flexible and amenable to different interpretations. To this end, David's mentioned that some Kiths will be merged, and that some of the old names may pop up on a particular Seeming's variant suggestion -- no different mechanics, just an additional idea for where that bonus could be coming from.

        In 1e, all the Kiths were written under the assumption that if you were taking it, you had already taken its associated Seeming. In that context, it made sense for all the Beast-associated Kiths to assume the character's an animal, because that was the story the Beast Seeming was trying to tell. The minor bonus from the Kith just helped tell a slightly different version of that story. Like you, I also kinda found this constricting. Nor are we alone; disassociating Seeming and Kith or making more allowances for fluff explanations on the mechanics seems to me to be one of the most common house rules around. It's also another benefit I see to giving the 2e Kiths more potent powers than they've had. It makes the Kith the key factor in the sort of story being told, while the six Seemings offer different ways to help tell it. Or, per the sense I'm getting, the two may wind up being more equal partners in helping to tell a Changeling's story.

        I do kinda wish 1e had gone more into the sort of looseness that lots of fans ultimately went for themselves. Like, taking your Kitsune example. There's nothing wrong with someone taking Beast-Hunterheart for that, if playing up the animal side, and the claws and fangs, is the story the player wants to tell. If the player is more keen on going for the illusion and trickery side of things like you (and, frankly, me), then Darkling-Mirrorskin should be just as valid.

        As a side-note, there may be good news for your phooka-themed character, regarding animal shapeshifting. They're moving away from the 5-dot progression, so a "turn into an animal" Contract would be a single purchase. I can only speak for myself, but I much prefer that to first needing 4 steps that may or may not be very useful and certainly aren't as central in order to get to it.

            Ah, well, I was editing my previous post to tack this on, but since you responded in the intervening time, Gaius, I might as well make it a full post...

            I guess I should be a bit clearer about what I mean... A lot of kiths, especially beast and elemental kiths, seemed to be more about just looking like something than about what players actually want to do with them. Elemental kiths had basically no bearing on the actual powers you could focus upon, so you could be a Fireheart and enjoy buying cheaper water-related powers. For that matter, it had little to particularly say about who your character really tried to do with their lives. In that Fear Maker's Promise supplement, you had a Winter Court Fireheart who enjoyed causing heartbreak for the giggles. There's no link there between kith and character concept. That Fireheart power is to give a boost to wits rolls, of all things.

            An Antiquaraian, meanwhile, says "I'm playing a character who is valued for being very knowledgable." A Gravewight says, "I want to have a game that involves ghosts, and not just living characters." An Oracle says, "I want to be a the voice of wisdom in the motley, always cautious, strategic, and sniffing out the traps ahead." A Gameplayer says, "I want to play some crafty gambler or chessmaster strategist." A Flowerling says, "I want to wield social influences like a spider weaves its webs." A Mirrorskin says, "I want to go doppelganger and make my enemies fight each other for me while I laugh from the shadows." A Gargantuan says, "HULK WANNA SMASH!"

            Kiths are so fascinating because they're the single biggest tip-off of what it is the player wants their character to be. It's the most defining and unique choice they make, and it plays as directly into where they want to take a character as the choice of virtue and vice. That's why I'm saying that there needs to be less focus upon the aesthetics, which you could free up to change as you will, and more upon the paths of what they make the player's characters actually capable of doing that others not of that kith could do.

            This is why there can never be enough kiths, and flexibility is a better way to go. It's both the core character concept and that one thing the player really wants to feel when they play the game. Players pick stealthy or manipulative character types because they enjoy that feeling of cleverness and skill when they manage to pull something off, while players might pick the hulking ogre types when they just enjoy a good beat-down on their enemies. Bring up all those discussions about different player types in RPGs, and you could probably pin kiths to each archetype as being custom-fitted for those player stereotypes. (Although there might be a dearth of "wacky" kiths in CtL...)

            Hence, I'm a bit uneasy about seeing Fireheart just recycled one more time. Fireheart really only said, "I am going to buy contracts relating to fire at some later point," but anyone could do that. It was a kith that existed largely just for the aesthetics of being a guy with fire hair, not for what they said about the player, or let the player do. Now, maybe that's changing, maybe it's some full-on set of fire-related contracts you can't get somewhere else, but I'm not sure that's the case, and even if it were, what's the theme? What's that thing a fire kith does that makes it blatantly obvious what the player wants to do? (I mean, barring wanting to be a Fire Bender and Kung Fu fighting with an Airtouched...) The only reason they'd recycle the kith is if they want to maintain continuity with the old line, so it wouldn't make sense to warp its intent TOO heavily, but without really restructuring it, they aren't going to make it a signal of the player's intent.

                To again give an example of what I mean, I saw somewhere in this thread a correlation between Flowerling and Woodblood, because "plants". But, again, the reason why someone would want a Flowerling, whose main purpose is seduction, (with a dryad/nymph-like aesthetic,) is drastically different from why someone would want a Woodblood, whose main purpose is toughness and a rather direct, literal connection to nature.

                If the name of the game, however, is to divine where the kiths are going from what's been said, the previous MachineIV post on what's being merged because of "similarities", provides the more obvious route of long-distance psychoanalysis.

                Originally posted by MachineIV View Post

                Windwing/Lurkglider Stonebones/Earthbones
                Cleareyes/Cyclopean
                Hunterheart/Razorhand Palewraith/Shadowsoul
                Waterborn/Swimmerskin
                Succubus/Romancer

                I think there are maybe some others; these were just from the first list brought up. But, these might be cases where you get one as the main kith, and one is just an example Seeming variant on it. Or something.
                Now, as MachineIV says, these are more rough-draft than final product, but the very fact that it's what they first bring up indicates that it's their innate reaction, the one that most indicates the governing philosophy they are using to make these decisions.

                Windwing/Lurkglider seem pretty obvious and harmless, since Lurkglider was originally sold pretty much as a Windwing for Darklings.

                Razorhand/Hunterheat shows an even more powerful correlation of use rather than aesthetics, because Razorhand had the aesthetics of Freddy Kreuger versus just any random predatory animal, although on a subtle level, they were different. (You always have a lethal weapon at hand versus your brawl being lethal.) However, where's the Draconic? Draconic was just a Hunterheart in Fairest clothing by mechanics...

                That said, some are a bit problematic. Stonebones is about being strong (strength buff), while Earthbones is about being tough (armor bonus). It seems these are bundled more by being "earth-based", since there's a big difference in a player's role in the motley when they say they want to go on offense and when they want to tank, even if both have to do with combat. Even if both are combat-focused, it's the difference between the berserker and the paladin.

                Palewraith and Shadowsoul is even stranger, as they have little but "darkness" in common. Shadowsoul is about intimidation in use, but the cross between shadow and beauty in aesthetics. Palewraith is about reinforcing the keep-to-the-shadows nature of Darklings in use, and about being the forgotten even among Darklings in aesthetics. I don't know where those cross over at all. It seems the idea was that Shadowsoul needed to be merged into something that was a "generic darkling" because the Seeming-blending purpose of the kith was gone, and they just grabbed Palewraith because it was one they didn't feel like keeping, rather than because it, specifically had anything in common with Shadowsoul.

                Combining Succubus and Romancer under the name of just differentiating kiths by seeming is a little strange, as well, being as they come from the same seeming to begin with! I get the sense Succubus needs to get the axe since vices don't work the same way, while Romancer's actual utility was completely different from its stated aesthetics. (That is, it makes you harder to identify to other people, rather than having anything to do with seduction.) In terms of function, Romancer has more to do with Mirrorskin...

                Also notable is that a lot of these are Beasts and Fairest. Someone else mentioned earlier in the thread how Wizened were basically all aesthetically the same, but their kiths were just different specialties, the jobs that you were going to dedicate yourself to, which makes them already solidly molded to the notion of being that signifier of intent. There aren't any Wizened on that list. Meanwhile, as I said before, Beasts and Elementals are the vaguest of the seemings, with kiths more focused upon just giving you the aesthetics of a general family of animals or elemental appearance, rather than actually giving you gameplay mechanics related to being one.

                If you look at a kith like Airtouched and Runnerswift, however, those are basically similar in effect, but wildly divergent in aesthetics, and they're not being merged, at least in this list. I again have to question whether or not it would be better to just wipe the list of elementals, almost entirely, and make them just the elemental aspects of a different kind of kith. A kith that focuses upon anger, like Hunterheart, and has an elemental seeming could be the equivalent of a Fireheart whose aesthetic is based around fire = passion/anger. Rather than being predatory claws and fangs, they may simply have a searing hot grasp. If you wanted to have more the "mind that is powerful and flowing like a fire", like the original effect was, you would take something like Riddleseeker with an elemental seeming. If you wanted a more stand-back-and-hurl-fireballs-like-a-wizard type of Fireheart, then you take... well, there's no kith that's really like that, at least not directly. If you want a boost to your fire contracts, you're better off just going for more Manipulation or Wits, since those are the stats you use for the contracts. (Although Troll and Chateline have Manipulation buffs, amusingly enough...) Which is kind of the problem I had with the old elemental kiths, as they had only to do with aesthetics, and not with the actual driving playstyle a player would enjoy playing.

                The Snowskin ironically has more in common with the Shadowsoul than Shadowsoul has with Palewraith, since it's bonuses to intimidation and subterfuge that are their main kith powers.

                Finally, the Winter Masks direct references to different cultural mythological creatures, like the Illes or Oni, are great as a way of telling people how to make additional kiths to suit a specific desired inspiration, but they open up a completely new can of worms when it comes to effects. For one, there are far more than one way of interpreting an Oni, from the punishers of sin on one hand to just plain demons on another to just wild, barbaric drunkards with comically huge strength on yet another, still. For another, they're rather specific takes on legends, so they wouldn't play well with the different flavors of seemings. They are pretty prime candidates for trying to strip down into what people would want to do with those kiths, and generalizing, rather than keeping as a specific take on a legend. If you focus on the "what they push players towards doing" parts, you can make an Illes or, for that matter, a Rusalka if you focus upon either the water-dwelling or temporary beauty parts. In fact, you could reproduce some legends of the Jurogumo, a giant spider monster, or the skin-stealing Hags by expanding that base glamour of a pretty face thrown over a hideous monster part.

From a play perspective, one of the reasons kiths were weak was because they were the same power with new characters as late-game characters that sneezed more glamour than a new character could wield.) For example, that water breathing kith might take a glamour to breathe underwater or only hold their breath at wyrd 1, but at wyrd 3 graduate to full water breathing for free, and could have outright underwater houses. The windwing kiths were lame, but flight is such a major advantage that it's understandable why it was left out. Maybe make it a wyrd 7 power-up, and even then, still cost glamour.

Merits that make kiths with high affinity for specific contracts either cheaper or innate free powers would be great. Alternately, you might make "merit trees" that branch a kith out in different powers. For example, Venombites might share a claw or fang merit chain with Hunterhearts and Gristlegrinders, letting them produce more fearsome bites that combine with their poison-delivery, or might take a merit that upgrades a weak venom into having more diverse effects, like a sleep poison or a hallucinogenic.

                                                In fact, again, I think it might be better to have a more generic set of kiths in general. Gristlegrinders, Hunterhearts, Draconics, Soldiers, and Razorhands might all be boiled down to a combat kith, and then given a minor option of differentiation by a couple free specialized kith merits over what precise specialization they gain. A lot of the Wizened can be made into a "dwarf" or "gnome" kith (differentiated by seeming) with a minor differentiation merit to determine their specific craft.

                                                Making them merits also means you could hypothetically go back to them and diversify or rank up a merit to specialize. If preventing people from just buying everything is a problem, you might set a limit to kith specializations to wyrd level or make a compounding cost formula (each kith specialization takes two more experience, no matter what tier it is from).
