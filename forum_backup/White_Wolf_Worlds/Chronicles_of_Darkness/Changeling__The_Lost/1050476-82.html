            Fetches have a specific flaw as per their five questions. I'd just assume that they're otherwise functionally human as their disguise is so good even the fetch tends to be fooled by it. That's way more powerful than the Mask of changelings that changelings themselves can't percieve without making an effort. Which is fitting since they were specifically created by some of the setting's most powerful creatures to blend in with humans.
            What a fetch is made of is stated to factor into their personalities, but it doesn't inform anything about their bodies. A scaled up doll can still chew and swallow food even though their mouth is literally sewn or painted on their face, and a dead wolf stuffed with rocks still only deals -1B if they were to bite anyone with their fanged jaws (unless they have something like Lethal Mien to represent their jaws as an advantage). Just look at the fetch on page 232 (whose human guise really looks like John Noble to me even though it's barely visible); there's no way he could fake any biological functions if taken at face value. It's obvious that his disguise is majorly backed up because even he should be possible to be fooled (and you're not tricked into thinking you're a human if you're incapable of ingesting any non-liquid food, and having liquids mostly just soaking your cloth skin).

            You can always add more flaws in their disguise than the one question if you want, but it's not necessary or assumed by the book.

                    How does the Flickering Hours contract actually work? What does it do exactly? What does it look like to other characters when they run into someone under the effect of the Contract? Does it have any effect on chases outside of the Fleet of Foot merit and the edge? If so, what effect does it have? If not, why not, it specifically points out being able to use it on people chasing you so it feels like it should? Yet there is no listed chase effect for having your time slowed, only one for having it sped up.

                    I'm assuming that speeding yourself up by a factor of 2 while slowing someone else down by a factor of 2 doesn't mean you get four times the actions they do should you somehow get into contact with them. But it's very unclear what the Contract actually does. It seems to try to limit itself to chases without clearly explaining what it actually accomplishes, but I'm having a hard time seeing how being quickened (if it's really related to time and not pathfinding) wouldn't have diverse other benefits.

                    "anyone quickened also gains..." => are the quickened people those for whom time passes twice as fast, or those for whom time has been slowed by half? Assuming the Contract has no effect on what you can accomplish in a "turn", having time go half as fast for you means you can get twice as many things done right? So then you are "quickened"? That sounds like it makes sense to me, but it's hard to read the author's intent since they used "twice as quickly" in the preceding sentence, which evokes a sort of link with the word "quickened", casting doubt on my conclusion.

                    If you had several people in a group, and you chose to speed up time for some of them and slow it down for others, could they continue to travel together normally without noticing anything, or is one part of the group speeding away at 4 times the speed of the other part then? If so, that is not sufficiently covered mechanically with just the rather weak fleet of foot merit and the edge right? If not, why not, what does having your time sped up actually do then?
