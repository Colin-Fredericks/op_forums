                                                11-15-2017, 02:27 PM
                                                I have some general feedback. It's mixed, but it always will be in the first draft, and I'm still digging into it, so I'll likely find more stuff I like as time goes by.

                                                Stuff I Like

                                                -That Bridge Burners now are targeting the things that lure the Gentry in, and, indeed, that those things do lure the Gentry in. Dreams, hope, creativity, etc., are now all dangerous. Bridge Burners being willing to cauterize the good parts of life to get rid of the painful ones is both a very understandable motivation and yet incredibly horrific. This dual aspect that all beauty also carries risk, even in mundane reality, plays really nicely into Changeling.

                                                -The Wyrd is now about balancing the equations of life, and it's that aspect of give and take that I like the most. While I've always sort of quibbled over Changeling 1e having a feeling of everything being out to take from you and give you little in return, the idea that the Wyrd always tries to balance things (not necessarily equally) does add the idea that, not only does every gain require a loss, but every loss will bring some measure of gain. It's a slightly hopeful note that I can see the Lost holding onto.

                                                -The Spring Courtier responding to a Huntsman with "Do I know you?" and it cutting like a knife, hot damn. The Seasonal Courts in general having a focus on why and how they keep the Gentry away is pretty great.

                                                -The structure of Kith Blessings are neat. Easier exceptionals are great.

                                                Stuff I Don't Like

                                                -Seeming Blessings. There's a bunch of problems here, as far as I see it. I can't tell what narrative role they're meant to fulfill. Are they meant to be small little utility tricks that just let the players show off aesthetically, or are they supposed to embed them thoroughly into their role? Is this just a neat little trick, or is this a real power?

                                                There's also some unevenness as far as accessibility goes. A Darkling can evaporate into smoke, mist, a sunbeam, a shadow, whatever. But an Elemental can only move their element; something that usually isn't an issue for one aspected to earth or air, but what about water? Fire? What about non-traditional elements, like smoke? Electricity? Surprise?

                                                Likewise, there seemed to be some move towards more conceptual freedom that some of these abilities walk back. Wouldn't it be more appropriate for an Ogre to be able to inflict a Beaten Down Tilt via social actions too? Then you could play someone who verbally beats someone into submission with blunt words; as much the abuser and vicious shark of a creature as a violent brute, but they just bruise the heart, not the flesh.

                                                Also, while we're at it, as a matter of personal taste, something I liked was the idea of Changelings being able to empower themselves by acting like the Gentry; hungrily feeding off of induced emotions to fuel their powers like an emotional brush fire. That some of these abilities use Willpower but not others, ostensibly for balance reasons, feels like it hurts that feeling. But who knows? Maybe Changelings can regain WP by feeding, which would fix this.

                                                And can Darklings move while merged with an object? A bit unclear there.

                                                -And on the subject of Seemings... Is the Fairest Blessing doing the right thing mechanically? And the Beast one, for that matter? If a Seeming's Blessing is meant to represent the feeling of that Seeming, I feel like a power that helps others shine in the spotlight sort of bucks the trend for a Seeming that is all about being in the spotlight. And what do claws and speed have to do with a freedom that comes with instinct? It's neat and all, but... It's also sorta dull.

                                                -Kiths. Are they optional? The book is unclear if you choose one automatically. And can we get a bit more variety? Thus far, they're mostly social. I'd like a sneaky Kith or two, and I'm sure others might like a few more violent ones. Really, it feels like the limitations here are going to hurt the line, but I expect we'll get a bit more, so...

                                                -And on the topic of Kiths... Hunterhearts. Why not make their bonus to all tracking, not just fae tracking?

                                                ---

                                                I guess the summary is that, mechanically, I've got some worries, but I'm fairly happy with the direction of the fluff thus far. I'm going to reserve total judgement for the Hedge section, though, which will make or break my feelings on the line.
