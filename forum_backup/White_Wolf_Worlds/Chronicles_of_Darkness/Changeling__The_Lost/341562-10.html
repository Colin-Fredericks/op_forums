    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    In either case, I don't quite see the link between Contracts and Seemings. That could be partly because the idea that Seemings be related to how you escape has led to my mind almost entirely divorcing them from the physical and metaphysical attributes assigned to them in 1e. An Ogre isn't an "ogre", just someone who used violence to get away. It might indicate a proclivity towards certain Contracts over others, but that's about it.

    Really, I think what it indicates is that I actually need to read the new Seeming write-ups to figure out what their newly defined meanings actually equate to in the context of the game's universe before I can make any judgement on whether or not the Contract limitations are appropriate.
    I'm still kinda feeling things out myself, admittedly, and further elaboration and examples would be very welcome. However, I wouldn't presume that an Ogre is "just" someone who used violence to get away. David's made some comments about "the mythology of the Durance,"* and how the Seeming describes an individual regaining agency after having lost it. Kith seems to reflect what the character was made to be in a tale, while Seeming reflects what the character made of herself. If a Changeling in part reflects his Durance's mythology, then surely the action that ended it should leave an impact. It's a powerful act, taking back one's agency; it's not unreasonable the Wyrd might respond in a significant way. Perhaps a sort of, "You've declared that this is what you are, and so this shall you be," thing.

    * Would adore hearing more about this, by the way. :3 It was likened to how other 2e books are building options into the origin story that reflect character elements, like a Werewolf's pack or a Vampire's Touchstones.**

        I guess what I mean is more that Seeming has come to mean something personal, intrinsic to your character, and fundamentally linked to your character's agency, but in doing so doesn't seem to hold much weight anymore in the realm of how that expresses itself in your being a mythological creature (although tying it into the mythology of the Durance, as mentioned, could go a long way towards sorting that out). It's a great concept and I love it, but it leaves me feeling like Seemings aren't all that related to the magical side of being a Changeling, making the decision to limit Contracts by Seeming a little odd. An Ogre used to be an actual fairytale Ogre, to some extent, but a Changeling being an ogre now seems part of their Kith while their Seeming is more indicative of whether or not they actually act like an Ogre.

        That said, the above is purely my initial reaction based on cliff notes, and I'm sure the actual write-ups will lend a great deal more physical weight to the Seeming than my initial reading indicated. Tying "how you gained agency" into "what you look like/the kind of magic you manifest" is naturally a concept that is hard to carry without the full write-ups, I feel.

            Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
            I think in this case, the trick is to not play 1e Changelings with 2e games.
            My post was retaining a purely 1e sensibility. The fact that vampires and werewolves seem to be getting a significant "toughness" increase in 2e doesn't help the impression, but we haven't seen what 2e changelings are bringing to the table yet either.

            Even in 1e, all vampires can heal non-agg reflexively and downgrade some sources of damage to bashing, all werewolves have increased healing and access to forums with increased combat stats, all mages had access to shielding spells and anyone could start with enough Life for self healing at a minimum, Prometheans and sin-eaters can revive post death on top of being fairly tough, etc.

            Changelings didn't get any innate boosts to survivability like that, and the main healing powers were locked behind being Spring Court or very limited in the form of goblin fruits.

            Originally posted by Azahul View Post
            By the end of my long running Changeling campaign, I had a Wyrd 8 Elemental player stacking Contracts of Elements and Stone (and Dual Kith Gargantuan). "Squishy" quickly stopped being something I associated with Changelings.
            The issue was never that you couldn't make tough changelings really. The problem was you had to really build a character for it. A combat focused changeling that's been in a campaign long enough to hit Wyrd 8 is nothing to sneeze at. The issue is more than a most of the other splats at power stat 8 have a large number of innate powers/low level powers boosted highly that even non-combat characters would be benefiting from.

                Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                Changelings didn't get any innate boosts to survivability like that, and the main healing powers were locked behind being Spring Court or very limited in the form of goblin fruits.
                Goblin fruit is limited in that access depends entirely up to the GM and in the fact that the game gives no advice at all on how to hand out the fruit in a fun way. If access is easy though, goblin fruit is remarkably cost free healing. It is just a tad too slow to use in combat. Out of combat you can savely assume a Changeling will be fully healed within minutes after gaining access to the right hollow/NPC

                Of course, their biggest strength is in subtility. Keeping them out is very difficult and they have some of the best protections against detection by mortals in the world. At the same time they can invade people's dreams (talk about security risk) or lead others into the Hedge with no hope of escape (Mage excepted perhaps).As a Vampire and a Werewolf I would be very hesitent to anger the local Changeling populace. Sure, killing individuals once found is easy, but I am sure as hell going to get burned indirectly

                Besides, how many people run mixed campaigns? Is the intent of 2e to allow GMs to run such campaigns with ease? Because ultimately, balance between the various supernaturals only matter when different supernaturals sit down at the same table...
