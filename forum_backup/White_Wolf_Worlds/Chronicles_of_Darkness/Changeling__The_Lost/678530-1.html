        The rules in regards to the True Fae are fairly light in 2E(Like, serious mechanics for them are summed up in a paragraph), and in their lightness there's real brutality. It really is intended to keep them in the background and terrifying when they take center stage. There's a reason we have Huntsmen(who are also brutal in the sharpest, most conniving ways possible-but they are manageable).

        Stuff like Equinox Road's logic can apply, there's no reason for it not to, but the game's about fairy tales, and never has any fairy played fair unless it was fun to do so(usually in service to their hubris).

        Consider, for a second, the simple act of stealing a person and turning them into a changeling to begin with. A mortal can take advantage of their Frailities as much as anyone else-certainly years of them going at it has left the tools lying around somewhere. Even if it doesn't, mortals can learn from observation what those are. They still can process information from a Domain to figure a way out. The answers are there.

        But would you ever call the act of kidnapping people fair? Would you ever call a Durance fair?

        Course not. The True Fae do what changelings do-they stack the deck in their favor as best they can, and they have a great many cards to play. Changelings might have the ability to hide cards in their sleeves where the True Fae don't, but the True Fae know every card so well that they probably don't need to. That's not to say that loopholes don't exist-changelings break free after all. That's not to say you can't break the game-changelings kill True Fae time from time. But if you think you're outside the game and it turns out you're not, gods help you.

        It's like tossing someone into a game of DnD and expecting someone to win against the dragon without telling them what to do. Everything you need is there, theoretically. Pay close attention and work it out, you might do it it. You learn the game by watching how others play it, you explore your options, maybe you get the skill checks, figure out that, hey, there's a hole in the monster's breast, and finally stab the hell out of it with a spear. But it'd be easier if someone let you at least read the books first, but nope, the DM's an asshole. You can figure out the rules as you play.

        That's the True Fae. They have their games, they have their rules, they have their weaknesses. They play in accordance to them, always, at pain of oblivion, but they never, ever play them fair.

        EDIT: So next time a True Fae comes in and has to play in accordance to every damn Approach you have, take some time to laugh at them. Where they can't hear you, of course.

        And then you should murderkill them with iron before they get the chance to make your life miserable.

            I'm on the tail end of Equinox Road in my readthrough, so I think it's all right for me to chime in here as regards 1e Fae:

            All the stuff about struggle is in the section talking about Legends and the limits of manifestation — Realms can't be literally inescapable or merciless deathtraps or even unacceptably inconvenient, Props can't have unknowable weaknesses or primary powers that cover every facet of their use, Actors and Wisps have finite stats and dicepools within a scope typically slightly above that of a powerful PC's range — it's a note that the Fae can never be omnilaterally superlative and that their traps and defenses are fallible; the Wild Hunt is an explicit means of feeding the need for narrative conflict, and a Fae in Realm form literally holds the power of life and death over their slaves.

            The force that drives the Fae forbids them from turning every conflict into a juggernaut roll or a clash of unstoppable-force-meets-immovable-object. It means the tools to thwart them at any given time exist, not that they're all within immediate reach.

                        Although I like Equinox Roads a lot, I felt like the book fell down a bit when it came to the mechanics for Actor manifestations. They're basically just powerful Changelings with a few minor differences (e.g. Immortal Flesh, Ruled by Passion, ability to change Kiths, flexible Contracts). Not nearly as cool as the rules (particularly Mien Blessings) from Autumn Nightmares, in my opinion. And The example Other in the book, The Baroness of Tortured Harmonies, possesses 3 special traits (similar to what Grim Fears calls Fae Aspects), but, when discussing creating Actors, ER doesn't even mention that they might possess other traits like that, much less provide a list of them to use or advice on creating your own.

                        I always looked at the various versions of the Gentry from 1e as a set of options to choose from, rather than revisions intended to usurp the earlier iterations. Especially since Equinox Roads references Autumn Nightmares and the core and tells you to use whichever version works best for the story you're telling (see "Variations on the Theme" after The Baroness's description in Chapter 4). Then there's the fact that Grim Fears, which came out just after ER, seems to ignore both Autumn Nightmares and Equinox Roads and presents True Fae in nearly the same format as the core book (with the exception of calling their innate powers Fae Aspects). In my games, I like to use most of the rules from ER, but create Actors more along the lines of Autumn Nightmares with a touch of Grim Fears here and there.

                        Even if you're taking Equinox Road as the definitive authority on the True Fae, it still leaves open the vague possibility of weaker True Fae, since an Actor can grow weaker if it is on Earth for awhile (like the Exiled). There's no mechanics presented, but I think it's fair to guess that this could reduce their Wyrd below 5. These are still True Fae, but they could be considerably weaker. I also feel like an Actor formed from a Title that's Dwindling could be considerably weaker, but that's just a pet theory. It could provide for an interesting story where Changelings actively attempt to frustrate a Fae's attempts to forge Legends somehow, so that they can weaken an Actor enough to destroy it. There's also the possibility of players facing an Actor that's popped up as an independent being after being lost to Dwindling by one of the Others. Such a creature probably thinks it's a "real" True Fae, and I don't see why the players wouldn't as well.

                        I don't see any reason to retcon away Blue Jenny's Keeper from the venerable demo. She could be a True Fae that's already significantly weakened in some way (Exiled, Dwindled, hobbled by some sort of pledge that weakens her significantly in the mortal world, already injured for some reason, etc.). Besides, even if she's defeated, she's only "killed", not killed. Side note: Weirdly, my copies of the quickstart don't actually list her Wyrd score at all.

                                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                        There's more in this than might be thought. Let me break into it.

                                        1)True, not everyone has all the supplements, and the standard, right now, is the first edition core book, which does not stipulate a Wyrd level, though examples are fairly high in there.

                                        2)Strictly speaking still, the game's assumption within canon is that a Stranger's incarnations are immensely powerful. But as you say, there's not a clear line of explanation in just the core alone.

                                        3)As always, a GM has the right to just do what they want.

                                        4)Changeling has various levels of authority when it comes to Stranger creation, with the core, Autumn Nightmares, and Equinox Road all positing different things and the latter two written with the assumption of surplanting the core books' rules.. Equinox Road is the most defined and comprehensive, and so if we ignore the core book base assumption, it's the clearest authority, and that books says the minimum a True Fae can even be is Wyrd 5, and they have to be in a shitty, shitty place for that to be the case. The way this book is written is to strongly suggest it to be the default canon as soon as one owns it, which is creates a kerfuffle for the entire "Only the core book is considered needed" attitude.

                                        5)Certainly within fandom discussion that uses canon as a centerpole for discussion and the basis for deviation, Equinox Road is treated as the authority above the core. Any conversation within the fandom quickly shifts attention over to that book.

                                        6)Changeling Second Edition has a very....opdefinite stance about this, with an emphasis on making them the big bad in the background, more menacing as an atmospheric element than an active piece on the board. I can't explain what exactly this stance is in mechanical terms, so I will simply say this- unless you are pretty damn powerful yourself or have a really damn good plan or argument for your freedom(and a way to get that argument out), your default answer when dealing with them should always fall on the side of running the hell away. You can fight a Hobgoblin, a Fetch, a Loyalist, or even a Huntsman, but only legends and lucky kids get away with confronting the True Fae when they move.

                                        TL; DR, First Edition is a kerfuffle that heavily relies on the idea that True Fae are powerful but doesn't necessarily enforce it, Second Edition does not have weak Strangers. All of this can be ignored if you're willing to ignore canon, which is certainly the right of any play group.
                                        Are there any open development posts about the true faerie and their mechanics by now?

                                                        Thing of the matter is that Dave said that if I wanted to, I could go ahead and toss it up. I actually have the power to do that.

                                                        But I'm not going to for a few reasons.

                                                        1) It's the True Fae, man, that should totally be toted out by the Dev himself, you know what I'm saying? Wouldn't be cool if I did it.

                                                        2) I have to think long and hard about what's going on with open playtests and what we're trying to test. The average game, as this point, shouldn't have a True Fae present-it's enough that you know that they linger in the background like a towering doom. What players should be testing against is the Huntsmen and other changelings, seeing how the character mechanics work for players and storyteller. By keeping them out of the picture, we incentivize more changelings, more experimentation and playing around with the player mechanics, and in particular get feedback from storyteller on how it works from the other side of the wall. By keeping them out of the picture, we're more likely to get feedback on the Huntsmen in play, if for no other reason than people will grudgingly use them. Introduce the True Fae, and we lose a really good opportunity to get more information. It's not much of a thing, but it's important for keeping the playtests on track with the goals of the playtests.

                                                        3) Even if I did put them up, I'm not sure how much use the players would get out of it. The True Fae are....

                                                        Well, I'm not going to tell you low-powered changelings can't beat them. They can. Is it hard? Oh yeah, but the way the True Fae are setup, that curve exists even if you're a high powered changeling, and high powered games are actually not a norm for most gamers. It's possible to have a fun game with the True Fae present, and even win.

                                                        But I honestly think that if you guys could look at the True Fae's mechanics, you'd leave them in the background. I think there is a real chance that the response is "Oh shit, do not touch." On a milder approach to it, I'm just not sure what the average group of players would do with them. Not in the sense of I can't imagine uses, but in that I have trouble seeing groups going ahead with those uses. It gives me enough pause that I don't feel bad about not being the one to pull the trigger on that.

                                                        The wheels are turning behind the scenes, and while I don't know when the playtests'll be done, it doesn't strike me as implausible that very soon some of my opinions about the timing of the True Fae's reveal will change. Because, yeah, we do want you people to playtest these guys too. There's a time for them, though.

                                                        I think that hour is sooner than I think.
