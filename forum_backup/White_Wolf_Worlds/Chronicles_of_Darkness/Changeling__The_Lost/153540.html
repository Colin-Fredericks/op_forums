    Kismet's Oneiromancy Alterations

    05-30-2014, 01:25 AM
    I've been working on revamping many parts of the dream rules based on how our group plays and some basic guiding principles. There are some aspects I haven't changed at all, such as the Intensity bonuses for the different types of dreams. There are some supplementary rules from later books that we'll be adding, like the special rules for mages and dream Intensity. I'll endeavor to explain the rationale behind the things I've changed or added, which become more obvious and numerous later on.

    ​I'm interested in constructive feedback, but since we've already been playtesting some of this, I'm posting this for discussion and some outside opinion rather than trying to convince anyone else to use it. If something is faulty or there's a possibility I haven't foreseen, I'll be glad to think it over and make further alterations.

    This is all drawn from a giant dreaming reference I'm making for the use of my group, so there are abbreviations and such. I hope you enjoy it. =)

    Changelings do not need to be in the Hedge or a Hollow to enter dreams, nor do they suffer from seeming curses on dream rolls of any kind.
    We've never restricted dreamwork to the Hedge, since our group does a LOT of dreamwork no matter where they are.
    Further, it seemed like a fun idea to me that changelings get to be what they imagine themselves to be in dreams, and that it's the one place they can leave their curses behind.

    Intensity of Dream: At the onset, roll Wits + Resolve + Supernatural Advantage + miscellaneous bonuses – penalties for loud noises and other distractions nearby.
    We've reasoned that supernatural creatures generally have more intense dreams (which provides them a modicum of basic protection).

    Vampires: While in torpor, vampires who have memory dreams roll Resolve + Composure with a penalty equal to 10 – Morality. If the rolls, the dream is a twisted version of the memory. On a dramatic failure, the altered memory supplants the true one.
    Werewolves: Gibbous Moon auspice werewolves can induce prophetic dreams in themselves; add their Renown to the Intensity.
    Mages: Mages can choose to add or subtract Gnosis from the Intensity of their own dreams.
    Since we've started to get tangled in dreams of vampires and such, I want to add these rules from the book I found them in.

    Miscellaneous bonuses to Intensity: Ambien (+1 die), B complex vitamins (+1 die), derangement or Condition (+1 die), hallucinogens (+2 dice), meditation (+2 dice).
    Because yeah, some things at least seem to affect how vividly you dream, so we've added bonuses for them. I'm also planning to take penalties into account more often due to discomfort and environmental factors. Many things can keep you from falling under deeply.

    Entering your Own Dreams: No roll or expenditure needed; can do so at will.
    Entering your Fetch’s Dream: 1 Willpower; no roll for entry; Storyteller rolls changeling's Presence + Wyrd each time changeling invades their fetch's dreams. Target number is the fetch's WP; once equalled or exceeded, fetch can learn to spend WP to haunt their changeling.
    Entering a Dream Pledge: 1 Glamour; no roll for entry.
    We found the rolling system to enter dreams tedious from the start. Even though we understand that it's supposed to reflect the difficulty of crossing into a dreamscape and how much time it takes to get there, over time it made less sense for us. If a dream is going to get interrupted, it's going to get interrupted, and that's ST fiat. If it takes too long to get into and the dreamer wakes up, again, ST fiat. But we figure, if you have an inherent connection or one forged by a pledge, you pay the cost and you're in.

      Dreamscaping involves interacting with a dream within a sleeper’s mind and consists of three basic categories of maneuvers: analysis, manipulation, and healing. Unless specified otherwise, an oneiromancer can only focus on one maneuver per night, and a dreamer can only benefit or suffer from one dreamscaping ability per night. An oneiromancer can engage the dreamer directly and socially without using a special maneuver, but no extra benefit will come out of the interaction (though the dreamer is quite likely to remember much of what was said).
      The first major move I'm making is to restructure what dreamscaping is and how it is divided up. There seemed to be natural divisions suggested in the types of maneuvers presented in the core book. We've also had characters speak to dreamers directly without really changing anything, which has regularly borne interesting fruits, so we wanted the rules to reflect that.

      Oneiromancers who are very successful in their work are liable to leave deep impressions on the dreamer. A low-Intensity dream can be overwritten by something much more powerful, but the dreamer is likely to recall the details upon waking and with an exceptional success, the dreamer might even have an idea of who’s responsible, whether they saw the oneiromancer or not (see specific bonuses and penalties below).
      While we understand why dreams might come apart from being messed with by an outside party, we did not enjoy how easy it was to tear the dream apart and have to spend Willpower to keep it together. It seemed to penalize changelings who are good at dreamwork, which got frustrating all around. My players wanted their characters to be good at it without having to hold back all the time, and I didn't blame them.

      While changelings can analyze, perform basic alterations, and scour the intensity of their own dreams, they cannot perform any other dream maneuvers for themselves, so changelings do it for each other.
      This remains the same, and motleys retain their value for helping each other in these ways.

      Changelings who dreamscape while sleeping in the Hedge or in a Hollow gain a +1 bonus. If the target dreamer is also sleeping in the Hedge or a Hollow, the bonus is +2, total.
      Dreamscaping might not be restricted to the Hedge in these rules, but being in that realm helps oneiromancy all around. The bonus applies to all dreamscaping maneuvers.

          Dream analysis maneuvers do not change aspects of the ongoing dream in any appreciable way. They are observational, and while an oneiromancer might be able to interact with the dreamer to a limited extent, too much interference will get in the way of being able to read the dream as it stands.
          This is the first new major category of dreamscaping, which gathers together the investigative maneuvers. Accordingly, the rolls are based on Intelligence rather than Wits, as analytic maneuvers tend to be based in Intelligence in the nWoD system.

          Analyze Dream: Intelligence + Empathy + Wyrd – Intensity to recognize the type of dream. Storyteller rolls the Intensity and adds any successes to the player's results if the oneiromancer is analyzing a prophetic dream.
          The last part is something we'll be trying out, going with the idea that true prophetic dreams stand out.

          Learn about the Dreamer: Intelligence + Empathy + Wyrd – Intensity. Each roll takes all night. Normally, every five successes allows you to learn one aspect of your choice: Virtue, Vice, permanent Willlpower rating, derangements or Conditions (one per 5 successes), Merits (Mental and Social only), Flaws (Mental and Social only), or any mental or emotional supernatural power used on dreamer within the last month. However, it only takes three successes to learn the following information: Virtue and Vice from wish fulfillment dreams; permanent Willpower rating from memory dreams; derangements or Conditions from nightmares; Merits and Flaws from recurring memory dreams; and supernatural powers used on the dreamer from recurring dreams.
          I wanted to integrate the different types of dreams further and this seemed like an ideal way to do that without overbalancing anything. It should also give motleys ways to learn about each other more easily.

          Search for Dream Poison: Wits + Empathy or Occult + Wyrd – Intensity. Each roll takes all night and requires a number of total successes equal to the Intensity of the contagion-dream. Oneiromancers can use this technique to search for other types of intruders in dreams, making the same roll vs. the intruder’s Wits or Manipulation + Stealth or Subterfuge + Wyrd – Intensity roll to hide or blend into the scene. Attacking an intruder leads to dream battle (see below) and is not handled through standard dreamscaping maneuvers.
          Since there are dream creatures, other changelings, and other such intruders that can venture into one's dreams, it seemed like a good idea to determine a way to look for them. Also, later on I add some complications which mean that more than one changeling can be in someone's dream at a time, so there's more to keep an eye out for in one's dreams.

              Dream manipulation: The second category of dreamscaping involves direct manipulation of a dream’s content, whether or not it is for the dreamer’s good. The oneiromancer will rewrite and overcome the natural plot and look of an ongoing dream (though oneiromancers often try to use the symbols they find to make their work seem less obvious or distressing).
              The main change to these maneuvers involves the rolls required. General alterations remain the standard Wits + Empathy + Wyrd, but social manipulation reverts to Manipulation, and teaching becomes an Intelligence action. Siphoning is added here because changelings so often twist dreams so they can get the most Glamour out of them.

              Alter Dream: Wits + Empathy + Wyrd – Intensity to make general changes. Gain 9 again when recreating elements from memory. These alterations can be impressive, but they are purely cosmetic and do not grant the creator or dreamer any bonuses or penalties. You can make general alterations and perform one other dream maneuver in the same night.
              The PCs in my games often recreate things they know so well from the waking world that it makes sense they'd have an easier time of it.

              Convince the Dreamer: Manipulation + Persuasion + Wyrd – Intensity to implant one subliminal message in the dreamer’s mind. When he encounters a trigger in real life he subconsciously reacts to it. Every success adds 1 die to Manipulation rolls when using that trigger on the dreamer in the waking world. You decide how many dice to use at a time, till they are used up or a week passes. Each dreamer can only have one subliminal message at a time, and each one lasts a week.
              Didn't need to change this one at all.

              Nightmares: Manipulation + Intimidation + Wyrd – Intensity. The oneiromancer can give such horrible nightmares to the dreamer that, if the result is equal or greater than the dreamer's permanent Willpower, they won't regain any Willpower from resting that evening. If the oneiromancer gains an exceptional success on a nightmare, they will suffer a -1 social penalty with the dreamer for 24 hours afterwards, as the dreamer is beset with a vague uneasiness or the sense that the character means them harm.
              The last part has to do with the mention above that doing an exceptional job on dreamwork can catch the dreamer's attention (whether they understand why or not). Just because you didn't tear the dream apart with your success doesn't mean there won't be some consequences.

              Siphoning Glamour: Composure + Wits + Wyrd – Intensity to harvest from a dream that has been unchanged, or the oneiromancer makes the appropriate roll (as determined by the type of action) + Wyrd – Intensity to change the dream as they see fit. Each success is a point of Glamour gained by the oneiromancer.
              Didn't need to change this one at all.

              Sleep Teaching: Intelligence + appropriate mental or social Skill + Wyrd – Intensity. The teacher must possess the Skill, Merit (Storyteller discretion), or Contract in question and cannot teach above their own ability level. The dreamer dreams about practicing skills in his sleep. One night of Dream Teaching is equivalent to a day of study. A critical success grants the dreamer a free experience point (or Beat) that can only be spent on the Skill, Merit, or Contract being taught. Can only have one point or Beat in storage per skill; once it is raised, another can be taught.
              This just needed a bit more clarification regarding what the teacher can teach.

                  Dream healing maneuvers are entirely beneficial for the dreamer, whether they realize it consciously or not. If an oneiromancer does exceptionally well with a healing maneuver, they will gain a +1 bonus to social interactions with the dreamer for the next 24 hours as the dreamer has the sense that the character means them well.
                  This is the positive counterpart to making nightmares that are too good; if you help someone in their dreams, it'll last a little while after in their attitude toward you. As with the last category, basic rolls have largely been changed. While I see the neatness in making everything based on Wits + Empathy, it's never felt right or made much sense to me. All changelings can engage in dreamscaping but not all of them will be good at every type of maneuver.

                  It also seemed like being able to calm and heal others would come from being able to remain calm yourself, while scouring and stress relief would rely more on your resolve to see it done.

                  Healing Sleep: Intelligence + Wits or Medicine + Wyrd – Intensity. The dreamer is put into an unusually deep restful sleep that allows the body to heal at a faster rate. Each night of Healing Sleep is equivalent to a day of rest; an exceptional success equals 2.
                  While healing someone in their dreams can be based on just a raw desire to help (Wits), there's no reason someone with real medical experience couldn't use it.

                  Psychotherapy: Manipulation + Empathy + Wyrd – Intensity. Each roll takes all night. Each night equals a week of normal psychotherapy.
                  Scour the Intensity: Wits + Empathy + Wyrd – Intensity. Done to reduce nightmares or get rid of dream poison. Each success lowers the Intensity of the dream by one. If the Intensity is reduced below 1, the dream ends. An oneiromancer can keep the dream together by spending a Willpower point, but it will remain at low Intensity and no other maneuvers will be possible. To scour dream poison, the roll has no penalty, but is opposed by a roll of the Intensity of dream-poison. Each success above the poison reduces its Intensity by 1. If the contagion wins, the oneiromancer takes 1 point of Willpower damage per success.
                  Stress Relief: Manipulation + Subterfuge or Persuasion + Wyrd – Intensity. If the dreamer’s Virtue and Vice are known, the oneiromancer can create a dream that will bolster the dreamer's sense of self. Normal success allows the dreamer to recover a point of Willpower as if he'd fulfilled a Vice. An exceptional success allows recovery of all Willpower as if fulfilling a Virtue.
                  This is going with the idea that you're either trying to trick the mind into feeling stronger, or you're trying to persuade the person that they are awesome.

                      Dreamweaving is the art of putting a dream inside of an object. Anyone who sleeps in contact with the dream vessel will experience the dream and its effect. Healing sleep, Stress Relief, Sleep Teaching, and Nightmares can be put into a dream vessel, as well as the powers listed below. It is rumored that certain Entitlements teach techniques to install other powers in dream vessels, as well. All creatures who dream can benefit from a dream vessel, not just beings who are naturally bound up in the Wyrd (thus, usability extends beyond changelings, fetches, and pledgebound mortals).
                      In our games, if you sleep with a dream vessel and can dream, it has a chance to activate for you. You don't have to be fae in any sense of the word, which is part of why dream vessels are so very valuable. I wanted to have that spelled out for the future because as the group meets different types of creatures, no doubt it will come up. In the book, frustratingly, one sentence says that "any dreamer may experience the dream," but the very next sentence restricts it to "any changeling, fetch or human bound in a dreaming-tasked pledge."

                      To make a dream vessel, first you must find an object that is associated with the type of dream bound within it. Then, spend 1 Glamour and make an extended Intelligence + Empathy + Wyrd roll to determine the Intensity of the dream. Each success represents half an hour of effort, and only one roll can be made per day. Then, roll for whatever effects you wish the dream to have. Only one effect roll is allowed per vessel. If the dreamer doesn't know the vessel is the source of the dreams, they resist reflexively with Resolve + Composure + Supernatural Advantage.
                      My group has asked for clarification on how long it actually takes to weave a dream vessel. Sure, you only get one roll per day, but how many minutes or hours in that day are filled with that one task? Changelings in my games tend to be quite busy and they don't engage in day-long tasks lightly. We've seen no reason that weaving a dream vessel should be the only thing you get to do that day.

                      Wyrd Dream: A Wyrd dream allows the use of Contracts (other than Dream) on the dreamer through a dream vessel. Only Contracts that can target people other than the user can be used. After the Intensity of the dream has been determined, the normal roll for the Contract is used. If that roll is too weak, let the Glamour in the vessel fade and try again at a -1 penalty. Either the Contract activation roll or the Intensity of the dream will affect the dreamer, whichever is lowest. If a resistance roll is allowed, those successes are subtracted from the Contract’s activation as normal.
                      Personalize Dream: If the dreamer is very well-known, through having learned at least two traits about the dreamer through dream analysis, a dream vessel can be made specifically for them. The final Intensity is increased by the oneiromancer's dots in Empathy or Wyrd, whichever is greater – but only for that dreamer.
                      Analyze Dream Vessel: If a changeling suspects that an object is a dream vessel, she can analyze the object. An extended Wits + Composure + Wyrd roll is required, difficulty of the dream vessel’s Intensity. Each roll takes 10 minutes, and a changeling can only roll as many times as their Wyrd rating per dream vessel. This reveals whether or not an object is a dream vessel, but it does not reveal the type of dream stored inside.
                      I added a stipulation on how many attempts you get to determine if an item is a dream vessel, based on how strong you are with the Wyrd. It feels right.

                          Changelings can engage each other, fetches, Incubi, Morpheans, lucid dreamers, or the Fae in dream combat. This violence is outside of any symbolic nightmare and can go on until a winner emerges; while oneiromachy does not count as a dream maneuver like those outlined above, it will interrupt efforts to perform a nightly dream maneuver. Any dreamer that has a battle inside their own dream automatically loses a point of Willpower from the trauma of having such wild Wyrd forces thrown about.
                          I didn't feel the need to change much about dream battle; I just wanted to specify who is capable of actually engaging in proper dream combat, taking into account the creatures of later books. Non-lucid dreamers cannot be engaged in this special way, which requires the ability to respond in kind. They can play the stage for a dream battle between other lucid dreamers but cannot enter into it themselves. Not only does this make dream battle special, but it also prevents some abuses which could come up later on. It's nice to want to show up in a werewolf's dream and start beating the Willpower out of them, but changelings are not (yet) the True Fae and are not capable of that level of abuse.

                              Group Dreams: The Dreaming task of a pledge can allow for group interactions. As long as each person agrees, the task can allow all of the pledgebound to gather in one member’s dream (provided enough of them are oneiromancers). Further, a group does not have to specify a particular direction for guardianship. The pledge allows each member to guard the dreams of any other person within the pledge, so long as each of the pledgebound receives dream services within the time period everyone agrees to (thus, a motley can agree that everyone will receive at least one dream visit a week by someone else in the motley).
                              This is our major addition to the dreaming rules. Since early on in our time with Changeling, we have allowed group interactions in dreams through the motley pledge. We've also allowed for flexibility in who tends to whom; so long as everyone is serving and getting served, it counts.

                              We have also gone on extended group adventures in a dreamscape, and I wanted some part of the rules to reflect this. The most recent example was when the party met in one motley member's dream and were guided along the Skein to the dream of a catatonic child none of them had had the chance to befriend or pledge with. They suspected that one of their Keepers had visited the girl and left her in such a state, and since the doctor's count help her, they had to try. Within her admittedly unique dreamscape, they found the parts of her psyche in disarray after a monster (a True Fae) had swept through and damaged her mental landscape. Her Courage, Memory, Safety, and Hope (all personified) were missing and the changelings went about trying to find them. They didn't use dream maneuvers as above but made general changes (to themselves) to navigate and to convince the aspects of her battered psyche to work with them, come back together, and try again. The one maneuver they finally made was changing the dream to bury the open wound left behind from when the Keeper rended into her dreams to read the Wyrd. They knew they'd gotten somewhere when she came to not long after. This is the kind of fun we've had through this sort of thing, so yes, I want to encourage it.

                              Within pledgebound dreams, a group can act as a team in order to produce a dream maneuver or make general alterations in one member’s dreamscape. This most often applies within a motley, but it can also apply when one person in the group has a pledge to guard someone else’s dreams and needs help to hold up their end of the bargain (as with scouring particularly potent dream poison). The first step is for the group to choose a primary actor (usually the changeling with the most experience in the type of maneuver they want to attempt). The number of assistants allowed on the roll are limited by the primary actor’s Wyrd. Neither a group nor an individual can engage non-lucid dreamers in a dream battle, but if a fae intruder is found, each member of the team can act on their own in dream combat.
                              Having a pledge with someone makes dreamwork easier, so I wanted the rules to reflect that.

                              Outside of pledgebound dreams, a group that is bound to each other can meet in one member’s dream and Skeinwalk together to another destination (provided they have a way to access the Skein). This requires designating one guide to lead everyone along the Skein to the correct dream. A changeling guide can only lead as many others along the Skein as they have dots in Wyrd. Once within the target dream, the group can act as a team to affect the dreamer and/or engage intruders, but any success on a maneuver or battle greater than the Intensity of the dream threatens to unravel it. A Willpower point can be spent to keep it intact, but each member of the team can only spend Willpower once for that purpose.
                              You can get into dreams without a pledge via the Skein but that doesn't mean you belong there. While I got rid of the idea that too many successes will tear apart the dream for pledgebound dreamwork, I brought it back for this situation. Likewise, just because you can take others with you doesn't mean you can lead an army across the Skein into one person's dream space, so I added a limitation based on the guide's Wyrd rating, which seemed appropriate.

                                  The Other can weave a single use of a Contract into a favored mortal’s dream, investing a point of Glamour to let the mortal hold onto the Contract until using it on a chosen target. The mortal must make a degeneration check when they do so, however, using their Morality rating – the power level of the Contract just used. A mortal at Morality 7 who just unleashed a level 5 Contract would be committing a Morality 2 sin.
                                  The only real change I made to the rules for dream warping was here. The book says "The mortal must make a degeneration check as though he'd just committed a sin against a Morality rating of 7 - the Wyrd prerequisite of the Contract just used." And as many times as I read it, it never made a lick more sense, because Contracts don't have Wyrd prerequisites. So I figure, the more powerful the Contract that's bound up into a mortal, the worse it is for their sanity. It's simple, straightforward, and doesn't make my head ache when trying to read it.

                                      I like the Hedge still being an attractive place for changelings to manipulate dreams from, since both are closely tied to the Wyrd. As some alternative food for thought, dreamscaping from the Hedge could provide a 'free' glamour point for use in every dream (or two if the target dreamer is also asleep). So while manipulating dreams from the Hedge is more convenient for changelings, it doesn't necessarily make them better at it.

                                      Analyze Dream Intelligence + Empathy + Wyrd – Intensity to recognize the type of dream. Storyteller rolls the Intensity and adds any successes to the player's results if the oneiromancer is analyzing a prophetic dream.
                                      The last part is something we'll be trying out, going with the idea that true prophetic dreams stand out.
                                      Definitely prefer this mechanic as an alternative to players saying "we check to see if it's a prophetic dream before we do anything else, 'cause we don't wanna ruin it." Starting every dream sequence with a routine procedure is counter to the whimsical, surreal, and mysterious mood they should have.

                                      Search for Dream Poison: Intelligence + Empathy + Wyrd – Intensity. Each roll takes all night and requires a number of total successes equal to the Intensity of the contagion-dream. Oneiromancers can use this technique to search for other types of intruders in dreams, making the same roll vs. the intruder’s Wits or Manipulation + Stealth or Subterfuge + Wyrd – Intensity roll to hide or blend into the scene. Attacking an intruder leads to dream battle (see below) and is not handled through standard dreamscaping maneuvers.
                                      I would have the roll be Dexterity + Stealth or Manipulation + Subterfuge. Physical Skills are typically paired with Physical Attributes, and they can still matter to dreamers by way of muscle memory.

                                      Otherwise, I like the change away from using Wits + Empathy for everything. It gives changelings who've invested in other Skills a chance to do more things in dreams.

                                      Sleep Teaching: Intelligence + appropriate mental or social Skill + Wyrd – Intensity. The teacher must possess the Skill, Merit (Storyteller discretion), or Contract in question and cannot teach above their own ability level. The dreamer dreams about practicing skills in his sleep. One night of Dream Teaching is equivalent to a day of study. A critical success grants the dreamer a free experience point that can only be spent on the Skill, Merit, or Contract being taught. Can only have one point in storage per skill; once it is raised, another can be taught.
                                      And just to update this to GMC rules, I'd have it award a Beat.

                                      Psychotherapy: Composure + Empathy + Wyrd – Intensity. Each roll takes all night. Each night equals a week of normal psychotherapy.
                                      I think this should be Presence + Empathy. Maybe Manipulation. Typically, power or finesse Attributes are used to actively accomplish tasks. From an in-universe perspective, a charismatic therapist is more likely to make an impression on his patients than a cool-headed one.

                                      In our games, if you sleep with a dream vessel and can dream, it has a chance to activate for you. You don't have to be fae in any sense of the word, which is part of why dream vessels are so very valuable. I wanted to have that spelled out for the future because as the group meets different types of creatures, no doubt it will come up. In the book, frustratingly, one sentence says that "any dreamer may experience the dream," but the very next sentence restricts it to "any changeling, fetch or human bound in a dreaming-tasked pledge."
                                      Totally agreed. Letting non-fae use 'em opens up more storytelling possibilities. I could see a changeling planting dream vessels under his mortal family's pillows to turn them against his fetch, or under happier circumstances, weaving particular dreams for them upon request. ("Daddy, can you have me fly tonight? Please?" "I wanna have dinosaurs in mine!" D'aww.)

                                      I didn't feel the need to change much about dream battle; I just wanted to specify who is capable of actually engaging in proper dream combat, taking into account the creatures of later books. Non-lucid dreamers cannot be engaged in this special way, which requires the ability to respond in kind. They can play the stage for a dream battle between other lucid dreamers but cannot enter into it themselves. Not only does this make dream battle special, but it also prevents some abuses which could come up later on. It's nice to want to show up in a werewolf's dream and start beating the Willpower out of them, but changelings are not (yet) the True Fae and are not capable of that level of abuse.
                                      I agree that changelings shouldn't be able to invade the dreams of other supernatural creatures willy-nilly, as said creatures don't have a universal way to retaliate in kind. That said, I think it might be more interesting to let changelings invade a supernatural foe's dreams in exchange for a nasty mental Condition (like Madness or Delusional), which affects them whether or not they win the dream combat. So if a motley is desperate for an edge against a potent foe, they can gamble for one, but there'll be a price tag attached.

                                      In general, I think it's more interesting for powerful options to carry hefty consequences than it is to take them off the board. Before I read Dancers in the Dusk, I liked the idea of letting changelings enter any mortal's dreams by spending extra glamour (DitD, of course, did something way cooler).

                                      We have also gone on extended group adventures in a dreamscape, and I wanted some part of the rules to reflect this. The most recent example was when the party met in one motley member's dream and were guided along the Skein to the dream of a catatonic child none of them had had the chance to befriend or pledge with. They suspected that one of their Keepers had visited the girl and left her in such a state, and since the doctor's count help her, they had to try. Within her admittedly unique dreamscape, they found the parts of her psyche in disarray after a monster (a True Fae) had swept through and damaged her mental landscape. Her Courage, Memory, Safety, and Hope (all personified) were missing and the changelings went about trying to find them. They didn't use dream maneuvers as above but made general changes (to themselves) to navigate and to convince the aspects of her battered psyche to work with them, come back together, and try again. The one maneuver they finally made was changing the dream to bury the open wound left behind from when the Keeper rended into her dreams to read the Wyrd. They knew they'd gotten somewhere when she came to not long after. This is the kind of fun we've had through this sort of thing, so yes, I want to encourage it.
                                      Oh wow, that's totally awesome. Pretty classic 'journey into the mind' trope to meet personifications of those kinds of concepts. Every story I've read/watched that used it was a delight to follow, so I can only imagine how much fun it was for your players to interact with in a game.

                                      Really, that's one of the things which is just so cool about dream sequences. Internal thoughts and feelings literally shape the scene, so they're pretty much the ultimate vehicle for character exploration and development.


                                      Thumbs up on all the other changes I didn't comment on! Once I get around to running a Changeling game I'd love to use these.
