        Originally posted by espritdecalmar View Post
        Both Hedgespinning and Dreamweaving list their banking successes effect as a Paradigm Shift. Should it be treated as one in all cases? (Ie, you can only do so on an exceptional success, doing so imposes an emotional/Shift Condition, etc.) It seems like something of a special case, not altering the environment drastically but merely building up momentum for greater shifts down the line, and so I'm left wondering if they're placed where they are simply for the sake of reference in relation to the effects for which it will most likely be the most relevant.
        Yes. The cost of banking successes is based on the fact that paradigm shifts allow you to use all of the successes on the roll, dreamweaving is a delicate balancing act for which Shift Conditions have their own benefits, emotional Conditions come into play only once per scene regardless of how many Hedgespinning shifts you make, and the most likely things for a character to roll an exceptional success at concern things like kith blessings, entitlement boons, Inspiration, and the exploitation of other emotional Conditions.

                        Originally posted by Omichli View Post
                        Since Dreams are connected to the Hedge and Arcadia with the Dreaming Roads, is it possible for Eidolons and Props to be taken out of one's Dreams and become materialized as normal?
                        Eidolons and props can be encountered in the spaces along the Dreaming Roads around Bastions, where oneiromancy works, and the True Fae (and hobgoblins with particular Goblin Contracts and/or Dread Powers) can take them out of that context (the True Fae doing so being the sole source of baubles), but by default it can be safely assumed that dream-things won't leave the place they come from in a useful manner otherwise without Plot Devices being brought to bear.

                        Which is to say it's as possible as anything is in the Chronicles, but there's a trick to it and without such a trick you'll have as much luck actually stealing abstractions and intangibles in the mortal world.

                                Originally posted by Omichli View Post
                                So it's possible to get the Goblin Contract?
                                If a goblin will sell it to you, yes.

                                What could it be called?
                                Whatever that particular Goblin Contract is called. It is not a published power, but it is a described power of some types of goblin in both editions of the game.

                                And I can pick it up from the Goblin Market, right?
                                Again, yes, if somebody's selling it.

                                Worth noting that, as the design tips in the corebook state, Goblin Contracts tend to be more specific than Court or Arcadian Contracts, so you're more likely to find a Goblin Contract for mining bits of a person's self-confidence out of their dreams as glittering ores or harnessing a nightmare into a terrifying steed than you are to come across a general-purpose "take things out of dreams and keep them for real" power without a lot of legwork and/or big trades.

                                        It's healing bashing wounds. Comma placement groups it like this:

                                        The target sheds:
                                        • fatigue penalties,
                                        • Tilts relating to fatigue and temporary illnesses (such as the Sick or Poisoned Tilt), and
                                        • bashing wounds.


                                        When the game is talking about penalties caused by damage it uses the term "wound penalties," and effects that ignore them specify how long before the penalty applies again if the wound is not healed.

                                                Originally posted by Caelican View Post
                                                Have another Contract question, this one Whispers of Morning. Just curious about the limits of the Contract. So does weightless mean floating/flying for the duration?
                                                The 1e clause Whispers of Morning most directly emulates (down to having the same catch/Loophole and Glamour cost), Phantom Glory, specifies that the character can't walk on air but can walk on water; Tread Lightly, the first clause in the same Contracts of Separation as Phantom Glory comes from, explicitly doesn't allow the character to fly or even jump higher than normal, but does allow them to (as specific examples) run across snow without breaking through the surface, negate the terminal velocity upgrade to lethal damage from long falls, and climb surfaces that would otherwise be too fragile to support their weight, due to the clause allowing them to move across any surface as though they weighed only a few ounces.

                                                And I assume that the regular/material world can still be seen clearly but can you still use other Contracts, like Paralyzing Presence for instance, while ethereal?
                                                "Can't be touched or attacked (or touch or attack others) save by magical means" suggests to me that the answer is "yes."
