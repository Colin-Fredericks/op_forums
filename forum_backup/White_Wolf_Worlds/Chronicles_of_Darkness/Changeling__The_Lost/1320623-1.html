    Originally posted by Unahim View Post
    But you get an "additional" advantage.
    That's more of the same advantage.

    In normal rolls you have 1 advantage, which is the "success", and then you get "another" advantage at 5 successes.
    OK. Go look at the 2e extended action rules and then revise this then, because they clearly disagree with you that rolls were the number of successes matters means more successes is their own reward. For something close to combat, investigations and chases - which are a series of instant rolls that you need to get in sequence to 'win' like combat instead of a simple extended action - also have an extra effect for exceptional successes.

    Just like in the jumping example, where if you roll an exceptional success but it is just good enough to make it, nothing "extra" is suggested to happen, which goes against your claim they're always supposed to, even if you disagree that extra damage already fills that function.
    The jumping example means that you need an exceptional success to make the jump at all. The extra advantage is you make the jump solidly, instead of having ended up hanging on the ledge or landing on a fire-escape two stories down, because you missed the total distance but didn't fail outright. Or to put it the other way, you always get the additional advantage of not losing an action for an exceptional success: either because you get a second because the jump was easy, or by not having more actions to make up for falling short.

    Anyway, I'm not against giving extra effects for 5 successes in a fight, but as Meghan points out, it seems rather superfluous after dealing that much damage most of the time, and will bog action down as people now have to figure out a tilt to suddenly apply.
    Clearly you've never been in a 2e combat where either Armor or Regeneration mean that exceptional successes don't actually mean you did that much damage, and Tilts are extremely important for keeping those sorts of fights from dragging on forever.

    There's also 12 personal Tilts, but pretty much half of them are not going to come up from an exceptional successes. Punches without magical powers don't poison you, and knives don't just randomly drive you crazy. And if you're already making Conditions happen for normal exceptional successes, this isn't exactly more difficult.

                        An exceptional success grants a Condition that benefits the character whose player made the roll. The player may choose that Condition, or they may delegate to the Storyteller.

                        Inspired is there in case you don't have a better idea. You may therefore always choose Inspired on an exceptional success.
