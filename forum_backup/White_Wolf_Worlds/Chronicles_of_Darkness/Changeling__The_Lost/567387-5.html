                Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                I don't like the idea that you can only become an Elemental if the Durance allows it.
                That wasn't what I was saying at all. The Seeming, IMO, isn't just "how you escape"; it's also "how you interpret your Durance".

                A Beast interprets the Durance as the trappings of civilization; thus it makes sense that that's what he leaves behind in his Escape. Etc., etc. (way too tired to write-up all five right now)

                The point is ultimately that regaining your agency is as much about choosing your perspective, choosing your role in the narrative, choosing how you break the existing story to write a new one, as it is the physical details of the escape. Otherwise, the social and mental consequences of your escape wouldn't be relevant.

                This is why the pre-Durance background is relevant to the Seeming description. What kind of person would choose to interpret their Durance as a trap of their own ego and release that in order to Escape? Someone who already thinks like that. You become an Elemental because you're set up to see the world that way, even before the Faeries found you.

                I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".

                                                06-27-2015, 06:56 AM
                                                To be honest, all my reading was influenced by my thinking "can I redo this one particular character in 2e?"

                                                In first edition, character is a singer. (The original idea that sparked the character was actually "what about someone abducted to serve as a musician or singer or something, but who isn't a Fairest?") Whenever they displease their keeper, they get put into an iron coffin and chucked into a fire for a while. Eventually, they melt and turn into a statue of glass. Then escape at some point afterward. A mix of horrifying torture with weird fairy tale logic, plus interesting powers in play. The one bad thing is that none of the Elemental kiths actually fit.

                                                With 2e, there's the advantage that you can just take the Artist kith. But the new interpretation on Seemings necessitates some re-imagining. So. New version. Turning into glass wasn't just something that happened at some point: it's how they escaped. The character leaves behind human frailty, turns into unfeeling matter and oozes away from their Keeper's clutches. Since then I've been waiting for the Elemental spoiler to drop so I can compare. (It's just my luck that the one Seeming I was waiting for the most was almost the last one shown.) And... The Escape is great. Almost perfect, really, if you remove the references to big wild nature and overwhelming forces. "Or maybe she went dim, fading, beginning to become nothing since she could never hope to matter anymore. And yet, at some point, brutal winds toughened her skin, or else the pain of hot steel dimmed and she felt only the sensation, not the agony. [...] In that moment, she decided to release her ego, her need to be special vanished in an instant, and her heart fell out, hitting the ground, and shattering to a thousand useless pieces all at once. Quickly, she filled that hole, her heart, herself, with the raw possibility of the elements [...]. And in making that choice, she used the element itself in escaping, becoming its child in the real world, free of the Hedge." Great. Except... there's no storm inside, no grand desert or earthquake, nothing great and terrible, nothing to lure in adrenaline junkies, no whimsical chaos and destruction.

                                                So: ambivalent. It reads well, but isn't quite what I was expecting. Since then I've been alternating between thinking about the character and wondering if my "complaint" is just a personal matter or if it genuinely closes down interesting options. Ice... snowstorms. Rock... earthquakes. Should rock and earth necessarily mean earthquakes and landslides and such? A great, ancient tree isn't really known for "whim and devastation" and "throwing caution to the wind"... Metal? Is metal chaotic with "order behind the destruction"?

                                                There were some fire elemental Contracts, maybe I should play up that part. The caged-in heat from the character's rebirth. Or maybe I should just go with an odd Ogre who happens to look glassy, they also have themes of protecting yourself from pain... Although the violence Blessing doesn't really fit either...

                                                And so on and so forth.
