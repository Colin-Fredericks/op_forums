    Originally posted by NLight View Post
    This is called Brainspew, so I'll take advantage of that. I'll try not to get anyone wet.

    I'll remain a stalwart defender of the 5-dot progressive structure. The removal of the progression, but still having the thematic progression, was something that made me close Werewolf: the Forsaken and smack the hardcover against my forehead. I was so happy to see things finally get organized, and then... "Nevermind! Fooled you!" *thwack*

    Crunch first. Having the structure and progression enforces balance within the Contract. It reinforces the Knowing/Compelling < Ruling / Shielding < Un/Weaving < Un/Making < Mastery/Becoming structure that Mage laid out quite well. Several existing early contracts did this wonderfully (with some noted hiccups like Dream 1 and Darkness). Yes, Vampire has gone through 20+ years of refining, and still sees tweaks and changes. oWoD wasn't made with power balance between games in its original structure either (Lupines vs Garou, very different creatures). nWoD is, and one of the things that has kept me in the Storyteller System has been the steps taken for a proper system balance across each title. Throwing out structure just seems like an open invite for early power creep and the "yeah, whatever, let's let them do this" like Gifts from Apocalypse that stray off to unthematic regions.
    Skipping levels in the progression also stunts the curve. Speaking as a player rather than a GM, knowing how to use the low level abilities effectively sets you up for applications of the higher end. Jumping right into the deep end of a contract will just leave you to drown in your own ideas, wondering "why did I pick this again?" Your mileage may vary, but I'm drawing on my experiences specifically with Reflections. It goes somewhere, and I'd be in unfamiliar waters if I jumped right into reaching through a reflective surface to grab the knife in my bathroom's toothbrush holder if I hadn't gone through the experience of grabbing my television and rewinding to find out how the hell I ended up on my couch when I was left for dead in the Hedge the night before.

    The Universal/Seeming contract XP structure fits with other game lines. You are X, and that means you're going to have an easier time being good at Y and Z, but not W. Oh, you're an A? B, C, and D are your strong points, but you're going to have to work harder for Y and Z. That's just core structure (Clan Disciplines, Path Arcana, for example).

    Coming up with a single ability that is thematic for Changelings to have is not a complete contract. If it's not part of a progressive theme, don't force it to be one. Have it be a single power similar to a Goblin Contract, but nix the drawbacks. Let's just call them Cantrips for the sake of argument (and pandering to CtD fans). X cantrip costs 2 dots, and lets you... know what a token/trifle/goblin fruit does. More successes means more info (drawbacks, catch, etc). Has a catch. Could you develop a whole "Contracts of the Hedge"? Sure. Do you need to? No. A Universal one-off 'Contract' that makes sense for Changelings to have and to learn, but isn't part of a larger structure and thematic whole. Single clause? Ok, cool. I can deal with that, but it's not a 'Contract'.

    This leads into the fluff.

    A Contract is a deal made with a concept. The seasons, Stone, elements, darkness, mirrors, and so forth. Somewhere, some changeling got the hutspah enough to address something and say "Hey, let's make a deal", because they're insane enough to do that... and conceptual things (*cough*totems*cough*) like to make deals in return for reinforcement of what they are, and what they represent. A Contract is a complete promise to uphold and utilize the very concept it's made with. Now, the original promise may have happened long, long ago in a kingdom far, far away, but it's still the same promise. "I solemnly swear to make Dreams special". "I will become the mirror and reflect both friend and foe". "Stone is solid, unmoving, and unbreakable. This is what I shall become". A modern changeling might not even realize that the push of emotional energy (Glamour) into fueling the power of a concept is empowering the very thing the Contract was bargained with, but they're doing exactly that! Centuries down the line, pushing the fuel of passion to twist someone's desires another direction empowers the concepts of a Fleeting Spring, and young fickle lovers. Even if no one else is around to witness it, the concept, the very core, knows.
    "Good job. Have a cookie. Oh, you've been doing a really good job? Let's see what you can do with a bit more oomph." And the contract progresses, following the line of the original promise. In time, you've grown in your upholding of the Contract until you're on par with the one who sealed the deal in the first place, and you have the full access to the concept's gifts. You're a champion of [insert stuff here]! Behold the awesome!
    Divorcing the progressive structure cheats the deal. You don't learn your way around using the concept creatively (which many clauses need to be utilized as more than parlor tricks). You just jump right in and say "Ha! I'm a giant fireball! FEAR ME!" without having any progression up to that point that proves to the concept that you'll even know WTF you're doing. See above about the curve. You start the promise, the Contract, and you fulfill your end of the bargain through application, and get paid with access to more and greater.

    Ok. That's my spew for now. I'll get the mop.
    I'm not going to line item. But I pretty much disagree with most of this. While I appreciate the critique, I don't feel that these arguments make for a strong case for rehashing the old structure, in accordance with my vision for the project.

        The 5-Dot tiered structure is appropriate to represent a singular skill or concept you gradually get better at. Changelign powers are presented as being more grab-bag-y "You can do X thing because you made an agreement with Y concept to do X." Its not about progressing gradually in a skillset (other than making contracts themselves). Its about finding another beneficial contract you can participate in.

        5-Dot Tier works in Blood and Smoke because each Discipline is really just one thing, then that one thing gets advancements or minor variants as the vampire levels up. Some exceptions apply, of course. Obfuscate 4 is measurably *better* at hiding themselves through vampire magic than Obfuscate 2. The differences of types of power are just to make that distinction felt more.

        Once you have a Contract with Potatos to grow spuds. You can't be better at that same exact contract to grow exploding spuds. The contract is the contract is the contract, with few exceptions. If you want exploding spuds, that costs extra (as a different contract). You aren't becoming better with your agreement, you are amending or expanding that. 5-Dot Tier system simple doesn't handle that as well.

                Not to beat this horse too dead, but on the progression vs. grab bag thing, we also know enough about Forsaken 2e to look at that in all this.

                Forsaken is taking a mixed approach of some 5-dot progression powers, and some not. I think this backs up a lot of the thoughts about breaking off the "everything in 5-dot progression."

                In Forsaken 2e your Auspice's Moon Gift is a 5-dot progression where you get better at doing the core things for your Auspice (similar to SoulGambit's point), and Shadow Gifts which are available to everyone and contain five Facets that key off of the five types of Renown (more similar to the stated idea for the new Seeming Contracts without being slanted for specific splats directly).

                While I don't think that the 5-dot progression concept is so flawed that is never worth using (and clearly the nWOD 2e teams don't since 4/6 2e games still use them in some fashion so far), dropping it when the flaws are getting in the way too much of the design goals of the rules is a good thing. The 5-dot progressions aren't necessary for a nWOD supernatural game to work, so using that model should be done to support the design goals of the developers and authors, not because it is the default way the WOD systems tend to handle supernatural powers.

                            The five dot progression only really makes sense when it is tracking a single ability in which the character is improving. It works well for Mage, as each dot of an Arcana is representative of greater mastery, not a new ability. The same thought was applied to the Disciplines in Requiem 2.0, hence why the later powers all call back to and improve the lower dots. But for something like Contracts, which are each a discreet new power tied to a central theme, the five dot progression makes little sense.

Faeries are traditionally depicted as a really diverse lot and part of the joy of it to me is just how creative you can get with your concepts. Lost was a real improvement over Dreaming in this respect and I'd like to see the last few impediments towards really free-form building removed.

                                                    Here is my own idea on the new contract system :

                                                    There is no "universal" or "seeming" contracts. There is just "contracts. Each contract has a power, a cost and a catch. For what would be a "universal" contract, that's it. For what would be a "seeming" contract, you add a spin on the contract for a few (or all) the seemings. One of those seeming spin can be a little stronger than the others (to make it "favored"), but I personally think they should all be about the same power level. Some seeming could be skipped, but I don't think there really is a need to skip one and only one all the time. Sometimes it could be more than one, sometimes there could be none missing. Also, you can never buy a contract with the seeming spin of an other seeming than yours.

                                                    What we get from that is a system that reinforce some thematics and liberate the mechanics from some artificial constraints.

                                                    - A changeling with no seeming can buy any contract. Their contract is just a little less powerful than a changeling with a seeming. If the seeming is a factor of the changeling's agency, the changeling with no agency does not get to put his personal spin on a contract. The contract is law and you follow it to the letter. You did not assert you individuality, so you're still just a prop in someone else's story (your keeper's).

                                                    - Your seeming is the expression of your agency. You're not only a prop anymore. You have taken some control over the rules that were imposed on you. That's why you can put your own spin on your contracts; you can bend it a little in your own way. That also means you cannot "fake" your agency, meaning you cannot invoke a contract with the spin of an other seeming.

                                                    In one of the books (I don't remember which) there was rule to shift your kith/seeming over time to something new. If at some point we allow a changeling to buy an other seeming's version of a contract, in my mind that would mean the changeling evolved and changed to that seeming first.

                                                    Also, all contracts are accessible in some form, even if a contract has no spin for your particular seeming.

                                                            First I'd like to say I'm happy with the "bag of trick" approach. Only iconic powers, at whatever level needed, with less "filling" powers. It looks like the discreet powers will also be also more versatile (like turning into an animal will also allow you to talk to similar animals). But it also look like 5 dots powers will be more easily picked up. How would you limit that in character creation ? And during character progression, wouldn't it be more attractive to bank XP have one 5 dots power rather than three 2 dots powers (I didn't do the math for XP equivalence, but you see what I mean) ?

                                                            As for Goblin contracts. Of course, the goblin version have a bad side effect, like having the dramatic failure result going along the success result of the contract, or some side effect especially designed for the goblin version.
