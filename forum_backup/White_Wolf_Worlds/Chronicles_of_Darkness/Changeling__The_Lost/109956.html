    So what Kith's/Seemings/etc are popular with your group?

    04-10-2014, 04:04 AM
    So I was wondering what Kiths, Seemings, Courts, Entitlements or combinations of Kiths are popular with your groups. What do people seem to be playing, or not playing? I'll list what I recently gathered in looking over old game notes.

                      With all the Changeling games I've ran, Seemings have ran the gamut with some mild leanings towards Fairest, Flora-based Elementals, Darklings, Ogres and Wizened (with Fairest, and Elementals having the most consistent interest). Of all the Seemings Beasts I've never had anyone take an active interest in yet, and it's been some years. I find that strange, but there you go between LARP and Tabletop. They're just getting no love, but I think some of that may be baggage and sour-tastes from folks C:tD days (Pooka, I'm looking at you even as I tell people to utterly forget anything they remember of C:tD in relation to C:tL).

                      The Fairest thing is likely some minor residual baggage (again) from C:tD and super-kith Sidhe; drastically unbalanced compared to the rest with the most goodies accessible, and the "Look at me, I'm awesome and I just get more shit" fairy princess crap all rolled neatly into one obnoxious package chock full of Arts and House-related goodies that overshadowed -everyone- by a good stretch. At least now it's far less "They're popular because they're the super-kith" and more "I want to be something regal, beautiful, commanding, frightening and impressive. The Fairest got that in spades built right in". Being socially strong sure doesn't hurt in games with heavy politics, so I don't begrudge that popular choice especially when I actually rather like the Fairest (and all the Seemings, honestly) and one is using the Social maneuvering systems.

                      Kiths have been very spread out. I've encouraged Dual-Kiths/Wyrd Progression so there isn't too heavy reuse of individual Kiths outside of a couple. Oddly enough once again something unexpected; Soldiers have seen interest consistently despite having one of the worst Kith Blessings out there. I think it may be folks overestimating what it does and wanting a bit of martial flair. There's been a heavy lean towards wilds/plant/outdoors Kiths; Farwalkers, Woodbloods, variants of Venomtouched/Blightbents with toxic flora, Flowering, Sleep Scent (modified from the Somnolent Perfume aspect in Autumn Nightmares) etc. but that could well change. There's been some repeated interest in variants of Stonebones as well.

                      Entitlements rarely get much interest. Honestly, I rarely find them all that relevant myself unless you're dealing with a very large setting filled to the brim with Lost npcs given how they generally operate; they lose quite a bit of interest factor when there's only a lone representative I find. I've had one Lord Sage of the Unknown Reaches and some initial interest in an Adjucator of the Wheel PC-wise, but not too much else. They're definitely one of those things that I feel make the greatest impact in a very bustling local Freehold that already has very filled out Courts; once people are more invested and interacting with them, -then- they tend to stray into the Entitlements if at all. I'm still hoping someone may go towards the Scarecrow Ministry sometime, and a few PCs really seemed to be going towards being Hedge Wardens.

                      PC-wise it has been the Spring Court that has had the most interest along with a nod towards the Night Court and Dusk Court. Winter Court seems to get the least interest overall.

                      Werewolf: the Forsaken Overhaul: A W:tF Shard and House Rules, Pre-Idigam Chronicles and taking different route. Constantly being Updated.
                      New Bloodlines and Blood and Smoke Updates: Homebrew Bloodlines and updates to prior ones.
                      Belial's Brood Blood and Smoke: An updated take on the Forsworn.

                                      Pooka are a Kith (like Seemings in CTL) which has the weakness of always needing to lie...they must make a WP roll to tell the truth. They have been typically portrayed as pranksters and a little goofy. Their powers are the ability to turn into an animal when unobserved, and an easy time getting people to open up to them and tell them secrets.

                                      I have no problem with Pooka in and of themselves. There is no draw for me, but I have seen them played very well. And while many probably even most do play them well, they tend to draw a certain sort of player to them who tends to be very disruptive and and ignore the tone of the game...the same sort who tends to play "fishmalks" and before that, Kender. Thus a lot of people tend to hate Pooka outright, despite the fact they can be played well. At the same time, I can't blame the GM who gets frustrated with a player who seems intent on using their character type as an excuse to mess up other player's play experiences.

                                      I can't speak about Exalted too much, but I suspect the choice words were about the sort of player who wanted to enter a serious Exalted game, turn people purple, and force people to sing about pants, or something like that.

                                      There's a line. Some people can be frustrated by Pooka who, unlike say Malkavians, actually have some reason to be fairly comedic with some concepts. I can kick a player whose Malkavian's derangement is apparently to yell "PANTS" at odd times...but Pooka tend to be a little goofy, being representative of childlike whimsy and imagination. A Pooka behaving that way is not necessarily indicative of a bad player...a certain amount of such is right there in the description, and it is systematically hard not to have them lie all the damn time for no reason. Some people don't like that.

                                      Is that fairly clear?

                                                          I have house ruled fan-based Contracts for the Sun/Day and Moon/Night; there were a couple versions kicking around the net before but there were a very big mixed bag, so they got revised further (The equivalents of "Fleeting" contracts for both need a further tweak though to dovetail more with Post-GMC Changeling house-rules present though). It helps make up for it, though post GMC has made more work on the roster to do some last revisions.

                                                          I don't worry too much about the two courts nomenclature; it's pretty interchangeable in my games and everyone knows what's being referred to anyhow. As is, the cultural identity isn't really the focus of them regardless; it's symbolism that has some traditional use, but not as a given. Human culture does not set the tone nor the Courts important elements; it's a convenience in symbolism that gets borrowed, the undercurrent behind it. Otherwise you start falling into characters from culture X should be this court, and those from Y that court... not something I like to encourage even if the roots might have nods to that.

                                                          Honestly, many of the "Slavic" elements in those two courts writeups really had more to do with much younger Christian revisions (and in at least one instance and outright deity fabrication to make it fit with that religious pov) than much honestly old Slavic anyhow, a personal pet peeve of mine. To be blunt the Dualistic approach is a very poor fit for a Slavic base regardless, so I make due without paying too heavy mind to it as a big thing.

                                                          The Directional have some very, very under-powered fan presentations out on the web that I'm updating as needed (player interest basically). There aren't enough active PC wise to worry about it too much yet, so it's update-as-needed; I've mostly not worried about them for NPCs, and PC's going for the fan-made contracts will get updates and discussion as they decide to snag them (hasn't happened yet). They weren't around when I first started running C:tL and the current fan-made need a -lot- of work, luckily nobody has had an issue with that thus far and it hasn't been problematic (Nobody can really help that the Seasonal got all the love). The goal is to get them all tweaked and updated, but it's a lot of work when other areas have had higher priority. If the Southern Courtier player wants a Contract or two to play up their Court aspects, I'll revise it then.

                                                          On the Courts in my main Chronicle ( very long, fair warning, but it may help give you more context Baron):


                                                          There is/was a Freehold dedicated to the Seasonal Courts with one lone Dusk Courtier that's fell into disarray with the remaining freehold members looking at building a new one to get from under some fairly draconian (if not near-insane) members. The Dusk Courtier originally being an agent of the Sun/Moon/Dusk Freehold elsewhere to keep it abreast of what was going on in the city proper. The Directional Courts have their own Freehold entrenched in the city's setting, and the smaller city that recently became a suburb plays host to the Day/Night/Dusk freehold across the settings major lake with the Dusk taking on a more mercenary role/neutral party between the two. Fatalism is a fairly common thing to crop up; they just ended up finding the greatest foothold among the Moon/Sun courts by fulfilling a handy role there.

                                                          There are no Dawn around, nor are there likely to be in the near future without something drastic occurring. I've no real interest in them as an ST, nor is there from my Co-St for the main chronicle at this time. Hope is a very, very rare commodity and the Martyrs usually just don't survive for long. Most that are aware of them locally see them as little more than Gentry-bait and sacrificial lambs to keep everyone else safe and sure as hell don't want them supping at their table for long. Great to have when one needs a distraction, and encouraged for just as long as it takes for them to live up to being a Martyr and divert Gentry (and others) away from the rest of the community. That could change, but there hasn't been anything towards that yet.

                                                          It's less a free-for-all and isn't focused around one Freehold. It's far more "If you want to be this Court, these are the folks NPC-wise you've likely worked with and had contact with in the past. This is also likely which Freehold that took you in initially unless you switched Courts in your past."

                                                          Cross Freehold interactions were fairly limited and sparse in the setting; nothing formal though individual private dealings weren't too rare, simply kept quiet.

                                                          The Seasonal Freehold (The Fortean Times) was overtly over-paranoid to the point of hostility towards outsiders beyond the one Dusk and a Courtless due to some rather low Clarity and rather aggressive Court rulers. The Autumn Court and Winter Court consistently had the most numbers, influence and power. Now a lot of the younger members, especially those with a better grip on reality are getting fed up and trying to make something less isolationist, secretive and at times downright vicious (the paranoia of Privateers and Loyalists led the original freehold to take very, very unpleasant approaches to untrustworthy new Changelings). The last Summer Court among them is kind of the last catalyst; if he leaves there won't be a proper cycle even if the season runs short regionally, and he's looking at potentially leaving that court entirely for the Dusk or Winter Courtiers if things keep going the way they have.

                                                          The second is the Directional (The 1000 Hells), with one Night Courtier who decided to live in the main city as a vassal to them in the central area to exist in the comfortably (and feed a bit back to the Night Court every now and then). They're fairly cosmopolitan in numbers compared to the prior, but -very- strict. They'll likely see more prominence as the strongest Seasonal Freehold finishes its troubled times. They relied more on the acquisition mundane power and the influence of humans than the old Seasonal Court and it allowed them to hide more readily in plain sight. They weren't the most potent, but they were the most stable. Now that the prior Freehold is starting to topple they're coming out of the woodwork more and more.

                                                          The third were small band of primarily Moon/Sun (The Blasted Rock) with a smattering of Dusk and off-the-radar until the main city's "acquisition" of the nearby large town/small city started bringing the city's problems to their doorstep. They want more of a foothold in the main city districts and have been sending out feelers and agents to muster that more actively; whether they'll butt heads with the Directional is tough to say yet. For the most part they're more worried about their survival as a distinct, separate Freehold and so have been taking steps to get inroads or alliances more recently to keep their asses covered.

                                                          It's a big city; the Freeholds tend to be rather insular with a tone set by a sort of informal policy where they didn't want the problems of another Freehold coming to their own door, with outright sabotage by other Freeholds at times due consistent issues with Loyalists and Privateers gutting newcomer Freeholds from within and without (an issue which really set the tone for the Seasonal and Directional Freeholds). The longest standing ones have seen others come and go time-line wise precisely for drawing too much attention to the Gentry or the ire of other more populous supernaturals by being too open, too trusting and too cosmopolitan in their interactions.

                                                          Of course, the truth of the matter is uglier than even that but you won't generally hear the Directional nor original Seasonal Freehold admit that some of those toppled short-lived Freeholds had more to do with power-plays, infighting, insanity and pure misinformation (including to their own Court members on what was occurring). Like any governing bodies in practice they tend to be self-serving assholes when they think they can get ahead at the cost of others who don't follow their outlooks, even if it might impact their mutual survival. Duck, cover, entrench and stay out of each others way and spy when you can on the surface. Back-stab, sabotage, gathering of leverage and who's fucking who behind closed doors before you even get legitimate Loyalists and Privateers involved. Latent human nature plus the mix of very real looming bogeyman, mad gods and stranger all around doesn't make for them suddenly playing nice.

                                                          Desperate times call for desperate measures (and that's why you see Dusk crop up frequently in the setting as an almost rogue element). There's a reason the Dusk, Winter and Autumn Court used crop up frequently or for the longest time had the most actual power under their belts. It's starting to switch gears however, with the Dusk, Spring and South seeing more activity in this chronicle lately.
