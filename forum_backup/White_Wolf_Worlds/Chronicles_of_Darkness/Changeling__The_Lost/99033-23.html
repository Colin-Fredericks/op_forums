                            The bond of trust within the Last Dance Motley has cracked. Can it be repaired? Many members try to escape the tension within the motley with their own pursuits, but some try to mend the wounds before they cannot be healed.

                            Session LIII

                            Gawain’s Memories

                            November 8th, afternoon

                            The sanctity of my home is broken. My motley is in shambles, with one member too uncomfortable around another to leave her room. The only so-called gain of last night was that a Changeling, a friend, lies in chains, her mind still not her own but now seething with hatred towards me. And it is all because the Monarchs are too afraid of losing some sliver of their power to see to the safety of their charges.

                            That’s not really fair, I guess, and sounds like Lord Bertilak besides. I... I can’t let myself do that right now - Calamity needs me to be me, to be her Gawain, not her liege lord. Still, if the thing that was so distracting to Charlotte yesterday was this business of the Day and Night Courts getting crowns...

                            I don’t know that it was, and I don’t know what was said over the phone yesterday afternoon. We certainly seemed to have few options, and less time. All that I do know is that mistakes were made, and plenty to go around. I think we managed to not compound them last night. And now... now we need to find a way to repair what we can.

                            As much as we can.


                            Lorelei’s Diary

                            Okay, so with a few shining bright spots as exceptions, the last two days have turned my life to absolute shit. And the real hell of it is that I know that some other members of my motley – most of them, in fact – are suffering far worse than I am, and there seems to be fuck-all that I can do about it. It’s hard to believe that just two days ago I was shouting out at the top of my lungs that this was the “best week ever.”

                            So I had just finished showering and was about to get started on my makeup when the doorbell rang. Figuring everyone else was either busy or elsewhere, I ran downstairs and answered the door. It was Joan and Gloria, who seemed pleased with how her meeting with Viscount Augustine went. She’s going to continue staying with us at the Dream House for a couple of days while she finds her feet, and then try to find more permanent accommodations.

                            “While I would be pleased to introduce you to the other denizens of the Freehold,” Joan was saying formally as I arrived on the scene, “it may be truly said that there are many others more suited to this task than I. Lorelei and her motley, for example, or Monica.”

                            I nodded. “We’d be happy to show you around once things settle down a little,” I said, “but Monica is a real sweetheart, and she knows everybody in the area. I’ll introduce you to her soon… or maybe I’ll let D handle that; those two are really close friends.” Gloria blushed at that, but did a pretty good job of hiding it, so I let it pass.

                            Joan, on the other hand, looked concerned. “Is everything all right, Lorelei?” she asked seriously.

                            I shook my head. “No,” I answered her quietly, “it’s really not.”

                            Joan turned to look at Gloria. “I apologize, but could you give us a moment, please?”

                            “It’s okay,” Gloria said, “I’ll just go see if anyone’s in the kitchen.”

                            Joan took my hand. “What is wrong, Lorelei?” she asked, her eyes full of concern.

                            I sighed. “Chalcedony came by here today. I don’t know how much you know about that situation, but… things didn’t go smoothly. Calamity and Insincere Marguerite had an argument about how to handle it, and they’re pretty mad at each other right now.” I turned away. “I hate it when my friends fight.”

                            She squeezed my hand. “If you are needed here… Andromeda and I will understand,” she said kindly.

                            I turned back to look at her. “No,” I said quietly, “they’ll work it out. I’ll do what I can to help them, of course, but eventually it’s going to have to come down to the two of them, and I don’t think either one of them is ready for that just yet. Unless you hear differently from me, I’ll see you both at seven, okay?”

                            Joan smiled then, and it was radiant. “We look forward to it. Best of luck in your efforts, my friend. My fiancé and I shall see you tonight.” She turned to go.

                            I put out an arm to stop her, my eyes wide. “Whoa, wait a minute. What was that word you just used?”

                            She just smiled, but said no more.

                            After she made her exit, I joined Gloria in the kitchen, and the two of us went upstairs in search of D. We found her at the base of the retractable attic stairs, looking grim.

                            “Marguerite is with Julia?” I asked softly as we approached.

                            She nodded, and leaned in close. “She’s making it worse,” she whispered tensely.

                            “I’ll talk to them,” I said, giving her a quick hug.

                            “Be careful,” D admonished, “I’ve never seen Julia this angry. She’s got her crazy going full blast.”

                            It was dark in the attic, but I could see Julia and Marguerite sort of huddled together a short distance away. “Hello,” I said softly as I approached.

                            Julia looked up. “Hello, Lori,” she said carefully. Marguerite didn’t say anything at all.

                            “How is she?” I asked.

                            “How do you think?” Julia spat.

                            I sighed. “Look, Julia, I’m trying to help here. I don’t want to take sides between the two of them…”

                            “Sounds like you’ve already taken a side,” Julia snapped. “Sounds to me like everyone has.”

                            “That’s not fair,” I gently rebuked her. “You know I love her. I’ve proven that to you hundreds of times. I’m upset about what happened too. So are the others. I’m just trying to find a way to get past this with our motley and our friendships intact.”

                            “That ship has sailed, Lori!” Julia was livid, and her eyes held a manic gleam I wasn’t used to seeing there. “Calamity hurt Insincere Marguerite. She hurt her badly. When she forced Insincere Marguerite to break her word, Bon-Bon lost Clarity. And Calamity is going to get away with it, because Calamity always gets away with it.”

                            I gasped as I took that in. I’ve heard some of the older and more experienced changelings talk about Clarity-With-A-Capital-C before. It’s a sort of unofficial gauge of how connected we are to reality. The more Clarity you have, the better you are at interacting with mortals and the mundane world. Lower Clarity allows greater connection to the Wyrd, but you risk losing yourself and becoming a creature of the Hedge and Arcadia. Lose too much Clarity, and you risk becoming a monster.

                            “Oh my God,” I whispered, my eyes widening. I leaned in to hug Julia; she was stiff but she allowed it, and even returned the embrace after a moment. “I’m so sorry, Julia, I had no idea.”

                            “She’s been hurt,” Julia said simply in my ear. “Something has to be done.”

                            “Something will be,” I assured her. “I don’t think the others know how serious this is, Julia. I don’t think any of us knew how badly Insincere Marguerite had been hurt by this. We’ll figure something out. Calamity will have to pay for what she’s done, don’t worry about that. But please promise me one thing, okay?”

                            “What?” Julia asked, leaning away with a guarded expression.

                            “Don’t make any snap decisions tonight. Don’t do anything you can’t take back.”

                            “Why not?” she asked. “Why should we wait to exact our… justice?”

                            “Because you’re angry right now,” I explained. “Don’t get me wrong… you have every right to be angry. I’m angry too, and so is D. But we shouldn’t make such a major decision based purely on that anger. What if we act rashly, and then have to live with the regret for the rest of our lives?”

                            Julia chewed on that for a minute, but I could tell she wasn’t happy. “Okay,” she said after a few moments. “I promise I won’t say or do anything about Calamity tonight, Lori. I can’t leave her alone like this anyway.”

                            I turned to regard Insincere Marguerite, who was curled up into a tight little ball, staring at nothing and making no sound. I walked over to her. “Insincere Marguerite?” I asked hesitantly. She didn’t respond.

                            I leaned in and kissed the top of her head. “I love you, Insincere Marguerite,” I said softly, “and I’ll do whatever I can to help you get through this. Just let me know how to help.”

                            She looked up at me with such pain and loss in her eyes that I almost couldn’t bear it, and she nodded. It wasn’t much, but it was the most she’d responded to anything since I entered the attic. I made my exit.

                            D waited anxiously at the foot of the stairs. “What’s up?” she asked.

                            “I think I talked Julia down for now,” I informed her, “but she’s unpredictable. Help me keep an eye on her… on both of them, please.”

                            D nodded. “You still going out tonight, then?” she asked.

                            I shook my head. “I won’t deny that I’d like to get away from this mess for a little while, but I’m not going out if you need me to stay,” I answered.

                            D thought about it. “Go on,” she said with a cocky grin, “I’ll look after things here. I’ll call you if we really need you for anything, I promise. Besides, Joan would never forgive me if I screwed this up for her.”

                            I embraced her. “Okay, if you’re sure,” I said.

                            Gloria stood to one side of us looking uncomfortable. “I… I’m not sure what’s going on, but… you’ve all been so kind to me… is there anything I can do to help?” she asked.

                            “It’s sweet of you to offer,” I said, “but I think the motley just has to work through this on our own.”

                            She nodded. “I’ll stay with de la Rocha,” she said after a minute, “and lend a hand where I can.”

                            I grinned at her. “You two have fun,” I said with a wink.

                            She colored. “That’s… not what I meant,” she stammered out.

                            I chuckled. “It’s okay, Gloria,” I said, “nobody’s going to rope you into anything you don’t want to do. But don’t rule anything out, okay? You don’t know what you might be missing.” I glanced fondly at my honey and made my way downstairs to look for Calamity.

                            Before I found her, I was struck by an idea. I ducked into an unused room and looked up Becky’s number on my phone. She seemed surprised to hear from me, and asked if everything was okay.

                            “No, not really,” I said simply. “I don’t know how much you know about the situation with Chalcedony, but she came over here this morning, and we were forced to step up the timetable. We had to subdue her and hand her over to the Autumn Court. There was a difference of opinion about this in the motley, and Calamity and Marguerite had a bit of a falling out. Calamity’s taking it hard. I think… if you’re free tonight, Becky, I think she could really use a friend.”

                            “I was going to come to the show tonight anyway,” Becky said, “I’ll be happy to do whatever I can to help.”

                            “Thanks, Becky,” I said. “Honestly, I think Calamity and Marguerite need a little time apart. If you want to take her out somewhere, that might be for the best. It’ll give Marguerite a little time to cool off and start thinking more clearly.”

                            “Okay,” she replied. “Thanks for letting me know, Lori. I’ll do what I can for her.”

                            “I know you will. Thanks for being such a good friend.”

                            We disconnected, and I went in search of Calamity. I found her a few minutes later, looking falsely chipper and going about her usual pre-show routines. “What are you doing?” I asked, frankly flabbergasted.

                            “I have two numbers tonight,” she replied in an awful bright voice. “Can’t let the crowds down, right? The show must go on. And with our star singer out for the evening, we can’t make too many more substitutions…”

                            “Ridiculous,” I said impatiently. “You’re in no state, Calamity.”

                            “I’m fine,” she said in that Everything-Is-Okay-So-What-Are-You-Worrying-About voice. I’ll admit it, she’s pretty good at hiding her emotions. If I hadn’t known what she was going through, I might have bought her act.

                            “You’re not,” I retorted. “Bridget and Giselle will take your numbers tonight; their acts have been ready for a week at least. Becky’s on her way over, and the two of you are going out tonight.”

                            “I can’t,” Calamity said, and her sunny look turned brittle, “I have to perform.”

                            I sighed. “No you don’t,” I said finally. I carefully put a hand on her shoulder and pulled her closer to me to soften my next words. “Take the night off, Calamity. Go with Becky. Get out of the house for a while. Let everything cool down, and take some time to process what’s going on.” She looked inclined to argue, so I leaned in closer. “I’ll pull rank if I have to. I’m the Creative Director of the Dream House, and I’m telling you that you are not performing here tonight. We’ll reassess the situation in a few days, okay?”

                            She sort of sagged in front of me. I don’t think I’ve ever seen her look so defeated. “Okay,” she said. After a moment she put her hand on top of mine and added “thanks, Lori.”

                            I squeezed her shoulder. “We’ll get through this,” I promised her. “Somehow, we’ll get through this. Everyone just needs a little time to put things in perspective.”

                            She nodded, and I went upstairs to get ready for my dinner date. I put on a slinky black dress (low neckline, high hemline, nothing underneath) and a pair of high heel shoes, touched up my makeup, packed up a few necessities in one of my smallest purses, and bid the troubles of the Dream House farewell for the evening.

                            Okay, well, in all honesty I made one more stop before I headed to the garage and revved up the ‘vette. I sat down by the fountain when I was sure that nobody was watching and I called to all the friends I’ve made among the area’s songbird population. A lot of them came fluttering up after a minute or two… Conrad, Vivian, Binky, Momo, Sassy Miss K. Hell, even Olga and Chicago Joe showed up eventually, though they had a pretty bad attitude throughout the conversation that followed (pigeons, man… what are you going to do?).

                            In any case, I asked the birds to watch over the Dream House for me that night… particularly the members of the motley. They’re birds, so they didn’t have a really clear idea of what I was asking them at first… but eventually I managed to get through to them that I wanted to be informed if any violent situations arose. They agreed to help me cheerfully (except for the pigeons, who groused), and fluttered off to station themselves at various windows.

                            As I was stretching and standing up, I noticed that I had an audience… Gawain had returned to the house via our local Hedge gate. I tried to think of a story which might explain what he had just seen, but in the end I opted to ignore it and act as though nothing had happened.

                            Gawain had adopted the oddly formal, almost cold persona he gets sometimes when he’s particularly upset. He informed me that he had been turned away at the Lighthouse, on the grounds that Chalcedony no longer trusted him. Apparently if he tried to enter her dreams in the wake of his “attack” on her, it could cause her real lasting trauma, and none of us wanted that, so he reluctantly agreed to withdraw and allow others to attempt to deal with her triggers. I could tell he was very unhappy with the situation, and I tried my best to soothe him, but I don’t know if it helped.

                            Half an hour later (damn traffic) I was ringing the bell at Joan and Andromeda’s place. Joan opened the door and favored me with a smile that lit up her whole face. “Please, come in,” she said simply.

                            I walked into the apartment and I think I said something clever like “thank you for having me over, you have a beautiful oh my God what is that wonderful smell?”

                            Andromeda leaned out of the kitchen, favored me with a grin, and said “Lasagna. I hope you like Italian.”

                            I do like Italian. When the Italian is prepared by someone with Andromeda’s skills, I love Italian. Dinner was fantastic, and the conversation was quite pleasant. I think Joan and I might have run out of things to talk about quickly if Andromeda hadn’t been there, but she kept things light and flirty, and kept the ball rolling whenever we hit one of those awkward pauses. I’m no doctor (sigh… maybe someday), but I’m ready to pronounce her completely recovered from her ordeal with the Table.

                            It’s a true testament to her hostess skills that Andromeda kept my mind off of the troubles back at the Dream House for pretty much the entire time that I was there. Oh, I’d occasionally glance at the windows to see if any of my little birds had turned up with news for me, but other than that I felt genuinely comfortable and at peace throughout the meal and the evening that followed.

                            We were enjoying a little wine in the sitting room after dinner when Joan turned all shy and nervous again. “So,” she began softly, “I am embarrassed to admit this, but our VCR is broken.”

                            “Oh,” I said with a mock pout, “and I was so looking forward to that feature film you mentioned.”

                            “I am very sorry,” she said with a small smile.

                            “I suppose we’ll have to find some other way to amuse ourselves,” I said, “I’m not ready to go home just yet.”

                            “You are of course welcome to stay for as long as you like, Lorelei,” Joan said breathlessly.

                            “I suppose we could listen to music,” I suggested with a twinkle, “or play a party game.”

                            “Or we could have sex,” Andromeda piped up at that point.

                            I looked from her to Joan. “I’m up for it if you are,” I said. I hope she could hear the warmth in my voice.

                            Joan smiled shyly. “I would like that very much,” she whispered, and the two of them took my hands and led me to the bedroom.

                            I did my best to make this evening about their relationship, about how much they mean to each other and how happy I was that they were letting me share in that. I tried to make sure that they were always in each other’s line of sight, and that when I was the center of attention, they were working together to bring me pleasure. I think it worked, and I detected no hint of jealousy or bad feelings from anyone concerned.

                            Though I don’t want to let this diary descend into the realms of poor taste, I would be remiss if I didn’t mention Andromeda’s… “collection.” We’ve known for some time that she’s an inventor and a tinkerer, and Calamity has let slip once or twice that she has turned those inclinations toward pushing the limits of erotic technology. I was fortunate enough to be able to sample some of her finished products, and… well… gosh… they’re terrific. The best. The absolute bee’s knees. The cat’s unforsaken roar.

                            (It is worth mentioning that I must have made quite an impression on Andromeda last night, as I returned home with a large unmarked brown cardboard box containing a few gifts from her collection. I pointed out a few toys that I had particularly enjoyed, but she made the final selections after… well… taking a few measurements. I can’t wait to show my new toys to D).

                            When we woke up the next morning in a tangle of limbs and blonde hair, none of us particularly wanted to get up. Joan was once more straightening and preening my feathers with a fascinated look on her face. Andromeda didn’t seem to mind, and told the two of us to have fun while she whipped something up for breakfast. “I can scramble a mean egg, but then,” she favored me with a remarkably dirty smirk, “you already know that.”

                            I think Joan might be forming a minor emotional attachment to me, but it’s hard to tell with her. She’s so guileless and earnest that pretty much anything she feels is magnified on her face and in her body language. I don’t think I’m a threat to her relationship with Andromeda, and if that ever changes, I swear I will break things off at once. If, however, I can have another night like that without endangering the cause of true love, I will jump on that chance like a flea on an Airedale.

                            After breakfast I sighed with resignation and said my goodbyes, giving each of my lovely hostesses a lingering kiss on my way out. I didn’t hurry back to the Dream House; I confess that I wasn’t particularly eager to get back into that mess. I parked the ‘vette and made it all the way upstairs and to my bedroom before I encountered anyone else at all, however.

                            D stirred sleepily as I entered. “Morning, Jules,” she said with a smile as she turned to look at me. “How was your night?”

                            “Amazing,” I replied softly, placing my box of new toys on the bureau, “and I brought back souvenirs. I can’t wait to show you some of these…” I trailed off as I saw her expression sag. “What is it, D? What’s wrong?”

                            “Nothing bad happened last night,” she assured me, “but things haven’t improved with those two, either. Last night, when I finally talked them down from the attic, they started…”

                            “What?” I asked.

                            “They were looking at apartments in Portland and the Bay Area,” she said miserably.

                            I felt like someone had punched me in the stomach. I turned on my heel and walked out into the hall with D right behind me. Though I knew I had to do it, it took nearly all of my courage to knock on that door.

                            Julia opened the door a minute later. She looked tense, but not nearly as tightly wound as she had the previous evening. “Hi, girls,” she said as she took in the sight of us. “She’s doing a little better now. You can come in if you want to.”

                            Marguerite greeted us morosely from her perch on the bed, but responded in kind when we offered her hugs and kisses. “Feeling any better, ma cheri?” I asked.

                            She shrugged. “Yes and no,” she responded. But hey, at least she was talking. “Julia brought me breakfast earlier, and I feel a bit stronger. But there are still certain things which do not feel right.”

                            “Can I talk to you about what’s going on?” I asked.

                            She nodded.

                            “D says that the two of you were looking at apartments. That you were thinking of moving away.” I couldn’t bear to say any more. I almost couldn’t get that out.

                            Marguerite shrugged. “It is… not certain. We shall try to see if we can work something out. But I can no longer trust Calamity. She tricked me. She hurt me. She might do so again, if she feels the cause is worthy. Every time I look at her I will see that, for the rest of my life. I do not know if I can remain in this motley while she is here. It may be necessary for Julia and I to leave this place.”

                            I shook my head. “I don’t want you to go,” I said simply. I was crying by that point; I couldn’t help it. I sound like such a stupid kid sometimes.

                            Marguerite took my hand. “I do not wish to lose what we have found either,” she said to me. “You and de la Rocha would of course be welcome to join us…”

                            “I don’t want you to go,” I said again, leaning in to her. “I can’t go with you. I’m pledge-bound to the Dream House.”

                            She nodded in understanding. “It may not come to that. We shall try to work things out, no? But if I cannot be comfortable in this place any longer, then Julia and I may have to go.”

                            And like leviathan rising from a black ocean, an image surfaced in my mind. When Liam returned the older man helped sit Rime up so they could change the shirt. He got a good look at the bruises and split skin on the young loyalist’s sides and started in surprise.


                                        “I didn’t realize…” He started.
                                        “Yeah, looks bad doesn’t it. The bruises from tonight won’t even show up for a little while yet. All that is from before.”

                                        Together they laid Rime back down and covered him with blankets. Rime did not wake for three days.



                                        Rime opened his eyes and blinked slowly. His vision was still somewhat hazy and his head felt like it was full of cotton. He turned his head to look around. He could not remember where he was but he knew there was something important that he needed to remember. As he turned a woman’s voice interrupted his thoughts.

                                        “You need to lie still Rime. Your injuries are still healing.”

                                        “My ‘ead feels funny. Like ‘m floating.” Rime slurred.

                                        “That’s the pain medicine you’re on right now. I managed to get some of the good stuff so you wouldn’t wake up in pain.” She gently brushed the hair out of his eyes. “I’m so sorry that Travers hurt you again. He’s not allowed to get near you as long as you are our guest here.”

                                        The name Travers brought back an image of an angry man standing over him and Rime started to try and move away.

                                        “No, Rime you need to lie still. Your ribs are still healing. There’s no one here but us. It’s okay.”

                                        Rime stopped moving and looked at the woman that was trying her best to calm him down.

                                        “Thas good…” He slurred and closed his eyes. He was asleep in moments.


                                        The next few days were more of the same. Rime would wake up for a few moments, remember where he was and have a mild panic attack before calming down again. The pain medicine that Diane had obtained did its job well and the young man did not feel much in the way of pain when he did wake.

                                        The sound of quiet voices brought Rime around and he peered through lidded eyes to see who was in the room. He spotted Diane and a young girl, no more than eleven or twelve talking near the toy bins against the wall. The girl looked like a yearling deer. She had soft looking ears the poked through her chestnut hair and her legs ended in dainty hooves. Her all brown eyes flitted from one bin to the next.

                                        “Come on, pick a toy or two then we can get something for lunch.” Diane whispered to her young friend.

                                        “I just need to find my Barbie’s red dress. I know it’s in here.” The young doe girl replied.

                                        Rime looked at the pair and something twinged in his chest. The girl was taken young if she was a Changeling at her age. He shook his head as if trying to clear the thoughts from his head. Diane noticed the movement and started. “Oh…I wasn’t expecting you to be awake. Do you need anything?”

                                        Rime’s forehead furrowed for a moment. “Water, if you dn’t mind.”

                                        “Of course.” She turned back to the young girl. “Felicity, find the red dress then join me in the kitchen.”

                                        “ ‘kay.” The young girl replied. She looked shyly at Rime then continued her search in the toy bin.

                                        “Why’re you here?” Rime asked, curious.

                                        “This is my room.” Felicity answered back quietly. “I get to sleep in my sleeping bag in Auntie Diane’s room while you use my room.” She smiled shyly. “Auntie Diane says that you need your rest.”

                                        “She’s pr’bly r’ght.” The pale man slurred. The twinge in his chest came back and he pushed the feeling aside. He was not sure how he was feeling anything right now with the drugs that were coursing through his veins. His head still felt like it was full of cotton, though perhaps not as much as it had before.

                                        Diane came back with a pitcher and a glass. She smiled at Rime then filled the glass. She gently helped him sit up enough to drink his fill before letting him back down on the pillows. Rime looked at the young girl. “Th’nk you fer letting me st’y in yer room.”

                                        “You’re welcome.” Felicity smiled shyly. “I hope you feel better soon Mr. Rime.”

                                        “Dun underst’nd.” Rime mumbled before drifting off to sleep.

                                        The next time Rime woke it was to Diane sitting in the chair next to the bed, drawing in a sketchbook.

                                        “Ah, you’re awake.” She smiled. “How are you feeling today?”

                                        Rime moved experimentally and found that while he could feel more of the aches in his body, they weren’t so bad. His arm snaked around his ribs and held his side unconsciously. “Better, thank you.”

                                        “I’m glad. I was happy that I managed to get some morphine this time for your pain. I’ve been scaling it back the past couple of days so you won’t feel so woozy.”

                                        “Thas good,” mumbled Rime.

                                        “You know, I remember you from the Lady’s palace. You are one of her Knights.” Diane paused. “I remember you protecting us, the Lady’s Treasures, when those thieves got into the palace. They tried to take us, but you and Rook stopped them.”

                                        Rime looked at Diane carefully, suddenly more awake. “What else do you remember?”

                                        “Not a lot. Some things here and there while I was in the Lady’s palace. Then one day I saw something that reminded me of the symbol on the side of an ambulance. You know, the caduceus?”

                                        Rime shook his head.

                                        “Anyway, it reminded me of that and I suddenly remembered where I came from and managed to find a way out. I think there was a gate in the woods near here.”

                                        “And you got out.” Rime finished for her.

                                        “Yeah, and I was lucky enough to meet Travers and Liam soon after that. They helped me make a new life for myself.” She indicated all around her. “Here I was only gone for a month or so. In the Lady’s realm it felt so much longer. When I got back I was able to create a new identity and slip into it. I chose to be an ER nurse this time around instead of an RN for a private practice.”

                                        “You were lucky.” Rime looked contemplative.

                                        “Yeah, no question there. What about you? What do you remember from your life before the Lady?


                                        Rime’s brow furrowed in thought. “I don’t remember a life before the Lady.” He said eventually. “I remember the cold, but that ended when I entered the Lady’s service.”

                                        “I’m sorry.”

                                        “For what?” Rime questioned.

                                        “That you don’t remember. Though I suppose if you did you wouldn’t have tried to bring me back to the Lady’s palace.”

                                        “I…”

                                        “I know. You were doing as you were asked. I don’t blame you.” Diane smiled. “Now, you should probably get some more rest. It’s getting late.”

                                        Rime nodded and closed his eyes. Within moments he was asleep.


                                        Rime’s life seemed to happen in moments since he woke up after Travers’ beating. He woke to the sound of someone putting a tray on the table next to him and he stirred awake.

                                        “Good afternoon.” Diane smiled. “You hungry?”

                                        “Sure.” Rime replied and propped himself up on the pillows. His ribs still ached but not nearly as sharp as they had mere days ago. At least he thought it was days. It might have been longer for all he knew. He wasn’t sure how much time had passed while he was on the morphine. As he was getting himself more comfortable he noticed Felicity slip into the room behind Diane.

                                        “Good afternoon.” He said to the young girl.

                                        Felicity stood by the door fanning herself with a paper fan and holding a glass of red juice. “It’s hot outside,” moaned the young doe girl. She took a sip of her juice.

                                        “Maybe I can help.” Rime offered.

                                        “How?” Felicity asked.

                                        Rime motioned towards the silverware on the try near him. “Put the spoon in your juice.”

                                        “Why?”

                                        “Just do it.”

                                        Felicity looked to Diane who nodded. Encouraged, Felicity put the spoon in her drink.

                                        “Now, come a little closer.” Rime asked.

                                        Diane nodded again and Felicity moved to stand near the bed.

                                        Rime reached out with a slightly shaking hand and touched the glass. He focused for a moment and frost started to form on the outside of the glass. Felicity started in surprise but held on to the cup. Soon the juice inside froze and Rime let his hand drop back down to the bed.

                                        “There, now pull on the spoon.” He suggested.

                                        Felicity pulled on the spoon and was delighted to see that she now had a cherry popsicle. “That’s wicked!” Felicity exclaimed. “How’d you do that?”

                                        “I can manipulate ice.” He responded.

                                        Felicity happily started licking her popsicle. “Thank you Mr. Rime.” Her smile was radiant.

                                        Rime felt that tightness in his chest again but brushed it aside assuming it was something to do with his broken ribs. He didn’t even realize that his lips quirked up in a small smile of his own.

                                        Felicity practically skipped out of the room and Rime could hear her exclaiming to the first person she saw that she had a popsicle. Rime heard Travers grunt but not say anything else. Diane smiled at Rime.


                                        “Thank you for that.”

                                        “It was nothing.”

                                        “Not to her it wasn’t. Did you see the smile on her face? I haven’t seen her so happy in a long time.” Diane paused as she fussed with the sheet covering Rime. “When Felicity came back she found that a Fetch had been sent in her place and she lost her family. We took her in, but she still misses her parents and her little brother. She’s usually so sad. She’s only been back a couple of months now. Not long enough to get over the loss just yet. So, to see her smile like that is encouraging.”

                                        “I’m sorry for her.”

                                        “You didn’t do anything wrong, in fact you did something right. So, thank you.”

                                        Rime nodded.


                                        The next couple of weeks passed quickly. Once Rime was able to stay awake for longer periods of time Diane brought in some books for him to read. Sometimes he pretended to sleep while Diane sketched in her book. Felicity would come to visit occasionally as well, and Rime made her several more popsicles of different flavors. Liam also came to talk, and true to his word, Travers did not come back or even show his face in the room. The antlered man did not try and ask questions that Rime had been reluctant to answer in the past. Instead he stuck to safe topics or asked general questions about what it was like to live in the Lady’s realm. Rime answered in broad terms, but did answer. He found much to his surprise that he liked the Spring Courtier and enjoyed his conversations with him.

                                        One day Liam was talking to Rime. “So, the option of that Oath is still on the table if you’re interested, Rime.

                                        “What exactly would it entail?” the younger man asked.

                                        “It would be a simple promise not to harm anyone in the freehold, including taking anyone to the Lady’s realm.”

                                        “You make it sound so simple.”

                                        “It is. If you agree then you can go where you please as soon as you’re able.” Liam smiled.

                                        “What happens if I were to break the oath? What is the consequence?”

                                        “The freehold would only agree on death. If we were to let you go free then we need a guarantee that you’ll keep your word.”

                                        “I’ll need to think about this. If you don’t mind.” Rime said softly.
                                        “That’s fine…”

                                        The door opened and a young man rushed in. His wooden joints creaked as he stopped in front on Liam. “We have a situation. The mo…You’re needed.”

                                        “Okay…I’ll go talk to the others.” He said to the young manikin. He then looked at Rime. “I’ll have to take my leave of you for now. Jesse here will be outside your door should you need anything.” He walked quickly out of the room leaving Jesse behind.

                                        “I’ll be right outside.” The manikin said with a tense smile.

                                        Rime nodded, his brow furrowed in confusion. As Jesse stepped out and closed the door the young elemental could hear the lock click in place and wondered what was going on. He was left alone for a short time until Jesse came in with a sandwich and glass of tea for dinner. He stayed just long enough to put the food on the table. Rime paced around the room or read one of the books that Diane had left for him. He’d been reading for an hour or so when the door suddenly burst open. An unknown man came rushing in and pulled Rime to his feet.

                                        “We need the bed, quick. Step to the side.” There was an edge of worry in the older man’s face. Rime did as he was asked and stepped back to the corner of the room near the door.

                                        A moment later Jesse came running in carrying a small bundle covered in blood. It took a moment for Rime to realize that the small bundle was Felicity. He stood in shock and stared as Diane ran in and knelt on the floor next to the bed. She quickly assessed where the worst of the damage was and put her hands on the child’s abdomen. She frowned in concentration as she poured healing energy into the small body. She worked for several minutes. Sweat started to pour off her forehead and the pale young lady just blinked through it. After what seemed like forever Diane knelt back with a sob and wrapped her arms around herself. She cried as she knelt on the floor. Jesse offered to get Liam, but Diane said that it was too late.

                                        “She’s gone. I couldn’t fix the damage fast enough.” Diane cried. Jesse knelt beside the upset woman and put his arms around her shoulders. Rime stood forgotten at the back of the room and stared at the little girl lying on the bed, her eyes looking at the ceiling unseeing. The young loyalist felt a sharp twinge in his chest and he rubbed his sternum to make the feeling go away, but it did not. If anything it got worse. A moment later Liam came in and made his way over to Diane.

                                        “I’m sorry I wasn’t here to help.” He said quietly.

                                        “There was just too much damage. She was so small.”

                                        Liam nodded and pulled the sheet up over the young fawn girl after closing her eyes.

                                        “How are the others?” Diane asked after a moment.

                                        “They’ll be fine. Roger was the worst off, but he’ll be fine in a couple of days.”

                                        Rime stepped forward and cleared his throat. “What happened?” He asked quietly.

                                        Diane wiped her eyes and stood up to face the young man. “It was Rook. He came looking for both of us.”
                                        “We eventually had enough people out that he figured discretion was better and he left.” Liam added.

                                        Jesse looked between his two friends and Rime before clearing his own throat. “I’ll take care of Felicity. We’ll get Sharon to handle things discreetly at the funeral parlor.” He leaned over and picked up the small bundle that was the little girl and headed out the door.

                                        Liam followed the manikin with his eyes before looking back to Rime. “You better stay here for the moment. I’m going to go check on things.”

                                        Rime nodded and watched the Spring Courtier leave the room.

                                        Diane stayed behind and fussed with the remaining sheets, balled them up and put them in the corner of the room. The blood went through to the mattress and Diane let out a little sob. She wiped her eyes and said, “I better get those sheets soaking in cold water.”

                                        Rime nodded and watched her leave. He sat on the straight backed chair and looked around the room. The same room that he’d gotten so used to over the past couple of weeks seemed like a foreign place now. He crossed his arms and studiously avoided looking at the blood stained mattress next to him. A few minutes later Diane came back into the room, agitated.

                                        “You need to get out of here.” She said with a hint of panic in her voice.

                                        “Why? What’s going on? I thought Liam wanted me to take that Oath before I left?”

                                        “Damn the Oath. They’re voting right now on whether or not to kill you! They say it’s too risky to keep you here, alive.”

                                        Rime’s reply was interrupted by the sound of voices in the hallway.

                                        “They’re voting now. I’m confident they’ll see reason and we can get rid of the damned loyalist.” Travers voice sounded almost smug.

                                        “I thought they were going to get him to promise not to hurt anyone?” Jesse replied.

                                        “An Oath doesn’t keep his shadowy buddy from showing up now does it?”

                                        “I suppose not. But will killing him really stop things?”

                                        “Yes. They’ll have nothing to come back here for. Diane is too well protected and their little buddy will be dead and gone. We also won’t have to worry about the little bastard doing something while he’s here. It’s his fault Felicity is dead. That guy only came because the loyalist was here.” The ogre growled.

                                        “Still doesn’t feel right to me.”

                                        “You’re still young. Trust me, this way is for the best.”

                                        Diane looked worriedly at Rime who had stood up and wrapped his arms around his middle. The voices outside the door quieted as the two speakers moved further down the hall.

                                        “It’s okay. I’ve been expecting this.” Rime looked at Diane.

                                        “It’s not fair. It’s not your fault. Any of it.” Diane’s gaze hardened. “I’m not going to let them kill you.”

                                        “It’s…” Rime started.

                                        “No.” Diane held up a hand. “I’m going to go talk to Travers and try and make him see reason. We may be gone for a few minutes.” Diane walked up to Rime and kissed him on the cheek. “You take care of yourself, okay?”

                                        Rime touched his cheek surprised at the sudden warmth and nodded. Diane quickly left the room and the young man could hear her haranguing Travers about his inflexible attitude. The voices got quieter as the pair moved further still down the hall way. A moment later Rime heard Jesse mutter something about a bathroom and his footsteps sounded down the hall. The young loyalist opened the door just a crack and looked out. There was no one in the hallway, though he heard muffled voices coming from two rooms down the hall to his left. He hesitated only a moment before slipping out. He took a second to orient himself, then headed down the hall to his right towards the front door. He found a grey hoodie hanging on a coat rack and pulled it on over his shirt and pulled up the hood. He peered out the window on the front door and saw that it was getting dark out and no one was on the street. After a moment he slowly opened the front door and slipped outside. The summer night air was still humid and made Rime uncomfortably hot in the borrowed clothes, but he wanted something that would hide his face. He casually walked down the street until he got closer to the park at which point he hopped the low fence and strode purposefully toward the walking trail at the back of the park. It led further into the woods and to a Hedge gate that Rime knew about. He made half way to the gate when he heard a familiar voice call out.

                                        “Rime! I’ve been looking for you. What happened?” Rooks low voice sounded from further up the trail.

                                        Rime stopped for a second before continuing forward. They both stopped when they got closer.

                                        “Are you alright?” The dark figure asked.

                                        “I’m okay.”

                                        “Then what kept you? You’ve been gone for so long the Lady was becoming concerned that you lost your way.”

                                        “I did not lose my way. I ran into trouble while trying to return Diane, er Delight. There was an ogre who beat be badly. I was just now able to get away.”

                                        “Is this something that we need to take care of?” Rooks voice took on a serious tone.

                                        “No, they are ready for you since your earlier visit.”

                                        “They may be ready for me, but they’re not ready for both of us.” Rook’s smile widened. “Come brother, let’s teach the people of this town why we are feared in Arcadia.”

                                        Rime hesitated. “I don’t think this is a good idea.”

                                        “Why not, brother? We don’t have to take revenge if you don’t want. Just let me know where we can find Delight and we’ll take care of our original mission and leave.” Rook frowned.

                                        “No.”

                                        “What do you mean, no?” Rook demanded.

                                        “I mean, we’re not taking Delight back.” Rime clenched his shaking hands.

                                        “What’s gotten into your head Rime? The Lady has ordered us to return her Treasure, and that’s exactly what we’ll do.”

                                        “No.” Rime repeated.

                                        “Are you betraying the Lady?” Rook asked incredulously.

                                        “In that I’m not going to let you take back Diane or hurt these people, then yes.”

                                        “Perhaps you were hit a bit too hard in the head by that ogre, Rime. We have a job to do.”

                                        “No. I’m not going to let you take Diane back.” The pale man swayed slightly on his feet.

                                        “Rime, you’re in no condition to fight me. I’ll win.” Rook pointed out.

                                        “Maybe, but I’m still not going to let you touch them.” Rime said determinedly and formed a dagger of ice out of the moisture in the air with some difficulty.

                                        “You would draw a blade on me, brother?” Rook drew a dagger from the sheath at his waist. “You’ll have to stop me then.”

                                        Rime nodded and launched himself forward holding his dagger against his arm, edge out. He feinted to the left and made an attack with his right hand. Rook blocked left and felt his brother’s dagger cut him along the side. He jumped back and spun in the direction the attack had come from and launched one of his own. His twin blades flashed and Rime parried and dodged each one. His arms shook with the effort it took. He tried to hide the involuntary movement by moving his left arm into an offensive position and twirling his ice blade in the other hand.

                                        Rook took the initiative and made another attack on his friend. He attacked with each hand, Rime defending each time but missed the quick kick that lashed out and collided with his knee. Rook threw his weight behind the last attack and swept Rime’s legs out from under him. Rime landed hard and tried to suppress a gasp of pain as his healing ribs were jostled.

                                        “Give up now Rime. I don’t really want to hurt you, but if you continue to do this I will have no choice.”

                                        Rime coughed painfully and levered himself to his feet. “Then do what you must, because I’m not letting you take her back.”

                                        Rook smiled ruefully. “What happened to you Rime? How did they get you to turn against the Lady?”

                                        Rime shook his head. “You killed a little girl Rook. She was only twelve. How could she have possibly been a threat?”

                                        “Collateral damage.” Rook replied. “You know how it goes. Sometimes people get caught in the crossfire. They don’t matter though. Only the mission matters.”

                                        “But she did matter Rook. She was just a little girl.”

                                        “She was one of ‘them’ Rime. One of those that betrayed their masters and ran away. She shouldn’t have been here anyway.”

                                        The pale knight swayed on his feet but stood blocking the path to town. “Then you’ll have to come through me.”

                                        “So be it.” Rook’s expression hardened and he attacked twice in quick succession. Rime realized that the attacks before were almost gentle compared to this. He dodged the first strike but the second got through. He gripped his side where Rook’s blade had cut deep. He lashed out with an attack of his own and tried to drive his one-time brother back toward the trees. Rook stepped back and Rime pressed the attack again. The younger man thought he’d gotten close enough but when his knife flashed out it hit nothing but air. Rook danced to the side and kicked up leaves and twigs from the side of the path. Rime’s hand immediately went to block the detritus and suddenly realized his mistake. He tried to step back but was too slow. Rook stepped forward and grabbed Rime’s shoulder with one hand and plunged a dagger into the younger man’s gut with the other. Rime gasped in shock.

                                        “Now, do you yield brother?” Rook asked the younger man as he withdrew his knife.

                                        “No.” Rime coughed.

                                        “Very well.” Rook rasped and his blades flashed two more times. Rime felt the first strike hit up and under his ribs while the second took him in the shoulder.

                                        Rime staggered and his knees started to buckle. His own blade fell from nerveless fingers and hit the forest floor. Rook sheathed his knives in one swift movement and stepped forward to catch Rime as he started to fall. He pulled Rime’s uninjured shoulder over his own and put his other arm around Rime’s waist.

                                        “You…can’t…have…her…” Rime rasped, blood flecked on his lips.

                                        “For now brother. Right now I need to bring you to the Lady. She will want to know of your betrayal.”

                                        “You could …just… leave… me here.” Rime said quietly.

                                        “No, I can’t.” Rook replied and started walking toward the gate. Rime stumbled as his feet staggered forward. He couldn’t seem to get his legs to work properly. Three more steps and he tripped over a root, slipped out of Rook’s grasp and fell hard to his knees. He caught himself on his good arm and wrapped the other around his middle.

                                        “Can’t…walk…” Rime choked out.

                                        “You’ve suffered worse. You can walk.” Rook reached down and grabbed his brother’s shoulder.

                                        Rime smiled sadly and his teeth were bloody. “Not…this time…Rook.” He coughed hard and grabbed the shadowed hand. His breathes came in short gasps as he struggled to take his next breath.

                                        “Shit.” Rook swore and leaned down to pick up his friend in a fireman’s carry. He walked with quick purpose to the gate and strode through to get to his Lady’s palace.

                                        In the world a sudden thunderstorm sprung up and washed the ground clean of blood or any sign of a fight. And a loan dagger made of ice slowly melted as the warm rain came down.
