        Huh. Just learned something new. Wrought iron is an alloy. ~98% iron and 2% slag (stones and rocks). Hand wrought iron means blacksmith forged wrought iron. So if it's the craftsmanship that is the main factor then I'd say any kind of artisan forged iron (whether its steel or wrought iron) should be effective. If, on the other hand, it's the slag in the alloy that makes the difference, then it shouldn't matter if it's hand forged or mass produced.

                                                I've yet to play a session of WoD / C:tL, so take it with a grain of salt, but I've done a bit of reading and I've got my opinions:

                                                It's "cold iron" if the Storyteller says it is. The inconsistency in the RAW gives them wiggle room since the definition itself is incomplete, unknown, and/or subject to change over generations. Common steel isn't going to cut it (...pun not intended) for whatever reasons made steel and the forging processes conceptually different in human/Changeling/Whatever consciousness. Breaking the fourth wall a bit, the idea is that a person has to go through some additional trouble and/or expense to get a viable cold iron weapon. I tell my Storyteller that I want a cold iron weapon, ask NPC scholars for their opinions, and roll my Occult skill checks. I go on a quest to retrieve a cold iron antique, hand work a ferrous meteorite into a knife shape, or embark on a zany chain-of-deals quest to get a "proven" cold iron weapon from a rather attached owner who'll trade it for a rare baseball card that just happens to be on sale at the Goblin Market for the price of a colorless red dream. (Hmm... Can I sleep furiously to get one?)

                                                On "supernatural": I simply think it's wrong-headed to acknowledge the category as meaningful beyond being a label for stuff we don't understand (yet?). There are rules that define how the world works, and we will never know all of them. To me, "supernatural" is a junk drawer category we put stuff into to give ourselves the illusion of understanding. As if everything we label as such had some proven common feature, aside from that poor understanding. We understand pretty well how the universe works in the absence of the Wyrd, Wyld, Wyrm, and whatevyr the wryters dream up. The WoD is a place that adds an additional variety of strange forces and variables that make a mess of our understanding of how the world works IRL. By design, these exotic forces and entities produce plot devices for a horror/mystery story. The data are noisy and sparse, but those people reading the Occult section are good at generating working hypotheses. If they're wrong, you'll just have to go to your backup plan. Or accept your doom.

                                                Horror is about uncertainty and the fear the unknown brings. We don't know what exactly makes something "cold iron" or why it works against the Gentry. We just have some lore that's risen out of a dubious set of seemingly conflicting data. If you can guarantee that my cold iron knife will work against my Keeper because the rules in the book absolutely define "cold iron", you remove a piece of that uncertainty. "Did I mess something up when making it? Is there some technicality we don't know about that I missed?" Clear knowledge is empowering. Horror is about lacking power, and mystery is about lacking knowledge.

                                                This isn't a chess tournament where rules have to be objective, fair, and aboveboard. Players don't "win" in the WoD by understanding how to work a concrete board where the rules are the same from one game to the next, or even determined ahead of time. This is a game where we have to trust a human Storyteller (you are human, right? ...Right?) to direct an engaging experience and not abuse that trust for the lulz of a Killer GM or to play favorites among the players. If your character wants to know how stuff bumps in the night or better ways of bumping back, well, he'll have to perform experiments just like we do in real life, get results from the Storyteller's description of the outcomes, and maybe, just maybe, he'll learn a little bit more and live to share that kernel of knowledge with future generations.

                                                Repeat to yourself, 'it's just a show. I really should relax.' For Mystery Science Theater 3000!

                                                        Originally posted by ReshyShira

                                                        They're beings of stories from long ago when we knew little about the world. One of the reasons why Iron is supposed to be effective against them is because smelted, highly pure iron is unnatural and thus the antithesis of the Fae.
                                                        I agree with the first sentence of this quote completely. I would say your "antithesis to nature" theory makes steel, plastic, concrete, drywall, lead paint, asbestos, and almost modern surface found in any western civilization would burn the True Fae, which wouldn't make a good story, which is more reliable descriptor of True Fae and Fairy Tales, than 'Embodies Nature' which your theory implicitly presumes. The Pied Piper, Cinderella's Fairy Godmother, The Frog Prince, and many other fairy tale characters who'd serve well in a Chronicle as a True Fae were not an agent or embodiment of nature.

                                                        While it is true many fairies represented natural phenomenon or places, such as pixies, sprites, nymphs, kelpies, drowning hags, and some incarnations of elves, others do not fit that category like household brownies/hobs, leprechauns, red caps and many other creatures. The noteworthy stories told about these creatures are their most common denominator.

                                                        Also looking at the history of iron based superstitions, it had power over witches, ghosts, foul spirits, the fair folk, and many malevolent supernatural beings and forces. One point was the insertion of an iron nail into the head of a corpse to prevent it from rising as a vampire.

                                                        Originally posted by ReshyShira

                                                        Also my dear friend, everything is chemistry.
                                                        Here I must disagree with you. For how the True Fae and Arcadia is described, portrayed, and explained, it seem literally true that for them the universe isn't made of atoms, but of stories. I mean "literally" in the sense of exactly as written without figure of speech. For a True Fae or in Arcadia it seems that the notion of 'Atoms' or even basic physics are not only meaningless but also completely inaccurate to cover any of the phenomenon internal to the True Fae or Arcadia. I would not be surprised in the slightest if an electron microscope, spectrometer, or even litmus paper from a a grade school chemistry set failed to get readings, got nonsensical readings (like an electron microscope producing a picture of a the True Fae face when used on a drop of water in its realm), or non-reproducible and thus non-scientific results.

                                                        True Fae and Arcadia give license to hang up a sign that reads "science need not apply".

                                                        Now to my theory why iron that has not been shaped by magic or machine is because iron has a magical property, perhaps because it has consistently and cross culturally played role as enemy of evil forces since truly ancient times, which is blotted out by other "magic" (to included more advanced technologies). I borrow the idea that technology is a form of hedge magic practicable by anyone with the knowledge from Mage the Ascension, which is very appropriate because of all the current supernatural types True Fae best embody non-objective reality based on concepts and/or belief.

                                                        How would I define Wrought Iron in my chronicle? I would say I'd make a distinction between Wrought Iron and Wrought Steel. The distinction would be slag, non alloyed silicon impurities which are mechanically present and increase the brittleness and jaggedness of the metal when it is snapped. The difference between cast and wrought would also be important, the metal being worked by a blacksmith by hand. I would rule these work in the same way condition catches for fae contracts, or listening to Radio Free Fae. I would keep the it can't be worked by magic or cut by machine because those sound like clauses and loopholes that fit the flavor of how True Fae deal with reality. Does that mean my tiny diamond edged drill bit and power drill that I use to punch a small inconspicuous hole in the tang of your dagger and replace the handle wrapping would stop your "wrought iron dagger" from working when you needed it most? Absolutely! Welcome to the paranoia of the Lost.



                                                        On the topic of "iron bullets" any problems would be caused by guns that use rifling, like all modern pistols and obviously rifles. You know what type of gun doesn't use rifling? Shotguns, even semi-automatic or fully-automatic shotguns.
