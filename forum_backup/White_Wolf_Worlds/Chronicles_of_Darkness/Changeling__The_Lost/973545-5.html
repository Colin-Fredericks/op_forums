                        The Hedge is the fae world as it relates to others. Painful, yes, but also beautiful and capable of giving enlightenment beyond the rational and the conscious. To invoke a favorite anime of mine, it is a world without an AT Field, where people simply cannot run away from the feeling of themselves and others. This is both extreme boon and unforgivable curse.

                        That's why the Keepers fear it so. The Hedge will not let you leave unless you feel. The Others build entire pocket universes out of their desire to not feel. They fear pain, and there's few worse pains than watching someone you care for be hurt, or to be reminded of your weaknesses. And that is why the Keepers can't emphasize-they really, really don't want to.

                                I feel like my point was thoroughly missed. I am not asking for in-universe justification; I'm looking for a narrative one. What does the Hedge add to Changeling? What does it produce? What themes does it create and/or exemplify? And, in that context, what does pain serve?

                                To me, the Hedge always existed as The Woods in fairytales; that place children are told not to wander into and do anyway, becoming lost (heh) in the process. It provides the narrative grounds whereby characters can stumble on inviting old cottages, dragon dens, dwarven mines, and such-like. I liked that aspect.

                                1e struggled, though, and always managed to make going into the Hedge about risk, rather than reward. It was a place you went when you had to, not when you wanted. That made all the usual fae tropes into warning signs that smart players avoided, rather than one they would be willing to risk. Granny's secretly a wolf, the dragon is not asleep, and the dwarves will make you their servant every single time. The Hedge is a place you go on sufferance, if you go at all.

                                Making the Hedge about pain, first, foremost, always, is not learning from this mistake; it is repeating it.

                                So I ask, because maybe there is something I am missing: Why pain? What benefit does it add that nothing else will?

                                                            Honestly, I think this is the disconnect.

                                                            Everything the Hedge does has the capacity to cause pain. "Can" is not the same as "will". As was made clear in the revisions, the pain could be the difficulties that inevitably come with true love with an actual person-it doesn't mean it isn't true love, it's just that life isn't a Harlequin romance. One day, no matter how much you love your partner mutually, one of you will die, and that will sting. But the best-case scenario has it that it will be of old age, after having lived through thick and thin together, the fire probably dulling but never ever truly fading. It can even be tangential to the actual treasures-in another example from the revisions, there's the summation of all knowledge, right there in the hoard, except oops, the very hungry and territorial dragon is guarding it, and you have to get through its very sharp and painful teeth somehow. It's only when something loses that capacity to cause pain does it vanish from the Hedge-and honestly, name five things in the world that cannot actually cause any degree of discomfort.

                                                            The Hedge isn't a place of endless misery, it's a place of endless adventure-of the actual, you-may-starve-to-death-or-get-eaten-by-a-bear kind. You should fear it, that means you have a survival instinct. But people want adventure for a reason, there's a treasure at the end of the road. It's just a very hard road.
