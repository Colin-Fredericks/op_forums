            Perfected materials ignore all secondary qualifications, of which "can't be made by magic" is one. Siderite counts as Cold Iron.

            By RAW, anyway. You can always say that in a changeling game that the prohibition against magically-made iron still applies, in which case its only naturally-occuring Siderite that would count. But the way Perfected Materials work was invented partly because we didn't know what the final definition of "cold" was/is going to be, and we wanted it to count no matter what.

            Plus, it's more interesting and easier than trying to figure out bonus damage or something like that.

            And really, given any Fate adept can horribly fuck up a Changeling's day by cancelling their Contracts, the game balance of mage/changeling crossovers is rather one sided anyway.

                                                Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                                You could always rule the existence of (and the Changeling's being a party of) any given Contract uses the Withstand and Clash of Wills ratings of the Wyrd Itself.
                                                The Wyrd is more than content to heap adversity on its constituents, and in fact the strongest defense changelings have against outside supernatural tampering is based on the stat that operates in near-direct opposition to Wyrd.

                                                That means any attempt by a Fate Mage to tamper with Contracts ends in "lol, no" (same as trying to mind control Angels - the God-Machine just always does way better than you possibly could).
                                                The entire deal with changeling magic has historically been that it's the Joker of supernatural effects — beats anything, gets beaten by anything. Most effects that aren't ongoing deals last half a day at best because you're using dream-logic transactions to make the world work like a fairy tale in particular way for long enough to accomplish a given narrative beat.

                                                The God-Machine uses angels to accomplish tasks and is an enormous material-world edifice built around those tasks being accomplished, with potentially disastrous reality-warping consequences for those tasks not being carried out. It makes sense for an activity that could force the creation of a gameline-PC character to not be allowed by mages without the level of magic that necessitates a story to facilitate.

                                                The Wyrd is a web of agreements and exchanges and consequences that connects the fae to the waking world — once an effect has been paid for through catches or Glamour, a Contract is out of its hands, because caveat emptor is a longstanding rule of the metaphysical marketplace it presides over. You don't buy magic-insurance with your fleeting and ephemeral supernatural gentlefae's agreement, because trading in dreams has never been the most secure of practices at the best of times. None of the things Contracts facilitate would break setting assumptions by their early termination.

                                                The comparison fits very poorly.
