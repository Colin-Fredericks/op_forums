      Cold iron is iron that has not been enchanted, shaped, conjured, or otherwise altered with magic. Hand-forged iron is cold iron that has not even been shaped by human-held fire but by raw force. Meteoric iron is a form of cold iron that is particularly sought-after because the heat of atmospheric entry has smelted the ore naturally, thus negating the need to work with cheats like the one added to the Smith kith blessing in Rites of Spring.

                                      Technically, "Cold Iron" is a poetic term. It has no meaning beyond sounding pretty. Like "cold hard cash." Cold Iron in literature and folklore can refer to be both metal or steel and I cannot think of any reference outside of modern fantasy and roleplaying games that make note of any special processing (or lack thereof) that is necessary to confer it's magical properties against the supernatural.

                                      However, the rules in the game are very specific when it comes to outlining what "Cold Iron" is, in the context of the setting.

                                              Using real world logic and metallurgy does not apply to "Cold Iron" in the context of Changeling: The Lost. In "Rites of Spring" there are several pages devoted to Iron, its different manifestations and its effect on fairies. Only iron that has not been heated by a man-made fire or manipulated via magic counts as "Cold Iron." Other types of iron (pig iron, wrought iron, etc etc) still affect fairies in different ways, but not to the same extent that the mythical "Cold Iron" is known for.

                                                          Something that got me through a lot of these same issues is asking the question, "Is it Iron?" Noticed how I capitolized that. Many things would be considered iron or to have iron in them, but to be Iron proper it has to be the pure elemental metal. For example, iron ore has many of the properties of Iron in regards to the Fae, but it isn't exactly Iron until it's been smelted. Once it's been made into "reasonably pure" Iron that is when you have to be delicate with it or else it could be ruined. A Smith has more options, because their Kith blessing says they know how to apply just a little bit of heat in order to make Hand Forged Iron that much more easily, meaning they get to bend the rules a little. Anything more dramatic and it loses the benefits of being Hand Forged to being Cold Iron (it is still pure iron, after all), so if it is cast into a mold, forged, tempered, etc., it is a little less effective. Once it is called something else though, like mixing in some carbon to make it steel, it isn't iron at all anymore. Anything that is naturally smelted, like meteoric Iron or the above example of a volcano, that would still count.
