        Winter Soldier* showcases a good summary of the problem with making the time gap too large. Yes, Steve is certainly Lost, but he's so lost that he has to forge new anchors in the world-he has nothing of his life to build his roads with. Lost characters, by contrast, are lost enough that getting home isn't easy, but they can still build their roads out of their ruined lives.

        *I am not saying that Winter Soldier is a bad movie, for the record, it's quite good, but it highlights the problems of the concept for the basic Lost chronicle.

                                My $0.02:

                                Out-of-time changelings (say, within the last hundred years or so) are possible, though rare. They generally need a very *strong* tie to the Mortal world in order to find their way back, and the tie needs to be something which won't have changed dramatically since their abduction: The ancestral family home, the office (maintained by a historical society) in which they performed the work which consumed their life, or the traditional barracks of the armed force in which they served their country could all serve as beacons for the return trip. Again, however, such recoveries are *much* more rare than those which occur within a natural human lifespan - say, 50 years or so to represent adulthood.

                                This allows for the fish out of temporal water storylines while also not watering down the thematics of the venue too much. As for Clarity, well, a newly arrived Changeling is always going to have Clarity troubles until their grasp on reality firms up again. I'd say these guys just have a bit more trouble than most.
