                                                    Well, then! A new human-splat for the changelings! Its certainly exciting to see the Lost join the ranks of the ghoul, new wolfblood, and sleepwalkers/proximi. It is certainly interesting!


                                                    The Good
                                                    Oh, my, where to start? I love the idea of the mortal+ splat being drawn by a promise to rescue their beloved friend/family/significant other. It feels like the fae touched are kind of like Search and Rescue people to the changeling's being Lost. Which is just wonderful balance. I can see a lot of potential there. I must admit, I do like the idea of being fundamentally bonded to one of the fae people, much like the ghouls are bound to a specific vampire, and it would be interesting to see how the Courts play out that bond. Especially if the one we're bound to goes bad. I must say that the mortal+ have been some of my favorite material in 2e, and Lost's version does not disappoint on that matter!

                                                    While the origin of the FT is pretty strict, what the actual touched can be like, do, or act is wonderfully detailed in great lengths. Court, outcast, and loyalist FT are rather nice touches. The loyalist touched actually have left me with a good idea for a plot going forwards that I'm interested in running as soon as the book comes out!

                                                    Abilities - giving them some pledge crafting is rather curious. Now, we haven't seen what we can do, but I do find the ability to pledge only the fae to be rather curious, making the unwary fae fall into a word trap. Can't really talk more about this without more information about the different kinds of pledges, we seem to be involved with. I can't really comment on the whole Contract bit, though I would like to say that I like the idea of being restricted to Seeming-only ones. The EXP tax, though, seems unnecessary

                                                    Hedge addiction and Madness - amusing in a "down the rabbit hole" way, keep going back. Though, I think it should be in the form of new Conditions, if not the standard addiction Conditions we already have. Its curious, how we crave the rush of being inflicted with Madness. I keep going back to Alice, who keeps going down the rabbit hole, only to come back Mad as a Hatter. Or maybe the Hatter she met was a touched with Madness.

                                                    Merits - with the exception of dream (see below), I do like the merits, a lot which emphases the role as promise keepers. The "Promised To" line is especially interesting, as it is a kind of reinforcement of fae-touched-seemings, imho. I liked it in vamp and wolf when the did it as well.

                                                    The Bad
                                                    I join the ranks of those who feel that the fae touched are portrayed as exceedingly rare and difficult to arrange. Now, I know that the actual rarity is up to the ST to decide, but I think that the portrayal, as written, is going to influence readers in a way that's less than desirable. Not only are we talking about 1) a promise of some kind, 2) realize the fetch isn't real person, and 3) stumbling into the Hedge, but also 4) finding the way out, 5) surviving hostile terrain, and possibly 6) finding a changeling to teach them. Its a very specific set of circumstances that likely requires some amount of skill. So, I'd almost like to see all of them have some guide for that initial entering of the Hedge, and perhaps something supernatural to guide them towards the changeling, and perhaps away from danger, and back home. This is touched upon in the Drawback section, but it really should have something in the advantage section too. Speaking of the Arcadian Dreams... the penalty should really come in the form of a Condition.

                                                    The relationship between changelings and fae touched seems a bit odd. In one part, it talks about how most changelings keep fae touched at a distance, then the very next paragraph, we're talking about how relationships are widely varied. Later on, under Oath Keepers, it talks about how they hope to be reunited with their others, but both earlier and later we're talking about how the fae touched entering the Hedge helps trigger an escape, and can meet up with the changeling. I feel that we're getting too many mixed messages here - a bit of clarification would be lovely on the bond between changeling and touched. Its such a central part of the character that I think it needs to be explored more.

                                                    I have to ask why keeping all promises is important to the fae touched - there are plenty of people that will only keep their words to close friends and family, and have no qualms about breaking any other promise. Or maybe only a promise sworn under certain circumstances. Why so restrictive? What's so markedly different than standard pledging? It feels like they're trying to be more truthful than the changelings are required to be! Especially when they can only pledge with fae.

                                                    Short Lifespan - just... yeah. Moving on.

                                                    Hedge Addiction and Madness - while the addiction to the hedge was nicely covered, there's a notable lack of the madness that fae touched will suffer from when they return from the Hedge. I think we should really have some word count devoted to that.

                                                    I'm not sure how I feel about the glamour pool being so high, and the lack of ability to use catches. My knee jerk reaction is that it should actually be reversed, activating Contracts only through catches (thanks to activating tokens via catches in the past). Now, unless catches are vastly different than 1e, the explanation doesn't make sense to me - catches are just certain actions you take, which are very easy to teach. Especially when they can form pledges and balance those out. I'm guessing its a thematic thing, where we don't want the touched to act and dress like the fae, or something to that effect? I'm not sure on it. I don't know if its bad, but I'd like to test it out, or hear some idea why its this way.

                                                    Merits - Dream merits are way too expensive for something that you basically need changeling help to do in the first place. Its a lot of investment for something that may not come up. In my experience, the dreams tend to be less important unless necessary for plot, at which point, we're looking at a tax to join a story. Not a fan.

                                                    Fae Mein - makes no sense from what we know of Seemings and the like. No reason to restrict Contracts based on having a mein or not - its arbitrarily restrictive.

                                                    The Ugly
                                                    Pedro. Naught, naughty boy for leaving his beloved friend to rot so long in the Hedge without telling anyone.
