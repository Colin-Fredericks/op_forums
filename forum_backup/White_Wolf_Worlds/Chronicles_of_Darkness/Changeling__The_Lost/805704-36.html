            So, there are two things I want to comment on in regards to my monolith back there.

            1) So, despite spending a huge time explaining why bleakness is important, I don't like it. I may have said I will never go back is what gives the series its' bite, but that was because I was making a point for the dark side of Changeling. I'm not doing that now, so truth be told, it's the Long Way Home that gives its' bite.

            Well, actually, as I alluded to, neither of those is true. It's a tug of war between the two, threatening to tear a changeling apart-but you will, or you should, spend more time on building home than risking pulling it apart. I know this pretty well, as a player. My first character, Noah Pyre, was someone I bugged my GM to let me do all sorts of side stories. I literally built my Hollow and Hedge Garden from the ground up, doing several stories of stealing stuff from the junkyard and making bargains at the Goblin Market. I researched contracts and scoured the Hedge on my own time. Noah ended up as an insanely powerful character because I spent a lot of my side time building home. Honestly, it was the most fun I ever had, and it had great unintended consequences, because I became this crazy powerful nuke of the Autumn Court that was reclusive. I wasn't far away from going True Fae in the eyes of the freehold, so when Candelabra, my keeper, attacked my Hollow, it was just me and my motley, and I was holding the door while getting everyone else out predominantly. That was such a prominent moment of Long Way Home versus I will never go back home for me. I was actually able to win it, too.

            Building a home and trust and hope-that was where a lot of the fun of the game was for me. I enjoyed tending to my garden and trying to keep it from devouring my flesh and Clarity. I enjoyed going through the Hedge to find forces to bargain with for Contracts, with nothing but an old, moldy book as a guide. But I needed the enemies at the gate to make the game effective-not because I constantly needed challenges, but because I had to think I constantly had challenges. I needed the paranoia to inevitably learn to not let it ruin Noah's life.

            2) Speaking of enemies at the gate, man, do I get not using the Loyalists in a Coup like manner, or anyone else for that matter. That was a recent awareness thing. In the beginning, I used them like the Wild Hunt, right alongside the True Fae. Looking back, I can see had a level of dissatisfaction with that, but the idea of the hunt was so strong in my mind, I just did it this way.

            But as I played the game and read and learned more, it started hitting me how that was a little unrealistic. It wasn't effective. It made more sense to have traitors, after a few readings of the core, people who found the niche place to make people disappear. But I enjoyed my outsiders at the gate too. I eventually fell out of favor of using loyalists all together, actually, focusing a lot on court politics. In the middle of that, the realization of how much any given changeling worries about their safety hit me.

            It took me contrasting Lost with Descent for me it finally get back into using the Loyalists, and I used them a lot to play games of three card monte with the freehold while also shaking their chains and getting people to think about jumping ship. Even then, the Loyalists as a whole never really did it for me-they were more a faceless mob, with every face I made for them kind of feeling too little. The Huntsmen fixed a lot of that for me.

            But anyways, the big thing is that Changeling is not actually a big game of Coup. Loyalists can and should be used that way, take advantage of the fact they are versatile in a way Huntsmen aren't, but Changeling isn't Coup. It's not Descent.

            I referenced my Inspiration from Demon thread shortly after the last because I wanted to support the idea that the big thing about Changeling is that any given character really is less at risk than they think they are. Not that they're safe, mind you-their paranoia is legitimate, their fear is real. But Changeling is more of the fear in your head than the fear without. In this way, you can't play the game like Coup or Descent. You can't constantly push the I will never go back button. And the game as it is now keeps that in Mind. The Huntsmen might come inevitably, but not now, and even when they do, not constantly. It's not a constant pressure on a freehold. It's a car accident. Most of the time, you spend your time driving, and you avoid shit, but you worry about it happening anyways, until you become calm and confident enough to not just crash willy nilly.

            When you come across a car accident, or worse get in one yourself, it naturally ramps up your fear of crashes again. And that's life until you get to a point where you are capable enough that even when you fear the car crash, you're ready to handle the possibilites. That's Changeling.

                                    Re: bleakness vs. hope in Changeling, I see them as two sides of the same coin. Changeling is only able to be the hopeful game that it is because it has the potential to be extremely bleak. Borrowing from Arc's wonderful post again, the hopeful message is that in spite of everything, you can win. You can make it home. But that victory is only as inspiring as the adversity it triumphed over. That's why I'm always confused when I see statements like "Changeling is the darkest line" or "Changeling is the brightest line." Because to me it's both. The dark is needed to give the light context.

                                            Originally posted by ShadowKnight1224 View Post
                                            I suppose I'm a bit worried that too much distrust and not enough trust will make changeling society hard to run as a ST. Sometimes I just want to run a game where the Freehold is mostly fine, at least for the time being, especially in small towns and the like.
                                            I think that's a reasonable concern, and I think this is exactly why the writeup cautions ST's to think carefully about when and if to use Huntsman. In the normal course of play, they should be a back-of-the-mind sort of danger. You might have your contingency plan for if you ever have to deal with a Huntsman, but you probably don't expect to have to use it. I love Arc's comparison to a car accident. That's exactly the sort of thing we all know is inevitable, but we tend to think "but it won't happen to me." Until someone you know gets in an accident, or you have a close call yourself.

                                            So most of the time, the Freehold is fine. Until the ST starts making a point of describing every time one of the players sees the words Bad Wolf. The players start to worry about the significance of those words, and the frequency with which they keep appearing. They start to wonder if it might be a Huntsman's Tell. Paranoia starts to build. You check and re-check the Bulwark rituals to make sure everything is still holding up. You make a trip to the Goblin Market to see if you can get your hands on a Token of Reprieve just in case. And maybe that's all that ever comes of it. Maybe the words stop appearing as often, and eventually, the ST stops pointing them out alltogether. Maybe you got yourself all worked up over nothing. Maybe things are finally back to normal.

                                            This gives the ST valves to control the flow of paranoia. It can be a constant slow drip, or a build up and then die down, or an occasional sudden burst that disappears as quickly and unexpectedly as it arrived. Most likely, some mix of all of the above. All of this without ever needing to actually use a Huntsman.

                                            The story where the Huntsman actually shows up can be a powerful one, but you don't want to use it every game. Just like you don't do an Idigam story every time you play Werewolf, or a Strix story every time you play Vampire. But their existance allows you to tease the possibility that maybe this is the game where he's actually going to break out the Huntsmen/Idigam/Strix.
