    [2E] How the Fairest aren't Leaders but Protagonists

    12-09-2015, 09:01 PM
    I was reading quite a bit of the Fairest discussion thread and people thinking Fairest are pigeonholed into heroic leadership positions, how they're too bright and kind and so unlike the True Fae. I wanted to share my two cents without necro-ing a long dead thread.

    Something resounded in me early in the thread about how they have a martyr-complex, how they take on the responsibility as "leader" or "hero". This can absolutely be something completely selfless, but also completely selfish. They can take the burden reluctantly, or be very egotistical.

    Regardless like all the Seemings your story changes fascilitating the escape. The narrative of the escape becomes about the Fairest, they become the protagonist and "hero" and the Wyrd marks them with these expectations. They internalize it as a blessing and a curse. When they live up to these expectations, they benefit from it. When things not mean't to happen in the protagonists story happens due to their actions, they are broken by it. They take a physically fairer form because protagonists in fairytales are always attractive, or atleast that's how the solipistic Gentry see themselves. That's the key right there.

    The Gentry of Arcadia are the "protagonists" of their worlds. They are the hero, they can do no wrong, the story is theirs and theirs alone. Everything else is props, setting, supporting characters, villains, but the story is about them.

    When the Elemental escapes, their ego and character falls away and they become like the Setting. When the Darkling escapes, betrayal and deceit mark them as the Villain. When the Fairest escapes they become the focus, the Protagonist. They become closer to what the True Fae are than any other.

                      I think this reading is exactly right but I caution against the notion that they are self-obsessed and vain by nature.

                      Imagine a vain person always WANTS to be the one at the center of the story, the protagonist, right? They try to make things about them, try to control things. That's where we get those ideas of vanity and self-obsession and other unflattering words.

                      The Fairest escaped through this leadership/protagonist effect, but it's not necessarily something they actively seek out, it's a part of their Changeling nature that people seem to look to them and stuff like that. Some might take it poorly, going the reluctant hero rought or trying to reject it. Other people might embrace it, and others still might abuse it and internalize it, BECOMING those vain, self-obsessed people.

                      The extra disturbing part is the subtle implication that this protagonist/leadership effect (particularly among changelings) is actually an echo of the True Fae themselves. One way of reading that is that your Wyrd nature is taking advantage of a low level of stockholm's syndrome to "take charge" in the story of the Changelings. Yikes!

                      Hence, the Fairest are still the most like the True Fae, but it's rendered in a deeper fashion. How characters react to this and their level of awareness of it will vary, of course.

                          I don't necessarily equate heroism or leadership with protagonism.

                          Plenty of stories have leaders and heroes in support positions. Many of them have them as antagonists, or foils for the anti-hero protagonist. I think reading the Fairest as protagonists is missing the point IMO. The Fairest is the person that stands up and does a good thing for someone else. That's it. That's what it boils down to. The Fairest regains agency when they take a stand and help someone else.

                          That means others expect them to be leaders and continue their heroism, but it doesn't mean they've snatched the "protagonism" of the story. It just means that the story has slotted them into that role and expects them to keep fulfilling it. A Fairest can play "Da Chief" to a Darkling rogue cop protagonist, or the authority figure that covers for the Wizened's social transgressions in pursuit of the solution for the mystery of the week (much like House MD), or the mentor that sacrifices himself for a young Ogre that must learn to open their heart, or the evil Gaston-like antagonist to the Beast protagonist (with Belle being an Ensorcelled mortal).

                          There are so many stories that can be told where the Fairest are not the protagonists.

                              Your character is the protagonist. Any player character is the protagonist. By virtue of the metaphysics of the game extending beyond the fourth wall, the game will react and respond to you as the protagonist.

                              Granted, some are more protagonistic than others-The hierarchy is those who have a Seeming above those who don't, with maybe an exception of the Loyalist Seeming. Regardless, a Seeming is a declaration that one is, for whatever virtue, larger than the world trying to hold them down, and as such is a subsequent promise to the Wyrd-if it ever comes up and I had a say in the matter, I would have a Seeming be a significant part of your 'True Name' as far as the WoD gives as shit about that. Anyways, in the acts that redefine you in the eyes of the Wyrd, you inherently are declaring your protagonistic nature.

                              A Fairest is not more or less protagonistic than a Beast, an Elemental, or an Ogre. The Fairest is merely a different kind of main character from other ones, one that presupposes different arcs than the others. It looks more protagonistic because of our bias towards/against figures of gravitational persona/fate, but the number of lone tricksters who don't have fiefdoms naturally spring up around them(if not inherently there) who are protagonists in fairy tales kills the argument.

                              On a tangential note, I could totally write a noir story with a Fairest protagonist as easily as I could a Darkling one.
