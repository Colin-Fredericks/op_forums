        Even a regular human can get away with kidnapping/murder with the right equipment and no discernible motivation. Not to mention the fact that the Fae are potentially infinite, while the Changelings are just a small percentage of their victims (the majority, I imagine, never escape Arcadia). Those Clauses you spoke of that allow you to predict the future don't really help you determine if a Fae you have never encountered is about to kidnap a human you have never met. Changelings don't have the manpower to protect every mortal on Earth, or even any noteworthy percentage of mortals on Earth, from beings that can strike at any time, anywhere, to take any one. Let's face it, even a fae with no powers and stats that are perfectly normal for an average human can still just rock up with a needle full of tranquiliser, come out of the Hedge into someone's house and stick the needle in their neck before they wake up. Protecting a single human close to the player characters from being taken is a story unto itself, and it's entirely possible that the players could be successful. I disagree with the notion that the same process could be replicated to create any form of systematic defence against Fae activity.

        Now, in my game, the group has faced Fae precisely twice. Once was a player's Keeper, required a story of research to discover his weaknesses, and ended in a climatic confrontation where the Keeper was slain. This was all despite the fact that, the whole time, the Keeper was statted out in such a way that a group of three combat characters could quite easily have killed him. In fact, two of the group's three players were perfectly capable of tackling this Keeper in a one-on-one fight. He wasn't even that strong a fighter. If your GM needs his monsters to have stupidly high stats in order to force the players into a story to defeat them, then I'm afraid he's just not trying to play his monsters intelligently.                 You're right, I have no idea what your game is like, but I do find it hard to believe that you need a monster to be capable of fighting the entire group for it to be anything but anti-climatic. You can give it Hobgoblin/Wisp retainers to slow down most of the group, for example. Or, better, you can play on the horror aspects of the game and run a monster that relies on separating the group and picking them off one by one. World of Darkness is a system that vastly preferences the side with the superior numbers, and a group is almost always going to have a fairly easy time rolling over any one monster in a straight fight, no matter how strong it is. To get a Fae that can't be beaten in a straight fight by a group of Changelings, especially if those Changelings are half-way competent player characters, you pretty much have to drop something like the dragon. The fact is that most Fae are not at this level. Even in Equinox Roads. A Fae with 10 in every conceivable stat, for example, still drops fairly fast to 3-4 characters with half-way decent combat stats using iron ranged weapons of some sort. Range to ignore defence, iron to ignore armour, assume a fairly low dice pool of 8 per combat character from 2-3 Attribute, 2-3 Skill, Specialty, and 2-3 Equipment bonus and add Willpower for somewhere between 2 and 4 successes per turn per character... if 4 characters get 4 successes and the Fae is "only" Size 6 Stamina 10 then they just killed it in one round.

                That doesn't mean the monster can't be terrifying, and that the final confrontation with a Fae can't be tense and exciting, even if they are comparatively "weak" by having no stat higher than 6. There was only one member of the group still standing by the time the confrontation came with the Keeper, and I can assure you that it was a tense fight. Raw stats, I would argue, are just about the last thing you need to make a story exciting and tense. I would wager most GMs with a good grasp of horror mechanics and a willingness to run their NPCs intelligently could create a gripping story with a primary antagonist in the form of a lone mortal with Strength 2, Weaponry 1, and an appropriate specialty. And I know this for a fact, because I've managed to do pretty much that using an Ensorcelled human. Even if the final fight with such a weak character can be exhilarating, if you pick the right location and circumstances and avoid having it be a front up gentleman's bout of boxing in a white room. If you can't do the same using a creature with all the heightened power of a Fae, I'm at a loss. Fae are strong as it is, and the only reason I can think of as to why you would need to make them stronger than they are in the books would be if you were trying to run the game like a D&D dungeon crawl with the Fae as a boss monster at the end. That's a perfectly valid way of playing, but it means you're working in a framework a little bit different from the one the World of Darkness books seem to be assuming you'll be using.

                We're getting a way off-topic though. Suffice to say that citing the way Fae work in your game, which you admit is different from the way they work in the books, is not hugely relevant in a discussion about how to change the rules of the magic system in said book.
