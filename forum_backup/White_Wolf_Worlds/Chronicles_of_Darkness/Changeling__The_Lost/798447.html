    Oaths made on false premises

    01-02-2016, 07:04 PM
    I'm wondering what sort of interest story quirks could be derived from situations like this:
    "But you swore an oath on your name that you'd get me through this safely"
    "The oath was 'I'll get you through this safely or my name isn't Jeremy Smith'. My name isn't Jeremy Smith. It's George Finch"

    Though perhaps the issue would be whether or not the Wyrd would even be willing to sanctify such an oath and/or if it would fit the setting to have some way (probably Goblin Contract) to falsify the sensation of the Wyrd sanctifying an oath.

    Alternatively, thoughts on how the Wyrd would punish this kind of smart ass.

      In the case of the example, the problem is that there's nothing for the Wyrd to anchor onto. The Wyrd is a connective force, but it can't connect a name to an action if there's no name to act on.

      Now, if you had a name not your own, literally owned it, it might work. But you'd have to be careful with how you owned it, less it become you, and the Wyrd snips you in the rear for breaking your promise.

      I could see a really cool Goblin Contract around that.

                          Oaths (with a capital O) need something strong to anchor onto, otherwise it can't support that sort of magical power. Names have power, thus you can use them for Oaths. Fake names that you invent on the fly... that's not as likely to work.

                          However, maybe if you put extreme effort into establishing a fake name, used it for years as your fake identity and it had real meaning then it could support an Oath (in other words you spend XP on the Fake Identity merit). A Fake Identity with that sort of weight behind it, would probably be perceived by the Wyrd as similar to a Gentry's Title. However breaking that Oath would then have real consequences. Personally I'd say the Wyrd would slap you upside the head any time you were using that fake identity from then on but leave you alone the rest of the time.

                              This is clearly a smartly worded Vow with an easy loop hole, that is cleverly disguised as an Oath on a True Name, This is a staple of changeling and fae trickery, and why you never accept someone swearing on their True Name unless you are really sure you know it. There is even an example of people not accepting a real Oath on a person's True Name in Changeling: the Lost because they don't believe that's his actual True Name. That example wouldn't exist if it wasn't possible to pull this type of shenanigans. I'll post the page number when I get home tonight and have access to my books, unless someone posts a reply with the page number first.

                              The Wyrd would totally sanctify this Vow, the fact that it appear to be an Oath and has a huge loop hole is immaterial. This is one reason why changelings are paranoid about wordings of Oaths and Vows. The Wyrd allows huge loop holes.

                              The Wyrd specifically doesn't punish this type "smart ass" which is exactly why the True Fae are such horrible monsters.

                                                          This took a little thinking. I couldn't quite put my finger on it but then I started trying to craft the pledge from the point of view of someone not trying to be tricky. Lots of changeling use false names after all. What would it look like for Mr Notmyrealname made this pledge. That's when it became obvious. They're swearing a Corporal pledge of a Mortal Emblem. They're risking their identity as that person. New Identity merit---gone. Ability to use that name anymore---Fate makes sure you can't.

                                                          Task: Endeavor Guide to Safety -3
                                                          Boon: Combat oriented Blessing +2
                                                          Sanctions: NONE
                                                          Duration: Day+1

                                                          If they betray the person they are supposed to protect the pledge still breaks but nothing bad actually happens because of it. Well, aside from the potential Clarity loss and the Wyrd forcibly divesting you of your assumed identity. But if they're amoral enough to do this in the first place, that's not likely going to deter them.
