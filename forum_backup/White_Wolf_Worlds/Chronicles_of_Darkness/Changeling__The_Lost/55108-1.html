            Not a ST, so I have not tested the question you are asking. But my guess would be you'd want to explore two things to get your answer.

            First, how much ST work do you want to do? How long has the Gentry been getting away with the Durance and Escape = the Lost cycle? If you are willing to have Lost who are over a hundred years old, that’s a different population answer than if between recapture or death by old age the Lost population is lowering as often as someone escapes the Durance.

            It is tempting (to me) to say that additions are balanced by deaths and therefore the Lost Courts are very interested in latest escapees. They could pretend to be indifferent, but would track and question them in either case.

            Second, what is the relationship of the Gentry ‘problem’ to the Real World? Another way to say this is, since the Lost population is directly a result of Gentry manipulation, and since the ST decides how often the characters 'escape' the Durance.... you have some logic path to deciding how much base population of escapees you are dealing with.

            My thought on this is that the Gentry appear to be tremendously powerful. You might compare this power to wealth in the Real World. If The Wealthy lose a favorite servant, They are annoyed but They replace them. If They lose Their favorite painting/sculpture, They start to push buttons and pay people to find Their Treasure. Perhaps for this reason, I see various Kith being more likely sought after than others---when they escape (Fairest or Elementals might have much harder time escaping Durance). Setting all that in context, if you have 4 to 6 escapees every 7 years or so, it is a tiny trickle, something like the POW escapes of WWII (where I should note, almost all escapees were recaptured, rarely did they truly escape as we see in movies.)

            But we want to play a game with more than a trickle of drama.... so perhaps we can look at it this way: extra-ordinary folks attract notice of the Gentry, so escapes are more common than you might expect. The Gentry are also easily distracted by Other Drama. They don't have Their fingers much in the Real World. Result: taking mortals to the Gentry lands is statistically common (replacements are needed and the Gentry have a powerful need for new amusements), but escapes are rare.

            If the Gentry were attracted to mortals and acting on a 1 in 10,000 model. They’d be kidnapping 3000 people from the USA a year. The statistics for USA quote over 800k missing persons a year. No wonder the Gentry trafficking problem isn’t being noticed!

            If you then figure, just for someplace to start, that 1 in a 100 folks escape, you have 30 returns (new Lost arriving back in the world) a year in the USA.

            Now that does not seem like enough people to tell stories about, but wait, let’s add some more layers.

            What if the USA has ten Lost regional courts? So each might see 3 new Lost a year.
            What if some Courts actually spend time making escapes easier, doing works in the Hedge, or providing infrastructure for a ‘Lost Railroad’ of freedom? Double those specific numbers could be a bonus in certain Courts. Or you could work with Nodes and Paths that have been hidden and protected that make some Railroads work better.

            Big Cities might be disadvantaged with more Gentry attention, more kidnappings, few if any escapees crossing the Hedge; small cities and towns might actually be the place the Lost escapees more often cross the Hedge. A Big City might also be a place where Gentry are more likely to reacquire escaped Lost.

            And what if rural locations then are Freeholds and smaller Courts that are important to the Big City Courts especially because they are more likely to get ‘new blood’?

            Regardless of whether you scale this up or down, the Lost community should be fairly stable, and narratives can expect very few Lost every few years to arrive. Maybe as a policy, not much fuss is made over new PCs, or maybe it just looks that way to the confused and traumatized new PCs.

            Small cities have small Courts, and more likely have active ‘escapee politics’.

            Small towns have Freeholds, and more likely have to be ‘politic’ about keeping new Lost that show up there. They record the latest Lost for the small city Courts. Who in turn advise the Big City Courts. At some point, the Courts make offers to Lost, like grants or scholarships from Universities competing for talent.

            So a small town of 30,000 may have a freehold of 30 Lost? But lots of visitors from Small or Big Courts who keep an eye on this ‘Hedge Railroad’ location?

                30 to 40 individuals in a social group is plenty dynamic when it comes to matters of intrigue. Especially when you factor in the broader fairy community consisting of Hedge dwellers, goblin markets, fetches, ghosts and dream-critters. Also remember that changelings don't live on an island, there is an entire mortal world that they must maintain contacts and alliances with ... which most likely includes family members, witches, paranormal investigators and the enchanted. With Hedge travel and modern transportation being what it is, changelings also aren't isolated from other freeholds and Lost that scheme there. You can still have vast conspiracies that shake your game to its core with a relatively small changeling support cast.

                However, just as a matter of personal taste, I do not like constant, antagonistic intra-freehold intrigues as an accepted norm in my Changeling games. A freehold's purpose is to provide a safe haven to heal for the psychologically and spiritually tormented survivors of fairy malice. There has to be an expected level of trust, safety and structured support present. If the freehold's courtiers and motleys are all bickering and backstabbing each other ... what the hell is the point of joining a court and freehold at all? Might as well be Courtless and live off the grid. Freeholds filled with infighting are freeholds that fall apart. Banishment from a freehold is one of the worst punishments for one of the Lost in my games; Not only have you been thrown out of a support structure that can provide you with protection from Them, but now a bad reputation follows you around. What freehold wants to bring a quarrelsome and duplicitous courtier into its midst? They have enough problems to worry about (Loyalists, privateers, dream poison, hunters etc etc etc) without taking on a potential traitorous courtier who has problems working with others.

                            A Court is your "subculture" (Hipster, Rocker, Hip Hop head etc etc), a freehold is your "scene" (a collection of local peeps that are all acquainted with each other at various levels and attend the same shows and hang out at the same spots) and a motley is your "clique" or your "crew" (your good friends that you hang out with all the time, have inside jokes with etc etc)

                                Everyone wants something, and sometimes they need help to get those things. Changelings in a small Freehold, at least in my case, rarely go after political power for political power's sake. Rather, they seek to cultivate friends and acquaintances that will be useful for them in their personal endeavours. The Queen of Autumn has a collection of Tokens, and is always looking to add to it. One of the Summer Court has the skill set to acquire those items for her. However, a bad break-up with a different Summer Court member has left the Queen not feeling too great towards the Summer Court, and the feelings are largely reciprocated (at least among that courtier's circle of friends, which the first Summer Courtier may or may not belong to). So the Queen will look for some way to exert some influence over the Summer Courtier, ideally through a member of her own Court who knows that Courtier (perhaps due to being in a Motley with him).

                                But really, I feel the politics won't be that different. People still lie and plot and try to twist others into doing what they want. The King of Summer is just as likely to seek the ruination of someone who insulted him in front of a gathering whether he's in a large Freehold or a small one. Vices like Pride, Greed, and Lust all crop up within small groups, and can have all the more devastating effects due to the size of the group and the personal nature of the betrayals. Personally, I find the raw number of participants way less important when it comes to dynamic and conflicting political ambitions than strong personalities and characters.

We were one house of one year of the school, so there were maybe 40 of us. It can accomplish feats that no one on their own can do, even with "only" a single class's worth of Changelings.

The motley give you allies you can fall back on who will (hopefully) have similar objectives to your own.

                                        PCs can fill out Pledge slots pretty quickly if they want, and it fills out fastest if you try to grab pledges with individuals rather than groups like a Motley. So not sure why that would be necessary.

                                        Your motley are your friends. You don't necessarily agree with them all the time, but they're your allies. They're the ones you can rely on to back you up. Your Freehold should do the same when faced with an external threat (the Fae generally, my games don't include any non-Changeling supernaturals), but not every issue is that big. If you have a problem with someone in the Freehold who is not in your Motley, then you should be able to rely on your Motley to back you up even if you can't rely on the entire Freehold. Just to take an easy example.

                                        I'm pretty baffled by the comment "the 35 person freehold is different form the 4 person motley because they are not the same". A 35 person Freehold is different from a 4 person Motley for a fairly obvious reason, I would think. There are 35 people in the Freehold, and 4 in the Motley. That means that the Motley is a small group, and the Freehold is a bigger group. That's literally all the two terms mean in terms of Changeling anyway. If your Freehold is small enough and perfect enough that every single member is a close friend, then maybe there is no difference between the terms.

                                        The easiest way I can think of to describe that is to use your own example of close-knit friends within a community. You don't always get along with your friends, no. You don't always get along with your community either. But you see a reason to draw a distinction between the group you call your close-knit friends and the community at large. The same distinction can probably be drawn to say why a Freehold is different from a Motley.

                                                Thanks for bringing up the Oath aspect of the changeling psyche in relation to changeling social structure, because I feel it is an important facet of this conversation.

                                                Changelings, for the most part, are all pretty much borderline and crazy. They struggle with their perceptions and emotions, and due to the victimization that they have experienced, have learned that trusting others can lead to a horrible end. Opening themselves to any sort of outside influence, especially when it concerns other fairy people, runs a serious risk when it comes to their well-being and sanity. Luckily, they have the ability to organize themselves through the use of arcane promises that provide tangible benefits and punishments concerning the sanctity of the pledge. The fact that they have a limited number of pledges they can swear to keeps their circle of trust relatively small. After all, the likelihood of dealing with an oathbreaker rises the more people are in on a promise. For most changelings, I'd imagine that divulging that level of trust (even if enforced by magic) to a group large enough that a significant portion of it is effectively anonymous would be nightmarish. Nothing frightens a changeling more than a fairy face that they do not recognize. That stranger could be a Loyalist, a privateer, or just an insane changeling who will bite your face off and leave your body to be eaten by goblin dogs off in the Hedge.

                                                I imagine that trust increases with Clarity and with Wyrd (after all, you can swear more oaths), which is why more established changelings join extended social circles like Entitlements, which have a much broader and expansive membership than a standard freehold.

                                                    Huh, I am genuinely surprised. I really was thinking of courts on a very different scale. When thinking about Changelings and what kinds of stories to tell, I thought about groups that suffer from t PTSD and alienation: people in the military and victims of human trafficking, as well as oppressed, outside groups like transgender people. Also, I was considering some particularities of Changelings:
                                                    1) since they harvest emotions and don't cause damage (or much damage) to the human population, they are not limited to being an apex predator like a vampire
                                                    2) there are weird (wyrd) things happening with Time and the Hedge-- sometimes your minute in Fae lands is 60 years in the mortal realm, sometimes your 120 years in Faery is an hour in the mortal realm. This would create some strange things in terms of Changelings returning: why wouldn't people from different times be showing up? I know the book has a sentence in it that claims no one before the 20th cen. will come out of the Hedge, but I won't let that ruin my fun. In short, there is a random influx and outflow of persons from the Hedge and there has been for a very long time. Since my game is set in the U.S., a percentage of my Changelings are native American and some are from a precolonial time period.
                                                    3) Fetches:What percentage of the population are fetches? It could be very, very high, or very, very low, who is to know? If you insist that Fetches are infertile, then in the U.S., that's about 6 million people. But that can't tell the whole story-- maybe that young mother was snatched right after her baby was born, so she's not infertile, she just only planned on having one child. When people become Lost, it's often an invisible crime. If a lot fewer Changelings make it out than are taken, then there are many fetches living their lives perhaps never aware of their fetch status.

                                                    So all these ideas were milling around and I decided that for my game, Changelings in the United States were between one half of one percent and 1% of the total U.S. population: so no less than about 1,570,000 So a small group, but not tiny. So in my central setting of Athens, Ohio, there are maybe 100 to 200 Lost. The local court, Hawk Woods Court of the Midwest Territories (in the old growth forest in Ohio) has about a thousand subjects, although not all of them congregate at the salamander earthwork barrow mound-- there are two Duchies (Salt and Copper Duchies) that serve the Midwest Territories. I've got about 20 Changeling Territories mapped on North America.
