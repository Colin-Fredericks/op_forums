                    Originally posted by Mr. Feral View Post
                    I think what I am saying here is being deeply misunderstood, as is the process of crafting these archetypes itself. What I want is a description of the defining features, not specific events. You can tell me a darkling is untrustworthy, duplicitous, and given to trusting his judgment over that of others and I can draw my own conclusions. The mechanical lattice supports and informs my character choices. These things can both be described by generalities and have a consistent theme. I don't see why you feel the need to draw the firm line in the sand that either a character is precisely one version of a mindset or a bland, meaningless caricature.
                    I don't think anyone is trying to say that. I think some of us just identify very strongly with this very accurate depiction of a particular abuse survivor narrative - without getting to personal here, this story rings very true to me, and from what Omegaphallic has said, presumably to them as well. We just don't want to lose that just so more concepts can be made to fit the Seeming.

                    Originally posted by Mr. Feral View Post
                    People become jaded and self-reliant for many reasons other than betrayal, but even if you want to say the theme is that of betrayal, it can be done while still making clear that there are many divergent types. I want the chance to breathe my own life into the character I create, not have that life given to me.
                    When I read this writeup, I don't see "Darklings are Jaded and self-reliant." I see an all too familiar narrative about someone who is preyed upon because they are too trusting, or too loyal, or too generous, and the only way they can escape from that is to change their attitude, to accept that what they thought was just "doing the right thing" was making them a target for that abuse, that it will only end if they change their habits. And even once they've gotten free, it still affects them for the rest of their lives. They have difficulty putting their faith in others because they've done that before and it got them hurt. They find comfort in their newfound power to do what needs to be done, even when it may not seem like "the right thing to do" because they've seen what "doing the right thing" can lead to.

                    Now there are a LOT of ways this particular story can play out. If your character concept doesn't fit within that narrative, you should very seriously consider the possibility that that character is a different Seeming, because they have evidently not experienced the same kind of trauma that leads one to become a Darkling.

                    Could the writeup stand to have more varied examples? Sure. I didn't feel that need because I immediately identified with what someone in this kind of narrative goes through, but I can certainly see the value in giving more examples for others who don't identify with it as intuitively. I could even get behind broadening some of the language to make it more clear that this is one way the story might go, but not the only way. But I definitely do not want this incredibly powerful look into one means of a victim reclaiming agency to be watered down so it can accommodate stories that don't actually fit this very real abuse survivor narrative.

                                        Hi. I'm the writer. Just wanted to drop some thoughts: Please keep in mind first and foremost, that this is a first draft, and I can and will tighten the language. Thank you for the input.

                                        1)I was very careful to say 'many' and 'most' or ever 'some' in the text. I talked about trends not absolutes. Take a look, it's in there. I did plot out a specific skeleton for players to build their own flesh on to, but how many arms, legs, heads, all that stuff, that's on you. I also, because I'm talking about trends, want you to know that you don't have to use my skeleton in whole.

                                        2)Why make it so specific instead of something more generic? Because I'm a professional writer and I want to tell stories with my work. If you don't want to use my story starters, you don't have to. I'm not going to come to your home and tell you that Murder Mc Loves No One is officially 'NOT A DARKLING' and burn your books. I don't care what you do with it at your table, I care that what I put in the book is evocative, tantalising, and inspirational. That means being less generic. If you look over the thread, you'll see that many people are inspired by the specificity here. And we're all adults (I hope) so of course we're all aware that everyone is going to take the parts they like and chunk the rest. That's kinda what gamers do.

                                        3)If you REALLY want me to make more generic material, my rate is 5 cents a word, half up front, half on turn over of the second draft.

                                        I can answer more specific questions if you want, but I don't want to spend too much time here because I'd rather be working on game material than answering the same six questions over and over, yanno?
