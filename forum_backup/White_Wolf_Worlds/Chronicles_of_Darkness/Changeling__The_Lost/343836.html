    Fae Actors - 2e Hack

    12-18-2014, 09:45 AM
    So banking on my excitement from preliminary outline for Changeling The Lost Second Edition, I basically decided to toss together all non-contradictory bits about the personified avatars of the True Fae from Autumn Nightmares and Equinox Road, the Actors, set down guidelines for building them with the simplified ephemeral entity stats, and add some little personal alterations for a second edition hack that is also compatible with the first edition. I'll admit that I aped the attribute allocation from the Strix and their scaling with Shadow Potency, but it seemed like it fit my purposes. Given the changes in the second edition, I also remade their Immortal Flesh trait, as noted in the second post.



    Actors

    True Fae manifested as Actors use the simplified stats for ephemeral entities as per the Second Edition rules update. All Actors possess the Immortal Flesh and Ruled By Passion traits. Like Changelings, they store, harvest, and expend Glamour, and possess Wyrd (with every associated advantage) at a minimum of 5. They also have up to a number of Mien Blessings that replicate Kith blessings, Seemings, or unique abilities. Their Wyrd and other characteristics scale with the amount of Titles the True Fae possesses.

    Number of Titles Wyrd Attribute/Skill Maximum Attribute Dots Maximum Mien Blessings
    0 5 5 10-15 2
    1 6 6 13-18 2
    2 7 7 13-18 3
    3 8 8 16-21 3
    4 9 9 16-21 3
    5 10 10 19-24 4
    6+ 10 10 19-24 4

    Mien: The otherworldly mien of the Actors grants them certain benefits (mien blessings), and it is as malleable and changeable as the Gentry wish. When the True Fae manifest an Actor, they can chose its appearance and change its blessings - a Title could as easily be a knight of flaming insides, or a titan of black ice - , but every possible form possesses the same characteristic that cannot be disguised. This Tell is inherent to the Title used to make the Actor.

    The Mask: It costs the True Fae one point of Glamour to turn his Mask on, and one point to turn it off. This mask is imperfect - not so much that it should rat them out, but it gives an odd or disconcerting edge to their appearance to a keen eye. It is hard for Changelings to pierce through the mask (they reflexively roll Wits+Occult+Wyrd Vs Power+Wyrd of the fae), but they can spend one point of Glamour to see through it for one turn. When they exit the Hedge or a mortal's dreams, a True Fae can spend two Glamour and assume the form of a mortal. To attempt to assume the appearance of a mortal they haven't seen before or a semi-original human form, they must succeed in a Power+Subterfuge roll at a -3. While bearing the mask, they do not benefit from any Blessing directly associated with their Mien (for the most part, this refers to those that have a physical manifestation), but can access their every other power. Seeing a Fae without its Mask tends to inspire awe and terror in mortals, though the strength of the reaction depends on their willpower. The vision is such that they contrive themselves to forget, justifying it in some strange but mundane way as soon as it is out of sight. They hide the memory within their own mind and compensate for it. The exception is in dreams, which the appearance of the Other may plague for weeks.

    Healing: Unlike Changelings, True Fae can expend Glamour to heal Bashing or Lethal damage on a one by one basis.

    Harvesting Glamour: The True Fae harvest Glamour much in the same way as Changelings do, except for two cases. The Fae gain no sustenance from emotion that lacks even the slightest tinge of selfishness. The Gentry require emotion to possess some flavor that is known to them. Once per stay in the human world, they may also gain three Glamour from destroying something utterly - it is not simply from the act of destruction that they gain this power. They are ultimately mimics and copies, not creators, but by breaking down something into parts, they chase an understanding of it, to draw them closer to the real thing.

    Skills: The Gentry have a base Skill dot spread of 20/12/6, adding 1 dot to every priority for Title they possess. The True Fae are also limited to one dot in Empathy, Expression, and Computers. In the Hedge or Arcadia, these skills can go up to three dots. They can also have as many Specialties as their amount of Titles.

    Merits: Actors begin with up to 10 dots of Merits to spend, plus one for every Title the True Fae holds. However, they rarely possess merits that are distinctly human.

    Advantages: True Fae possess Virtues and Vices, but only have Morality scores if they pick up human habits - and even then this is rarely true Integrity or Clarity, only a pale mirror of human nature. The Others can adjust Actors’ Size and Speed species factors’ at the moment of manifestation. Begin with a base of 10 dots to divide between each factor (minimum one dot each) and add 1 more dot per Title. Otherwise, calculate these as you would for a human.

    Contracts: The True Fae have at least 20 dots of Contracts, and they have no restrictions in which ones they may hold. At the Storyteller's discretion they may also possess more control over these and hold Contracts with more complex effects.

    Glamour: True Fae begin with maximum Glamour, and in Arcadia recover Glamour equal to their Wyrd at the start of every scene.

    Frailities: All Actors begin with one Minor Fraility, and possess more appropriate to their level of Wyrd.

    Banes: Actors all share the common banes of Cold Iron and Hand-Forged Iron. Weapons of these materials ignore any defenses from Contracts, Mien Blessings, or equipment that comes with the Actor. Cold Iron deals Lethal damage, while Hand-Forged Iron not only deals Aggravated damage, it also causes them to lose a point of Glamour for every point of damage dealt.

    Other Traits: All Actors possess the Immortal Flesh and Ruled By Passion traits.

    Equipment: Actors often come with items that adhere to a certain motif. They may be drawn from the theme of a specific Realm the True Fae also holds, follow the design of the Actor's Mien, synergize with the abilities of the avatar, or belong to the central domain of the Other. Sometimes they possess Tokens or Hedgespun wear, but most of the time their possessions have effectively the same characteristics as the real world items they resemble. The difference is solely that they are made from the matter of Arcadia, so they may work a little strangely - be slightly better in some way, while having a strange appearance or mark. Armor of this kind also offers no protection from the ravages of Cold Iron.

    Language: The Fae have no language of their own. Like other things, they pilfer it from the mortal world and different realms. Most of the time, Actors can speak in nearly every human language, as well as some that do not belong to mortals - such as First Tongue of spirits or the Atlantean High Speech.

    What's In A Title?
    Not all Titles are necessarily created the same. Intrinsically, they are complex and powerful contracts with the Wyrd; they do not set down hard, unbending rules and defined effects so much as guidelines and negotiable terms for their traits in any form the True Fae chooses to manifest them in. Some of these contracts are stronger than others - not because the agreements they represent have greater weight, but because they are bound by looser terms, given wider allowance of power. Assume that when an Actor manifests from a mightier title, its total number of attribute dots leans towards the maximum allowed for its level of Wyrd and that it has a greater amount of Merit and Contract dots than lesser aspects of the True Fae. They may also have the most Specialties and drastic Mien Blessings compared to other Titles.

      Entering The Play

      When the True Fae enter the mortal world or reveal themselves without the mask, their presence is felt with nothing short of ominous threat, though in ways not necessarily reflected in the Storyteller system mechanics. The weather darkens. Animals grow agitated, and people feel uneasy. The day seems gloomier. The approach of the Actor may cause strange, subtle effects. As they draw near, the shadows may seem alive, the wind howls in protest or salute, cheer and life hide or run, daylight and people avoid his path.


      Experience, Transience And Exile

      The Fae create, discard, and remake Actors at their whims, but it is not uncommon that they grow attached to a particular personality or certain version of an Actor. They become fascinated with the struggles of this character, and strive to follow them. Therefore, a True Fae can earn Experiences as a particular Actor and use them to improve the form's traits. These benefits do not extend to other Actors however.

      The world of mortals does not agree with True Fae. Their mercurial, fantastic nature cannot endure the ravages of the mundane forever, and eventually they wither and change. Actors can only stay on Earth for a given amount of time before reality begins to weight down on them and ruin their mien. They start to bleed out Glamour, their monstrous forms deteriorate, their gifts weaken. How long this takes depends on the Wyrd of the Actor. The greater their Wyrd, the longer they can stay on Earth without incurring negative effects.

      Actors who’ve been on Earth for a long time start to lose their powers in a more permanent fashion. Their ties to Arcadia fade, diminishing their traits over time, making them no better than powerful changelings. Some even acquire Clarity. There are many myths about Fae who forget their true natures. Once an Actor learns the way of Clarity, it loses dynamic control over the Wyrd, and can’t change its abilities. They become subject to the full weaknesses of the flesh and an earthly psyche, losing the Immortal Flesh and Ruled By Passion traits. Their mien becomes lesser, more grounded, and their mask becomes the basis of their mortal appearance.

      The exact time that it takes for these things to happen is up to storyteller discretion, as are the specifics and mechanics of these consequences. It should be noted that the process may depend on the circumstances around the Actor, and there may be more than one possible outcome, and more than one possible reason behind it. Not all such faerie aspects become depowered - some may simply die. And sometimes, it may have nothing to do with the time limit and the natural erosion of their power. Maybe the Fae was banished from Arcadia, reduced to a miserable piece of what it once was. Or perhaps the Actor was tainted by humanity, bound to Earth by a genuine connection with a mortal.

      On Earth, Actors cannot communicate with the faerie aspects left behind in Arcadia, and the True Fae can't tell what's happened to them either. If an Actor forgets what it is, the Fae is unable to help it remember unless they risk sending more manifestations to look for them.

      Negotiating Power
      Once manifested, a True Fae cannot make drastic changes to the form of an Actor, but the Title is still a fluid thing. At the storyteller's discretion, the Fae can renegotiate the terms of this unique contract with the Wyrd, swapping the Contract dots of the Actor and creating new ones, as well as changing its less fundamental Mien Blessings. However, they can only do this in Arcadia. The human world or even the Hedge will not do, for they both contaminate the Title with the brittle nature of reality (even if to very different levels).


      Mien Blessings

      These characteristics from the matter of dreams and legends are dependent on the forms the Others take, and they are as varied as the terrible appearances of their Actors. Most manifestations will have at least two of such blessings, with a myriad of possible effects. Any Seeming or Kith blessing can be taken as one of these, and the mien of the True Fae, free in a way Changelings' is not, can possess them in any combination. Or they may be unique and potent abilities exclusive to the Gentry. (see pg. 68 of Autumn Nightmares for examples and inspiration)


      Core Traits

      Immortal Flesh: True Fae are fantastic beings made of the fabric of dreams and madness. When manifested in the mortal world, they can shrug off the attempts of the mundane to damage them, and even the most destructive of harms will not be as severe to them. Any physical damage that does not come from Cold Iron will deal only bashing to the True Fae. Certain supernatural sources of damage can deal lethal to the Gentry, but only Hand-Forged Iron will deliver aggravated damage. Additionally, they are not affected by Combat Tilts unless they were caused by cold iron, hand-forged iron, or supernatural powers. Their flesh is also immune to natural infirmities. They do suffer wound penalties, but they can't be knocked unconscious by Bashing damage.

      Ruled By Passion: The True Fae are entities almost defined by their vices, with higher morality and ethical standards little more than whims to them. For them, passion (or what passes for it) is virtue, and denial of passion an affectation. This amoral nature is reflected in an inverted ability to regain Willpower. True Fae gain one Willpower from indulging their Virtue, and regain all of their Willpower from satiating their Vice.

                                                  I decided to use this hack as a base for representing the power level of Gentry inside Arcadia and the characteristics of Wisps/Props.

                                                  Fisher Kings And Queens

                                                  Prerequisites: A Title in Actor form, a Title in Realm form

                                                  Treat Actors who are inside their Realms as ephemeral beings of Rank 7. Look at the spells from the Common Practices of any Arcana that fit their influences for inspiration. Do not assume they know all or even most of the spells, though. It is recommended to not give them sensory range for their Arcana-As-Contracts. The Imperial Practices of Dynamics, Excision and Entities are normally reserved for turning mortals into changelings. Besides, the escaped changelings who are entering the Realm are immune to spells that cause instant death, extreme physical transformation or soul tampering. They received protection against such greater sorcery when they reclaimed their Agency. When an Actor mimics one of the Common Practices as a Contract, roll a Simplified Attribute + Skill + Wyrd and spend glamour as appropriate. They achieve bonus successes on rolls relating to their influence equal to their [Wyrd/2 round up]. However, they still can't heal aggravated damage.

                                                  The Actors also have the following benefits:

                                                  Nightmarish Endurance: they gain extra Health dots equal to [Wyrd].

                                                  Greater Healing: each point of Glamour spent to cure wounds heals 3 lethal or bashing damage.

                                                  Eyes And Ears Everywhere: at the start of each scene, the Actor may roll Finesse + Wyrd to have a general idea of what is going on inside its Realm. This roll does not benefit from the either of the bonus successes described above or willpower points. Success gives them a correct yes or no answer to one question. Exceptional Success allows complementary information. This benefit cannot be contested. Frequent questions and complementary information include:

                                                  1) Are there intruders in my Realm? If so, what are their numbers and location?

                                                  2) Are one or more of my servants/playthings trying to escape? If so, which ones, and how are they trying to accomplish that?

                                                  3) Are one or more of my kind taking actions against me in the Feud right now? If so, what are they doing?

                                                  4) Is there something important to me missing from my Realm? If so, what is it, when was it taken and by whom?

                                                  5) Are most of the goblin fruits in my garden ripe? If so, how many and what new properties will they manifest?

                                                  6) Have one or more inanimate objects brought into my Realm become magical or unusual in some way? If so, where are they and what blessings/curses did they acquire?

                                                  Mercurial Landscape: they may reshape any part of their Realms which are perceptible to them. When not contested, assume that they may reshape a number of Size units equal to [Wyrd x 10] per turn. Most of each Realm is made out of ephemera and will dissolve at the touch of Cold and Hand-Forged Iron. Tactical use of Mercurial Landscape can be done in three ways: offensive, defensive and movement.

                                                  1) Offensive: roll Power + Skill - Defense, and apply Armor. A number of targets equal to your Wyrd suffer your successes in bashing or lethal damage. Add [Wyrd/2 round down] as bonus successes. Reshaping the ground into large swords uses the Weaponry Skill, commanding the skies to bring down fire balls uses Intimidation, raising the corpses of defeated adversaries uses Occult and so on. This means that Gentry are often comfortable with repeating the same attacks, which can be explored to the characters' advantage.

                                                  2) Defensive: create a number of barriers with a total Size of [Wyrd x 10] and Durability equal to [Wyrd/2 round up].

                                                  3) Movement: you may create or remove bridges, doors, windows and the like.

                                                  Props:

                                                  While being used by an Actor inside Arcadia, Props give 8 Again on their powers, NOT bonus successes. Actors gain [Wyrd/2 round down] bonus successes on influence related rolls just by virtue of being inside the Realm. In the Hedge and elsewhere, Props may give bonus successes on their powers equal to [5 + number of Titles]. If the Prop can deal damage, convert each point of damage bonus above 5 into a point of Armor Piercing. If the Prop grants Armor, each point above 5 cancels 1 point of Armor Piercing instead of a success.

                                                  Wisps:

                                                  The system for Wisps in Equinox Road, while versatile, is a tad messy and not very impressive. Assume that each Title in Wisp form creates a number of beings equal to [5 + number of Titles x 4]. Each member of the Wisp has Wyrd 5, may have Simplified Attributes/Skills of up to 6 and has 5 to 15 dots of Contracts.

                                                  What counts as "Influences"?

                                                  Consider the general style, themes and atmosphere of the Actor and Realm. The Fae Influence may be defined with a few words by taking these characteristics into account. Assume that most Gentry have 2 or 3 Fae Influences. Suggestions:

                                                  1) Foundations Of The Earth: fire, water, air, plants, stone, electricity, light, darkness and more. Choose one type, with a few closely related ones. Gentry cannot have Fae Influence over every element.

                                                  2) Insights Of Man: technology, art or both. May be old and new.

                                                  3) Beasts Great And Small: may involve normal creatures, fantastical ones or both.

                                                  4) The Heart's Many Strings: emotions and thoughts. Choose one type, with a few closely related types. A Virtue, a Vice or Seasonal Emotion may serve as this focus. Gentry cannot have Fae Influence over every emotion and thought.

                                                  5) Hands Of Fate: inflicting fair and foul luck of every sort.

                                                  6) Sands Of Time: accelerating, slowing and stopping the flow of Time.

                                                  Omnipotence Constrained

                                                  It is extremely rare for the Gentry to use the full extent of their Arcadian might. Without a chance to lose, there is no conflict. With no conflict, there is no story to tell the Wyrd, and the Gentry begin to Dwindle. Below are some suggestions for making the power of an Actor in Arcadia less overwhelming:

                                                  1) David And Goliath: assume that those who have the courage to invade Arcadia are favored by the Wyrd for reenacting such an epic tale. Every time a willpower point is spent, gain a dice bonus equal to [Wyrd] and the Rote Quality. All Contract invocations against the Actor that spend 1 willpower point are considered an Exceptional Success with 3 successes. If the Contract makes no distinction between Success and Exceptional Success, the invocation gains bonus successes equal to [lowest between Resolve and Composure of the character]. Every character in the group that is fighting the Actor regains 1 willpower point every [10 - highest Wyrd] turns.

                                                  2) Hubris: assume that even the most cunning Gentry underestimate others once in a while. Even after managing to enter the Realm, the Actor will not take greater precautions against the characters until a significant amount of its monsters, magics and other defenses have been overcome. Characters have an extra scene to further infiltrate the Realm and act out any plans they might have. Characters may even use their guile to actively appear incompetent or desperate, such as allowing one or more of them to act as bait while others split up, or begging on their knees to be taken back.

                                                  3) The Trojan Horse: your characters may decide that pretending to be friendly is a valid option to improve the element of surprise. They take advantage of the vices and aspirations of the Actor in some especially tempting way, allowing them ingress into the Realm without immediate battles. This process of manipulation may be initiated outside of Arcadia and well in advance of the actual invasion. This may take the form of difficult Social Maneuvering: assume that the base number of Doors that must be opened so that the Actor will let its guard down is equal to its [Wyrd x 2].

                                                  4) The Enemy Of My Enemy: simply put, the characters may take advantage of the Feud in some way.

                                                  5) The Heart In The Treasure Chest: perhaps the True Fae are ageless because they lock up their Deaths deep within their Realms. Unlocking their Death throws the Realm and Actor into a rapid descent of enfeeblement, as if Death is eagerly strangling all the owed years out of them. This is not enough to defeat them, but it makes them considerably more vulnerable. You may consider that they become "Rank 5" ephemeral beings with no god-like bonus successes and/or no Immortal Flesh. Characters must be careful, as the Actor can catch its Death and lock it up again.

                                                      While interesting, I feel like a few of your ideas (especially the "represent them as Rank 7 entities in their Realms" part) go a bit too much outside the paradigms I used for my hack (and indeed the paradigms I think we're supposed to look at the True Fae with).

                                                      It all makes them too powerful. With all those abilities inside their Realms, victory is all but a foregone conclusion for them, which severely goes against their themes. Let's look at it as Equinox Road tells us for a moment. Yes, in Arcadia the True Fae could just become an omnipotent titan you cannot hope to ever defeat. This is also meaningless for them. They won't do it. Because without a chance to lose, they have nothing to gain. Without meaningful conflict, they cannot endure as entities separate from the Wyrd.

                                                      As a side-note, I also don't believe strict mechanics are required to describe the power they have over their own Realms and any prisoners within their domain. We already have more than satisfactory explanations in Equinox Road (and some in Autumn Nightmares) as to how they control their Realms and how their influence over Arcadia and the beings under them work.

                                                      So really, I suppose my major critiques to your work are that great parts of it are redundant, and it has little place in the themes of the True Fae. I stress this is just my opinion, and everything considered, I'd say you still have some interesting suggestions there.
