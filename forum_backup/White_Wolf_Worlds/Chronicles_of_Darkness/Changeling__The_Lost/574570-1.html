    Hmm... I don't know that I'm completely feeling this one yet. May need to roll it around a little more.

    All of the Seemings have a strong component of the Changeling leaving behind some personal trait that had held her back from escape. A Darkling gives up her trust, an Elemental gives up her ego, an Ogre gives up her willingness to feel, a Fairest gives up her passivity, a Beast gives up her civility, and a Wizened gives up... everything about her that's getting in the way of her craft?

    It's interesting, but I don't know that I find it as compelling or engaging as the other 5 in terms of characters produced. Then again, I'm also quite tired. Maybe it'll click better for me after a good night's sleep.

        Originally posted by Wildfire View Post
        Off of this, Wizened are coming off a lot more like Elementals from First edition, with physically incorporating things into them. I'm also finding it a bit hard to see how they've escaped... it feels like they're damaged in their time in Arcadia, so they make do by sowing themselves back up with whatever things that are around, but that doesn't seem to lead into the choice to escape that the new Seemings are attempting to do, as it feels like what they've been doing in Arcadia already.
        My reading is that they're forcing order and rhythm and perfection upon Arcadia, and in doing so, are no longer a part of it. Their perception of Arcadia is Fickle Fate, that it's a tangled mess of chaos, but the core of the Wizened, the "something inside the would-be Wizened" is about correctness and sense.

        I think that's how I've settled on thinking about Seemings; they're not so much things you aspire to or really identify with (though they are that as well): they're what you've rejected. It's placing the blame of your Durance, of your pre-Durance life, upon something comprehensible and pushing it away. And in doing so, you're out. Is that entirely healthy? Not really, which would explain why the Escape is the beginning of the story, and not its end... but I think it's a good way to think about them, and it helps make sense of what a specific character's Escape demands from a psychological standpoint.

        It also means that you could work nearly any Seeming into the physical details of an Escape; as long as you set up the right psychological cues to riff off, you can get all six Seemings from the same set of actual events. That's something I like.
