    A Grimm FAQ

    04-06-2016, 11:29 AM
    We now have access to the Grimm Seeming, thanks to the release of the Dark Eras backer pdf. I've noticed on other threads that there seem to be a lot of questions regarding the Grimm Seeming. I'm not the one with the answers, sadly, but I thought that if we collected the questions here, perhaps we could convince the good people that created the Seeming to shed some light on the subject for us. Here's a few of the questions that I have about it, as well as some of the ones that I've seen. I'm sure others will have other ones to add.

    1. What is the intended blessing for the Grimm Seeming that is unrelated to Clarity? I'm just going to invert their curse for my games until we get an official answer, but I still thought I'd ask.

    2. What constitutes a role, and how does a Grimm adopt one or change between them?

    3. It mentions that other changelings can always recognize the Grimm, even when they are in a role, but that the role predominates their appearance and that no magic can tear away or pierce the role. How does that work with their mien? Do other changelings see the pretty princess, but instinctively know that she is also the brave little tailor and their pal Jack from the bar, even though they bear no resemblance to each other? Does it look to them like Jack from the bar is now wearing a wig and a dress, but somehow everyone else seems to think that she's the real deal?

    4. On a related note, how does the Grimm's kith affect their mien when they are in a role? Does the Grimm Stonebones seem to be a princess made of rock with crude storybook pictures carved into her? Does a Grimm Razorhand in the role of the Big Bad Wolf seem to be a Beast Hunterheart?

    5. I know that it is a Clarity breaking point if they act outside of their role, but what effect does that have on the role itself? Does the Damsel in Distress lose the role if she suddenly embraces her Bloodbrute kith to fight back against her captors? It says that Sinbad is Sinbad even if he doesn't know how to sail, but do people realize that he's not really Sinbad if he's put to the test and can't do so?

    6. What is the plural for the Seeming? The chapter seems to indicate that Grimm and Grimms can be used interchangeably.

    7. Do they have Seeming effects for some Contracts as well, but the book lacked space to include any, or do Grimms follow a different rule for favored effects? What does this mean for a Grimm character during creation?

    8. What happens to a role if the Grimm travels through an area where the role isn't culturally significant? Does the Grimm find themselves exposed without a role and need to assume a local one quickly, or does the role maintain itself even though the Grimm is now interacting with people that have never heard of the role before?

    9. Can a Grimm create a new role to play through time and effort? Would a modern Grimm creating a new urban legend then be able to step into that legend to live it?

    10. Does utilizing a Contract count as acting outside of a role if the role in question is not a specifically magical one? I could see an argument for the Gingerbread Witch being able to wield magic, but would the Big Bad Wolf out himself if he wielded a Contract focused on fire rather than using a wind based one? What about kith blessings? Is a Grimm Stonebones or Metalflesh revealed for what they are if they fail to crumple from an Ogre's blow while portraying the princess from "The Princess and the Pea"?

    11. It mentions that the Grimm is a new nickname for the Seeming in the Grimm Era. What was their original name? I could see something like Storytellers, Bards, or Skalds being possibilities, but I'm still curious as to what they would have been called in earlier eras.
