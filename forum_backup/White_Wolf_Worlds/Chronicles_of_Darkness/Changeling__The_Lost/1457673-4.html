        Originally posted by Stupid Loserman View Post

        A lot of written characters are shared, but the pronunciation is completely different. (This is even true for different Chinese dialects.) For example, 悪魔 is read in Japanese as "akuma," but while it is also a word in Chinese with a comparable meaning, the Mandarin Chinese word is something like (I am not a Chinese speaker) "èmó."

        This, incidentally, is why even Kindred of the East's "native" term for its titular monsters is glaringly off. "Kuei-jin" is clearly based on the characters 鬼人, but a Japanese speaker would read these characters "kijin," and a Mandarin speaker would read them "guĭrén" ("kuei" and "gui" just being two transliterations of the same sound). You literally can't write "kuei-jin" with its own proper characters without outside notation.

        All of this also is specifically in terms of borrowing from Chinese into other languages. India does not share this relationship of borrowing from Japanese linguistically; that relationship goes in the other direction, though China as an intermediary. Japan might use words derived from Sanskrit, but India does not use words derived from Japanese, except maybe among pop culture fans in the modern age of anime.

        I still object to the idea that there would be an "emperor" of all the Japanese fae courts as ringing false, but I will assent that "youkai" is a good fit for a general Japanese term for the fae, especially inhuman fae like hobs and the Gentry.

        I agree that there would be different dialects for different terms for Gentry, hobgoblins, and loyalists, though I do think certain regions might share terms. The idea of there being a Japanese changeling emperor was inspired by the Japanese imperial emperor and that I wanted the Japanese changeling courts to have their own distinct court system. When I looked up Nurarihyon on Youkai wiki, it stated that he was the ruler of all monsters.

            Originally posted by 21C Hermit View Post
            What Stupid Loserman said, and a little nitpicking; the idea of Nurarihyon as a leader of yokai, and the very word and concept of Mazoku, are relatively modern concepts. They’d work for modern Japan, but you may want something more/else for historical settings.

            When I looked up Nurarihyon on Youkai wiki, it stated that he is an actual Youkai from the Okayama prefecture.

            Here is his description on Youkai wiki.

            Nurarihyon (滑瓢, Nurarihyon), or Nūrihyon (ぬうりひょん, Nūrihyon), is a Japanese yōkai said to originate from Wakayama Prefecture. It is also sometimes believed to be descended from the Umibōzu of Okayama Prefecture.

            Nurarihyon is a mysterious and powerful yokai encountered all across Japan. Appearances can be deceiving, and nurarihyon is the perfect illustration of that saying. Overall, he is rather benign-looking, his head elongated and gourd-shaped. His face is wizened and wrinkled, resembling a cross between and old man and a catfish. He wears elegant clothing – often a splendid silk kimono or the rich robes of a Buddhist abbot – and carries himself in the quiet manner of a sophisticated gentleman.

            The short, comical, elderly nurarihyon is actually the most powerful and elite of all the yokai in the world. He travels in an ornate palanquin carried by human or yokai servants, often visiting red light districts, but occasionally stopping at mountain villas as well. He is known as “the Supreme Commander of All Monsters,” and every yokai listens to his words and pays him respect, treating him as the elder and leader in all yokai meetings. Along with otoroshi and nozuchi, nurarihyon leads the procession known as "Hyakki Yakō" (the night parade of one hundred demons) through the streets of Japan on dark, rainy nights. He fits the role of supreme commander every bit as much when he interacts with humans as well.

            Nurarihyon shows up on evenings when a household is extremely busy. He arrives at homes unexpectedly in his splendid palanquin and slips into the house, unnoticed by anyone. He helps himself to the familys tea, tobacco, and other luxuries, acting in all respects as if he were the master of the house. His power is so great that even the real owners of the house, when they finally notice his presence, can do nothing to stop him. In fact, while he is there, the owners actually believe the nurarihyon to actually be the rightful master of the house. Eventually he leaves just as he came, quietly and politely slipping out of the house and into his palanquin, as the owners of the house obsequiously bow and wave him farewell. Only after he has left does anyone become suspicious of the mysterious old man who just visited.

            As to nurarihyon’s origins there is only speculation, for the oldest records of his existence are mere sketches and paintings. His name connotes a slippery evasiveness – which he employs when posing as master of the house. Its name comes from “nurari” (to slip away) and “hyon” (an onomatopoeia describing floating upwards) written with the kanji for gourd (due to the shape of his head).
            In Okayama, some evidence exists linking nurarihyon to umi-bōzu. There, nurarihyon are globe-shaped sea creatures, about the size of a mans head, which float about in the Seto Inland Sea. When fisherman try to catch one, the sphere sinks down into the water just out reach and then bobs back up mockingly. It has been theorized that some of these slippery globes migrate to land, where they gradually gain influence and power, becoming the nurarihyon known throughout the rest of Japan. Whether this theory is the true origin of the Supreme Commander of All Monsters or just one more of his many mysteries is yet to be solved.

                My discussions with 21C Hermit and Stupid Loserman are strengthening by belief that we need a 2e corebook that focuses on Asia, with different chapters dedicated to China, Japan, Korea, Vietnam, and especially the Asian American changelings, because Asians are such an important part of globalization and the history of the United States.

                The two types of Asian American changelings I described earlier in the thread are meant to represent the immigrant experience AND the rising influence of Asian nations like China and Japan. Thanks to globalization, the Asian courts are having an easier time traveling to the West and establishing their courts in West. They often have support groups of other Asian changelings and the Asian American communities to help them adjust to life in the West. Also, the Asia towns help provide with the sense of familiarity.

                The Asians who were made changelings by Western Keepers are the ones who represent the earliest immigrants from East to West AND how the immigrant experience is about suffering. In Western Arcadia, there were no images or structures to give them a sense of familiarity. Most of their fellow slaves were Westerners AND they often needed to take a crash course in English, French, and German to survive. They also needed to adapt to Western customs to avoid their Keeper’s wrath. It was worse if you were your Keeper’s consort in that you were often forced to act to Asian stereotypes.

                When they escape Arcadia, they find themselves in the West rather than the East. Worse, their country of origin has moved on without them, no longer practicing many of the customs and rituals of their human life. Because their speaking and mannerisms are now more Western rather then Eastern the Eastern court changelings often regard them with suspicion.

                                                I had previously shared interest in there being a changeling rulebook that focuses on Asia, partly because of my own fascination with Asian culture and also because Asian lore is filled with fascinating fae like creatures. There have been several comic book series that have touched on this lore and would make good sources if inspiration including Horns, and My Date with Monsters.

                                                Another reason I think it would be cool to do a book that focuses on Asian changeling culture is to explore the long historical, cultural, and political connections between the Asia, and the West, especially the United States.

                                                That is why I wanted to do a Changeling chronicle to introduced three types of Asian American Changelings;
                                                1. The Courts and Changelings from Asia that immigrated either to follow their mortal families or to build power bases in the U.S. This Became MUCH more common in in the 1990's during Pax Americana and Globalization began picking up steam with countries and companies expanding their business and political ties beyond their countries borders.
                                                2. The peoples of Asia that were kidnapped and made changelings of Western True Fae. This was especially common during the time of European Imperial Power, China's century of National Humiliation, and the US occupation of Japan. I chose the term Cathayan for this type of Asian American Changeling, because I wanted to comment on the bias, bigotry, and stereotyping Asians have had to deal with. ALSO, I felt oriental and Yellow were WAYYY more offensive, so I chose Cathayan as the lesser evil.
                                                3. Changelings born in the US of Asian descent. They are the most Western of the Asian American Changelings.
