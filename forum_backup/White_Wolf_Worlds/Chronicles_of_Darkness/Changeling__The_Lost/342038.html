    Masquerade Ball Murder

    12-16-2014, 12:58 PM
    I make a start in mine episodic CtL chronicle for our RPG club in two days with game session about escape of PCs on Christmas from their common Keeper in small town in New England. Today I got idea for our next session - the PCs and new players will be asked in time of carnival to come and join Masquerade Ball in old, deserted mansion by mysterious invitations. They will not know at first their host, only that they need to worn mask attached to massage - and masks are reinforcing their Masks so even they only see their "human guise".

    Players will have time to socialize and forge the Courts in starting freehold. I even considering running mini LARP for this. And, of course, there will be murder on the ball.

    I have some questions with that you could help me...

    1. What being could be the host? True Fae, hobgoblin, changeling, other? Come to think about it, maybe interesting would be Charlatan Fae?

    2. I need many NPCs to make a PC group party in to ball - who could make for extras on the ball? Could mortals be compelled to join part for one night and not remembering it after? Because having about 50 of Lost in the town of about 2500 population I do not see on this.

                                              I think it can't be emphasized enough that everybody needs to work together. If players try to write themselves out of game that's bad but the ST needs to consider the motivations of the characters and not just shove them around like chess pieces.

                                              True Fae should be used sparingly or they lose their impact and changeling have good reason to be wary of any fae creature the could be linked to the Gentry. As such, if I could suggest, there’s no reason Changelings can’t run into a fine ghost story.

                                              The Backstory

                                              50 years ago there was a masquerade ball at the old mansion but fire broke out and many of the guests were killed. The family fixed the place up but it never really felt the same and they moved out the following summer letting the old grounds rot.

                                              Several (13? 25?) years later, on the anniversary of the ball invitations were received by the surviving members of the ball and their kin. Those who chose to attend found the ballroom was set for a party and there were already a number of guests milling and dancing to music. The hosts were not easy to identify beneath their masks but those who did catch a glimpse beneath their costumes could swear they were those same souls lost so many years ago in this very house.

                                              When morning came most of the guests had no memory of the nights entertainment while others were found dead in the old manner. Suspicion fell on many of the guests who had attended the phantom ball but no case held against any of them and ultimately the police had to accept that they simply did not know who the killer was.

                                              Now the invitations have been received again. The ghosts of the masquerade seek to expand their number again.

                                              The PCs

                                              If one or more PC’s are willing they might be decedents of the survivors, or even survivors themselves. Heck, a changeling that only felt Arcadia as a short time might think the invitation is from the original family, their old friends!

                                              Autumn courtiers may be curious and wish to study the ball while Summer changelings might attend with the goal of protecting the mortals.

                                              Or maybe the PC’s don’t know anything about the old house and just get invitations in the mail inviting them to a party with promises of free food and drink. In this case the invitations should imply that the event is some kind of public party, such as a charity auction.

                                              The Truth

                                              The phantom ball is hosted by the ghosts of those killed in the original fire, as well as the other souls they’ve gathered along the way. Their magics wipe the memories of mortals afterwords, making the whole affair feel as nothing more than a dream. Supernaturals, including changelings, are immune to the memory editing effects.

                                              The important question is why the ghosts are hosting this dance?

                                              The ghosts might simply be lonely and pool their power to reunite with the living and gather fresh blood. Perhaps the original fire was a sacrifice to some powerful death god or spirit and now the ghosts are trying to gather other souls to buy their own freedom from this dark entity.

                                              Maybe the ghosts have no control over the dance. It could be a naturally occurring intersection of the living world and the great below. Murders follow because it’s generally not a good idea to mix the living and the dead - there is just too much jealousy from the later towards the former. Alternatively the ghosts don’t kill anyone. Instead another supernatural has realized these parties happen every so often and wipe the mortal minds making it the ideal place to kill off human rivals without any risk of witnesses remembering.

                                                      I am running a Masquerade ball in the next few sessions of my game, and have run the hell out of Castle Falkenstein, so I ahve some insights.

                                                      One question is...what do they get out of it? What are their goals as PCs? In my case, for most it is Build approval with Courts (Mantle, Court Affinity), for some it is to publicly display their affection toward their paramour by publicly doing a minor ritual of putting some ivy in their hair, The Spring Queen is choosing a new paramour, and some PCs plan to...affect those proceedings based on who they would prefer she wind up with (none are making the play themselves as far as I know, but it was an option). Some are helping a clandestine meeting of the Autumn and Night (Moon) Court and covering up the meeting. Some fear an attacker who has caused chaos before may again so seek to guard the ball from it. And others just want to get people talking about their outfits/dancing and/or score with lovely men/women/both. Pretty much everyone has at least a couple things they want out of the ball. Your players should too. Note this is not to get them to show up...assumedly they have already shown interest. This is to give them stuff they enjoy.

                                                      What do you plan to have occur? Depending you may want to talk to your players about some of it. Certainly, if you EXPECT the PCs to do something, you should discuss it with the players. Now I haven't as my ball will be very sandboxy...the PCs can make of it what they will. But depending on your intentions it can be good to discuss.

                                                      What do the PLAYERS want from the ball? Ask em! They want romance? A chance to begin relationships? what are they after. Having a bunch of mortals who will forget it all would...limit my own interest, because whats the point of being social/beautiful/awesome if people forget? Of course, the intentions of whoever I play might affect that. Knowing what they think would be cool may be important to what they will enjoy. Some players will be disappointed if there is not combat. Others will be disappointed if there is combat. You can't please everyone, but you can know their interest.

                                                      This kind of thing I have seen really be awesome and I have seen be the GM talking mostly to himself for a couple hours. Player investment is a major factor.
