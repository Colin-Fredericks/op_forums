    Originally posted by Illuminated Minutia View Post
    So the Fairest are about accepting responsibility. They're basically the flip side of Darklings, then. I really wasn't expecting them to go this route but I think its an interesting direction for them to go.
    In a way, the Darklings actually talk about the Fairest. The document describes the Fairest as the shining heroes, and the Darkling writeup talks about them falling into darkness as the hero marches off.




    ------------------------------------------------



    So, after reading the Fairest writeup, I feel like its describing the police almost to a T. Well, the good, non-a-hole peoples anyways. A lot of people end up joining those professions because they feel that they have to give stuff up for protecting and leading people. Certainly, they don't join because of the money. When they're on patrol, so much is left up to making snap judgement and "reasonable suspicion," try to shove a plan together at the snap second; so much can go so wrong so fast, and it often crushes their souls so quickly. Righteousness gets tainted really quickly when you're constantly faced with the worst troublemakers and disappointments before it crushes your idealism.

    In a real way, the Fairest are the embodiment of fairy tale heroes. I can't tell if a lot of Fairest would be drawn to that job after they return, thinking they could do things right in the mortal world with their hero complex with jobs that attract highly moral people. That's the intent I feel, anyways.



    On that same vein, however. I feel like the Fairest.... aren't anymore. Mirror mirror, on the wall, who's the fairest of them all? That was a huge thing before, but now... I don't feel it. Snow White and Prince Charming could both be Fairest. In 1e, so was the Evil Queen. Now? She's not. Instead, we could have even ugly heroes as Fairest.

    The name feels 100% like a misnomer. There is nothing about the Fairest that are fair.

You can play Fairy tale: PTSD or you can play Fables or Sandman or just weirdly powered politics. Most games have a balance of all of these.

The beast write up had elements of this, the character concepts well vague enough you could think of a few different animialistic characters, (except the shark broker, I'd have preferred a write up that gives the impression of a predator thats all tooth rather than be so explict).

        Perhaps this comes from the drafts being shown out of context and the new focus on Seeming as your escape-choice naturally draws out the abuse themes. The writing is not a problem for me, its good. Its just the choice on what is being written doesn't fully capture the full tone of the game. Presumably seemings will be one of the first things in the book someone will read? If so then I think a little bit more time on the other aspects of the game is important.

        If it was my choice, I would limit background sections to a sentance or two and use the extra space to add more fairy tale weirdness. If background is defintely staying in, then please re-write the character concepts to be more fairy-tale esqe.

        This is just my opinion. It is an attempt at constructive criticism. I won't be offended if the drafts stay as is, I ]will ignore them, or run Changeling 1e with 2e mechanics.
        As a counterpoint: I feel like there's probably plenty of space in the book to present and explore "fairy tale weirdness". Even if it's not heavily prevalent in the individual Seeming write-ups, the taking charge of agency is evidently connected to the narrative of Arcadia and wrestling yours specifically back from your Keeper, and this aspect will likely be explored in the introduction chapter. I'm sure the beautiful madness, the wonder, and the oddity of it all will be emphasized through the book.

        Additionally, let's remember how David said that Seeming is the one section of the book that can't be much about trauma (also as a nod to those commenting the Fairest seem "too bright"), and in very much the same way (though this is just my interpretation), Seeming is probably one section of the book that can't be too much about the strangeness of Faerie. Because it is also about how you grounded yourself in your escape and away from the madness of your captivity and Arcadia. In fact, that's exactly what the Blessing is.

            I'm going to offer what I can on the topic.

            Originally posted by Filamena View Post
            1)Someone asked if the Clarity check is for when you make hard choices and people get hurt or also when you're 'job' makes you punch a dude, and they get hurt. I can clarify that. It is in fact when the damage is splash-over or accidentally. It's meant to be about collateral damage, not about 'I need to punch a guy because that guy needs to be punched.' It's meant to be, "I punched a guy because he needed to be punched, and then he died, and now his widow can't support her family any more." This plays into the idea that ever if you're a good leader (which not all Fairest are) the choices you make, even perfectly legit choices, are going to have consequence. And for the tragic Fairest, that eats at you. See, the Fairest didn't ask to be a leader, didn't ask for people to look up to them, but they kinda have to do it. And even though they generally don't 'sign up for it' so to speak, it still eats at them and slowly kills them that they fuck up even when they don't fuck up. That's the tragedy of the Fairest. And the horror.
            I agree, and see in here a lot of what the Fairest in this write up are really about. I do think the writing needs tightened up, as that might help the feeling that Fairest are getting pigeon-holed, that they can't be anything other than the Court Monarch or Motley Leader. For instance, as you pointed out Filamena, the Fairest aren't about necessarily being in the official leadership position (I actually think maybe trying the write-up without using the word "leader" at all would an interesting way to try this) they're all about taking up responsibility. This doesn't necessarily mean becoming King of the Summer Court (though certainly a Fairest could!) but what it does mean (in my interpretation of the Fairest) is this;

            Benjamin is probably one of the most iconic Summer King's the Court has ever had, no one doubts the Ifrit Beast's rage at the Gentry, and he proved it when that Huntsmen's fire station went up in smoke. What everyone realized quick, though, was that when you needed anything other than a whole lot of fire you didn't visit the king, you walked straight into the hall and took a right, to a small office with a flowering fairest sitting behind it and a name plaque reading "Tulip" (the name being part of the campaign scheme). Tulip doesn't mind though, and listens to all your troubles with a smile. Having trouble with a loan? She'll tell you how to deal the creditors, can't bring yourself to mess with with those creepy Autumn Court types? Tulip will handle it for you.

            What nobody ever bothers to wonder is how Karen (her real name, not that anyone bothers to ask) got the job in the first place. That Benjamin thought he needed someone sociable to handle the reception work. That the only experience she had was as a Computer Science major, and giving advice on how to keep Mizer from smashing your knees for his money without calling a significant chunk of the Winter Court down on you was something she had no idea how to do. She has to though, she knows that, the Court can't look to Benjamin for this, so she'll have to do.

            She just prays what she's doing won't make it worse. But she's gotten this far, and no matter what happens she won't let what happened with Charlotte's motley happen again, she can't.

            (ugh, apologize if that's terrible, I just hope it gets my point across)

                            After my first read through, it's not something I like. Now, I'm not going to go Edition wars, because each edition will be valid in its own way, but there was something about the Beautiful Madness that floated around Fairest in First edition that I really liked, and made Fairest stand out. Now, it seems more like that has been excised, and the leader archetype has been thrust on them, and while that is a nice concept, it's removed one of the signatures of Fairest. Fairest aren't supposed to be 'nicer' than other people, or more like a leader (though, the leader thing might be the case with this new write-up), they've just previously been creatures that may be a little more like the Gentry and that causes its own problems which other characters can admire... and fear.

                            Do we want it in the system that Fairest are most likely to be Monarchs? Maybe, we can see, but I don't want 1/6th of Seemings to be based around it. It tilts the game a lot more than the Beast and Darkling seeming write ups have appeared to.
