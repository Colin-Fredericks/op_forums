    Running my first game

    01-05-2014, 09:51 PM
    I've been playing nWoD for about 2 years now, and have been tossing around the idea of running my own game. I finally got some more inquiries from our party this weekend, so I've decided to really start working on putting something together.
    So far I've decided that everyone will be Changelings, and that the setting is going to be somewhere in the either Washington or Oregon. I want to run a mystery campaign with lots of disappearances.

    If anyone has any insight or suggestions for a first time Storyteller, it would be much appreciated.

          I was going to write some notes earlier, but I got kind of distracted.

          First and foremost, know what you're comfortable with - I learned STing Changeling while playing with players who both knew the system better than I did, but also had more experience running games.

          Don't be afraid to veto things! Changeling has a lot of material (although it pales in comparison to Vampire) which you might not be comfortable with or knowledgeable at how it works yet. The level of knowledge you need to run a game is a lot more than if you're just playing. You're allowed to go 'Nothing but the core book's contracts/merits/kiths' or something.

          Allow yourself to experiment - I tried a lot of things until I got comfortable in my slot, some worked and I kept them, others didn't and I didn't.

          Make sure you clearly manage player expectations of your players...I was fairly frank about the fact I was new and wanted to learn, so I think my players had a bit more fun with it than if they were expecting an experienced ST with no other context. Likewise, I kept asking them for feedback and help and I usually got it. Even from a 'I'm not/am enjoying playing this character' perspective.

          Take notes! (Unless your memory is perfect, then you don't need to). They're useful when you need to remember something eight sessions down the line.

          Also, you've picked a great game to start Changeling is excellent.

                          I've been running Changeling ongoing campaigns for a couple of years now. I love using fairy tale and mythic themes, characters, and tokens. One way I've used fairy tales is to help inspire me to imagine what Durance was like for each character. As part of character creation, I ask them to pick out their character's favorite fairy tale when s/he was a child. In my version of Changeling, fairy tales are Keeper propaganda, so instead of being the protagonist, I imagine the human-turned-fey as a side character or villain of the story. For example, if a player picked Beauty and the Beast, I would likely make them an Elemental Manikin-- so s/he would play a role in the Keeper's household like Lumière or r Mrs. Potts, rather than Belle. Someone chose Peter Pan, so his Keeper was the most terrifying tyrant than regressed people and assigned them roles (lost boy, pirate, Indian) and made them battle each other for his amusement (this is not too far off from the original).

                                  As a primary ST for my group with Changeling being one of my favorites (was finally trumped by Demon) I can let you know of a few pitfalls that I had to work through.

                                  Players:
                                  • ​As previously stated, don't be afraid to veto. A player may want something that you are neither prepared for or it would massively complicate the story or run diagonally to what you have created. It's OK to outright say no, after all, you know more about what is coming up then they do and they may not know that the thing they want will make the entire game unbearable to them. Also things like: wanting some cross-over stuff (regular access to the Underworld but you are playing changeling) is all up to you to decide whether your setting can handle it (and whether it would ruin the themes).
                                  • near contrary to the former, don't be afraid to change your story for the players. If you are using Aspirations these are invaluable for that purpose. If you notice they move in a different direction then your plot (or seem to have fun with something else that wasn't really included in your plot) than don't be afraid to change your plot to fit their level of fun or, if the case may be, completely change the plot. Remember the game is about having fun (it's simple but during game, you have no idea how easy it is to simply deny players and railroad their characters. it's hard to keep up with them rather than to make them fit the story)


                                  Frequent Story Teller for the Circle of Five gaming group.

                                      I love to talk about characters and plot, but I was thinking about what I would have liked to know before starting my first Changeling campaign.... The main text is desperately hard to navigate and the index is terrible. I've added post-it page markers to make access faster, which helps a lot when players new to the system have questions. The contracts in the main book are of limited use and frustrated my players who were used to less subtle magic-- the antidote to this is the Winter Masques supplement. The seeming contracts are much, much more practical and fun for players. Also, the expanded kiths are fun and fill in some obvious gaps in the original (e.g. you couldn't be a traditional "tinkerbell" type of fairy in the original). Even better for the Storyteller though, Winter Masques talks about about Durance in a way that gives you clear ideas about what could have happened to the characters and how it would be different for each seeming (which in turn means that offering them glimpses of their past through dreams gets a lot easier).

                                      The Autumn Nights supplement is all antagonists, which is good for the ST, but I was frustrated that it was not very easy to just grab a villain and run him/her on the go. There aren't that many antagonists per chapter and for my purposes, I needed to make some changes with them before I used them (also the layout for these NPCs is not great and it's hard to just glance at the book and use the stats for battle). I just didn't find it well written and only moderately helpful as a ST (when it looked like it would be very helpful).

                                      What did help me a lot was getting the Fearmaker's Promise Module and running that very early in the campaign. Throughout my role playing, I've rarely used or played in modules, but Fearmaker helped me by summing up several of the members of a Changeling court and offered up several antagonists that were shades of grey-- and one scary minor Keeper with an interesting, loose plot. I filled out the material with more Hedge encounters and more court members and it became part of the campaign foundation. The module helped me think in a different way about how to prep for a game, what kind of info is really needed for NPCs and how to get players to reflect on their choices in the game.

                                      If you get Rites of Spring, it will explain more fully many elements mentioned in the original book-- for example, what is the magic of the mask? Does it work on all of the senses? Does the mask change with your Wyrd? Also, there is an alternative set of rules for vows and oaths that makes them a lot simpler and easier to incorporate as a regular part of changeling life. The Hollow merits offered in this book are great and if your players find out about them, they will beg you to use the supplement (build Baba Yaga's hut? Yes, you can!). It is by far the least organized book with useless chapter titles and no index. You need to read it cover to cover and make your own page number notes-- I'm a academic and I find that tedious.

                                                          That advice is wonderful for almost any game, but I find it especially fits Changeling. The game revolves around characters who have good reason for paranoia about the nature of the world around them, and the impact on it by vastly potent beings for inscrutable, arbitrary reasons. An ST making a lot of rolls that may or may not govern anything and then not explaining them feels perfectly in theme. Also adds a bit of an edge to a horror game. Nothing Is Scarier, and all that.
