                                                          Originally posted by Xenesis View Post
                                                          Because, realistically they can never leave without help. The Hedge is a horrifying and torturous place to even those who have an affinity for it, knowingly leaving one form of bondage to freely enter another, completely alien and unfathomable one is likely be to unattractive to most Demons.
                                                          Maybe? I think this statement has a lot of assumptions about the how the Hedge and the GM interact. We know that the God Machine likely has gears in the Hedge that aren't being torn apart, so modifying those wouldn't be too bad.

                                                          That said, there's a very good reason why they're not moving en masse into the Hedge. Only one of four Agendas (Tempters/Builders) thinks that Hell is a physical place. The others either want to escape it, focus on destroying the God Machine, or reach some form of demonic enlightenment. In the Tempter Agenda, there's a good portion of them that appreciate the creature comforts of reality, or are in love with humans, or want to keep their cultists, not run away from them - stuff that generally won't work out in the Hedge. These guys want Hell on Earth.

                                                          So, the majority of demons aren't moving into the Hedge (or Hisil, Astral, Underworld, Inferno, etc) for the simple reason of it not fitting the vast majority of what they want out of Hell.



                                                          On the topic of Arcadia versus the God Machine. There's actually some pretty strong thematic connections between the God Machine and Wyrd, most of which revolve around them both being opposites. The fae are whimsical, transformative, pretty chaotic when all is said and done. In many ways, its art personified. It is Lady Luck smiling and mocking you, twisting things as suit her fancy. The God Machine is Order. It has plans. It is the inevitable march of Fate going towards its inevitable conclusion with mathematical precision, heavily grounded in reality. Meanwhile, all the stories about Iron all contain different things. The most popular seems to be Iron as a sentient being pissed at the Fae for broken a contract. One was about making a deal with humans against all the things in the night. The third was "Foe of All Unreason," which iron is the embodiment of science-driven development instead of imagination, which fits with the whole God Machine.

                                                          Now, given that the "aspects of reality" that changelings make Contracts with are very ill defined (I can argue that spirits and astral reflections are, by definition, aspects of reality), I see no reason why we can't say that the God Machine is somehow tied to Iron in 1e. In 2e? There's a shift towards being more Narrative driven, and powers changing depending on what your Seeming is (more like werewolf gifts?) so I don't want to talk about that without more information on 2e Lost. Even then, all the comparisons pale before the idea for a good story.


                                                          So, in this light, I don't see why there can't be a connection.
