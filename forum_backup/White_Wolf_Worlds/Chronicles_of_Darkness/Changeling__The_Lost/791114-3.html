                            So now that we have rules for the Huntsmen, I have wondered how the Fairest Curse will interact with them. It seems a little odd and overkill to me to make such masters of the hunt even better when chasing a Fairest quarry, especially since the Hunstmen are antagonists. It's just that generally speaking, I doesn't make sense to me to make rolls with an enemy who hasn't be introduced yet. If I wanted to introduced a Huntsman in my chronicles, I would want to make it appear where and when I want. Especially since it's repeatedly stated the Huntsmen are incredibly driven by narration.

                            I have two suggestions:
                            - what if the Fairest were considered by all Huntsmen as a prospective target. What if all Huntsmen, even the ones who weren't designed to hunt them, could take a Fairest to his master and be considered successful. It make sense since the Fairest are protagonists and all. The problem here is that it diffuses the tension of the hunt for other changelings.
                            - alternatively, what if it was tougher for Fairests to make deal with the Huntsmen. What if they couldn't collect Tokens of Reprieve of their own as easily as other changelings. After all, they became Fairests because they extended and reached out a hand, it should be difficult for them to betray fellow changelings, even if the changeling in question is a sworn enemy.

If I wanted to introduced a Huntsman in my chronicles, I would want to make it appear where and when I want. Especially since it's repeatedly stated the Huntsmen are incredibly driven by narration.

                                I have two suggestions:
                                - what if the Fairest were considered by all Huntsmen as a prospective target. What if all Huntsmen, even the ones who weren't designed to hunt them, could take a Fairest to his master and be considered successful. It make sense since the Fairest are protagonists and all. The problem here is that it diffuses the tension of the hunt for other changelings.
                                - alternatively, what if it was tougher for Fairests to make deal with the Huntsmen. What if they couldn't collect Tokens of Reprieve of their own as easily as other changelings. After all, they became Fairests because they extended and reached out a hand, it should be difficult for them to betray fellow changelings, even if the changeling in question is a sworn enemy.
                                I like the second one a lot more, but a Seeming is permanent, so players might hesitate to play a Fairest it if comes with that kind of drawback. Huntsmen sound like they should be feared by all Seemings in equal measure.

                                Here are my suggestions:

                                Ogres: I have to agree that immunity to wound penalties doesn't cut it as a Seeming Blessing. Not every Ogre gets into combat all the time, and there's room for scientist and scholar Ogres who solve problems with cold, calculating reason. I believe they should get a greater resistance against not only Social Maneuvering, but also mind-affecting powers that may destabilize their callous hearts. Again, this greater resistance should go beyond universally available Merits like Iron Will and Indomitable. Their affinity for Stone is not just about breaking bones, but also about the Sticks-And-Stones saying.

                                Elementals: I am very leery of letting players retroactively declare heat-of-the-moment decisions as clever plans, even with the caveat of their Curse. I figured Elementals in 2ED would still suffer some kind of Social penalty, though the devs appear to be moving away from that kind of drawback. I am also very glad to see that immunity to harmful manifestations of their chosen element is now an inherent advantage of the Seeming, rather than a meager scene-long boost to Health that only becomes useful at high Wyrd.

                                Wizened: I believe what defines this Seeming is not specialization, but perfectionism, so a Curse that limits their capacity to use and craft Tokens doesn't sound appealing to me. If I play a Wizened, I'd like the option to eventually adopt a new talent to define myself (perhaps with a different Kith along the way), and the possibility to become the closest thing to a fae polymath or Mr. Fixit that I can. There ain't no rule saying that versatility can't be my defining talent, is there? I know perfecting versatility is more about broadness in skills rather than any actual perfection, but I'm sure you got my point.

                                Also, I assume that coming across a situation that triggers their Curses calls for a roll, rather than immediately dropping their Clarity. What kind of circumstantial modifiers have been discussed for this roll, if any?
