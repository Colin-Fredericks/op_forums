                As a note, different journals cover different events, so this is a session that if you usually read a couple you might want to hit all of them.                 Session LXXVI

                Lorelei’s Diary

                Okay, checking psyche… examining shifting mood parameters… recalling events of the last twenty-four hours… yep, still giddy.

                So after returning to the Dream House in the wee hours of the morning and hastily scrawling down my last – probably nearly incomprehensible – diary entry, D and I pepped ourselves up with Spring contracts, grabbed a quick shower, and got dressed for church. Since I knew Dad was probably bursting at the seams to congratulate us in person, I would have begged her to come with me… but I didn’t even have to ask.

                The sermon was about love and partnership, and I sincerely doubt that was a coincidence. I didn’t mention the engagement to any of my friends in the choir; I sort of wanted to wait until I had a ring to show off. This whole proposal was so spontaneous that I guess we’re sort of doing things backwards, but I don’t care. It was easy to raise my voice to the heavens today; I half felt like I was flying already.

                D was waiting for me in the pews after I had changed out of my choir robes, and the two of us made our way to the tail-end of the line of parishioners speaking with Dad at the doors of the church. “Lorelei,” he said warmly, taking my hand in his, “your voice was, as always, an inspiration to us all. And Maria, I am always happy to see you among my humble flock. And I understand…” he said with a surreptitious glance at the departing congregation, “I understand that congratulations are in order!”

                D smiled and took Dad’s hand firmly. “Thanks, padre,” she said, and then her cheeks colored ever so slightly and she lowered her gaze. “Thank you, sir,” she started again. “I’m sorry I didn’t stop by or call to ask for your blessing first, but it just sort of… happened yesterday morning. I mean… I love Lori very much, and I’ve never been as sure about anything in my life, but… the proposal itself was… unplanned.”

                Dad nodded sagely. “I understand. It was much the same when I proposed to Juliette’s mother, actually. When God shows you that a given time and place is right, it is foolish to delay.” He got sort of a misty look in his eyes. “A few months ago, I had believed myself alone in this world, bereft of my family. Then, in a miracle beyond imagining, my Juliette was returned to me. And now, our family grows once again. Welcome, Maria.”

                “Thank you, sir,” she said, allowing herself to be drawn into a somewhat awkward hug. “I’ll do everything I can to keep her safe and happy.”

                “I know you will,” he responded. “Oh… and you may continue to call me ‘padre,’ if you like. Of course, that form of address is more properly applied to a Catholic or Anglican priest, yet… from you, I find the term endearing.”

                D’s face broke into a genuine smile. I think he made her day with that.

                There was one more person I had to tell before we started informing the Freehold at large. Oleander’s house is relatively close to the church, so I asked D if she wouldn’t mind heading over there for a few minutes. She dutifully agreed, and about fifteen minutes later I was knocking on her door.

                “Hey, Angel,” she said as she opened her door, “this is a pleasant surprise.” She glanced over my shoulder at my car in the driveway. “De la Rocha can come in if you want, Lori… it doesn’t bother me.”

                “Thanks,” I said, “but I can really only stay for a couple of minutes. We have a full day planned. I… wanted to tell you about something in person before you hear it from the Freehold’s grapevine. You see… just after you left yesterday, D proposed to me.”

                There wasn’t even a flicker of negative emotion on her face. “Congratulations!” she said immediately with a dazzling smile.

                “You’re okay with it?” I asked, a little surprised after Rowan’s reaction the previous night.

                “Of course,” she said, “I’m not stupid, Lori. I’ve known this was coming for a long time. And not to be self-deprecating, but… I knew it wasn’t going to be me. You and de la Rocha make each other very happy, and I’m happy for both of you.”

                “Thank you so much!” I said, throwing my arms around her neck. Though she hadn’t been toxic the previous day, I guess I was making sort of a dangerous assumption there… but I also know that she would have warned me if it wasn’t safe for us to touch. “D and I have already talked about it… we’re going to have an open marriage. I still want to spend time with you, Imelda… I still love you.”

                “Right back at you, Angel,” she said as we disengaged. “Thanks for coming by to tell me in person… you didn’t have to do that, but I appreciate it. Now go on… I’m sure the two of you have a lot to discuss.” She turned toward the driveway and called out “congratulations!” D shot her a thumbs-up in response.

                As we were driving back to the Dream House, however, D started asking questions, some of which really blindsided me. “So, do you want to go ring shopping today, or Tuesday?” she asked (that one didn’t blindside me so much). “I have to go to work tomorrow, since I took Friday off, but I could make time the day after…”

                “Today is fine,” I said with easily-penetrated false nonchalance. “We’ve got all afternoon; five or six hours until I really need to be back for the show. That should be enough time.”

                D sighed. “I… Lori, I don’t know shit about wedding rings and jewelry and shit like that,” she said after a minute. “I want to get you something you’ll really like, but I don’t want to fuck it up, and I probably will.”

                I laughed. “D, you could get me a ring out of a box of crackerjacks and I’d be thrilled with it,” I said. Then something occurred to me. “Do they still have those? Crackerjacks? Do people still know what they are?”

                That broke the tension a little. “Yeah,” she replied, “but I don’t think they put the prizes in them anymore.”

                That threw me for a loop, as the prizes were always sort of the point of getting crackerjacks in my opinion. Without them, you’re left with stale caramel-covered popcorn.

                “Anyway,” D continued after a moment, “I was thinking about asking Julia for advice. She’s already done all the research and looked into the local stores and stuff, so… would you mind if she came with us?”

                “Sure,” I said, “it’ll make her happy, too. Actually… I’ve been meaning to ask you something about Marguerite and Julia. I know they’re both pushing for a combined wedding, but… I’d really like my Dad to oversee our ceremony, and I kind of want it to be about just you and me. Does that make me selfish?”

                D sniffed. “Nah,” she said. “It’s your wedding. Most people only get like three or four of them in a lifetime, so you want it to be perfect, right?”

                “Bite your tongue,” I quipped back. “This bird mates for life. But anyway, I was thinking… I don’t think I want to have a combined wedding, but I do want those two to know how much we care about them. Maybe it’s time we finally said yes and pledged ourselves.”

                D nodded. “I’d be okay with that,” she said after thinking about it for a moment. “It’s not really a concession… just taking a different step to distinguish the relationships a little. I’ll talk with Mags about it when we get back, and let her talk to Julia. That’ll probably go better for everyone.”

                Things were quiet for another couple of minutes, and then D spoke up again. “Babe?” she asked softly (here comes the blindsiding).

                “Yeah?”

                “Does one of us have to… like… convert now? And if so, which one?”

                I didn’t understand the question at first. “I… don’t understand the question,” I said.

                “Well, you’re a Protestant and I’m a Catholic,” she elaborated quietly. “I mean, I’m not a very good Catholic, and I haven’t really even thought much about being a Catholic for the last few years, but now… well, I’m getting married.”

                “Oh,” I said. I confess that D’s religion has come up so infrequently that I hitherto hadn’t even considered it a factor. “Well… I’m not going to ask you to join the Methodist church. If you decided to do so, I’d be thrilled, but if not, it’s not going to make me unhappy, if you see what I mean. As for me converting to Catholicism…”

                I chuckled. “Dad might actually take that worse than the ‘gay’ and the ‘changeling’ revelations, honestly. My faith is important to me, D… but if that’s what it takes for us to be together, then I’m in.”

                D shook her head. “I… I don’t think so. I’ll look into it, see what the church has to say, but don’t worry about it too much for now. I wouldn’t ask you to do that anyway… but there’s a part of me that wants to get married by the laws of the Holy Church. It’s… more important to me than I thought it would be.”

                I nodded. “I get it; you want to do this right. You might only get three or four chances, after all…” I threw her a smirk to let her know I was teasing.

                “Eyes on the road, wise-ass,” she shot back at me. “Anyway, worst case scenario as I see it, we have two ceremonies. I looked it up; there’s a Catholic church in L.A. that will perform marriages for lesbians. Though if we have a traditional Catholic ceremony, it’ll probably take all day, so we might want to set aside a whole weekend…”

                “I’m going to have to meet your family some time,” I said quietly after a moment. It’s always been a sore spot with D.

                She sighed. “I know. I’m working up to it. I just want to punch them all so bad every time I see them…”

                I thought about it. “What about your brother? You get along with him okay, right?”

                She nodded. “Him I only want to punch with one fist,” she admitted.

                “Maybe you could introduce me to just him first, before we go for the whole family experience. Sort of get our toes wet before we dive in head first…”

                She nodded again. “Not a bad idea, babe. I’ll give it some thought… and I’ll give you some warning before I set anything up.”

                So we got back to the Dream House about this time, had crepes, and spoke with Marguerite and Julia about engagement ring shopping. Julia was excited by the prospect and offered to help us out almost before D asked her.

                Gawain overheard the conversation, and sort of shyly asked if any of us would like him to specially craft a set of wedding bands (speaking to all four of us, you understand). I don’t think any of us hesitated in taking him up on his very kind offer… Gawain’s metalwork is absolutely beautiful, and I can’t think of any more special or appropriate way to celebrate the occasion. He looked pleased by our enthusiastic responses, and shuffled off to his forge to practice.

                I think Marguerite was planning to come with us as we looked at engagement rings, but fate conspired against her. Okay, so some backstory first. Calamity’s best friend before she was taken was a girl named Stasia. One of Calamity’s biggest regrets has been that her fetch basically destroyed her relationship with Stasia. It did horrible things to her, causing her to hate Calamity with a fiery passion. Calamity has been hoping to rectify this for some time, and after doing some digging on the internet, Marguerite managed to locate her old friend in a suburb of Seattle. Calamity then asked Marguerite to check up on Stasia and make sure she was safe, and Marguerite sent her a mirror, and has been looking in on her periodically.

                Well, Calamity decided that it was time to make contact at last, and asked Marguerite to take her to Seattle so that she could speak with Stasia. Of course Marguerite agreed… her relationship with Calamity may never be what it used to be, but we are still a motley, and we support each other however we can. So she couldn’t join us in the ring-shopping expedition.

                I pulled Calamity aside before any of us left the Dream House. I told her that I knew she had to speak with her friend, and that she had to do it alone, but I wanted to make sure she knew that I would be there for her afterward, for anything she might need. It sounded a little feeble in my own mind, since there was very little I could do to help her through the most difficult part of this trial, but I think she appreciated it nonetheless.

                So… ring shopping. Julia knew a couple of good places in the area, and offered up plenty of great advice on the type of ring, stone, and setting… which was nice, since neither D nor I had any idea of what we were looking for. Julia’s parents know someone who owns a chain of jewelry stores… all hand-crafted stuff, which makes it quite nice even if it’s maybe not the cheapest. We looked in a few places she knew, but ended up going back there.

                In the end, we settled on a fairly simple ring… simple but beautiful. It’s a plain slim yellow-gold band without etchings or traceries in it. The gem is a three-quarter carat round-cut brilliant white diamond, surrounded by four tiny diamond chips to give the setting a little body.

                I am frankly in love with this ring. We kept looking after we saw it, and even visited a few other stores, but I knew almost as soon as I tried it on that it was perfect. They didn’t even have to resize it for me. D hemmed and hawed about getting a ring with a bigger stone, and they had some lovely examples… but this ring has an understated elegance that I can’t help but admire. D signed a few papers, and I walked out of the store wearing it.

                Oh, and while we were filling out the paperwork, Calamity texted the motley to let us know that things had gone well and that Stasia would be joining us for the show! Of course I was super-happy for my gal pal, and immediately texted a response to the tune of OMG-congrats-smiley-face-smiley-face-smiley-face-less-than-three. I think I’m finally starting to get the hang of this texting thing!

                By a fortunate coincidence, most of us arrived back at the Dream House at around the same time. Calamity’s friend Stasia is an attractive woman in her thirties with a two-tone punk hairstyle that really suits her. Calamity had enchanted her, so she could see us as we really are, and though she was obviously a bit overwhelmed by the whole situation, it seemed like she was taking it remarkably well. When I mentioned this to her, she sort of smirked.

                “I always knew something had happened to Kat,” she said by way of an explanation. “Her behavior changed overnight… and she somehow spontaneously became left-handed at the exact same time. I had always assumed it was some sort of government cloning project… but this makes sense too in a weird way.”

                She’s really smart, and incredibly perceptive. She has a wry, sarcastic streak a mile wide. I’d call her bitter, but considering what Calamity’s fetch put her through, I guess that’s understandable. In any case, it’s not hard to see how she and Calamity became such good friends as children; they each have this sardonic wit that plays really well off each other. Watching them together, you can see how they used to be as kids.

                It honestly made me a little melancholy, as it sort of reminded me of the way Missy, Jane and I used to finish each other’s sentences and have ideas that just clicked together. I have to rescue them. I have to.

                D approached me a couple of hours before the show. “Can I talk to you for a second, babe?” she asked seriously.

                “Sure, honey,” I said, giving her a quick kiss. “What’s on your mind?”

                “I’m gonna head out for a while,” she said. “I might not be here when the show starts, but I’ll be back before it’s over, I promise. Okay?”

                “Of course,” I said. “What’s up?”

                She shuffled her feet a bit. “I… need to tell Gladys about the engagement,” she said after a moment. “I owe it to her to tell her in person.”

                “Of course you do,” I said softly, pulling her into a hug. “I understand, Maria, don’t worry about it. Do you… think she’ll take it well?” I asked, trying not to show how very nervous that question made me.

                D shrugged. “I doubt she’ll be particularly pleased by the news, but I don’t think she’ll be angry or anything like that. Look, Lori… Friday night was our last goodbye, and she realizes that. When we split up before, there was a big fight, and the last thing I said to her before I left her was that I hated her. It’s not true. We were so close once… Friday night was the way our relationship should have ended.”

                I nodded. “Take all the time you need. And D… I’m sorry that my stupid jealousy stood in the way of that last night for so long.”

                D kissed me. “No worries, babe… totally not your fault. I had two years to make it happen before I ever met you, remember?”

                “Okay,” I relented. “Well, do what you have to do. Make sure she’s okay, and… thank her again for me, for keeping you safe.” D nodded, we kissed, and my fiancée headed out to inform her old flame of our impending nuptials.

                I had rearranged that evening’s acts once already to cover for Calamity’s potential absence, but since things went so well with Stasia, she was eager to strut her stuff, and I found myself shuffling the program once more. Calamity wanted to do her “Mistress for Christmas” number, and I suggested that the two of us should show off a bit with “Bloody Creature Poster Girl.” Gwen and Sarah grumbled a bit about having their solo acts bumped from a weekend show, but I promised I’d let them take the stage next Friday and Saturday, respectively.

                And speaking of those two… apparently they hooked up with Julia’s parents on Saturday night. John and Linda are a bit more adventurous than I had previously believed, and were happy to take advantage of their surroundings by inviting a couple of attractive young ladies to join them at their hotel. My two backup singers were giggling about it (they said the Oldacres had some really good weed), but I admonished them not to brag about it in front of Julia.

                I did tell Marguerite about the incident, so that she could shield her dearest love from the information more effectively. Unfortunately, she also had some news for me. It seems that Felicity had been intending to grace us with her presence last night, but had been forced to change her plans following a phone call from Rowan. My shyest girlfriend is not taking the news of my engagement well, and intended to get “blackout drunk,” but wanted to make sure she had someone else around for safety’s sake. Felicity agreed to keep her company, which is some comfort, at least.

                I wanted to call her up and comfort her, but rationally I knew that would probably be the worst thing I could have done. She needs to process this, and she needs to work through it without me distracting and confusing her. If I run to her while she’s hurting, I might be able to make her feel better… but it wouldn’t help her really deal with things in the long run. I don’t want to make things worse.

                I hate feeling helpless when someone I care about is suffering.

                No. I’m not going to get depressed or feel sad about this; not when I have so much to be happy about. I refuse.

                The show went well. I gave my performances my all, and let me tell you Calamity gave me a run for my money. She’s really taken to life in the spotlight, and I think she put everything she could into those particular acts in an attempt to impress her old friend. As usual, in our shared act I sort of angled my glamour to keep the audience’s attention on her… but I really opened it up during my later solo, and I think I gave most of our patrons a night to remember.

                Speaking of our patrons, Joan and Andromeda were there, so I sidled up to their table after I’d finished performing for the evening. I’d been wearing my new ring all night, of course, but I made sure to put my hands on the table fairly conspicuously anyway. Joan was oblivious at first, but Andromeda squealed after a moment and said “welcome to the club!”

                I guess Joan thought she was talking about my bisexuality or something, because her brow furrowed as she said “what are you talking about? She’s already in the… oh,” she said as she glanced at my hand. Then her face split in one of those rare, genuinely happy smiles of hers. “Congratulations, Lorelei. I am certain you and de la Rocha will have a long and happy life together.” Sometimes I forget that even before Joan and I became lovers, she and D were best friends.

                Anyway, I chatted with the two of them for a while before joining everybody at the girlfriend table. Gawain was already there, and he let me know that Moon wanted to speak with me if I was available. Now, I like Moon, but ze has never really gone out of hir way to speak with me, so I was a little confused about this. It turns out that ze wants me to speak with Dionysia. Ze thinks that hir friend is maybe a little too fond of Talecrafting. In fact, ze thinks she might be addicted to it.

                I don’t know a whole lot about Talecrafting, but I am given to understand that this is a serious issue. Talecrafting is incredibly dangerous if used recklessly, and while Dionysia is reputedly very good at it, it’s only a matter of time before something gives. Eventually, reality will sort of snap back at her, and she’ll suffer some sort of dire consequence. No one wants that, but every time Moon tries to bring this up in conversation, Dionysia pooh-poohs hir concerns and claims she has it under control. Ze thinks that I might have more luck, since, well… I have the reputation in this Freehold of being very persuasive. I agreed to give it a shot, though I warned hir that I don’t know Dionysia very well, so she might not be particularly inclined to listen to me.

                Moon also wanted to tell me about something else… something too cool for words! Apparently Oleander has been working on a way to help us when we eventually travel to the Empress’ realm to rescue Missy and Jane. She’s been developing… something. Some sort of tracking doohickey. It will have Hawk’s scent, and it will be able to follow him through the Hedge to the point where he enters Arcadia proper. With luck, it should help us find Her realm more quickly.

                No, I don’t know what it is, exactly. Moon made reference to something called a “wheeled warrior,” but like most references made by most people in this Freehold, I had no hope of understanding it. Still, the next time I see Oleander, I need to remember to give her a big sloppy kiss. You know, an extra one. To thank her for her efforts.

While I did my best to join in on the conversations around us, I was mostly just sitting there thinking about my honey and how strong and smart and soft and sexy and… and… and she smells nice. Have I ever mentioned that before? Well she does.

There was a lot of talk… most of it was explaining things to Stasia, as I recall, but there tended to be two or three conversations going on at any given time, so I could be wrong. I know that both Jacob and I talked about the weird time-dilation effect of Arcadia, albeit from different directions. I know we talked about fetches, and how they can be perfectly innocuous (like Becky’s) or incredibly dangerous (like Mary’s).

The appreciative glances she gave me and several of the others over the course of the evening was more than enough to indicate that she likes women, but I’ve sort of filed that information away until I can determine how Calamity would feel about that. It’s obvious that she loves Stasia a great deal, but I’m not yet sure what kind of love that is, or how she would feel about sharing her.

She went to a fine arts school hoping to make a living as an artist, and nearly washed out before she discovered a talent for photography. She sort of casually mentioned that one of the fringe benefits of the job was that she got to spend a lot of time with attractive young women, and I took that as my cue.
