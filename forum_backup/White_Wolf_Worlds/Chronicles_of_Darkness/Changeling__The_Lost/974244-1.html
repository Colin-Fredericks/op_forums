                                        On the whole "why have drawbacks" thing:

                                        The basic design logic is pretty simple. A drawback lets you reduce the cost of a Merit so that it remains accessible. This is important for Merits that have a lot of flavor, but also a lot of potency in concept. If you valued Dual Kith at what it can be effectively worth at the high-mid level of optimization, it could easily be worth 5 Merit dots. Which is fine for people that are just crunching numbers, but doesn't work for people that care more about the narrative aspect of the Merit to have to sink half their starting Merit points into it.

                                        So drawbacks let you balance that out. The mechanical value is decreased, so the cost can be decreased to a level where the narrative value isn't being overridden.
