                        Originally posted by MachineIV View Post

                        Yeah. IMO, if background and identity doesn't affect your choices, then your choices are arbitrary and random.
                        Affect, yes, but not define, and I think the language around that is the sticking point around which this all revolves. Our past informs our future, but it does not dictate it, and I think that distinction is especially significant in terms of an abuse analogy. It does damage to assert to the abused that they are defined more by what happened to them than the choices they make for themselves; those decisions are more defining of a person than their abuse, says more about their demeanor/psychology, and (most importantly) can erupt from many types of backgrounds

                        An example: someone abused in a situation that might create an ogre. Pushed far by bullying, that person can break the cycle many ways, perhaps by reacting violently and becoming an ogre, pushing that bully towards someone else and becoming a darkling, or forgiving that person and overcoming it.

                        I know the immediate response will be that which of the decisions that person make is defined by how they reacted to that abuse, but I think that is a disservice to the human spirit and to the autonomy with which we are meant to create player characters. I love that the seemings are defined by the manner in which characters seized their own agency. I am disturbed by the implication that they also decide who you were before then and why you needed to at all. I don't think it does damage to the system to suggest they can do so, but that there are other open avenues that are just as valid.

                                                            So, it's taken me a while to figure out how exactly I want to express my response, but here's my own feelings on the matter.

                                                            Seeming in 1e said little to nothing about who your character was. It was a vehicle for delivering a set of affinity contracts attached to a superficial description of how it made your character look. As an afterthought, it also gave a reason people of that seeming were usually taken (lured into the wild for Beasts, as punishment for some unknown transgression for Darklings, specifically desired by their furure Keeper for Elementals, coveted for their beauty and skill for Fairest, used as a weapon for Ogres, and no reason at all for Wizened). Most people I played with ignored that part anyway. As a result, characters were often extremely superficial, i.e. "I'm a Dual-Kith something something of the whatever court" with no further explanation. The reason, I feel, that the sample backgrounds in 2e are so detailed and specifically tied into the character's escape is to get people thinking about the character as a person with a history that lead them to become who they are today, not just a collection of superficial traits. Don't just tell me what your character looks like, tell me a story about who they were before, why they were taken, and how their life experiences lead them to their escape. It doesn't have to be the same story that the Seeming's write up tells; that should go without saying. I want to hear your character's story, but I want that story to tell me something about who your character is and how they got to be that way. The sample backgrounds and escapes, to me, are just examples of what a good character background should look like.
