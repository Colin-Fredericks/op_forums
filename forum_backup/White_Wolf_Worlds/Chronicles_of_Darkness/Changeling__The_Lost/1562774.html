    Changeling the lost Clarity Trigger Questions

    08-06-2026, 11:35 AM
    I've always been kinda pissed that there wasnt anything like the questions for breaking points in the core book, so after some time and looking though Hill's 2e, I managed to create a list of questions for that very thing.

    All the old rules still apply of course.



    What was the most painful experience you have been subjected too?
    The Durance is not a kind time, and the keepers are at best aloof and at worst, unimaginably cruel.
    Though this doesn’t have to relate to direct pain, yes starving to death might have you loose grip with reality, but so could being forced to attend a wedding for that was your durance, wedding after wedding, it could being forced to look at a beast, the very one that you where turned into.

    Point being, the pain should remind you of where you where placed, and how such pain can erode your perception.

    Example
    Tammy was isolated and unable to feed, Bath, or even realy have any independence, which makes when she’s ill kept or starving(Glamour or Food wise) it makes Her think that’s back, trapped into the Tamagotchi.


    What is the most horrid thing you where forced to do?
    The Keeper forces their changelings to do things, form the minor to the extreme, form gathering bit of glass to Purposely isolating ones self killing any intruders.
    This could be some large horrific act, eating children, slaughtering inncoits, or poisoning the water hole, it could be giving out bad information, keeping knowledge that should have been shared a secret, or just being regular old Isolated, never meant to talk to someone.

    Example
    Tammy’s biggest Regert form her Durance is that she was a weight, she wasnt able to do much and everyone had to focus on Her.
    She was just a Burden, had to rely on others to be a
    When she’s a Burden, she feels like she might as well be back in Her Tamagotchi shell.


    What do you regret doing to escape?
    While tales of the escape are seen as grand and powerful for the changelings, they also carry a hit of tragedy, for there is often an act that they did that was nessary to escape but also something that muddies the tale.
    Form selling out a friend to a keeper, to Using other changelings as stepping stones, to even something just as minor of just kinda being a sleezy dick to someone imporant to you can remind you your not as far form Aracadia as you wish.

    Example
    Tammy left a love and a… it’s complicated, in Aracadia when she escaped as a Giant digtal dog, too caught up with Her pain and Her own troubles.
    She regrets it deeply and wants to never leave someone behind again, which also isnt a healthy move.


    What reminds you most of your durance?
    The keeper reflect much form mankind, and that can pop up in the real world.
    Certian mannerisms, places, items, actions, etc can make a changeling feel like there staring right back into it.
    This does not need to be a big thing, it could be as minor as some jack ass talking in Buiness lingo while trying to sell you some shady investment or as major as a Slaughter house reminding you of your durance.

    Example
    Tammy was sent to the island of misfit toys, which means that a large amount of toys fucks with Her head. A single plushie or two doesn’t Hurt her so, but a gathering of toys, especially if they are arranged into a toy town thing, really fucks with her.


    What lost did you learn of hurt you the most?
    The Durance takes time, or no time at all, or all the time to you but no time to any one else.
    Or negative time.
    Because of this, the Changeling loose something fomr the durance, form peoples, places, to ideals and much more.
    It’s part of the reason why the Fetches are so hated, they are walking reminds of what they lost, all while living a life they want, even if superficially.

    Examples
    Tammy’s lost a lot, she was trapped in Aracadia since 91’, Her family has moved on, most of Her friends where also taken into Aracaida.
    That being siad Tammy is Mostly okay with this, Or at least is the least of Her worries.
    What’s really fucking Her up is the fact she can no longer hide form Her sins and how Horrifc Her past actions where.
    No that she doesn’t try, but being reminded of Her past tends to send Her down into a spiral.
    Made worst by the fact she’s a true crime star.

              I ask mainly because the current system has regaining memories of your durance through Icons and (even in 1e) dreaming with greater clarity/frequency of your time in Arcadia proportionate to your Wyrd leading to new criteria for "reenacting or reliving a memory from your durance." Between that and actively searching out memories of your durance being a breaking point it's pretty clear that Clarity is meant to make aversion to acknowledging concrete events from Faerie a common point of changeling psychology.

              This instead seems to suggest defining all the potential psychological landmines a character can have as a starting character (with a Clarity maximum as low as 2 and the attendant perceptual issues) irrespective of whether they actually remember them in the same way as Integrity is meant to loosely establish the boundaries of a mortal character's mundane worldview, self-concept, and capacity for moral injury, despite 2e Clarity already being one of the more easily-tested Integrity analogues.

              Like, it absolutely makes sense to keep track of things that your character would recognize as similar to parts of their metaphysically significant traumatic backstory, but the way the game is written is consistent on the idea that the root defense mechanism for the game's title characters to functionally operate in human society is that a bunch of the stuff that happened to them isn't consciously known to them without getting more deeply involved with faerie magic and the territory of the beings they escaped from, to the point where regaining lost memories and vividly re-experiencing events as they become more magical are both elements of the setting with associated mechanics.

                      Yes. The base game models improving your Clarity without adding new fracture points as handled by increasing your Wits and Composure (which also affects your Touchstone capacity and available buffer for attachment), maintaining Touchstones, and refraining from increasing your Wyrd too high (in addition to maintaining behavior that penalizes Clarity attack rolls by playing into your Needle and/or Thread, spending Willpower, taking steps to avoid being in a position where they can be coerced or forced into action, avoiding proximity to people they know well and are emotionally invested in, etc when it comes to actions that risk breaking points).

                      The principle tension Clarity provides to the game is that the character will have things they care deeply about, but the best way to reduce risk to Clarity is to be in control of your actions and not care too much about what you're doing or who you're doing it to when you do things that remind you of the Fae, and the main way you recover from damage to your Clarity that isn't deep enough to have mechanical effects is to interact with things that you deeply care about (with deeper Clarity damage allowing you to process your way out of it at a less efficient rate).

                      Since the durance is a turbulent time in a changeling's life (at least for those present enough to choose to escape), a lot of the breaking points you "unlock" over time are more likely to do severe damage to your Clarity and may more easily overlap with emotionally significant moments in their new life, and the earlier days of a changeling's return to earth are likely to have emotionally significant moments by dint of the usual troubles a freshly escaped changeling has to deal with.

                      Local courts can help provide a safety net, but learning the hard lesson of "talking about being abducted by aliens is going to get you rejected, shunned, and/or unintentionally gaslit" is one of the main risks at low Clarity that low Wyrd gives some insulation for even as the inability to keep your story straight discourages you from talking about what happened to you in Faerie that you can remember. (The fragility of the bonus from max Clarity in turn serves as a soft pressure to take the Acute Senses Merit and raise your Wyrd instead.)

                              08-07-2026, 12:12 AM
                              The game as-written assumes a character will not be reminded of events that are absent from their conscious memory, provides means of recovering that character's memories in concrete form, and says that characters with higher Wyrd ratings dream of their time in Arcadia more often and in greater detail (and notes that all of the Lost are lucid dreamers) and can better remember and interpret their dreams in general.

                              The game also specifically states that every changeling character has at least one Icon, that Icons can provide access to information the character had forgotten about their live in or before Arcadia, and that the degree to which individual changelings remember and are affected by their durance varies widely but that most of them keep their trauma in the back of their minds unless things are tense and/or emotional.

                              Regardless of how much a character has forgotten, I do not think it is appropriate for breaking points based on being reminded of one's durance to be universally applicable to characters without regard for their ability to actually remember them — this isn't Geist, where crisis points setting off your broken superpowered other half without warning because you don't know enough about their past is the primary narrative reason for a character to follow the breadcrumb trail that leads to the thing that made them take a drink that turned their spirit inside-out. Suppressed memories of the durance might emerge in moments of desperation, but the broad strokes of How Your Durance Sucked are represented in more universal things like the Seeming Curses or the rest of the list of example breaking points, which Kith and Kin does a decent job of elaborating upon in terms of how being lied to, having promises broken, and being told your experiences aren't real can happen organically in the course of everyday life.

                              I would also note that a lot of the things these questions are asking are also asked in the corebook's "Setting the Stage" chronicle prep tool (p297-302), and that the things that endanger Clarity besides specific reminders of your durance are one-size-fits-all for the simple reason that Clarity models, as the book puts it, your ownership of your own perceptions. Much as Demon: the Descent's equivalent of the Five Questions for Cover are less concerned with customizing compromise triggers than identifying potential weak links in your demon character's social network for revealing your true nature, the questions in Setting the Stage address the broad strokes of the character's background in a way that establishes important relationships, painful subjects, sense memories, and particular ways the character's new life might remind them of their time in Faerie.

                                      For simplicity's I would take cues from Setting the Stage, get a list of prominent durance elements from each player, remind the player whenever something significant and resonant with one or more of those elements comes up that if they take Clarity damage they get a Beat, and ask if they want that element to trigger Clarity attacks from now on.

                                      The majority of issues with Clarity are going to come from more immediate ways in which the character is given to doubt their ability to correctly interpret and participate in reality. A sin list wouldn't make much sense and the sample breaking points are both non-exhaustive and adjustable. If it helps you to have something like the Five Questions, that's great, but the reason Integrity has those questions is because mortal characters don't have figuring out a good portion of their otherworldly traumatic backstory as part of the character creation process and need to define weak points in their self-concept relative to common socialization topics like "murder is bad" and "my neighbor was a witch."
