            Yes, the Mask changes to accomodate - it's why powers such as Chrysalis or Props And Scenary, which change a Changeling into an animal or an object respectively, hide them away from people who can't see past the Mask. Otherwise, they would see normal people acting like animals or standing awkwardly. This would also apply to transformations forced upon a Changeling by another being.

            Rule of thumb is, unless it specifies a Changeling's Mien or Mask, presume a transformation affects both.

            Originally posted by Firefly Night View Post
            From what I understand about the Mask, it works to hide the existence of fairy magic from the mortal world. If the shapeshifting affect alters the changeling's physical form so that it it is no longer possesses Wyrd tainted abnormalities, what does the Mask have to hide?

            I guess the real question is: Can magical effects that operate outside of the Wyrd erase the mutations inflicted upon a changeling's body by the Gentry? Like, if a mage uses their magic to a transform a changeling with stone skin into a fox ... does the changeling become a normal fox, or does he become a fox with stone fur that just looks like a fox?
            A Changeling's transformation is complete and total - from their blood to their bones to their very soul, they are considered fae, otherwise iron would not affect them as it does. The closest that a fae would have to "Wyrd tained abnormalities" would be a Fae-Touched, that being a person who has made a significant promise to someone who became a Changeling, journeyed into the Hedge and absorbed some of its ambient magic, and managed to escape in turn. Even in that case, the "abnormalities" would be that fae magic itself, and a Fae-Touched is still changed so innately they are harmed by iron like a Changeling is. A Mage cannot change a Changeling back into a human anymore than they could turn a normal mortal into a Changeling, unless they were an Archmage and even then that change could be dispelled. As noted above, even if a Mage transforms a Changeling so they are a fox, they will look like a fox both to Mask and Mien...but as for whether their Mien would lack their stone fur depends on the Storyteller, and or if the spell specifically was to change a Changeling into a fox or a normal person into a fox. If it was for just a normal person, I'd rule no because it wasn't specifically meant to affect the fae nature of the person. If it was for a Changeling...well that'd still be quite a feat, and they'd still have a Changeling on their hands, not just a fox.

            Even if someone could change a Changeling's Mien so it no longer looks fae, that doesn't mean that they aren't. The Mask serves not only to keep other people from realizing a Changeling is anything but human, but also serves to keep other fae from tracking a Changeling down easily. Without their Mask, a Changeling leaves a trail that any fae can follow, which is why journeying through the Hedge (where their Mask is innately down) is a risky prospect for them. The Mask suppresses their fae nature, to the point that when a Changeling eschews it when it's normally up their contracts are innately more potent (as in they always result in an Exceptional Success).

            One final note on the subject. Given a Changeling's durance was so horrific that they were changed innately, their mien itself being changed by an outside force would probably be an attack on their clarity and thus sanity, since yet another being is transforming them outside of their control. If anything, it being a non-fae source would probably be worse, since it'd be from a power they wouldn't have an innate understanding unlike the Wyrd. If they willingly consented or even asked for it, it may not be as bad but it is a significant transformation.

                        I apologize if I came (and come) across as lecturing, but your definitions are a little...odd, considering that by that meaning every single supernatural creature a player can play has "abnormalities". From a Vampire's fangs and requirements for Vitae, to a Werewolf's rage and otherworldly forms, to a Mage's ability to affect the universe with their magic, anything that a player can play that isn't mundane human is abnormal and mutated. "Normal" is a very subjective term in real life, and in a world where someone can meet or become virtually anything they can imagine it becomes even more flexible.

                        A Changeling's physicality isn't any easier to change than most mortals. Changelings are humans who were taken to Arcadia and forced to become the property of a True Fae, allowing said True Fae to basically alter them as they please. The True Fae, Gentry, Keepers or however you want to refer to them are practically gods in their domain, and can change their surroundings and property to fit their whims. They possess a level of power not even obtainable by a Changeling at Wyrd 10, even when compared to a Gentry who is effectively at Wyrd 6. Even taking out the factor of the Gentry, the Hedge by its very nature turns people into "stranger, truer versions of themselves", meaning any normal mortal (or potentially any other supernatural) who wanders the Hedge long enough becomes a Hobgoblin that in some way represents who and what they were in reality. Everything involved here is not only on a scale of power practically no playable supernatural can achieve, it is a process that is practically part of the laws of physics for the realm the Hedge and Arcadia inhabit. Just as everything degrades on the molecular level in the normal realm, so does everything change in the Hedge until it fits that particular system, and once it fits then that form becomes all but permanent for them. In exchange for these transformations, these beings become tied to the Wyrd and gain power through it.

                        This isn't like being tainted by the Abyss and transformed in ways that don't make logistical sense - the Hedge and Gentry have a logic to their transformations, but it's not a human logic.

                                        Other Changelings, as well as Fae-touched, Hobgoblins and probably some sentient Tokens (though those might just be weird Hobgoblins) can see through The Mask. The general rule seems to be if it has a Mask itself, it can see through it too. I'm not sure if True Fae can see through it (I think in 2e they have more raw power than the Mask can contain), but it's not a big issue as being Ironside is taxing for them and not something they do when they have other choices. Huntsmen can see through the Mask, but making pledges with humans (standard house fairy stuff like protecting someone's herd in exchange for food left on the doorstep or protecting their dreams from nightmares and supernatural intrusions) gives you a sort of disguise as a natural supernatural element of Ironside instead of a Changeling.

                                        The Mask, however, does not conceal obvious shows of fae magic. So you may not see a clockwork man suddenly become surrounded by a whirlwind of scrap metal, but you'll still see a pale guy with slightly stiff movements suddenly conjure up a whirlwind of scrap metal out of nowhere.

                                        You can Strengthen the Mask for a scene for a point of Glamour to have it appear to other Changelings, but in exchange, your shadow will show the shape of your Mien to not only Changelings, but everyone who sees it.

                                        The Mask does not work in the Hedge or Arcadia, so anyone who winds up there sees the Mien of any Fairy or fae-made magical object (Tokens and Hedge-spun items).

                                        The way other monsters' powers affect the Mask is weird, as its considered an innate feature rather than an active power. I'll let someone else explain this part.

                                        Dropping the Mask, which can only be done Ironside, trades the obvious downsides of revealing your true nature for some sort of boost to Contracts. It is also a prerequisite for making the kind of pledges that mask you from the Huntsmen, as they must be made with full knowledge on the mortal's part of the nature and terms of the bargain from what I gather.

                                                            Ok, thanks. I was confused by Taidragon's comment earlier: "The Mask serves not only to keep other people from realizing a Changeling is anything but human, but also serves to keep other fae from tracking a Changeling down easily. Without their Mask, a Changeling leaves a trail that any fae can follow, which is why journeying through the Hedge (where their Mask is innately down) is a risky prospect for them."

                                                            Perhaps I conflated "seeing the Changeling as what it is" with "tracking a Changeling down easily"--in careful reflection they do not have to be the same thing, I suppose. Taidragon refers in the remainder of this quote to the "trail any fae can follow", which gets at tracking and chase and the like, not staring one in the face....

                                                            Ok, so in summary (because others with this question will read the thread):

                                                            1. The Mask covers the appearance of the Changeling and her abnormalities to non-Glamour-wielding creatures.
                                                            2. Supernatural creatures of all stripes may be able to pierce the Mask and perceive her as she really is using their supernatural abilities. (Clash of Wills I assume.)
                                                            3. When in the Hedge, the Mask slips. Everything in the Hedge is more True to itself, and that definitely includes a Changeling's appearance!
                                                            4. Changelings may drop their Mask for benefits. This could be done by scouring it (which is... unsubtle) or through certain Contracts.
                                                            5. The Mask carries even through supernatural transformations to other physical forms--especially those triggered by Contracts, but also (keeping in theme) other splat powers.

                                                            Bonus question, then: since dream bastions are in the Hedge, does that mean that when the Changeling appears in a dream she manifests without her Mask? I presume that dream-shaping abilities would enable her to cover up, so to speak, but does she have no automatic concealment? Is it any different if she appears in the dreams of another supernatural creature?
