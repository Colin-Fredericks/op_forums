    What are the core conflicts in the Changeling chronicles you've ran or played in?

    05-10-2021, 12:25 PM
    I'm asking specifically about games you've actually run or played in, not theoretical answers about Changeling in general or future chronicles you have planned.

    What have the core conflicts in your games been?

    It seems a lot of the rulebook pushes you towards a cycle of different attempts by the Fae to recapture your PCs, either directly or via huntsmen or loyalists, and the different shennanigans PCs engage in to try to avoid capture - or to try and "power up" to make themselves more difficult to capture in the future.

    But when I hear people talking about their changeling games, it feels like most of the times the core conflicts in their chronicles is some kind of Freehold politics, although often they tie together with Fae threats ("one of the Monarch is betraying the Freehold to the Fae" seems a hugely popular plotline).

    Anyways, I'm kind of curious what people actually end up doing in actual play and if my perceptions are correct.

              We haven't gotten that far yet in our campaign (where I'm a player; not the ST) so we're still finding a lot more questions than answers. That said, a lot of focus have been in finding weird stuff in the Hedge and I believe that'll be quite a big part in the future as well. Especially a place we only know as "the Kingdom" that seems to be connected to the Hedge via the dreams of certain mortals. My guess is that whatever it is will attract the attention of True Fae sooner or later (if it hasn't already), and the two Courts that founded the Freehold (three more Courts have joined since) are almost certainly connected to this realm in some way, so despite all the mystery we might actually end up with at least some political conflicts in the Freehold.

              So neither True Fae nor Huntsmen nor Loyalists have made a (recognisable) impact on the story yet. I do think they'll show up at some point but only in personal story arcs rather than as a main focus.

                  i've only run one shots for 2nd edition, but i ran several changeling games that ran for years back in the aughts and the early teens. since the themes of the editions are pretty similar, i think the experiences should pan:

                  1) loyalists were a HUGE issue in my games, in specific the "cylon" take on the loyalist ... you never know who in the freehold is a sellout. the fairies and their pawns were always working to undermine freeholds and their rulers in an attempt to capture their lost charges. this added a very cold war vibe to court intrigue, with changelings only saying half of what they mean unless their were bound in an oath with who they were interacting with.

                  2) the fetches were always a big part of my games during the early stages, i usually run two concurrent storylines, one with changelings doing changeling things and the other with fetches slowly learning the horrors of the World of Darkness,

                  3) hobgoblins, spirits and ghosts: i am not a huge fan of murder and violent conflict as the go to strategy to resolving problems ... especially when it concerns the killing of other people. i try to minimize human on human violence save for moments of high drama... but if i need to have a combat set piece, i almost always use hobgoblins as the fodder.

                  4) I made "witches" a big thorn in the side of changeling society. in my game, mages weren't a thing and almost all magic was the result of humans learning contracts and enlisting the servitude of a fairy servant by exploiting their banes and taboos.

                  5) while there was some inter-court issues... there jus wasn't enough changelings in the average freehold to keep up the political intrigues for long.

                      I have have recently started running a CtL 2e chronicle revolving around a Tier 2 kind of mystery: there is a unknown supernatural phenomenon from the Hedge that is threatening the whole Freehold/city/region.
                      The way I designed the setting, there is also room for some political conflict, if the players wish to go for that.
                      Only one player seems to be interested in dealing with his Fetch, while the others don't really care.

                      The way it's presented in the 2e book, and judging by some posts on this forum, it seems that the game is very oriented towards the "getting your life back" aspect. Personally, I think it takes a bit more gaming experience for me and my group to focus on more personal conflicts. A "supernatural mystery threat" story is more straightforward, keeps the group together, and is easier to pull off as ST, at least for me.

                                  We're playing from right after their escape.
                                  The idea is that all of the PCs as humans made some pretty strong promises to one another in an area "close to the Hedge" and so those promises resonated strongly despite them being mortal at the time. But they broke their promises. Later one of them was captured and these broken bonds were noticed. Somehow, that Keeper enlisted the aid of a fae being capable of following broken oaths from one individual to the other, and the True Fae wanted the "whole set" as it were.

                                  After escaping together they now find themselves trying to get a grip on what happened to their lives in their absence (a whole lot of that), avoiding the shadowy monsters being sent after them to recapture them, and finding out how they keep finding them as well as how to mend the rifts between them (and thus make their pursuers lose the scent).

                                  They all let me have a lot of control over their Durances and what happened in their absence, so they all have a ton of personal problems to deal with now. That honestly forms the bigger part of the sessions (we've played 20-24 hours in total now), with them being hunted being a backdrop and applying urgency.
                                  The Freehold in the city is monitoring them but avoiding them, since they can tell what's going on with their being tracked, and the belief is that if they made any promises to any Freehold member, the "scent" might follow the path of Freehold bonds and put everyone at risk.

                                  It's an Elizabethan era game and all of their fetches are designed with maximum impact in mind:
                                  - One of them has become the Sheriff of London. He doesn't believe he's a fetch, but has noticed all the supernatural stuff going on in town, so he has founded "The Vigil" and is hunting Changelings especially, believing the supernaturals are demons (biblical sense) and up to no good.
                                  - One of them is kingpin of the criminal underground.
                                  - One of them is the most famous actor in town, so famous in fact that it has warped opinions of theatre away from what they historically were, towards more public and higher-class acceptance.
                                  - One of them is a serial killer. (This one I like especially, for how do you deal with a serial killer who has the same face as you? Who might have loose ends you don't know about that could come to bite you if you just kill him and take his place? Very fun!)
                                  - One of them is considered a traitor to the realm for mysterious reasons.
                                  - One of them became a protestant priest. Which doesn't sound too bad until you realize the Changeling (and his family) are secret catholics at a time when the crackdown against catholicism got severe. So he's pretty despised by his family for now, and his father is locked up in the Tower of London...

                                  They also all have several NPCs of interest from their backgrounds, and since they escaped at the start of the first session, I ruled that all had no Touchstone (but one free touchstone slot open, which they can fill simply by having a meaningful interaction with a human they want as Touchstone... so far only one player has done so xD). And they started with both the Hunted and Oathbreaker persistent conditions. Which combined with having no touchstone, makes all Clarity damage rolls against them 5 dice despite being Wyrd 1. And they keep finding out shocking things, being told they're crazy by old human friends and families, etc...
                                  Fun, fun times. xD The beats flow freely and plentifully.

                                  Extremely happy with how it is all going so far. Never have my players managed to decide their Aspirations quite so smoothly in previous campaigns. ^^

                                          I think that constant attacks by one's keeper get frustrating fast or else become a kind of joke if they aren't threatening enough so it's better to use the threat sparingly. If you just rotate who's keeper is attacking each session and they can be summarily defeated by the end of the session the True Fae become Team Rocket.

                                          As for actual game examples, how many do you want? I've run and played allot of Changeling, albeit all 1e.

                                          My longest running CtL game I'd say the central conflict was the question of how to (re)build your life. For some characters this meant freehold politics and magical adventure as they focused on their new identity as a changeling, for others it meant carving out a new place for themselves in the mortal world, and for a small few it meant trying to regain what they lost when they were taken. The threat of the Others was ever-present and became deadly on multiple occasions but probably 80% of sessions had no sign of the Good Neighbors outside the characters own paranoia.

                                          My favorite game as a player ... The game was set in a city that one of the Gentry had turned into their realm-on-earth and the ST's secret goal (revealed after game ended) was to turn every PC into a loyalist, so I guess that would be our central conflict.

                                          Fast forward to today and the Lost game I'm currently in is indeed, finally, for the first time, centrally focused on our Keepers efforts to reclaim our PC's and the fugitive struggle to stay a step ahead. So that's one game out of I-don't-even-remember-how-many by the book, I guess.

                                                  Originally posted by Madhatter View Post
                                                  In my thread True Fae perception, I started a dispute about whether a changeling could become one of the Gentry or not. This made me consider. Why not do a changeling chronicle about a changeling who WANTS to become one of the True Fae? What are the obstacles in his path and how far is he willing to go? Also, what does the character give up on becoming one of the Gentry?
                                                  That's an interesting series of questions -- for a character. But PCs don't ever really get to see those motivations/sacrifices except on a superficial level. The question that needs to be asked here is: what problems, conflicts, or situations arise because of this changeling's quest? Because that's where your game is. That's what will spawn the things the players wind up needing to deal with.

                                                  For instance, in the game I'm running, the primary driver of conflict is the Gentry craftsman who needs to fulfill his vow to make creations that are "better than real people." His creations are Fetches, so he frequently kidnaps people to sell to other Gentry. He saw the existing Freehold in the town he settled in as an impediment to his plans, and so he wiped them out, allowing the Gentry and local greedy hobgoblins to plunder the area without worrying about the Seasonal Bargains. In his quest to figure out what will best "prove" to the Wyrd his creations are better, he's inserted a contract into his creations that pulls their changeling counterparts back from Arcadia to the mortal world, so he can instigate conflicts between the changelings and the fetches to see who comes out on top. Also, he's tried to create Fetches ex nihilo, and wound up tapping into The Divine Fire to create Prometheans (which he then discards because he is eventually tainted with Disquiet) and Pandorans (which he has no use for, and has sold them around town as art sculptures...meaning that whenever one of his wayward Prometheans comes within range, they wake up and go on rampages).

                                                  All of this stems from one central question: How does this character, with this goal, cause problems for the players to encounter and deal with? It doesn't matter if it's actually possible for a Changeling to become one of the Gentry -- what matters is the damage the character does in pursuit of that goal as long as they believe it's possible.
