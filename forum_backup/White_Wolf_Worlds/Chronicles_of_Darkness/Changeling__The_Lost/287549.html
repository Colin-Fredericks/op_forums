    Plating Armor with Iron - Ways to do it without iron losing its properties?

    10-20-2014, 09:47 AM
    Basically, my assumption is that iron as second most stable element in universe (actually nickel-62 is more stable than iron, but it's also rare. Within my assumption it's even more effective than iron, but any artifact made for it will be rare enough to make it not as widespread as iron ones) thus it's an anathema to fluid reality that True Fae embody, and it also affects Spirits and Mages (the latter not in the sense of iron harming them, but certain artifacts made from cold iron could give enhanced resistance against Arcana effects).

    Hunter Conspiracy I craft makes use of Iron plated armors and suits, those enviromentally sealed being able to remarkably stave off effects of Hedge on mortals (before you ask how they get there, they maintain positive relationship with freeholds, especially Summer Court as they share similar militant attitude towards the threat of Gentry). Protective Iron artifacts like that could be part of Endowments.

    The question is, what could be a method of covering such suits/armors with Iron plating without it losing its anti-magic properties?

          • Hand-forged iron that is improved or forged in any way with magic (such as Contracts of Artifice or the Smith’s kith blessing) becomes immediately and permanently treated as cold iron. Cold iron improved or forged with magic loses all its beneficial effects against the supernatural.

              If you're going with "The Foe of All Unreason" option, then the standard issues with handling iron all still apply. In some senses it is actually easier to do things like iron armor if you can work iron appropriately, because you could do something like scale, lamellar, or plated mail style (where you just need to be able to reliably produce relatively small and simple shapes). Then you can lace them over the environmental suit and use that to replace the padding layers used in traditional armors. This is going to be much easier to do and avoid depowering the iron compared to trying to make full plate armor with it. Though obviously this armor would provide less actual protection from damage than full plate.

              Though I would be extremely hesitant to apply "The Foe of All Unreason" in anything but a pure Changeling game (though Changeling + Hunter I can see). Forcing the other supernaturals to follow this is kind of taking a massive dump on their metaphysics. "It's so not special it degrades magic" really doesn't make any sense in the other game lines.

                          How thick do you want the iron?

                          Theoretically, you could mould the iron into any shape you want with a strong enough magnetic field; I'm thinking 2.5 cm thick sheets (flattens through magnetism).

                          Because then you just need some method of cutting it, and then you can apply it to a mold.

                          I'm thinking the mold would look a bit like a dress mold, with a powerful electromagnet at the core. As it powers up, the iron would deform to the right shape. You just need to stop it periodically to adjust the layering of the iron, use a hammer for the final shaping, and then bolt it together.

                          You need about 1.9 m2 of this plating to cover an average adult male; that comes out to about 37.5 kg (a bit under 87 lbs) spread over your body. Once you've learned to move in it, you shouldn't have much of a problem. Now, the question is whether you want to layer the rest of your armor on top of that, or under it. Because if the latter, it'll get a few kilos heavier.

                                                          Fae and iron is a result of some Contract according to the line's lore. Cold iron isn't necessarily all that pure. It does have a very low carbon content. It can be welded to softer steel, beaten into sheets or drawn into wire (if it is very pure iron). The welding would mess with the mojo at the joints, but you might be able to creatively weld it chemically or magically so the joint is on the inside so it wouldn't matter.

                                                          The iron can be crafted into common types of archaic armor like plate, chain or scale. The biggest problem is it isn't as strong as steel and Gentry can use steel weapons. Iron on the outside would be effective against magic, but not as much against mundane weapon attacks. It might help with Gentry magic but be vulnerable to Goblin attacks. Just a point to consider.

                                                          I think magnetism would be fine as long as the force was not stronger than the hammer. Cold iron typically has impurities in the form of strings within the piece of metal so too much force would crack and break the piece of iron. I don't see any advantage to magnetism, but if you just want to use it as a replacement for cold working (similar craft time) then it sounds fine.
