    CtL: The Tellurian Courts

    10-10-2016, 11:55 AM
    I wrote this in a bizarre attempt at the concept of Dante's Divine Comedy, with their being a Court for the Inferno, Purgatory, and Heaven. Thing is, I'm having trouble making the middle one interesting and unique. Any suggestions and ideas, especially for tokens, goblin fruit, and heraldry?

    Empyreal Court
    Aka Court of Angels, the Alabaster Throne, the Court of Zeal





    The Empyreal Court seeks to repent, some believing they were dragged to Hell for justly deserved reasons. Others see it as a call to arms--to conduct a crusade against the Others worthy of the gods! And finally, there are those who see it an excuse to abuse their power while decorated in the mantle of righteousness. You don’t have to be religious to join the court, however. In truth, the original reason for the court was because there was a doubt in the divine. When the Others kidnapped, tortured, and took everything away from their slaves, no prayers were answered. Seeing a world devoid of angels, the Empyreal Court was made to become such beings, to defend humanity against the monsters that dwelt beyond the Hedge. By any means necessary.

    The Court has affinity for the Contracts of Potential (Swords at Dawn pg. 138-140).

    Courtiers
    The Alabaster Throne welcomes all who believe it their duty to combat the injustice of the Kindly Ones. While their emotion of zeal generally ensures proactivity, each courtier dedicates themselves to upholding a virtue they wish to exemplify. Whether this process actually works varies from member to member, and at times, this can lead to conflict between fellow courtiers. A courtier of justice, for example, becomes heckled when a courtier of compassion demands leniency on the man who stabbed the store clerk out of desperation to put food on his family’s table. Such disputes become judicated by the senior members of the court when possible, but violence does happen now and again.

    Rituals
    Penitent’s Week – not necessarily on a specific date, Penitent’s Week is one of the few times in which the Empyreal Court will parlay with the Infernal Court to work on. Chosen by lottery, request, or as a test of character, a single member of the Alabaster Throne is forced to endure an hour and seven days’ worth of temptation from one-to-seven Infernal Courtiers. Naturally, an Oath is sworn to protect all involved from reprisal or cheating. A prize is offered to the court that wins, though often the Infernal Courtier who succeeds in tempting the Penitent gets the spoils unless otherwise noted. This often leads to infighting between the Fiends involved, a contest within a contest. In some freeholds, the Penitent is an ensorcelled mortal, chosen by the Pale Court, and the Empyreal Courtier must act as their counselor against the Fiends.




    Heraldry
    White, gold, and deep blues. Bladed armaments such as swords, shields, halberds, and morning stars are common, as are feathered creatures, including eagles, doves, and falcons.

    Mantle
    • The Courtier’s mien becomes transcendental, resonating with angelic imagery. Features might become metallic, or their eyes grow vivid like polished crystal. Even if otherwise, the courtier appears resolute in her word and deed. Those attempting to trick or mislead her suffer a -1 penalty to Subterfuge checks.
    •• Grant the courtier a dot in the Goblin Vow (Zeal).
    ••• The courtier becomes magnificent to behold, his strengths made visible no matter how terrible his mien. An Antiquarian holds an air of sagacious contemplation, while a stonebone ogre seems like an Olympian statue made from marble. He receives the Striking Looks merit. They gain Striking Looks (Advanced) if they already possess Striking Looks, or a +2 on Social checks if also already acquired.
    •••• Courtiers gain a Personal Approach related to how they fulfill the ideals and needs of the court. Huntsmen cannot gain Yearning from a Court Approach unless they fulfill the Personal Approach first.
    ••••• Dauntless is the courtier, a paragon of resolute zealotry, for good or ill. A Fireheart glows with a corona in the shape of wings, while a Hunter Heart dons a mantle of radiant gold. Once per day, she may add her Willpower score to any Physical roll as bonus dice.

    Zeal
    Virtue is not an emotion, but an act towards a higher calling, the personality trait that represents who someone is when she is being her best. Behaving in accordance with Virtue gives a character a sense of satisfaction and re-affirms her sense of self. Zeal is the emotion of the Empyreal Court. Not necessarily religious or violent, zeal is the enthusiasm found in pursuit of a cause or an objective. Courtiers often dedicate themselves to inspiring zeal towards their favored virtue, though as with any emotion, too much can lead to dangerous pitfalls.
    Infernal Court
    Aka Court of Fiends, the Umbral Flame, the Court of Vice





    The Infernal Court seeks to revel, believing themselves already damned, their souls tainted by their Keepers--but dammit if they won't give 'em the finger. They believe that by draining people of sinful glamour, the vibrancy of said-glamour gradually deadens, till the Others become less and less interested in then as potential slaves (though plenty in the Court succumb to monstrous temptations by corrupting the innocent for a quick fix).

    The Court has affinity for the Contracts of the Inferno.

    Courtiers
    In general, courtiers are expected to specialize in a particular vice, a process that begins to reflect itself within their individual miens as they rise within the court hierarchy. If their numbers are large enough, it becomes common for entire motleys to focus on a particular sin, with each member representing a different way of indulging said-vice. These “Houses of the Damned” naturally become competitive in acquiring resources, as well as recruiting new members into their ranks (or converting old ones).

    Rituals
    Walpurgis Night – A night of revelry lasting between April 30th to May 1st. During this time, courtiers make effigies of that which they fear most, and toss them into a ceremonial bonfire, after which they're expected to drink, fuck, and be marry. Not exclusively in that order.
    Daemon Cults – Utilized as both a resource and a past-time, Infernal Courtiers often form small cults of ensorcelled humans, portraying themselves as demons or avatars thereof. In addition to gaining followers and a steady source of glamour, the cult also allows the court to keep tabs on the surrounding neighborhoods thanks to indoctrinated informants, who hopefully become less likely to attract the Others by being constantly drained of sinful glamour. Traditionally, would-be patrons devise a infernal-sounding name to go by, adding to the mystique as well as difficulty of uncovering the ruse. Though in most circles, only the more trusted of cultists are ensorcelled, and told the truth.





    Heraldry
    The major colors of the Infernal Court are black, bronze (sometimes brass), and red. Stars such as the pentagram or the heptagram (representing the seven deadly sins) are common, as are the heads of horned beasts such as the bull, ram, or goat.

    Mantle
    • The courtier’s mien becomes corrupted, appearing infernal in its imagery. Colors become vivid, or darken. Decorative horns may appear beneath blackening eyes. The courtier gains a +1 to dicepools when trying to tempt the target towards vice.
    •• Grant the courtier a dot of the Goblin Vow (Vice of choice).
    ••• By this point, the courtier’s mien often betrays their personal vice. Gluttonous changelings may grow soft around the edges, or sharpened teeth, while lustful ones move with sensual, suggestive grace. Perhaps seeing them as a fellow sinner, those who make pledges with courtiers prove all too trusting. The courtier treats this as having Pledgesmith (3 dots). If they already own said-merit, they get a bonus +3.
    •••• Grants the courtier a Personal Approach, usually related his favorite type of sin. A Huntsman cannot gain Yearning from a Court Approach unless it fulfills the Personal Approach first.
    ••••• The courtier has become a true master of the pit. Shadows darken. Voices grow hushed. Theirs’ are words that thrum with authority and power. Choose either Persuasion or Intimidation. Reroll all failures on checks with said-skill. Once chosen, it cannot be changed, even should the courtier lose dots and then regain them later on.

    Vice
    Sometimes called "Indulgence" by members of the court, Vice is a character’s short-term comfort. It is the easy way out, the personality trait she takes comfort in to avoid confronting her actual feelings or problems. Like Virtue, acting on a Vice reinforces the character’s sense of herself, even if it is self-destructive. It still helps relieve stress and allows her to refresh herself, though not always healthy behavior in the long term. Infernal Courtiers aren’t incapable of goodwill or virtue, they just prefer the more indulgent route.
    Pale Court
    Aka Court of Purgatory, the Veiled Mountain, the Court of Longing





    Of all the Tellurian Courts, the Veiled Mountain is the closest to something an outsider would recognize. Pale Courtiers remain fae-like in appearance, neither fiendish nor angelic. They stand neutral in rivalries of the freehold, a sanctuary against the eternal war surrounding it on all sides. Where the Empyreal would demand action against the Others, and the Infernal rollicking in jubilation, the Pale Court wishes stability and caution.

    Courtiers
    These are the desperate, the hesitant, the changelings who care little for religious trappings or extremes therein. Some are atheists, or feel more in touch with their humanity by avoiding such questions of gods, faith, and the afterlife. Often they become the voice of reason within freehold disputes, till they become pragmatic, or as mercurial as the fairytale creatures they now exemplify. They are living ghosts, aching over a life few will ever get back.

    Rituals
    X





    Heraldry
    Colors such as dark grey, silver, and pale yellow epitomize the Pale Court. Mountains are common, as are symbols relating to barriers such as gates, walls, and even natural border marks like rivers or milestones. In some freeholds, symbols of death are just as common, to represent how all changelings are equal in the end, regardless of durance, kith, or court.

    Mantle
    • The courtier’s mien becomes demure or faint of color, leaving a haunting look in their eyes while making them harder to notice. Gain a +1 to stealth checks.
    •• Grant the courtier a dot of the Goblin Vow (Desperation).
    ••• Gain the Charmed Life Merit. If already owned, you may choose between the dice rolls made by your attacker when determining damage, rather than defaulting to the second roll.
    •••• Courtiers gain a Personal Approach related to how they fulfill the ideals and story of the court. Huntsmen cannot gain Yearning from a Court Approach unless they fulfill the Personal Approach first.

      New Goblin Fruit

      Cruel Ambrosia: recognized as a black durian with rose-tinted barbs, the stench of this fruit belays the treasure within. If cut open, one will find something akin to pomegranate seeds, the juice of which must be boiled with the barbs before serving or bottling for later. The flavor is always different depending on its consumer, tasting of something that intoxicates the senses, and pleasures the soul. Upon drinking, the milk not only acts as a potent aphrodisiac, but causes the character to exude a sweet, fragrant milk from one of her orifices: usually the mouth, though other more… unusual exits are possible. This milk is a drug that, when consumed, simulates the effects of a potent hallucinogen, and is highly addictive.

      New Oddments

      Hellglass: looks much like obsidian, a gleaming treacle-black rock with a mirror-like surface when polished. It is semitransparent, like smoked glass but has a far greater tensile strength. Deposits of hellglass are a hazard of the Infernum (read: Hedge), as heat or the impacts of meteors can smash them into sharp edges and pointed slivers. A desert region where a layer of sand or dust overlays hellglass shards is treated with a -3 penalty to walk across. Exposed hellglass can be avoided if characters move at half speed. A person without protection that touches jagged hellglass suffers 4d10 worth of bashing damage on contact. An inch of Hellglass has Durability of 5, and Structure of 10.

      New Tokens

      Belt of the Horrid Maw (•••)
      By donning this token, the changeling grows a slavering maw that manifests over one location of their body. It can be anywhere, but is fixed once designated. The naval, genitals, and neck are all possible, maximizing the horror of gnashing teeth and an animated tongue.
      The mouth acts as a +3 weapon doing lethal damage in brawl attacks. Characters with this mutation may bite without grappling the target, but gain a 9-again effect for damage if done so.

      Action: Standard

      Mien: The token appears as a raggedy old belt that widens around the front. When worn, it stretches to match the wearer’s size, all while snarling as gnashing teeth bite into the wearer’s flesh. While harmless, this is nevertheless uncomfortable.

      Drawback: Unfortunately, the mouth appears to have a mind of its own (sometimes even talking), and requires feeding equal to half the average amount the character normally eats in a day, lest it begin eating things at random, or worse, itself. Furthermore, while active, the belt melds into the user’s flesh, requiring him to spend 1 Willpower point to remove.

      Catch: The wearer must suffer 1 Lethal damage as the belt violently burrows into position.


      Coveter's Mirror (••••)
      This token is a hand-held, two-way mirror with tarnished silver edges, about five inches in diameter. When activated, it allows a person to assume the appearance and mannerisms of someone the wielder admires and wants to emulate. In order to use the mirror, the wielder reflects the target’s face and whispers, "That’s me." The wielder then takes on the face, mien, build and mannerisms of the target, and keeps them for one hour. At the end of that time, the effect ends, though it can be immediately reactivated. Note, too, that the mirror doesn't affect the target in any way, meaning the wielder should probably incapacitate him before assuming his identity (depending upon what he wants to accomplish).
      While the mirror’s power is in effect, the player might need to make Wits + Subterfuge rolls to behave as the target, but the mirror provides a +3 to such rolls. It does not, however, give the wielder any specific knowledge or acumen in Skills, meaning that if the police captain assumes the identity of the deadeye-shot officer, he’s out of luck if called upon to demonstrate his shooting skill.

      Action: Standard

      Mien: When activated, the mirror glows vibrant silver, its glass tinted an acid green.

      Drawback: The mirror does not function on people of lower station than the wielder unless that target is noticeably "better" in some way. So, a police captain couldn't use it on a uniformed officer, unless the officer was, for instance, better looking (Striking Looks Merit), in better shape (higher Strength and/or Athletics) or a better shot (higher Firearms rating as demonstrated at the range).

      Catch: Each time the token is used without paying glamour, the changeling begins to show greater signs of an inferiority complex when not impersonating someone, becoming antisocial, even hostile, to the point of needing the mirror just to socialize with others.


      Joyous Rings (•••)
      An ironic name, Joyous Rings actually come in a number of forms, from bolts to barbells to spirals and any other form of piercing you can imagine. Often the rings change shape to better accentuate the wearer’s mien, perhaps becoming a type of hedge-ivory for beasts, or growing larger in size and width for ogres. There is no limit to how many of these tokens a changeling can wear, though the drawback ensures fatal injury for those unprepared for pain.

      With each ring, the wearer benefits from +1 Armor, absorbing the damage even if nowhere near the site of impact. If the changeling would suffer Aggravated damage, she may choose to have the ring destroyed by reflexively spending 1 Willpower point.

      The final benefit of these rings is that they compel obedience. When a ring-wearer has visibly fewer of these rings than another creature, they suffer a –2 penalty to checks against resisting commands made unless suicidal or drastically out of character. This has no effect on those immune to social wiles, such as automatons or mundane animals. Mortals are sometimes persuaded to accept a ring as a “reward”, only to find that it makes it far harder for them to escape from abject submission to their “dominants”. Of course, if they are serving willingly, then they most likely welcome this domination...

      Action: Standard

      Mien: These odd metal barbs and piercings seem to shift out of the corner of your eye, as through writhing in salacious ecstasy.

      Drawback: While worn, each ring ensures the changeling suffers 1 point of Lethal that cannot be healed until the piercing is removed (though there's no risk of bleeding out). Then it heals naturally.

      Catch: The piercings must be placed on areas of flesh sensitive to the touch without any form of anesthetic.

              New Token: Spawning Pit (• to •••••; special)

              Prerequisites: Hollow with Size 2 or more.
              A pseudo-token of sorts, the spawning pit allows whosoever to create semi-subservient hobgoblins. Generally loyal to their creator, each cacodemon is capable of making pledges with anyone else, provided they are not sworn to do otherwise. Depending on what ingredients are used to create them, these creatures can range the gamut of characteristics. All however, are humanoid, often reflecting features of their creator, her court, or a mixture of whatever poor bastards were thrown into the muck.

              Cost: 1 WP + 5 Glamour per cacodemon

              Catch: In addition to ingredients used, 5 pints worth of fresh blood per would-be minion.

              Drawback: The pit requires appropriate materials to be sacrificed to it in order to form the desired hellspawn. Said-creatures must also be fed, housed, and even clothed to ensure survival.

              Mechanics: Dots in this merit are divided between Quantity, and Quality. Quantity determines how many hobs can be birthed at a time. Quality determines how many free “points” you can spend on designing the cacodemon. This merit can go beyond the normal limit of 5, and can be shared with among characters.

              • Points spent contribute to the Cacodemon’s overall Attributes and Skills (at a rate of two points per one Attribute dot and one point per one Skill dot).
              • Every Cacodemon begins with 1 dot in each Attribute, free of charge. They also start at Wyrd 1, and gain 1 Turn (Contract). They can only ever have 1 Turn. Their Wyrd maxes at 3 (3 points per +1 Wyrd).
              • All Cacodemons begin at Size 1 to 3, and require a point to increase Size level by 1.
              • Each must be fed 1 Glamour x its Size per week.
              • Cacodemons be given physical or mental Merits at a rate of 1 point per dot.
              • The process takes seven days to fully mature a cacodemon.
