    Raising Renown

    04-21-2015, 12:22 AM
    So I started a werewolf chronicle recently. I've run a lot of various splats before so I thought I was prepared for anything, lol. However, renown seems to be unique to the uratha. It is both a power source (makes you better the higher it is) and "fame" at the same time. And that's cool, I like it.

    My problem is that I have no idea how to deal with it. Two of my players have enough xp to buy up their renown (they're both going for Glory). And at first they were really excited, then they realized that they have to do things that would be "glorious." So far nothing they've faced or done has really been super impressive (or maybe our outlook on what is impressive is too much?). So they decided they wanted to seek out something "glorious" to hunt. But then they realized that would completely sidetrack the chronicle's direction (as well as focus mostly on them). So there's a lot of mixed feelings. They want to spend their xp, not wait for a "glorious fight," but they don't want to steal the game from the other players.

    I had imagined renown as being a sort of "story reward". I.E. The storyteller could dole out situations to possibly increase various renown and then the players made use of those situations. But I can see their point, they earned the xp, and they want to spend it on raising their primary "supernatural attribute". Or it might just be a failing on my part as I'm new to werewolf and not used to this yet. But I would love some advice on dealing with renown. Are we being too "strict" in our readings of the types of things to gain it? Should my players be more patient because renown is not a thing that should increase so often?

    One of my players offered the suggestion of perhaps just making renown not be an xp thing at all, but instead doled out solely by the storyteller.
    How do you all deal with renown?

    Thanks!

              I mentioned this in the other thread, but in order to avoid this issue, I first find out what my players are interested in buying next, and then plan for that in the chronicle. So, two players tell me they both want to buy a dot of Glory. Cool. I know that Renown dots cost 3 experiences, and I know my players earn an average of 4 Beats per session. At that rate, they'll have enough to buy that dot of Glory in about 4 sessions. So, I plan an opportunity for them to do something Glorious, and prepare a 4 session questline culminating in that event. If they get up to 3 xp a little early, that's fine, they have a bit of a head start on what ever they want to pursue next. If they're still a few Beats short by the time they do the Glorious thing, that's fine too, I'll just note that they're a few Beats in debt.

              In general, my games are very player-directed. At the most basic level, they set the goals, and I put obstacles in the way of those goals. If possible, I try to do it in a way that poses dramatic questions.

                  I guess I expected more of a vague "this is how I do it" rather than responding exactly to what we have done so far. I'm not sure why, honestly.

                  So our chronicle has been fairly short so far. And due to time constraints, what was supposed to be a single nights adventure became three nights/weeks of adventure.

                  The pack has 6 uratha, one in each auspice, but two of the crescent moons (I haven't memorized all the names yet.)
                  The chronicle began by paying/dealing with a spirit that lives on their territory. This has been set up as a prior arrangement, every month on the new moon, they gift this spirit with three essence and it crafts them a talen. Sort of a trade of mutual respect. This time, the spirit told them of some new misery spirits that had entered the area, and it didnt like them. The pack agreed to check it out.
                  -In the area they found 8 misery spirits. 7 rank 1, and a single rank 2. The pack immediately attacked them, to clear them out. It was not a hard battle, there was little damage to the uratha and only a single one even took gauru form at all.
                  -Once the spirits were dealt with, the pack investigated. They found some clues and with a combination of allies and contacts, plus talking to local spirits, they concluded that a man (he was claimed, they just didnt know that yet) used some water magics (Influence) to back up sewage in the area (he was testing his new powers). A little more contact use and they knew where the man was staying.
                  -They performed the sacred hunt rite, declaring the "man" as their target, with the goal of ending his "spreading of misery".
                  -They went to the hotel he was staying, they distracted the front desk clerk and made a keycard to his room.
                  -Inside his room they investigated some more. With various clues (newspaper clippings, pamphlets, etc) they concluded he was very interested in a nearby inlet, and that was likely his next target (they are in a coastal city).
                  -They split up, two uratha started tracking him, from the hotel (in case they were wrong) and the other four went straight to the inlet. He was there, so they got the other two to conclude the hunt.
                  -There was a battle, one claimed (rank 2 spirit inside the guy) an four rank 1 water spirits. He had the dread powers of armor 1 and claws 2 (the damaging one, I don't recall its name) as well as his influence. It was tougher than the misery spirits, due to his higher defense, damage output, and water making it more difficult to get a solid hit in. But overall it was still fairly easy. Only two players even took gauru. And at the end I believe the most damage anyone had was 3 lethal. (Which would be different if we were playing Hunter or Changeling, but this is Werewolf, that's just a scratch) They concluded the hunt.

                  So overall their trek hasn't been that difficult or hard. So no one believes they've "earned" glory and they want to go find something "glorious" to fight.

                  I understand what you're saying and our group doesn't work that way. I've tried in the past, but we have a mix of very "self-driven" players and "I just want to follow what's happening" players. The self driven players always have goals, ideas, things they want to do. But then they also live in fear of stealing the spotlight from everyone constantly. The other players don't know what they want to do, but they know it isn't just follow the self driven characters around on personal goals.
                  We've found a healthy way to deal with it, and that is to have a chronicle "direction" or "stated goal." An underlying theme, if you will. And I, as storyteller, craft situations or stories that line up with that. So if our theme was "protect your territory from outside incursions" the players want those outside incursions. So they're afraid if they decide to "chase down something glorious to fight" then it would distract from the "protecting territory" theme and make the "follow what's happening" players sad.

                  Also, my players are really, really, really, bad at deciding ahead of time what they want to spend xp on. So in your example, those two players would tell me they want to buy a dot of Glory and they have the xp right now. The only "saving" xp they do is because they can't decide between two merits that both cost 1xp, then suddenly they have 2xp, so lets go buy a dot of brawl.

                      No, it's actually my group that's slow at gaining Beats. It's not their fault, I use a homebrewed hybrid system that includes both personal and group Beats, which slows down individual progress a bit, but keeps the group advancing at a similar pace. Specifically, I add a group Beat pool. Instead of everyone gaining 1 Beat for showing up, I put 1 Beat in the group pool each session. I also have the group come up with a single Group Aspiration (I started doing this in my vampire game, but for Werewolf I'd replace this with the Totem Aspiration), and whenever they complete it I put 1 Beat in the group pool. When the group pool hits 5, everyone gets 1 Experience. Character Aspirations, Conditions, and Dramatic Failures still all award individual Beats, and my players usually get around 3 or 4 of these per session.

                          I haven't ran Werewolf under 2nd edition yet, but, in first edition, the cost of Reknown was generally so high that by the time PC's had the xp to buy the reknown, they'd accomplished something that they could hold up and claim as the justification for that reknown purchase.

                                  I'd suggest a couple things:

                                  1) Lower the bar for what's glorious enough. Any Werewolf interested in getting 1 dot in a Renown should be able to do it with only a smidgen of effort. The accomplishment isn't really huge; it's like a teenager getting their driver's license. It's a milestone from the perspective of "you're the protagonist and growing up", but to society at large, it's mostly worth a pat on the back. Renown 1 is something cool to your family. Renown 2 is something cool to your neighborhood. All the neighbors say, "Oh, and that's Sam, who's attending John Hopkins." That kind of thing.

                                  2) Diversify what you consider glorious. It doesn't have to be a fight; it just has to be a story worth telling. Pull ahead of your packmaates while chasing something down and do something crazy to make yourself stand out. Pull an MIT prank. Resolve a confrontation by singing your heart out and getting through that way. Make someone feel special. The point of Glory is being the hero of the story, but heroism takes many, many, many forms.

                                  3) I swear I had a three but I forgot it.

                                  I'd honestly say that any exceptional success qualifies as Renown 1 if you can explain how it fits. Get the packmates in on it: have them come up with ways to talk up the accomplishment, to explain why it's was a big deal. Part of the challenge of Renown is that you have to justify it. You can either justify it by going out and finding something, or you can justify it by looking back and recognizing the significance of what you did.

                                  I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".

                                      The thing about Renown is that it isn't fame, it's a meritocratic phenomena of the People. A werewolf has to have actually done these things to justify the expenditure and to have the actual Experience to spend. They can't just lie or stage a publicity campaign and get a free Gift in the process. In purely meta terms, any PC contemplating a dot in a Renown has already earned it, they just haven't justified it.

                                      The cool thing is that your players are taking the questions of the Renown system and driving the story themselves. That's great, because they're telling you what they want, and they are prompting discussion of what qualifies as Glory, and failing their own standards. To me, that says to trust their judgment on Renown; they aren't gaming the system, and they're looking for most satisfactory means for themselves to meet the system's guidelines.

                                      I'd also say not to worry about the narrative getting too far off course. The Wolf Must Hunt, so finding Glorious prey is a matter of planning, not distraction. Likewise, if you're worried that protecting the territory is going to get lost in the narrative, then something moves in on the territory when they get lax. A Wolf-blooded gets torn to bits or addicted to a strange red drink that comes in vials.

                                                  Haven't read 2nd ed yet, so Beats are beyond me.

                                                  1st ed had a few examples of feats for each specific Renown. If 2nd ed has the same things, you can offer them as examples for the players.

                                                  I've found that instead of telling the players what they need to do to earn Renown, it's best I just drive on with the story, and at the end of the session(or at home) I'll go through the feats of Renown, see if the players have performed any and note down what Renown they're eligible to buy when they have enough XP.

                                                  If you're doing this and the players know what to aim for, your players will soon have no trouble getting some Glory.

                                                  Also, as someone above suggested, lower the expectations for renown, even down to a few hard rolls if possible (after they've role-played intensively of course).

                                                  As for increasingly harder feats required for higher renown, why bother with that headache? You've got 6 players already, that should be headache enough. Just rule that feats of Renown are cumulative, with many small stories of glory adding ONE BIG GLORY RENOWN. As a possible variation of this, a particularly notable glorious feat nets the player/players eligibility for two dots of the Glory Renown.

                                                  If you've implemented any of this and are worried about an explosion of renown, just make it more expensive to buy XP-wise.

                                                  One man's opinion, folks. Don't bite my head off. Skull is hard to chew anyway.

                                                      Originally posted by Tiarn View Post
                                                      I can't respond on a lot right now, busy heading to work, but I noticed that I need to clarify one thing, this isn't the first dot of renown these characters are seeking.
                                                      For one character, it's the second dot of Glory. For the other, the third.
                                                      I think it would be really helpful to hear what the characters did for their first (and second) dots of Glory. Taking down a Claimed surrounded by a cadre of attending spirits might not have been too hard for your group, but its still a reasonably impressive feat. The end damage toll to your pack may have taken less than an hour to clear out, but they might well have saved the town (and thus, their territory).
                                                      The main issue in increasing Renown is that your significant action be a bigger deal than the one before it. Is the defeat of a Claimed and the saving of their city a bigger deal than what they did? Then the Renown could well be theirs. If not, then have something bigger and scarier stalk into town next time. It sounds like the chalenges you've thrown at them so far have been somewhat minor, so ramping things up a bit might not be a bad plan in general.

                                                      Edit: It might not be a bad idea to, on top of having your players tell you what they did to earn the dots they already have, ask your players for their thoughts on what actions might be worth the new dots. My character, for example, got his first Wisdom for supplicating a Lune. His second was for helping create a Fettish. His third was for discovering (if not recording and keeping) a lost Rite. I think leading the creation of a 4-dot Fettish would be worth the fourth (or maybe a 3-dot one depending on the circumstances) and supplicating a major spirit (like Death Wolf or Luna herself) for something would be worth the fifth.

                                                      You mentioned that they don't feel like they've done something Glorious enough. Find out what they would see as sufficient.
