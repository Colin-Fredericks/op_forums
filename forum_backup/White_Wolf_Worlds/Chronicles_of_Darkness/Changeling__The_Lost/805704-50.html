        Okay,stop. Need to get back on proper track.

        There's a balance, here, between two things being chatted about. On the one hand, Changelings are inherently paranoid, distrustful, and opportunistic....and on the other hand, a freehold needs to operate on bringing people together, building trust, and giving people a safe space to become free in. And somewhere between these two, we have "Make it a law not to accept a token of reprieve from the Huntsmen", essentially, and as an extension of that, "Don't help the Gentry."

        Truth be told, everything exists in the middle. On the negative hand, changelings, even high up in the freehold, don't want to stop the sweet, sweet revenue of certainty that comes from a Token of Reprieve and therefore keep open the possibility of betrayal just so the income...comes in. On the positive hand, changelings, even high up in the freehold, want people to not be alienated, thrown under a bus, or feel like they're being scrutinized too much.

        Truth of the matter is that all these reasons and more apply. Yes, your rulers want people to be safe, but if people aren't going to be safe, they might as well try and get some benefit out of it. Yes, your rulers want people to stick together, but they also don't want bad guys to come on in. We're asking questions people have been asking since the dawn of time. Which is why we have a question of "Would you complain to the police if they stopped an assassin from killing you?", which ties into larger questions of justice, mercy, security, safety, etc.

        Of course we have very divergent ideas of how to handle the problems of outsiders and criminal behaviors and lots and lots of questions about psychology. And in particular, it asks the questions any trauma victim asks. It's a tough ass question, and we're not going to get far with it.

        I feel like Changeling the Lost does take a definitive stance by the way it frames and discusses True Fae, particularly in light of the Becoming Nightmare mechanic(Which, admittedly, is optional and therefore not as significant a matter of discussion in this as I'm suggesting it does), and I think that's something that can go double when you look at what trauma victims have to get over and how they work to get over it. I think it's worth talking about.

        But I'll be honest. I think we've all gotten really super deep into this discussion and we're tangled up and heated and such. And I think, being a little more objective about it, it's not a subject we can really be right on.

        So, I'ma gonna propose a cease fire for now. Let's all leave the thread alone for, like, a day or two, come back to it later. Because we have crossed into heavy shit, and I think it'll help to move away for a bit.

                    Originally posted by ReshyShira View Post
                    So, accept everyone without a filter, even those with radically different concepts of morality? Seems like a recipe for disaster. If they can't agree to even basic principles or tenets of the freehold, then why are they there? If your reason is that a freehold isn't free if it expects the Changelings that are apart of it to agree to a code of conduct, then I would say that it's not really any form of governance or authority because there is no baseline.
                    You scare me, Reshy. It's not the core principle of mutual protection that's being questioned, it's that you're trying to use the methodology of the Gentry to accomplish it and don't realize how Changelings who've spent a chunk of their life being continually screwed by the Wyrd will react. The Wyrd is a soulless computer with the occasional viciousness of a jerk genie. The Wyrd has no sense of temperance, and will punish innocent mistakes and technicalities with the same severity as outright, intentional betrayal. The way you talk about the power of this collection of Wyrd-enforced laws and just seem to assume that it's common sense to put everyone on trial if the Wyrd marks them as an Oathbreaker and that the innocent have nothing to fear is what scares me. It's dangerously naive about the difference between theory and practice in law. It's dangerously naive about the psychology of witch hunts, scapegoating, and the seduction of easy answers. It's dangerously naive about how supposedly impartial and beneficial systems can be manipulated by those with radical politics and criminal intent.

                    There are probably parts of Arcadia that would look nice, safe, and even heavenly in theory, but they still wind up being horrible in practice because the rules don't care about the intentions of someone who trips over a technicality. The same is true of human police states, and tyrants often use that pretty theory as their selling point to the naive. Human laws in free and just societies have purpose and can be bent if extenuating circumstances subvert those purposes. Errors can be forgiven and violators can be given warnings and explanations of their mistakes. Knowing that is why we don't all have to become lawyers. The Wyrd does not care about any of that. The Wyrd is more easily the ally of scoundrels and con artists than it is for compassionate, merciful people. The Gentry's weakness is that they don't understand the human social mindset. The more standardization and transparency you have of the Wyrd, the easier it is for the Gentry to adapt because the Wyrd is how they understand what they can get away with. Relying on a unified, Wyrd-enforced law is like trying to protect a computer network by posting the source code to its operating system on the company website, where the hackers and crackers can read it.

                    Having human individuality and the freedom to keep secrets and prioritize friends over strangers means the Gentry will probe and fail more often. If a Motley mate breaks his vow, his close friends are going to hear him out and judge whether or not he had a good reason for the result because they are more familiar with his circumstances. False flags aren't automatically escalated and are thus less likely to reinforce the fear that enemy intends to manipulate. The freehold court isn't going to know everything about his circumstances, and with an enemy as dangerous and hated as the Gentry, far too many Changelings are going to assume the worst about a relative stranger by default and look for any excuse to confirm their biases. Having changelings of many different backgrounds, moral and ethical theories create a heterogeneous network of motleys and overlapping social circles means that they can distinguish signal from noise using human intuition better than a brittle, overly transparent society that jumps at every ping on the radar. The more you rely on any one detection system, the easier it is for the enemy to deceive and thus manipulate you.

                    Using the Wyrd to foster unity makes you look like a pseudo-benevolent Faerie trying to form a human society despite not understanding that it's not the laws themselves that foster trust among the community. There are unwritten social rules that are brought up before we subject someone to the harsh eye of the law that the Wyrd skips entirely. Being too quick to assume that anyone who breaks those laws is doing so maliciously makes you look like one of those angelic members of the Gentry who go on about how they love everyone and then show no mercy anyone who's the tiniest bit disobedient. The problem with so many utopias is that they're built with angels or Faeries in mind, not humans or changelings.
