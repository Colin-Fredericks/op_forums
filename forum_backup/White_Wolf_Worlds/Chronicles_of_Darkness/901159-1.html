        I see a situation like this -

        Three friends spend the weekend trapped in a Haunted House of Terror, and are subjected to all sorts of supernatural phenomena over the course of 2 days and barely escape with their lives.

        Character A remembers everything, and goes rushing to Cops and the Media. Her story is outlandish by conventional wisdom, and will likely be dismissed by most credible authorities. Even the other people that were there can't (or won't) back up her story. There is even a good chance that a supernatural conspirator in the Police or Media will actively smear her credibility to prevent her stories from ever being taken seriously. She knows what she saw and can't let it go. If she keeps pursuing the mysteries of what she experienced, she will wind up dead, broken, or become part of the darkness herself. Her response is the rarest - while she makes a good protagonist in a game or story, she is an unusual outlier in most mortal populations.

        Character B also remembers everything, but if asked will lie about it and blame perfectly mundane reasons for his injuries. He believes that there is nothing to be gained and so much to lose by meddling with the supernatural, especially since he was lucky to survive his last encounter. He will likely spend the rest of his days noticing the hand prints of the supernatural in ways he never noticed before, but will do nothing. He might carry that burden of knowledge but continue to live pretty normal live. Or the secrets and paranoia will slowly eat away at him. His response is considered a common reaction. Even if you do not account for the major templates and documented creatures, the CofD is still crawling with strangeness that doesn't fit into a neat box. If you are a regular, vulnerable mortal, the only safe, sane, and probably wise response is to ignore it, go on with your life, and Never.Speak.Of.It.Again.

        Character C will remember very little, and rationalize the rest. There are powerful forces at work in the world that conceal the supernatural from mortal eyes. Even if their weren't , a typical humans mind trembles when confronted by supernatural events. Their memories will be hazy or entirely missing, they will alter details and rationalize events. Anything to make their experience easier to deal with on a mental and emotional level. This effect is so basic and widespread that even individuals with the personality and capability to expose the supernatural world might not remember their experience enough to act on it. And this is like the response for the bulk of humanity.

            Originally posted by Mr.F.I.X. View Post
            Most supernatural beings, the major templates at least, remain hidden from the world to such a degree that they often are hidden from each other. It is not a certainty that all templates are aware of each other by default. There may be many beings in a city but they are all hiden from each other.
            This is no longer true in 2e. Vampires can instantly tell someone is a werewolf, mage, changeling, and I believe Beast on sight*. Werewolf wolf senses can detect other supernaturals and sniff them out. Mages have their Sights. Changelings and their Kenning seem to pick up on magical beings much like Mage Sight does. Beasts can auto-detect supernaturals as part of their template.

            This pretty much leaves things in Twilight or Demons as the only possible "hidden from each other" supernatural types by default. If you want to hide from the at-large supernatural community, you need to invest in Obfuscate, strengthen your Mask, and use other hiding powers. The default is knowing each other rather than not.

            That's why STs need to decide what supernaturals exist in a given game rather than assume they're all in. Or the distribution. Even if you include mages in your vampire game, if their isn't a major Mystery in the city, chances are there's no local mage society. Werewolves tend to be spread out and claim large tracks of land as territory, keeping all other wolves (and Hosts) out. There's only a handful of Mummies in the entire world, and they spend most of their time asleep - honestly, I'm still hardpressed to figure out how they can have a society or guilds or anything.

            My big issue with the CofD crossover, however? Exarchs versus God Machine versus Judges, etc. Just how many different god-level supernaturals claim to control the world and its fate? Why aren't they at war with each other, considering they're not working together? There are just too many world spanning powers that control everything to the point it doesn't all fit. I don't think we can have them all working in the same setting, even with the whole "shrouded in mystery" bits. Something has to give.


            * Vampires' Predator Aura ability detect anyone with auras automatically, so that means they can instantly tell werewolves and mages. The Changeling playtest material shows that anyone with supernatural senses instantly sees past the Mask. And I believe that any supernatural can see the Horror super-imposed over the Beast.
