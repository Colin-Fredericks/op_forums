    164) Mages have started using mythological & famous historical names as shadow names, with far greater frequency.
    Examples include: Joan d'Arc, Odin, Tyr, Fenrir, Achilles, Arthur Pendragon, Lancelot, Napoleon Bonaparte, & many others.
    But there are consequences to using names like these, which are starting to pile up.
    Lancelot has entered into a relationship with their best friend's spouse, Arthur Pendragon. Fenrir has developed an insatiable hunger. Tyr has lost a hand in the process of trapping Fenrir. Napoleon Bonaparte has started a revolution.
    What's up with this new trend? And how can it be stopped before something really bad happens?

                    168) The tectonic plaques are rapidly shifting and with them countries drift and separate, akin to ships. The booms of Hallows being created and destroyed are throwing the supernatural into disarray. Amid this chaos, Sleeper minds grapple to separate "real knowledge" from the "fakes that seem to crowd all references".                     169) (Inspired by Don't Rest Your Head) Sometimes mortals go on too long without sleep and then they see a gateway to a city of nightmares. The poor fools that step through enter its Astral realm, where they gain Numina, Influences and apply dream logic to stats. Greater power can be gained by damaging one's sanity. Those caught asleep by the nightmares risk becoming them. Now that they crossed the threshold, the nightmares can also cross back to capture them. Knowing this, how do you plan to rescue them ?

                            172) A Free Council Arcanthus discovers a Grimoire which in horrific explicit detail tells a cautionary tale about a Silver Ladder Arcanthus arrogant enough to attempt to summon and bind a particularly nasty Gentry of the Fair Folk known for their cannibalistic appetites, and how he received a boon along with a letter describing what what he going to ask for without even having to bother casting the spell but then all the terrible things which ended up happening to the mage including having to feed the creature both of his legs, an eye and his right arm in magically resistant wounds or lose even more people he cared about. There is a dire warning against attempting to use Fate to summon and Bind one of the Gentry of the Fair Folk. After the warning is the spell and True Name of that True Fae. Of course the Free Council Arcanthus scoffs, because she believes she knows where the Silver Ladder went astray. Obviously he shouldn't have been trying to use the True Fae for corrupt presumably self serving ends, shouldn't have chosen such a cruel and wicked monster, and should have completed the summoning and binding instead of just accepting the preemptive Trojan-horse gift. Satisfied at her own cleverness and feeling quite superior to the previous owner of the Grimoire she research a wee spritely Gentry with a reputation for loving fun and freedom as it gallivants on gossamer wings which would surely want to help her in the goal of stopping the Seers of the Throne from bulldozing a Homeless encampment and city green space so they could build yet another skyscaper to overlay aerial Hallow with a greedy corporate board room to monopolize the mana and turn even more of the city's resonance to oppressive greed. Secure that she's chosen the right True Fae and that with noble intentions surely such a freedom loving being would work with her willingly.

                            She is ready to cast the spell, going to go through with it no matter what... and right before she begins a magical note from the future appears... just like one did for that arrogant foolish Silver ladder... it explains how she now has allies at the city council whom will listen to her in order to prevent the seers of the throne from inflicting more tyranny or building a tower. Then she reads the last line and her blood turns cold. Just like the grimoire describes the ending the note the Silver Ladder had the same ominous "careful what you wish for...". Taking a break an realizing she still has all the prep to cast the spell she relaxes and focuses. She'll just summon and bind the Fae to see what is going on, that's one of the places the Silver Ladder messed up and she wasn't at all like him, she thinks to herself. But The summoning does NOTHING, she feels Fate completely ignoring her pushes and pulls. She begins really looking at note with her Mage sight and what she sees deeply bothers her as the Note is a Mystery and even the surface isn't good news. Before Delving any deeper she phones a group of friends to come over and help her sort this all out and investigate the mysterious note from the future and the curse its warning conveys.

                            And the PCs are whom she calls. I mean odds are the PC know an idealistic Free Council Arcanthus whom is an activist for the down trodden and against corporate Seers of the Throne, if not then introducing someone like that who is affable in one story and then starting this story after a couple other stories is a good idea.

                                The neighborhood kids have taken to talking about some crazy stuff. It takes a while, but eventually, mages catch wind and hear about the ritual that supposedly grants wishes. There is a list of rules to it all, from when to where, to how and, most importantly what can be wished for.                                         175.Ki-En-Gir
                                        A great victory over a Scelestus archmage has occurred, truly a victory for Supernal Truth! But now comes the TRULY difficult part: sifting through the lore and magical items assembled over nearly a century of Awakened life by an Aswadim. Much of this lore is tainted, but enough of it is salvageable that it bears careful cautious investigation.

                                        The story of the origins of Sumeria are supposedly revealed, of s land the Scelesti called Ki-En-Gir, a kingdom of blasphemies where worship of the Abyss stood supreme and antinomian sorcery was first learnt.

                                        Apparently, some other culture---a rival empire of some sort---laid Ki-En-Gir low, and, from the ashes arose Sumeria.

                                        The dead Aswadim had the belief it was the Exarchs who demolished Ki-En-Gir, as he could find no information whatsoever of Ki-En-Gir's destroyer, other than that they were led by priests who were also gods.

                                        Several locations were identified by the Aswadim as being temples containing Abyssal lore. Most of these places have been plundered, but one has not.

                                        The players get sent it to explore this ruin and brave its dangers. What horrors await them in the Middle East? What buried torments of Ki-En-Gir will they encounter? And what of this mysterious rival empire that seems to have wiped from the memory of Sleeper historians? Was this other empire from the Time Before, or were they slaves of the Exarchs?

                                            176.The Well
                                            A mage buys an old, dilapidated mansion with the intent of turning it into a Sanctum loaded with magical features...and, a week into the mundane side of the remodeling, he finds a well in the basement that seems to be infinitely deep.
                                            At first fearing it an Avernian Gate or a Schism, the well leads...nowhere. It just keeps going down, into the dark, without end.
                                            A week later, the mage, using Sleeper climbing gear, a winch and his mastery of Matter magic, plunges into the well's deeps. He then...vanishes.
                                            Unfortunately, the Hierarch has become Obsessed with this Mystery and swiftly followed suit. Now the players must delve down in to rescue the Hierarch. As they plunge ever further downwards, they realize they're somewhere strange quickly after hitting the 50-mile-deep mark. The well shaft grows cold, and faces seem to appear in the rime. They're still impossibly in the material Realm, however.
                                            Do they keep going deeper, hoping to find their missing Hierarch? Is there even an end to the well shaft? If so, what's down there? Who dug the well, in the first place?

                                                Winter in the hills of Keenalee see a strange visitor: Winter's Tear. The creature steals an animal from each field until it finds a building with a boiled custard sitting under a holly branch. After finding that, it sets a blessing on the building and its owner. (it has some odd rules about how it interprets that.) In either case, it leaves a centered fox print in muddy snow. It leaves no tracks, otherwise.

                                                It only comes out at sunset after the first snow and the first rain that follows it before the equinox; it doesn't show up every year. Apparently, there are other possible conditions, which is why the region tends to put out the "winter custard" and visit each other as part of their winter festivities. It's safe to drink all the custard after twilight ends. The path it follows is extremely unpredictable.

                                                The locals are actually not generally aware of the legends anymore. The incidents of strange animal disappearances has increased as locals stopped being as fond of the festival, but the number of people still practicing is high enough that the incidence is pretty low.

                                                The Conciluim governing that mystery only has a few elderly mages.

                                                One of these became extremely agitated and called out to their outside allies to help find his friend. He believes that Winter's Tear took him in an unprecedented attack on a human. The signs are there.

                                                Perhaps he got too close? Where could it have ever gone?

                                                        In 1913, following the publication of Wegener's continental drift theory, mages joined into that debate. As expected, many free Council members were in the thick of it challenging or supporting the research. For years, it was assumed that the errors of the early mage researchers were largely errors of methodology or records that paralleled the Sleepers' own..

                                                        Recently, in one of the Assembly Archives, it was discovered that one of the more obsessed FC of that era had gone farther than most knew. His cabal went so far as to create a legacy devoted to measuring and mapping large scale geological features. Their methods were supernaturally precise and they verified with solid mundane methods. (albeit much less accurate) Their maps were extremely precise and performing two independent surveys was their standard approach. When something was particularly important, they used five.

                                                        Something they found lead to them quietly walking away from the Free Assemblies, research nearly complete, in 1961. The controversy surrounding their research fades quickly. The Councilium handling the question of continents essentially shuttered completely in 1968.

                                                        The recent researchers, having investigated the methods, cannot account for the level of error in the maps with the level of rigor their cartographers applied. Perhaps some of the mages still live. What does it say if most of the team left the FC for the Guardians or Mysterium? Were the maps falsified or could things have dramatically changed? What happened to the legacy?

                                                        Grump, grouse, and/or gripe.
