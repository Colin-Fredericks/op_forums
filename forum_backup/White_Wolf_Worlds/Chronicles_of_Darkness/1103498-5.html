    I don't want to make a being that many people in the world believe in as a literal symbol of tyranny.
    Neither do I. My point is that your version of God is not unfair to the biblical character, but that's not exactly the God that most people believe in. Like, a lot of people think of the biblical stories as allegories to varying degrees and the ones who think of them literally true are probably not going to like you turning them into a game setting in the first place.

    Keeping God's identity mysterious isn't exactly helpful either, because they're still acting in the setting in a way that a lot of people are going to find odd. You've already fictionalised God as a rather weird inscrutable and kinda sinister entity with the Holy of Holies bending space and time and being filled with demons... It's a little late to stop is my point.

    but I am not going to establish God as being a force of selfish tyranny and human flaws as the canon of the Era, anymore than that I would make Hindu gods into demons or Norse deities into "simply spirits". The gods are a mystery, and should be treated as such, IMO.
    The Awakening eras don't shy away from doing that, and by not properly addressing the issue, your setting is a little light in comparison. Admittedly that's not necessarily a problem, but it does make the era feel more like Hunter stylistically.

        The only thing I can say is that I have my own style of writing, and there are things I am willing and not willing to do. For now, the First Temple is not an exclusive Mage Era, so I can live with it not being the same style as other Mage settings. The concept of Solomon binding demons to his will and using them to build the Temple is not something that I've made up, so I again have little qualms about doing so- those things like the twisting of space or the whispers of bound demons are traits of Solomon, his builder, not of God himself (there are angels in the Temple too, after all- and nothing was said about the Holy of Holiest itself, only about the corridors of the Temple). God has to be adapted differently because the canon setting is not monotheistic, so I can play with things a little bit- but no, I'm not making God as an entity which is objectively bad as the Exarchs are. I'm not setting harsh truths because I don;'t like them, and I can't satisfy everyone who would read the Era. I can only do what that feel right in my eyes, and with all of the respect, I can't see a way in which I'll be reading such an Era myself and won't end up grinding my teeth. Again, I still remember my feeling when I've read that story from Tales of the Dark Eras.

        In sort, if you want to make God into an Exarch, I'm not going to stop you- I may even suggest you how to do it. But I'm not going to do it in my own setting or my own writing, especially since we don't need it. Rose has said more than once that in a Vampire setting, the chances are that God, the biblical character, probably exist, even if not in the way it is told in the book- and I believe the same would be valid for Hunter. We can have God and the Exarchs in the same setting and nothing bad would happen, IMO. As I said, it is not that I can't do it- I don't want to, like, at all.

                Ok, so you think that the way I represent God does not respect real world beliefs, and that by avoiding giving God a definite existence in Mage's terms I weaken my setting. I don't really agree with the first part, but I don't really care much about it right now- after all, in the end, I can only write things I love and passionate about, and that include the way God exist in the setting. With the second part, about "not having the confidence" to giving God an exact nature harming the setting, I strongly disagree- because I have placed God inside Mage's cosmology. A Mystery. As I've said in the piece, God is THE Mystery of the setting. He is the most non understood thing in the whole Era, for both Israelite, Canaanite and foreigners. Investigating what God actually is is a big part of what a Mage is going to do in the Era- they are going to try to get to the Holy of Holies, they are going to anger Solomon and try to bypass the guard of priests, demons and angels. They are going to have to withstand the space twisting effects of the building and the powers of a great sorcerer king, fighting prophets and challenging holy men. And maybe, if the storyteller is kind enough, they would discover the answer. God is a Mystery- and just as the setting Mysteries are usually left open for the Story teller to decide them, that is the path I've chosem. Not out of lack of confidence, but because I think that making up who and what God is should be the ST's job, and that it should be the player's role to find out.
