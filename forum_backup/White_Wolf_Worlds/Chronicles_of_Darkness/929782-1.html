            I think errand running is still the best way to treat this, with a starting focus on feeling at least initially mundane. If you want Mages to meet up with mentors in other Orders, have them show off their passions and beliefs in some way and let the Magister decide where they belong by introducing them to other people to do work for.

            Have the Thyrsus heal people, the Mastigos keep an eye on someone, etc.

            Then, one day, something comes up and they all have to work together. The Thyrsus encounters an outbreak of a magical disease; the Mastigos's target is murdered while clutching a tattered scroll scrawled in Aramaic. Something goes on that unites them all and makes them ask fundamental questions. Ask players what Orders they want to join, give them chances to abide that Order's philosophy. The would-be mystagogue fights to preserve the knowledge, the guardian hides the evidence of magic, the thearch speaks passionately about the need to protect and guide mortals, the fledgling revels in the challenge, the libertine... Uh... Does whatever libertines do?

            When it comes time to give their status report, you now have reasons for them to be introduced to new mentors; someone who needs a research assistant; the "cleaner" who mops up after the local cults needs a spare set of hands; a thearch running a soup kitchen could do with someone who can help with rehab; a dojo leader looks for new students; a libertine looks for another libertine to do libertine things that are libertine-ish.

                    There is actually one job that elder mage can perfectly do, but quickly be unwilling to due to actual costs that aren't devastating to the younglings:
                    Managing and restarting magical infrastructure around.

                    Basically, a lot of Mages will act like small God-Machines; trying to carry out large scale occult projects that involve a lot of legwork and lots of spells being put in motion. Spells that need to be either maintained or relinquished. Relinquishing spells permanently drops permanent willpower, so for big projects requiring a lot of individual spells this is a no no; unsafe relinquishing demands temporary Willpower, and even elder mages have only X of it at any given time.
                    So, basically, you could have your players go and check on some magical infrastructure, make sure that these spells didn't get corrupted, deal with any potential corruption, and recast spells if possible.
                    This way you have legwork that actually demands a mage, isn't too hard for a newbie, has a reason for elder mage to not just magically solve it, and might foreshadow something bigger for the future, or lead straight into an adventure.
                    This obviously assumes that your Consilium doesn't consider unsafe resolving of spell a DANGEROUS CRIME!, which looked pretty weird to me anyway and little overreacting looking at the actual implementation of these rules.

                                                    Forming a cabal can be done any number of ways depending on the context of the Consilium. For instance its possible for members of the prospective cabal to go to the Consilium and say that they're a cabal and gain official recognition. Or they could just start to act like a cabal even without official recognition until it's just accepted that they are in fact a cabal (Kind of the common law way of doing it). Or the mentors could petition the Consilium to put the prospective members together to form a cabal. Or the mentors could just push them together and tell them to get along and let the Mages figure out on their own if the work as a cabal or not. I could keep going, there are a lot of options.

                                                    Anyway, you don't -need- the PCs to be in the same cabal. All you really need is for them to work on the same plots and design those to encourage collaboration or at least familiarization with each other. If you can get them to work together often enough then you can start subtly exerting outside pressure to make them prefer working with each other rather than others. This could lead to them eventually forming a cabal organically.
