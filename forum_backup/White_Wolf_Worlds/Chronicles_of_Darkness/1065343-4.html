            They're all pretty accurate summaries, especially if you want to run a blended game. The personalized 'intrigue' tends to be a bit melodramatic at best, especially given its emphasis on Thematic Angst, and at worst actively works against cohesive storytelling and narrative. They work, as they are, ideally in a vacuum with no impetus for real change; this despite that every template has a sect devoted exclusively to change.
            The thing is, you're not expected to run them straight out of the book, and that conceit is something that should probably be better advertised :P

            And just an addendum, I've never had a Sin-Eater attend a party that wasn't some kind of memorial holiday, and I made sure people were culturally appropriate for the same. People tend to be more excited about the powers and death-tainted world they can explore than the loose mishmash 'culture' presented in the book; some of the fiction supports that too.

                                                This (speaking on your last chapter read) is something that I always strive for but am usually not satisfied with my attempts. Show-not-telling as far as violence goes.

                                                One of the things that I do focus on in my games is portraying action scenes rather than fight scenes for basically anything that would require initiative rolls. I treat them more how a movie or a show would treat scenes when the tempo of the music swells to erratic levels. This I'm proud of. What I struggle with, I think, is portraying each turn as more narrativly important than "doing this much damage in this area" or "you pull ahead in the foot chase". Sometimes these scenes feel organic but other times I think I fall back on the descriptions.

                                                Anyway that was just something I thought about while you read the chapter.

                                                (||EDIT: sorry, I started writing this before you reigned in the topic and it took me a while to write ^.^; The above part is topic appropriate though ^.^;||)

                                                And here's my brief descriptions of the splats, I've read most of the splats and played about half of them so here's my attempt at clarifying them for you. I think other's have done a better job than I can with explaining the splats to you but i'm going to try for laconic explanations.

                                                Vampire: The Requiem: Humans that are turned (or sired) into one of the Kindred with traits depending on the clan (or typical vampire tropes) of their Sire.
                                                Werewolf: The Forsaken: Half human/Half Wolf-Spirit Uratha (forsaken in spirit-tongue) who are tasked by the spirit of the moon with keeping the borders between the Shadow (spirit) and physical realms balanced.
                                                Mage: The Awakened: A human whose soul becomes connected to a Supernal Realm of magic allowing them to work their will on physical realm depending on their studies and knowledge.
                                                Hunter: The Vigil: A loose collection of humans who've seen what goes bump in the night and so have become inspired to hunt (a vigil is sparked) and fight various things for various reasons.
                                                Promethean: The Created: An artificial being* brought to life by the Divine Fire (a fake soul) who pilgrimages to refine their Divine Fire into a human soul.
                                                Changeling: The Lost: Human stolen to Arcadia (the Faerie realm) by a True Fae and tormented and broken (physically, emotionally, and mentally) till they do whatever job (**) given to them. Some find their way back only to find their life was replaced by a thing, they are still irreparably changed by their durance, and they are actively hunted by their captors.
                                                Geist: The Sin-Eaters: Humans who agree to fuse with Geists (very powerful Ghosts essentially) in return for coming back to life who now can see ghosts and share their Geist's ghost-like powers.
                                                Mummy: The Curse: An ancient immortal that does the bidding of a Judge (a type of death-god) and protects an artifact but can only be awake for a period of time before resting for centuries.
                                                Demon: The Descent: Angels of the God-Machine who become unhooked and Fall (becoming physical) after questioning or disobeying (***) one of their functions and now is hunted to be Recycled (regardless of the circumstances of the Fall).
                                                Beast: The Primordial: A (seemingly) human whose soul is that of a Primordial Fear Horror causing them to feed on mortals in a plethora of ways but must fight and survive Heroes (People who have the compulsion to hunt a specific Beast).
                                                Deviant: The Renegades: (take this with a grain of salt as it's not out yet) A human who had something done to them by someone (scientists, monsters, yourself, &c) giving you abilities that, are at best, unstable and are now hunted by various organizations and individuals.

                                                ~~~

                                                *Note on Promethean: The being can be created out of anything but used flesh is pretty common. The important thing is they are artificial (Frankenstein's Monster, the Golem, the boy from A.I., &c)

                                                **Note on Changeling: They are changed depending on their master's intended function for them which can be anything from caterer, sex-slave, gardener, doll, &c to things like a plant, statue, candle fire, hunter's hounds, the sun, the walls of a cliff, one of many glow-worms in a cave, and other weirder things. Additional note: Changelings become at least humanoid when entering the Physical realm.

                                                ***Note on Demon: Sometimes the Fall happens on accident and sometimes the Angel's function seems to be counter, say, another angels function. So the now Demon may not have ever wanted to fall. Regardless, they are now vigorously hunted with the end result being dismantled for spare parts.

                                                    Okay, I haven't caught up to Ekorren yet, but I have shit to say about the first part of the chapter, so I'm going to get it out.

                                                    Chapter 1: Stories of Violence

                                                    Storytelling Violence (aka Oh God the Feels)

                                                    David is awesome. David's team is awesome. MachineIV, if you are reading this, you are awesome. This entire section is stuff that is so hard to right, so easy to phone in, but it wasn't. Hell, I'd go as far to say that this should be outright required reading for all GMs in all game systems. Wes Schneider wrote a similar section in Pathfinder's Horror Adventures about the dos and don'ts of running a horror game. They are different topics, but they compliment each other fantastically.

                                                    (In fact, I can think of one Dracula-esque nuWWP personality who could really learn something from both books, but I digress...)

                                                    I'm going to go a bit out of order here, but that is to compose my thoughts in a rational manner. The Stories About Lasting Harm and Stories About Moving on sections really reasonate with me. I am somewhat luck in that the only major violence event in my life was at the hands of nature rather than the hands of a person and I have been sheltered from most possible forms of violence. That said, I still got nailed hard by PTSD. I also moved on from it after a solid 11 years. This section is so senstive to the uniqueness and individuality of trauma and it warms my heart. I think that using Vices to represent difficulty moving on is a really cool idea and I LOVE using the idea of specialities to represent moving on with your life. If I were stating myself up in real life, I could see giving myself Survival (avalanches) or Medicine (CPR) for exactly that reason. I threw myself into both and have kept them up to date for most of my adult life.

                                                    Moving backwards to the Eschewing Labels sidebar and the Stories About Victims/Victimizers sections. The whole concept of avoiding both labels and moral judgement here is so important. Here's my real-life justification. I teach at a school that specializes in students with learning disabilities, although I prefer to think of it as aneurotypicallity. But then again, I would think that because I have had effective strategies to deal with my own LD since Middle School. It is similar to ADHD at a glance but is actually an entirely different neurology, which means that drugs do nothing because it is the result of my long term memory being in the 95th percentile and while my short term memory is in the 9th percentile based on the WISC-III and later the WIAT-II.

                                                    Anyhow, one of the things that we observe in our student populations (and those of us staff who have LDs ourselves) is that there is a very high incidence of low-esteem, low-self-worth, and low-affect when they come to our school. You could make a strong argument that this is all trauma and violence based. It is important to note, though, that I'm not talking about violence. I'm talking about the experience of trying to make the square peg fit in the round hole. The experience of not being able to relate to your peers. The experience of socially, emotionally, and/or academically behind your peers because your brain is developing at different rates and in different ways than "normal." Accidental (as well as intentional) mental violence happens to these populations. Does that mean that other schools who don't know how or can't appropriately accommodate these individuals are victimizers? Probably not. Are the students themselves victims? I suspect that most of them would say no. It depends on context. A well-meaning person that is out of their depth or not properly supported can do tons of harm without even realizing it. Any mature treatment of violence has to acknowledge that nuance.

                                                    I liked the summaries of the different gamelines, but I'm a bit disappointed that there was nothing mentioned past Hunter. Geist, Mummy, Demon, and Beast all could have benefited from that discussion.

                                                    I will fully admit to completely skipping Integrity by Attrition on my first skim. I think that was the point when turned straight to the micro-template merits. It is a pretty cool system, though, and both of the merits are solid. I may use a variant of it in my next Vampire game. The optional system won't work with all game lines, but Vampire, Mage, Hunter, and possibly Changeling should be able to utilize it.

                                                        Changeling: the Lost

                                                        First of all: Congratulations! You escaped the most seductive and addicting form of slavery in the cosmos! Too bad the humanity your former owner removed to fit a collar didn't come back with you.

                                                        Yeah, all those stories about faerie lords and ladies abducting human children and replacing them with their own species? Yeah, two errors; one, it's not so much a fae so much as it is a golem formed from a piece of their new possession's soul, and two, they abduct everyone. What the rationale was varies and, let's be frank, does not matter; it all comes down to the fact that a True Fae, the lunatic aristocracy-gods of Arcadia, decided he or she (it, zhe, he and she...) needed you for something, or because you broke a meaningless law, or both (probably more likely both than the second option). You were stolen and replaced by a fetch, a clone of you with something missing (although in well-made fetches that thing tends to be trivial or something you would be glad to be rid of), and taken on a rough ride through Hedge, the border-world that lies between Earth and Arcadia, losing most of your soul on its Thorns in the process. Now properly malleable, your Keeper reshaped you into an ideal servant-but because the True Fae are psychotically self-centered (to the point where they fear the Hedge even more than changelings; the Thorns are composed of the pain of all sentient life, and thus a Keeper cut by them starts to emphasize), you were never good enough-and yet, your Keeper still valued you, because of how versatile you were as a servant. Your life, what little your now-mostly-human intellect recalls, was a wild swing between joy and misery, usually the latter. At some point though, there was a break; perhaps the shifting politics of Faerie suddenly manifested you in a physical form, perhaps even as a song you had enough of a mind to recognize how you were treated; in any case, you realized how addictive Arcadia is really does not balance the horror of eternal servitude, and so you tore out the part of you convinced that, somehow, this was worth it (literally, it's a magic artifact called an icon), and fled into-and out of-the Hedge.

                                                        Unfortunately, the True Fae love their servants, in their sick, myopic way. They notice when you're gone-and even beyond that, Lost like you are prize catches by other Keepers due to the allure of your mustang soul.If that wasn't enough, you're no longer sane in any human way; how can you be, when your very body is composed of living metaphor for how you escaped and your intended duty? (On a good day, you're not actually insane, either-about half of your hallucinations are prophetic visions, the problem is telling the difference.) You desperately need someone who understands-but thankfully, you aren't alone. You may be a rare breed, but only in proportion, and it's always a goal of some brave and/or stupid changelings to give their Kept brethren opportunity to escape. So you find a freehold of fellow escapees and rebels, and use your newfound relationship with Fate (the Wyrd that all fae, you included, loves it some tales) and made yourselves characters in a legend about how you keep the True Fae away-and they have no choice but to obey, because they've never been anything but story (as far as they know or care).

                                                        But be warned-besides a fetch who feels it's their life, thank you very much (you may or may not agree), legends demand conflict-and that means politics between a race of people composed entirely of those too stubborn to give up, And the True Fae are crafty; they can't insert themselves in the legend, but their servants can...

                                                            Catching, catching, caught up. Time for my part 2 of Chapter 1: Why didn't I read these parts the first time?

                                                            Framing Scenes
                                                            I definitely did not process these correctly the first time I read them. They make much more sense now. Framing scenes are an easy, punchy system to use that can give some much-needed pizzazz to a scene. The examples are all good, and some them are straight up "build around me" ideas like speeding train, free fall, and high steel.

                                                            Violence Groups
                                                            Another section I mostly skipped on my first pass. Absolutely fantastic. Treating the group similar to an NPC with simplified stats is a sublime way to make them an actual threat. A bunch of gangbangers don't stand a chance against a Mummy in a head to head conflict, but as a collective, they can still cause problems. Giving the groups aspirations, "virtues", and vices is also a good way to give the mooks some personality and some insight into how the group functions. I bet you could translate Violence Groups into specific gamelines pretty effectively. Even if I flesh out every member of a werewolf pack, defining the pack's aspirations, virtue, and vice is a good practice for the storyteller. It's also a nice way to have cults crop up that aren't overtly supernatural like the Mystery Cult Initiation implies.
