    A Swarm ?

    07-29-2016, 02:33 PM
    I was reading in my book, and the have in the section of Antagonist some stats for a trained guard dog in the nWoD Corebook 2.0
    They say we can use the same stats to represent a swarm of vermin.

    That's an easy enough way... but imagine something like a swarm of japanese giant hornets, or like one million of bullet ants, or like 2000 brazilian wandering spiders... A real swarn that can almost cover your body at anytime... and sting/bites you to death. I find it difficult to work with this kind of swarm like if it was one simple dog that you must hit to kill. Im I missing something ?

    Did any of you house rule something out of that ?

    New tilt/conditions ? "Swarmed" or something similar ?

    Using grappling rules would make you more adept at fleing those swarm with Strength and Brawl... wich is not totaly accurate in my point of view.

    How do you deal damage dealt to it ? Fire spray would work wonders but gunshot would obviously not be working very good.

    What are your point of view on this ?

      The Swarm rules are scattered across a few different books. In first edition, they can be found in Innocents (pg. 198-199) Mage: the Awakening (pg. 182) and Promethean:the Created (pg.239)


      The 2nd Edition version appears in Demon:The Descent (pg.179)


      Swarm (Environmental Tilt)
      Effect: Animals of Size 1 are generally best represented by swarms, flocks and other groupings of the animals. Swarms are measured by their radius in yards. A swarm inflicts one point of bashing damage to anyone within its radius. A swarm can inflict more damage by condensing. Every time the swarm condenses to cover half of its full area, it inflicts one additional point of damage per turn. Therefore, a swarm of eight yards in radius inflicts two bashing damage per turn if it constricts down to a four yard radius, three bashing if it halves that again to a two-yard radius, and four bashing damage per turn it if condensed itself down to a one-yard radius. Though condensing doesn’t usually happen all that often in nature (save in the case of creatures such as killer bees), it is an easy enough thing for a being with supernatural powers to command them to do so.
      Armor is effective against a swarm only if it covers one’s full body, but even then it provides only half its rating. In addition, targets are distracted by the swarm, suffering –2 dice on all rolls while they are within the radius, even if they’re not specifically attacked.
      Causing the Tilt: Some animals such (bees or wasps) swarm naturally. Others can be compelled by supernatural powers to swarm a particular target.
      Ending the Tilt: The swarm cannot be attacked with fists, clubs, swords or guns. Only area-affect attacks such as a torch affect it. Each point of damage inflicted by a flame or other applicable attack halves the swarm’s size. Once the swarm is reduced below a one-yard radius, either all the animals are dead or the few remaining disperse.

                          You know Marcus, it is YOUR GAME.
                          Those rules are provided to us as a guideline to play around with a solid system that takes into account some sort of balance. However, in my eyes, fun may comes before codification. In the end, you can create two swarm tilt. Insect swarm. Vermin swarm.

                          Insect would deliver less actual damage, but has they can sting everywhere, and try to enter you nose, mouth and ears, would maybe cause more distraction or a greater penalty. Also, some poisonous spiders and wasp/hornets would deliver painful venom that might inflict a sickness or a poison.

                          Vermin swarm... or whatever name you came by may inflict more direct damage, but less likely to inflict a sickness or venom... depending on the creature of course. Armor would be more effective vs those vermin has rats and birds are less likely to go under your armor and clothes than a bunch of wasp and hornets or ants. However, a bunch of rats and birds can probably slow you down cause when you have them everywhere on you, and around you, under pressure and pain, you may walk on some, even trips on them, while they bite at your feets and legs. Wasp and Ants would be more easily crushed when you step on them.

                          Yep... we have cool stuff here. I think...

                          Finally, a flock of angry bird would be something... but for a vampire with some supernatural resistance or a raging Gauru, they would be only a minor nuisance...
