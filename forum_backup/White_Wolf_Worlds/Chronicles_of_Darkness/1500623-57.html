    Originally posted by Obsidian Pharaoh View Post
    I'd peg it at Time 5, but not sure about the Practice, though leaning more towards Unmaking, where the spell is flavoured as excising the subject from regular temporal progression and locking them in a repeating timeline. This same description can be used for Making though, "creating" the loop.
    My head goes to Making, for the image of creating a kind of bypass in time that functions as the underlying mechanic that becomes experienced as a time loop.

    Originally posted by Obsidian Pharaoh
    What I'm having difficulty with is the actual mechanics of implementing the spell beyond inflicting the Tilt, like how does Duration work for the spell? Does it just dictate how long the spell lasts from the perception of those outside the loop, or does it determine how long the loop lasts in terms of its own internal time, or both?
    I think it's consistent with how other such spells work that Potency functions as the measure of how much it imposes upon the target (with Reach to determine how many units each point of Potency corresponds to), while Duration remains consistent as how long the spell continues to exist from an objective stand point.

    Originally posted by Obsidian Pharaoh
    Does the loop start when the spell is cast or does the loop start from the past (according to Duration?) and end at the point the spell is cast, making it naturally withstood by temporal sympathy?
    I think the most straightforward way it works is that it hits the target and basically points their forward momentum through time back upon themselves, in a way that shunts them back through time into as much of their own past as the Potency can grasp. It basically becomes like an advanced form of Shifting Sands, causing them to disappear from the perspective of people in the present, but it's locked in on itself in a way that means it can't actually create Lasting effects on the past. I'd say that by default, the person experiencing it is aware of the loop, but Reach can make it so that they continuously reset as well.

    I don't think this would require temporal sympathy, as it's not actually casting magic on the past.

    Originally posted by Obsidian Pharaoh
    Would you allow the spell in the first place before Archmastery?
    I think it's fair game within the scope of as many targets as such a spell could normally get and a loop that can probably only encompass days at best, with archmastery having space to create something more complex or with greater scope and longer lasting impact. A Traveller's spell is mostly an elaborate way to trap somebody for a while, maybe something that they can reflect on, while archmastery creates an effect that kind of distorts which past for the target even remains true, for instance. Or creating a time loop that keeps forcing a scenario to repeat itself until it cultivates a specifically desired outcome.

                        The Exarchs are never seen directly. They occupy a place in the cosmology where what they look like would be a filtered-down mask to prevent their visitor's soul from exploding, and their closest servants (ochemata) are all capable of very broad shapeshifting to look however they need to in a given situation.

                                Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                                I´m trying to use one splat as a simple fight against my players. How normally a cabal of mages(Arcanas 3 max, Gnosis 2) tend to view other supernatural splats in terms of power?
                                Assume a "monster of the week" situation, without strings attached to secret societies. Does a Cabal of 3 mages(different paths) have difficulties against Kindred, Lost, Beggoten, etc?

                                The objective is to scare them with some cool supernatural powers, but I don't want this to be a cakewalk.
                                It will depend on a lot of things, including available tools and general preparedness, as well as what "a simple fight" entails. An enemy with intel who's choosing their moment to act when and where the PCs aren't on their guard has different approaches to an enemy who is on a berserk rampage due to external circumstances. A lone enemy going into a three-on-one fight without backup has worse odds of making a dent than a team with equal or greater headcount and/or support.

                                Conventional wisdom would be to have the opposition fight smart and/or hit hard and fast before the character(s) have time to marshal their resources; at the described Gnosis and Arcana, what rotes everybody's got will have a strong impact on what can be safely done in a turn, and taking the action to somewhere Sleepers can see will crank up the risk of Paradox and loss of Wisdom (this relates to the Kindred's classical approach in the old "the Big Three splats have a tactical rock-paper-scissors thing going on" idea).

                                Generally speaking the more lasting scare in Mage's thematics will be "dealing with this with direct magic could blow up in a way we can't walk back," such that forcing avenues that might trigger Quiescence and/or Paradox is a big push. If "without strings attached to secret societies" includes the more general phenomenon of mortal retinues being attached to monsters, that means leaning into the fact that most non-Supernal magic doesn't have operational constraints based around the presence of witnesses and pushing the fight near crowded areas.

                                The general balance of power for gameline-having supernatural beings with a conventional Supernatural Potency progression tends to taper off to the 4-6 range, and specializing powers and skills toward the available heights of a specific ability will tend to make a stronger impression. Monster lairs and nests will generally give the benefit of preparation and home field advantage.

                                Even outside of the particulars of the monster, how a mage or group of mages will view them threatwise will vary by experience, Wisdom, and overall personality. Confidence (misplaced or otherwise) based on prior encounters or study, as well as the degree to which one's magic has gone to one's head, will directly affect the difference between "I'm an Obrimos, this is the fifteenth vampire I've encountered in the last decade, and I wrote my thesis on how to deal with them" and "what is that clockwork birdman?"

                                        Lets say you have a Time primary, Space optional Legacy. You currently have Time 4 and Space 2. If you are using the main Attainment, with only time, you have 4 pseudo-dice to distribute in spell factors. Lets say its Potency primary, you have advanced duration and normal scale. You could use all 4 dice to boost duration to a week. Alternatively, you could boost duration to a day and scale for two people. A third option is to keep duration to a scene and boost the Potency from 4 to 6 (since primary factor rules still apply).

                                        Now lets say you have the optional Attainment, same factors, so you have 3 pseudo-dice to use, since the number is the minimum between the Time and Space ratings. So in this case you could make it last a day or affect a second target, but not both.

                                        The pseudo-dice can be allocated on the fly and represent the most flexibility an Attainment can have for adjusting factors. Hope that proves helpful.

                                                Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                                But Knit can fix personal tilts with one reach, as a higher spell, is the same effect implicit on Mend? Was this an omission?
                                                It's omitted because most Personal Tilts in the vein of Arm Wrack are caused by some amount of damage to the relevant limbs or sense organs and Mend heals lethal damage at a higher rate than Knit; it would be redundant, unlike Knit's Reach effect which is presumably there to allow you to heal a Personal Tilt before restoring the leftmost box of damage that caused it.

                                                On the other end of things, Tilts caused by aggravated damage tend to result in permanent loss of function of those body parts, and regenerating missing limbs and organs is a separate spell, so Mend doesn't have much need for a specific extra effect on its "heal a good amount of lethal damage" to turn a broken arm into an unbroken arm.
