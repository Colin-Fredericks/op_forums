    Shouldn't we, y'know, be visibly complaining at Paradox for quietly killing ChroD?

    08-30-2024, 11:35 PM
    TL;DR: Help me figure out what to say to Paradox so they can at least break their silence.

    I can't be the only one annoyed with how Paradox is quiet-killing the entirety of ChroD, seemingly just because they feel like it might cut into their attempts at WoD 5e. I've followed Paradox for about as long as I've followed ChroD, and I can tell you that they do, in fact, have established lines of communication with players of their stuff, such as their dedicated subreddit r/ParadoxPlaza or even their own forum. And yet, I haven't seen anyone taking them to task for their shabby treatment of our community (which, admittedly, I am also guilty of not doing, so here's me trying to show up I guess).

    Someone should finally go and write a profanity-laden poorly formatted deeply unhinged rant politely worded, properly polished, cogent and succinct post explaining why this isn't a way to treat their customer base and why they don't need to fear ChroD (or, failing that, at least acknowledge that we matter enough to officially acknowledge whatever their policy is), and since I'm a someone I guess I'm implicitly volunteering myself. However, I refuse to make a post that gets 7 views and 1 reply from some random forum-goer saying "lol sucks to be a chrod fan I guess".

    So, that brings us to this thread. I want to know what other members of our community think:

    - Are there other ChroD fans here who would join/boost any efforts to air our grievances at Paradox? Reddit and Paradox Interactive Forums would be the main locations; if you can only lend support on one, which is it?
    - If this is to mean anything, we need an ask, something concrete that they can give us. I think as an ask from them, we should demand clarity on what their plans are for our favorite games - if they're cancelling our games, they should have the decency to look us in the eye and say so, a decency they HAVE shown to fans of their cancelled video game projects. Do you think this is a worthwhile ask, or do you think there's a better one? (Note that obviously "you guys should greenlight more ChroD books" will be in any post that I make, but if a forum post was all it took to get that ask then we'd already have more in the pipeline)
    - What are your best arguments - be they economical or emotional - for why Paradox should continue with ChroD, and/or should talk to the fucking community at fucking all?

    By the God-Machine, I sure hope I'm not screaming into the digital void on this one.

                  While there could have been a number of solutions to this, most of them have lost their windows to really be functional. At this point, there's really only one thing that could revive the CofD: someone that actually wants to make CofD books buying the IP from Paradox. Paradox paid for it, but isn't using it. They don't need to own the IP to use mechanics like Touchstones. Their shareholders are not going to be OK with the company sitting on an unused asset that's only going to depreciate in value if a good enough offer comes along.

                  The problem is (1) figuring out how much Paradox would want for it and (2) someone having the resources to get it done.

                  While I don't intend to be a buzzkill, the big problem with some sort of fan-campaign is that Paradox can silence it on any social media under their control, and ignore it anywhere else given how tiny the TTRPG industry really is (and CofD fans are a tiny fraction of that). Even organizing anything here has it's limits as it's against the rules to promote cross-site drama, and the Exalted fans don't deserve to have their game in Paradox's hands pulled if Onyx Path doesn't enforce that rule strictly enough for Paradox's liking.

                  Considering that Paradox doesn't think the passive income of just licensing CofD to Onyx Path like they're doing with Exalted is worth it for their desire to use CofD for WoD5e spare parts, we have no real power to push them to do anything.

                      There are two big problems in dealing with Paradox, here:

                      First, they're a video game publisher. Their core business is far from TTRPGs and generates far more revenue. CoD is a negligible market for them, one they have no pressure to cater to.

                      Second, if the last years showed something about them, is that they're not the smartest player around even in their main market. So while any IP can be groomed to become a successful video game franchise, they're not the ones for this job.

                      At this point, as sad as it is, our only hope is for Paradox to blunder enough to end up having to sell a very devalued IP to an interested party. And while this seems to be actually a likely outcome, we have no sign that it will happen soon.

                          I'm a little offended at the implication that the Chronicles fanbase has been quiet in the past about this, but putting that aside, I've got some things about why the fan campaign, in this way, wouldn't do jack and/or shit, and some problems with the "someone buys Chronicles" solution (not that it's not the only way to solve this, but it has some caveats that makes it harder), but in the interest of time, I'm gonna skip those for now and just address two things that more realistically would help. Well. One of them is realistic.

                          Option 1) The portion of the Chronicles fanbase that is still buying Paradox products would need to stop completely.

                          Paradox has a long history of demonstrably not listening to anyone, either the fanbase at large or with seasoned professionals, about Chronicles and any of the reasons they would want to keep supporting it, but the exploration of that is for another time-just suffice it to say that a flood of sternly worded letters won't do it. But Paradox is a not the largest company out there, and they are one of those companies that relies on a very dedicated fanbase to basically buy their products when it comes out, so if a sizable portion of that said base suddenly dropped out, that would force them to give some attention, which might serve as a grounds for re-evaluating the handling of Chronicles.. Boycotting the World products alone wouldn't really be enough, because that's small enough and already having enough trouble that basically it'd be a negligible loss-but if there's a continual and notable dip because products just aren't being bought like they used to, then that might do it.

                          I say this one is an option, but honestly it's not. Putting aside the question of just how much of the Chronicles fanbase makes up Paradox's regular purchase base probably having an answer of "not so much as to be viable in the first place", fans also just won't do it-or rather, not enough fans would be willing to commit to it to actually have it done. Too many consumers don't want to give up the things they are comfortable with to make otherwise make a statement on something that is important to them in some way, so even if Chronicles fans were to be vocal, they'd probably still buy Paradox products because they like Paradox products and aren't willing to not have them just so they can get this other thing.

                          Which leaves us with Option 2.

                          Option 2) Back and promote the shit out of Curseborne's Kickstarter in October.

                          This might not sound intuitive, but follow me here.

                          Paradox itself may not have been planning on their money coming from the World of Darkness RPG's themselves-they expected to see money come in from the other products, like games, shows, etc. once the brand firmly had it's feet under it-but for Martin Ericcson and the rest of the team that was in Paradox's White Wolf before they closed it, it's not too hard to see from the way they were talking about that they expected their World of Darkness releases to hit at least Exalted 3rd Edition numbers on initial launch (at the time of the purchase of World and Chronicles, Exalted was one of the best performing RPG Kickstarters ever), and I'm willing to bet that Paradox has been glaring sourly at the successes of the Cosmere RPG and Avatar Legends Kickstarters in contrast to their increasingly underperforming releases. That said, Chronicles' Kickstarters, while demonstrating there was a reliable and consistent purchasing audience that would reliably put money into their pockets, never had the gangbusters numbers that would otherwise convince them(the Kickstarters of Primordial, Lost, and Sin-Eaters are relatively close to each other and in the period of the time the purchase was done, and all have very similar numbers to each other, Changeling being the best at $166,290 between 2,500 backers), and that could be part of the reason why, despite industry experts pointing out the advantages for keeping Chronicles going along with their new World, Paradox went the way they did.

                          But that alumni of of old White Wolf hands who stuck with Onyx Path and the new wave of hit writers, developers, and promoters who emerged from the Chronicles Renaissance have pretty well gotten into and behind Curseborne(either by directly working on it or by being active in the developing fanbase for it), and Curseborne is a chance to prove the power and value of that crew's skills and philosophy to an otherwise unimpressed business, by putting a urban horror RPG up on the block and getting smash numbers without having the advantage of brand legacy behind it (or, rather, worn on it's sleeves). So if Curseborne generates the numbers Paradox was hoping for, that would be a clear and sharp message that, hey, as it turns out, Onyx Path not only has been reliably your best content creators for this kind of thing whenever you partner with them, they really know what they're talking about. And that could be enough to get them to rely on them more for World's design moving forward and, more pressingly for this conversation, be a reason to put sustaining Chronicles back on the table.

                          That's not a guarentee-Heavy Arm's answer is basically the only sure fire way to get Chronicles back- but it'd some real damn leverage anytime the conversation came up after that, so yeah-you want to do your part for trying to bring Chronicles back, help the people who were demonstrating Chronicle's viability prove they know what they are talking about by dumping as much as you can into Curseborne and evangelize the shit out of it before and during the Kickstarter.

                          The better Curseborne performs, the more reason Paradox has to listen to Onyx Path for IP management advice.

                                      I'm not sure why you think any of those are meaningful distinctions; broadly or for this specific thread.
