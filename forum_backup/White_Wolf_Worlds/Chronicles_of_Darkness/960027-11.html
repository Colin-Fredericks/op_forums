                        Alan Moore's Swamp Thing and the post-Morrison Animal Man come to mind: creatures that represent raw, primal aspects of life without necessarily being an environmentalist screed. I picture something that's thematically a sort of inversion of Prometheus while reversing the Touchstone model from Requiem. You start out as human, but you're a vessel for some higher form: maybe it's vegetative or animal life or the endless depths of the ocean. You have the potential to become something alien and elemental, something that can better protect the people you love and the world they live in, but each stage in your ascension means shedding the things you love and becoming something more surreal. I could see sprites, dryads, nymphs, and djinn being the sort of template basis, and like Prometheus, it would be set with a definitive goal in mind, a hopeful if bittersweet end as your characters slowly gives up their identitie to become something greater than themselves.

                        I too would like to see an actual spiritual successor to Wraith come into being. I think it could coexist well with Geist without the two games stepping on one another's toes.

                        I also think some variation of a zombie or revenant game is doable, though I think it comes down to making something that doesn't seem too derivative of Requiem. Perhaps, like Beast, you focus more on crossover? Your body is dying, and while you start by feeding on animals and corpses, that's eventually not enough to sustain you. Human bodies will last you a bit, but other supernaturals is where the money's at. Something about your transformation makes it so that eating the flesh of something more than human transfers a level of their power to you. Maybe? I don't know... I'm not sure a game where all the characters are cannibals would be all that... palatable.

                        Maybe a twist on Lovecraftian mythology? You play a cultist but, contrary to the typical spin where cultists are trying to raise Old Gods from the Depths to feast on All Existence, you're there to preserve our being. Keeping this terribly, otherworldly thing asleep and placid is your responsibility, and sometimes that means doing horrible, horrible things. You're tethered to this extradimensional creature, and being so tethered gives you access to a portion of its powers, but you're forced to further its agendas, its power plays against other slumbering gods, because doing so ensures that it doesn't awaken in a rage, ready to devour everything you know. Your powers grow stronger the closer it comes to wakefulness. So you want your powers to be weak. You want to be able to accomplish the agendas set out for you using practical and mundane tools, because your powers growing is a terrifying sign that you aren't doing good enough, that the apocalypse is nigh. I'd imagine each player would play a different member of the cult, and they'd work together to build their god at the beginning of the game, with each PC getting some distinct portion of their god's powers. The cult would have a shared experience pool sort of similar to practical experience in Hunter, but rather than being used to buy tactics and new traits for cell members, it would be used to expand the cult via a sort of merit system: getting politicians and policemen in the cult's pocket, expanding their base of operations, creating sleeper cells in other cities, possibly with a late game that's more strategic and focused on inter-cult espionage and politics (not dissimilar from what was introduced in Damnation City). Meanwhile, you know that a lot of these cultists are in the same predicament you are, trying to stave off the awakening of something equally as monstrous as your patron.

                                            Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                                            I think Deviant is going to be kind of like that, in that gaining power is super risky and something a character could conceivably be trying to stop.

                                            Yeah. It sounds like they're doing the body horror thing where you just get progressively worse and worse until you self-destruct. I think it's a great idea that really plays to a sense of Cronenberg horror.

                                            I think where this game would differentiate itself is in the group aspect. Every game is about a team of people working together, but so often, players are put at odds. Mummy is about cults, but it's about the horrible god-monster that everyone else worships.

                                            Thrall: The Supplication (or whatever it would hypothetically be called), would foster a sense of community from the start, in terms of both character and mechanics. You're all members of the same cult. The players work together to create their patron monster, and they have to cooperate to decide on the form that their cult takes through collaborative XP spending. Your power stat is linked, so that doomsday clock is scaled to everyone in the party, but there'd be differentiation in how the individual respond to this sort of growth. I think Integrity (or whatever the equivalent trait is) would relate closely to your power rating. The closer your god comes to awakening, the more at danger you are of suffering derangement and becoming a creature for this thing you're keeping in check. What do you do when your brother or sister in the cult becomes a dangerous liability because exposure to this Old God has made them crack? And how far are you willing to push your cult for the greater cause? You have a noble ambition, as a custodian of a sleeping monster baby, but keeping it placid requires ruthless decisions. How far will you go with brainwashing, blackmail, and indoctrination to keep your people loyal to the cause? How much will you upset the delicate balance of society for the sake of keeping that society afloat? Will you turn a blind eye while one of your cultists goes on a ritualistic murder spree to feed your master's sick desires? Will you encourage it? Will you use your ties in the PD and the media to cover it up? Or will your natural, moralistic instincts force you to clamp down hard, knowing that doing so will make this Old God stir in its slumber, and potentially upend your delicate grasp on sanity?

                                            I think the crux of the game would be about the horror of cults, these groups where zealotry and the righteousness of the cause is used as pretext to convince members to do monstrous things. The twist is that you're right. Your sins do keep the world alive. But how far are you willing to go, and what are you willing to lose in the process
