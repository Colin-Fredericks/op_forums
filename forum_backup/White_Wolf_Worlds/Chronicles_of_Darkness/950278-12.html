    Going back to the "If you did all that, it wouldn't be Genius" thing.

    I just recognized what sort of plea this is, and, well.

    The problem is that Genius isn't anything. This isn't like Promethean, where trying to change it to fit transhumanist defies it's humanist core that literally everything else is built on top of. Genius tries to say it has a core, an idea, a theme, but it doesn't. It has nothing that makes up an identity. It is a game about People Doing Things and it Works until it Doesn't for Reasons.

    I took some of the things that are said, ideas patched around that void, and tried to offer ideas on how give them roots, how to make it reach down and create an identity. Because right now, Genius isn't anything other than a really badly handled GURPs. And I'm already not impressed with GURPs.

    I don't have to worry about Genius not being Genius, because Genius isn't even Genius. This is a game that tells you your mad scientists aren't doing science. You have to solve that fallacy first before that argument works.

    Mind you, if I want mad scientists, I have tons of games for that. it is not a mark in Genius's favor that I would happily leave it alone.

        Originally posted by TidyGamer View Post
        I guess it just boils down to me not seeing any kind of horror in "you don't matter and nothing you do matters" angle that people seem to be trying to explain is the central horror aspect of Genius. All I see is magical powers tied to mental illness being granted to people with no rhyme or reason, but they get to ride around on jetpacks and shoot death rays while fighting the moon nazi/hollow earth nazi alliance in space as a consolation prize. In that equation, all I'm seeing is that the horror aspect is characters getting slapped with "suddenly you're crazy" brush, which I find as insulting as I think a lot of people would if a game's horror element was being slapped with the "suddenly you're queer" brush
        I don't think Genius is aiming at "nothing you do matters", exactly. I take the intended tension in the game to be this: a mad scientist wants, above all else, to have his theories acknowledged as true, and his inventions valued, by ordinary people; but that's the one thing mad scientists cannot ever have. Mad science can achieve anything imaginable, except the mad scientist's true desire.

        One of the problems Genius has is that while this frustration (you must acknowledge my brilliance!) is part of the mad scientist trope, it's not the thing that makes mad scientists monstrous. The mad scientist becomes a monster because he steps beyond the limits of human knowledge and morality to seize power; he rushes in where angels fear to tread, and leaves disaster in his wake. Genius doesn't look at the horrors its characters can inflict on other people - it shows its world from their perspective, and tries to generate horror from their mental states. (In that respect, it is rather like a canon CofD game: Beast the Primordial. But since that's generally seen as a flaw in Beast, putting Genius in its category isn't exactly flattering...) As a result it falls flat, because the frustration of not being seen as brilliant isn't particularly horrific.

                            Something to chew on, for a Genius 2nd Edition...

                            If you use Mage: the Awakening as a start, and use the Second Edition for the new Paradox rules as a start...that'll put you on fairly solid ground. But to my further point...

                            I can see Geniuses being,in many ways, the opposite of Mages. Mages latch into the Supernal world. What if Geniuses are designed (somewhat haphazardly) to hook into the Abyss?

                            The Abyss is where all the stuff deemed "impossible" dwells. What if Geniuses tap into that impossibility to build what they build? What if they're supposed to do that, in some sort of ecological way, bleeding the Abyss in and filtering it as a way to neutralize it, recycle it? Their Axioms measure how much "impossibility" they can safely manage, but they'd have the option of going further, and in the process creating something with potentially nasty drawbacks. In this scenario, they aren't scientists (though they need the trappings of science to contain something impossible in this reality), but shaman-engineers. They understand science, and can do science, but like mages, they are prone to obsessions and fixations, and sometimes the need to build an orbital death laser is too great to be ignored. If he pushes too hard, he begins to spill out Abyssal-laced Inspiration that makes reality act in certain small ways like the flawed theories the genius needs to work with impossible things, and after awhile, said Genius can get lost in this semi-reality.

                            Because their inventions are Abyss-laced, poking and prodding it can cause it to "malfunction" (when it's really working as intended, because it's the Abyss), but for awhile it can work and be used just like any other piece of technology. Even if there's not really any way it should be working. Or living. Or thinking. This is because our reality is slowly using the Genius's work to detoxify that nugget of the Abyss that's powering it. Kind of like getting tiny injections of substances you're allergic to, in order to build up a tolerance.

                            I don't have any useful suggestions on how to address the more problematic parts you mentioned. The review was certainly an eye-opener!
