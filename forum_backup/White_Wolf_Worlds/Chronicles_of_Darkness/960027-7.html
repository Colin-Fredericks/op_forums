    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    Perhaps the question that should be asked is not, "what monsters are left," but rather, "what kind of monster stories are left?"
    In that vein, we don't really have a monster that's a parallel evolution alongside humanity - a metaphysical and moral equal, for all the better and worse that entails. The Creature from the Black Lagoon is one such, but looked at another way, so could a djinn be (even if a lot of djinn might argue the point).

    For those monsters with access to otherworlds, said otherworlds are usually a double-edged resource. We don't really have a monster for whom the otherworld is home, and for whom Earth is the double-edged resource. Again, djinn might fit that bill, as might a game based on Changeling's hobgoblins, or perhaps naga.

    All the monsters we have come through a very Western lens. To take a nonwestern monster, and extend its own metaphysical context into something global would be hard, but rewarding. Mummy does this to a certain extent, but still with a monster rooted in Western pop culture. The aforementioned djinn and naga could work, but really, there's an embarrassment of riches.

        Mario Acevedo has an interesting take on how to keep aliens balanced in a World of Darkness-type setting. In his Felix Gomez series, Earth is sort of a Wild West for the aliens. Officially, they're not supposed to interfere . . . but the aliens aren't putting the resources in necessary to keep everyone out. Naturalist/sociologists run the barricade to study the humans in their natural habitat. Scientists to perform research on the strange supernatural phenomena native to Earth . . .or conduct their own "side projects". Criminals to hide out. Slavers to supply the burgeoning black market in exotic "pets" . . .

        And since none of them are supposed to be here, they can't exactly radio for reinforcements or supply drops . . .

        A setup like that should translate well into a WoD game. Alien PCs would still have the usual need for "Resources" and "Contacts", with new merits/backgrounds for "Alien Tech" or "Off-world Connections". Crossovers when the aliens run afoul of other splats ("Gomez" revolves around escalating tensions at the vampires and aliens become aware of each other), whether for better or worse . . .

        Originally posted by Errol216 View Post
        Something I haven't seen played with a lot is service to humanity. One of the unifying themes of CofD is that humanity is an unalloyed good thing, for all its warts, and doing a light subversion of that seems like an interesting direction to go. It overlaps a little with Deviant and Promethean, but the tone is significantly different from revenge or redemption... but I'm not really sure what that tone is. But make a splat with some created purpose, where it's clear that being subservient makes sense and is a good thing... but... it's ultimately pretty terrible for you and you need to figure out how to balance that. The antagonists would be those who go all-in on service, to make it more clear that that's not what you're actually supposed to do.

        (. . .)

        I would like to see a mature take on religion. Not really sure how to go about that.
        Have you ever run across the Witchcraft/Armageddon RPGs? It has both angels and saints as playable splats, handled in WoD style. As they remember it, the angels were ordered to "look after" humanity. Tens of thousands of years later, they're still trying to follow that order. But dissension has fractured their ranks into three factions, every day humanity comes up with new ways to tempt them, and . . . its just been so long!

        One faction leader preaches a very fascist version of hardcore fundamentalism as a solution. Another, wiping out humanity. And the third is pro-humanity largely because its members are hooked on vices that only humanity can supply. . .

        Saints/prophets/etc. could be an interesting addition to the WoD. Humans touched by divinity (for better or worse), and having to deal with the "profane" world Might take a bit of work, but keeping it distinct from Demon and Mage should be doable.

        Also wonder if it'd be possible to do economic horror. I know Red Markets is aiming somewhere around that; don't know where exactly it'll land.
        Interesting looking game . . . although I get more of a "Shadowrun meets the zombie apocalypse" vibe. Who knows, though

            I feel like an Alien game is one that could be done well, but it needs to fit the CofD setting: Demon is about as close to sci-fi as I think anyone is comfortable with (some might say it's already passed that point, but I think they're wrong), and that game succeeds because it's full of occultism and other High Strangeness dressed in a technological skin. Doing an actual "from another planet and smashed to earth in a space ship" Alien would be a thematic mismatch that would chafe with the wider setting, but drawing on some of the stuff hinted at by Werewol'f material on the idigam as strange things foreign to reality and uncomfortable with the physical world. A game about aliens from somewhere else - some sort of inner realm, or the depths of a known realm - who do not belong here and want to go home but cannot for whatever reason might work well.

            Beyond that, I think there's a point to be made that maybe we don't need too many more gamelines. There's eleven of them now, and trying to get at least a single piece of new content out for each of them a year seems like a Herculean task. Vampire still doesn't have any supplements, and Werewolf and Mage each only have one, Changeling, Hunter, Geist, and Mummy still need 2e updates, Demon could use at least an antagonist book, Beast and Deviant will need full suites of supplements... I'd sooner see things focus on depth than breadth.

            As a side note: Errol216, Red Markets has been awesome so far; there are several actual play podcasts for it, and I have the backer draft of the rules.

            Remi. she/her. game designer.
