    Space arcana in a Sci Fi

    01-10-2017, 08:58 PM
    I've been working on a Sci Fi setting that uses the CofD rules. Specially mages. The setting is self made but examples of it would be the Mass Effect series or Warhammer 40k. Galaxy spanning, multiple races, starships, all that fun stuff.

    ​So while developing how travel between worlds works, which mostly involves starships, FTL drives, and Jump Gates for even faster travel, the obvious question came up. What about the Space Arcana? Now straight off the bat I want to say that I don't think this is a right or wrong answer type question, I'm looking for feed, view points and how you think it would work best, or logically should work.

    ​In standard 1st edition nWoD we know space couldn't get past earth without being archmage level. However this wasn't much of a big deal since you didn't have many places to go other than on earth. With space 3 you can already make portals to anywhere your familiar with, space 4 gives you teleport. Potentially anywhere on the globe, or "anywhere in the play area".

    ​Now if we jump to a galaxy spanning game, the ability to teleport on just one planet is a MASSIVE reduction on spaces abilities. You could have a game enviorment with over 50 worlds and you can only teleport on the one your on. That's like saying space only works around a country (or that's the compliant one of my players brought up). On the other hand, space is massive, traveling it is part of the fun and the fear. You cant easily get from this planet, to that planet, and you could get attacked on the way, being disabled in space is a real risk. If you have teleporting across the a galaxy, instantly, it seems to really take the space out of the game.

    ​Additionally in this setting magic isn't a secret, its common knowledge (much like Force is reasonably common knowledge in Star Wars)..

    I'm working out my approach to this and seek feedback on the matter.

          Second Edition's nixed the atmospheric limitation in favor of the more prosaic issue of getting the necessary sympathetic Instruments and connections — most long-distance Space magic requires the initial legwork of getting to the location once and/or acquiring a representative chunk of the destination.

          The bigger issue is that, even with the Attainment available to an Adept of Space, the limits on how big an object you can target is pretty low — Gnosis 10 and Space 5 still effectively limit a hypothetical Hyperion the Transporter.

          Gnosis 10 + Space 5 + Yantras [Sympathy 0 + Mudra 6 + Mantra 2 + Runes 2 + Persona 4 + Demesne 2] 16 + Extra Time 5 = 36 dice, which can theoretically be reduced to a pool size of -5 before the autofail floor kicks in; since spell factors go in -2 intervals, that floor is effectively -4 for a total of 40 dice in penalties.

          Teleportation's got Potency as its Primary Factor and its effects neither scale nor require persistence, so we can pretty safely dump all of the penalties into Scale, adding 20*5 to the base Size of 5 for a limit of Size 105 on a roll that is highly unlikely to succeed even with the rote quality on a self-designed rote, or an effective coin-flip if we scale the penalties down to a positive number of remaining dice (2 dice after subtracting 34 dice, adding 17*5 to the base 5 for a cap of Size 90). Teamwork and Order tricks can tweak those numbers a bit, but using the former without the latter means you effectively need at least two dedicated Adepts of Space to properly benefit. For contrast, teleporting people can be accomplished by any Adept or suitable Imbued Item.

          In other words, actual space exploration isn't hampered overmuch by the existence of teleporters, but getting from one planet to another is going to be less of an issue for places that have had the relevant materials and infrastructure set up and substantially more of an issue if you're trying to get somewhere new.

              Which raises the question: what do you want?

              You've got a custom setting, and thereby you've got a custom set of assumptions that you've baked in. How much of CofD!M:tAw are you pulling in? Are there Exarchs and the Lie, or is everyone fully exposed to the Supernal beyond "hokey religions"? Do Mages Awaken or are there successful spacemagicacademies admitting anyone who feels like giving wizardry a spin?

              The reason I'm asking is that a big part of every Arcanum's shtick is that they're ways of breaking through the Fallen norm. You've given some thought to how Space defies some of the point of a travel-heavy space opera, but you haven't asked what the consequences of the Time Arcanum would be on a black hole, or how the Forces Arcanum could power a spaceship by itself through several dozen different strategies (and laugh at the Kardashev scale on the way), or how xenobiology is literally trivia when the Life Arcanum is in play, or how Matter is the Arcanum of terraforming entire planets, possibly out of thin air. And that's just the Gross Arcana. To really express the Arcana, you kinda need to have a Fallen norm that they're breaking, because breaking those norms is the essence of the game.

              So I mean... what the hell is a Mage, in your setting? Is everyone a Mage, with varying skill in the ten Arcana? What niche is the Space Arcanum supposed to fill? What exactly do you want your players to be able to do? What kind of characters are they likely to try to make? What roles would they be trying to fill?

              Because what you actually might want to do is limit everyone to two dots. You can do a lot with two dots, but the reality-breaking stuff is generally not available. And if Space 3 isn't in your game in the first place, your entire question kinda becomes moot.

              (P.S., the less laborious answer I'd give is the same as Mrmdubois's. Yes, you can teleport across the galaxy because this ain't The One Ring.)

                          Originally posted by MysticJackal View Post
                          ​We took the mechanics of the mage system, not its fluff, although i'm happy to keep what works.
                          For the record, I'm not advocating that you keep the fluff. It's perfectly fine to pull out the magic system entirely; it just wasn't clear to me what you were doing.

                          Originally posted by MysticJackal View Post
                          ​I don't think we need a fallen world for the arcana to work, though I do agree we still have a normal world, so to speak, that has its laws broken by mages.
                          The reason that the Fallen World is relevant is because that's what the "Awakening" in the title means. You have to awaken from something. It's not that the Arcana can't work without the Fallen World: it's that this specific interpretation of the Arcana can't work without the Fallen World.

                          Space Mages can't teleport merely because that's just the power they have; Space Mages can teleport specifically because they have a fuller understanding of what space is, and when and why that concept doesn't apply in certain situations, specifically ones where sympathetic ties can supercede mere physical distance.

                          Is that the approach you want your in-setting Mages to take? If so, then there's no reason not to allow galaxy-spanning teleports, because distance is a Lie. If not, then you have two options: you either come up with a justification, or you don't. The latter is obviously easier, and it may be a better option for you if you're juggling a lot of other things in your universe.

                          An example justification off the top of my head would be that Space Mages are who they are because their understanding is that space is big and vast and empty, and no one matters in the face of an eternal and infinite Black, so it might not make sense for them to be doing teleportation at all; if anything, their signature power should be one of lengthening distances, not collapsing them. No matter how much fuel a spaceship burns, it never seems to get anywhere under the gaze of a Space Mage. But this kind of thing might also be nothing close to what you're looking for: that's something you and yours would have to decide for yourselves.
