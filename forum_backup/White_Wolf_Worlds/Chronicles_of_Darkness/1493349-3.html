        She's got a great smile... But, I digress. Let's talk about the Order of Sir Martin.

        In a quirk of development, all the Nosferatu bloodlines were written by one author — except for the Order of Sir Martin, who went to a different author. Originally. Unfortunately, that other author had to drop out pretty early on in production (for entirely understandable reasons), so I ended up taking on his material rather than hiring someone else; we were just a little too far in, and I'd more or less finished my own writing work, so I figured I'd save some back and forth. Originally, I was just writing the Corajosos, because, for some reason, on every single project, I fool myself into thinking I'll do a bit less writing and a bit more admin. I like writing, and (I like to think) I'm good at writing, but I find it to be a very tiring process, whereas my brain is not as taxed by art, editing, etc. Cut to me doing 5 bloodlines instead of 1.

        Anyway, this is all to say, despite the sudden new workload, I found a hook for this bloodline almost right away. I've always liked the Order of Sir Martin, and my version is ultimately not too different from the First Edition version. "Knightly Nosferatu who are so repugnant they serve as secret police" is a very fun idea, and while I think the original doesn't quite take that far enough, it was a solid basis to build new stuff. My main issue with the original was that it was a bit too on the nose; I wanted their low status in the Invictus to be a bit more complicated.

        The big change was making their advantages mostly Merit-based. Specifically, Oaths. I love Invictus Oaths. They're such an interesting mechanic, and there really isn't anything like them in any other CofD game (I need to find more reasons to do two-way, asymmetric Merits). So, it made sense to me that the Order of Sir Martin, being ostensibly knights, would have a lot of their own Oaths, and the liege/vassal structure of those effects gave me the idea of them being designated Scapegoats for the First Estate: Want to be the life of the party? Go see a Leper. Need to hide your diablerie? The Martinists will do it. Happily! All they ask is the freedom to do their work, and the lower they fall in the Invictus — the more oaths they take to be punching bags while you party in the Ivory Tower — the better they get at it. That allowed me to make their "Leper" designation more metaphorical, but also very deliberate on the bloodline's part. This also allowed me to adjust their bane, which is the only thing in the original I more or less just changed outright. Instead of, uh, dyspepsia, their Lonely Curse is worsened, and they have difficulty making Socialize actions with other Kindred. I almost had the Nos bane apply to other vampires, but A) we've done that before (with the Azerkatil, I believe) and B) I didn't want them to be too screwed socially by default considering what some of their Oaths do to them.

        The other thing I wanted to do was give them a bit more of a reason to be in other covenants, but without breaking their ties to the Invictus. I did consider just making them more mercenary, but I liked the idea of them having their own Oaths, which are actually probably the hardest covenant advantages to rip off (maybe apart from Carthian Law?) So, I gave them a loophole (see below).

        So, here's the Order of Sir Martin. I'm including a few extras in this one, as their intro is a bit short, and I think showing a little more of their mechanics will give you all a better sense of how they work.

        Tomorrow, let's take a break from the Nosferatu and check in on the Shadows: the Iltani. As always, like and subscribe consider leaving us a review if you're enjoying the book!

        Order of Sir Martin: The Ones You Blame

        "I die to serve."

        Everyone needs a fall guy.

        The Invictus was built on the idea that you can get ahead by stepping on the little people, so it should come as no surprise that this includes other members of the First Estate. Few Unconquered think twice about this fact, and fewer still would be bothered much if they did. Of course, resistance is also part of climbing that ladder. Reaching the top means fighting for it at every rung, and for each victory you earn, another Lick is waiting a few feet below to scramble past your corpse as you fall. You can’t really expect Kindred to just roll over for you.

        Ah, but that’s where you’d be wrong. The Order of Sir Martin will let you shit on them with a smile. Indeed, as one of the oldest knightly organizations of the First Estate, these Haunts are professionally obligated to take your abuse. Scapegoats in the most literal sense of the word, public derision and ostracization are this bloodline’s primary sources of strength, and with their own special Oaths, the so-called Leper Knights assume the mantle of their lieges’ sins to grow ever more powerful. To serve the Conspiracy of Silence and the Masquerade, a Martinist becomes anathema to her Kindred: Contemptible. Repugnant. Beneath notice…

        But only fools judge Lepers by their appearance rather than their reputation. Intellectually, if not emotionally, the Invictus knows the burden these knights take on is fictitious (at least in part), but the Conspiracy of Silence also knows how powerful a well-told story can be. Who would think the sanitation worker reeking of raw sewage is the deadliest and wealthiest assassin in the city? Who would suspect the shy secretary whose presence feels like absence of being the finest swordswoman in the state? And who would ever believe the ranting, pockmarked derelict who uses the stoop as a toilet is the master of the largest spy ring in the country?

        In other words, it’s not bad work if you can get it. Haunts whose curses would otherwise relegate them to the margins of the First Estate — and the All Night Society — find acceptance among the Lepers, turning their personal horror into a valuable and deadly asset. However, knighthood is a privilege, not a right. The Order only takes those Haunts it deems worthy, and just having a particularly crusty skin condition won’t cut it. Their training regimen puts the Marine Corps to shame, a physical, mental, and spiritual crucible that, at the best of times, amounts to torture. But one can’t argue with results. Leper Knights are some of the most elite secret police in the Invictus, and even those Unconquered who openly deride them are careful not to earn their wrath, or say anything incriminating in their presence.

        The Lepers know where all the bodies are buried, and they can and will dig them up when it serves their purposes, whether in defending the Masquerade or fucking with their self-proclaimed betters. Just the suggestion of their presence is enough to set a prince’s teeth on edge, and even in domains where the Order has no official presence, it can exert a surprising amount of influence — after all, how would you ever know we’re here?

        Why you want to be us

        Because no one else wants you. No one can see the strength behind the squirming skin or the jet-black eyes. They call you a leper already, so why not become one in truth? There’s power in being at rock bottom; that’s where everyone winds up in the end, and you can be there first, mouth wide open.

        Why you should fear us

        We can’t sink any lower than this, and if you don’t think that makes us dangerous, our blades will make quick work of your ignorance. Remember: The rules don’t apply to us — but they do to you.

        Why we should fear ourselves

        The problem with being everyone’s punching bag is that your start buying into the press. Maybe you really are only good for killing and creeping. Maybe you really are just a worm who managed to wriggle ahead of all the other worms. What you think you are is far less important than what everyone else believes.

        Bloodline Origins
        • When the Crusades came to the Holy Land, the violence awakened a Nosferatu known as Martinus Leprosus, a legionnaire of Rome who was killed and Embraced into the Army of the Damned during Titus’s siege of Jerusalem. An outcast among his Kindred for both his allegiance and the awful blight that covered his skin, Martinus believed survival meant being too disgusting for anyone to look too closely at what you’re doing. The ancient liked the invaders, for they reminded him of his nights as a soldier, and he began to use them as a herd when they slaughtered most of the local populace. He learned much from the pilgrims, and he soon Embraced a handful so they might teach him of modern, holy warfare. Taking on the titles and rites of a Catholic military order, “Sir Martin the Leper” and his knights began a campaign of slaughter on behalf of any who could pay their price, spreading their philosophy of power through repugnance to any Haunts who would listen. Eventually, the Invictus proved to be their most reliable customer, and it’s been a lucrative hate–hate relationship ever since.
        • The legend of Sir Martin is just that: a legend. The first Leper Knights belonged to a powerful faction of Invictus Nosferatu that controlled vast domains in the Levant during the Crusades. Fearing their growing influence, the First Estate tricked them into a massacre, using mercenaries to decimate their numbers. Humiliated and chastened in defeat, they were forced to swear new Oaths, ones that guaranteed they would never again claim prestige or power in the Invictus. However, even in defeat, these Haunts knew they’d only lost the battle, not the war. They crafted the story of Sir Martin as a rallying cry for other Nosferatu, a way to teach them to revel in their repulsiveness so the Establishment won’t ever realize what they’re really up to.
        • Once upon a midnight darkly, a pious knight-errant searched the whole world over for the Holy Grail. He fought many battles and rescued many maids, but none brought him any closer to his prize. Eventually, he came upon a loathly lady, who told the knight she knew the secret of the Grail, but that she would not reveal it unless he married her. The gallant agreed, and on their wedding night, he asked the hag to fulfill their bargain. In a blinding light, she transformed into a beauty without peer, revealing she was the holy prize he sought. However, he could only keep her if he passed a final test: to choose whether she should be comely in the day and hideous in the night or lovely in the moonlight and frightful in the sun. The knight replied that she should choose for herself, for a wife should always have her sovereynté. Oh, how his lady smiled! And so, she chose to be beautiful day and night, and with the sacred blood from her bosom, she fed her loyal knight all her hideousness. Thus he serves his wife and sovereign still — loathsomely ever after.

        Parent Clan: Nosferatu

        Nicknames: Leper Knights, Lepers, Scapegoats, Martinists

        Bloodline Bane (The Blighted Curse): They ain’t called lepers for nothing. Whatever a Scapegoat’s flaw before her becoming, it magnifies in Sir Martin’s blood: A Leper Knight treats her Humanity as an additional dot lower for the purposes of the Lonely Curse. In addition, Socialize actions with other Kindred [REDACTED]. The latter effect does not apply to other Lepers, nor to vampires who know the Scapegoat very well, such as coterie-mates, mentors, retainers, etc.

        Disciplines: Nightmare, Obfuscate, Resilience, Vigor

        New Invictus Oaths

        The following are Oaths available only to Leper Knights. Squires must swear the Oath of the Scapegoat (p. 81) to join the Order, and if they ever break it, all other Martinist Oaths are considered null and void. Any Notary can preside over these Oaths, and any member of the Invictus can act as the liege unless otherwise noted, but only a Scapegoat can be the vassal.

        In addition, due to the Order’s rigorous training, Scapegoats who meet the prerequisites gain a dot of Kindred Dueling for free. If she has five dots already, she takes an Experience instead.

        Note that not all Leper Knights are Nosferatu. While rare, Invictus members of any clan can join the Order of Sir Martin if they can prove themselves, though only Haunts can be inducted into the associated bloodline, which tends to occupy a place of privilege, such as it is.

        Oath of the Oprichnik (••)

        Prerequisite: Obfuscate •

        Effect: Even beyond their supernatural repugnance, the Order of Sir Martin is hated and feared for its secret police services, and this Oath is the source of that reputation. With the Oath of the Oprichnik, the vassal swears to protect the Silence even at the cost of her identity.

        A high official of the Invictus (often a prince) serves as liege, representing a kind of stand-in for the Masquerade itself. The vampire becomes no one, gaining the Forgotten Condition. As long as this Condition lasts, she gains the rote quality on Perception, and any Investigation actions to track Masquerade breaches exceptionally succeed on three successes instead of five. Finally, since she is considered to have Face in the Crowd active at all times, she can treat Cloak of Night and The Familiar Stranger reflexive actions, as well as any other non-instant effects that rely on it.

        It’s very rare for Leper Knights to swear this Oath for longer than a few weeks at the very most, or for the duration of a particular investigation, as such intense isolation invariably leads to… issues.

        Forgotten (Persistent)

        Oath of the Scapegoat (• to •••••)

        Effect: The vassal becomes a member of the Order of Sir Martin, with the Order itself serving as her feudal liege. This Oath is always administered as a Nosferatu is inducted into the bloodline.

        Oath of the Scapegoat effectively serves as Kindred Status for the bloodline and its knightly order. A character who swears it loses any Invictus Status (Sanctity of Merit apples), but her player can use dots in this Oath to purchase benefits that scale with Invictus Status or have it as a prerequisite.

        For each dot in this Merit, the character suffers a penalty on all actions with Invictus members where Covenant Status would normally apply, but she also [REDACTED GOOD THING].

        This Oath’s duration is indefinite, but it breaks if the Leper ever gains a dot of Invictus Status.

        Oath of the Scapegoat is an exception to the drawback of the Oath of Penance (Vampire, p. 117); it cannot be obviated in any way by normal Invictus Oaths.

        Drawback: Rank is a commodity among the Lepers. Each time you want to increase your dots in this Oath, your character must have it renewed by a Notary with the permission of a superior in the Order.

                    Alright, let's have a look at the Iltani.

                    This is one of the rare Mekhet bloodlines I didn't know that much about before we got writing. I think that may be because they have one of the sparser write-ups (only two pages), with most of their word count going toward their backstory (which is elaborated in Ancient Mysteries), followed by a long section on Mérges Sorcery. Ultimately, they end up coming off as a riff on the Banu Haqim from Vampire: The Masquerade. There's not much to the original Iltani, so the big goal here was to elevate their concept beyond that of poisonous assassins, which was especially tricky given that that's what the Brothers of Ypres are. The Trenchers are slightly different poisonous assassins, but they're also much more interesting poisonous assassins.

                    This is all to say that I think the author really nailed this one. The Iltani in Second Edition aren't assassins so much as "cleaners" for the gods. They aid the Kindred by guiding them to following the laws of the universe — the fundamental strictures of civilization — thus preventing creatures like the Strix and... other... agents of anarchy from taking advantage of any moral hiccups. They hesitate to kill, but when they have to, they're well equipped.

                    Which brings us to Mérges Sorcery. In the original, this power was essentially Theban Sorcery with a poison twist. It's also the only case where a 1E bloodline had access to a form of sorcery that wasn't part of their Discipline spread, which is not important but I find interesting (I have a theory about that, but it's just wild guessing). Anyway, it's got some interesting enough powers, but we ultimately felt it was too narrow in concept for a Ritual Discipline, especially because I have a possibly-too-rigid rule about including new sorcery Disciplines in books: they've got to have 10 rituals. The way I see it, the whole point of a Ritual Discipline is that you have different options at each level, and to my mind, you have to have at least two for each dot to have any sort of meaningful choice.

                    As you might imagine, that rule broke pretty hard against Mérges Sorcery. There are only so many ways to do poison, and ten was just too many. I believe I had the suggestion of turning it into a set of special rituals for Crúac or Theban Sorcery, but that also lacked the flavor of the original bloodline. So, the author decided to turn Mérges Sorcery into Mérges Admixture, a Merit, giving the bloodline Protean to represent that "Viper" aspect. in hindsight, I wish I'd thought to give them some snakey/spidery Protean powers in dev, but you can ultimately repurpose the ones in the core or Wild Hunt.

                    The other big change was their bane. The original is actually not too bad, but it feels like something that would come up just a bit too often to be fun. Despite wanting them to be a less like the Banu Haqim, I actually think the bane they ended up with suits them really well.

                    Not too much changed in development; I fussed with the Merit a bit, and I added another one (the animal ghoul one), and I swapped around their bloodline gift a bit to tie into their emotional removal a bit more strongly. The biggest changes I made were to the Edimmu, their associated horror; mostly that was just simplifying them a bit, though.

                    Anyway, I could honestly go on about how great I think this update is and all the concepts that went into it, but I know the author lurks on the forums, and they had some really interesting background on the mythological and cultural elements this new version draws upon, so they're free to add some context to what I've said here.

                    Anyway, here are the Iltani! As noted in an earlier post there's a discrepancy with the Discipline spread, which will be corrected in the errata. I also noticed their bane is slightly different from what I had in my final dev file, and combined with the Discipline mistake (and a couple of other discrepancies), I suspect that means I copied the wrong file somewhere along the line. So, that will also be fixed, but you'll have to wait for me to get through the previews, as those are helpful for me to catch typos and such. The bane below is the correct version.

                    Tomorrow, I'll be back with the Shepherds!

                    Iltani: The Ones Who Despise You

                    “Don’t waste your words on me. I’ve seen what’s inside you.”

                    Hatred is so stigmatized. We’re taught to fear our anger and swallow it down from the time we’re children. The truth is, hatred is a natural part of the human condition, and what’s natural for humans is natural for vampires. As fortune would have it, the Kindred are especially attuned to hate.

                    In Sumerian theology, the gods gifted humans with the me, the qualities necessary for civilization. More than just the ability to create architecture or laws, the me drove conflict: enmity, warfare, and fear. Without these, humans would never be forced to confront that which needs to change. They would lie indolent in the fields, doing only what was necessary to survive and provide comfort. Strife, too, is one of the me, and this is what the Iltani embody. Anger is useful. You just have to point it in the right direction, whether your own or someone else’s. So go ahead. Give in — just this once. It’s in your blood, and there’s so much to rage against.

                    The Iltani have always lurked at the corner of civilization’s visions, and they claim to have been born at its very dawn. In the shadows of Babylon’s walls, most lurked as ashipu, gods-blessed physicians who could banish evil spirits through magical concoctions. Rather than proper physicians one might visit in a temple, they were a last resort whose names and deeds were spread through rumor, and rather than the medicinal herbs a mortal ashipu might use, they used the powers of the Blood. They’ve gone more underground since then, but spirits haven’t gone anywhere in the past few millennia. Nothing will save you when one comes to pay a visit, and there are some that are very, very interested in the Kindred.

                    This necessitates keeping up certain standards of behavior, and the Iltani expect their fellow Licks to do the same. Most of these protocols are enshrined within the modern-day Traditions, but one outweighs all others in terms of importance: There is no sin greater than the Amaranth. It opens the soul to things that should not be there. Alu. Edimmu. Strix. The Widows know just how dangerous the arrival of any one of these evil spirits might be, even if others don’t. Best to never risk their appearance. Unwise Kindred must be guided on the proper path and become the right kind of monster.

                    This has given them a slightly unwarranted reputation as venomous alchemists and dark sorcerers. They’re happy to let these rumors remain in place, however; it helps that the distinction between magical practice and medicine was blurred in their earliest nights, when they worked as ashipu. Despite their repute, Vipers are unusually hesitant to kill, given that corpses tend not to make useful tools of the me. For the Iltani, frenzy and hatred have swallowed deep into their being and curdled into a very literal toxin. This means they have a shockingly long fuse for a bloodline so fixated on the power of hatred and fear. Falling to frenzy without being able to control it is a serious sign of weakness.

                    It would be a mistake to think of the Widows as anything approaching merciful, though. Like most Shadows, they prefer not to get their hands dirty. They have ways of making sure their targets’ sins are exposed, and those who can’t channel their negative impulses for a righteous cause deserve whatever punishment’s coming to them.

                    Ultimately, just because the me are no longer inscribed on clay tablets for people to read doesn’t mean they’re not necessary. The veneer has changed — different faces, different names, different words — but the human mind remains the same, and the gifts the gods gave humanity abide. So do the Iltani. Younger Widows may no longer adhere to a specific list, and they might not teach it as a divine imperative, but the general philosophy remains. The modern concept of dialectics has provided a useful framework to explain and justify it, and this allows them to root out certain weaknesses and vices within the Kindred. They say there are terrible forces that might seize hold of the All Night Society. Civilization is built on certain cruelties… yet the alternative is far worse. But they would say that, wouldn’t they?

                    Why you want to be us

                    Absolute control is within your grasp, and that’s a rare thing for any Kindred: control over your anger, over others, over the very physical nature of your being. You just have to reach out and take it.

                    Why you should fear us

                    But you, of course, are not so refined. It’s so easy to push your buttons. Your weaknesses are written so clearly on your face and in your every action. And if you can’t keep a grip on yourself? We’ll be there.

                    Why we should fear ourselves

                    The truth is, absolute control is impossible. Human psychologists have managed to piece together that trying to bury your emotions is a lost cause. They’re still there, and no matter how much your own twists of the blood alchemize them, you’ll never, ever completely cage the Beast. And when you fail, and you’re drained dry, there’s something else that will step in to take your place.

                    Parent Clan: Mekhet

                    Nicknames: Vipers, Widows

                    Bloodline Bane (The Hemolytic Curse): The hateful blood of the Iltani does not take well to intrusion. If a Viper ingests Kindred Vitae, whether willingly or otherwise, she suffers a bashing damage per point. Furthermore, the tendency of Mekhet blood to draw in strange secrets and occultic qualities exerts itself, and the Widow gains one acquired bane that the vampire she drank possesses until it inconveniences or harms her, at which point, it fades. This bane does not apply to drinking the Vitae of other Vipers.

                    Disciplines: Animalism, Auspex, Obfuscate, Protean

                    In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

                    One Thousand and One Nightmares — 832 CE (DE2, p. 124): The Iltani no longer hold a vice-grip over Middle Eastern civilization as their elders claim they once did, and to make matters worse, the secrets of the All Night Society leak out through the visions of mere mortals, and the Vipers’ dreams are filled with fire coming to the domains of the Damned. It’s clear they need to emerge from the shadows at last. They form strange alliances with the children of nightmares but fear that this might be the end of everything that the Iltani and lesser Kindred have fought for. Elders prepare for the worst in a massive upheaval that may tear away the Masquerade.

                    Bloodline Gift: Focused Hatred

                    An Iltani can focus her poisonous hate into a kind of Zen state to aid in her work. Once per night, when facing a provocation to rage frenzy, a Viper can spend a Willpower to take the Stoic Condition. She must still resist frenzy, but she immediately benefits from the effect of Stoic. Some Iltani only use this ability when facing dire situations, while others deliberately provoke themselves in order to attain calm.

                    Stoic

                    Your emotions turn as cold as your dead heart and lifeless veins, and you find it difficult to open up or show any emotion at all. Gain a +2 on rolls to hide your emotions or resist rage frenzy, and do not take the Tempted Condition on a normal success. However, your Humanity is treated as though it were two dots lower for the purposes of relating to mortals. This Condition fades without resolving at the end of the night.
                    Resolution: Fall to frenzy.

                    New Merit: Mérges Admixture (• to •••••)

                    Prerequisites: Iltani, Medicine ••, Safe Place •

                    Effect: The line between magic and medicine was often blurred in ancient Mesopotamia. Some illnesses were said to be caused by evil spirits, and exorcisms were necessary to banish the offending creature. When the Iltani were still young, they practiced medicine following this principle, as though it were a religious ritual, and the end result is that their gift for alchemizing poisons is often mistaken for a primitive form of blood sorcery. While this isn’t true — it’s a natural process of their bodies rather than witchcraft — but it’s a misconception the Iltani have let persist to keep up a more sinister reputation.

                    The creation of a Mérges Admixture (from a Sumerian word meaning anger or poisonous) requires alchemizing the vampire’s own Vitae. Many perform ritualistic actions as their blood transmutes to keep up traditions, whether it be invoking long-lost gods, consuming herbs they claim to have magical qualities, or making a sacrificial offering. The creation of a venom inflicts one level of bashing damage on the vampire per turn it takes to achieve the target successes. Unless noted otherwise, the venom must be injected to work. Nothing prevents the vampire from coating her own fangs to use them as makeshift syringes, but most Widows prefer to have greater control over when and where their venom is used.

                    However, it’s not unheard of for Iltani to turn their own poisons on themselves. Most have certain benefits, at a steep cost, but desperate times call for desperate measures, especially when facing off against their ancestral enemies.

                                        And now that I remember my password...

                                        That one is mine! Yay! While I would have loved to bring in some connections to Mummy 2e, unfortunately I was already leaning in hard on it with one of my other bloodlines and I was worried about things getting same-y. Also, my regular gaming group would have teased me for being predictable given Mummy is one of my favorite games and I love crossover-friendly content.

                                        Quick crosspost of some things I said on another forum and my Twitter for posterity:

                                        I'm a religious studies major (recently graduated as of a few months ago so I'm now allowed to call myself one, I guess) and by sheer coincidence my comparative mythology class did a unit on Mesopotamian (including Babylonian and Akkadian) mythology which is where about 80% of the inspiration for this came from. I'm pretty sure you could audibly hear the gears starting to grind back to life in my brain once I read that "strife" was one of the several dozen. The me are very dialectical in nature, and the concept of traits that we almost always think of as negative being a positive thing that helps with building civilization felt like it'd fit in with their M.O. really well. As for their practices, evil spirits are a major presence in Mesopotamian and Babylonian mythology...not to say that they aren't in most cultures, but as my professor put it, in ancient Mesopotamia your physician was likely your priest. Ailments were often blamed on wicked spirits. I decided to tie that back into the Iltani being less practitioners of magic and more early chemists and alchemists. And I like it when there's some flexibility with powers so I made it so that they have certain niche uses if you use them on yourself if you're willing to accept the drawbacks.

                                        Let's see, what else. Their earliest draft had them taking Vile Blood from Guide to the Night as a free Devotion. That got ditched because we didn't want to force people to buy non-core books, and honestly it wasn't as compelling as something wholly customized anyways. If anyone decides to use them, though, I'd say it'd be fair to allow them to buy it at a reduced cost, it is very them.

                                        And a side note entirely unrelated to my design process: their first creation myth does go back into an actual myth, the Atra-Hasis. According to that the gods created humans to tend to the land. I was having a fun time with reading Mesopotamian mythology so I kept going and decided to bring in one of my favorite mythological chimeras, the scorpion-men. The third one...well, all I can say is I can never resist a crossover concept.

                                        But yeah, the original one was a little sparse on the ground so I kind of just went wild with it. I'm happy it came together like it did! :> It probably took the longest out of all of mine but I think it paid off.

                                            Iltani, those guys really hit on memory lane. Curiously, the one time i used them was not as Mekhet but a Akhud bloodline instead (along with the branches of the Betrayed from VII).
