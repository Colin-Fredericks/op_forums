                        Depends on whose name is stamped on the living space.

                        It's probably preferable to live under the Corpus than the Grineer, if only because most people end up working for one of their component businesses. Yes, being a Crewman often sucks, but the Corpus is nothing if not pragmatic; I suspect they treat their actual employees fairly well, because a happy employee is a productive employee, a productive employee is more efficient, efficiency helps profit, hence why the Corpus Crewmen get vacation days according to this video. If you're one of their sweatshop workers or it becomes profitable to hang you out to dry, well, them's the breaks, but at least you have a fair shot at getting back into the relatively good life if you become something worthwhile to treat well again. It's still mostly a relative thing though, as the other major power option is the Grineer, who are everything wrong with xenophobia turned up to 30 and then multiplied by the ability to clone "purity" so as to never sully their lineage with other genomes, caught in a death spiral they may or may not be aware of. For you, the one benefit is you won't be killed by Tenno or Syndicates intentionally, otherwise you get to be kicked around by mutant cyborgs all damn day to do the various labors they haven't gotten around to decanting their own workers for on pain of death or worse. The one escape from that life is being able to sneak onto refugee camps in Syndicate or Corpus territory, where life is hard and bitter, but at least your new overlords don't actively begrudge the fact they can't kill you yet. Corpus engineers may even like your Grineer-brand skills at repair and refurbishment.

                        Preferably, you'd want to live under the aegis of Syndicates, who are generally messed up from the mercenary perspective of Tenno, but messed up in ways recognizable as human. New Loka and Red Veil are probably the worst to live under but again, it's relative. New Loka just doesn't like transhumanists and probably doesn't quibble with fixing genetic diseases to go back to a purer human form (I doubt they actually quibble with longevity after having lived with that culture for so long), and may actually have a way to fix Grinner decay via introduction of healthier DNA from other sources (possibly why they look askance at Steel Meridian, who are fine with being mutant cyborgs, thank you), and Red Veil is an army of anarchists with a gleefully no-holds-barred approach to warfare, but they're at least ethical enough to get Steel Meridian (an organisation dedicated to protecting refugees, when it comes down to it) on their side.

                                            Werewolves
                                            The Uratha were regarded as little more than beasts in the time of the Orokin, who claimed the Moon for their seat of power. At best they were subhumans who were afflicted by illness, at worst, the werewolves pests to be forced out or put into cages.
                                            With the Moon and its spirits buried under golden spires and gleaming archetecture and the Gauntlet being kept under control via mass use of spacewarping technologies, the distinction between the Pure and the Forsaken eroded to nothing. How could the ancient vendettas be maintained when Lunes rarely showed themselves, when Auspices could no longer be given, and when the Firstborn were bound to an Earth that was declared offlimits to Father Wolf’s Children?

                                            They were all forced to survive via whatever means they could, to bind and bargain with spirits that had never heard of their Father, their Mother, or even the Firstborn. Centuries of Orokin dominance had broken and destroyed much of the Urathan culture, for few ever lived so long to convey the stories to the younger generation. Many went their whole lives knowing nothing but what the Orokin said of them or even heading into the spirit world that they did not know and for anyone in such a time, said lives could be quite long.
                                            Ironically, it was the loss of the Moon that led to the ressurenge for the people. Without masters clad in gold ruling above them, Earth became accessible once more and werewolves came to find themselves some place someplace very different.
                                            The Orokin tended to Old Earth like it was a garden, away from the hands of human intervention to despoil it. When they fell, they left behind a world that harkened back to the Predator’s Paradise that was lost so long ago, a world that ran wild and free. With the Luna lost to parts unknown and the Void technologies used to artificially prevent the Gauntlet from forming weakpoints no longer maintained, the Firstborn, free to step into the physical world for the first time in an untold number of years, hunted as a single pack for the first time in since the Death of Urfarah. They took their wayward and unknowing fleshy cousins and showed them much of what they forgot and the werewolves finally found their place to belong.
                                            Since then, the powers across the Origin system have tried to take over the New Pangea, to subdue the forces that lay claim to it. Most especially, the diseased and unclean descendants of the Orokin servant caste, the Grineer. Under the leadership of Councilor Vey Hek, the armies and fleets above the planet sought to claim it for themselves.
                                            The planet might now have genetically engineered creatures populating every corner of it and normal wolves are a dying breed on the world, but to the werewolves, it is still their home. The Tribes of Urfarah rise to defend it, as outnumbered as they may be.

                                            The Moon’s Return.
                                            In recent memory, the most ominous event to happen to the People as of late, is without a doubt the Moon returning from an extended stay in the Void. When it appeared, the People saw the face of one of their progenitors for the first time in an eon, a damaged and warped planetoid that had been marred by the Orokin and damaged further by its stay in the Void.
                                            Lunes appeared in the New Pangea, bringing with them a power thought long lost. Though Mother Luna has remained silent and confusing as ever, the power known as the Auspices were bestowed to her children all over the Solar System… even those that belong to what were once the Pure Tribes.
                                            This has created a bit of a schism within the Tribes of Urfarah, about whether or not they should accept this power, about whether or not that the old blood vendettas that lied long dormant should begin again. Even the Firstborn seem torn over this, as ultimately, Pangea was restored, the very reason their conflict started in the first place.
                                            The Moon does not speak, only watches.






                                            Werewolves in the Origin Systeml
                                            Werewolves continue their hunt. Their prey has changed to include the successor races that came from the old humankind.

                                            Grineer: The Despoilers of New Pangea are not to be tolerated. They have no claim to their restored homeworld. Though many werewolves can shred even the heaviest Bombard with their claws, they are still massively outnumbered. There are rumors though of Werewolves who had lived their whole lives as one of the Diseased, only to become something pure when the First Change was visited upon him. If this has indeed happened, they do not speak of it.
                                            Corpus: Werewolves rarely care about the Corpus, as they stick largely to the far reaches of the solar system, more than far enough away for most werewolves to care. Though there is hints that the Orokin’s successors might have information… or even werewolves in their employ or higher ranks, forming in essence, a Ninth Tribe.
                                            Infested: The Disease is the second greatest threat to New Pangea after the Grineer as far as the werewolves are concern. It violates mind, body, and place, and seeks to consume all life into itself to feed its twisted hunger. The werewolves believe the bio-weapon to actually be the makings of one of their ancient foes, the Idigam, for the only thing more horifing than what the disease does to flesh is the terror of its shadow.
                                            Tenno: To the People, the Tenno are the only ones left in the Origin System that know Honor. It was they who slew the Orokin, and it is they that hold back the Grineer. Those are the things most werewolves care about when it comes to them. Though, there’s more than a few packs that have decided to select the deathless slayers as their favored prey, seeking to test themselves for reason that are difficult to define.

                                                        Tenno
                                                        "I am the Lotus. I will guide you, but we must hurry. Vor is coming for you. I see the cryosleep has taken your memory. It does not matter. You are Tenno. I will prepare you." ~The Lotus

                                                        There are many monsters that walked alongside humankind over the countless ages, living in the shadows where man is afraid to go. When the Orokin rose to power, they put an end to this, exploring and choosing to exploit the boundless human potential without fear of the dark. When the War broke out, this Dream ended. The Orokin were forced to return to their home system, to fortify their homelands against traitorous machines.
                                                        In the Orokin Empire’s darkest hour, those in charge turned towards the twisted and unnatural. They looked towards the a ship full of children, the survivors from the only ship ever to return from a Void jump failure, the Zarimon. Each child was touched by the Void, manifesting powers that defied the natural laws of the universe, a perfect weapon to use against creatures that were engineered to have the Void as a weakness. They gave the survivors of the Ten-0 incident weapons and armor to fight and die for the Orokin; they made their own monsters.
                                                        And it worked.
                                                        The Sentients were being forced back, the War was not over yet. For a time, long enough for these warriors clad in armor to develop their own subculture, it seemed like the Orokin were about to win. They began talking about how to best dispose of their would-be saviors, how to keep them away.
                                                        And yet, in the final chapters of the Old War, the Orokin were still defeated, by their own creation. The Tenno betrayed them, destroyed their creators for reasons of their own devising. Perhaps they came to realize it was not themselves that were the monsters. Perhaps the Orokin did not realize the entity known as the Lotus that came preside over the Tenno.
                                                        The Tenno then faded into the dark like all the other creatures that lurked in the shadow, their exploits and power becoming a thing of Myth.


                                                        Second Dream
                                                        When awakened from the Cryosleep, it was as though the Origin System itself shook. Each warrior seemingly unkillable and with veterans equipped with the greatest technology the Orokin could devise. It is not unheard of for entire bases to go missing since every soldier on board was slain down to the last man.

                                                        Tenno do not often operate their Warframes directly, instead remotely controlling their equipment via a method known only as Transference, in effect, using the Warframe as one would use their own bodies. Transference however requires special equipment, but most Tenno do not leave their Orbiters. And should a Warframe be destroyed? The Orbiter is often able to just teleport the defeated Warframe and rebuild it back to the Oribiter.
                                                        However, there are times when a Tenno may seek to leave the isolation of their own personal pocket dimensions. Should they do, they give up the majority of their protections… however it is the best way to find companionship… or to handle matters in a way that the sword alone may not.

                                                        Most Tenno are of the same apparent age as they were when the Ten-0 Incident occured, largely the result of excessively long lives and being in stasis pods for most of their existence, but due to errors, deliberate choice, and other factors, Tenno can often appear to be significantly older than the norm.


                                                        Tenno and the Supernatural
                                                        The Tenno are the survivors and elite warriors of the Old War.

                                                        Vampires: The Tenno are wary of the living dead, and are willing to strike against them should they ever become a threat as great as the Grineer or the Corpus. But for now, they are underwatch.
                                                        Werewolves: The Tenno view these predators with some very obvious distrust, but they understand that these “people” are unlikely to ever achieve the same destructive capacity as the other races in the solar system due to in large part of how small their populations are and their relatively slow growth. They do however realize these creatures play some role in the balance of a world that the Orokin never quite fully masters.
                                                        Mages: Dangerous not because of the power they wield, but the fact they keep asking questions too much. The Tenno’s Orbiter compartments are shielded enough to prevent a Mage from harming a Tenno through his Warframe, but there is still a fear they will know too much.
                                                        Changelings: Far too small and rare to be of any relevance, but every now and again, the Tenno find themselves in strange thorny wildlands.
                                                        Prometheans: Dangerous and threatening madness like some sort of plague, but minor in comparison to other creatures due to massively smaller populations.
                                                        Giest: The Sineaters often stalk the Tenno, very sensitive to the sheer amount of killing a single person is capable of.
                                                        Mummy: These ancients find and odd sympathy with the Tenno, for like themselves, the Tenno seem deathless, returning and destroying all that is asked of them by a higher power. In a strange way, the Tenno seeming in a way acting their successors is not unexpected, after all, every civilization harkened back to old Irem, copying things imperfectly from then… it was only a matter of time before the Deathless themselves were iterated upon.
                                                        Demon: Perhaps the only people other than a select few to know the truth about the Tenno, several demons having lived long enough to have seen the Tenno when they were first created and having insider knowledge. Demons are very interested in using the Tenno as the Orokin did, as weapons to destroy their enemies.
