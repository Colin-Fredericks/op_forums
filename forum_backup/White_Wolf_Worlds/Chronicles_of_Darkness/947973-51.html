                                "Being in a Twilight state does not protect an angel from aetheric resonance, but demons hidden by Cover and angels masked by Infrastructure are not immediately revealed."
                                p.184

                                Since angels in Covers are masked by Infrastructure (it's how angelic Covers are applied), it's pretty explicit that it protects against being detected through aetheric resonance.
                                Just don't forget the very next sentence: "This defense lapses only briefly when they expend Aether or Essence."


                                Afaik angels are never mentioned to have an equivalent to the spoofing ability. That means they have zero defence against Aura Sight even when in Cover. The one saving grace is that Aura Sight requires the demon to ask the right questions.
                                Or you could assume that angelic Covers have all the advantages of demonic Covers, in which case angels get to roll their Cover rating as an attempt to Spoof. It really just depends on how you run angelic Covers since they're utterly lacking in rules beyond angel-jacking.

                                    Originally posted by Ur-Than View Post
                                    How do you use Integrator Demons NPC in your chronicles ? I don't mean Integrator players because I think those are very much in need of said player and not just the Chronicler to develop. But Integrators ? In any other game of the line, they'd probably be the opponents of the other kind of Demons playable, but here we have Angels and Exiles who are clearly a step above them.

                                    I feel like it'd be easy to make entirely wrong (or right!) and I think it'd defeat the purpose of Demons and the Integrators themselves...
                                    I think it helps to establish what sort of Integrator the NPC is. They Fell for a reason, and they've got a reason for not just turning themselves in to the nearest angels to get recycled. Do they think there's something wrong with the G-M that can only be fixed from outside? Do they think that they've still got some kind of godly mission, even as demons? Are they trying to bargain their way back in, on their own terms, with their selfhood intact?

                                    An NPC who's a known Integrator could make a good red herring, drawing the ring's suspicion away from an actual threat; an Integrator NPC who's trying to hide their true Agenda from the PCs makes an even better one. They're not all double agents or fifth columnists, nor should they be, but the possibility amps up the usual conflict between demons of different Agendas. The PCs want to destroy some Infrastructure, a rival ring wants to preserve it and suborn it instead - that's a common set-up. If the other ring is mostly Integrators, though, their long-term goal with the Infrastructure might be a bigger threat, or there's a possibility that they're being duped by the G-M into defending the site on Its behalf.

                                    In my campaign, I've just added an Guardian Integrator NPC, though she's very, very recently Fallen. She's currently acting as a wild card for the PCs, because they're honestly not sure what she's going to do next (and frankly, neither is she) - and her interest in Integration means things like "sell out everyone so the GM loves me again" isn't off the table. (She's also got a sulky teenager for a Cover, so that's fun to portray.)
