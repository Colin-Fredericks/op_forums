We're long past playing werewolves or vampires, but have fond memories of the system, so we were trying it in a generic, modern special ops missions kinda of sessions, just to get a grip on the rules, and how combat works (we're also gun nuts, so we do care a lot about combat).

    - Are there rules for minigun in any rulebook or suplement? Yes, I know that it is overkill, but the characters called for backup, and the support chopper was supposed to have one. I changed to a HMG, but it got me curious;
    - Defense and firearms attack - we got it the first time: page 88 is unforgiven - "Defense does not apply against Firearms attacks.", but what about spending a point of Willpower to get +2 defense, does it helps against firearms attacks? It don't make sense if page 88 states that defense does not apply against Firearms attacks!
    - Also, about Defense, Dodge is statistically worse than common defense, as you're exchanging "automatic success" equals to your defense for a Dice Pool totalling Defense x2, as your standard chance of success in each die is 30%, you, in reallity, are spending an action to actually lower your defenses by 40%! Am I wrong? Are there any modifiers that I am not considering?

    Thanks for your patience, and sorry for the long post.

          9 Again does count against Dodging. Just to clarify a thing: It's 8 to 10 that counts as successes, not 7.
          It does make more sense to only apply it to damage since 9 Again in this case is about increasing damage without accuracy, but that's extra bookkeeping that can slow down the game. But you do what is best for your group.

          - Defense and firearms attack - we got it the first time: page 88 is unforgiven - "Defense does not apply against Firearms attacks.", but what about spending a point of Willpower to get +2 defense, does it helps against firearms attacks?
          No. It increases your existing Defense, which does not apply against ranged attacks.

          And what about page 90, Firearms in Close Combat, which states that the target's Defense score is increased by the attacker's gun's size+1? It don't make sense if page 88 states that defense does not apply against Firearms attacks!
          Point blank is an exception to not applying Defense. If you're in hand-to-hand range you can try to manoeuvre yourself in a way that you can't for ranged attacks (other than just dropping prone). Note that the book only says "ranged" rather than Firearms.

          - Also, about Defense, Dodge is statistically worse than common defense, as you're exchanging "automatic success" equals to your defense for a Dice Pool totalling Defense x2, as your standard chance of success in each die is 30%, you, in reallity, are spending an action to actually lower your defenses by 40%! Am I wrong? Are there any modifiers that I am not considering?
          I think I see the mistake. Defense doesn't remove successes. Defense removes dice from the dice pool before it's rolled.

          3 Defense reduces the opponent's dice pool by 3, which is on average 1 success. If you instead Dodge and roll 6 dice, you'll get on average 2 successes, and cancel 2 of the opponent's successes. Dodging actually doubles the effectiveness of your Defense.

              Tessie wrapped things up pretty well, so just to add something I observed:

              Originally posted by vitorrossi View Post
              - Some weapons are listed as having "Attack rolls gain the 9-again quality" - this is for the entire attack roll, or just for damage sake? Let me explain: if Russo shots a thug with a shotgun, rolls 6 dice, getting 1,4,5,6,5 and 9, which is re-rolled, resulting in 7, but the thug rolls for dodge, getting 1 success, Russo hits the thug or not? We interpreted that in those cases, the extra success were just for damage, so the thugs ins unscathed, but, if he insted could not dodge, Russo would do 3 (shotgun) plus 2 (successes) lethal damage;
              9-Again applies to the roll normally, not just being restricted to damage, but in your particular cited example, 9-Again did nothing. 8, 9, and 10 are rolled successes in CofD. 7 isn't.

              This points to two things that can be missed easily in CofD:
              1) 9-Again isn't as useful as it seems at first glance. It never turns an uncontested failed roll into a successful one, and its yield is very limited unless your dice pool is already rather large.
              2) 8 is a high target number to meet, meaning there remain fair odds even a large dice pool will fail. This is why, unlike the older World of Darkness games, CofD doesn't measure gradations of success beyond dramatic failure, regular failure, regular success, and exceptional success. An uncontested roll with even a single rolled success should be treated as a normal success, and not a partial or limited one, because this is not an uncommon result even for skilled specialists.
