    Originally posted by raygungoth View Post
    Men in black mythology dates back to the 1940s. Slenderman mythology retroactively includes German and Russian mythology and is very heavily tied into occult circles - I know people who think the damn thing is real and its appearance on the internet was caused by psychic backlash of said entity trying to create itself backwards through the past.

    Modern mythology makes heavy use of the astral plane, vampirism, witchcraft, werewolves, and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Lilly#Solid_State_Intelligence"]god-machine like entities[/URL]. If you're going to bring in the MKULTRA experiments, DMT, the New Age movement, and so on, those things are already there - in spades.

    You do know reptilian alien mythology also incorporates goddamn SATAN, alternate psychic dimensions, vampires, and basically the entire content of Mage: the Awakening, right?

    You are aware that the modern New Age shamanism movements that include alien greys and blues and nordics also includes m-shifting and p-shifting components of werewolf mythology, right?

    You know that Demon: the Descent and Changeling: the Lost are essentially Modern UFO Lore: the RPG with almost zero fiddling required, right?

    I'm trying to ask where the damn line is, for crying out loud. - Be more specific than just a few examples. What's in, what's out? What is it about vampires that puts them outside your ideas (I can stat The Thing as a Gangrel with just about 10xp)? What is it about Mage: the Awakening that differentiates it from a psychic out of modern mythology (I'll give you a hint: http://www.crystalinks.com/psychic.html"]almost nothing[/URL])? You can't honestly tell me you can reconcile yeerks but not animal-based shapeshifters.
    I don't want the high fantasy/Gothic/romanticised style of those splats. As soon as some one hears the name of a old world mythological creature, the modern myth is consumed by it. Yes I knew all that stuff, but the trappings and names need to be taken away. vampires might become face morphing parasites that eat brain matter instead of sucking blood. Changelings lose all the bestial/tree/elf aspects and become cyborgs or augmented psychics. Do you understand?

        Okay, I think I get what you're saying now. Alright. This can be worked with. Honestly, the Chronicles of Darkness has done away with a lot of the gothic/romanticised aspects of the old setting and has made things a lot more personal and localized - as well as blurred the lines between what is high fantasy and what isn't (which a lot of modern occult mythology has already done for us - Men in black mythology, for example, is often conflated and identical to with older stories about directly confronting devils).

        I have run a "Demon" game in which the PCs were turned into quantum self-observers due to an accident regarding their work on a particle accelerator. Another of my STs runs a hard-SF space travel campaign where our problems tend to be stuff like "oh no, we're out of neodymium and therefore going to die because we can't repair our fusion generators during cycle down periods!"

        I think the trappings behind the names can only be taken away if you apply the name and then challenge the assumptions behind it. If your players hear "vampire" and start planning to deal with gothic undead bloodsuckers, they are totally unprepared for fear-based psychic parasites that reproduce by scaring people to death, or for alien bug-monsters that camouflage themselves asthe palate and lower sinuses whose mandibles stick out where you'd expect to see fangs.

        I submit that allowing a player's assumptions to inform them as to what they think they know might be a valid technique for introducing those concepts - that's what I was getting at earlier with those questions about linking DMT visions to Geists and UFO mythology to Demon.

                        I'm honestly not sure what you're asking or what you object to in Chronicles of Darkness. ChroD has a ton of scifi horror elements. To give some examples, Star Wars is scifi-fantasy, The Martian is pure hard scifi, Alien is scifi-horror, and Warhammer 40k is scifi-horror-fantasy. Which of those are you going for?

                        If you want a cosmic horror game that goes for pure science fiction without fantasy, try Eclipse Phase. It's spooky, it's fairly hard as scifi goes, and it's fantastic.
