    [Open Call] The Mythos Chronicles

    08-23-2016, 05:22 PM


    Having just picked up the 7th Edition of Call of Cthulhu and being inundated with a Mythos Adventure Path in Pathfinder, I got myself thinking, where do the Mythos fit in Chronicles of Darkness?

    This thread has two goals.
    1. Brainstorm plots, ideas, hooks, monsters and NPCs for a Mythos-themed CofD game.
    2. Develop a Dark Era based off of the 1920s that incorporate said themes. I would say that the primary regional focus of such an era would be the Northwest Atlantic (aka Lovecraft Country) but that it would also include events worldwide. Mythos Characters are surprising well-heeled travellers.

    Historical Themes in the 1920s
    • American Isolation
    • Prohibition and moral temperance
    • Rise of the Mob
    • Boom Days of Pulp Magazines
    Where do the Mythos fit?
    The short, flippant answer is probably everywhere. Every realm in the Chronicles of Darkness has the potential for creatures from the Cthulhu Mythos to appear. That being said, I think some realms are more appropriate than others. For the most part, the Supernal may play host to entities that are Cthulhian Cosmic Horrors, but they don’t interact with sleepers with any regularity. Part of the cosmic horror of the Mythos is that the horrors that protagonists encounter don't only exist, but they are a fundamental part of our world. That strongly suggests to me that while some Mythos monsters may originate from the Abyss, the vast majority of them originate from the Fallen World, that is our material realm, The Shadow, The Astral, or The Underworld.

    (That said I often joke that Mastigos fight the Abyss by summoning Ringwraiths and the Hounds of Tindalos).

    Since dreams and the Dreamlands are so important to many stories in the Mythos cannon, that means many creatures may have connections the Astral. Perhaps the Denizens of Leng are specialized Goetia that have found a way to walk out of dreams and into reality? And do they have some connection with Beasts? The Idigam are already fully loaded for cosmic horrors from the dawn of time, but there have been references to stranger spirits from beyond the Lunar perimeter reaching Earth too. What happens when a spirit of light lands in a pack’s territory if it originated near Carcoasa? The Underworld bears mentioning too, since the Chthonians are about as alien as you can get and are capable of wrecking havoc even before they pass through an Aventian Gate.

    Arcadia can’t be overlooked either. I would be less willing to place Mythos characters there, although it can be done, as the Arkham Lost thread has shown us. That said, if stories can be told about it, you can find Changelings and the Wyrd in the mix. How exactly did the Hedge get changed by HPL and company?

    And finally, there is the terrifying, horrifying possibility that the Mythos monsters don’t need to come from beyond, and that they already exist in the Material realm. The God-Machine is Lovecraftian enough already, no changes needed. Mummy, on the other hand, exists almost wholly in the Material Realm and has dropped hints of both Narlythotep and the King in Yellow.

    Fun times. Watch your Sanity.

      First things first: I wrote up modern Arkham as a Changeling 1e setting here and described Delta Green as a Hunter Compact here.

      Second things second: Years ago, I was one of many people working on a fangame that eventually failed called Outsider: the Calling, which was focused on normal human beings unwillingly warped by the Mythos and trying to figure out where to go from there, which in practice looked like a rough middle ground between Changeling and Deviant with a Lovecraftian coat of paint. While the rules were incredibly shoddy and the project stalled pretty quickly, you may want to skim through it; a quick Google search should pull it up.

      As for what already exists in the CofD that can be made Mythos-y (as opposed to pulling it in wholecloth to an admittedly crowded universe), you have quite a few options. Many of the heftier Mythos entities fit pretty well as Arcadian Keepers (imagine a musician like Eric Zahn dragged to the court of Azathoth to keep the blind idiot god asleep with unending music, or those called by dreams to serve Cthulhu who may eventually just wander into the surf im search of Rl'yeh) - and I think that's our easiest way in. There's a compelling argument to be made for Deep Ones and Mi-Go to be hobgoblins, I think, and that's to say nothing of how easily ghouls fit into Changeling as a race that wanders dreams with ease.

      While Mummy is ripe for this (as you mention, the Herald of Forgotten Stars and Ochre-Masked King are Nyarlathotep and Hastur in all but name), I feel like the Judges, demons, and black huriyah are all too remote from reality for use in the average CofD game by default. I'm all for making them less so, but that would require some doing to explain how they get out of Duat/the orbit of their dead stars.

      Mage is ripe with Lovecraftian potential, with the Temenos an easy analogue to the Dreamlands and the Abyss giving us gifts like sentient hostile alternate timelines. Some will argue that the Exarchs are Mythos critters, but I think they're too fundamentally tied into the humanist core of Awakening to fit that bill; the Great Old Ones don't care about oppressin mankind, they scarcely even notice it.

      God-Machine Angels are always a fun option for this sort of thing, but I think the qashmallim are much more terrifying because of their inscrutability; Angels serve the God-Machine, but qashmallim just act in accordance with the invisible whims of whatever the Divine Fire is.
