    As for the other new Opuses they are looking good. I remember that Sound, Beauty and Water are universally favored. My earlier comment was that it was good that there are some Opuses that aren't really favored by any Waters as they extend to concepts not inherent to the Sirens usual expertise. And with so many Opuses (24+3+?) there's a point where the concepts become esoteric enough that they are harder to learn although the Song ripples through each of them.

    Spirit is a prime example, as while Polaria reflects on Death as a passing none looked attuned to the amortal animistic spirit world. Dream would likely follow. My read on Deluge is that while its something all Sirens experience, it's not really their 'birthright' or domain that exists only because there are Sirens in the world. The Deluge is the end-stop of all futures however climatic. Plus there's more of an in character reasoning, as I imagine Verses done on Earth trying to use the Deluge would be difficult to bridge the future with the past. Although that really depends on the Introduction and Conclusion.

    Would there be favored Opuses swapped out with the newer ones? There's kind of a nice pattern with 3 universal Opuses, 3 favored per water and 3-ish or more that aren't. And making a new pattern would require upping things to 4 and finding many more Opuses. This extends to a clarification I wanted to ask about. At character creation, does a Siren only start with her Water's Opuses? Or could they be traded with the Universal ones, which are favored but are implied to only be available with post-creation experience.

    That being said, Time as an Opus sounds like a step backwards. In particular that it brings up the timey-whimey question again of how much time travel Sirens can or can't do outside of journeying the Deluge. And unlike other Opuses it has an immediate exception to how certain Verse components are used, like Self and Travel, ones that other Opuses do not have in these groupings. The criteria of Introductions and Conclusions are meant to apply consistent tiers of effects no matter the Opus. I think you could also get both aspects of Time from metaphorical application of Freeze and Temper (slow and haste, respectively). Let alone that Sirens already have a scouring of time with their Trace, and with the expanded Trace mechanics can retroactively use their Verses in situations where enough successes were applied that it allows for more controlled blatant time trickery. With all that a Time Opus seems redundant, even with the notion some Opuses have mild overlap.

    Off the top of my head with the various Conclusions, Time is just too linear of a concept. All of the various Verses are only going to gain time or lose time with the only difference being how the same effect came about, leaving only the Introduction to alter target of effect in terms of Verse variability.

                    Along with an extra section I've put into the Celestial Ocean Document on uses for it. :3

                    Clues of the Past
                    As the Celestial Ocean holds the ruins of the past within it, it’s simple for Sirens to sift through the wreckage to find pieces they can Trace on that lead back to the present reality. Perhaps they find a serial killer’s knife, rusted with blood and covered in barnacles at his last crime scene, or a waterlogged safe containing the tax returns of a corrupt company. Either way, a Siren may make (Intelligence or Wits) + Investigation rolls to find a clue for a Trace. And depending on what event the clue is tied to, it’s usually tied to a circumstance or purview of one of the six Waters. In which case, the Siren may add the Water’s Prevalance as a bonus to her (Wits or Intelligence) + Investigation roll. For example events dealing with Freshwater’s Purviews (Technology, Humanity, Fate and Civilization in general) would add bonuses to searching for clues dealing with Civilization’s problems.
                    Uses of the Celestial Ocean
                    Now with all of the dangers of the Celestial Ocean made clear, the Diasters, Survivors, Therons, Vodianoi, and even other Sirens, there’s undoubtedly one question that crosses every Siren’s mind at one point or another. “Why even go there in the first place?” Like most treasures in the sea, it takes looking below the water’s surface to find the answers to these questions.
                    Safety: There’s one thing that’s hard to deny - The Celestial Ocean is a dangerous place and nobody in their right mind would think of casually setting up shop there. And yet that’s what makes the Celestial Ocean so useful. It’s someplace that nobody thinks of staying at for long, and is an ideal place for people who desire safety from the mortal world. Due to this factor, it’s common for Sirens of all Currents to think of finding safety within the Celestial Ocean. It’s the biggest reason why Grotto building is so popular, afterall.
                    Treasures and Trinkets: The most obvious benefit of the Celestial Ocean is the ability to find Treasures and Trinkets as stated in their section above. No Siren knows why they only appear in the Deluge and not in normal reality, but for what it’s worth, they truly are treasures amongst the ruins. No Siren can also assume where they may find any, but more than a few (namely those in Cocytus) are aware that they can be cultivated from major events in reality. More than a few Sirens make it their profession for Treasure Seeking.
                    Clues and Survivors: As stated above, within the bad future of the Celestial Ocean may the Siren find clues of events and tragedies that she can enforce or prevent in the present. The case of Clues are simple to find and deal with, as they may be an object or place connected to events the Siren hopes to interfere with.
                    However, the Survivors show a peculiar inclination when it comes to Clues. More than a few of them however can show surprising knowledge on events. Sirens are surprised that the Survivors can show such an affinity for the world before the apocalypse, but then again the existence of the Celestial Ocean relies on the existence of reality. These special kinds of Survivors tend to be descendents of those in important events (and yet they strangely appear like the people they’re connected to), or even self-professed Flensers who’ve survived the Deluge and regret it ever happening. Whatever the case is on these special Survivors, no Siren knows. However, for the right price, these survivors are willing to divulge all the information they can in hopes their future will be fixed.

                    [1+] Theronic Companion
                    Whereas the animals of the earth serve as man's best friend, a Siren's best friend lies within the Celestial Ocean and can be found within the Therons of the Celestial Ocean. A Theronic Companion is an animal loyal to the owning Siren, capable of carrying out her orders as she gives them. They can only understand commands like the smartest of dogs can understand, such as "Go find food outside" or "Go find shelter" is fine. "Go get me the Clue at the bottom of the ruins 20 miles from here" or other complex orders aren't. Each dot in Theronic Companion accounts for how many Alterations the Theron owns.                         What I like about the Deluge as presented is that it is a real place. An ever shifting potential reality but real nonetheless. That it has no other supernatural realms of its own is a powerful feature as to how real and isolated the Deluge is.

                        What is key is how the Waters bridge the Earth of the modern present with the Deluge, that they not only reflect biomes on the planet but the harsh waters of the Celestial Ocean as well. And from each Water's purview (the range of Opuses) you can link major cataclysms and hazards to a category of Water. When it comes to basic travel to the Deluge, especially for non-Sirens, it could occur during a similar disaster of a lesser scale.

                        Those lost at sea, that disappear during violent storms, that were trapped under the ice. For a brief moment (from the perspective of the present) that person is gone, thrust into the raging Deluge where the disaster continues past their present area on arrival. Events that, even before the Dangers of the Deluge are faced, would constitute facing a Breaking Point just from the sheer trauma of being thrust through the Deluge. It's not easy and not pretty, for even to get back requires further strain to your Integrity.

                        Taken to the full extreme, when present in a fitting disaster travel to or from the Deluge requires a loss of Integrity. On a dramatic failure from such a roll the travel is involuntary. It fits the tidbit that most are unable to survive exposure to the Deluge.

                        The earlier idea of Quagmires connecting to the Deluge would work similarly. Along with their punishments, if they have no use for a victim they could be flung into the Deluge fitting the type of Quagmire. Sirens have an easier time with travel to the Deluge. For one, they can try to barter passage with the Quagmire/Fury, and would not risk their Lucidity in the above example. Let alone that with the Deluge Opus and the right Introductions and Conclusions, they can interact with the Deluge in various ways. Travel to it safely, Sense it while remaining in the present, Invoke some of its environmental tilts...

                        As for Survivors and Therons, there are no exits from the Deluge. No Quagmires to appease, no departure points from a former 'present' because they did not come from the pre-Deluge earth. It is only with a Siren to guide them (or the Vodinaoi route) that they could leave the Deluge. They are real people so they do not emerge via Twilight, and as the Deluge has no Twilight state they'll have no advantage to use it. However, the 'present' would be as uncomfortable to them as we would be as modern people going back in time to the Neolithic era. Survivors and other Deluge residents are adapted to survive its cataclysms. It' like they're locked into their own Diluvian form, basically losing health where a Siren would lose Pneuma unless they dwell in waters fitting that of the Deluge equivalent.


                        I fear I did a disservice comparing Furies and Slyphs to Qasmilim when the idea was just starting out. Non-Siren aspects of the Song sound like a novel idea and if their gatekeeper status is kept they could offer passage to a calm or at least benign section of the Deluge. Though like any late development addition, it requires a bit more effort to find the nessecary niche for Slyphs.

                            Frailty Chronicles Disclaimer* The follow Material is re-written homebrew adapting rules from Danse Macabre, Mirrors, and Walking Shadows (http://forum.theonyxpath.


                            So far, my ideas at the moment are as follows based on my ideas and the Supports System.

                            The Relationships Merit will be like a modified version of the Touchstones Merit. Like the Touchstones merit, Relationships will go on a rating from one to five. Unlike a Touchstone though, Relationships are much more intimate to Sirens than other Supernaturals. As such, this Merit is much more personal and includes more Storytelling than normal Touchstones. As such, the Relationship merit doesn't keep track of the number of relationships, but rather the Strength of the Relationship the merit represents.

                            The Relationship merit can be bought multiple times, and each Relationship represents a single person or being. Each dot in the Relationship as said is the Strength of connection. A Siren with a single dot in a Relationship may have that person as an aquaintence or a disconnected friend, but a Siren with five dots in a Relationship may have the person as a lover, a longtime and trusted friend or they're the Siren's parent(s), guardian(s) or even beloved pets. The type of Love can be any of the four types: Familial, Romantic, Platonic or Unconditional. But it must be listed underneath one of these types.

                            For each Dot a Relationship has, it has one Health box. The Health Boxes are not representative of physical health like they normally denote, but the health of the Relationship. This health is still in the flavor of physical health, being committed in Bashing, Lethal and then Aggravated Damage. Bashing overdone can become Lethal, and Lethal overdone can become Aggravated.

                            Relationships require upkeep and support, but they also face stress and times where it seems it may break off. When it comes to measuring Stress, roll the Siren's Lucidity. For each Failure on the roll, fill one box with Bashing damage. Stress is usually rolled at the beginning of each Story, but a Siren may spend a point of Willpower to roll 3 additional dice separate from the Stress roll. Each success on these dice negate one of the Failures on the original Stress roll.

                            Stress can be rerolled throughout a story, but it's primarily caused from negative interaction between the Siren and her Relationship, or the lack of interaction. Examples of Stress rolls are primarily:
                            • Beaching: When a Siren commits an action according to her Beaching, or a mortal does the same with their Vice, roll Stress.
                            • Kink: Each Relationship has at least one Kink, one point of dysfunctionality present that can be annoying and attributed to one or both sides of a relationship. Afterall, perfect relationships only exist in fairy tales, and real ones need maintenence and upkeep to keep the flames going. When the Kink presents itself, roll Stress.
                            • Supernatural Nature: Mortals are not conditioned to accept the supernatural, and many freak out over it. When a Relationship faces a breaking point from the Siren indirectly, roll Stress, the damage here is Lethal. If the Siren was intentionally revealing her nature to her beloved, then the damage is upgraded to Aggravated.
                            • Neglect: Mostly done as a thematic approach, but neglect can kill relationships on one or both ends. If the mortal neglects the Siren or Vice versa, roll Stress. For a Siren to count as Neglecting her Relationship, the Siren has either not made any contact (no matter how brief) to the Relationship within three sessions, or doesn't interact with them during downtime.
                            If a Siren ever manages to fill the Relationship's Health Boxes with Aggravated, clear them all and have the Siren lose a dot in that Relationship. It represents losing the once higher amount of connection they once had, the pain of simply walking past someone you once talked all day every day with. Should the Siren lose the last dot in her Relationship, then the Relationship breaks off, lost to the Siren. The Siren can spend Experience to raise her Relationship score back up, to represent her getting back in touch with those she cares about.

                            The Relationship can be healed of Damage. Spending time, trying to get problems (caused by Stress) to become water under the bridge. This is primarily done with spending time with the Relationship and showing why it's a meaningful relationship.


                            I think I may have gone overboard with describing the Relationship ideas/conceptualization, but I hope this covers the general ideas I have for them. :3

                                                            Pardon for my lack of posting as of late. I think I've hit another block, or at least I'm out of inspiration at the moment.

                                                            And I would like to make note of a few things I've been thinking of. First, I'm thinking of not fully developing the Crossovers section, at least for now. As much fun as it has been making the sections for it, it's heavily draining on Inspiration. And given that a Crossover section isn't required to fully finish the corebook, I will be holding off on Crossovers until Siren as a Corebook is complete.

                                                            Second, given that thought, I was thinking on what parts of the Corebook need to be completed, which so far includes three sections: Choruses, Ascension/Descension and Honolulu. So far, Honolulu has at least ideas on how it should be written and Ascension/Descension was liked by the people who looked at it. All that's left is Choruses, the Z-Splat of Siren. But I'm having problems as it were with them or designing them.

                                                            Chorus Thoughts
                                                            My thoughts so far on Choruses remain as I said they were. Powers include powering up Verses better, making it easier to trace out other members or the like, etc. The only problem I'm having at the moment is what their general theme should be. Either focusing on Operas and Musical Productions, or basing off of Musical Genres. There's Pros and Cons to each of them.

                                                            For Operas or Musical Productions, the pros are as follows: They grant a theme to the Sirens within them, making it easier to write about them, the general character of the Sirens who join them and the like, and maybe even including a special Verse or power that only the Sirens in the chorus can take up, and the possibility that Opera/Musical Choruses could belong to either Celestial or Abyssal Currents. The only con I can see with this is that it may be abit too limiting (in terms of Verse style and "belongs to either Celestial or Abyssal) and the Opera schtick feels like something that should be in Changeling (given how the use of Story tropes and the like are used alot in Changeling).

                                                            For Musical Genres, the pros are as follows: It makes it much more versatile in terms of powering up Verses (meaning if you're in a Chorus whose genre is Punk Rock, then performing a Verse to Punk Rock will grant a bigger bonus), and following a theme of music feels fitting for Sirens. Plus, it just feels right in Siren and gives something outside of Celestial and Abyssal duties to take part in. The cons I can see with this though is that it may be too open, not to mention it feels like writing on the various Genres would be painting with a wide brush and annoy people who like the Genre and go "but *insert genre here* isn't just about *insert general theme the songs within the genre covers*". One way around that would be to insert subgenres as various powers or extra effects for Verses, which could be a pro.


                                                            Anyways, going up further, my last thought. When it comes to Survivors and Breathless, I was thinking. They both serve as special conditions that can be inflicted onto humans due to the Song's influence. Given Karavolo's thoughts on Survivors being able to make but not own Pneuma and how that sounds like Breathless, what if Survivors and Breathless are the same kind of being? Instead of being two separate splats, they're the same one with different living spaces?

                                                            What do you guys think of these thoughts?
