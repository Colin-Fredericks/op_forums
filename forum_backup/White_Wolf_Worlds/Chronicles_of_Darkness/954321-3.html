                                                        I interpret Disquiet like this: Prometheans are created beings, but they are not completed beings. One might almost call them "quasi modo." The alchemical reactor within them does not have great containment. This makes it an amazing conduit for the Divine Fire, as it seeks the path of least resistance, but also allows the Divine Fire to leak into the world around them in its raw form. Disquiet is a side effect of the unfiltered Divine Fire touching the world in a way it normally can't, but the Promethean is not the source of the Divine Fire or the Disquiet it generates any more than you are the source of the air in your lungs.

                                                        Personally, I think its too easy to get hung up on what kind of metaphor Promethean is. Focusing in on any one metaphor that's "best" really only serves to pigeonhole the game and limit the kinds of stories you can tell with it. You can view stories through the lens of metaphor, but it shouldn't serve the metaphor at the expense of the story. Promethean emphatically is a metaphor for all of those things and simultaneously emphatically is also literally about lightning-powered flesh-monsters. All interpretations are made poorer by the absence of the others.

                                                        EDIT: To elaborate a little further on the second point. Star Trek would not have the enduring impact that it does today if it was just a show about spaceships. At the same time though, no one was ever going to give a shit about the show's social commentary if it weren't also an entertaining escapist fantasy about fucking spaceships.

                                                            Why do Prometheans have to be a metaphor for something? Why can't they just be a particular type of creature inspired by created beings in literature and legend, put into a game designed to explore themes of isolation and what it means to be human? I understand that some people may identify personally with certain themes or project their own meaning onto aspects of the game and that's great, but it is a mistake to assume your interpretation of the game was intended by the designers. I can't speak for the other writers, but the parts I wrote weren't intended to reflect any real-world groups or issues, they were entirely written to serve the structure and themes of the game as laid out by the developer. Conversations with other writers never discussed parallels or metaphors, they were concerned with things like internal consistency, rewarding game play, clarity of writing, and mechanical cohesion. The rules and mechanics of the game are to serve the purpose of the game. You may disagree with how effectively they do so, but treating them as commentary on some aspect of society is a mistake.
