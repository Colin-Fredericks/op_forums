    Please help me understand 2e "modifiers' :(

    04-30-2023, 03:33 AM
    Hi all, I am sorry if you have already seen my questions on any related discords, but I, after many years of playing these game lines, have run into a couple of weird interpretations of core and fundamental rules that I would like to know if anyone has worked out or has game designer knowledge. Iv'e had like 10 responses from other area's and nearly everyone's interpretation is different in some way, some quite divergently.

    Basically, in the forming a dice pool section of these game lines main books, it discusses adding your total relevant attribute and skill or attribute and attribute together, and then add and subtract "modifiers", with the example modifiers being circumstances, equipment, and people acting against you . All things, outside of the effective dots in attributes and skills, are modifiers.

    "Modifiers: Bonuses add the indicated number of dice; penalties remove them. Unless otherwise specified, modifiers never exceed +/–5. Add all bonuses before subtracting penalties." DtR, CtL2e ect

    Now the main interpretation I have seen, and the one I've run and played with for a decade, is that each "modifier" is a separate distinct thing, capped at a +/- 5 individual influence over a dice pool, however, on looking back at the rules, I have found that is just explicitly wrong, at least with respect to a bunch of things. The integrity section of CoD mentions that while "Modifiers are cumulative, but the total modifier from circumstances should not exceed +/– five dice." for the table of modifiers that it shows. Likewise the equipment section "Most equipment offers a bonus to dice rolls pertaining to its use. Multiple items can influence a given roll, but a roll should not receive more than a +5 bonus.", equipment which covers distinct and different things like items, plans and all sorts of other relevant bonuses to a pool.

    It also specifically gives an example of when you CAN stack a modifier, in resisted actions such as applying defence states that "This resistance is over and above any other modifiers applied to the dice pool" implying obviously that it does in fact stack, as per the rule above for "Unless otherwise stated". If modifiers naturally cumulatively stacked, there would be no need to state that, as far as I can see.

    Other game lines have specific and unique exceptions to this +5/-5 rule while still referencing it, for example in making dice pools for spellcasting MtA2e and for Conspiracy actions in DtR, both of which state the action taker can cumulatively stack modifiers to certain specific points for the relevant action.

    The interpretations I have seen in the last 2 days include
    1) dice pools can't have more than a Plus or Minus 5 total of modifiers applied to them, you sum all individual bonuses (with equipment being a combined group of up to +5) and then subtract all penalties, and excess bonus and penalties are ignored (the, "dice pools aren't supposed to be massive in a horror game, and modifiers should be limited")
    2) dice pools can have infinite modifiers to them, where each individual bonus or penalty doesn't exceed +-5 ( the "I didn't read the rulebook ST" interpretation)
    3) dice pools can have infinite modifiers to them, with the sum of all circumstances adding to no more than +=5, the sum of equipment adding to +-5, and all other bonuses and penalties separately applied (the average interpretation)
    4) dice pools can have infinite separate modifiers to them, but circumstances and equipment are added together as a combined modifier, and equipment can't influence that modifier more than +5, and the end result of this combined pool can't be more than a +5 -5, and all other bonus and penalty numbers separately applied ("Convoluted but mechanically supported" interpretation)
    5) Dice pools can have infinite separate modifiers to them, but similar modifiers are lumped together, so you can't galaxy brain 3x +2 interdisciplinary specialities together for a +6, apply multiple static merits like mantle effects, different applicable status, striking looks, menacing and fame for a huge passive modifier, or stack more than -5 of combat stuff like called shots without increasing the combat modifier pool using things like aim or all out attacks, or have 4 +5 modifier magical effects cast upon you. (a "reasonable power gamer limiter interpretation")

    interesting things to note,

    Willpower is applied to the rolls before penalties are applied, meaning that you can still be reduced to a chance die by a large penalty.

    Tilts are circumstantial modifiers, and so if they /do/ cumulatively stack together they would cap out at -5 +5

    Conditions that stack when reapplied like tempted cap out at -5 or +5 as a bonus/ penalty, unclear if multiple conditions all add capped and cumulative together if they benefit or penalise the same action

    Vampire physical intensity and Frenzy may each inadvertently be capped at +5 dice for a roll if you utilise more updated rules from CoD, as its a bonus to a pool like willpower, and not adding functional dots of strength. Obviously this is ridiculous and would be an example where they would say "in addition and above other modifiers" but as there is no generic modifier rules section in this book, just a circumstances and equipment section, which leads me to believe that it wasn't added until CoD2e was released the next year.
    "By spending a point of Vitae, he may add two dice to rolls involving his choice of Strength, Dexterity, or Stamina for the turn."​

    DtR has examples of mitigating equipment which kind of implies that the equipment must modify pools before applying the total sum to the attribute and skill pool, if this mitigating equipment is capped by a modifier or added to the normal equipment modifier is unclear to me
    "Environmental Tilts affect the whole scene, although they can often be mitigated by special equipment (such as protective eyewear), actions (taking shelter against flying debris, for example), or supernatural powers. Any Equipment bonus granted by such equipment cannot more than negate the penalties imposed by the Tilt."


    If anyone has thought about this a lot, or has some form of clarity or alternate interpretations that come from the rules and isn't "I ignore what it says and stack everything together", please tell me how you interpret these rules.

It's the interpretation I find most internally consistent while still leaving the game playable and/or fun.

                      If things start getting silly the ST can just say the action is impossible.

                      You've already heard some of my thoughts on the Onyx Path Discord but for the record my position is:

                      * Net modifiers can't go above +5 or below -5
                      * An individual circumstantial modifier should not be outside +/-5, but there can be as many different ones as you like
                      * A particular item of built equipment is limited to +5 and more importantly it has to make sense. The ST can always veto someone forging the Sword of Destiny in their garage in their lunch break. However supernatural things can create larger bonuses. There is no equipment bonus subcategory cap. I take the sentence in the blue book (p.100) to be a reminder of the overall cap and not an extra rule particular to equipment. This also gets you around your Deviant concern.
                      * Exceptions to the individual caps should be specified like Spell Factor penalties in Mage stacking as much as you like. Note that Mage explicitly says a spell dice pool reduced beyond -5 is too complex and automatically fails.
                      * I don't know Vampire very well but I'd say just use common sense and fudge it

                      Basically once you abandon the idea of these subcategory caps between the individual and the overall you no longer need to worry about whether any particular modifier belongs to this or that category and many of your listed problems disappear.
