                        The idea of geniuses being forced to take responsibility is discussed in "The Peerage and the Law" (GtT 129). As it turns out...

                        Originally posted by Page 129
                        The Peerage vacillates between being a hierarchical society and a loose fraternity of mad scientists. Currently the organization is as loose as it has been in almost a century, with the foundations little more than mailing-lists, vestigial societies wrapped around their magazines and earlier history. This was no accident: the approach of the millennium saw increasingly paranoid and exclusionary behavior from the Peerage, which seemed to be eating itself after the collapse of Lemuria. Witch hunts and purges rooted out the corruption in the Demiurges, but then went farther...too far...and the whole Inspired world threatened to tilt toward madness and civil war. Over the course of several months in 1998, geniuses from all the foundations met and forged the Lucidity Concord (sometimes called the Pact of ARPAnet) that dismantled most of the formal structures of the foundations, proscribed the more aggressive and inquisitorial fellowships, and generally ended "The Peerage" as a unified organization with hierarchies and secret decoder rings.

                        ...

                        But for now, there is no formal law among the Peerage. Nonetheless, there are guidelines, vestigial moral structures from the old days. While there's no formal punishment system in place anymore, violating these rules can get a Peer alienated, snubbed, or―if his crimes are egregious enough―simply "taken care of" in order to prevent a descent into dangerous public violence.
                        So yeah, the Peerage isn't this organization that will police Geniuses any more. In terms of responsibilities and consequences, you'd probably want to look at "Drawbacks of Obligation" (GtT 87), "The Resources Merit" (GtD 88), "Antagonism" (GtD 317), and various sections on unmada and Illuminated (GtD, 34, 290, 348, 365, etc.)

                        One of the weak points of Genius is that realistic consequences cut most Geniuses off from most of what makes them special. This is especially described in detail in "The Resources Merit." If you cut them off from society (and thus funding) and send a mob after them a Promethean will still have all their Transmutations, a Beast their Atavisms and Nightmares, a Princess their Charms, and a Genius... whatever they can carry with them, whatever they invested in Merits like Laboratory or Dumpster Diver, and a few minor abilities that stem from their ability to spend Mania. The author says that this weak point should be treated as an opportunity for story hooks and plot as opposed to a threat to bring the game to a halt by turning its PCs into broke people wishing they had enough money to do all their cool stuff, but it's a tension I get the sense some feel is not adequately resolved.

                            I can't deny that alienation is a meaningful consequence, but it feels thematically weak compared to Mage. Sure, left-handed Mages are also hunted down if they're obvious enough to get noticed, but Mages face supernatural consequences of their actions regardless if they get noticed by other Mages. Mutating spells, falling Wisdom, Paradox Conditions, and Abyssal incursions are all very real possibilities if a Mage does not keep a handle on their magic. The lesson is unequivocally "You should not do this", not "You should not be caught doing this".

                            Edit: I've read some of your page suggestions as well.
                            Resources doesn't really fit what I assumed responsibility meant in this context; that a Genius should take responsibility for their own actions. It feels more like the responsibility one has as a business owner or freelancer. YMMV how well that plays out in a game.

                            Antagonizing the Masses is definitely more like it, but it lacks a sense of urgency. That should be expanded from one page to being a much larger part of the book, imo. It feels a lot more related to the concept of mad scientists than Lemuria which gets a much bigger focus.

                            And then Obligation/Illuminated. Low Obligation really doesn't feel very punishing, but that's probably because it has so weak penalties compared to low Humanity in Vampire, and I found no other mechanical advantage as in Mage. And becoming an Illuminated... Well, it sounds more like something to which one should aspire, but terminology aside they seem like a fitting type of antagonist. I just don't see the reason why Illuminated exists when they seem to act like morally bankrupt Geniuses. Both Draugr and Mad seems a lot more distinct since they are quite clearly something that normal Vampires and Mages aren't.
                            But Illuminated as a punishment for transgression... It just feels like a cop out for explaining why your character becomes unplayable. If I designed Genius I'd try to emulate increasing carelessness as a Genius becomes too wrapped up in their work to pay attention to safety. Or take a page from Promethean and add something similar to Disquiet.

                                Here here Tessie,

                                I would also add that Genius gets too caught up in the Science! aesthetic when it comes to designing the consequences of being a Genius (I.E. Alienation from the Peerage and financial bankruptcy.) The whole opening paragraph of the book lays down the idea that Mania might be an alien influence shaping a Genius for some unknowable purpose, and that a Genius' own ideas and inspiration might not even be their own...and then they do nothing with it. (They try with the Illuminated, but it winds up being more of a twisted ego trip more than anything.)

                                It's funny, but Demon the Descent actually does give us an example of how to do the "being reshaped by an alien intelligence" thing right with the Half-Demons and their Reverse Cipher. Essentially the more attention they draw from/the more they work with the God Machine the more powerful they become as their Cipher unlocks like a normal Demon...until they unlock the equivalent of the Final Secret and become either a literal or figurative slaves to the Machine. This would be perfect for mechanically illustrating a Genius' descent into Illumination, and hinting back at that potential for a great and terrible force working it's will through the Genii.

                                                        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                                                        This turned out to be more of an opinion piece, if you have any thoughts on the points brought up please post them below.


                                                        I like Genius: the Transgression, never though I'd be saying that but I do none the less. It shows what creativity a fan game can bring to the table, and it's ideas can inspire stories that wouldn't normally come up in a Chronicle of Darkness.

                                                        This is what make me so disappointed in Genius' failings though. For all it's themes of inspiration, potential, and creativity the game itself is one of the most stifling gamelines of the fan-made series. And here's why, my own thesis if you will: Geniuses must be scientists.

                                                        Sure, this might be the obvious point of the game. Play as a mad scientists, create impossible inventions, explore infinite dimensions, Ect. But not all geniuses are scientists, but in Genius the Inspired must become scientist or inventors in order to do anything involving Wonders. There are no artists who seek to create masterpieces in mediums never before attempted, no athlete so dedicated to the craft that their mind masters the physics of the game to elevate it to new dimensions, no musician who writes a song so inspiring that it births a sentient thought form in the depths of the human subconsciousness. If any of those examples became Inspired, their only hope to be playable is to don the white lab coat, strap on goggles, or take oration lessons because there is no way their Wonders will ever see the light of gameplay the way the rules are now. And that is the very opposite of inspiring.

                                                        Again, this might just be the point of the game though. Being a mad scientist. But consider this: having the option to be a Genius from any background and profession opens up unlimited possibilities for character creation. Having the option to only play a Genius who's from a scientific field limits players options at character creation. Playing a mad scientist is achievable in both of the above options, while any other option is only available in one.
                                                        I consider this thread to have conned me. I was promised a critique/analysis and what do I get? A single complaint that is not even true.

                                                        One, this complaint is like saying "You know what makes Vampire: the Masquerade bad? That you can only play as a vampire. I feel that this really ruins the game."

                                                        But, two, IT'S NOT EVEN TRUE!

                                                        Geniuses can come from any background and the book explains at length that most of the Dr., Prof., etc. titles are actually made up and have no academic background behind them, they often just picked to look trendy. Certainly Geniuses by fluff are meant to enter a kind of fevered, obsessed inspired state, captivated by a brilliant idea they just had when they engage in Wonder-making, as this is part of their aesthetic, but this can happen to literally any type of character. You can even make that a story, a character who doesn't quite understand where these ideas and compulsions come from.

                                                        Even this too much for you? Ignore even that! I can't say -I-'ve never been guilty of it, slapping the Genius template on a character just to explain away the cool gadgets he owned for which the template would be a prerequisite. It's a roll-player and not role-player action but sure, go for it. Bend the system to fit your playstyle.

                                                        And other than Geniuses there are other mini templates in the book. I did play as a rouge (Orphan) robot or runaway Beholden before, and on one occassion an old guy who was a Clockstopper who'd antagonize the party.

                                                        I can't find it now but there was another comment above asking why you could not make a Genius who was an artist trying to make a never before seen masterpiece.
                                                        You most certainly can; you'll need the Metaptropi track. 1st dots creates artifical sensations, higher dots create impossible spaces and materials.
                                                        Be a bit creative and don't complain as soon as something not handed to you ready made for Pete's sake.

                                                        ---
                                                        Maybe I'll make a post explaining it one day if I felt OP Forums crowd was worth the bother, but...
                                                        Genius is a game about creativity and self-expression.
                                                        If you want to understand the game, skip right to Wonder-making and start making fun stuff using the variables, and try to come up with ways to build your favorite devices from films, stories you like. From that exercise campaigns, PCs, NPCs will all flow. If customization and expression is NOT the type of fun you look for in RPGs (see "8 types of fun" by the AngryGM) then you will likely not realize its merits so easily but that does not make it a bad game, tho it may not be your type of game.
