                            I'd like to point out that the story of The Fall of Atlantis and the story of Wolf and Moon are both myths, from very different cultures, neither of which generally view their respective myths as the literal Truth, with the possible exception of a few extreme fundamentalist individuals within either group. You're comparing similarities between what are essentially just-so stories from two very different cultures and drawing the conclusion that they must be describing the same fall. How many real-world cultures have Great Flood myths? The more likely conclusion is that people tell similar stories to explain universal facets of the human condition than that there was a single worldwide flood and all of these cultures are unknowingly telling the story of the same event.

                                            It is also a setting where cultural stories can - and perhaps often do - have grains of truth at their core. Just as in reality, as well, people who have flood myths often have to deal with floods as a major source of catastrophe. Volcanoes feature prominently in the world creation myths of those who live near volcanoes.

                                            I'm partial to the idea of Mythic History being bigger than people can perceive. Hellboy's my go-to source of inspiration in that regard; with a grand, sprawling history whose truth is often hard to grasp because you see small fragments of a bigger whole and never get the full story. Lemuria, Hyperborea, Atlantis, Mu, they all existed and all rewrote the world, but... How? All we see is fragmentary pieces of magical empires that spanned thousands of years, perhaps tens or hundreds of thousands. And throughout this, their reach seems impressive, but we still also have creatures like the Bog Roosh dwelling in the ocean depths and the fae lurking in their hidden places, which do not seem inclined to treat these supposedly great empires as anything at all, much less passing fancies. They don't look down, they don't look up; the world is simply so big that immense magical empires weren't something particularly important to them.

                                            If there is a Mythic History to the World of Darkness, this is the way I like to use it. The question of "Did Atlantis exist?" is interesting, but Mythic History should never feel like you know all the answers. You should always stumble onto things that make you go "Wait, how does this fit into the big picture?" as the map stretches ever wider and every dark patch you fill in adds a thousand new miles of undiscovered country.

                                                        The Werewolf myth has always been interesting to me as a werewolf fan, but even with the few similarities I don't really see it coinciding with the Mage origin story.

                                                        But looking into what we do know about the various myths could suggest other things. For instance Dave Brookshaw pointed out that the Gauntlet may be a 'gaping wound left in reality' after the fall of Father Wolf, providing that story is true, and the fall of the Border Marches, similarly. But if you look around a bit more, tied in with the Maeljin who are currently considered entities of the Inferno, you'll see the myth of the First Wound. According to Werewolf legend, the First Wound is where Father Wolf was killed, and from that act was born the Maeljin of Violence, Thurifuge, who built a palace on or from Father Wolf's bones. Thurifuge is now considered a high-ranking demon of the Inferno, I think Maeljin are around rank 8. So if you wanted you could have a story in your game connecting the Gauntlet and the death of the Border Marches to the Inferno or, from a Mage point of view, the Lower Depths.
