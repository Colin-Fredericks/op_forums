    How to intruduce new players to M:TAw right now?

    11-25-2014, 06:29 AM
    So I got asked by a friend of mine if I would be interested in Storytelling a Mage: The Awakening game for him and his friends. I ran a short-lived chronicle years ago, and he really liked the idea, but honestly it has been a while since I sat down with the books.

    Anyway, I am pretty sure that this would be the group's first exposure to the World of Darkness, new or old.

    As I recall, Awakening can get a little labyrinthine as you explore the spellcasting rules, so does anyone have any recommendations on teaching the game system and concepts to the players?

    System-wise, I know the paradox system is a little too soft. Should I house rule this to give Dox a little more bite? Any suggestions on house rules?

    What about ritual casting? Any recommendations on reeling in this aspect of the game?

    Also, While I have a decent collection of Awakening books, I don't have them all, and I know from reading the forum that certain paths and orders have evolved a bit from their core book presentation. I don't think it's quite fair to hand the players the various supplements and say "If you really want to understand the Free Council, read this... the core book got some things wrong." So what is a ST to do?

    I know they are working on a 2nd edition of mage right now, and while I would love to wait for it to hit the presses, I know these things can take some time. Would you guys recommend trying the GMC rules for mage *before* the 2nd edition rule book is finished? Have you noticed any hiccups between how Mage rules work and how the GMC rules update works?

      Pretty much all of the issues you bring up are being addressed in the second edition, so it's kind of difficult to say anything other than "wait for second edition". For example, a lot of the caveats and gotchas are being removed from spellcasting rules, paradox has been completely reworked from the ground up, ritual casting is totally redone, and the developers are working to clarify a lot of the misconceptions and misrepresentations in the original core book.

      The problem you mention with Paradox isn't necessarily that it's too lenient, but that it doesn't accomplish what it's supposed to - which is to create hubristic moral dilemmas. The key parts of 2nd edition Paradox are that it's always caused by your decisions, and can never hurt you. Paradoxes can only harm other people unless you specifically choose to contain it within yourself. The player is pressed with the dilemma of whether they choose to put themselves in danger by containing a paradox, or put innocent bystanders at risk by releasing the paradox and hoping the spell does what you wanted it to anyway.

                  As I recall, Awakening can get a little labyrinthine as you explore the spellcasting rules, so does anyone have any recommendations on teaching the game system and concepts to the players?

It helps explain things like why "patterns" work the way they do, and why the Prime arcana has "phantasms" (The phantasms are shadow puppets on the walls of Plato's cave).                                   Or you could let them go in blind. Let them find out the meat of Awakening by virtue of play.

                                  For the waiting game, wyrdhamster's idea is quite a solid start. If your players are fine with it, let them start entrenched in the various cults and intrigues of the Awakened. Mages are supposed to be the ones that follow the truth, even though it hurts. If they could turn away from a mystery, they wouldn't have become mages in the first place.

                                  Let them earn their Awakening.

                                  Depending on your storytelling-pace, and your groups timetable, that could well last you until 2E is released (in general, everyone hopes for a 1. quartal of 2015 release)

                                  On your other questions.

                                  Personally, in the current 1E rules, I prefer a weaker Paradox for my players. They have little say when it happens or how. Frankly, its not fun when it happens at all.

                                  Rituals have never been a problematic issue in my campaigns. I encourge my players to go nuts. I'm prone to saying "Yes" more often than "No" if the question is "Can I *?" By the time the truly gamebreaking rituals ( eg healing 1 aggravated wound every nanosecond, deathstar laserpointers, combusting a sizeable portion of the visible universe) become feasible, your players understand just how many people would object to such spells being used and have the power and ressources to stop them.

                                  For the books, I understand just throwing books at your players is not quite what you want, but in regard to Awakening books, I fear its quite necessary. The setting lives on its supplements. If you think you know which order the would like, point them at the book. They might enjoy them more than you think.

                                                      Just a small note:
                                                      1 - If your players are new to nWOD, the Order politics may become confusing, so either create a strong backstory to explain why they SHOULD have each other's back and not their order's, or have all players recruited by the same order, and if variety is demanded by players, try to guide them into complimentary orders for your story.

                                                      2 - Try to keep all relationshhips dynamic and recoverable (at first), a 3 party relationship helps. (e.g. the player has a mentor (little 'm', not the Background, in the same Order and when the player wants to go against one party, the other can support their desire), It also help deflect punishment to the alternate party, when needed.

                                                      3 - If you browse through David's Broken Diamond campaign, he has the players in a New Cabal, since the Concilium politics, make growing any established cabal too threatening the D.C. Council.

                                                          As an addendum to Gideon's suggestions, make sure the PCs have a back-story establishing that they are used to working together. It's easy for mage games to become paralyzed by over-cautious introduction scenes ("Does this guy know about magic?"). Either place them all in a cabal together, or if you have them start as Sleepers, make sure they all know and work together pre-Awakening.

                                                          In terms of the splats getting better fleshed out, here's what I've gathered:

                                                          1.Order Themes: Dave lists them here about mid-way through -> http://theonyxpath.com/new-order/ I've found that first listing those gets players excited about the Orders in a way that reciting the Atlantean caste system does not. I think it's also more immersive, since "Magic is Alive" is a better guide for RPing a Mystagogue than "scholar-mage, here's some doctrine bullet points." The "scholar" identity is central to the Order, but it's more meaningful when contextualized as "Magic is Alive, therefore we should devote our lives to chasing it down and studying it with scholarly devotion."

                                                          2. Path Themes: right at the beginning of this post -> http://theonyxpath.com/pathfinding/ Unlike other WoD lines, Mages deliberately make themselves supernatural. There are no accidental Awakenings; someone finds their Lighthouse and signs their name by becoming Obsessed with a Mystery (or Mysteries) related to one or both of their soon-to-be-Path's themes, and pursuing that Mystery well past the point when any normal World of Darkness mortal would give up and go home. When they finally overcome the Lie, that initial Obsession gives way to their Path, which will continue to be defined by those themes. Paths are a much more meaningful guide to developing a character, and specifically, a mage, when they're not just power-sets, but also an indicator of the kind of things a PC is so consumed with investigating that they tear apart the laws of physics to find out. This will be better supported by the mechanics of 2E, but I think you can start incorporating it already.
