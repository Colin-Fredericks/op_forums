        In honor of the newest Warframe, I present the mummies.
        Mummy
        The Arisen have seen the Dawn of Civilization and in many ways the Orokin harkened back to their days.
        Yet at the same time, the Orokin were different. Humans practically lived dozens of times longer than even the most esteemed elder in those days, and the Orokin studied and observed phenomena that previously only were the domain of gods. Traveling the endless darkness to visit distant worlds, this new generation of man, seemingly earning their gold decorations, grew at a frightening pace.
        Even more alarming were the creatures, the new life the Orokin made, the machine intelligences known as Cephalons and the nigh all powerful Sentients, were entities that were proof of their mastery.
        Many of the Arisen came to wonder if there was something of the Shan'iatu or even the Judges themselves spoke of their secret to them… before this new generation held their scaleps against the ancestor’s chests.
        But like all Empires, Nameless or Orokin, the wheel of time shifts. The Orokin fell, undone by their own creations. Some wonder if that is the fate of Irem.

        Another Cycle
        It is a distant epoch, a future that many struggle to even grasp. The sands that the nameless empire was founded upon have been overrun by a synthetic jungle, and the history of the dozen nations that grew on the remains of Shan'iatus’ work are distant memories. The world that the Arisen came to call home is no longer as important anymore, most of the populace having spread out amongst the myriad of worlds within the Origin System.
        In this age, it is exceedingly rare to find any Arisen that still inhabits the same body it did in its original life, the remains having rotted or been eroded over hundreds of lifetimes at this point. Most these days, have undergone the ritual to transfer their Essence to new host bodies at least once.
        One noticable upside to everyone being long lived is that the Arisen can often go away from their cults for decades or more and still expect to see the same faces… allbeit older, but less aged than in previous time peroids.

        Mummu in the Origin Systeml
        The Judges and Fate still order their servants to act. Not bound by planet, they may still be called to serve. Cults still have need of their gods as well, for even technology does not provide enough power.

        Grineer: These crude men of flesh and metal are of interest to the Arisen, if not for their technology, but for the potential for ever loyal and eternal servants. Still, at least one mad scientist in this… cult wishes to hold a few Deathless captive for his own purposes.
        Corpus: The Deathless know of a secret Heresy among the greedy merchant cult, a sect of mummy worshipers who pride themselves on collecting Relics that improve the value of wealth… all owned of course by their patron deities. These fanatics of course would wish their leaders were more active… but what’s a year to these people?
        Infested: Aberrations, monsters. Corruptions of life. The Deathless are immune to the plague, but their bodies when they lie dormant. And even if they die… well...
        Tenno: The Arisen look at the Tenno and often wonder if that these creatures of the Void might have been set aside for a similar to role to the Arisen themselves, kept asleep for an age so that their patron could awaken them in a time of need. There’s surprisingly many points of similarity.

                    The Arisen wear a simulation of living flesh. Their bodies
                    can barely be called animated corpses, as Sekhem replaces
                    tissue lost to injury (including the inevitable erosion of the
                    ages) with a simulacrum the Arisen call the sahu. A sahu is
                    solidified Sekhem, modeled on the Arisen’s original body—
                    that is, the body that underwent embalming and a few
                    necromantic rites in lost Irem. At the start of a new Descent,
                    the mummy’s Sekhem recreates this template perfectly; for all
                    his power, he appears to be a withered, dead thingdriven by
                    unnatural vitality. Fortunately, as the Descent progresses, the
                    mummy’s Sekhem loosens its grasp on his sahu. The mummy’s
                    soul reflexively reshapes it into an idealized duplicate of his
                    original, living form. He can then pass for a normal human
                    until he is forced to call upon his eldritch magic.

                    Based as it is on a ritually altered “khat” (body), the
                    sahu is an anatomical oddity. Iremite embalmers removed
                    every internal organ except for the heart, and scraped
                    the brain out of its skull. The empty body cavities were
                    filled with perfumed herbs. As a result, the Arisen possess
                    a distinctive sweet and bitter scent. At high Sekhem, the
                    mummy’s flesh turns to its true gray-blue tone and withers
                    due to his body’s ancient exposure to natron salts.
                    The mummy’s sahu doesn’t rely on these lost organs
                    (largely because that is how the Arisen knows and recalls
                    life), but the Rite of Return allows him to simulate their
                    functions. The mask of life includes its pleasures... and
                    to a certain extent, its pains. Mummies can eat, drink,
                    and bleed, just like the living. Digested matter dissolves
                    upon contact with a mummy’s empty abdomen. The
                    Deathless can make love (and those who can recall the act
                    occasionally feel urged to do so again, with the frequency
                    of the urges dependent on the personality of the soul in
                    question), but they are universally infertile, and can thus
                    neither bear nor sire children, mortal or otherwise. While
                    the Arisen are truly undead—and thus immune to hazards
                    such as drowning, hypothermia, and disease—some injuries
                    that logically should not afflict them do.

                                            So, I took a glance back at this, and since I am the resident "Mage and Mummy guy" in this thread, I've decided to update the Mages in DarkFrame a bit:


                                            Mage

                                            The Mages think of the Orokin with fear, revulsion, and most of all, regret.
                                            Of all the things that hid in the Shadows, the mages favored the Orokin, for they felt like they had a chance to restore Mankind’s rightful place in the universe, for never before had humanity been so powerful: Understanding of other realities and their exploitation for travel and storage, the ability to grant longevity to everyone, and self aware intelligences. The mages campaigned and supported their rise from the shadows, hoping to cement their hopes for the future, to champion a new era.
                                            It was not meant to be.

                                            When Orokin saw the mages, they did not see equals and brothers or teachers to send mankind to the next phase; they saw potential threats and resources to be exploited. The mages rose to defend themselves, but fighting the Orokin was difficult. While no doubt the mages had powerful Masters, the Orokin empire was all too large to be destroyed so easily and there were only so many mages of that to go around; they couldn’t hope to stop entire fleets by themselves.

                                            The mages were forced to hide and flee. The unfortunate few who were captured were forced into cruel and twisted experiments. The very same cunning and knowledge the Orokin used to empower humanity was now turned against those who wanted to enlighten humanity to the Truth of things.
                                            When the mages leave the operating table they were not themselves, but rather pawns of some other entity, enslaved in their own minds and able to use the Supernal Truths for the Orokin’s lies.

                                            The “Neural Sentry” was created to bring the “human-derived” creatures back into the fold, so that they might serve the Orokin once more. Mages were among the first to be successfully captured and “pacified”. There were even rumors that things were getting worse, with rumors circulating about the Orokin devising a method to predict Awakenings before hand.
                                            Mages did what they could to survive, mostly by fleeing into the shadows or other realms. They hoped and sought the powers above them to destroy their enemy and well into the Old War, the Orokin were undone by their own creations.
                                            When the Orokin fell and all those who were left were stragglers who had to scavenge to get by, the mages breathed a sigh of relief. Maybe it was for the better that the Orokin fell, in the hopes that their successors would be more receptive of the Truth.

                                            Chains of the Void
                                            Why the Orokin turned on the Mages is a mystery. Even more is how they were even to best and defeat several Masters and enthrall lesser mages to their wills.
                                            The Pentacle Orders however propose a simple theory, that the Void, the dimension that the Orokin relied so heavily on was infact a realm that held a connection the Abyss. The technologies used by the Orokin to hunt mages used Void energies in their construction and functions. Even the Neural Sentry must use the taint in its workings for it to be so effective. The Void might have corrupted the Orokin, convincing them to embrace the Lie so utterly.
                                            There a however holes with this theory, most notably of all, Tenno, humans that were corrupted by high levels of Void energy are not beacons of Paradox made manifest. And certainly, actually being in the Void doesn’t really affect a mage’s own powers that much. In fact, some say they have become stronger… Still, officially, the Pentacle maintans the stance the Void is not to be entered.
                                            The Seer Ministries do not know the truth of the matter anymore than the Pentacle, but they’re willing to perpetuate the rumor, especially since it means that they could access the Orokin towers while their rivals may not. Yet, there is always a hint of doubt, a fear that something within the shadows would come and claim them.
                                            Still, as mages are driven to seek truth above all else, the mysteries of the Void and the Orokin continue to tantalize them so.


                                            Mages enslaved by a Neural Sentry may never increase their Arcana or Gnosis, they may only benefit from Creative Thaumaturgy if they include at least one Yantra inspired or created by the Orokin of old. They may use Legacy Attainments, Praxes and normal Arcana Attainments freely, but Rotes must incorporate Orokin symbols to work.

                                            Mages in the Origin System
                                            Mages as always will continue to seek out mysteries as is their nature. The challenges of the Origin System have not changed that.

                                            Grineer: Problematic, but manageable. Mages do not really care for the Grineer both because they are worldly problem and because the Grineer rarely produce mages of their own. The Grineer however always constantly act like big brutes, forcing anyone they find to serve them, mage or otherwise. Unlike the Orokin before them, the Grineer are nowhere near as good at mage hunting. Still, most mages do not have plans in the event that a Balor Fomorian and its escort ships aim their guns at a mage’s Sanctum.

                                            Corpus: As the direct successors to the Orokin, the Corpus hold the highest number of Void based technologies, most especially the “Nullifier” portable bubble shield. The Pentacle Orders maintain never to catch Corpus attention for good reason.

                                            Infested: A serious threat, especially since the speed at which they can infect a body can often kill and convert a mage into a slavering mutant. Still, most mages do not concern themselves with the infestation, though a few cabals exist to try and find a cure - has not worked yet so far.

                                            Tenno: The Tenno are distrusted by the Pentacle mages, mostly because of their known connection as creatures tainted by the Void. Still, one radical theory held by some, especially some members of the Free Council posit that the Tenno are mages themselves, those that Awakened to a Sixth Watchtower.

                                            "There is a remedy for everything but death, a hope for everything but wickedness, and everything will lapse except righteousness."
