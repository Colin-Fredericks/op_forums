                    Sorry for the double post, but I wanted to respond to xenoterracide and I figured I should separate this out. I've put together some ideas for a Proto-Mekhet template; I might have gone a bit overboard with the fluff on this one, but I'd love to know what people think:

                    Hollow Ones

                    Throughout the villages of the People, elder hunters and well-traveled traders share stories of the Hollow One. The story always begins the same way, with a traveler who becomes separated from their companions somewhere deep in the wilderness. Perhaps they fell into a swift-moving stream and were carried away faster than their fellows could follow, or into a ravine to steep to be safely descended. In some versions of the story, the traveler is snatched up by some horror that sends their companions fleeing, and are abandoned to their fate.

                    In any case, the traveler is left for dead, with no grave or funeral to set their spirit to rest. The traveler’s companions return home and tell the story of their lost companion, and the community mourns but can do no more. In time, the mourning period passes, and life continues much as it always has; travel is very dangerous, after all, and such tragedies are hardly unheard of.

                    And then, one night, when the moon is new and the stars are covered by clouds, the traveler returns, seemingly alive and whole. At first the community is reluctant to embrace the traveler, for ghosts and worse things have been known to take the body or likeness of a loved one in order to work evil in a village of the people. But the traveler knows their friends and loved ones, and seems at first unchanged, if confused and troubled by their ordeal, and before the night is through they are joyfully embraced once more by their family, even if other villagers still have misgivings.

                    When the traveler sleeps like a stone through the whole of the next day, their loved ones make little of it. They had traveled far in the night, and had gone long without the comforts of a roof and sleeping-mat. Soon, however, it seems as if the traveler has contracted some strange sickness, for each day they remain as still as a corpse, and each night they rise complaining of hunger but unable to keep down any food that is given to them. The light of fire hurts their eyes, and they take to wandering in dark places, creeping silently while they mouthe a constant stream of soundless babble to themselves. When confronted about these lonely sojourns, the traveler reacts with the dazed confusion of a sleepwalker, and cannot remember what possessed them to behave so strangely.

                    The traveler’s family consults with one of the Wise who lives in the village, a young willworker on the Path of Blood, but neither medicine nor magic seems to provide any relief for their condition. Before long, however, someone close to the traveler discovers something that no amount of wishful thinking can explain away. In some versions of the story, the traveler’s spouse follows them in secret on one of their somnambulant rambles, only to find the traveler bent over the body of some small beast, sucking desperately at a ragged hole in its throat with hot blood dripping down their chin. In others, a friend comes to call on a night when the moon is clear and bright, and gradually comes to to the horrifying realization that the traveler casts no shadow in the pale light.

                    The traveler’s family fears that the traveler has been possessed or claimed by some baleful spirit, and the traveler offers no resistance when they are brought before the local Wise. Although the holy man can offer no promises of salvation, he agrees to examine the traveler in order to determine the nature of their condition. What the Wise One discovers horrifies him, for while he can find no spirit nor sign of spiritual claiming, when he peers into the soul of the afflicted traveler he sees only a hungry darkness staring back at him. In his shock he turns upon the traveler with a burning brand, and to his surprise the traveler suddenly vanishes from his sight.

                    For many nights thereafter, the people of the village hear the forlorn wailing of the Hollow One, emerging from the darkness beyond the reach of the furthest hearth fire.The family of the traveler is left to mourn once more, and none dare venture beyond the lights of the village until the wailing has at last grown distant enough that it no longer troubles the sleep of the living.


                    As with many such tales, versions of the story of the Hollow One is far more common in the lands of the People than the creatures which they describe. Hollow Ones are a rare sort of walking corpse, little understood even by the Wise who study such creatures. Like many others of their ilk, the Hollow Ones arise from the bodies of the abandoned dead and hunger for the flesh and blood of the living, but they are marked by many peculiarities. Many, like the Hollow One in the story above, do not fully understand the nature of their condition, and strive to return to some semblance of their former life. This never ends well for them.

                    The Hollow One template consists of the following:
                    • A Sleep Like Death: Unlike certain other, similar beings Hollow Ones are not physically harmed by sunlight, but this doesn’t mean that they can go about freely during the day. From Dawn until Dusk, a Hollow One slips into a dormant state indistinguishable from death. The creature’s illusion of humanity slips away, and through the daylight hours it resembles nothing more than a corpse, if a strangely well-preserved one. While resting in this state, Hollow Ones are invariably troubled by vivid and surreal dreams. A few learn to interpret these strange visions, and gain seemingly impossible insights into other times and places.
                    • Feeding: The Hollow Ones require the blood of the living to maintain their undead state, and there is nothing elegant or subtle about the way in which they feed. They Possess no fangs, and in order to feed they must either incapacitate or immobilize their target and then inflict enough bite attacks to start dealing lethal damage. Once blood is drawn, the Hollow One can feed as described in Vampire: the Requiem 2nd Edition, but their bite confers neither the Scarred nor the Swooning condition, and they have no ability to close the wounds their feeding inflicts. Most Hollow Ones can feed on animals, but more potent bloodsuckers may require human or even supernatural blood.
                    • Hungry Darkness: The darkness that fills the wounds in a Hollow One’s soul is an animate, hungry thing, but it is patient and subtle compared to the Beast that resides in the heart of every modern Kindred. Hollow Ones are not subject to Frenzy, but creature inside of them threatens to take control when it is not adequately fed. The triggers and roll to resist this condition are the same as those for a hunger frenzy as described in Vampire: the Requiem 2nd Edition, save that the Hollow One also risks losing control any time it wakes with two or fewer vitae. When overcome with hunger, a Hollow One gains no bonuses to physical rolls, but adds her Potency to any rolls related to stalking and capturing prey.
                    • Imitation of Life: During the night, Hollow Ones are superficially indistinguishable from living humans, although this can change as the creature ages. Although they have no need to carry out the normal processes of biological life, they still breathe and their heart keeps beating, if at a constant, measured pace. It’s a cherade, of course, a sort of instinctual prey mimicry combined with memories of true life. The illusion is fragile, as well: Hollow Ones do not bleed when cut or bruise when struck, have no need to excrete, and cannot perform sexually. Their body is cool to the touch, and they cannot consume more than a little food without becoming violently ill and emptying their stomach in a hideous and bloody manner. A Hollow One might be able to pass as a very ill human, for a time, but their disguise doesn’t stand up to prolonged scrutiny.
                    • Mimicry: Perhaps as a consequence of their keen senses, Hollow Ones are blessed with an uncanny talent for mimicry. If given the opportunity to closely observe a subject for at least an hour, the Hollow One can thereafter flawlessly imitate the subject’s voice, manner of speech, and physical mannerisms. This does not allow a Hollow One to actually change their physical appearance, but it does grant a +2 bonus to Subterfuge related to disguise.
                    • Preternatural Senses: One of the most distinctive characteristics of the Hollow Ones is the acuity if their senses, which extend far beyond the capabilities of any physical sense organ. They gain all of the benefits of Kindred Senses and the Acute Senses merit, as described in Vampire: the Requiem 2nd edition, and additionally never suffer any die penalties from darkness. Additionally, they can sense the presence of ghosts and other beings in Twilight, although this ability is not precise enough to determine a being’s exact location. A Hollow One can distinguish a supernatural being from an ordinary mortal with a successful Wits + Composure roll, although they must still taste the subject’s blood in order to learn any specific or detailed information about their nature.
                    • Wandering Shadow: The Hollow Ones cast no shadows, nor do they possess reflections or echoes, and they leave no footprints no matter how soft the ground. The Wise believe that the Hollow Ones have lost some crucial part of themselves, leaving a metaphysical “hole” in their being which has been filled with shadows and otherworldly whispers. In this they are more right than they know, for this “missing piece” is both real and active, a ghostly double that is forced to restlessly wander the earth for as long as the Hollow One remains, endlessly hungry and hateful. These spirits manifest as shadows cast by flame or reflections cast in stagnant ponds, and can feed on carrion and other rotting organic matter despite lacking a physical form.
                    • The Lonely Rebirth: Hollow Ones cannot embrace, nor can they create Ghouls or Revenants. When a Hollow One’s ghostly double encounters an unburied but relatively intact human corpse, it will sometimes attempt to enter the body as if it were a possessing ghost or spirit. Its reasons for doing this are mysterious to those few who are aware of the origin of the Hollow Ones, but the result is always the same: the first ghost emerges, followed shortly by an incorporeal double of the deceased. At the beginning of the following night the body arises as a Hollow One, possessing the memories it had in life but no knowledge of its new nature or how it was created. Bodies which have been ritually consecrated by any sort of formal funerary rite seem to be immune to this transformation unless they have first been deliberately desecrated in some way, even if the body is not physically buried.
                    • Vampiric Traits: Hollow Ones suffer damage and the consequences thereof in the same manner as the Kindred. They may spend vitae to heal or activate Physical Intensity, but they do not have a Predatory Aura and cannot Lash Out. They begin with and can gain Touchstones as normal, and begin with 7 Humanity at character creation. In addition to their basic abilities, Hollow Ones may purchase the Bloodhound, Distinguished Palate, Dream Vision, Haven, and Touchstone merits, and the Auspex, Celerity, Obfuscate, Potence, and Resilience Disciplines, as well as appropriate Devotions.

                                                Those Who Dream
                                                Though most of the surviving Pangaeans lived on as ethereal spirits or physical Horrors, some escaped the Sundering by other means. These primal gods chose to flee into the Temenos, sacrificing their earthly forms in order to live on as creatures of the Dream. In doing so, they retained their ties to the Arcana, and learned how to shape the world through the collective dreams of Humanity. These Pangaeans would go on to become conceptual gods found in and around the Soul of the World. Some would even immerse themselves in the Dream's most primordial depths, where they would undergo another transformation.
