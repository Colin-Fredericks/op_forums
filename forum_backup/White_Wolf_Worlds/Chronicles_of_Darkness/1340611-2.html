                    I had it as a sidenote that is largely unimportant to the main thrust of the conversation and other people decided they wanted to confront me about it. In fact, it was literally you who decided to make an issue out of it.

                    As it relates to Princess, if we must go through this, then

                    The Chronicles of Darkness is a game fundamentally about monsters and using the various ways in which their splats are monsters as a means of looking at people and the world for it's humanist (and other) themes and ideas-to this end, every splat must account for the fact that it's entity is a monster and answer How That Is, Why That Is, and What The Implies. Internal, personal horror is an extension of that, that there are things about the way the various splats view things or interact with the world or experience it that belies the monstrous nature, that enforces an angle of alienation. Predacity is also a significant force and metaphor at work within any of the splat, a temptation if not a neccesity that invariably works it's way in, that people will "eat" one another given the right or wrong set of circumstances. Chronicles is also a game of dark mystery, where in the main form of darkness is not predicated on "evil", "immoral", "unethical", or "squicky" but upon uncertainty, upon information not being clear, with true intentions being murky, with being, well, mysterious. Every other gameline engages in the splat type as monster, how that works, how they may prey upon others, and the mystery and hard decisions of the game.

                    Princess the Hopeful is a game that predicated on it's main source of horror being external, that it's not the condition of being a magical girl that is where it's horror comes from, but from the atrocity and despair that surrounds them. While minor elements feed into the theme of predacity and what drives people to commiting acts of it, by and large the Nobility doesn't really predate on others, even in their darkest hours without popping over into the villain groups. Nothing about it used either as a monster or in that way the Monster-As-Lens idea is used within the rest of the canon. They also fundamentally dodge the dark mystery theme and genre because, while every noble is fundmentally different and have their own angle on things, it's hard to feel the dread uncertainty so important for the setting when every Noble is essentially aligned to a metaphysical force of Unapologetic, Indisputable Good.

                    It does not fit in with the rest of the Chronicles worlds on this tonal and thematic level-and honestly, a Lot of people love it For That Reason. I like it In Spite of That, leaning on everything else that it does well. It's not a problem, it is simply a thing to note for people who may be looking for certain things.

                    Now if you want to keep arguing about Mages and Sin-Eaters, make another fucking thread for it already. Otherwise, stop wasting our time and distracting from the thread's focus.

                                                            I like Princess quite a lot. I know there's dedicated systems out there to play anime-style games, but I'm not familiar with those. I'm a horror fan who's an incidental anime fan, and an incidental anime fan who's an even more incidental magical girl fan. Princess took a genre I thought was neat and made it accessible to me by tying it to a setting and game system I was more familiar with.

                                                            At the same time, I'd have a hard time integrating Princess into most CofD games. (And this is speaking as someone who's run part of a game in all-girls high school...) It's not the themes so much as it is the feel. I frankly could not drop something like this into a session with a straight face. My players would all start laughing. And I'd laugh along with them. It's just too out of whack with the larger setting's atmosphere.

                                                            I guess you could swap out a princess' transformation and other "cosmetic" elements of the game for something lower-key. But I think you risk throwing out the baby with the bathwater by making it low-key enough for the CofD. The sparkles and flashy effects, the pimped-out dresses, the cutesy factor, the whole premise of innocent young waifs with superpowers... it's sort of the point of the genre. Taking that away feels like trying to fit a square peg into a round hole.

                                                            Then again, I haven't watched/read all that many magical girl stories. Maybe it is possible to do a magical girl story with an ambiance that's less sugar-sweet, more grimdark, and all-around lower-key. I'm not sure how that would look, though. Puella Magi Madoka was a pretty grimdark and deconstructionist take on the genre, and it still has a lot of cosmetic magical girl trappings that would feel out of place to me in the CofD. The kawaii-looking companion critter might actually be a ruthless cannibal, but even a superficially kawaii critter doesn't have a place in the setting I prefer.

                                                            Next to revising the game's atmosphere, tweaking its themes is easy. Get rid of the queens as all-powerful forces of cosmic good in favor of a setup like Puella's. Have the girls empowered by a force with more ambiguous and potentially sinister motives, and make unraveling its purpose a bigger element of the game. Turn the "bad" queens into ascended princesses who've cut their ties to that force and argue their way is the only way to make a lasting difference in the world.

                                                            Heck, that setup actually interests me more than 2e Geist's "sin-eater social activists fighting the good fight against the 1%" does. 1e Geist lacked a clear purpose and direction for sin-eaters, but turning them into one of the closest things the CofD has to a good guy faction bored me to tears. At least hunters had PC-inclusive factions like Ashwood Abbey and the Cheiron Group who upheld the Vigil for purely dickish reasons.

                                                            Princesses being turned into monsters by external rather than internal forces is an interesting point, but I think the CofD has room and precedent for more thematically divergent splats. Beast celebrates being the PCs being monsters rather than making feel bad for it, down to having no Integrity-equivalent stat. Granted, I also didn't like Beast, so gamelines with bigger thematic divergences are less likely to appeal to as many fans.

                                                            Blood and Bourbon, my New Orleans-based Vampire chronicle.
