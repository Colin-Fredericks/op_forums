            Dominate weak? Haaaa.... That is a gross misunderstanding. Dominate is plenty powerful. That said, it absolutely does lend itself to certain styles of play over others. All Disciplines do. So, you really can't say that one is more powerful than the others, just that your personal playstyle, or your table's style, favors one over the other. I've seen games where hair trigger Lashing Out makes Nightmare and Majesty less useful on other vamps, for instance. In games with less investigations, Auspex becomes less useful, while more useful in heavy investigation games.

            Every discipline has things it can do no one else can. They all have their niches that they excell at, themes they relflect. None of them are overall weaker than others; its a question of using the right tool for the job.

                I think the usefulness of different disciplines depends mostly on play styles. By which I mean it depends both on how you play your character, but also how your group portray the game.
                Preferable all disciplines are portrayed and well rounded in your game, but any discipline can be the be-all-end-all in your game depending on how you/your group/your ST plays/portrays your game.

                For example, in our combat focused game Resilience is the most important discipline of them all (followed by Vigor). It started out as incredibly useful by its own, but after over a year of playing (and our characters ranging from BP 7 to 10) it's a must because it's the prerequisite for Juggernaut's Gait. In a game where combat is rare or practically non-existent Resilience and Vigor would probably be the worst disciplines to invest in.

                Edit: My point is that if the niche a discipline fills doesn't come up in your game the entire discipline can become worthless regardless of how much mechanical oomph it packs.

                            Originally posted by tsusasi View Post
                            Auspex undoes every application of Dominate save level 5, invalidates foundational uses of Nightmare and stealth applications of Protean, unravels Majesty, counters Obfuscate. The only discipline it doesn't really adversely affect is the physicals. Even using Animalism for spying or ambush can be thwarted and expose the user.
                            Knowing that someone (or yourself) has been victim to a discipline can be incredibly important, but it does not undo the effects of that discipline. You still carry out commands, remembers the same implanted memories (except with Auspex 4, see below), feel drawn the same way, feel the same fear, see the same things, etc, even if you know it's due to a discipline.
                            When it comes to Nightmare 1 and 4 you'd still hallucinate, but knowing the hallucinations aren't real would practically counter them, but the main things with Nightmare 1 is the Intimidate bonus and inability to spend Willpower offensively while Nightmare 4 is the most effective as long as you don't suspect it to be a hallucination in the first place.

                            The only two thing Auspex actually does counter is Obfuscate and Dominate 4.
                            Obfuscate can only be countered for yourself unless you use the devotion Shatter the Shroud (Auspex ••, Vigor •). The other thing is Dominate 4, but that would require that someone realizes the memories has been tampered with, and it requires Auspex 4 to trigger a Clash of Wills. And you can't do it on yourself, so find someone you trust with Auspex 4 to take a look inside your head.

                            There's a third power that might look like it would be countered by Auspex, but it really won't. Nightmare 3 grants Delusional which can be resolved before the duration ends by facing proof that the delusion isn't real. Auspex can certainly help, but only as an investigative tool. And since you're literally delusional you don't think something is off so why would you use Auspex in the first place?
