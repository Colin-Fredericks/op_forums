        I've seen art of old werewolves, but, you know, that's creative-license.
        Werewolves are not connected to the Shadow in a similar fashion to the Purified, who are immortals made what they are by ritual means.

        There's no published content in 2e, though the 1e book Blood of the Wolf says that becoming a werewolf doesn't reverse aging, but aging seldom has an effect on Uratha until their 90s or 100s, and they tend to look no more than half their age at that point (counting the age they had their First Change normally in contrast) — the difficulties of attending a 30-year high school reunion still looking like you're in your twenties is highlighted, and high Primal Urge seems to enhance the effect.

        Given that 2e's material on the First Change restoring mobility and senses that one had lost as a mortal is contrary to the 1e material, How Werewolves Age is ultimately going to have to be a judgment call in your individual games.

                    There is, in fact, an Azlu-werewolf hybrid in 1e supplement Hunting Ground: the Rockies — see Broken Web on page 109 — but it's an Empty Wolf created by the idigam that was defeated within living memory.

                    Going from more recent material, Night Horrors: Shunned by the Moon's chapter on the Hosts includes on its first page a set of notes that the Shards of Hosts are spiritually incompatible with Claimed and nonhuman animals and that werewolves' regeneration prevents the Shard from killing them to take over their body.

                    There's the very loose story hook that powerful Shards might be able to overcome these limits, but just running through the general principles the fact that the process kills the body and then uses more Shards to further empower the hybrid indicates that the only practical differences are liable to stem from plot hooks based on power in the flesh of the person thusly subverted. Werewolves have the precedent of being more nourishing to eat for Hosts, but between Claiming alone making a subject off-limits for typical Shards and about half the splats having some type of eject button, regeneration, or spiritual weirdness about them (up to and including "a legitimate case can be made that this body is a particularly durable and elaborate construct made of ghost snot") we go well outside the realm of Simple Questions with Simple Answers unless "if they can it's significant enough to be a plot device that we can't tell you how to run" is satisfactory.

                                            Originally posted by Bwhite24 View Post
                                            In a game like werewolf combat is almost guaranteed. But for a character that’s less combat focused how do you avoid being useless?
                                            Social/political maneuvering to get more assets for the pack, investigating mundane and occult aspects of your locale to arm yourself with knowledge, sabotaging prey with hit-and-run tactics, and making use of the multiple Merits that let you specialize your forms so you at least have some niche circumstances in which you can be more physically threatening than a newly Changed werewolf.

                                            Also, one advantage to the way Creating The Pack works is that everyone has at least one extra character who isn't a werewolf that you can leverage to split the hunting party up to pursue on different leads on a story's focal problem; the Ithaeur doesn't need to be as deadly a combatant as the Rahu if they're accompanying two Wolf-Blooded packmates to do research in a spirit-haunted library.

                                            Finally and most simply, in the absence of mechanics that specifically stop this, every extra attacker in a fight pings the prey's Defense down further whenever they opt to use it before the Initiative cycles around again. Unless you're not just less combat-focused but actively bad at combat in spite of the natural advantages of the Uratha, having an extra pair of claws to throw at a problem is usually pretty helpful.

                                                    Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
                                                    Since vamps don't appear on film (traditionally), would video-footage of the People in Garau, incite the delirium? Maybe willpower bonus? Kinda like the reverse of what vamps get?
                                                    The phenomenon you are asking about in Forsaken is called Lunacy; Delirium is an Apocalypse thing. Vampires in Requiem 2e likewise only outright don't appear in recorded media if they're the sort that also don't show up in mirrors, cast shadows, or have an echo, because those things belong to the unfriendly ghost that serves as their modified clan bane; most of the Kindred just instinctively know where to stand and/or are the subject of weirdly persistent errors in whatever image recording technology is being used to try to capture their likeness against their intention.

                                                    1e had a note that most people with a Willpower of 8 or lower would refuse to believe a picture of a werewolf in Gauru form isn't a hoax, in that version of the game's model of Lunacy as scaling in effects with the observer's Willpower rating. While 2e does not have a similar note, the "Concepts in Werewolf: the Forsaken" section of the corebook's Storytelling chapter suggests expanding the scope of Lunacy to hints of honesty about being a werewolf to emphasize the alienation it brings to the Uratha; the closest thing to a "Willpower bonus" involved is the fact that Lunacy is a modified Integrity breaking point roll for which exceptional success provides the equivalent of having Willpower 10 in the old system.
