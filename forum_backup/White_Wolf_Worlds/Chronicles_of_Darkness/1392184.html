              Werewolf 2e will be a good reference for the Shadow which is compatible with Mage 2e.

              The Shadow is not the Umbra. For the most part it's one realm that corresponds to and reflects the nature of locations in the material realm, not a spectrum of shallower and deeper levels. (There are some places that exist in Shadow but not in the Material Realm, and the larger and more secret of these are sometimes referred to as "deep" places in the Shadow. But they're more like pockets than entrances into some other layer of Shadow, and they're rare exceptions.)

              The Astral Realms are more like what you're picturing, but are still not quite the Umbra. The Astral Realms do divide into successive layers of deeper submersion, with deeper layers of the Astral Realms reflecting more universal and cosmic expressions of the world-soul. But the "shallower" astral worlds aren't closer reflections of the Material Realm like the Penumbra would be. Instead the "shallower" astral worlds are more individual: one typically enters the internal world of a single person's soul, then can venture deeper into the collective unconscious of groups and humanity, and finally deeper into the world-soul of animals, plants and seemingly inanimate matter. Mages also typically don't travel to the Astral Realms like a spatial location, but meditate and project their soul-consciousness to awareness within them, while their body remains in the Material Realm, dreaming.

                  I don't know much about the Umbra and the White Wolf wiki isn't very helpful. It appears that the Shadow resembles Penumbra as perceived by Garou, but without previous knowledge I can't say that for certain. The Shadow reflects not just Earth but also outer space, so the Deep Shadow isn't treated as a separate realm - it's just challenging to get there. Some things visit from there often - Sun and Moon Spirits, sent by Helios and Luna. Other things are not meant to breach Lunar orbit. If you want to know more about them, check "Shunned by the Moon" - a book of antagonists for Werewolf: the Forsaken. Also, check for Idigam - bizarre Spirits trapped on the Moon, some of whom escaped piggybacking on Apollo missions. They're described in "Werewolf: the Forsaken 2nd Edition" and "Night Horrors - Wolfsbane". You can also search the Forums for examples, especially those posted by Acrozatarim. The term "Deep Shadow" exists also in Chronicles of Darkness, but refers to inaccessible parts of the Shadow. Little has been written about it.

                  The nature of the Shadow remains unchanged between editions and gamelines. "Book of Spirits" describes it from a Mortal perspective and "Predators" (for Werewolf: the Forsaken) describes Spirits from Uratha perspective. "Fallen Worlds" for Mage: the Awakening has only ever been announced, so it doesn't count. Take note that some of Spirit mechanics are different between 1st and 2nd Edition, so you need to pay closer attention:
                  • Bans were split into Bans and Banes.
                  • Numina were split into Numina and Manifestations.
                  • 2nd Edition introduced Manifestation Conditions.

                          Correct. Bans and Banes will demand some creativity on your part. In 2nd Edition, every Spirit has a Ban (something that limits or controls their behavior), and a Bane (something that hurts them, even when intangible). In 1st Edition Spirits only had one or the other. Manifestations and related Conditions will require you to familiarize yourself with the rules - no way around it.

                          Book of Spirits also has some Merits, cursed objects and miscellaneous mechanics, but you could probably use them without any changes.

                              The Shadow reflects the spiritual resonance of the Earthly area it corresponds to, typically more directly in places where the Gauntlet is thinner; this means the Shadow of human population centers tends to get a little weird compared to the faux-megafauna and scaled-up geography of the wilderness, with the artificial choirs taking a more organic cast to their features and an awful lot of Silent Hill-esque body-architecture.

                              Places-That-Aren't are the aforementioned areas that lack material reflections, and they're often claimed as palaces and hideouts by more powerful spirits and their courts; the lack of direct correspondence with mundane reality allows them to get a little weirder than usual for a place where everything is alive and hungry and conceptually egocentric.

                              The Shadow's also got a nebulous and poorly-understood connection to the deepest major layer of the Astral, the Dreaming Earth; werewolves experiencing particularly potent dreams project their consciousnesses there, unaware of and immune to the usual hazards of being in the raw manifestation of the world's soul, and the core of the Begotten's power ultimately hails from this root spiritual reality with a heavy layer of myth, legend, and nightmare connecting it to the human memescape.

                              The dwelling of two of the Aeons in the deep depths of the Astral can notionally reach into the Dreaming Earth and out of the Astral frame of reference altogether to touch the Shadow; it's possible that one of them and/or some of their other "siblings" are responsible for giving birth to the Families of Horrors of Beast: the Primordial.

                              On the opposite end of things, the historic destruction of an entire intervening border-world between the Shadow and the material sphere has produced a lot of long-lasting complications — Pangaeans, pillars of the world bridging flesh and spirit, were powerful manifestations of concepts like seasons and animals and geographic features, and of the ones that weren't completely killed off, most either gradually turning into spirits over centuries of time in the Shadow or dividing their forms and consciousnesses into countless pieces known as the Hosts to reunite and splinter and meddle with the world (especially the Gauntlet).

                              Pangaeans were notable for a couple of things, not least being that they and the Aeons are the only two non-Supernal unAwakened beings with access to Arcana and they could all physically meditate themselves into the Astral on either side of the division between the human soul and the world-soul.

                              Adding to all this, the way that ley lines and Hallows work connects loosely to the flow of Essence in the Shadow; ley lines produce consistent Resonance that affects the forms ephemera takes in the Shadow, forming complex nodes where they intersect, and powerful Hallows tend to exist along ley lines and conduct their Resonance as long as long as they're producing Mana (which they do as long as it's not allowed to accumulate to its limit as tass). These phenomena in turn sometimes serve as preconditions for the creation of a Supernal Verge, if not an Iris into Emanation Realms expressing particular Truths about an Arcanum (like Yggdrasil with Sacrifice [Spirit] or the Singularity with Progress [Time]).

                              The thing to remember about the Shadow is that, while exceptions exist and ancient mages often treated unexplored individual locales as separate realms because the dimension is hostile territory, the Shadow is the spiritual reflection of the whole world, connected top-to-bottom and left-to-right in much the same way as mundane geography. Mages deal with a lot of stuff that's obscure even by supernatural standards, though, and nothing demonstrates that better than the wealth of other worlds their powers open up to them as tourists and interlopers; werewolves handle the Shadow as a place in a larger context that affects them because they have to live with it, but mere curiosity and personal circumstances are the more common reasons for a mage to deal with anything in another world, and Places-That-Aren't and other oddities beyond the quirks of Shadow ecology are very curious indeed.

                                                          None of the realms you described appear in the Shadow. All of them would, however, fit in the Astral Realms (with the Near Umbral realms being mostly Temenotic realms, and the Far Umbral realms being parts of the Anima Mundi). I also happen to know that there's a part of the Umbra (don't know if it's Near or Far or something else) that is a lot closer to the Underworld in CofD.
                                                          The Shadow is a (somewhat distorted) reflection of the physical world. Not a reflection of every concept that may appear in the physical world; but the physical world itself.*
                                                          Many spirits do reflect more abstract concepts (emotions being quite common anywhere humans gather), but they are all always spirits first with their nature (what concept they reflect) being their "flavour". A spirit of fire, a spirit of serenity, and a spirit of hedgehogs will have quite different personalities, but they will all be spirits and share the same base drive of wanting Essence (which they either gain from sources of their nature, like a monk being serene or a hedgehog, or by hunting compatible spirits to gain their Essence). To this purpose they may try to influence the physical world to gain additional lr stronger sources of Essence. The spirit of serenity might induce calmness (quite possibly to the point of apathy) in people, the spirit of fire will fuel fires or start new ones, and the spirit of hedgehogs might bind itself to a hedgehog to ensure a steady supply of Essence while constantly being close enough to protect it (or just straight up Claim it, creating a physical and mutated spirit-hedgehog fusion horror). This is generally what the player faction of werewolves try to prevent (though there are many other threats as well).

                                                          *With a few exceptions, but they tend to be somewhat rare and special in their own way.
