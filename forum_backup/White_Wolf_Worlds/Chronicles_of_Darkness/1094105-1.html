    The thing is that the 1e pledge system was both a piece of changeling society - much like the 2e pledge system - as well as a general buff. Was it mathematically taxing - maybe at first, but it provided something that the 2e system has yet to really do: Incentive to actually -use- it.

    Let's take a look at Sealings. From what I am understanding from your last post - it doesn't matter that the cost is negligible - all that matters is the cost is there, and because there is a cost, there is an incentive to be careful about your words. You don't, after all, want to have to spend a glamour to undo another changeling's Sealings. Except, in the same respect, that implies there is -also- an incentive not to use Sealings - I mean you could, but either your going to spend a glamour committing a person to their word, or your going to be spending a glamour to make them spend a glamour, just so they can know your serious about the subject that you were referring to. There isn't really much net gain.

    Compare it to when someone makes a promise to you in 1e. You accept that promise and acknowledge it. That person now receives a bonus, a duration in which to keep/fulfill his promise, and a penalty if he does not keep it. There -is- a glamour cost IIRC - but at the very least that glamour cost will -do- something. It will benefit a person who means to follow through, and detriment a person that doesn't. 2e Sealings don't really have this kind of enforcement.

    The addendum on bargaining was a statement added at the end of that to explain why, to me, the bargain is less comfortable than the ensorcellment which arguably provided the same threat - the real difference is your already relatively safe with one than you are with the other. I was not stating they had a similar narrative function, or even a similar function besides the end result of them -both- being a leap of trust in part of the mortal.

    The prerequisitive of being in another dimension or them seeing you with a dropped mask does not somehow make the bargaining mechanic better. If I make a mistake in trusting that person, it's still a mess I have to clean up rather immediately - and sometimes those cleanups are not so cut and dry as 'removing the person'.

    As far as the last paragraph, it's a matter of opinion really; 2e's pledge system isn't horrible - it's just (to me, because its all opinion at this point) like comparing a colored picture to a black and white picture. There is no real -allure- to that system and how it drives itself. I will admit - it's a LOT simpler than the 1e version because there is no math involved - but simple does not actually mean 'interesting'.

    That said, we've only seen the initial previews - and the finished product may be something that combines the best of both words.

    As a side note, Satchel - I believe if we continue this conversation any more, we may be distracting from the OP's original question. If you'd like to continue it, I'd love to debate the pros and cons of the prospective 2e vs 1e pledges (heck, even the systems themselves) in either another forum or in PMs. I am always happy to debate with you, as it very much challenges my perspective of things . If not, that's fine too.

                            If you use the existing Goblin Vow system? Its pretty amazingly strong. You might not be able to make Bargains and Oaths with awesome benefits, but even a single dot of Goblin Vow (and I'm going to assume that Mantle 2 still gives everyone a Goblin Vow appropriate for the Court, so many changelings are going to have it) can get you significant bonuses.

                            +3 to +7 on any skill roll of your choice
                            +1 to +5 dice bonus to any attribute,
                            Up to a three dot merit or Contract
                            All the above can last up to a season

                            Now, you need to have a task equal to the benefit, which can be something that completely alters your life or involves routine risk to said life. There's no 1e hack of loading up on sanctions if you break the Vow - the Task is the only balancing factor.

                            And, yes, Goblin Vows do need to be thematically appropriate for whatever purview you pick. But, in my experience, that basically sums up to "stuff I was going to do anyways." If you're a fire-based elemental, then taking "small flames" as your Vow can grant you bonuses to occult, intimidation, expression, Intelligence, Wits, Presence, and give access to Cloak of the Elements, Control Elements, and Elemental Fighting FS, as well as Vainglory, Enchanting Performance. I'm sure there are others, but those are the ones that come off the top of my head.

                            Our theoretical fire elemental can start the game with Phantom Glory (elemental clause) and this Goblin Vow, and pretty much have every fire elemental-based power in the game, no XP required, as well as some significant bonuses to our dice pools for said powers. Granted, the Tasks required of you for these bonuses would probably cripple your roleplaying potential, so I don't recommend it, but that's beside the point. Actually, no, that is a big part of the point.

                            So, what is the point? All the 1e pledge benefits have made its way into Goblin Vows. You can't share it with an entire motley, or any random mortals, but you can absolutely benefit from the potent bonuses here. If anything, I think that the benefits are stronger than 1e's pledges, because Adriotness' +1 bonus can go significantly higher here and you can affect attribute rolls as well. This is balanced out because, unlike 1e, you can't skip out on paying for those benefits. 1e pledges were broken in no small part because you could load up on Sanctions and get amazing benefits at no cost. Now, you can't do that. There is always a cost, and breaking the Vow comes with Oathbreaker. Oathbreaker is scary as hell. Do Not Want. Ever. So, yeah. All the abilites of 1e pledges still exist in Goblin Vows, but its got a bit of teeth now. And that's good.

                            In my experience of playing with Goblin Vows, I've come to the conclusion that, unlike 1e where you loaded up on merits? Here we want to load up on dice pool bonuses. Why? Contracts have exceptionally good Exceptional Success benefits. Its almost like the Exceptional Success is an entirely different power on its own in many cases. You want to be able to hit the exceptional success threshold on your main powers with consistancy. Goblin Vows give you that extra oomph to be able to get that. Meanwhile? Contracts and merits are dirt cheap in 2e, and people put their xp towards those, saving the xp from skill/attribute bumps. And, yes, there are people who do buff up their combat pools to the extreme, and that's fine. Changelings don't have lots of ways of increasing weapon-auto-damage, (or armor!) like vamps, wolves, mages, or beasts.

                            Sometimes, people do get merits and contracts, but its usually on a case-by-case basis of "I just need a quick use of this power, but don't want to spend all the xp for it." Gobling Vows make great fae sorcerers, in my opinion. An Autumn Courtier with all the right Goblin Vows can pretty much do anything they need to do, and the Tasks basically serve as the magic ritual.
