    Chronicles of Occult Fantasy

    07-04-2021, 01:18 AM
    Or, my homebrew Chronicles of Darkness.

    The vast majority of my content has been trying to hone in on this peculiar distillation of influences and worldbuilding that comprise my World of Darkness. Starting back in the early 2000's when I ran my first Dark Ages: Vampire game and experimented with changing the setting, and history itself to accommodate my rowdy, and anarchic players. Ever since then I have had a taste of making settings my own. By the time I run a game the setting barely resembles the World of Darkness except for the main focus in which my players wish to run as. In the past this has taken the form of a New World of Darkness (or Chronicles of Darkness now) setting with the Kindred of Vampire: the Masquerade surgically implemented in place of the vampires of Requiem.

    As I have incorporated more influences even that has changed a bit with the parts players enjoyed being the only surefire thing that exists, with everything else morphing around them. Over the years I've come back to the same themes, symbols, and inclusions to the setting, but each game has been demonstratively different. With the advent of a toolbox setting this has felt right, but I have been accused of never having once run a World of Darkness game before. Which, in a way, is true. It's influence is diluted into the dozens of things I like, from comic books, to games, to other RPGs, to bits and strips of occult lore and conspiracy theories.

    I make this post, mainly, to have a place to maybe discuss some of my thoughts. My players are less concerned with this other than having the ability to play what they want, but as I expand out more, I want a base setting to land with, even if it comes out quite different. I realize quickly that this is going to diverge from the Chronicles at a fast pace, and I am really pulling the work of the authors out and recombining them in interesting ways. The influences and goals that come to mind are;

    1) Weird Tales
    While stories of personal horror should be doable they have not been the focus in many, many years. My influences tend towards pulp stories, weird tales, comics (especially comic-based movies), urban fantasy, and the occult. Many of these genres share traits in an almost incestuous relationship. I find this to be a boon rather than a flaw because it allows me to modulate the level and prominence of traits that they share to come to a whole that works. A distillation of the media that I enjoy.


    2) Crossover Compatibility
    It's been years since everyone wanted to play just the same splat. Chronicles is by and large more cross-over compatible than any other iteration of the system, but I intend to rewrite things to fit more seamlessly, even at the loss of some uniqueness or fullness of each splat. The aim is to keep the pieces I want, and change them to fit the new whole without losing the luster that attracted me to the idea in the first place. Along those lines, factions will grow out into more global reach and be more inclusive of all the nightfolk and metahumans that exist.


    3) Hidden, not Unknown
    In line with most weird tales, or even comic book genre conventions, the masquerade wears thin. It's not front page news, but organizations and people are aware of the fact that weird otherworldly things go on, but reliable concrete information is hard to come by. Real magic can be found in a bookshop or on the internet, but to get reliable results and rumors of vigilantes, heroes and hunters fill the street. Batman may lurk the night of Gotham, but reliable information is scarce, and the pall of myth hangs over the entire ordeal. In the light of day, and the public rooms people often don't consider the weird, but everyone has an occult encounter they keep quiet, and keep to themselves. Those with the drive to know can stumble into secret societies, conspiracies, and the occult with enough determination, but once you go there the stench hangs around you, and the reality of the world seems to run like candle wax.


    Power Stat and Fuel Source
    Every supernatural creature has a Power Stat. This isn't too different from standard Chronicles of Darkness, barring some outliers. The largest difference is everything runs on Essence. It is your power stat that tells you how you can gain and spend Essence, and ties the power of your divergence.

    Gifts and Sorcery
    Most supernatural powers will work the same way, and use the same terminology, and compatible system. Gifts are the quick, and inherent uses of Essence. From the vampires unnatural speed, to the Sorcerer's ability to project blasts of energy, or control the mind's of a mortal with but a word and a wave of their hand. Sorcery is the ways to twist and program Essence to do complex tasks, and covers all ways for Ritual Spells. Sorcery has two components, a Style which sets the Ritual Foci, Motifs, Linked Ritual Skills, Dice Pools and Spell Lists, with the second being the six Ritual Skills which influence how the ways in which you can effect the world with Essence, and the skill level to do so.

    Tier and Scale
    A concept imported from the Mage Chronicler's Guide and from the Trinity Continuum, Normal humans are Tier 1. A minor template is Tier 1.5 to Tier 2. A Major Template starts at Tier 2, and reaches Tier 3 when they hit Power Stat 6+, while Tier 4 can only be reached with an Evolved Template. (i.e. Methuselah, or Archmage templates).


    The Playable Major Templates
    Not all of these are written, or rewritten, but these are the supernatural splats which are common in the All Night Society of the occult underworld. There are other 'major template' power splats that are generally going to be NPC only, but I haven't gotten to those yet. The most notable missing are Sin-Eaters and Mummies. Irem probably existed, and a lot of Mummy backstory goes into my setting, and I love their whole schtick, but getting them to work with the rest of the crossover is a pain I haven't even begun on. Sin-Eaters are not on the list because, for one, I don't own the 2nd Edition book of it, and lastly I think the more generic Sorcerers will take the place of Sin-Eaters in having Necromancers in the setting.



    Vampire: the Masquiem
    The Vampires are separated into Lineages which are bloodlines which set their favored Dark Gifts, and their Bloodline Gift. Vampiric myth describes each Lineage descending from some dark pact, or curse given in ancient times and wending their way to modern nights. Vampires are dark aristocrats of the occult underworld. They hoard wealth and mystic power and often times will rise to the top of every local domain ensuring that they have places to feed. Vampires can only get Essence from life, and measure their strength in Blood Potency. Their Dark Gifts give them psychic and physical powers and allow them to hunt in the night. Every Lineage has a mythic Bane, and vampires will gain Banes through their unlife. They are all weak in sunlight and find themselves vulnerable, and tired in the day as nocturnal predators.


    Sorcerer: the Gifted
    One of the biggest departures from the baseline of Mage. Sorcerers are marked by supreme mental powers, and the ability to find and discern occult phenomenon. Sorcerers wield Mystic Gifts techniques for utilizing the Essence of the body and mind to affect the world with their Will. Sorcery is, obviously, a mainstay and Sorcerers have the easiest time of any splat in learning Sorcery. Sorcerers belong to a Tradition of other Sorcerers that taught them Gifts and Sorceries. This can be Hermetic Orders who teaches High Magic to Mystery Cults like the Orphic Circle with their penchant for Necromancy. Sorcerers are an integral part of the occult underworld's Covenants, and the Atlantean Order is one such Covenant dominated by Sorcerers. Sorcerers increase their Psyche (or maybe Insight... undecided on power stat name) strengthening their souls and patterns allowing them to channel more Essence. Mystic Gifts are based off Psychic Merits and some Mage Rotes. They have the greatest access to Sorcery, and can gain Essence back from rest (ie Respiration), Places of Power, and ritual oblations to channel Essence from the Otherworld. The question of Paradox will be addressed as Spell Risk during casting, but all sorcery deals with that. Using Gifts do not incur any Backlash effect, but use of Supernatural power can trigger amnesia and Breaking Points per usual, in mortals.


    Werewolf: the....?
    The moon-beasts are a disparate lot Both made and born, they can infect humans with a virulent disease that often ends in death or delirium, or they can be born of the blood. In either case, the delirium of the First change sends the soul of the Werewolf to the Otherworld connecting them with the alien Essence of the Shadow of the Earth. Some of them are brought before Gaea, while other meet the ancestors of the Tribe the Great Beasts and pledge loyalty to them. All children of Luna in the end, the werebeasts live a life between two worlds. Werebeasts increase their connection to the natural world through their Primal Urge, and and master Wolf Gifts and Spirit Gifts.


    Demons: the.....?
    Are perhaps the least done of all the splats. As much as the God-Machine fits the tone of the setting, and something like it will be kept in the form of Saturn/The Demiurge/Patron of Space-Time it is not known how demons will shake out with a more unified world. Keeping the Unchained with Embedded Gifts, and Demonic Gifts (Exploits) with a new ability to learn and teach Infernal Sorcery is an interesting idea. On the other hand, replacing the Unchained with the Possessed and turning them into a full splat could also be worth it.



    Mutant: the Revolution?
    One of the major splay changes. This is really a combination of Deviants and Aberrant. The idea I have is to use the Deviant Variation list as the basis for the Dynamic Gifts of the Mutants (perhaps keep Deviant as the name?). It covers much the same ground as Deviant, but without the emphasis on Scars and Conspiracies. Mutants happen, and they have always happened in this setting. I tend to skew with intertwining the occult with mutants, but it isn't always so. This will, in the end, increase the power of Mutants and put them on the same tier as the other Major Templates, which is intended. They will have an Essence pool like everything else, and many of the Dynamic Gifts will require an expenditure of Essence. This one will need the most help. I haven't played Deviant, and don't have an ST or player's interested enough in it. I do know that more Marvel style Mutants, or more Nova-like Deviants conflict with some of the themes of the game, even if Mutants play around the same ballpark, the ideas conflict enough to do a rewrite of Deviant. Power Stat of the Mutants would end up being Adaptation most likely, which measures the way their bodies have accommodated the new mutations, and adapted to the use of Essence.


    Dragon: the Inheritance
    One of the most rare splats, but Dragons exist in the setting. This will be taking more from Scion: Dragon than any WoD or CoD splat. There was an old Dragon: the Inheritance that could be repurposed for this splat, but it will by and large adhere to Scion: Dragon with Heirs being agents of older, less active dragons who once fought against Heroes and Sorcerers who served Sky-Beings and Gods. They'd have Inheritance as a power stat, and have Dragon Form, Draconic Gifts, and Dragon Sorcery as their powers.




    Playable Minor Templates
    Minor templates, in this sense, are those that have a power stat rated 1 - 5, and have their own, less powerful, list of Gifts. Minor Templates cannot use Sorcery by themselves (unless noted otherwise), but can learn them like a Cultist. They can attempt a Rote Spell like a mortal following instructions from a Grimoire, but they cannot develop Ritual Skills and dots in a Magic Style; the above require the ability to freely channel and manipulate Essence that goes beyond the Minor template.

    Dhampir
    Half-vampires they are living breathing humans who can use Dark Gifts, and have the ability to feed. More Blade than VtR Dhampir. They have Blood Inheritance as their power stat, and gain Essence through rest, or from feeding.



    Inspired
    Also called Talents, the Inspired are the lesser template for the Gifted. They control Essence unconsciously to give them uncanny luck, and enhance their skills. Genius Inventors, Batman, or Action Movie Heroes are all examples of the Inspired. An Inspired can Awaken through preparation, and begin to learn Sorcery like a Sorcerer, and many start there. Their Power Stat is Inspiration, which grants them one extra Willpower per point of Inspiration, and access to Inspired Gifts.



    Wolf-Blooded
    The Wolf-Blooded are those who never made the connection to the spirit world. They shift forms involuntary, and cannot make use of Wolf Gifts, but can buy Spirit Gifts. They have a power stat called Instincts which they use in place of Primal Urge, and it gives them a small Essence pool to spend on Gifts.



    Nephilim
    Children of demons who have access to Demonic Gifts and Infernal Sorcery.


    Servant Templates
    These are templates that enslave a mortal to the will of a supernatural creature. The mot common are the blood-slaves of vampires, ghouls, or sorcerous Cultists who bargain for scraps of power in agreement of service to any kind of Sorcerer.


    Ghouls
    Pretty much the same, per VtR.


    Cultists
    Mortals who can cast spells through Grimoires or by learning them via gaining exposure to the style, and the ability to cast Rote Rituals learned from Grimoires.



    A lot of this is very unrefined, as I said, so the specifics are being worked on, but I think that is a good overview of the material. I am currently working on Sorcerers, and may post the character creation bit of it. To any who read all this, thank you for indulging me, and I welcome all discussion as it helps me decide on a lot of the unknowns that I might otherwise not think of.
