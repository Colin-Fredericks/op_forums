    Think Like a Demon

    09-02-2014, 10:28 PM
    A while back I was playing a game of Demon with a friend whose character had the Play on Words Exploit. After we wrapped the story for the time being, he commented how much more aware the power made him. He was always on the lookout for a way in which to use it, and potential ruin someone by turning their heir into air or something. It put him into a distinctly exploitative mindset.

    Similarly I related to the concept of Going Loud. Going Loud is a thing you can always do as a Demon. Sure it's almost always the worst idea, and sure it's almost always going to fuck you royally, but it's flashy and it's potent as Hell and it'll get the job done. You've always got Going Loud in your back pocket, as a Nuclear Option. If ever things are absolutely, 100% totally FUBAR, you can Go Loud. Always. You, as a player, always have that ludicrously potent power to fall back on if all else fails.

    And you know what? That's pretty empowering. It's a lot of responsibility to NOT do that once you realize you can at almost any given juncture, especially when you start imagining how it will play out with the mad amount of glitches you will then manifest.

    And that's what I appreciate about this game. It gets you into a completely different, totally alien mindset, from the powers on up through the actual being of the character.

    How do YOU think like a Demon?

      Go to a local bank, look around, and figure out how to rob it. Obviously, don't do anything that is going to get yourself arrested, but go in, check out the camera locations, guard stations, entrances and exits, and the vault location.
      Make up an objective, make up plans to achieve that objective, think of things that could go wrong, think of contingency plans.
      Use Google maps to find out what the nearest police stations are, use the same to determine the best escape route, multiple escape routes.
      Listen to a police band scanner for a few hours.

                  I'd say that if a player really gets into extra preparation they should be rewarded with a beat. I'd still have them make the necessary rolls but if they're getting that into the game they deserve a little incentive to keep going.

                  As for my mindset, I love getting to look at the World of Darkness now and think: How can this be God Machine Infrastructure?
                  (P.S. Be warned, the second example gets pretty dark)

                  Employees at the local supermarket must complete "computer training" before they can be officially hired by the company. It's tedious, long, and they have to sit in an office in the back of the store where the merchandise is kept and listen to a dull droning voice give a tutorial for all of the company policies. And the questions they have to answer aren't really questions at all since even a five year old could guess the correct one. But strangely enough after their boring eight hour training is completer they are better, faster, and more friendly employees. Well, at least to customers and other employees. People who finish computer training end up more hostile and irritable to their former friends and family and start spending more and more time with their fellow store workers, but maybe that's just because they can relate to them better now. Computer training is mandatory after all.

                  A woman is going for a morning jog and happens upon a ghastly sight. A man has hung himself in the park, and seven crows sit in the branches of the tree and caw at the lady as she stares on in horror. She calls the police on her cell phone and all the birds at once promptly take to the skies. Over the next seven weeks people the woman cares about begin to die. One by one she falls deeper and deeper into grief and despair. Finally she cannot take it any more as her fiance becomes the sixth person to die when he gets into a car accident and she hangs herself in their apartment. Before she takes that final step she looks out her window, she can the seven crows watching her from a nearby tree.

                  There's an old basketball court down on 7th Street. It's been there forever it seems, since no one seems to recall when it was built. Your friends and schoolmates all tell you that the lots haunted, and that every night at exactly 12:00 the entire concrete court comes alive as wriggling worms only to revert back to normal at 12:01. You didn't believe the stories and went out after dark to prove your friends wrong. You were there when the strangers in the trench-coats came, and you saw that they threw a man bound and gagged into the court and just stood back. And you'll never forget what happened next, as the court came alive as thousand upon thousands of worms began to devour the bound man alive. But the thing you'll never be able to get out your head is how the bound man looked as he died, like he was made out of sheet metal and burned with an internal fire that slowly died as the worms chewed through his metallic flesh. You never go neat the old basketball court down on 7th Street, hell you'll be lucky if your parents ever get you to come out of your room again.

                  All Infrastructure of the God Machine

But for Storyteller master class, make sure you tie in a narrative award beyond that: information about the target, a short description of the character doing something cool or clever to justify creating the condition.
