                        Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                        Yeah, that movie is fine Demon fodder.

                        Also, adding to my last post, I should explain where I'm coming from with the power evaluations. I tend to look on things through the eyes of the storyteller (as I primarily am), and thus look on 'balance' through the eye of what makes a good story. For instance, the Werewolves attacking from the shadow argument is fantastic plot fodder, and I can already think of interesting plots and challenges such a character could inspire. As a result what I tend to view as 'most powerful' has most to do with how hard it is to interact with. Allow me an example: Werewolves in the Shadow are tough to deal with, but may provide great opportunities to employ Loci and Abjuration rules, telling an exciting story about fighting unseen forces. Compare that to something like a Demon using Four Minutes Ago or a Mage with Shifting Sands, unless you are yourself a Mage (with two dots of time) it is functionally impossible to interact with these powers. The Demon pops sideways in time and the last four minutes didn't happen, nothing can be done about this.

                        Mages and Demons have a lot of powers that can't easily be responded to. Take the Embed Lucky Break for example, no matter how hard you hide, no matter how many layers of obfuscation you put forward a Demon with Lucky Break can always find a lead, in a matter of minutes. And it can do more, it can help a Demon get out of jail, win a fight, take down doors, and so much more. Demons and Mages are loaded with effects like this, which make them fun to play, but potentially unpleasant to play alongside or against. Of course much of that comes down to the player, a disruptive player will be disruptive regardless of the splat, but it can still be a hurdle for crossover.

                                        ​Most of what you've said about Vampire disciplines is wrong:
                                        Celerity is activated reflexively. It lets you do this: "Interrupt the action of another character with a brief action of her own. This could be an attack, making it possible to disable an opponent in mid-action. It may be movement, avoiding harm by shifting out of reach. Or it could be any other instant action, like activating a Discipline or dodging." This means you can, in response to any action by any character, make an attack on them. This does not use your instant action for the turn, as you can activate it up to Celerity dots times in a turn.
                                        Vigor lets you do this: Lift and hurl objects normally far too unwieldy to use as weapons, such as boulders, cars, and other people. Any object she can lift, with Size no greater than her Strength, can be used as an improvised melee or throwing weapon​.

                                        Picking up and throwing a nearby object is a single instant action as far as I'm concerned.

                                        ​So Celerity and Vigor very explicitly allow throwing of cars at people's face as a reflexive action, when used in conjunction. Physics saying you can't do this violates rule of cool, and gets ignored.

                                        Dominate One isn't a mind wipe a ability, it's a memory altering ability. You need Mind 3 to duplicate it, and going indefinite is a lot of dice penalties and leaves it open to dispellation, or if you don't spend a Willpower *dot* in relinquishing it, that the memory alteration will eventually start to change on you.

                                        Oubliette is a full sensory hallucination applied to a location that impacts everyone and everything inside of it, that can be updated at will. At minimum you're looking at needing Mind 4 and some amount of Space, and depending on how you read the rules on scale factors and what's actually being targeted, it may not be possible to apply the correct set of spells to impact everyone in the area in the same way that Oubliette does. Imposter and Incognito Presence are no where near sufficient. And even with properly scaled versions of Hallucinate, you'd have to recast the spell every single time you wanted to make a change. This is more or less impractical.


                                        ​But I think I failed spectacularly at making something clear; I am not attempting to pit the splats in competition with each other, the context from which I'm operating is a mixed splat PC group. I am assuming cooperation between the splats. I was *trying* to say that in such a group the Dominate Vampire is not at all overshadowed by the Mind mage when it comes to using Dominate. In such a group the Mage is generally going to provide more utility by doing things like boosting the Vampire's Intelligence or lowering the target's Resolve than they are by trying to use Psychic Domination. Unless they really need to force a suicidal action or act outside the limits of Dominate, it's better to let the Vampire in the group do the mind control bits.
