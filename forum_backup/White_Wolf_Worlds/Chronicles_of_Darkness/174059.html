              Here's a klaive for you, off the top of my head:

              Gungnir - Level 5 Klaive
              In Norse mythology, Gungnir was the spear of Odin, king of the Aesir. According to the legends, the spear was so well balanced that it could strike any target, no matter the strength or skill of the wielder. In the legends of the Uratha, a Storm Lord alpha bound a powerful spirit of war and a great eagle spirit together to create Gungnir - a klaive capable of terrible destruction, capable of unerringly striking its chosen target.

              Gungnir does 4 lethal damage, and grants its wielder +1 Defense in close combat against any combatants who aren't themselves wielding staves, spears or polebased weapons, due to the Klaive's superior reach. By paying a point of Willpower and making a successful Harmony roll to activate the klaive, the wielder may ignore the Defense of one opponent for one strike. Armor and environmental conditions penalize this roll as usual.
              Action: Reflexive

                                                  Yeah but that sorta transition is the course of CENTURIES of work. The original migration era of the Norse religions into the original fertility religions of the time can be viewed (theoretically at any rate) with the battle of the Aesir and Vanir, which some scholars believe to be symbolic of the clashing of two world views. This can be backed by the idea that Tyr was originally the lead God of the pantheon (furthered by the definition of Tyr being -god- and one of Odin's name's being , I may be mispelling this, Hanga-Tyr, or hanging god.) It's looked upon in some circles as a rationalizing of the local peoples for the two world views coming together, with one becoming the dominant force (the germanics/Proto Norse). I would argue that in that situation you would be looking at acculturation over outright out kicking.

                                                  As for the Lap folk, the Saami are one of the few native tribes in the world to retain their religious beliefs and customs, and have a reputation for producing powerful sorcerors and witches in Nordic epics (probably due to their "strange" customs.) Remember almost all of our Norse lore that we know is written by christian scholars some 200+ years after the events happened, so naturally it has a Christian tinge to it, making it look like the take over was damn near universal. It sure wasn't. The only place it appears to be the case was Iceland in 999 where it was more due to the King being a prick and black mailing the 'ruling families' with their children's heads over any actual desire to convert. The law speaker just made the land of one religion to avoid confrontation between the two camps in the future. In other parts of Scandinavia (Sweden in particular) it took till the mid 1300's to fully Christianize. Norway went back and forth between a few kings, with seemingly every other king changing their mind on the matter.

                                                      A really cool, Scandinavian weapon you could use is the swordstaff. It's basically what it sounds like; a broadsword on a stick. Kinda like a European naginata. It is also important to note that the swordstaff still had the parry guard to aid in defence.
                                                      It is a cool, big, weapon that never really caught on anywhere else, and it looks brutal enough to look like a good weapon for an Uratha.

                                                      If you want classic viking stuff though you should go for long axes and spears really. There were very few good sword smiths in Scandinavia back in the day, so axes and spears were much more common. If you want fetishes from that actually ARE from that era though, go for swords, they were rare and special, and thus the obvious choice when one made a fetish.
