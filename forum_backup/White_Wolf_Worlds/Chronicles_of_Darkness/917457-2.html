                Number one is probably Genius. For me, while there are enough bumps and inconsistencies that keep it from being the best ChroD game, the central theme is the one that resonates with me the most, and it's why I started my remake of Awakening to incorporate the ideas of Genius more organically into ChroD.

                Demon is probably the canon line that fits the most with me, for a lot of reasons. The aesthetic, the weirdness, the mechanics, all of that stuff- it just speaks to me. And it handles themes of personal identity/lends itself to an SJ reading much better than either Promethean or Beast, which is something else that's super important to me. It's ultimately about fighting to be yourself, even if reality itself is against you- and unlike in Promethean, "yourself" can be a towering robotic colossus or a collective of disparate human identities, or conceivably anything else.

                Changeling is another one that really resonates with me, and although the themes aren't as personally relevant to me as those of Genius or Demon I still consider it overall the best ChroD game.

                I've always loved the novel Frankenstein and related to the character of The Creature more than any other fictional character, and that's why I don't like Promethean- to me, it really misses the point of the story and taints the parts that I felt a connection to.

                Vampire, Hunter, Mummy, Geist, and Leviathan are my other favorite lines, and while there are individual things in each of them that I can relate to a lot, I'm mostly interested in them because the mechanics/plot/settings are interesting.

                                    My Top 3

                                    Changeling. There's a lot of freedom with Character Creation, Also the stories you can create with your character if not creating NPCs.

                                    Hunter. I've always have been a fan of humanity facing monsters and dangers. Be it through Will, Faith, Science. Mortals facing darkness whether to triumph or be broken by it... maybe become one with it if there not careful. It's a struggle not just against the supernatural, but also with ourselves

                                    Mage. Once a mere mortal, now given So much power. But what to do? Always like the possibilities to go above and beyond, but to remember that their still consequences to your actions. Just because you gain power, doesn't mean all your problems go away and most of the time your just gonna get more.

                                    Ultimately. Changeling is my Favorite game. But Hunters Virgil speaks to me on a deep personal level.

                                                Vampire is kind of a weird case for me because I've always been fascinated by immortality, and vampires as a specific kind of immortality, but there isn't really anything about the vampire condition that I can personally relate to, since I'm not actually an immortal vice-ruled night creature. But I still find it super interesting, in more of a hypothetical way. The parts that "speak" to me most are the parts that aren't as much about addiction as about desire- the desire for immortality, or to be a better/stronger/more attractive/more popular/different person.

                                                I've always hated anything that harps on about mortality, so I'm not very interested in that as a theme in anything unless transcending it is also an option.

                                                    Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
                                                    What would be the hopeful messages and warnings of the other Paths, in your opinion?
                                                    Mastigos has quite a few. But then, so do all of the Paths. One that sticks in my mind is that everyone has a dark side, something in us that we don't like or even fear. We can't lie to ourselves about it, or wall ourselves off from other people. It's okay to be flawed. That's part of being human. We need to accept who we are, and each other, for better or worse. To confront our dark side and find a way to live with it. Or overcome it. Obviously, the ordeal to do so will be tremendously difficult, but we will be better for having made the effort.

                                                    On the flip side, knowing that everyone has this dark side means that everyone has a weakness that can be exploited. The security afforded by minds, distances or walls is not absolute.
