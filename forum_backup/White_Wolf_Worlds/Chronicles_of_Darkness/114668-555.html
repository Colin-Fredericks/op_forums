    Different comparison to further highlight the way in which this does not work: adding Death to a Ban to only forbid souls from crossing it should logically not prevent souls from vanishing at the point of death just on the strength of incorporating Death into the Ban, because that is a different thing from preventing souls from entering or leaving the Ban. This isn't an Exorcism/Banishment/Familiar/Bind [Ephemeral Being] situation — Ban is a Space spell that works on Spatial effects, not the normal consequences of killing something. It would be like Banning liquids from crossing the Ban somehow translating to not being able to boil water.

                The issue I'm seeing is with the idea that cancelling the spell causes the projected mind to "return" to the body as though it is crossing a space to do so that it can be blocked from. What I think of as the baseline for how Supernal magic works would mean that doesn't really make sense.

                It reads to me that there's no transitional process between states when a spell is involved; it's more like a binary state to reality where things change discretely between "normal" and "effect defined by imago present".

                If you cast a spell to have a longer limb, it's not creating a process that allows it to grow, it's changing the essence of how long it is; you couldn't hold it in a vice to keep it from getting shorter when the spell ends.

                So you can't put a mental projection into a space where it would matter that the originator switched off the state of reality where their mind can be separate from their body.

                Now, I do think that you can trap somebody's mental projection, but it's not based in creating a boundary for them (at least not at first), but making it so that their projected state is based on your spell, not theirs. Cast that spell on them while they're in your clutches, and it won't matter that they shut off their spell, their state is now defined by yours.

                                            I think it could be logical for objects to have sympathetic connections to other objects that were of significance to creating their state of being; a sculpture connected to the tools used to carve it, for instance. I don't think such connections would ever be stronger than Medium, and I don't think they would last very long, there's not much to maintain the association.

                                            Even then I feel a bit tentative. I read sympathy in terms of the game's overall metaphysics for the Space Arcanum, and the basic axiom of that Arcanum is "actual distance is an illusion, the truth is a matter of perception", which I would say privileges sympathy as a property of conscious beings. My main reservation is in thinking about how objects can be targeted and it feels as though it should be possible to do so with something like a piece of it taken away; a sliver of brick taken out of a building's wall, for instance.

                                            But sympathy isn't really a matter of abstract, conceptual associations, nor are such things generally possible to target with spells.

                                            I think the best bet for that sort of thing is not in Space but in Fate. That's the Arcanum with a basis for giving things certain qualifiers; conditional duration pegs spell length to particular circumstances, Oaths Fulfilled allows you to give yourself awareness of some particular outcome befalling a subject (it could alert you if a person writes a book that is a sequel to a previous work of theirs), Serendipity guides you towards desired objectives, and Strings of Fate allows you to just kind of declare some wanted result will be. I feel those give it a certain purview over abstract relationships.

                                            Either way, I think this is a Legacy where in the early stages you can give yourself some of the diagnostic tools, but are still going to have to put in a lot of the legwork.
