        The term 'Funding' is hampered by trying to match the pseudo-academic classifications used in the guide. Much like the Resources merit it doesn't pertain to an exact monetary value, and the 'wealth' of Resources can vary from one location to the next from where your chronicle is set.

        To look at your examples, Weird, the middle class or scrounger Genius starting out would have at most 2 or 3 Funding under certain kind of fields. Like work at a personal or locale business (Entrepreneur) or foraging/junking their needs for their Wonders (Salvage). Your Bond-level super villains likely have multiple sources of Funding at high values in the 4 to 5 range each.

        The reason to separate Funding into its own system is to allow for easy changes, as ideally sources of Funding will change over the chronicle in lieu with a collaborative's development, progression and travel. Although a single source of Funding (the various tag words) is rated from 1-5 dots, there is no theoretical cap to what the Genius could sustain if they can upkeep the Funding. For one Genius, Chuck, he may have 3 dots of Inheritance representing royalty checks from a prior family member's patent. Another (Roxelle) may have 3 total split amongst a part time job (Entrepreneur 1), tapping the abandoned mine for veins (Salvage 1) and stealing anything else she's missing (Crime 1).

        What each source of Funding is and how it works can be customized by players and Storytellers. Ideally each Funding should require a means of upkeep, a slight bonus and criteria that will hamper the Genius. Thus the pull into Obligation; does the Genius stick to their lifestyle means if they feel it a shackle, or do they abandon the Funding in search of other ventures? How the Genius interacts with their Funding brings a modifier to their Obligation checks.

        Using my placeholder names, Chuck only has his ancestor's royalty checks that lets him raise mycological colonies and template new diseases in the back shed. Provided he upholds the family legacy (which is a household cleaning product and here he is growing more voliate strains of mold) he has a potent bonus to his Obligation, +3 from the rating, to meet the family's financial planner's approval. However if Chucks 'fungal freaks' cause too many incidents that Chuck doesn't bother to reign in, the rating becomes -3 to the Obligation check. Not only does Chuck's Obligation lower he loses access to the Inheritance when he cuts ties with the family's estate.

        Roxelle may have lesser ratings to support or hamper her Obligation (+/-1) but she has more versatility than Chuck if she loses access to one Funding over the other. She might find her work hours, however little, to be personally offensive and quits her job. She's out that Funding dot but she has the two others from Salvage and Crime. She does much better as a budding super villain that later in the chronicle her rating of Crime increases. Conversely, anything to scrap at the abandoned mine runs out as the city collapses the entrance to prevent youngsters from harming themselves within. How Roxelle felt about this (either helping the city do the right thing or sabotaging their efforts to no avail) determines if the rating becomes a bonus or penalty to Obligation before the Funding is lost. If Roxelle buckles down to accept more work hours her Entrepreneur raises later in the story.


        In the grand scheme of things a Genius may have no Funding, which doesn't bar them from Wonder creation although their efforts are constantly plagued by improper materials, housing or energy representing continued penalties. The total sum of a Genius's available Funding serves as a budget for what they can work on simultaneously, along only a single variety of Funding can modify an Obligation check at a time. If Roxelle wanted to build two Skafoi 5 vehicles in time for beach season she hopefully would have secured 10 ratings of Funding from various sources. Whether its the major heists (Crime 5), overtime at work (Entrepreneur 3) or managing her new GoFundMe page (Appeal 2) that prevents her from attending her sister's wedding determines which rating becomes the modifier to the Obligation check.

        Perhaps more lucrative or themed Fundings could be allowed from select Foundations (and Baramins on the flip side). Whether this is an Experience discount, role-play rewards or a rating cap removal I'm not sure which is better. Funding types should change as much as Obligation would, although losing it should hurt. Not like Sanctity of Merits, where lost Merit dots can be turned into something else, although I know that having Funding be too expensive would cause Experience waste when it is gone. Although there would be nothing baring a Genius from tweaking their current Funding slightly between stories, to represent the change in setting.

                        Mage and Genius, both in terms of the themes of the game and culture of the characters, are fundamentally opposed. That has to be respected, but that said there's definitely room to improve over 1E's "Mage's think Geniuses are Abysal" but fundamentally Mage's idea that they alone see the Truth will look uncomfortably like an Unmada - even Peers who know Mages can't go Unmada will find it uncomfortable.

                        Meanwhile one thing the Diamond (and the Seers) agree on is that Mages should be responsible with power, Mage society is structured to ensure that there's plenty of checks and balances on the Awakened. Geniuses are nothing but irresponsible, and they just finished taking all those checks and balances apart and replaced them with a support group. I mean, wouldn't you be horrified if you discovered someone with the personality of a Grimm or Neid had enough weaponry to level a city block in their basement?


                        But we don't need to do more than that. We can let the Geniuses be uncomfortable that Mages think they have a unique access to the Truth and leave it at that. We can let Mages be horrified that when the leaders of the peerage see nothing wrong with letting kids build giant death robots so long as they regularly get peer review. We don't need to go beyond that to Mages thinking that Geniuses are Abyssal.


                        BTW I actually did have a Mage in a Genius game (it wasn't really crossover, it was a Genius game, we just had a Mage PC in it). The general contrast we came to was:

                        Mages are the bad kind of academics. They have claim to work together for the good of humanity but in practice they're a seething mass of politics.
                        Geniuses are tech start-ups. They're officially rivals and don't work together (without being paid for it) but they socialise more and actually like each other.

                        The game also included the relevation that there's a perpetual mania storm caused by children leaning there is no Santa Clause so the Peerage have to shoot down his sleigh every year before he leaves enough Havoc vulnerable presents to destroy large parts of the world. (I would have thought Lemuria handled stuff like that, but ah well)

                        Also Santa can make his deliveries because he is a quantum wave form, simultaneously under every tree unless somebody observes his location.

                                                        An economist creates a perfect economy, based on principles of perfectly-balanced supply and demand. it shouldn't work because of a number of reasons, but somehow, it does for as long as he's in control.

                                                        An artist creates paintings so real-looking, you swear you could step into them if you tried. When you ask him about his technique, he gets irrationally angry and demands you leave.

                                                        A philosopher has claimed to have "solved" morality, and can justify almost any crime he commits. His reasoning is flawless when he says it, but it loses something when you try to repeat it later.
