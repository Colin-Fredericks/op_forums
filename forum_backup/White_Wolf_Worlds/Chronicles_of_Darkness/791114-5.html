    Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
    ... blue and orange in their morality. I am just concerned that this new perspective will make it excessively hard for changelings to connect with non-changelings, especially mortals.
    I would like to paraphrase my understanding of what you are saying to be sure I understand.

    Fundamentally your question is something like this:

    "Aren't the socially unacceptable behaviors promoted by Seeming Blessings Clarity gain likely to result in a higher than average number of characters who pursue those behaviors to be deemed as social outcasts, dangerously mentally ill, criminal and/or outright monstrous from a sane human perspective depending on how extreme they follow those behaviors?"

    My answer is:
    Many changelings are emotionally, mentally, and spiritually scarred to the point that only providence would prevent them from being social outcasts, dangerously mentally ill, criminal and/or outright monstrous from a sane human perspective. Seeming "blessings" reflect the damage to their psyche and souls. It, in a non-figurative sense, makes their minds unlike healthy balanced mortal minds or even like the minds of other supernatural beings who don't happen to have the exact same Seeming Blessing. This means even within 2e Changeling: the Lost what is mentally bearable/acceptable clashes in ways that cause great potential for conflict.

    My favorite examples involves confining a Beast for "their own good" or "greater good" with force, which is a Clarity gaining act for Darklings, Fairest, and Ogres and a Clarity break for the Beast. It is likely the Beast would view its captors as tyrants or monsters.

                        Seeing as I've read up on the playtest compilation, can I make suggestions for expanding the Blessings and Curses, especially the latter (assuming said expansions will still get the green light).

                        -=-

                        Curses

                        Darkling: A Darkling has been injured in the past, and believes she’s injured others to survive. A Darkling trusted once, a long time ago, and anyone asking her to trust may be pushing her too hard. To trust anyone, even one’s closest allies, following them blindly, causes the Darkling to suffer a Clarity break. Furthermore, dice bonuses from having multiple Touchstones cap out at +3.

                        Fairest: With measured response and skill, or blind luck and hope, making the right choice feels damn good. But sooner or later, failure weighs on the Fairest, and the crown on her head gets heavier and heavier. Any time a Fairest makes a call as a leader, and someone gets seriously hurt physically or otherwise, it is a breaking point. Additionally, she suffers a -4 penalty to all breaking point rolls without any attached Touchstones.

                        Wizened: The line between genius and perfection is thin, and the considerable talent that the Wizened has devoted himself to can also consume him. Any time a Wizened fails an action related to his unique talent, it’s a breaking point. This does not have to be a roll for direct performance (and in fact, usually isn’t); for example, a painter may fail in procuring a very specific pigment, and suffer, or a politician might suffer a scandal that hurts her poll numbers, or a fencer breaks her weapon on the way to a duel. Failing any rolls which involve the Wizened's talent inflicts the Distracted Condition on him for a scene.

                        -=-

                        For the Darkling and Fairest, I thought their life curse could tie into aspects of Clarity because they're both Seemings that deal heavily with people. For Darklings, it's the extent they place their trust (and are trusted by) those people they regard as their Touchstones. For Fairest, the proposed life curse should showcase how badly they can get screwed when they got no support system to help shoulder some of the burden behind their bad decisions.

                        On the Wizened suffering the Distracted Condition, it just seems fitting. When you obsess so much on perfection, it can end up being pursued to the exclusion of everything else as they get overwhelmed in their own tunnel vision.

                            I also have additions/suggestions for the Ogres and Elementals in terms of Blessing and Curse. Feel free to critique on them as well.

                            -+-

                            Elemental

                            Blessing: Elementals are also treated as being near and/or in contact with their element for the purpose of invoking Contracts and Kith blessings.

                            Curse: Cold Iron also nullifies the passive benefits of the Elemental's Seeming Blessing.


                            Ogre

                            Blessing: Ogres downgrade all damage from mundane sources of harm by one level. In the case of bashing damage, they take half the amount inflicted in a turn. This blessing does not apply to damage received from deprivation, frailties, or iron.

                            Curse: Ogres cannot make use of Clarity-based bonuses while under the Delusional Condition. At Storyteller discretion, this can extend to similar Conditions which affect the mind and/or emotions of the changeling.
