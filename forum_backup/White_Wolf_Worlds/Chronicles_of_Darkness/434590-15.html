    Originally posted by Opprobrium View Post
    To return to the subject of rituals, I'm seeing potential issue with the Vestige = Arcana parralel. The themes don't seem to fit quite as clearly with a few : Awareness, Elements, Fecundity and Sanctity seem well disposed to ritual use but what of the others? What kind of rituals fall under Predation? What about Vitality given that bestowing genuine benefits on Cultists should be off the table?
    Yeah, I know that's an issue. There was a half written 1E ritual rewrite that should have some ideas. Let me dig them out

    Awareness

    Rituals that incorporate the Vestige of Awareness can pear across time and space to answer questions. A Leviathan can predict the future, unearth the past, or just watch that Atoll from afar. The curse of Awareness is that it can only uncover facts, not answers. The Tribe can gain any knowledge they wish, but they cannot answer the questions that truly matter.

    Elements

    Rituals that incorporate the Vestige of Elements can warp the world itself. The sea and sky, the Earth end even the sun's light all bend before a master of Elements. Rituals can raise terrible storms, or quell them and even twist the fabric of space and time. The curse of Elements is that every place touched by it's power is unnatural and tinged with the nature of the Tempest. Even when the Ritual ends a storm the result is not calm but an oppressive silence that can cause madness to those within.

    Fecundity

    Rituals that incorporate the Vestige of Fecundity can bestow the Tribe's life giving elements upon others. They can gift, or curse, other's with strange biological mutations. Reproduction, both physically and as a concept, fall under the purview of Fecundity. The curse of Fecundity is that no life but the Tribe can safely hold divine Ichor. The Tribe's gifts are cancerous corrupting things.

    Might

    Rituals that incorporate the Vestige of might can control physical forces. They can bestow strength and move mountains, sending crushing forces at the Leviathan's enemies. The curse of might is that the Tribe's power is to large for this world, it cannot be restrained to delicate tasks or distinguish between friend or foe.

    Predation

    Predation is the most vicious of the Vestiges. Poisons, toxins and diseases - the fruits of life turned toxic - are the tools of a master of Predation. The curse of Predation is that it is an imprecise weapon at best, a Leviathan can pick his targets one by one but she cannot blanket the land with toxins tailored to her enemies.

    Sanctity

    Rituals that incorporate the Vestige of Sanctity cover the very concepts of religion and godhood. With Sanctity a Leviathan can force others to recognise his divinity or bind people's social identities to religious faith. The curse of Sanctity forever links the Tribe to ancient ideas, the god who demands worship and punishes heresy. The Tribe can only force others to kneel, they cannot ask.

    Vitality

    Rituals that incorporate the Vestige of Vitality can protect. A Leviathan can bestow strange armours upon their subjects, bar intruders from their domains and protect against even stranger threats. The curse of Vitality is that it forms barrier between the world and the "blessed". People touched by Vitality become lethargic and unmotivated, and ever more dependent upon the Leviathan's protection.

    I hope this helps. Don't take it as an iron hard rule - especially the bit about the specific draw backs. This was just rough notes. Ultiamtely Sanctity and Elements will always be inherently better than Vitality or Might at powering rituals. I'm ok with that so long as we get at least a few good ones under all the Vestiges.

                                        To clarify things on my end, since TKR actually risked asking...

                                        One big problem is that, until just now, I had no idea that the Old Men of Marduk were still a thing. I remembered them, vaguely, from 1e, but I had no idea from what I saw presented of Marduk that they weren't retconned out and that Marduk hadn't been Squeaky Clean Perfection since the beginning.

                                        The other big problem is that, even with them still being present, there's the problem of "if they chucked the Old Men centuries/decades ago, why haven't they fixed the world? If they only just got rid of them, why are they a significant threat at all - why aren't they too busy with cleaning house and reorganizing themselves?"

                                        The biggest issue here is that it felt like the Old Men were being handwaved away - hypocritically, that Marduk is claiming to be perfection now when the truth is that they have their own dirty laundry, which means they're stained by gray from the beginning. They might be the lightest of grays NOW, but at least it should be acknowledged, instead of insisting that they're totally white and pure and incorruptible and perfect, which is a feeling I got from the talk about them there.

                                        Let's not beat around the bush here: I hate the idea of Post War Marduk being "perfect". Like Almarak had said, I'd be a whole hell of a lot less bothered if emphasis had been placed on the post war Marduk's vulnerability, and even less so if, like Princesses, they had been presented as having flaws that reasonably explained WHY the World of Darkness is still the way it is - hampered by their idealism, inherently restricted, just in some way not able to peel away the shadows completely.

                                        I actually agree with the comment earlier that the whole "Leviathans used to need a self-sacrificing, probably die in the attempt hero bolstered by magic to be stopped; now a simple unit of SWAT can probably scatter one non-epic tier Leviathan or even mortally wound it" thing quite accurately conveys how humanity is progressed beyond the Leviathans, but I admit that the idea of lightning wielding science-heroes is too tempting an idea for a Hunter conspiracy to pass up, so I'll just pass that by.

                                        Even so, I think that the whole idea of "The Light of Reason" being used to justify Marduk's perfection is an inherently flawed one, simply because of the world that this game is set in. Humanity still is in the grip of the primordial darkness that Leviathans represent in the real world, but in the WoD, that grip is even stronger. The whole idea of WoD is a darker, grimmer take on our world; it makes too much sense that any group based on that symbolism would not be as strong as they'd be in a NobleBright or a GrimBright setting. Hell! TKR even says that you should "blame mortals for making the world so crapsack".

                                        This is a world where even spirits of Love and Joy can ruin people's lives or kill them. As commented by CalvaryKeter on page 15:
                                        Reason, etc. haven't made humanity perfectly ethical people, and can be leveraged towards ill ends (i.e. nukes in most situations, electroshock torture, enabling refinement of sweatshops, building an unfriendly AI that kills us all to make paperclips).
                                        Even beyond this, in the World of Darkness, the Light of Reason is by definition a pale and flickering thing. It cannot chase all of the darkness away, and it probably never will. That's what defines this world. I would not expect a group like Marduk to feel the way that TKR is adamant that they should feel, and the insistence only puts me off of it.

                                        ...I'm tired and not thinking straight. But that's my problem in a nutshell: Marduk is far too squeaky clean, even though they shouldn't be seen that way, and far more powerful than they have a right to be in the World of Darkness. It can be one, or the other. NEVER both.

                                        There's lots of interesting ways to handle Marduk. I've seen several versions of the Society drafted up in the last couple of pages that I'd happily pit my Leviathans against. But the only use I'd have for the current, canon version would be to watch it wiped out by a charge of angry Prometheans who have found peace through acceptance into a Leviathan's cult. See how much good your fancy occult science does against monster-kin who heal from lightning!

                                        Also, really iffy on the idea of the Wake being a permanently-on soul-scouring effect. There's black and gray morality, and then there's forcefeeding the "you are a total monster and if you had any decency you'd kill yourself". Seriously, even Prometheans, who have "Thing That Should Not Be" written all over their concept sheet (hence Disquiet/Wasteland) and Beasts (who are blatant Villain protagonists) don't get that message shoved down their throat so hard. Hell, Prometheans are even getting ways to dampen their aura of unnaturalness.
