    In a sense. Leviathan had a 1-7 scale (The number 7 is a reoccurring motif in Leviathan) with no basic/advanced split, but you needed Sheol 6/7 to unlock Fervor 6/7. I look Mummy's Grasp and expanded it by two extra dots to get the Fervor 6/7 ratings. It looks like Arcanist then took my Fervour chart and stretched it out to ten dots.

    Reach/Piety is a bit more complex. Leviathan's lovecraftian cults are crazier than Mummy's scorpion cults. Piety 3 is a bit weaker than Reach 3, but at 4 dots they're about equal and Leviathans can only go up to 3 without the right foundation or high Sheol. Arcanist's version appears to be a blend of both Mummy and Leviathan's.

    Lore is completely new. In a Leviathan Cult Zeal is used to power rituals and doesn't particularly relate to accurate occult knowledge. A good singing voice for chanting probably matters more. So maybe the Advanced Lore question would be better directed at Arcanist; though looking at the pattern I can see having loyal major template members and the ability to train ordinary members with the right traits (e.g. Intelligence or Resolve 5) into magic users along the lines of Second Sight's Thamaturges. Being able to teach anybody (like the Malleus Maleficirum teaching construction workers how to use Benediction) would be more a matter of luck than lore. The Loyalists of Thule have way more lore than the Canites for example, but the Canites have the Endowment.

                    To be honest, I'd suggest getting rid of lore entirely. Leviathan's use Zeal to power a unique supernatural ability.

                    Instead I'd make a Cult Merit called Lore and say that if you use Reach/Grasp on a supernatural opponent then you use the lower of Reach/Grasp and Lore. E.G. Send your Cult to negociate the sale of artefacts to a vampire. No Lore, they get hit by Majesty and give the goods away for a pittance. High lore, they politely but firmly agree to do the deed over email and get a fair price.

                    You could also have a Lore Speciality merit which counts as two dots of lore for a specific type of supernatural and none for anyone else. The only way to get "Advanced Lore" is to combine Lore and Lore Speciality. You need the generalised Lore to form a framework and the specialist knowledge to focus that framework.

                    Finally you could have a Merit that lets you bypass Lore entirely for one kind of supernatural creature. With the fluff saying this usually means that kind of supernatural creature founded the Cult.

                            04-04-2016, 10:29 AM
                            Wow- thanks for all the feedback guys, it's honestly amazingly helpful. The idea of Lore not being a part of the core Cult Merit, and empowered Cultists building off of it, is a great idea. I'll definitely be working that in. Empowered Cultists (whether via Supernatural Merits, Gutter Magic, or other things) are probably going to be separate from Lore, as is appropriate. This system in particular was designed to complement the Pact system. At the moment, I'm working on a chain of Altar Merits. Still not entirely sure what that entails, but I'm taking suggestions.

                            I'm considering the suggestions on Militarized in terms of "free floating" Fighting Style dots, because it seems really thematic, but I wouldn't use them to give them equipment- that's what Arsenal and the Building Equipment rules below are for. Also, Isolated is supposed to be really punishing. You're the creepy compound in the middle of nowhere, your Cultists are definitely going to have trouble moving in regular circles unless they have Legitimacy, Leverage, or Conspirators. It's a matter of control versus access, I think. Home Turf and Isolated are meant for Cults with supreme control over their membership and territory- that kind of intense inward focus leaves them without resources to dedicate to the outside world. Which is where Conspirators come in, and probably why they could Merit expansion into Agents (Which I'm going to work on).

                            As for Damnation City, a lot of the Primacy rules are fairly close to how Cults already function with Reach and Grasp and such, but the idea of expanding Conspirators into Agents (with an attendant Merit chain for it) is an extremely cool idea that I'm definitely going to exploit.

                            *****
                            Step 3- Using the Cult

                            How big is a Cult Anyways? - That is an excellent question, and one without a straight answer, but a good general guideline is the proportions written in Book of the Deceived describing how many Cultists will possess the appropriate Merit that equates to status within the Cult. I figure this is as good a figure as any to work as a guideline for how many people for each level of Status or Initiation are in the Cult.
                            o - Casual Member: 35%
                            oo - Full Member: 30%
                            ooo - Dedicated Acolyte: 20%
                            oooo - Priest: 10%
                            ooooo - High Priest: 5%
                            When you summon your Cultists to battle, you get a maximum of one per dot of Reach and Grasp (See Battle Fodder below), representing a member of your Cult who is invested enough to be willing to die (or at least kill/suffer a great deal of pain) for your organization. Let’s say that’s Status 4/Initiation 4 or so. So, your average starting Cult, which must have one dot dedicated to it, and that dot is usually Reach or Grasp, is going to have three combatants. Those three make up ten percent of the Cult, so a three-dot Cult is going to have about thirty members, which translates to ten members per dot of Reach and Grasp. (A one-dot Cult probably doesn’t have a high priest or equivalent position; it would be centered directly on the Cult Leader.) Think about what Merit dots that raise effective Reach and Grasp do, in-character.

                            A Conspirator isn’t just a lone individual; though that lone individual is generally the linchpin, but it’s also other people they’ve made contact with that they can influence who aren’t actually members of the Cult. Having Home Turf means that, in addition to your actual Cult members, part of the general populous of your Home Turf is involved tangentially in its affairs. Legitimacy means there’s laypeople, chartered accountants, contractors, ordinary employees, and others attached to your organization. Advanced Reach and Advance Grasp do more than that (See Battle Fodder below) multiplying Cult membership by ten for every dot invested.

                            Mortal Organizations: Cults can stand in for much, much more than mysterious fraternities and occluded networks of strange faiths. Cults can be governments, Cults can be militaries, Cults can be police forces and businesses. So, how do mortal organizations shape up? Despite conspiracy theories running rampant, world governments are generally not all that cloak-and-dagger. They can’t be, they’re just too big. A specific government agency may have Advanced Grasp in order to pull off mysterious black ops, but the government itself likely won’t.

                            This is, ultimately, in the hands of the Storyteller, but something small- like a Hunter Cell or a small town sheriff’s office -would probably have two dots to distribute between Reach and Grasp, focusing more on connections with the community (IE. Conspirators or Home Turf) to get stuff done. A citywide police force will likely have something more like four dots to divide up, and a national organization will likely have eight dots.

                            Corporations and governments favor Reach, militaries, intelligence agencies, and criminal organizations favor Grasp. Remember that their raw influence or member list might not be enough to get the job done, and plan accordingly with Merits like Legitimacy (which all government offices likely possess), Conspirators, Leverage, and Home Turf. In order to represent a large, inter-connected set of organizations- like a country’s government, a multi-national corporation, a global religion, or other such massive groups -consider building the pyramid, starting at the bottom with municipal governments and regional offices, and making them Splinter Organizations of a larger group. Guidelines for that will be provided below in the Crossover section.

                            When Cults Roll: Some things a Cult can just do without a fuss because it is well within their realm of influence. But, other times, they need to push their influence to the limit. In that case, the Cult rolls. Depending on the nature of what they need done, roll Reach or Grasp, factoring benefits or penalties from other Merits and Conditions as suitable. However, there is only so much the Cult can do. Your basic Conspiracy is roughly 20 people willing to go reasonably outside the law in order to fulfill their goals. Those 20 people are not going to collapse a government on their own, regardless of how good they are. See the table below for the successes necessary to overreach or extend your grasp.

                            A Cult takes Actions just like a character might. They may perform only one Instant Action (rolled or unrolled) at their full level of either Reach, Grasp, or Lore. They may perform two Reflexive Actions at Reach/Grasp/Lore -1, and an unlimited amount of Reflexive Actions at Reach/Grasp/Lore -2. While they are termed Reflexive or Instant Actions, the Cult’s work occurs over a period of time, generally one day per dot of Reach, Grasp, or Lore.
                            Reach or Grasp Exceeded Successes Needed
                            1 dot 1 Success
                            2 dots 3 Successes
                            3 dots 9 Successes
                            4 dots 12 Successes
                            5 dots 25 Successes
                            Roll Results
                            • Dramatic Failure: Inflict any number of debilitating Conditions listed below in the Appendix.
                            • Failure: Inflict the Flagged or Contender Condition
                            • Success: Yay! It worked!
                            • Exceptional Success: Gain the Inspired or Informed Condition

                            Blocked: Organizations undercut and work against each other all the time. This can be either a casual thing, or serious acts of sabotage. A Cult may dedicate a number of dots of Reach, Grasp, or Lore to oppose the actions of a rival Cult. Dots dedicated in this way reduce their opponent’s traits by the same amount, but also reduces the effectiveness of your own Cult. You could act as a minor annoyance to another organization, or dedicate time and resources into seriously impeding their efforts. Just remember that action tends to encourage reprisal. If you dedicate a number of dots equal to the lowest of Reach, Grasp, or Lore, your Cult is considered to have taken an Instant Action as appropriate. (See When Cults Roll above) Dots dedicated in this way are temporarily suppressed for a number of days equal to the dots dedicated. Conspirators can be dedicated to this action as well.

                            Social Maneuvering with Cults: This isn’t so much trying to get someone to do what you want as it is performing acts of diplomacy. Declare a Goal and decide Initial Impression as normal. A Cult possesses doors equal to the lower of their Reach or Grasp. If the action would be considered a Breaking Point, add two Doors. The Cult’s Virtue, Vice, and Aspirations should be based on their Doctrine, Foundation, and Purpose respectively. Going against or appealing to these traits adds and removes Doors normally. When rolling, use Reach, Grasp, or Lore depending on what’s appropriate. Factor in the benefits of the Leverage and Conspirator Merits. (Note: A Cult can also engage in Social Maneuvering with a single character, but that generally entails the Cult Leader or a Conspirator approaching them and rolling normally. Benefits from Leverage and multiple Conspirators in one organization apply normally.)

                            Other than that, Social Maneuvering functions as written.

                            Holy Wars: Cults can always just go to war directly, miniature crusades in back alleys, bombs in mailboxes and Molotovs through storefronts and safe-house windows. In such a case, roll Grasp vs. Grasp as an Instant Action, as combat is always a fickle and unpredictable situation. Much like Down and Dirty Combat, state an Intent going in- that you want to destroy weapons stockpiles, for example, or assassinate a particular Conspirator. If successful, the result depends on your dots of Grasp. If you have lower Grasp than your opponent, nothing serious happens, and the reprisal is likely to be swift. If you have higher Grasp, then you destroy a Merit dot per dot of Grasp that exceeds that of your opponent.

                            Such an action could outright destroy Reach and Grasp through killing Cult members, destroy Lore by killing the right people, defacing altars, burning books, destroy Benefits by shaking the reputation and mindset of the enemy Cult (Mystery Cult Initiation is immune to this), and stranger things besides. An assault upon a powerful gang could lead to a loss of Home Turf as people recognize they don’t totally control the streets. Conspirators die, Leverage is lost, and Legitimacy could collapse under a ‘False Flag’ operation or a frame up to discredit the enemy Cult. When Cults go to war, nothing is truly holy.

                            Investigating Cults: The number of Clues you need to Uncover the Truth about a given Cult is equal to their dots of Grasp.

                            Building Equipment: Cults can use the Building Equipment rules, substituting Reach, Grasp, and Lore for regular dice pools where appropriate. The equipment thus established can benefit the whole Cult, but generally goes away if it is targeted in an attack (see Holy Wars above. Each piece of equipment counts for one point).

                                    *****

                                    Battle Fodder: There is a final common, albeit unwise, use for Cults. Their master may call upon them to pick up weapons and fight by his side. The trappings vary based on Foundation. An Enterprise or Elite Club might hire bodyguards and private security, Gangs and Cult of Personality send in wild-eyed youths wearing symbols and colors, Conspiracies favor inconspicuous infiltrators and gunmen. A group of Tribal combatants goes into battle wearing holy symbols and chanting hymns, while an Unorganized Cult charges in, ragged and varied anarchists hell-bent on destruction. These fighters aren’t necessarily competent, but they’re keen. Their numbers depend on how long the Cult takes to muster its forces. Remember to factor in the Wrathful Benefit if you have it.
                                    • Under 24 Hours: Roll Reach + Grasp. You get a combatant per success.
                                    • 24 Hours: You get a number of minions equal to the higher of Reach or Grasp
                                    • 48 Hours: You get an armed fighter for each dot of Reach and Grasp
                                    There is a drawback to this though. If you have any kind of Cult except for a Gang, and someone dies in the fighting, you lose a point of Reach or Grasp (whichever is higher). Death tends to dissuade people from working for you. If more than ten people die in a single battle, you lose an additional dot of Reach or Grasp.

                                    Fighting for Advanced Cults: For every dot of Advanced Reach or Advanced Grasp, you get ten combatants for every success or dot. This does stack, so an Advanced Grasp Conspiracy with three dots in the dedicated Merit would gain 30 combatants per dot and/or success.

                                        Alright, revised Lore and new stuff regarding Agents and Operatives, which are really just the elder siblings of Conspirators. Honestly, something that's really coming out as I continue to develop this is that, as satisfying as it is to have a massive Cult all to yourself, a massive Cult with lots of personal Reach and Grasp just isn't all that Experience efficient. With these new sets of rules, establishing far-reaching, many-tentacled organizations through Conspirators as they evolve into Agents and Operatives isn't just more thematic, but more effective as well.

                                        That said, you can establish a ton of different kinds of Cults. This play style, relatively small but with far-reaching and influential Agents and Operatives, is more suited to a Conspiracy, Enterprise, or Elite Club than anything else. Another equally valid play style is a Cult that is all-powerful in their own territory, but not all that influential elsewhere... which leads back to Conspirators again. Conspirators and its companion Merits are going to be an excellent way to build up a Cult and single out a couple signature characters within it. I intend to elaborate more on that in the crossover section, "Building the Pyramid".

                                        *****

                                        Lore (o - ooooo)
                                        Prerequisite: Cult 1+
                                        Effect: Lore is, effectively, a measure of the Occult Skill spread across the entire Cult- how aware of the supernatural they all are. Members of the Cult can be guaranteed to have as many dots in Occult as they have dots in Initiation or Status equal to (5-Lore). So, a not terribly knowledgeable Cult is grasping at straws (Werewolves don’t like silver, right?) While a very knowledgeable one knows the ins and outs of the supernatural community; the average Cult member has some idea, while the High Priests are experts. (No! I am not just going to talk to a faerie/demon/spirit- not without a lawyer present.) Bigger Cults tend to have less Lore, if only because it’s harder to distribute amongst the members, and- more importantly -because it’s safer for the supernatural being at the heart of the Cult.

                                        Second Sight (o)
                                        Prerequisite: Lore 3+
                                        Effect: Cult members are more likely to possess Supernatural Merits, whether by virtue of their studies, some part of the Initiation practice, or simple exposure to weirdness. A Cult that deals heavily with the Shadow will likely have a few Mediums and/or people exhibiting Tells, for example, while a Cult that interacts with Vampires may possess Clear-Sighted so that concealing Disciplines cannot fool them. These Supernatural Merits are independent of any gained through a Mystery Cult Initiation tethered to the same Cult. The Cultists gain access to a single Merit worth (Lore-2, minimum 1) Experiences, and it kicks in when the occult knowledge of Lore would. This Merit may be purchased multiple times to represent multiple gifts from joining the Cult. While generally only Supernatural Merits apply, something like Iron Will would be suitable as well.
                                        Drawback: Unless this Merit is paired with the Blasé Benefit above, or the Merit is something like Sleepwalker or a Wolfblooded Tell, then for every instance of this Merit the Cultists take a -1 penalty to Integrity rolls due to supernatural causes. The forbidden lore is starting to claw a bit at their mind.

                                        Theurgy (o)
                                        Prerequisite:                                         Second Sight
                                        Effect: The Cultists are possessed of more than some paltry gifts, and are instead more comparable to relatively competent mediums or hedge witches. Double the Merit dots granted by Second Sight; this can grant a wider range of abilities to the Cultists, adding some variation to the group, and it can further build upon a theme.
                                        Drawback: Cultists with Supernatural Merits gain a suitable Persistent Condition, something like Bewitched or Receptive. It can be both a blessing, and a curse, as it makes using their supernatural abilities easier, but not necessarily safer. Just as with Second Sight, this Merit may be taken multiple times and tagged to a separate Second Sight Merit (or perhaps the same one), in which case use only the Conditions that apply based on the selected Merits.

                                        Thaumaturgy (o)
                                        Prerequisite: Theurgy
                                        Effect: Double the Merit dots again, granting a total of twelve maximum. The Cultists possess a wide range of abilities, ranging from the small and paltry to the vast and powerful, but one thing that is certain is that they are skilled and potentially dangerous practitioners of various forms of magic.
                                        Drawback: Cultists with this much magical power find that it puts quite a bit of strain on their souls. Their Integrity is capped at 9. As with Theurgy and Second Sight, this Merit may be purchased multiple times, reducing maximum Integrity on Cultists benefitting from it with each purchase.

                                        Patron (o)
                                        Prerequisite: Second Sight
                                        Effect: Your Cult serves, worships, or is otherwise involved with a particular kind of supernatural being, giving them access to abilities they normally would not possess. In addition to Supernatural Merits, the Cult may access Dread Powers, Rites, Disciplines, Rotes, Contracts, Affinities, and other such abilities with Storyteller discretion.
                                        Drawback: Your Cult is specifically bound to a supernatural being, and is expected to do their bidding. Take a suitable Persistent Condition that applies to all Cultists, and add take a Short Term Aspiration related to the commands of the Patron.

                                        Endowment (ooooo)
                                        Prerequisite: Lore 1+
                                        Effect: You Cult comes with an Endowment. Design one that is thematically appropriate. The Cult possessing the Endowment Merit does not mean that Cultists will automatically possess it without the Second Sight > Theurgy > Thaumaturgy Merit chain.

                                        Agent (o - ooo)
                                        Prerequisite: Conspirator
                                        Effect: As it stands, a Conspirator functions like a two-dot Ally/Retainer, without counting the boost to Reach and Grasp they grant. By investing more heavily in one particular Conspirator, the Cult can make them an Agent- a formidable asset more than capable of acting alone. First, add dots in Agent to Conspirator to determine what level of Retainer they are. Secondly, distribute dots of Agent between Reach and Grasp to illustrate the Agent’s personal level of influence. By spending a point of Willpower, a Cult Leader may have an Agent act alone for a Chapter the way they would use the base Cult’s Reach or Grasp, with one major exception. The Agent can still act as a Retainer would, performing an individual rather than an institutional task.
                                        Drawback: An Agent who acts alone can get themselves killed on a mission gone badly, and in acting alone they also get tagged with Conditions specifically to themselves, and not to their parent Cult. This is something of a mixed blessing, putting the Agent at much higher risk than before, while also providing the Cult with a bit more temporary security.

                                        Operative (o - ooo)
                                        Prerequisite: Agent at equal or higher value
                                        Effect: For every dot of the parent Cult’s Reach and Grasp (not the Agent’s personal Reach and Grasp) the Operative gains a number of additional Merit dots equal to their dots in Operative. This is purely to benefit the Operative, representing their widespread influence and personal resources.
                                        Drawback: An Operative is far, far more independent than an Agent or a Conspirator, and no longer acts as a Retainer, but as an Ally. While you can still spend a point of Willpower to get an Operative to act on their own for the Chapter, you’ll need to roll Manipulation + Persuasion - (Reach or Grasp over what the Operative possesses) they’re already putting a very prosperous life on the line just working for you, and they’re starting to resent working for someone. Whether the roll fails or succeeds, you will not be able to use the Operative next chapter. On a Dramatic Failure, the Operative goes rogue and seeks retribution, giving you the Rogue Cult condition.

                                                *****

                                                Altar (ooo)
                                                Prerequisite: Lore 1+, Safe Place 1+
                                                Effect: A Cult with such an artifact, either found, made by hand, or otherwise acquired, may use the Teamwork rules to use Supernatural Merits or other powers of the Cult, even if they don’t possess any dots in the Merit, or the necessary metaphysical prerequisites to wield the magic in the first place. The Extended Action to use this Altar is generally 30 minutes per roll, but this doubles if supporting actors don’t have all the necessary traits.
                                                Drawback: You need time to gather the cultists necessary to perform a ritual together, and an Altar generally isn’t mobile. The time to gather ritualists is roughly the same as the time it takes to gather combatants.

                                                Dedicated Altar (ooo)
                                                Prerequisite: Altar, Safe Place 2+
                                                Effect: The mystical symbolism embedded in the Cult’s Altar is much, much more potent than is was before, even serving as a kind of reliquary for the mystical energies of a ritual. Cultists may worship before the Dedicated Altar in an Extended Presence + Expression roll lead by the Cult Leader, with all attendant cultists acting as supporting actors. Each success on the roll generates a point of the Fuel Stat used by the Cult Leader and/or their Patron, and can hold a number of points equal to three times the Merit dots spent on the Dedicated Altar. So, Willpower/Essence/Aether/Vitae/Pillars/Aether etc. These points may be spent on anything they would normally use the Altar for, and drain away at the rate of a point every (Lore) days.
                                                Drawback: You still need to gather up ritualists, and more importantly, now the Altar smells delicious to the appropriate supernatural beings, pinging on their senses. Cultists just might have to fight to avoid sharing their Altar with an unwanted Spirit or prevent it from being co-opted by an industrious Angel.

                                                Consecrated Altar (ooo)
                                                Prerequisite: Dedicated Altar, Patron, Safe Place 3+
                                                Effect: The Altar now permanently possesses a Base Condition that could, theoretically, produce the necessary supernatural fuel for the rites. This means that if it was alluring before, now it is irresistibly desirable. A Consecrated Altar for a God-Machine Cult is Infrastructure, while a carved standing stone becomes Resonant thanks to the attention of a group of Spirit worshippers. A Consecrated Altar could be incorporated into, or form the base of, something like a Locus, Tomb/Temple, Haunt, or Subverted Infrastructure.
                                                Drawback: The trouble that could come from the Condition now tied to the Consecrated Altar is drawback enough. Have fun defending your place of worship.

                                                Appendix- Conditions

                                                Admirer (Persistent): Someone has latched onto your Cult, or you personally, and is expressing nothing but the utmost admiration for your capabilities and what you can do. In other words, an obsessive stalker who’s going to cause you trouble or who is trouble.
                                                Beat: Your admirer causes you, or your Cult, a significant problem
                                                Resolution: The admirer dies, joins your Cult, or somehow loses track of you

                                                Civil War (Persistent): Something has pushed your Cult to the tipping point, and now relatively normal tension or conflict has erupted into an outright war. You cannot use your Cult for anything so long as this Condition persists. Upon gaining this Condition, or at the beginning of the next session if it persists for more than one, roll Reach vs. Grasp following the sidebar Holy War above. If the sides are equal, or if one is higher than the other, then the other trait loses a number of dots equal to the difference (minimum 1)
                                                Possible Source: Dramatic Failure on a Cult roll, Failure with the Contender Condition
                                                Beat: You are severely inconvenienced by your lack of a cult
                                                Resolution: Do something drastic to bring the Cult to heel- this usually imposes a Breaking Point on the Cult Leader.

                                                Contender: Someone in the Cult is waiting in the wings to rise up in the ranks, and doesn’t mind stepping over their fellow Cultists in order to do so. Your next Failure with a Cult action is considered a Dramatic Failure- upgrade this Condition to the Civil War or Rogue Cult Condition as appropriate.
                                                Resolution: Find the upstart and take them out. Alternatively, give them what they want. Fail a roll and have this Condition upgraded to Civil War or Rogue Cult.

                                                Flagged: Someone is suspicious of you. It could be the police or social services investigating what they think is a mundane problem, or Hunters investigating what they’re sure is a supernatural problem. Just remember, even a parking ticket can become a problem if a police officer finds evidence of human sacrifice. Mechanically, another Cult starts an Investigation action against yours.
                                                Resolution: The Investigation concludes, upgrading the Condition to Hunted. The Investigation concludes, and they fail to find anything of value.

                                                Hunted (Persistent): The jig is up and someone is out to arrest, or more likely, kill you.
                                                Beat: Your pursuers cause you a significant problem
                                                Resolution: Convince the Hunters to leave you be, give them what they want, kill them, or otherwise lose them for good.

                                                Record (Persistent): You’ve dealt with the immediate fallout of a newsworthy event, or moved into the public eye, and now there’s a paper trail. Any attempt to investigate the Cult (not you personally) benefit, gaining an additional Clue on a successful Investigation action per instance of the Record Condition.
                                                Beat: An investigation succeeds or comes dangerously close because of the Record
                                                Resolution: Become big enough to lean on the organization that previously tracked you, completely reorganize the Cult so that it has cut ties with its past.

                                                Rogue Cult (Persistent): An organization has cropped up without your consent or knowledge and is causing trouble- affiliating themselves with you or your own Cult. Alternatively, part of your Cult has broken free and now seeks to oppose you. A Rogue Cult possesses a Foundation and all other basic parts of a Cult, and except in instances when a Cult breaks off from your own organization (outlined above) the Rogue Cult gains a number of dots to distribute amongst the various Cult Merits equal to the lower of your Reach or Grasp.
                                                Beat: The Cult causes a significant problem for you or your original organization
                                                Resolution: The Rogue Cult is brought under control (requires either an Extended Action or a Breaking Point), the Rogue Cult is destroyed or disbanded.

                                                Wronged (Persistent): You have offended someone personally, and they are bringing their not inconsiderable resources to bear against your organization. If they ever learn of the Cult’s supernatural nature (if it has one) the person is rather liable to snap and become a Hunter. Mechanically, treat them as a Retainer with the lower of Reach or Grasp as dots dedicated to the Merit.
                                                Beat: The person pursuing you cause a significant problem
                                                Resolution: They die, get to confront you (even if it’s just to give you a piece of their mind), or are inducted into the Cult (requires an Extended Action with successes equal to their effective dots of Retainer x 5)

                                                            Other Cults can intervene with your Cults cabal, by blocking your attempts of gaining ingredients in the same way as they would block your Grasp or Reach. Further more they can target & attack members of your cabal. If enemy cults kill enough of your spellcaster that you no longer have the minimum amount needed for casting a spell then casting that spell becomes impossible until you recruit more spellcaster.
