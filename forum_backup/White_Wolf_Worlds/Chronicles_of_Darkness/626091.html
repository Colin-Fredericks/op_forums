                  I would argue the strix seem too capricious and venal to be the force preventing the Imperial Archmasters from lifting the Curse from the history of man, but they may be akin in nature.                                           Given Mage's platonic cosmology, Vampires wouldn't need a sponsor per se. The Supernal is an expanded world of forms, or ideas at their purest, most perfect form. Vampires could be perceived as a corruption of the idea of Humanity. Vampires could be the hubris of the Exarchs' actions (provided they have indeed made a prison of the world), their caricature of what Humanity should be like in order to be kept in line, taking shape and form in Kindred. Their blood sorcery is almost like a response to the dwindling of magic from the material, a kind of magic that has nothing to do with the Supernal, but instead is of this place.

                                          But yes, Vampire has a cosmology of its own. Belial is all too real, and a Demon nothing like the rebellious angels of the God-Machine.

                                                      In my cosmology, there is no united "sponsor" for vampires- instead, there is a loose alliance of many parasitic blood gods who use the strixs as a communication system between themselves and the vampires as some sort of "angels". Each has its own agenda it wish to advance by using vampires, some a very selfish (like "feed me") or very world changing (like "creating hell upon earth"). The one to manage that alliance (called "the Coalition of Blood") is the one to spawn the strixs, so the very ability to participate in the alliance is by acknowledging her (I'm calling her "Lilith" for obvious reasons) and accept her monopoly over the dead. Those who do not obey her, yet dare to play with the undead, are called the "VII Opposition". The exact list of who belongs to who is kinda blurry, making even high level blood gods fear of betrayal and play political games.
