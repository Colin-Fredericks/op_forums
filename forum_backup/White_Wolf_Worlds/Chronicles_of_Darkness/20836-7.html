                                        Originally posted by Jesuncolo View Post
                                        I changed Wood a little bit. Allergen is now a bit more streamlined, and I added an appropriate Tilt for GMC. Harvest got merged into Bloom, Bloom has become more synthetic and I created a new Law in replacement of Harvest. What do you think?


                                        Allergen
                                        Attribute: The lower of Stamina or Intelligence
                                        "Let the pollen cloud their mind"
                                        The proclaiming Oroboros triggers a spray of pollen from all nearby plant sources. Anyone within range suffers an immediate sneezing and coughing fit, even if they are not particularly allergic to outdoor irritants. This law requires plants to be nearby in order to have an effect. The plants affected gain the highly allergenic pollen permanently; new beings entering the area suffer the same fit but are permanently acclimated to the irritant once they finish sneezing and coughing.
                                        Dice Pool: Stamina/Intelligence + Wood + Furnace
                                        Roll Results
                                        Dramatic Failure: The Oroboros triggers the pollen spray, but subjects herself to the allergic fit.
                                        Failure: Nothing happens.
                                        Success: Within a number of yards equal to the Effect Rating, hyper-allergenic pollen clouds the air. Anyone within this cloud suffers a sneezing and coughing fit, suffering 1 point of bashing damage per turn of combat, and must roll Stamina + Resolve -3 or lose the action. A creature that successfully acts for three consecutive turns from contesting the allergen becomes immune to the allergen for the entire scene. This fit lasts for a number of turns equal to the Effect Rating of the proclamation.
                                        Exceptional Success: The duration is improved by 3 turns.

                                        [GMC: Apply the Poisoned (moderate) Tilt, but add that the failed Stamina + Resolve -3 to resist the poison makes the creature lose the action for failing to sneeze and cough the allergen out]
                                        I don't like that it does damage; wood is the element of life and there are much better Edicts to use for the purpose of inflicting harm. Also, sneezing and coughing are rarely severe enough to leave a lasting impact, even bruises (It's have to be a very violent reaction and even then, the choking does more damage than the coughing). The blind effect of the original version was actually intended to reduce the damage the dragon will suffer during the effect, since fighting blind is worse than rolling chance dice. Overcoming the allergen with a roll is nice, but I think it might work better with cumulative rolls, and with those counting up to the effect rating. By which I mean, after five successful actions (actions not impaired by the pollen because the Stamina + Resolve - The Proclaiming Dragon's Furnace roll succeeded) they adjust to the pollen and become immune for the scene, but the successful actions need not occur in a row.
                                        As for the penalty for sneezing and coughing--full blindness unless the aforementioned Stamina + Resolve - Furnace roll succeeds in which case it is treated as substantial darkness (I don't have the WoD core book on hand, but I think it's -3 to act, -3 sight perception).


                                        Originally posted by Jesuncolo View Post
                                        Bloom
                                        Attribute: The lower of Resolve or Manipulation
                                        "Let them grow strong, reaching for the light."
                                        The proclaiming Oroboros seeds the growth of plant matter, sprouting a bed of flowers as well as a number of saplings equal to the Effect Rating within the target region. If any plants are already within the area, they are sped along their natural lifespan, grow strong and lively, and eventually can lead dying plants to rot and die.
                                        Dice Pool: Resolve/Manipulation + Wood + Furnace
                                        Roll Results
                                        Dramatic Failure: The Oroboros accidentally kills the plant or suffers 2 bashing damage while trying to kill vegetation.
                                        Failure: Nothing happens.
                                        Success: All plants in the area (see the Targets)or specific plants can either grow from seed to young plant, mature plant, and eventually to death and decay, at choice of the Oroboros. The plants don't need an appropriate terrain to grow, and can grow roots into stone, concrete and similar harsh terrains. The Oroboros can grow the plant so greatly that they can be used for narrative purpose to create means to reach impervious places expending 1 Breath. A plant that bears fruit can be enchanted using 1 Breath to sprout a magical fruit capable of healing 1 bashing or lethal damage per Furnace dot. A single plant can only bear one fruit of this kind.
                                        Exceptional Success: When using the Law to grow plants, the Dragon can choose to turn it into a hindering terrain, halving the Speed and inflicting -2 to Initiative to everyone inside except for the Dragon itself. When used for growing the magical fruit, the fruit will heal the Effective Rating instead of his Furnace (if higher).
                                        I like the hindering terrain, and I believe that should be incorporated into the base effect, rather than an exceptional bonus. I do not like that it absorbed Harvest, however; this law was mainly for the purpose of generating raw materials for the Wood Edict and for producing storytelling-based advantages, such as a tree to hide behind or climb (or to filter the toxic air around a volcano or in a post-apocalyptic wasteland).
                                        Letting an Oroboros benefit multiple times from the golden fruit in a scene breaks the Breath balance, since it's much more energy effective to grow and eat a bushel of golden fruit than it is to use the furnace to repair wounds (1 Breath for Furnace Lethal removed against 2 Breath for one Lethal removed). The limit of benefiting once per scene from the fruit circumvents this. Also, by making it only a single fruit the Oroboros cannot assist her friends with the proclamation; there's never enough to help more than one person. And finally, the Edict cannot backfire if the plant only grows one fruit; in the original version of Harvest a hostile target could--if knowledgeable of Oroboroi magic--steal one of the fruit from a tree and benefit himself. This is especially easy if the Oroboros is depending on old growth to produce the tree--most parks will produce areas of effect that could spawn dozens of golden fruit.
                                        I believe Bloom and Harvest need to remain separate laws.


                                        Originally posted by Jesuncolo View Post
                                        Land sense
                                        Attribute:The lower of Wits or Composure
                                        "Let the nature be my eyes"
                                        The proclamation makes the Oroboros' senses open to the surrounding nature. He can see, smell and hear through the vegetation, and know the terrain better, to find dangers and avoid them, or know how to best fight it.
                                        Dice Pool: Wits/Composure + Wood + Furnace
                                        Success: The Oroboros gains information from the landscape within 50 yards per Effective Rating. He gains knowledge of the position of every single creature within the range of the Law, gains +3 to Perception rolls, +2 to Initiative and +1 to all actions while inside the affected area. The Oroboros is also protected by ambushes, as plants perceive everyone in the area which is not in a state of Twilight or incorporeal. The dragon doesn't need to roll for surprise, and simply rolls Initiative like he won the surprise roll. Concealing magic can defeat the land sense with a Clash of Wills, using the rating of the concealing power + Supernatural tolerance (or 3 if the power rating is undefined) versus Wood + Furnace.
                                        Exceptional Success: The Oroboros gains rote action to Perception rolls, +3 to Initiative and +2 to all actions while inside the affected area
                                        I see that you're going for a Boneyard feel with this, and I can imagine one of the possible combinations with Land Sense: the remote trigger of a pollen burst. However, this law conflicts with one of the primary features of the Edict system: each Edict has a fixed target regardless of the law proclaimed. For Wood, that target is a space with yard radius equal to the effect rating, or rather an area in square yards equal to Pi * ER^2. Land Sense breaks this pattern, and HAS to break this pattern to be worthwhile. However, that makes combinations with it rough, because the limited size of the other two Laws force repeated uses to make Land Sense viable, especially with Bloom.
                                        What's worse, the potency of Land Sense remains much larger than the other two Laws combined: a level 1 proclamation of Land Sense covers more than the entire area of a level 10 proclamation of Bloom. This is a problem, since players can pay experience to learn how to perform two proclamations in a single instant action (a Law Corollary). If they were to combine Bloom and Land Sense, they would only be able to see within the confines of the Bloom space, which they could see anyway because it has to be within the range of their voice to form. It's also a problem for Land Sense because the only component being controlled by the effect rating is essentially nullified for all combinations.
                                        I'm not saying that Land Sense isn't awesome; I am saying that it doesn't work as a starting level Law. In a high-octane supplement (one that supplies powers for 6+ dots), I could see listing Land Sense as a viable Law (perhaps with a prerequisite of Wood 4).

                                                        Originally posted by Shock View Post
                                                        Edit- as for the Elders themselves.

                                                        The issue with them is akin to bring any Kaiju into nWoD game. How do you accurately portray a creature who is as large as a skyscraper and can crush a car flat just by stepping on it without getting bogged down with mechanics?
                                                        Short Answer: When dealing with Kaiju, the Kaiju use game stats and the players are treated like a swarm. Yep, that means every time the Kaiju takes an action to reduce the size of the swarm, somebody dies.

                                                        Long Answer: I was actually toying around with something like this, building a system based off the damage reduction True Form used to supply and a "Scale" mechanic which was actually an Exalted homebrew by Requim_Jeer (Source: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...cale-and-scope).

                                                        Scale is a measure of half the character's longest dimension in yards (We'd do the calculations and throw them alongside a size chart). When a character is a scale larger than her attacker, all damage she would sustain is downgraded by one grade and all damage she would inflict is upgraded by one grade.

                                                        Swarm Size Damage (Bashing) -> Bashing -> Lethal -> Aggravated -> Health Dots -> Reduce Swarm Size (Instant Slay)

                                                        This means that, eventually, beings are so small that a single attacker becomes insignificant; the attackers must work in tandem (becoming one game entity) to increase their scale to the point where they would be viable as a swarm, and even then they only inflict fleeting damage. The Oroboroi are the exception, of course, because as they manifest Ablutions they become increasingly enormous, somewhat rivaling the scale of a giant monster.

                                                        If Scale is too much of a pain to track, we could instead do multiples of seven (since the WoD core only covers creatures up to size 7). Anything within sizes 1-7 is Scale 1, everything in sizes 8-14 is Scale 2, and so forth. Since skyscrapers are only like... size 60, the Kaiju would only be Scale 9. That's still enough to squash human beings like ants (Compared to Scale 1, it's a damage decrease of 8 and damage increase of 8, meaning a bashing attack would fly off the charts), but a Divinity 8 Oroboros would be able to squeeze into the chart (With an average size of 48, that makes them Scale 7. Their agg damage will be bashing, but they'd only take agg damage from the Kaiju's bashing attacks. If Godzilla decides to breathe fire, though... bye bye Oroboros!).

                                                        There's always problems when the size ratings go through the roof... if we're going to have the Elders/Nidhoggr be as massive as this we'll need to supply structure to the mechanics or make it a narrative affair (When fighting something that big, you don't fight it as an entity but as individual parts, such as a leg or the head).


                                                        On the matter of terminology, as I thought about it Zmeu is actually a better fit for the usurpers than it is for the Ancestors... perhaps we'll throw it in the slang for eastern European Oroboroi and leave the Ancestors as just The Long or The Five.
