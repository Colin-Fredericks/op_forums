    Chronicles of Darkness Video Games

    10-01-2016, 11:48 AM
    So,we can say pretty certainly that any multimedia expansions for the immediate future will revolve around the cWoD, but I'm of the mind that ChroD is actually a more interesting system for adaptation into video games, especially games that focus more on emergent gameplay and simulation. So let's do an exercise. How would you like to see the ChroD gamelines reflected in the world of PC and console games? I'll start by tossing out a couple of ideas of my own.

    Vampire the Requiem: Regency
    When Paradox announced they were buying White Wolf, everyone's first response was Europa Universalis: Elder Edition please! And I definitely think that high strategy is a great fit for Vampire: The Masquerade, but rather than essentially have an EU mod for Vampire, I'd rather see them take Requiem and implement those ideas on a smaller scale. Instead of an elder trying to create a kingdom in Europe, you're a kindred in the (relatively) modern nights, trying to take or maintain your hold of a city. The book Damnation City already creates a pretty great template for developers to pull from. Use the Regency system as a basis. Your job is to keep the Primogen happy, maintain the complicated tithes involved in the tangled web of lords and vassals, and keep potential threats complacent with deeds to territory or otherwise shut them down using politics or violence. Keeping your hold means dominating, ghouling, bribing, or otherwise coercing notable mortals in the city, but they're a short-lived resource in the long game. The necessity of torpor means grooming a brood of childer to take over when you go to sleep.Rather than creating an expansive map of Europe and the surrounding areas, give us a half dozen maps of cities to serve as playgrounds. Differing geographies and relationships between the major factions means players will have to learn to adapt to different styles of play. Add in a toolkit where players can create their own maps and hierarchies and share them with others, and you have an expansive platform that would never get old.

    Vampire the Requiem: Bloodlines
    Everyone loves Bloodlines, but I think Requiem has some strong elements that could take that mode of gameplay and make it unique. Make Touchstones one of the central corners of gameplay. You have an overarching (if open-ended) storyline, but things are further complicated by the mortals and their organizations that you use as anchors. Is it worth staging a siege on your rival's nightclub if it means your ex-husband will lose his job in the process? Will you climb into bed with the sadistic Daeva harpy because she funds the opera house where you used to perform? Even premade touchstones will allow players to engage in roleplaying by creating their own stories, and there's a natural push and pull for what players will want to do because it advances the greater story and what they want to do because it helps the people and things their character cares for.

    Hunter the Vigil: Street Tactics
    Hunter by way of XCom. You have a map with territories carved out by all kinds of monsters, and it's the job of your plucky hunters to take back the city. Combat plays out as turn-based tactics, but there's a macro layer of resource and time management strategy that requires researching the monsters you'll be fighting, outfitting your characters with new weaponry, making sure they get the rest they need to maintain their personal lives and hold onto their sanity, and train and develop tactics for whatever they may be encountering. How you choose to approach the neighborhoods is as important as the actual street melees.

    Hunter the Vigil: Night Horrors
    Oh my god guys, how cool would a co-op action game in the style of Left 4 Dead or Evolve (or the upcoming Friday the 13th game) be? "Chronicles" are broken up into 3 - 5 successive maps that can shift based off of secondary objectives. They're generated semi-randomly, which means players have to get good at predicting what kind of creatures they may encounter and outfitting themselves accordingly. Stock characters level using a similar system to the whole score of competitive multiplayer shooters out there, and success means balancing your team. Do you soup your character up with pure combat perks like fighting styles; support your team with merits like resources (which lets you better arm yourself between maps), safehouse (which gives you more space to resupply and recover), influence (which provides you with respawn points and access to strategic points on the map), and contacts (which determine how much information you have about the monsters you might be facing; or investing deeply in a compact or conspiracy to pump your character up with endowments?

      Werewolf the Forsaken: Hunting Ground

      A werewolf the forsaken RPG/Grand Theft Auto 5 and Payday 2 hybrid. Wolf Bloods and human packmates take roles similar to the hired contractors in GTA 5.

              Mortals - GTA V (in first person) in a really really screwed up world.
              Vampire - A mixture between Dishonoured and a Dating Sim. Dating-Sim might seem odd at first, but the covenant/touchstone aspects of the game would be nice to actually get interaction.
              Werewolf - as said previously GTA V + Payday 2.
              Mage -.... Don't actually know what mechanics would be suitable... Don't know of any mystery games with actual good mechanics... It's hard to do interactive mysteries that aren't ridiculously railroaded to the point where it doesn't feel like your investigating anything. So maybe a tell-tale style game, where it's more the stories focus and the mysteries mechanics are less shoved in?
              Promethean - Very non-standard survival game set in a single city and it's outlying rural area? The promethean needs a specific thing in the city for their pilgrimage, but they have to be very careful in finding shelter, minimizing socialization, not awakening too many pandorans, trying to fight off pandorans without making the city go all zombie-apocalypse-survival game on you.
              Changeling - Could be a lot of things... From dungeon-defense where you make a hallow and defend in from hedgebeasts and hunters by shaping the area into hazards, to Devil May Cry style over the top action in the hedge and in dream-scapes, to watchdog's type game except instead of hacking tech you get contracts that let you convince things to do things.
              Hunter - Like payday but monsterhunting instead of heists.
              Geist - Persona
              Mummy - Darksiders/God of War clone, where you start off as a superpowerful being that can annihilate everything but the game grows more difficult as you progress and your powers become weaker. Make sure it's full of flashbacks as you regain your memories.
              Demon - Assassins Creed + GTA V style sandbox-y city.
              Beast.... CofD MMO?

              Genius templates: Super Science Mini-Template for Demon: the Descent

              Oracle the Endbringers: Time-Manipulator Fan-Splat
