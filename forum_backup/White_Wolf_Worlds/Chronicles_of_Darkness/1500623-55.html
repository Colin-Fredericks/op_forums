                                                        A persistent item provides its effect for as long as the spell is supposed to last and the item exists, in contrast to a contingent item requiring a trigger. Since Imbued Items do not gain a dice bonus from Yantras and cannot contain Paradoxes to mitigate the resulting penalties to the spellcasting pool, using a contingent item to trigger an indefinite effect is less likely to succeed without exploiting Word Of Command (and, considering that without Flexible Reach such an item would have the indefinite duration baked in, it would seem reasonable to ask why such an item wasn't persistent already).

                                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                            A persistent item provides its effect for as long as the spell is supposed to last and the item exists, in contrast to a contingent item requiring a trigger.
                                                            Yes, obviously, I know that, but how? Mechanically speaking. Reach? Spell factors?

                                                            I know how it works for contingent items. The creator sets Reach while imbuing, if it exceeds their free Reach the item always has a risk of Paradox when activated, even if you reduce it later with Flexible Reach. Spell factors are set by the user every time they activate it, affecting the spellcasting dice pool as usual.

                                                            And like I said, I can see how I could use those rules to make an item that casts a spell with an indefinite (or at least long) duration, but I don't see where anything is said about how persistent items work.

                                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                            using a contingent item to trigger an indefinite effect is less likely to succeed without exploiting Word Of Command
                                                            Is that the consensus of how Word of Command interacts with imbued items? That seems rather ripe for abuse. Since it would basically bypass the roll completely, wouldn't that mean you could max out every spell factor and tank the dice pool into the deep negatives... and then not care at all, because you just get Potency successes automatically (in a game where a single success is pretty much always enough)?

                                                            I would have assumed the only thing it does is let you bypass the trigger to activate the item directly (handy if some joker made it annoyingly complicated to use their item), letting spellcasting happen as normal. Since imbued items can be used by anyone, the trigger is the only condition that decides whether the item "will release its magic". By comparison, a werewolf fetish requires that you be Uratha and that you succeed a Resolve+Composure roll, so the spell lets you bypass the Uratha condition and also automatically succeed that roll. But if you use a talen, those don't have activation rolls, so that part of the spell does nothing, and you just roll normally for the Facet or Influence.

                                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                            without Flexible Reach such an item would have the indefinite duration baked in
                                                            Did I miss something? Flexible Reach only talks about reducing Reach, going from Advanced to Basic and removing options, not the reverse.
