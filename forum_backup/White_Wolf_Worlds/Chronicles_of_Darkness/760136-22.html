                Really rough draft of modified Colony rules. Looking at converting zombie swarm rules made by Claire into something usable for us.


                New Trait: Colony
                All Monarchs are tended to by many lesser creatures, all various strains of the Endless made to serve her. They act as everything from a labor force, a private army, personal servants, and everything in between, all at her beck and call.
                Traditionally, Colonies are located in Beneath, a place underground that skirts between realworld location and alternate reality, usually accessed through wormholes or through spatially folded physical locations. As a general rule a Monarch may access or pull forces from her Colony from any Wormholes from her own Court or via physically entering the Beneath through the realspace anchors of her Colony. Generally speaking, Wormholes are safer and more expensive, but the anchor point of a Colony is a vulnerability that anyone with the knowledge can find.

                But as many servants and warriors a Monarch is able to own, there are limits to how much this force can do, its capabilities, its ability to replenish losses, its own lacks from incomplete Geneweb, and other factors.
                Colonies are represented by five attributes, each representing an estimation of the Colony’s abilities at given tasks. These are:
                Military
                Knowledge
                Industry
                Espionage
                Diplomacy
                All are rated from 1 to 10, with a single dot in each at the start, plus an extra dot in the Monarch’s primary area of focus. Raising Colony Attributes is a single merit dot.
                At any time, a Monarch or Servant may order the Colony to dispatch a unit of Drones to fulfill an assigned task, rolling one of the attributes above for the dice pool for an appropriate task. Alternatively, there are few tasks that can be done without a dice roll. Colonies have some basic default Tasks, with more being unlocked as the Monarch grows in Principalities.
                Aside from attributes, Colonies possess a resource pool called Wealth, representing the total output of the Monarch’s controlled Territory and resource harvesting abilities.. Wealth has no real cap, but it does not replenish in the middle of the Chronicle. Territories will be detailed later in their own section.




                Starting Colony Actions

                Conscription Create an army. Swarm rules with “successes” as points to spend on improving. Spending Wealth allows you to add extra points ontop of it.


                Spy Spy on a target.
                Create: Raw Resources The Monarch commands his industrial centres to synthesize materials or commodities, either to trade with another colony, for an individual construction project or simply to supply Swarms or Servants working in the field. This can only create raw materials or form them into simple shapes. For example, this could be used to produce blocks of stone but not a statue.
                Although the process used to enact this ability are a combination of biological factories and psi-chemistry that can produce most anything from most anything else the Storyteller may limit exceedingly rare materials such as precious metals or gems. Bear in mind that things produced through Create: Raw Resources will not necessarily be a perfect copy of their earthly counterparts. Wood will behave similarly but might not have a grain or rings.
                The amount of points that must be spent to create a given resource is dependent upon the rarity, complexity and desired bulk of the final product. (Edit to accomidate new mechanics.)
                Note: “Create” has multiple uses, with Principalities unlocking more things to create. For now, only basic harvesting is available.

                Research Study a topic via experimentation or knowledge. “Passively” roll dice for extended research actions? Spend Wealth for bonuses.


                Influence Something involving propaganda or political maneuvers.

                    Created an alternate dot-5 for Quills; re-worded the first dot; modified Noble Form powers to give -agains instead of flat bonuses; everything else is a re-post. Personally, I think there should be some kind of pattern to Noble Form benefits, something like: each dot gets a new bonus. Bonuses can be Attributes, Skills, or Traits. Each Principality gets two, and aren't shared by any other. One bonus starts at the first dot; the other at the second dot. Attributes increase by 1 every other level. Skills gain a greater -again every other level (9-again --> 8-again --> rote). Traits increase by 2 every other level. Stuff that doesn't fall into those categories should go into Active Powers, given a dot rating, and be made free (or cheaper) when in Noble Form.

                    As for colony mechanics, I like them. I'm at a loss as to what conscription does, though (the rest I can see how to mechanize, but "conscription" is vague: is the Colony gearing up its own parts for war, or picking up fodder from the local populace, or building weapons/armor, or what?).

                    Quills
                    The mind is a mutable thing, and can be transformed and manipulated even by sloppy human science. To a Monarch, a brain is as parchment, able to be read at will.

                    Active Powers
                    [1] Cryptogram
                    With little effort, a Monarch can leave secret messages for only a select few to read.
                    Cost: 1 Psi
                    Action: Instant
                    Duration: Indefinite
                    Effect: When creating a message, the Monarch can choose to have it understandable by a finite class of people (such as Monarchs, Diplomats, a single Court, those who have read a particular book, those who hunt vampires, those who can talk to spirits, only the writer, etc.). Anyone else reading sees it as gibberish; an extended Intelligence + Academics - the Monarch's Authority + Quills can be made to decipher it if the reader suspects the nonsense is something more. This power lasts as long as the message exists.

                    [2] Treat as Equals
                    A Monarch can pick another being to mentally communicate with, as though they were both of Endless stock.
                    Cost: 1 Psi (free in Noble Form)
                    Action: Instant, Contested (optional)
                    Dice Pool: Presence + Authority vs Composure + Power Trait (can choose not to resist)
                    Duration: Scene
                    Dramatic Failure: The Monarch cannot communicate mentally with the target, and cannot try again with that target for a week.
                    Failure: The Monarch cannot communicate mentally with the target. She can try again at a cumulative -1.
                    Success: If target is willing, or if the Monarch gains more successes, the Monarch can mentally communicate with the target as though they were both Monarchs. The target's effective authority is equal to their Power Trait or Resolve, whichever is higher. The target cannot communicate telepathically with any other Monarch, only ones that use this power.
                    Exceptional Success: The effects last for a full day.

                    [3] Read Mind
                    Thoughts are more complex than books, but a Monarch can still leaf through and find what he seeks.
                    Cost: 2 Psi (1 Psi in Noble Form)
                    Action: Extended (one roll per minute), Contested (optional)
                    Dice Pool: Wits + Authority vs Resolve + Power Trait (can choose not to resist)
                    Duration: Variable
                    Dramatic Failure: The reader becomes the read; the target learns one thing from the Monarch's mind that he'd rather keep hidden.
                    Failure: The Monarch gains no successes, or fewer than the target, and learns nothing more.
                    Success: The Monarch gains successes. Based on the information he is trying to find, he needs a certain total. The target experiences the memories as the Noble does, and is not incapacitated by this power.
                    1 success: current thoughts, significant events of the last week
                    3 successes: common thoughts, significant events of the last year, insignificant events of the last week
                    6 successes: hidden or private thoughts, significant older life events, insignificant events of the last year
                    10 successes: suppressed thoughts, trauma, insignificant older life events
                    +2 successes: each scene's worth of memories beyond the first
                    Exceptional Success: The Monarch makes a great deal of progress, and the target is briefly stunned, losing her next action.

                    [4] Suppression
                    At their core, skills are no more than neural connections created through training. A Monarch can strike these connections out, potentially crippling her foes.
                    Cost: 1 Psi
                    Action: Instant, Resisted
                    Dice Pool: Intelligence + Authority - Resolve
                    Duration: Scene
                    Dramatic Failure: Rather than removing connections, the Monarch reinforces them. The target is immune to any Quills power for the scene.
                    Failure: The Monarch changes no one's mind. She can try again at a cumulative -1.
                    Success: For each success, the Monarch causes a target to temporarily lose a dot in any Skill. If the target has no dots in the Skill (or no dots remaining), the target loses the ability to use that skill at all, and may suffer other effects at Storyteller discretion (Academics loss makes the target illiterate; Athletics loss causes dangerous clumsiness; Empathy loss removes facial recognition).
                    Exceptional Success: No effect beyond further successes.

                    [5] Hive Mind
                    At the height of his power, a Monarch can not only read minds, but connect them to each other. This provides a staggering benefit for those connected, allowing them to share knowledge, skills, and even Principalities.
                    Cost: 2 Psi (1 in Noble Form)
                    Action: Instant, Contested (optional)
                    Dice Pool: Resolve + Authority vs. Resolve + Power Trait (can choose not to resist)
                    Duration: Scene
                    Dramatic Failure: Mental feedback severs the connection before it's formed. The Monarch takes three resistant lethal damage, and suffers from the Insensate Tilt for Authority rounds.
                    Failure: The minds remain unlinked. The Monarch can try again at a cumulative -1 penalty.
                    Success: The Monarch connects his mind to another, on a deeper level than mere telepathy. This provides all the benefits of normal telepathy, as well as the ability to borrow dots in Skills and Principalities. Only one Skill or Principality can be borrowed in a turn, and the total dots borrowed per turn cannot exceed the Successes on this roll. The Skill or Principality can be changed on the user's turn as a Reflexive action, and borrowing a Skill or Principality does not cause it to be removed from the borrowed-from. Additionally, both the Monarch and the target can read each other's minds as though they used Read Mind with 2*successes.
                    The Monarch can choose to link multiple minds. Each mind beyond the first gives a cumulative -2 penalty to the roll. Targets can borrow from any mind linked, not just the Monarch's.
                    Exceptional Success: As success, and two different Skills or Principalities can be borrowed at a time. The total dots borrowed cannot exceed successes.

                    Noble Form Bonuses
                    [1] 9-again Empathy
                    [2] 9-again Empathy, 9-again Academics
                    [3] 8-again Empathy, 9-again Academics
                    [4] 8-again Empathy, 8-again Academics
                    [5] Rote Empathy, 8-again Academics

                                                            The following rules are adapted from the zombie swarm rules in Claire Redfields’s Zombie Creation Project upgraded into GMC, which were originated and modified, FWC, which in turn are adapted from the 1st edition rules and David Hill's excellent swarm form rules for Vampire. Basically, this is similar to what Valve does with the Source Engine with many authors copying and modifying a base system in succession.

                                                            Swarms
                                                            Every Monarch has the power of a nation at her beck and call, able to conjure up actual armies to slaughter her enemies. Whereas many other forces in the supernatural world rely on small, more hardened and better trained forces, even really powerful guardians, the Endless live up to their name and send uncountable numbers against their foes. To truly understand the Wrath of the Endless, is to face a tidal wave of chitin and scale: the Swarm.



                                                            Swarms possess traits similar to those of ephemeral beings, including a Rank, three broad Attributes and Advantages. While some ephemeral entities like spirits can form into swarms, such things are best handled by their own specific rules. A swarm’s Rank is not a measure of its supernatural power (and doesn’t act as Supernatural Tolerance), but rather its size and durability. Like ephemeral beings, a swarm’s traits technically range from 1-10, but rarely ever surpass Rank 5. A swarm possesses Power, Finesse, and Resistance Attributes, like ephemeral beings.
                                                            Power governs the swarm’s raw strength when it exerts itself and ability to overcome victims, but most swarms have relatively low Power.
                                                            Finesse describes how mobile the swarm is, and how well it can get around things like barriers.
                                                            Resistance represents the swarm’s overall durability to harm and environmental conditions.
                                                            Unlike other Swarms, Endless Swarms are capable of Mental and Social interaction, largely as a result of having a number of “elite” Foremen or even more elite and powerful Servants acting as figureheads and controllers.

                                                            In order to create a Swarm, a Monarch orders a Conscription, ordering her Colony to mass produce new fighters, allocate resources to producing more fighters, or even temporarily suspending other preexisting Drones and ordering them to fight...
                                                            She rolls her Colony’s Military + Authority, successes translating to a size from the chart below, where the Monarch is able to assemble a Swarm of the appropriate size and allocate attribute dots as she desires.
                                                            Willpower may not be spent on this roll, but each point of Wealth spent on Conscription adds 1 dice to the roll.

                                                            Advantages
                                                            Swarms don’t have Integrity traits, Virtues and Vices, or Willpower. They are mindless agglomerations of frenzied creatures. Neither do they have a Size trait, since they are treated as a single entity rather than individual creatures.
                                                            Instead of Numina, they have Aspects, special abilities bestowed to them from their Monarch representing unique powers, special equipment, or other factors.. Unlike for a regular Swarm, there’s no limit to the number of Aspects a Swarm may take.
                                                            However, gaining Swarm Aspects requires that the Monarch has Principalities that allow for them.
                                                            Second, each Aspect makes the Swarm more expensive to field, thus in order to save costs and be efficient, the number of fighters has to drop, in essence, shrinking the Swarm to give it more capabilities.
                                                            Thankfully, larger more powerful Swarms have some leeway into how many Aspects they may have largely due to the advantages of mass production and redundancy. In the Swarm table, it lists the number of Aspects a Swarm of a given size may field without it impacting the Swarm’s size. Each Aspect above that limit requires that the Swarm shrinks by 1 rank or size category. It does not gain the Free Aspects of its new size category, but does gain the Trait limits and attribute dots of the new category.


                                                            Health: A swarm’s Health is equal to its Rank + Resistance.
                                                            Initiative: Initiative is equal to Finesse.
                                                            Defense: Defense is equal to Power or Finesse, whichever is higher.
                                                            Speed: Speed is equal to Power + Finesse + a species factor. Take an average species factor if the swarm is composed of multiple creatures, like insects and spiders. Swarms possess any special movement abilities of the base creature, like flight or wall-crawling.

                                                            Fighting the Swarm
                                                            Swarms attack by surrounding their prey and suffocating, biting and stinging them to death. Everyone caught in the swarm suffers from panic and limited visibility and hearing. Victims suffer the persistent Distracted Condition until they escape. A swarm rolls its Power to attack. The swarm can condense its size by one or more Ranks to increase the damage done by an additional die per step. For example, a Rank 4 swarm (8-yard radius) with Power 2 could condense into a 1-yard swarm and attack a single target with 5 dice. Condensing like this leaves the swarm more vulnerable, and swarms cannot swell to larger size Ranks. For each step by which it condenses, add one die to any attacker’s dice pool. Only full-body armor protects against the swarm, and then only at half its rating. Defense does not apply, though supernatural powers like Celerity work normally. Damage caused by Endless Swarms is lethal.
                                                            Swarms are difficult to harm. After rolling attacks against the swarm as normal, factoring in armor and other modifiers, the swarm takes one point of damage of the appropriate type, or two points with an exceptional success. Damage suffered reduces its Health. Fire, explosions, chemical sprays and other area effects cause normal damage or even no damage depending on the circumstances and the opponent. The swarm’s maximum Rank is limited by its Health, so as it takes damage, the swarm shrinks in size. Past rank 6, only especially potent or wide reaching fire, explosions, or chemical attacks would do normal, the swarm having grown to enormous size and having become nigh impregnable to even area of affect weapons. At rank 6, even area of effect weapons may only do 1 point of damage or even no damage, while other types of weapons do no damage without special caveats. Also, at really extreme numerical differences, the GM may decide to resolve combat with only a single roll such as with Down and Dirty Combat. In such cases, the Swarm adds its Rank to the roll.
                                                            Against other swarms, both do full damage to each other.



                                                            Endless Swarms Summary
                                                            -Able to Mentally and Socially interact.
                                                            -Are telepathic and subservient to Monarchs, are able to rebel if necessary.
                                                            -Formed by Conscription (Colony Military + Monarch Authority), each success dictating size. Wealth may be spent for a +1 bonus to this roll.
