    Supernal Entities in the Sundered World

    The Wise believe that the Pangaeans are fallen gods, former inhabitants of the Supernal World. When the Time Before came crashing down to its end, these divine beings were forever sundered from the realms they called home. But there were other gods who did not suffer this fate, entities who tied themselves so deeply with their domains that they cannot stray from them without risking their destruction. Particularly experienced Wise can learn and perform ceremonies which call these deities forth for a time, but some have been known to manifest on their own in the World of Flesh through sacred places not unlike the Border Marches. Regardless of how they appear, though, the appearance of such beings is considered an auspicious omen by the People.

    Beings native to the Supernal World are more powerful in this era than they are in succeeding millennia. Part of this stems from the wilder and more fiery nature of the realms at the time. But the state of the world before the Sundering is another reason why they thrive as they do.

    In this world, magic is abundant and a part of everyday reality. The Sleeping Curse is not as pervasive an influence among mortals as it will be in modern nights, and many remnants from the Time Before have yet to erode from centuries of Dissonance. All this makes it possible for Supernal Entities to exist a little longer, but they must still return to their home realms, lest they be dragged into the depths of the Outside.

    Mechanically, the rules for Supernal Entities remain the same. However, they are able to exist in the Fallen World for longer stretches of time due to the world having more tentative ties to the Supernal World. A Supernal being summoned by one of the Wise suffers a point of Corpus damage for each day that it stays in the circle. If it leaves the circle, it suffers this damage every twelve hours. And if it comes into contact with Sleepers, this time interval is further reduced to six hours.


    The End of an Era

    The death of the Great Predator, and the subsequent rise of the Gauntlet created ripples that extended all the way to the Supernal World. And just as the Wise have their influence wane in the aftermath, so too do Supernal Entities find their ability to spend time in the Fallen World lessen. Dissonance eventually erases the scars of the Time Before, and the Pangaeans gradually shed their knowledge of the Arcana in order to cope in the changed world. With fewer tethers to the Supernal Realms, the World of Flesh becomes increasingly more corrosive to these otherwise powerful gods. The Awakened who continue to summon them for guidance now do so sparingly, as few are loathe to cause the death of such a being by trapping it in the Fallen World.

            To answer the question of what Compromise looks like for the Unchained in the Sundered World, you could always use the rules for the Exclusion Zone on pg. 114 of the Demon Storyteller's Guide. Mechanically, it adds +3 to all Compromises and the Unchained don't have to roll for Compromise for remaining in Demonic Form for more than one scene.

            In regards to Turtle, Calm and Contemplative, a good place to start would probably be Demantu the Abyssal Queen, a powerful Lune in War Against the Pure on pg. 127 It is worth noting that beyond being symbols of wisdom and longevity, turtle symbolism actually has a rather vicious dark side. In Nigeria the turtle is a trickster, the most cunning of all animals. In Ancient Egypt it was called "an abomination to Ra" and killed as an evil creature. It is associated with the Underworld in Ancient Egypt and in other cultures, and a leaf cut to resemble a turtle could supposedly banish ghosts. In some Polynesian cultures, the turtle was even the symbol of the war god. The thing uniting all of these depictions is tenacity, endurance, and seeming invincibility paired with intelligence, bottomless wisdom, and a rather terrifying ability to be patient.

                                            Perhaps that could spawn ideas for putting the Pangaean in an Arisen-related setting? Lol. Anyway, here's more stuff for me on Turtle.

                                            Description: One of the greater gods of the Border Marches, it is said that this Pangaean rivals Urfarah in age as well as power. Such a claim is backed by his ability to venture out of the Border Marches unchallenged in order to roam the Worlds of Spirit and Flesh.

                                            Turtle is honored by the People. His animals make their homes in the River, and priests and shamans invoke his name to honor or tame the river-spirits so that their villages thrive. However, he is also called to act as a guide for the dead, so that they may reach the Underworld and make their final descent into its dark waters. Hence, his role is a dualistic one in the pantheon of the gods. Creator and Destroyer. Life and Death.

                                            Many among the Wise and Uratha have tales on why Turtle is given a wide berth by even the Great Predator. Some say that the two Pangaeans clashed in a great battle not unlike the one between Wolf and Bull. Others suspect that Turtle found some secret of the Marches that gave him an edge against his fellow gods, including Urfarah. Whatever the reason, Turtle wanders freely as a result, and often travels to a world on either end of Pangaea in pursuit of his own agendas. Not only that, but the Wise have discovered the Pangaean also spending much time in the Astral Realm, and even the Ocean Beyond Life.

                                            In the World of Flesh, the People seldom find themselves graced by Turtle's presence. The Pangaean secludes himself in the oceans of whichever world he is in. And he seldom strays far from the shore in those few times he sets foot on land. This relative isolation may be another reason why Wolf refrains from hunting him.

                                            Storytelling Hints: Turtle is revered by other tribes besides the People. Islanders and seafarers feel the Pangaean's influence more strongly in the waters of their homes, and the Wise among their number often seek him out for knowledge and a number of other boons. These Awakened cultivate a relationship with Turtle and other waterborne gods of the Marches that is not always positive, but nowhere near as hostile as that of the land-based Vinca.
