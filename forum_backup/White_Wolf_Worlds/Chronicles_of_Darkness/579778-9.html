                            Originally posted by Magnum Opus View Post
                            (neat word for "removing" but without the metaphysical effects of killing almost every supers in the game)
                            does nobody know how the soulless condition works

                            is that what is happening

                            did people just not read what losing your soul actually does

                            because this is at least the second distinct category of failure of comprehension that has been demonstrated on this issue

                            You lose the ability to regain as much of your sense of self (i.e. Willpower) from your Virtue and the stability of your self-concept (i.e. your Integrity) starts to waver as you try to shore up your ego with guilty comforts (i.e. indulge your Vice and trigger severe breaking points) until you have the barest scraps of your personal identity and start losing your core personhood (i.e. Willpower dots), whereupon the same stress fractures can eventually prevent you from developing the character further. That's it. That is the entire spectrum of effects that replicating the Soulless Condition on a character presents, aside from incidentals like less resistance to possession. You flip the rates at which you regain Willpower from Virtue and Vice, Integrity eventually drops to 1, whereby your Virtue loses its satisfaction altogether and your Willpower capacity eventually collapses to 0, whereupon you're subject to the effects of the Broken Condition and can't spend Experiences or defend yourself.

                            Translating some of these concepts to other supernaturals may not be totally straightforward, but the games with mechanics that specifically interact with soul loss have less dire consequences in store than death, and the bulk of the other character types nevertheless evince a greater degree of influence by wills not their own with falling Integrity-equivalents to begin with, whether in the form of spiritual directives taking greater precedence or just generally having more difficulty relating to and interacting with humanity.

                            It may be ambiguous whether the long-term suppression of a vampire's soul might eventually rob them of their ability to regain Willpower through their Mask and fracture their Dirge, but losing Humanity and Willpower and becoming more susceptible to possession are in line with the narrative effects of both soul loss and becoming more monstrous; ditto for the Bone and Blood and Harmony of werewolf souls. Mage has the rules for soul loss baked in, and having demonic Cover degrade in convincingness with the replication of soul loss when it's at least partially a memetic construct whose degradation can often result in the development of inhuman qualities isn't too much of a stretch. We don't yet know if Promethean 2e has different Virtue/Vice equivalents, but losing autonomy and progress on the Pilgrimage is, again, in line with the effects of soul loss while still not being a "you are dead" flag; Mummy is about the farthest of any line from a second edition, but "suppression of the soul" is basically an exact description of what low-to-no Memory represents and the entire reason why that line's character development mechanics incentivize raising Memory and the qualities that correspond to soul-anatomy in the first place.

                            You Are Meat has its problems, but "metaphysically speaking this should logically kill or deprotagonize every playable supernatural being it hits" is not one of them.
