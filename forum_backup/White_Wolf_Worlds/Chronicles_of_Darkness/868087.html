    Visitor: the Harvest (UFO fansplat brainstorming, no strings attached)

    04-18-2016, 10:57 PM
    I want to mention in advance that I have not much time or intention to start a project at the current time. (although I'd still love to see people's related ideas!)
    However, seeing how I keep returning to old concept threads, I think dumping what you do have like this could help someone with a similar concept down the line.
    ------

    Visitor: the Harvest -
    original name and idea by PneumaPilot

    SUGGESTED INSPIRATION:
    The Man Who Fell to Earth. (book/movie) Good insight into the psychological struggle that a lone infiltrator in a completely enviroment must face, regardless of how well he was prepared.
    Invader Zim (cartoon). Plenty of horror ideas in an animatged sit-com about a stupid alien invader trying to conquer an equally stupid humanity.
    Fire in the Sky (movie): Probably a best portrayal of the distrust and media circus a witness of an alien kidnapping would need to deal with.
    Any good internet source on the history and research of UFO phenomena. There's no substitute for learning about the real thing.
    Any UFO magazine or collection of UFO encounter stories. Thought their factuality is often questionable, they definitely make for great horror stories.
    Retro sci-fi magazine covers. Always good for visual inspiration.

    INTRO 1: The aliens

    A couple's car breaks down on the road after they start following a shining object in the sky. When the come by, they realize that hours have passed and they have no recollection of it; later under hypnosis, they describe an encounter with human-like, but nonhuman creatures.
    A fighter pilot chases what he believes to be an enemy aircraft, coining the term "flying saucer" in the process of describing how the object moved.
    A pair of pranksters create circles in local crop, but when they reveal it no-one believes them.
    A radio show about a fictional alien attack causes mass panic when listeners assume the report to be real.
    Hundreds of people contact a radio talk show to report about flying saucers over a highway, even though the first call turns out to be an intentional hoax created by a skeptic to test how other callers will react.

    The UFO craze was one of the prominent phenomena of the 2nd half of the 20th century. Post-World War I and II, with an already ingrained fear of invasion by foreign powers, people started to worry about an entirely different type of invasion that this time would come from beyond the stars.
    In one sense, the new craze followed the pattern of previous paranormal fashions such as spiritualism and psi, but it also reflected people's worries and fears at the time: a fear or nuclear weapons and particularly biological weapons, the new dangers opened up by space travel that preoccupied the sci-fi shows of the age.

    In the 21st century, public interest in UFOs seems to have died down. With the end of the Space Race governments started to focus on more earthly matters, and we gave up our space-faring dreams. We entered what we call the Information Age where quick connections, digital phones and instantaneous messages rule everything, and the idea that UFOs could remain hidden from us started to look increasingly improbable.
    But who knows? Abduction stories always included elements of aliens inferfering with human perception and technology.
    If there's anything about alien encounter reports, it's that they make us re-think just how reliable the human mind and our perception of reality is.

    INTRO 2: The Harvesters

    "Visitor: The Harvest" is intended to evoke the mood of urban legends about encounters of the third kind with humanoids and other possibly-alien cryptids;
    as well as various sci-fi horror movies.
    A Visitor, simply put, is an alien monster, but one that, unlike some other movie horrors, is not an unique being or an aberration, but a member of a highly successful race of similar monsters which seeks to rival and possibly out-compete the human race. The extra layer of horror with alien horrors is all the ways they are COLLECTIVELY better than all of us.

    But "Visitor" is also a game about a more specific alien subtype.
    "Visitor" players take on the role of infiltrators, disguised and dropped on a newly discovered inhabited planet (Earth) to prepare it for a specific end for their species.
    Some Visitors are collectors, others exploiters or invaders. What is true for all of them that without exception, their lone masquerade takes its toll on them;
    and the Visitors CHANGE. Some bond with humans more closely than intended in an attempt to ease the burden of their lone mission. Others lose their ability to keep their mask intact and become monsters of legend. Most become something in between: a monstrous stranger who is as likely to bring destruction as gifts and knowledge undreamt of.

    THEME: UFO mystery-horror. The fear of the unknown, the alien. The idea that something more powerful than you is out there, able to kidnap you at any time even from your own bed, and your powerlessness against it. The horror of being haunted by something you are unable to prove to anybody, yourself included. But also of curiosity and wonder, a taste of something new and transcendent and exciting
    MOOD: Alienation, estrangement, loneliness, homesickness. Visitors have to leave their homes to enter hostile worlds, to fake comradeship and affection for creatures they find menacing and repulsive. No matter how prepared all Visitors eventually experience identity loss,
    This struggle between going mad - which leads to mistakes and usually ends with the capture and death of the Visitor, the failure of their mission, or, if especially unlucky, maybe even the doom of their entire species - is at the core of the Visitor game.

    Y-splat: Mission[?][Attitude? what's a good for a group's attitude towards dealing with other groups?]

    The Mission[?] is how the species views and treats humans (and possibly other species).

    Scientists:

    aim: knowledge
    Dominant stats: mental stats and academical skills
    Why you should symapthize with them: They can be peaceful observers who rarely disturb humans. In are cases they are bringers of scientific knowledge.
    Why you shold hate them: They're the ones who'll dissect you to see what makes you tick. They'll treat you as a lab animal, force you to do endure physical tests psychological or psychological mind games to see what makes you tick. Then they'll put you in a zoo to be an exhibit millions of lightyears away from home.

    Exploiters:

    aim: some type of resource: water, rare metals, etc.
    Dominant stats: 'power' stats; business and industry
    Why you should sympathize with them: Many come from overpopulated worlds with most natural resources already spent. Their work may be saving an entire species from extinction.
    Why you should hate them: They are the ones who use humans as a food source. And they rarely care how what they take affects the world they take it from.
    They'd steal all our air or build a Dyson sphere around the Sun or pump it clean and let us die if it meant their own race can be fed.

    Invaders:

    aim: to destroy other life forms. (advance parties survey the technological level and strenght of military defenses and sabotage them before the main fleet decides if they are worth conquering.
    Dominant stats: physical
    Why you should sympathize with them: Many (not all) Invader races are xenophobic or and see delivering the first strike as a way to protect their own species. Sometimes their target isn't even actually humans, but some other form of Earth life that is a potential danger to them, eg. a virus to which humans are carriers.
    Why you should hate them: They are out to get you, and your family, and your neighbor, and your dog. Other Visitors may pick off humans one at a time but Invaders rate their progress based on how many humans (along with enviroment and wildlife) could they murder at the same time if needed.

    Guardians:

    aim: to aid lesser species on their development path, and "uplift" them to the civilizational level of older alien cultures.
    Dominant stats: 'defense' stats; supernatural, occult, religion, etc.
    Why you should sympathize with them: They are possibly the only type of alien civilization who truly wants to help you.
    Why you should hate them: The future they imagine for humans may not be one you like, and may to a human even seem downright terrifying. Stories of people transformed into slug-like lifeforms that the aliens considered more "adaptable" or "evolved" and similar "gifts" are examples of the "goodwill" of the Guardians. Some smother their protegees with affection, drowning out any possibility of progress to save the species of the "evils" of civilization they see theirs to suffer from. Others help, but present extraordinary bills for it, which typically involves forcing the other race into slave status.
    And since they rarely consider us mentally evolved enough to know what's good for us, they rarely take "no" for an answer...

    Infiltrators:

    aim: to live on Earth and mix their genetic material with Earthlings
    Dominant stats: finesse/social, medicine/biology
    Why you should sympathize with them: They are the only ones who'd consider coexisting with humans as equals. And knowing of all the other Visitor races out there, fusing with another alien race may be weak, underdeveloped humanity's only chance of survival. And at least their methods are gentle - a nonhostile takeover that you won't even notice until it's too late.
    Why you should hate them: They want to take your planet from you. They are insidous. They make horrifying hybrid children. And they are "unfaithful", and have done this with many other species already.

    New merit (safe place SHIP

    A Ship is a unique for of "safe place" that a Visitor may possess.
    Some ships descend on the surface physically and stay hidden by clever cloaking; others are constantly in orbit and approachable though some sort of teleportation device.

    Not all Visitors begin with a ship of their own. Some are dropped off and abandoned or are just one of several units sent down in a pod from a mothership or launched as seeds into space at random. Others crash the ship during landing, or have it discovered and confiscated by human authorities in some manner.

    A ship offers the following benefits:
    As long as a Visitor's Identity stat remains 7 or higher, entering to the ship can recover 1 WP per day as it helps to reaffirm the character's sense of self as an alien visitor.
    (ST: don't give it for free; somebody will eventually question where the character disappears to every day for an hour.)

    Ships may possess facilities that must be purchased with exp:

    -medical bay: Offers the same bonus as a hospital for both humans and Visitors.

    - library: Functions as a normal library, but : other star systems, species, encountered,
    It allows a character to research
    Of course, it can also be turned against the Visitor in the wrong hands...

    - aircraft engine/travel:
    All Ships can travel through space, but only a handful are also operational as flying machines in Earth orbits. (There may be any number of differences between Earth and the homeworld to explain why some aren't
    A Ship with this functionality can be on any point of the Earth faster than any commercial airplane.

    THE RETURN
    Even if they have ships, Visitors can not return home until their mission is fulfilled.
    Every species protects the coordinates of their homeworld from others, so the return is to permission from command, who are fully aware of the extreme psychological reaction some Visitors experience upon first contact with the enemy, and will not permit a return without a VERY good reason.
    If they can return, they will likely be evaluated, then returned to Earth or allowed to retire. (A too good report can be as dangerous for those who do not wish for one of the two outcomes as a too bad one.)

    THE "MASK"
    Some visitors use space suits and cloaking devices, others are wrapped into lizard-skin. There's been talk about incorporeal aliens that can possess human bodies.
    The truth however is that the greater majority of Visitors are biologically altered so that they can survive on Earth. Without this, they'd fall victim to extreme chemical reactions, minor infections, or starvation.
    However, they still retain a partially alien biology, which is why they avoid any examination by humans.

    DRAWBACKS OF THE VISITOR TEMPLATE:
    - Visitors can never benefit from the bonus offered by human hospitals, and if examined by a human doctor, the first rolls do not recover damage. Instead, they determine whether the doctor spots that they possess nonhuman biology. If the doctors are still intent on continuing after, only then can real healing begin. Both rolls are done with a -3 penalty.

    Their alternative is the medical bay of a Ship. (see the related merit)

    modified Integrity: IDENTITY

    Some Visitors are elite warriors, the pride of their species picked from thousands and prepared for years for their task. Others are disposable, two a penny drones, whose survival and arrival on Earth is only a happy accident. Again others are their own masters, coming to Earth and staying by their own free will. Once here though, they all play the same game of pretend. And the longer they play it, the trickier it becomes. Deprived of contact with their homeworlds, the carefully constructed human side they have to maintain 23 hours of the day starts to feel real, no: it starts to feel like the true self of the invader.
    The Visitor may entertain thoughts of revealing herself to Earthlings, maybe even side with them.
    Naturally they know this is impossible; that they'd be killed or captured if they learned the truth; but the craving to to take off the mask and be honest just once is often too strong.

    Being far away from anyone who could reprimand them for it, and overcinfidence in their success at being undiscovered are other reasons why the Visitor's self-control eventually erodes, and she start to openly display habits that hint at her nonhuman nature.

    Those who fail to assimilate are prone to making mistakes for a different reason.
    Forced to stay in a world that has remained alien and uncomfortable for them, they experience increasing hunger to complete this mission and finally return home. Their methods become hasty and overly aggressive

    This tendency of being driven crazier and crazier as time spent on Earth passed is what we call Identity.
    The lower it is, the less certain the Visitor is who she is, and how she should be acting. Disgust over having become something else and fear that one day the mask will become stronger and take over, and simultanously: the disgust and fear over no longer being disgusted by the body and human culture; ready to give everything so that you can go home, yet hesitant and secretly glad that it does not have to happen yet; these eventually tear a Visitor apart if not attended to.

    -------------------------
    MAGIC A IS MAGIC A (SUPERNATURAL JAM (footnotes):
    Visitor powers are the result of tech and their normal biology, and not "supernatural" at all.
    Mechnically though there is little difference; even "Unseen Sense (Visitors)" characters are possible.

    Visitors do possess souls and can possess supernatural merits. (They are too far removed from humans that they could acquire a full splat template, but it is possible they have equivalents of these at home.) When Visitors interact with the supernatural realms, you should emphasize how their perfection differs. A Visitor may enter the Astral for example, but the concepts he sees there are entirely different, instead showing concepts that are appropriate for their species. (Which raises some interesting questions about the nature of the Astral, and how much of it are human visitors REALLY seeing.) Certain supernatural phenomena may be invisible or transformed; others will be similar. Use these to emphasize the nonhuman, otherworldly mind of the Visitors.

    ---------------------------
    STORYTELLING TIPS

    ROLEPLAYING THE STRANGE (roleplaying advice):

    Visitor are alien. ALIEN. Their mental processes are not like anything you'd expect from a human. While their psychology and behavior isn't impossible to decypher, it is certainly is a task big enough to occupy a group of xenopsychologists for years. To humans, especially those who encounter them for the first time, they may seem erratic and often illogical creatures.

    That's quite a roleplaying challenge, isn't it? When was the last time you tried to put yourself in the shoes of a four-brained silicon slug from Orion?

    While we'd like you to put some effort into bringing the STRANGENESS of the Visitor to the table, we'd also like to remind you that it is fine to treat it as icing on the cake, so to speak - it should be there when it enhances the story, but should be ignored whenever it seems to make empathizing with the other Visitor characters or immersing yourself in your character too difficult.
    There are actually some good reasons why a Visitor would act in a human-like manner:
    - all sentient species likely share psychological similarities. Instincts may force them to behave erratically sometimes, and they may consciously decide to act in self-destructive ways, but we can at least assume that they will act in a similar manner when an evident solution to a problem exists.
    - Visitors are trained to SEEM human, so they will follow that behavior pattern for most of the game regardless of what they are ACTUALLY thinking )

    You should try to treat the Visitor like you'd treat an Earth foreigner foremost. Try to imagine what their character's "home" life is like and what they consider "normal", and what their reaction to similar situations on Earth would be like as a result.

    You will find more ideas in the "Quirks" section.

    QUIRKS
    This list contains ideas that can help you portray your character more alien, and to get a better picture about what life as a member of their species is like.
    (Any sci-fi readers among you should be able to come up with similar, or better and more elaborate concepts, however.)

    Some difficulties apply to all Visitors directly after their first landing:
    - Breathing: Upon encountering Earth atmosphere for the first time, many Visitors are struck by an illusion of drowning. They can in fact breathe in and out automatically with subconsciou motor control just like humans, but the panic reaction their conscious mind experiences as it still attempts to operate in a different enviroment often overrides these initially. Although this phase passes fast, a Visitor may have to focus to breath (in order to suppress these habits) for days after.
    - Eating: For those species that do not normally eat, consuming fuel for the body is a messy and strange process; many are disgusted by the image of their own intestines and stomachs, and just swallowing is a challenge for them. Others know how to eat, but Earth food looks strange and unappealing. Most Visitors vomit their food back the first time they attempt to eat on Earth.
    - Social interaction:
    Visitors are initially paranoid, constantly expecting to be foound out. Humans seem repulsive and dangerous; not quite able to read them yet, they may misinterpret any visual clue as a possible sign of hostility.
    Even after this, interacting with the enemy usually remains the greatest challenge in the daily life of a Visitor.
    - Human behavior and culture: In this regard, Visitors act like any Earth foreigner (in fact, it is a common tactic to pretend to be from a foreign country, preferably one that locals already associate with strange habits; claiming to belong to an obscure religion with strict ordinances and bans is another favorite). Namely, their attitude towards local customs is often bemusement and instinctive comparision to how it differs from home. Visitors should be vary of relying on the protection this gives them; it could be believable that somebody does not know who the President is, but not knowing
    - Pet peeves: All Visitors have likes and dislikes (rarely even fears or obsessions) towards something on Earth based on its similarity to something on their home planet. A strange fear of cats; an obsession with an awful band; insisting on drinking maple syrup every day; etc. These allow the Visitor to feel more at home (or remind them how far from home they are).

    Below are some additional quirks you can use to color your character - some biological, some cultural.

    - Accelerated: The species uses a lightning-fast communication method (or just experiences the flow of time differently). As a result, human speech seems painfully slow and ineffective to the character, and impatience and frustration wherever needing to communicate with someone is the result.
    - Scavenger: In the Visitor's culture the dead are consumed, and there are various beliefs about how this lets his memories and strenght pass on to others (and this may even be a real species trait, not just a belief). The character may avoid funerals, and may not eat processed meat from other animals, and will feel compelled to acqauire and eat the body of close friends and family they lose.
    - Cut off hiveling: The alien normally has a psychic link with its mates and knowledge and decisions are transferred to the individual though this net. They have a problem with the concept of individuality; they feel lost and isolated, don't make decisions easily and loathe seeing humans protect their own freedom and individuality.
    (etc.)

    STORY IDEAS:

    - A Visitor from your [Y-splat] went crazy and is kept at a mental institute after dropping his mask and atttacking some humans. Find him, find out how if the human authorities suspect him or learned anything from him, then depending on his cooperativeness, free him or eliminate him. If the former, bring him back to the ship so that the leaders may judge this errant and decide if he can stay on Earth or is sent back to the homeworld for a trial.

    - The Prince of another alien dynasty that your homeworld is allied with will be forced to land on Earth because of certain circumstances. The Prince is a trained infiltrator and should have no problems fitting in, but it is believed that Visitors of a 3rd race currently at war with them will attempt to kill the Prince. Try to find and stop the assassin before he can strike, or act as the Prince's bodyguard until he has left Earth. Needless to say, his death means your execution as well.

    - Another Visitor went against her superiors' explicit orders and broke off all connection with her command. As per usual, the rouge is considered a major risk and secrecy breach, andthey will be sending a ship to eliminate her. Oh, and she used to be your best friend. You know she is most likely tending her human family, and does not intend to betrea anyone to the authorities, but that is not going to matter once the ship arrives.

    - Yours and another Visitor group are out for the acquisition of the same precious object. Be the first to get it. Don't forget, they may have powers you are unfamiliar with.
