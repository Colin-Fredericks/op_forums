                        Replying to the topic and at risk of opening the old scab wound:

                        The design choice of hard coding mortals to forget everything about the supernatural rather than the idea that they know about it but simply don't want to admit to it and would rather just drop it in the core game.

                        One invites mystery, wonder, and the agency to encounter worlds beyond this if one only has the courage to pursue. Other simply closes the door on these things.

                        I've already said my piece on this many a time and won't get into it but figured it was relevant to mention here.

                        That being said, my other opinion comes as follows:

                        WoD markets it's self as first and foremost, a horror game. The problem is a sizeable camp of players don't play it as such. They play it as either Urban Fantasy or Dark Urban Fantasy. Which is fine for the most part but leads to such things as downplaying the Abuse and Trauma themes of Changeling or the pains of Alienation of Promethean which isn't what the game is designed for. But in the end, the game is in the players, not the developer's hands when it comes to these things.

                                            I'm a major personal horror advocate too (despite having a fondness for pulp, action, and fantasy stories), which is exactly why the elements of ChroD that I don't like rub me the wrong way- I actually don't think Werewolf has much of a problem with it, since ChroD's sort of horror is about BEING a monster, and Forsaken (esp 2e) hammers in that being Good Werewolf Spirit Cop Human Defender is a hard fucking thing to be, and even if you pull it off you're gonna have be leaving bodies in your wake.

                                            Mage and Promethean have the most problems with being personal horror games to me- Mages are too shiny and enlightened and Prometheans have their handy little escape hatch (and they're Playing Wrong if they don't pursue it). Beast has this problem in a lesser way- Beasts are undeniably monstrous, but the game makes constant excuses for them and treats them like 3edgy5u coolkids who are just misunderstood, maaaaaaan. One of the reasons I like Leviathan more (in addition to the more coherent themes and setting) is that it really goes into how being an ancient primordial monster who fucks with people's minds without even meaning to would fucking suck if you actually want to live a stable life with meaningful relationships.

                                            Leviathan is a near perfect example of how to do personal "you are the monster" horror- you're an ancient and incredibly powerful being who could probably bench a tank, but at the end of the day you have to face the very real possibility that the world would be better off without you, and the guys with ray guns chasing you might be right after all. That's a hell of a lot scarier than "you're totally helping humanity by terrorizing them and the people who want to stop you are just dorks".

                                            EDIT: Atamajakki, you hit the nail right on the head with that one. If there's ever a New New World of Darkness or a Chronicles of Darkness 3e that deserves to be in the corebook.
