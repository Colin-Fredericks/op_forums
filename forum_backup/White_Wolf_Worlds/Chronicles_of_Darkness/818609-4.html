    Originally posted by Vree View Post
    Which comment would that be, is it #7 on the first page?

    I think that a Genius who is trying to cure herself of her Inspired condition can be a fascinating character. Certainly a good story in that.

    That said I'm not sure if I agree with the way you went about it.
    (Now, you can do in your own campaigns what you want, of course, you don't need to follow the book.
    Still, these are some pretty crucial elements.)

    When you say:

    "In this process, the genius takes one of his or her wonders, and proceeds to turn it into something that regular people can use"

    This isn't possible and this is why.

    Behind real technological advances there is research. Ie. an attempt to understand how natural processes work, then trying to see if any of what that allows for that could be beneficial for humans.
    How the real laws work is not up to the researcher. Sometimes you get unexpected or unwanted results. Sometimes it turns nature simply does not allow for something, or that the cost is too high to afford.

    Wonders definitely DO give you what you wish for. But, although the laws that hold them together may SEEM real, they are really a product of the Genius' imagination plus the mystical mumbo-jumbo wecall Mania and Inspiration.
    Remove Mania and the laws the Genius made up also disappear.
    (Of course some Genii may argue that these laws are also true and real on some meta-level of a "higher" reality, but it does not make much difference from the POV of science in our "mundane" reality.)

    Making a true, mass reproducable scientific equivalent of a Wondernot as simple as taking a Wonder and substituting the "real" laws. There may not even be an equivalent real law, or the same result may only be achievable through a completely different process.
    You can't force the laws you need on reality, you can only learn more about its set laws. (And that's what Mania is all about, forcing the laws you want on nature.)

    A genius who gets it into her head that she can make a Wonder "real", what she's actually doing is replacing make-believe laws with other make-believe laws, still all held together by Mania.

    I'm not saying Wonders can never be turned into "real" machines. All Wonders by their very nature have at least a little real science in them. Replace the Aether Mechanism in a clock with a regular mechanism, you've got a clock. It may not be able to show you the Universal Countdown anymore, but it will still tell the time. For a Genius who's trying to reconnect with the munande scientist she used to be, rebuilding her Wonders in this manner can certainly offer some solace and clarity.

    But this is very different from actual scientific advances, which can only be achieved by regular, mudane research, research and research.
    If a Genius can somehow make a recovery from her Maniac state she may be able to resume doing this. She may spend decades trying to remake the Quantum Mechanical Butterfly she once could create from just fantasy and air, but for real this time. She may find that natural laws do not allow it, or allow only for a much weaker version
    She may find that even if it is possible in theory, research will still need decades or even centuries to get there. That is the lot of a mundane scientist.

    (On the bright side, she may realize once again that reality can be more creative than human imagination and that there are unexpected, unexplored possibilities in this type of world too.
    Maybe she'll even end up preferring things being like this to what humans (her included) can dream up for themselves.)

    IF SHE CAN ACCEPT ALL THAT, then she may be able to make a recovery.
    If she is still trying to make Wonders turn real though, still chasing dreams, then actual recovery can't even begin yet.

    What a Genius who wants to be a real researcher has to do is this:

    First, she has to try to become a non-Genius. At least temporarily. To overcome a madness that clouds her eyes and think critically once more.
    The obvious problem is that any laws that a Genius can dream up BECOME real. It's not so easy to find criteria by which one can separate fact from fantasy.
    Of course, many of them ARE real too. Many Genii have a good theory which has ONE critical flaw. Sometimes it is a real flaw - the numbers don't add up, the logical process followed misses some required step. And sometimes the flaw is simply not being backed up by evidence.
    But the Genius can convince herself quite easily that thing ARE being backed up by evidence. How will she know it's not? People have accepted various scientific theories for centuries because they mostly fit and anything that didn't was not confirmed to be (un)true.
    But even if the Genius did try to confirm everything: Mania conspires to pull the wool over her eyes.
    Whole BARDOS spring up to seemingly support one theory or another.

    So here's what I think a Genius could do.

    1. force herself to become aware of the flaws in her own theory.

    It's really a self-seeking process.
    Do any results seem to come to her too fast, too easily? Those MAY well be a product of her madness. (A bigger problem is if they aren't, yet the Genius still can't trust them. See how this is not easy?)

    What happened during her Catalysation? When she thought she discovered something, it probably FELT real. Like she stumbled upon actual
    Yes, there probably all sorts of small bits that did NOT fit but she could not see them.
    Was there any time when she was going too fast, was too obsessed to pay attention?
    Is there anything that would have seemed surprising or unbelievable, before she became a Genius?

    The more successful she is at finding the tangly bits in her own theories, the better chances she has.

    2. seek therapy

    Madness is often routed in some sort of trauma for the Genius, often related to her Catalyst.
    If she realizes what ulterior desires keep her in the madness place and solve them, that could "cure" her too.
    It may actually be more sensible to take this approach.

    Many Geniuses have the desire to prove themselves feel wronged by their peers and society, and so on.
    So this COULD be where Inspiration comes from, too.

    3. surgery
    Well, not neccessarily real surgery. The idea here is that you identify the physical or mystical source of Inspiration and remove it by force.
    Of course, one problem is, nobody's really sure what the source of Inspiration IS.
    One option is to simply destroy random parts of oneself one by one until one is no longer a Genius..
    Another option is to try this on somebody else first. Number 3 is definitely the preferred option of many mad scientists. Genii who study Inspiration are dangerous and feared for this reason - they need OTHER Genii to be their test subjects.)

    IF the Genius completed the process (and nobody says any of these even really work), then he/she has to deal with the after-effects.
    Like an addiction, Inspiration tends to leave behind a void.

    Some Genii relapse or become Beholden, no longer able to believe in their own ideas but desiring the comforting feeling of there being a higher meaning, or organizedness, or a sense of morality, to nature. They need that certainty with which somebody declares the laws of nature to be so, and the only one they can get this from is another Genius.

    A rare unlucky few turn Clockstopper, or a similar creature. Having lost faith in ALL science, Inspiration takes a 180 turn and what previously warped the world to contain MORE science now forces it to hold LESS science, STILL proving the former Genius right.

    All of this is they make it in the first place. But here is a better question:
    If somebody believed in REAL science and was content with it this much, how did they become an Inspired in the first place?

    Every Genius transgresses at some point, venturing into the unknown driven by...something. They all sacrifice something, physically or inside themselves.
    Faust sold his soul to the devil. Frankenstein stole corpses. Pretorius was excommunicated by his peers.
    Inspiration is really a sort of madness. Not something you get out of the blue and definitely not something you get against your will.
    If that spark is not lit, if the Genius can not put his knowledge and work before what other humans hold dear in order of importance, then he will not Catalyze.
    Every genius once throws away conventions - including scientific conventions - in exchange for the secrets of the universe.

    I don't think it's impossible to try to go back on this choice - a Genius' path is fraught with disappointment and dissatisfaction.

    (Though this also has to do with their unsatiable hunger - a Genius may not neccessarily think his condition is WRONG or not better than the mundane method, they just still don't feel like it's ENOUGH.)

    But if you go by how mad scientists normally operate, would they drop a tear for their colleagues or the public, for the cowards who were too WEAK to see the truth?
    No way! You, alone, a human superior than the rest of humankind, can truly understand its meaning, truly appreciate its beauty.
    Without madness there is no "mad" science. If you are a scientist, but you're not mad. If you're not mad, how could you ever catalyze?

    ------------
    Now you don't have to actually answer any of that, your character, your game, your story.
    But I THINK I'd probably ask if I was GMing, how did this character become a Genius and what caused him to turn his back on it?
    Just to round out the character a bit.
    -----------

    If you want to do something for the world:
    onders may not be able to help scientific progress, but they can still do a lot of good. If you can cure a man's cancer with a pill, who cares that the pill was a one-of-a-kind? If you crush an evildoer with a giant robot, even if the robot will only exist as long as the Genius does, they are still crushed.

    Plus, the Inspired are a world to themselves. What they can do as a tiny society and how you can help that tiny society are also noble goals.
    You make excellent points and your arguments are fantastic, if "healing" the genius were my objective which is not.
    I don't know why people read my comments that way, because it's not my intention.
    For me, the ultimate "victory" for my genius is to act as a bridge between the world of pure raw inspiration and the mundane world. To be the light that inspires humanity to ever greater achievements. To give people the courage to try and make the impossible possible, because even if they fail they will obtain valuable experience and knowledge.
    When the genius tries to fix all the worlds ploblems by himself he's falling in the trap of pride. No person can fix the world alone, we're all in this toguether. Forcing the world to work according to your vision only makes things worst, because your vision is not perfect. It's as flawed as the rest of us, even if it's more wide and ambituous.
    The process that I described is for the genius to learn humility, compassion, and that all their fantastic inventions and creations are worthless if they cannot share them with humanity. Because at the end of the day knowldege is meant to help all of us to move forward. Going through the process of mundane research helps the genius understand the difficulties and efforts of real research, and with that appreciate the victories of mundane scientists.
    Making a mundane version of a wonder creates a watered down device that has only a tenth of the power and convenience of the original wonder, but it can be replicated and understood by the rest of the scientific community. Maybe you're not capable of offering everyone your awesome vita ray device that can heal all wounds, but you just created an electromagnetic scanner than can improve the diagnostics of certain illnesses.
    That discovery will help countless sick people, and will open new lines of research. You haven't saved the world, but you have given people the means to imporve the world, even if it's only a little bit. That in my book is a victory.
    The Genius commits a trangression when he dares to go into the world of unlimited possibilities that is inspiration, but he does it to bring back some of that fire to our dark world, because even if the fire dies and only a small spark remains, it will ignite a fire inside the hearts of men that will lighten our world. A genius risks his life, his sanity, and his happiness for this, and I say that is a transgression worth makin.

Unmada are Inspired whose mad certainty has broken the bounds of their mere thoughts and theories and reach out to bend their surroundings towards their mad vision. [Add Unmada mechanics, such as Mania loss, Unmada field, etc.]
                                                Possible Sources: Dramatically Failing a Transgression check. Resolving an Aspiration in a manner conductive with their [Vice]. Failing an Unmada check (such as after performing Deep Inspiration).
                                                Beat: Whenever your madness impairs your Aspirations.
                                                Resolution: Dramatically Failing a Transgression check (in which case the Genius becomes Illuminated). Being confronted with their failures and accepting they are at fault. Exceptional Success on a Transgression check.

                                                Temporary Sanity
                                                Sometimes even the mad get a breath of clarity, however fleeting. During these times, a Genius feels the manic drive of their Inspiration, if not disappear, at least fade. The Inspired find their thoughts more ordered, less clouded by Mania, and themselves able to engage in rational scientific research once more. [Add actual mechanical impact here] This Condition fades after one day per dot of Obligation. If it fades, it does not grant a Beat.
                                                Possible Sources: Exceptional Success on a Transgression check. Resolving an Aspiration in a manner conductive with their [Virtue].
                                                Resolution: Making a Transgression check (regardless of success or failure).

                                                Beholden (Persistent)
                                                There are those who lead, and those who follow - and you are firmly in column B. Your mind has brushed up against the light of Inspiration and had your own will burned by its source. Those who've become Beholden substitute their worldview (and one of their Aspirations) for those of the Genius they serve. Their Integrity is treated as the Genius's Obligation (mad Genii tend toward mad followers, while more restrained Inspired cultivate the same in their assistants). The Genius they serve is treated as having a Perfect Impression for Social interaction - even if circumstances would lower this impression, it returns to Perfect the next day. Beholden no longer provoke Havoc when interacting with Wonders from the Genius they serve, and have a +5 bonus to avoid causing Havoc with any other Wonder.

                                                Possible Sources: Being Beholden and losing their Genius. Having one's mind broken by Epikrato.
                                                Beat: Whenever you would gain Willpower, except through sleeping.
                                                Resolution: Find a Genius to serve (replace this Condition with Beholden, tied to that Genius). Complete a Long Term Aspiration (in which case, they resolve this Condition and regain their previous Integrity).
