    I have, at one point, totally wanted to hash out Geist, Beast, and Demon into a Lovecraft-inspired post apocalypse setting that is probably more "Shin Megami Tensei meets MASK" than I was looking to do at the outset - players are "ronin," young adults who live in "the Zone," a sort of apocalyptic landscape created by breaking down the barriers between the real world and the Dreamlands, and they've all got cyberware that lets them use little cards that burn out instead of their own sanity when using magic stolen from the elder beings who wander the landscape under the employ of a mad scientist turned megalomaniac. The general theme was to let horror come from the sorts of things you'd allow yourself to do and think when you're just trying to survive. Also, that to even use the technology reserved for interacting with the monsters, you have to face your own inadequacies and shortcomings and come out on top. Not sure how I'd enforce that with the rules, though.

                Giant: the Unhallowed
                There were giants, once, but now they're all gone -- their corpses decomposed into the Firmament that composed them, which became part of the hills, graveyards, corpses and everything else a la the way everything in the universe is made up of everything else. "Giants' graves," where the giant actually died, are saturated with Firmament, so that the land remembers when it was a living creature; occasionally, when a mortal resonates enough with the giant buried in a grave, the Firmament in them remembers when it was a giant and grants them dominion and authority. This process isn't quite a transformation of a mortal into a giant so much as a reincarnation of the giant -- it's not quite Jolene, any more; it's the giant buried in the grave, incarnated with Jolene's personality and memories, although her connection to these is slowly ablated away as the endless history of the giant replaces her own brief mayfly life.

                A giant is capable of reminding the Firmament in everything that it was once part of them, awakening stones, mortals and laws to act as their limbs. It's not enough, though, to be able to remember the past -- when a giant inhales Firmament, they build up their fragile, temporal, mortal body into a colossus, a smaller manifestation of their predecessors and their ancient heritage. The problem with this, of course, is that all the giants are dead. A giant who resembles a mortal gets some allowances from Time, but when they assume their mighty antediluvian Stature, reality begins to ablate it away, removing them from human perceptions first before slowly fossilizing them and turning them to dust. By saturating an environment with their Firmament, a giant can claim it, making it their own

                X-Axis: Primogeniture -- there are many lineages of giants, but based on the circumstances of their graves and the values of their children, many can be divided thus:
                Asuras, giants who were buried amongst and embody death and war; they command fealty from the deceased and slaughter and consume that which would endanger their houses.
                Fomorians, giants who were buried in and embody liminal places; prophets, storytellers and historians, they know that time is cyclical and thus remember what they will see.
                Jotunn, giants who were buried in and embody chaos and the wild; with rain for blood and ice in their eyes, they can survive outside the borders of the civilized like none other.
                Nephilim, giants who were buried in and embody sites of creation; as the children of angels, the Nephilim build up religions, monuments and towers as extensions of themselves.
                Uranians, giants who were buried in and embody temples and palaces of mastery; there is a divine order which each recognizes, and they train themselves, and everything, to obey.

                Y-Axis: Legacy -- while most giants have a hand in temporal power structures, certain schools of thought regarding breaking the chain of succession have persisted.
                Gold: Hierophants of the Golden Legacy venerate and seek out their original selves, in the hopes that some means of escaping the vicious cycle exists. Many form multi-generational religions that worship the Colossi, giants who have persisted long enough that they can no longer exist inside the flow of time, as living records of the past.
                Silver: Sages of the Silver Legacy seek to amass knowledge and pass it on to their successors, considering the memory loss between deaths to be the primary problem with gigantism; by preserving their knowledge and transplanting their own memories into artifacts of Firmament, they seek immortality through their descendants.
                Bronze: Fisher-Kings of the Bronze Legacy seek peace through conquest. By establishing little kingdoms of their own, in accumulated power and land that can be attended and gardened, they seek to satisfy the constant dominance drive infused in them by their long-dead ancestors -- and, maybe, create worlds where Time can't hunt them down.
                Iron: Demiurges of the Iron Legacy transform themselves. Immortality is nice, but having your personality slowly eclipsed by a long-dead terror and reincarnating through human bodies makes gigantism far worse than is acceptable; the Demiurges forge Firmament to take different forms, ones that can survive a world trying constantly to kill them.

                Theme: Sins of the Fathers. A giant is an inheritor of a legacy dating back to the beginning of the universe; the continuity of a giant's psyche over multiple descendants means that long-lived rivals may eliminate you for something you did in a past incarnation, as a person you can no longer recall being. Breaking from the chain of succession is death. Think of it as inheriting an estate from your grandfather -- and finding evidence of deep debt to the Russian mob in the basement. You could become incompetent and die young, or you could assume the mantle of power and responsibility and desperation... and someday wind up the dead old man who wills his estate and power to a fresh-faced stranger.

                Power: Testament, the degree to which the giant's "will" is enacted through her descendant.

                Fuel: Firmament -- measuring the amount of "free" Firmament the giant has inside, to be shaped into the half-literal, half-metaphysical body of a titan.

                Integrity: Vitality -- a giant's psychology demands that they take control, rather than surrender to outside forces; those who fail to act begin to stagnate, eventually decaying or fossilizing unless they take steps to reclaim control.

                Powers: Remembrances, ways the giant forces her Firmament to remember when it was part of someone or something else. A mortal whose Firmament is awakened obeys the giant as a father; stone may remember being sand, or metal slag and ore, and the dead, who are all memories, may be changed and restored.
                Claims, places and objects saturated with Firmament and made the property of a giant; the land and the king are one, and so a giant exercises divine right over all her Claims, mastering them and knowing them as herself.
                Stature, the last-ditch weapon; a giant who swallows Firmament in mass quantities, allowing it to redesign her body, gains the strength and magical mettle of her predecessors, but exposes herself to the ravages of Time in doing so.

                        Originally posted by Chuckles View Post
                        Cool concept, Keter. Do you have an antagonist in mind? Or do you intend the physical conflict to just be between the Giants themselves?
                        Most conflict, I figured, would be between giants -- there's a general understanding in their society that the next person to inherit a giant's spirit is that giant, and thus there are a hell of a lot of long-standing grudges that Butterfly Effected out over time. Also, two giants can't Claim the same place at the same time, which should provoke squabbles and politics. The Colossi are especially guilty of this -- with long memories and deep pockets, they can make a giant's life a living hell, and they actually need Claims to exist, as they're incapable of staying in the real world without fossilizing and burning away.

                        As for external threats, I've considered the "Theoi," or "New Gods" -- the deities of Mt. Olympus, Asgard and various other pantheons, the ones worshipped by humans, those deities who brought the flood and slew the giants. They lack Firmament in their bodies, divorced entirely from the raw earth of giants, and either claim or are claimed to be the ones who originally slaughtered the giants in the Titanomachy; if a giant is someone who inherited a mansion hiding an ancient mystery from her deceased uncle, the Theoi are the hitmen who come to the door, knock her out and burn the mansion down with the mystery unsolved, because "it is better that you never find out."

                                            Part of it is that, as giants become stronger -- both personally and in terms of political power -- their original personalities become closer and closer to those of their primogenitors, who do believe, in general, that dead grudges matter. The basic concept is an old boys' club -- giant society is controlled by your elders and parents; by the time someone on the bottom has reached the top, they've become the exact same kind of person.

                                            (Of course, there's also general, all-purpose political infighting, and a concept I'm working on, a giant's "Bequests" -- political organizations, buildings and important possessions marked by a giant's former incarnations. A giant who awakens doesn't necessarily awaken alone -- many will have Merits representing their Bequests, sources of support, aides, retainers and servitors created by their past incarnations. A "Crown" can be set up to supply certain supernatural benefits to mortals, as long as they faithfully serve the will of the giant who created it -- many use Crowns to create sources of wealth and power for their descendants, and, at the same time, act as strong influences, with apparent authority and wisdom, on confused new giants. Often, giants whose ancestors collaborated are given Bequests that draw them together. Ultimately, I see giant society as being very much about lines of inheritance and influence -- you can trust Smith because his father and your father worked together, although of course you can't trust him *too* much because his father and your father were in a tenuous business alliance and those tend to fall through.)

                                                            So, this is what came up in a different thread, but I’ve heavily added and brainstormed the topic on this board since then. Monarch is a game where you play an alien bug monster queen human hybrid in charge of your own little "Hive".


                                                            Monarchs as they are called are a fusion of alien and human life that exist to inessence act as the intermediaries of a vast, but fragmented Hive Mind. Most Monarchs were originally humans exposed to a virus that infected them and twisted their bodies. Most lose their will and completely degenerate, but a lucky few evolve all to better the greater whole that is the Swarm. Their free will is left to them entirely at this point and they are thrust into a position of potentially great power. However, choosing to go against the Hive as a whole practically means death. If you would not serve, you are easily replaced.
                                                            The Swarm, collectively can be best described as an interdimensional race that take resemble a mix of bugs, reptiles, and dinosaurs, mixed with odd traits such as resembling shadows and metal. Most are barely the more than animals, but in a way they don’t need to be too smart as they are controlled by the Monarchs, the Agents of the Hive Mind’s fractured will.
                                                            While it is known that the Swarm possesses its own world or perhaps even worlds, Earth’s environment seems to actively reject the Swarm’s presence, in effect killing them. Monarchs, being meldings of native and alien, are able to survive on Earth unaided. Although, one wonders why go to the trouble of going through Earth in the first place since logically, there should be other, less hazardous worlds to visit. The Monarchs certainly don’t know.

                                                            Key Powers
                                                            Monarchs are able to shift from human form into a form more fitting to the Swarm’s needs. When the Monarch assumes her “Lord” form, she is able to use her access to studies to the best of her abilities at the cost of horrific transformation into an inhuman creature. Otherwise, Monarchs are perfectly indistinguishable from normal humans… and just about as capable. Without assuming a Swarm form, they lack access to the majority of their powers.
                                                            As a further limitation, a Monarch’s powers require that she invests her Authority to keep them active. While Authority is more of a pool rather than a “fuel” trait, this limitation often prevents the Monarch from using herself or her swarm to their fullest. Authority must be divided up evenly between the Monarch and her servants.
                                                            All Monarchs possess a small degree of telepathy. Specifically, the ability to send messages at Will, but for the most part this has a few weaknesses. First, outside of her own Broods, a Monarch’s innate telepathy is rather short range and by default only other Monarchs and telepaths can interpret it. On top of that it is also “unsecured” allowing any telepath within range to catch wind of it, in particular other monarchs. Thankfully, “in-Brood” telepathy has an effective range of 1 Mile, allowing the Monarch to command her Brood from range.
                                                            All Monarchs possess the ability to create a Hive Cluster or Domain. Essentially, it is a pocket dimension where the the Monarch may create her forces on Earth. While Earth’s environment is hazardous to the Swarm, a Hive Cluster is “xenoformed” enough to allow the Swarm’s creatures to exist unaided. Though the Hive Cluster is a pocket dimension, it does have one weakness and liability: Hive Clusters require a physical gateway within real space to access it, such as either a pit or doorway.
                                                            Monarchs may Create Broods within their domains, essentially, part cult, part… swarm.
                                                            One unique feature of Monarchs is their ability to create special “retainers” for themselves either by “birthing” specialized minions or by converting an existing humans to the fold. In either case, the Monarch imbues a small portion of her own powers onto the new “Heralds”.
                                                            However, as Earth’s environment literally sears away any Swarm creature caught within it, a Monarch’s agents cannot act within the normal or even the magical worlds connected to the world… at least, not without the Monarch’s investment. By investing a portion of her own will directly into her Swarm, the Monarch grants limited protection from the environment to her agents, preventing them from perish just by breathing. However, this protection is limited. So long as the Monarch can keep channeling, her agents are protected, once her Authority is removed, her agents rapidly turn into vapor as even their remains break down. This limitation is what keeps the Swarm from growing out of control, as even will full Authority invested, the swarm will never be large enough to wipe out a city or even a town all by itself.
                                                            Themes:
                                                            You are in charge: Monarchs all rule over their own little Hives, effectively making the Kings and Queens of their personal domains. They however have to handle the political issues that come with that such as controlling their Swarm.




                                                            Castes: Representation of the intended original purpose of the Hivemind behind a Monarch’s “rebirth”.
                                                            Warrior- Born to fight and kill, either for offense or defense of the Hive. Focus on combat, warfare, conquest, though not necessarily overt.
                                                            Tender- Created to maintain the conditions and health of the hive. Primary directive is support, recovery, resource management. Can be rather brutal if defense is needed.
                                                            Scribe- Made for thinking, exploration, crate work. Strength comes from knowledge and obtaining it is necessary. Experimentation and exploration are the primary focuses.
                                                            Envoy- Exists to coerce and coordinate other Monarchs together, and on Earth, to allow for diplomacy. Also acts as the primary interpreters of the Hive Mind’s Will.

                                                            Ideology. In essence, as rulers, Monarchs determine what policies and practices their Swarm works towards. While the Hive Mind is the overriding will of the Swarm, it doesn’t have any real strategy beyond survival of the race as a whole. It responds to threats and violence, and also incompetence by its own rulers, but to it, everything else is fair game. Monarchs determine the finer points and ideologies define the difference.
                                                            Current ideas for Ideologies.
                                                            Prointergration - Further adaptation to the world.
                                                            Migration - Find a different place more suited to the swarm.
                                                            Terraformers - Change the world, not the Swarm.



                                                            Power Stat- Evolution
                                                            The measure of how advanced and mutated the original host has become integrated with Swarm material. It functions like other power stats.

                                                            “Mana” trait, Authority: The total measure of how much “power” a Monarch can use all at once. It does not deplete per say, when using powers or investing in her Swarm. Simply put, activating and maintaining powers costs Authority. Once the powers expire or are canceled, Authority slowly climbs back up to your maximum at the rate provided by your evolution.
                                                            Morality- Cohesion: The measure of not just your own personal control and well being, but also that of a Monarch’s Brood. As Broods are a reflection of their owner’s will, it stands to reason that poor self control leads to poor Brood control.
                                                            At low levels of Cohesion, the Brood has too much initiative and automatically goes out of its way to act according to its Monarch’s Caste… even when such actions would be unnecessary or problematic. At 0, the Monarch’s failure at controlling her Hive becomes so apparent, the Hive Mind abandons and casts away the offending Monarch and potentially its brood. At this point, the Monarch is barely more than a monster.

                                                            Studies: Studies refer to the individual topics and distinctions that make up a Monarch’s access to powers. These range from topics like corrosive acids, bone scythes, hardened carapace, but also more unusual topics like telekinesis or upgraded telepathy.
                                                            Studies function in many respects function similarly to Disciplines in the manner of which they progress. Unlike Disciples, at each increase of a given Subject above 1, a Monarch is given a choice on how to change or add to the Subject. Once the choice is made, it cannot be changed easily, requiring some action be done.


                                                            A Hive Cluster is for the most part treated as a group entity similar to a Mummy Cult in terms of operation and use. However, unlike a cult, a Hive Cluster can easily provide disposable warriors and workers on behalf of their Sovereign Monarch called a Swarm. The bigger and more powerful the Swarm called, the more Authority is drained from the use when warriors are sent into the toxic Earth’s atmosphere. Once the Authority of the Monarch is withdrawn, the Swarm quickly dies off.

                                                            Vassals, are somewhat more unique and more powerful. While the Swarm is mostly made up of smaller creatures, Vassals are subagents underneath the Monarch and designed to suit a purpose numbers alone do not fulfill.
                                                            Each Vassal is built via the use of “templates” owned by a Monarch. Like the Swarm the Monarch may summon, Vassals require the Monarch spend Authority to allow them to persist outside the Hive Cluster, though it’s usually much easier to maintain than a whole mess of smaller creatures.
                                                            Each template comes with a preselected statistics array and special abilities as well as template specific upgrades called Mutations that can augment or change a template at the cost of more Authority.Still, powerful Vassals often cost more power just to keep functioning.
                                                            Monarchs all start with a unique template that comes from their Caste when they begin play.

                                                            Notes:

                                                            I’ve gone through a few revisions in structure and how to handle powers, but I think I got a solid overarching system to mess with.
                                                            Now I just need to provide more data as well as define player options. I’m kinda stumped on how I want to handle the Social splat and I need to think up a good list of themes to play with for Moraility and Cohesion mechanics.
                                                            While I have considered a more freeform approach to building minions, over time , I have decided that a more limited system might better simply because the freeform approach would have taken so much resources to build and would have been way more complicated. So, yeah, a simpler system I feel kinda makes more sense.


                                                            Mechs: Because even the Chronicles of Darkness needs robot fights.
                                                            DarkFrame: Crossover setting that puts Chronicles of Darkness in the far future that is Warframe.
                                                            Monarch: The Endless You are an alien ruler, charged with maintaining a people who you shape to suit your needs.
