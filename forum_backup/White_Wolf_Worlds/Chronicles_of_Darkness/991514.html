    Dragon Age as a Fantasy Shard

    10-22-2016, 04:46 PM
    So, I have long thought that the Dragon Age setting resembles ChroD strongly in a lot of ways. The Tevinter Magisters invading the Golden City and letting the Blight into the world as a result of their hubris, for example, is a direct parallel to the Exarchs entering the Supernal and creating the Abyss. The relatiomship between the material world, the Veil, and the Fade is incredibly similar to flesh world, the Gauntlet, and the Shadow in ChroD. The way Solas talks in Inquisition about how magic used to be different, spirits were just another part of the natural world calls to mind the pre-Sundering ChroD world and Pangaea. The distinction between Mages who are subtly tempted by spirits/demons, those who become possessed, and full-on abominations is exactly like the progression from Urged to Possessed to Claimed. So much of the setting reminds me so strongly of ChroD metaphysics that I can't help but want to fit the two together.

    At the same time, there are parts that just don't quite fit. ChroD has myriad otherworlds, where DA lumps aspects of all of them together under the umbrella of the Fade - it's the place lucid dreamers and Somnoari go in their sleep, it's world of spirits on the other side of a sort of barrier, it's the former home of the Gods that the Magisters crossed into, etc. The Blight is basically a zombie plague as opposed to an insidious anti-reality that magic invites into the world. Lyrium seems to be like a naturally occurring mineral that behaves like Tass, and then there'a the red stuff which is a whole other can of worms. Non-Mages aren't harmed by witnessing Magic and can even combat it via special abilities that are for some reason not considered magic and are fueled by Lyrium.?And, of course, there's the tiny detail of Thedas being a tolkienesque medieval European fantasy setting complete with elves, dwarves, etc. instead of the real world plus monsters. Among other things I'm probably forgetting.

    Still, these are two settings I adore that share a lot of common ground, particularly in terms of metaphysics, and I can't escape the urge to want to mash them together. Not too long ago in another thread, I mentioned wanting to some day brew up a fantasy Shard possibly set during the Time Before. I can't help but wonder if it would end up looking an awful lot like Dragon Age - certainly it would be one of my influences. So, I'd be interested in hearing what other folks think. Have you ever considered mashing up ChroD and DA? What would you change about each setting to accommodate the other? Or am I trying to force square pegs into round holes?

          More of a parallel universe kind of thing, since Thedas can't really be the ancient past of Earth for obvious reasons. Mostly I wanted to do sort of a thought experiment to see what it would take to run a Dragon Age game using ChroD rules and ChroD metaphysics. How could you fudge each setting to make it believable that they're part of the same game universe.

          I'm thinking I could say that in the time DA is set in, understanding of the Realms Invisible is still developing. Ephemeral entities are all referred to as spirits or demons by laymen, and all but the most learned of Mages (like Solas and Flemmeth, for example) assume that they all come from the same place, the Fade. But the reality is that it's much more complicated than that, and the Astral, the Shadow, the Underworld, etc. are all different places, and the Golden City and the Seat of the Maker are not really in any of them, but are part of the Supernal. Alternatively, maybe it's all pre-Fall of Atlantis, and the Realms Invisible actually are all the same place, separating them was one of the knock-on effects of the fall. These are the sorts of theories I was hoping to discuss.
