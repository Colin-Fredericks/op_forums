    The Usage of High Rank Angels

    04-07-2014, 09:27 PM
    Hi all,

    So recently I read the final PDF version of Demon (my previous read through had been the preview text on the Kickstarter page) and got a POD version of the God Machine Chronicles book with my Kickstarter backer discount.

    I noticed a lot of sample angels in both books are between rank 1-3. There are examples of Rank 4+ Angels obviously, but they are kinda rare. I can list three off the top of my head:

    Y2K from Demon's core (Rank 4)
    Black Nathaniel from the GMC book (Rank 5)
    Triage from GMC book (Rank 6)

    Now, I know the issue of the implementation of Angels in a Demon game is a complex issue anyway.

    Namely that each ST has to decide where on the "There are always more Angels" vs "Its a big deal for the God Machine to make/summon and Angel" scale they rest. As both of these are themes within the game.

    Likewise, I know there is some issue with the fact that Demons are actually far more versatile then Angels as written mainly due to Angels being modeled using the PFR system for Spirits. Some people I have seen argue that a Demon with a good junk of XP is more then able to handle any angel below Rank 4. I'm not so sure about that myself, as while Angles might lack the power spread of Demons (at least for now, I imagine every splat book for Demon will include more Angelic Numina) the raw dice pools that a Rank 4 or 5 Demon can be rolling can just overpower most things (30+ dice will do that).

    Also added into this that if the Demon gains the right condition the its up the ST what servant of the God-Machine shows up to hunt them. One of the options is of course a single Rank 4 or 5 Angel.

    So I was kinda wondering if people have brought out Rank 4 or 5 Angels a lot in their Demon games? As I have yet to run or play in a game I have no practical experience with this aspect, but I find it interesting to see how often other ST's might bring out a serious opponent as the result of Cover issues.

    Also I am curious and interested to hear about what new Rank 4 and 5 Angels people have come up with for their games. Since these are the Angels most likely to have more fleshed out characters it seem, or at least thats implied by some of the fluff in the Demon book.

    "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny

              Umm..... I don't know where these people are getting their ideas from, but... a Rank 5 angel is generally scary. It has enough stats to absorb the damage from an attack that deals 10 damage in a single turn. And the essence to not only heal it all, but respond with an attack that could kill a demon without defenses in a single attack as well. Remember, Rank 5 angels have an average of 12~15 in each stat. That means that they can have up to 25 health at the high end, before any powers that boost health.


              And remember that they can get the same Embeds and Exploits as Numina that a Demon can. Its quite common in any game - ghosts can get Manifestation powers as numina, astrals/supernals getting mage rotes, spirits getting werewolf gifts. So, you pump out your Demon with 30+ dice to attack an angel (assuming its manifested in some meaningful way the demon can actually hurt the thing!) and it uses Just Bruised. 1 damage. How about take that huge amount of damage, then touch a demon with Shift Consequences. All that damage, gone, and into the demon's body? Ouch.

              And, like I said, that's assuming that the Demon can strike the angel in question. Angels aren't dumb - they're not going to materialize in a way the demon can easily "kill them." Note that ephemerals are a lot harder to permanently kill, as they need to be robbed of essence as well to kill. Angels, also tend to want to hunt the demons, and shred their Covers. Or, heaven forbid, an ambush. Any scenario where a rank 5 being is "easily killed" is one where people are setting up white room, D&D esk encounters and expecting the "monsters" to be idiots and just stand there, or use dumb tactics, before their power builds.

              The simple truth is that whomever told you that angels can't match up to a demon is simply full of hot air. Angels can do everything a demon can, plus access to more Numina and Influences that a Demon cannot. They have better stats, they have bodies that regenerate upon destruction. They've access to the backup systems of the God Machine, who can simply download new powers, information, aid, you name it.




              Also I am curious and interested to hear about what new Rank 4 and 5 Angels people have come up with for their games. Since these are the Angels most likely to have more fleshed out characters it seem, or at least that's implied by some of the fluff in the Demon book.
              I've got a rank 5 angel that's watching over a major city that's a giant Infrastructure being built towards a huge occult matrix. It's got a lot of underlings too.

              The idea here is that, since the God-Machine itself is basically a supernatural internet, built upon thousands and thousands of servers and nodes and connections, it can never really have a face. So, Mr Rank 5 Angel is actually a stand in for the leader of the angel army of the local city instead of the big GM. He's the face, the director, of all this. Also, the Linchpin.

                  This makes sense - but keep in mind that doing this changes the setting and theme of your setting. In this scenario, the god machine becomes an understandable entity rather than a malevolent, alien force. Part of the fun of making an adventure against the god machine is plotting random events that don't really make sense and never being able to get the big picture, but this changes it so that yes, one entity can and does understand what's going on.

                  An idea I had for a setting is to take a city (Toronto) and throw in several high ranking angels with competing agendas - all trying to compete for resources on a large scale for different projects. These angels have only a vague idea that they're actually fighting each other in a weird, city wide A-B test from hell. There would of course be other entities in the city (Vampires, maybe some Geists) but the adventure will ultimately revolve around the demons figuring out that even if they bring down one infrastructure, two other projects greedily gobble up the resources and become more powerful. Ultimately, they can't face off against the big bads head on (they're too powerful) but they could find ways to play the angels against each other and use the GM against itself. The GM itself would still play a part, enacting smaller operations in the midst of this huge, long term infrastructure that is the city.

                                      I'd just like to be clear that I think the system for making Angels is fine for allowing ST's to make Angels which are challenging and threatening to PC Demons. I'm a fan of the system.

                                      I think between the innate ephemeral traits, the inclusion of Embeds (as influences or numina) and some creative Numina (either brand new or based of other powers) Angels are plenty dangerous enough as it is.

                                      My personal preference for High Rank Angels (4 or 5) is to give them a lot of Numina that have the same effect as other powers (Exploits/Demonic Form abilities) but achieve them in slightly different ways. To go back the comparison between Incendiary (Exploit) and Blast (Numina) both end up with something getting blown to bits/set on fire (if that's blasts trapping). They have slightly different systems to get them to that effect, and I like that because it makes them feel different. I acknowledge this as a purely personal preference on my part.

                                      I think having that sort of wallup with the sheer number of dice they can roll for basic attacks and their innate ephemeral toughness is enough to compensate for say powerful Demonic Form abilities.

                                      I'm kinda focused on this issue a bit because Angels are so important for a Demon game I feel. They need to be a real danger/threat without appearing to just be ST fiat (another form of "rocks fall everyone dies"). The moment the players get the impression that they can "take" Angels on a regular basis I think Demon looses the majority of its thematic point.

                                      Interlinked to this actually is an thought I had about Bans and Banes for Angels. Considering the God-Machine rejigs its Angels between missions, do you think it would be fair to have a recurring Angel antagonist have his bane and ban altered between confrontations (if the PC's used those things to defeat him previously)? As the God-Machine "adapts" its servant to negate the tools of its enemy. The Angel must always have a ban and bane (due to occult physics) but the God-Machine might play around with it.

                                              I'm going to be contrary and disagree here - I would not allow it. Why? Changing a spirits ban and bane is essentially changing its fundamental nature, and it implies that the angel is adapting at an extreme level - which thematically isn't supposed to be something an angel can do without falling. When the machine creates an Angel, it creates it with a purpose in mind - the ban and bane should represent that fundamental purpose. A slight alteration might be allowed (such as saying although your ban is you can't leave your worksite - which worksite can be changed) but changing it much more than that should be tantamount to building a different angel from scratch.

                                              An angel is fundamentally a tool as far as the G-M is concerned - if it's proven ineffectual at its job, it has a choice to make: send another tool to help and change the circumstances, or get a new tool. For instance, if the hunter angels ban is discovered by the demons and they learn to exploit it to become relatively safe, the G-M might have no choice but to send in a replacement that attempts entirely new tactics. If the angel built for infrastructure construction is thwarted due to demon intervention, the G-M might decide that it's still a useful tool for what it was built for (building things) and just set it to a new project, perhaps with a guardian this time.

                                                          Originally posted by Oddness View Post
                                                          This makes sense - but keep in mind that doing this changes the setting and theme of your setting. In this scenario, the god machine becomes an understandable entity rather than a malevolent, alien force. Part of the fun of making an adventure against the god machine is plotting random events that don't really make sense and never being able to get the big picture, but this changes it so that yes, one entity can and does understand what's going on.
                                                          One, the angel is only a stand in; it puts a face to it, which Angels are meant to do. The God Machine is like the Internet. Its everywhere and no where. Its like... think St Michael the Archangel for the chronicle. The head of God's Army.

                                                          Two, the God Machine isn't malevolent. Its a cold and uncaring machine that doesn't understand the question of morality. It can and does act to save humanity at times. Malevolent implies outright evil, of which it bluntly is not. It might not be a caring God, but its not an evil God either.

                                                          Three, the random events that don't really make sense are designed to be there as part of the "occult matrix" (ie ritual spells), which are complex beyond normal understanding. And you don't understand it since its supposed to be the ultimate Chess Master gambit stuff. That doesn't mean you can't make allegories and metaphors to make parts of more understandable.



                                                          None of this is changing the core setting or themes of the game. Its playing within it.
