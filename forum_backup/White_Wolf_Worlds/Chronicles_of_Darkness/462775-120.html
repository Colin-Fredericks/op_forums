    Originally posted by Cainite View Post
    Any tips on how to make fights in Werewolf exiting and challenging to the players? I have 3 players regularly and am having a tough time keeping them on their toes. The only thing that seems to work is having more enemies than there are players, but I find it hard to keep track of combat when there are 3-4 good guys against 4-5 bad guys. Especially with tracking initiatives with different weapons and the werewolves changing forms from turn to turn. Do you maybe have a good system of how to keep track of health and initiative?
    Just the way action economy works, multiple antagonists are probably one of the best ways to deal with it. I don't have any particularly good system but I've never had trouble just writing down their Health and Initiative orders.

    As an alternative, you can have fewer antagonists but load them up with Numina or Dread Powers and extra Health to make them last longer. Or play more 'guile' like and have them be harder to actually get at, as werewolves are not as good at range. That could involve flying spirits, or something like a Mountain spirit who can create obstacles at a whim.

    A big part of making things more exciting or challenging is knowing what your players are capable of. Checking out their sheets you can see that, if you have an Ithaeur with the first auspice Gift you probably won't be able to throw a spirit at them and leave its bane a mystery, as the first Gift can let you know what a spirits bane is. Look at how much damage your players are likely to do in one round, individually and as a group, so you can see what you should do for Health. See how good their combat pools and Defense are. And if you want to keep things interesting, don't fall into the 'tank' game, it's not an MMO and spirits shouldn't be treated like monsters with Threat, uselessly flailing at the toughest character while others act to lower its Defense and plink away its Health.

    The Hurt Locker would probably help. One of the aspects of the first chapter is about making dangerous areas, like fighting on top of a speeding semi-truck trailer. Scenery is really important if you want to make things interesting and exciting. The Batman the Animated Series creator's bible specifically points out that, at the climax of each episode, the confrontation with antagonists should appear in an interesting fashion. 'Each episode will also feature a big set piece, an incredible visual action visual that will be a looked-forward-to element in each show. This will be the climax, centerpiece or show-stopper of each episode.' Things like fights on the top of skyscrapers or massive clocktowers, or hunt-and-kills in a House of Mirrors or dark sewer. Not only does this allow you to use the environment (the spirit winds almost blow you off the top of the cathedral, though you manage to keep your ground, but your packmate is clinging feebly to a gargoyle at the lip of the roof, do you help them or continue fighting the Bird of Pray?), but allows a spirits aspects come into play in force, even in Claimed, providing you've tailored the situation to the spirit's advantage. A spirit of water might have Influences that never come into play in the parking lot next to the dock, but why would it fight there, instead have the fight occur on a boat in storm-tossed waters. This doesn't mean spirits can't be lured out of their comfort zone but there's no reason to play them like lambs to the slaughter.
