    Forming the cabal

    07-20-2017, 07:30 AM
    I've just finished my first read through of the core and I'm stoked to run this as a ST.
    I'd like to start off the Chronicle with the character's debutante into Mage society,
    with them finishing their apprenticeship and being introduced to the Consilium at large.
    At this point, the characters probably won't know each other as they were studying under different Mentors.
    As a result, I'm having trouble coming up with an organic way of having the characters work together in the first story and, later on, form a Cabal.
    I would love some suggestions

      Introduce mystery in their future territory, then reasons to move there or nearby, or have a mystery that pertains to them. An easy way would be having the mystery be an empty sanctum, ready to move into. But no one has any memory of any other mages living there, and there's no historical record of it either.

      Note that, politically, having a cabal will afford them the respect being a lone mage wouldn't. This isn't something you have to lecture them about, but show by in-game example.

      Also, once in play, Mentors of the merit-kind are only meant to help PCs out in exchange for the PCs doing things for them. An easy route is 'Help me by finding other mages to help you.' But a more subtle form of, 'You need to find friends your own age to play with,' could probably help too. You could just have the Mentors being more interested in their own thing, which can easily be made exceedingly boring or otherwise something the PCs won't be interested in or be able to help with. 'I'm analyzing the number of black haired children born at night, I'm sure there's some connection, but I need more data!'

      Putting at least some of it in the hands of the players can't hurt. If they're told to make connections between their characters they might take a lot of work off of you.

              As an example: Local Spirits begin contacting Mortals and teaching them secrets of alchemy in return for favors. These select Mortals start using this knowledge for their own benefit, also causing a stir in the occult underground.

              1. Character number one is Obsessed with alchemy, and is attracted to this Mystery like a bee to honey. However, snatching second-hand tidbits from unAwakened occultists is not enough. A proper Mystagogue, the character wants to reach the source, but knows nothing of the Spirit Realm. He reaches to a colleague...
              2. Character number two, who has facility with the Spirit Arcanum. Perhaps the characters strike some sort of deal, or perhaps access to the Mystery is reward enough. After all, Spirits don't normally know alchemy, so the entire affair is really fascinating. Sadly, the two academics are too eager and often get carried away in their studies...
              3. They need a bodyguard - character number three. Through personal connections or their Mentors' assistance they get an Adamantine Arrow as a member. They now make a functional team. However, there is more to the situation. There is a stir among Hedge occultists and the remaining Orders want to stay abreast of developments.
              4. Guardians of the Veil, Silver Ladder and Free council all want in on the action. Either to screen the alchemists for ethical issues, or to absorb them into their networks. The core trio of characters doesn't care for politics, so they don't mind others taking over the alchemist issue. A wheeling-and-dealing Thearch successfully pleads their case offering to unite the alchemists under the leadership of both Silver Ladder and Mysterium, thus becoming the fourth character.
              5. Not only that, they suggest taking in a Guardian, on the basis that since they'll snoop anyway, it's better to have them where they're visible. The fiendish plan is to saddle the Guardian with alchemist oversight, so they're too tired to bother the Cabal, while the Ladder deals with a lessened workload.

              This leaves the Free Council, who wanted a more equal partnership with the occultists, left behind and fuming. All the while the Ladder, Mysterium and Guardians attain 100% smugness. There will be consequences down the line.

                  Depending on how authoritarian you want to portray the Diamond, it's quite possible for mentors to simply assign their apprentices to a cabal. There's a mild resemblence to an arranged marriage in the way Sanctum & Sigil describes it; mentors making strategic alliances via their students. But even beyond that, the Diamond has a heavy social expectation that a mage will be part of a cabal and be actively looking for the opportunity to join or create one.

                  It's also worth remembering that a cabal is kinda like a small corporation, it might be nice to be friends, but ultimately members are liable for each other. They're as much there to watch each other as they are to support each other.

                      How do you build teams in any other social setting?
                      ​For a less authoritarian Pentacle you can have the group of newbies be assigned a starter task that has them working together (something annoying that no one with more pull wants to do).
                      ​You can even just have them introduced to the larger Concillium in a group, all standing together before the Heirarch with everyone else watching. Perhaps they are all seated at the same table (you know, the little one for children).
                      ​Drive home that their mentors are all members of other Cabals and show some of the advantages they reap from it, political, social and academic. Given a hint most players will jump on the bandwagon.
                      ​If you really want to go the route of high coincidence have them realize that they all live in the same apartment building, attend the same school or just tend to take in the sun in the same park, something they share in common that never meant anything before but now brings them into frequent proximity of each other.
                      A last idea, one I used for my first Mage chronicle, hit them with a common threat and make it clear they have a better chance of facing it together.

                      ​To help encourage them to group up, throw in another newbie a real prick who doesn't want to team up, at least not with them. Nothing brings people together like a common jerk.

                                  Originally posted by Darkemor View Post
                                  Hmm...I was under the impression mages, unlike vampire and werewolves, don't think in terms of territories, but rather in terms of specific magical holdings (Sanctums, Hallows, Verges...).
                                  You should check out the Sanctum and Sigil book, it really shows the bad side of all that stuff. While mages don't really have a territory that they patrol, like werewolves, I suppose it would be more like vampires with certain feeding grounds. Mages tend to have certain things that are 'theirs' with the Sanctum being their equivalent of a vampire Haven, other things being equivalent to Feeding Grounds or Wyrm Nests, basically places of interest, these all together and potentially some more areas like their homes or a proximi's place create what I meant by their territory.

                                  And as an official cabal, they'll have these rights they can claim. The first one is the Right of Crossing that allows other mages to cross through their territory unmolested, providing they use no offensive spells and pass through relatively quickly. It's up to the cabal to mark their territory, usually with signs one can see with mage sight but potentially other ways, like graffiti in the form of High Speech, usually offering a quick 'path' through the cabal's territory. The book mentions some variations of the Right, like Arrow Cabals tend to not allow traveling mages to have a weapon openly, or the Free Council doesn't want their property destroyed and will force you to do restitution if you do so.

                                  It's a good book, very informative about building cabals, though it does have the early first edition problem of thinking most cabals are all one Order.

                                      Hey Darkemor I felt the same way starting our chronicle. Our group took a cue from the Marvel cinematic universe, and had the PCs meet, compete, fight, and work out their differences until a greater threat caused them to band together. As organic goes, they finally formed a cabal at the end of the second story arc. (No easy task given 3 Guardians, a Mystagogue, Libertine, and a Nameless) It didn't feel rushed, and the length of time allowed character development and more authentic relationships between the mages.

                                      Some ideas:
                                      1) Draw them to a common Mystery
                                      Mages chase Mysteries, solving what they can, and hoarding what they can't. The PCs are sent in to assess the same magical phenomenon by rival masters. If there's a Guardian PC, she can be secretly tailing another PC hoping to steal the relic at the last moment. A lot of cat and mouse games.

                                      2) Direct them to a common threat
                                      Part of our group is an ongoing rivalry between the Libertine and one of the Guardians. Both were sent in by their orders to shut down a child pornography den - the Free Council, because it wanted to bring the business owner to justice, and the Guardians of the Veil, because they wanted to leverage the owner for some other shady deal.

                                      3) Consilium needs an ad hoc team
                                      Yes it's a trope, but it can work when all things fail. Their masters are unavailable (predisposed, injured, or vanished), and Consilium is desperate to form a team either temporarily or permanently. It could be to solve a murder case, stop an Abyssal intrusion, or to establish a cult.

                                      Let me know how your chronicle comes along. Good luck!
