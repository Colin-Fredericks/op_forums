                            06-06-2016, 11:23 PM
                            Originally posted by MugaSofer View Post
                            I think we should start with the themes and build up from there. What are the themes of Genius?

                            Struggling with mental illness, the possibility and potential in the world, feeling alienated from one's peers... that "technology" encompasses almost everything humans do, and abandoning that is terrible. Probably a couple I'm forgetting? We should isolate the key themes and try to tie everything back to that.
                            I think the central theme is "Grandiose Designs, Flawed Executions". I don't like to boil it down to just "Failure", though. As I've said before, while the characters might stumble upon obstacles and tragedies that make their Inspiration feel worthless, the players should not feel that way. There should be a focus on what can wrong AND what can go right.

                            We also have to delve deeper into the subtitle. Do you know any mad scientists that conform to the rules and expectations of their societies just fine? I certainly don't. Much more than just psychiatric labels, being a Genius is about not fitting in, for one reason or another. But isolation is not a sure sign of talent or insight. The Inspired are torn between the desire for acceptance or at least recognition, and the desire to say and create whatever they wish. Perhaps this can be incorporated into the new Obligation (or whatever we might choose to name it) in the following ways. Feedback is greatly appreciated, especially on how to streamline the good parts.

                            1) Geniuses MUST create. This is no mere compulsion, it is a psycho-spiritual necessity. Going for too long without exposure and interaction with Wondrous phenomena moves you upwards in the scale. Your capacity to build Wonders may be lessened somehow, but there may be benefits such as reduction of Jabir. Allowing your Inspiration to flow more freely may allow you to access more Postulates simultaneously (and perhaps give you temporary Postulates, to address the lack of spontaneity pointed out by Amechra), but Havoc and Jabir will become more dangerous for yourself and others.

                            2) Approaching the extremes of the Obligation scale does not necessarily mean going off the deep end and becoming unplayable. I believe Obligation should be like Satiety (i.e. it can very wildly in a single session, with each extreme being a valid play style).

                            3) As an alternative to the traditional 1 to 10 scale, we can innovate by not assigning a number to Obligation. Instead, Geniuses could start out with one of two Conditions, with one favoring carefulness and the other spontaneity (not sure on the precise bonuses/penalties). Geniuses would be capable of switching between the two Conditions more or less at will, with a cost of time and/or Mania being included to "switch gears". Each Genius would have a third Condition that is unique to them, which they get in a moment of equilibrium between passion and planning. This Condition would be acquired mostly through roleplaying, but there would be one dice roll involved (with a flexible dice pool). There should be a limit on how often you can try to get this Condition, with a few suggested modifiers. Results on the roll would look more or less like this:

                            Dramatic Failure: The Genius feels their Inspiration flare intensely with a supernova of ideas, but does not realize they are flawed or corrupted. Whatever Wondrous thing they want to create or Inspired conflict they wish to solve, the result will be a Dramatic Failure. They will receive an entire Wondrous Experience, but also a serious Persistent Condition such as Broken, Notoriety or Fugue. You may also consider Persistent Condition based on critical malfunction of a Wonder, such as Amnesia for Epikrato and Deformed for Metaptropi/Exelixi.

                            Failure: The Genius' ideas flash briefly but do not take flight. Apply a Condition of your choice to represent the circumstances behind this (e.g. Obsession if they can't let it go, Embarrassing Secret if they made a rookie mistake, Starved/Exhausted if they ignored their physical needs).

                            Success: The Genius gains the Informed Condition, which must be applied to a roll pertaining to Wondrous/Inspired phenomena (it need to be Wonder creation, it can also be a way to resolve conflicts between Foundations/Baramins).

                            Exceptional Success: Besides the benefit of Success, a full refill of Mania OR a number of the Inspired Condition equal to [Inspiration/2 round up], which can be applied to any roll.


                            Bye.

                                            Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
                                            amechra What do you think the main themes should be in 2ED? Is there too much overlap with Mage?
                                            [Caveat: not amechra]
                                            Well, both "investigate" and delight in "mysteries". I think the subtle distinction is that Geniuses pursue "real" (or to Mage, Fallen) mysteries on their own merits, or for their insight into the rest of the world (not just the Symbols behind them).

                                            I think Genii might also be a bit more pro-active: rather than looking for pre-existing Mysteries, Genii often seem more willing to conduct their own experiments (for SCIENCE!!!). There is overlap, of course, but I can see more Genius Chronicles being about events that the players originate, rather than events they encounter.

                                            I think some of the themes might be slightly different: in Mage, an external force (Exarchs, the Fallen World) are holding you back, while in Genius, I think more personal obstructions should be focused on (Havoc is incidental and avoidable, but it's your personal failings should doom your grand vision).

                                            Maybe some Themes could also be Dangerous Knowledge (be it in it acquisition, or what the knowledge reveals itself) and Uncontrollable Change (in the manner of once some technology or insight is let out of the bottle, it can't be stopped)?

                                            EDIT: Actually, I think a theme of You are the cause of your own misfortune might be a strong one for Genius overall. Both personal causes, and other Genii at large, are the reason for much of the conflict, especially if the X Chronicle is/remains focused upon the Illuminated.

                                            EDIT SECUNDUS: But this isn't to be confused with "Failure". Yes, you (and other Inspired) are the reason for your life's difficulties, but that doesn't mean you can't achieve something wonderful as well.
