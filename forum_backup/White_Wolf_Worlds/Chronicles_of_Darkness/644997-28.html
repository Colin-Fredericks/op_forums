                                    It is entirely possible to believe something exists, even observe signs of its existence, without ever witnessing the thing itself. Hell it's been outright stated that there are Sleepers who know Mages exist, they just cannot observe or recall Supernal magic (much in the same way I know Blackholes exist, even though they cannot actually be seen).

                                    Take the hunter scenario I put forward several pages back: You've got a group of hunters who wake up with foggy memories. They recall overhearing a report of suspicious activity at the local cemetery, where they went and met a man (whom they remember fairly well) doing...something. The details of the scene are foggy. However, what they do know is that the following day two of them were injured with what looked like human claw and bite marks, seventeen of the most recent graves are now empty, and the bodies that were in those graves cropped up three blocks away looking to all eyes as though they'd walked there on their own.

                                    Now I don't think it's too big a leap for those hunters to think, "That man we met yesterday is some scary necromancer." Though they might also tag on, "And he fucked with our memories!" They might also leap to conclusions like, "He must be using some form of blood magic, like those vampires we stamped out last year" or "He's clearly possessed!" Because off the mark judgement about the supernatural are a common theme of Hunter. But it's fair to say that they can absolutely reason out that he's a risk, even if they cannot recall the exact act of animating the dead.

                                                    FWIW, this post is intended solely to clarify where I've been coming from.
                                                    Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw View Post
                                                    I actually think a game of Sleepers of the edge of mage society, knowing that they can't trust what they remember happened half the time and slowly being driven mad by their exposure to supernal magic, would be pretty neat. Of undoubtedly limited appeal, though, which is why I say to make hunters Sleepwalkers unless you're interested in that kind of game. What you can't do with Mage as written is have Sleeper characters who act like Sleepwalkers but increase Paradox risk. If you don't want to make hunters Sleepwalkers and you don't want that weird Nolan-esque feeling to the game, you shouldn't use the Awakened but knock up your own Witches instead.

                                                    But, of course, the material on playing a Sleeper in Mage isn't necessarily aligned to the hostility of a hunter. Do you, as an Awakened character, try to tell your boyfriend what you're experiencing? If someone's badly injured, should you risk their Integrity to heal them? Many mages try to use their powers for whatever they think is Sleepers' own good, but Sleepers are incapable of giving informed consent - how far do you go in adjusting your friends, relatives, rivals' lives?
                                                    Someone posted a "Intentionally Obtuse" Merit, or something to that effect. When they did, I commented that it was representative of the sort of "doing an end run around the Curse" stuff that I've been advocating (in that it allows a Sleeper to avoid taking a Breaking Point for witnessing Supernal Magic), but that it wasn't really suitable for hunters. I'm assuming it is an official Merit, as it was said to have come from a leak (though I haven't personally seen said leak). As well, the way it was described gives the impression that, while it gives what could be characterized as a supernatural benefit, the Merit itself isn't supernatural, instead relying exclusively on the Sleeper's psychology.

                                                    As mentioned before, and in keeping with Dave's post above, this isn't really suitable for a hunter's more antagonistic relationship with a mage; but it does demonstrate how a Sleeper, without supernatural assistance, could have some means of coping with the Curse, making long-term interactions with mages and their magic viable without an Integrity death-spiral. And that's really all that I was looking for.

                                                    That, and I don't mind Nolan-esqe hunter games. I'm not interested in Sleeper hunters "who behave like Sleepwalkers except that they trigger Paradox"; the closest I've suggesting such a thing has involved trading off some of the traditional problems of being a Sleeper for other problems. This preserves the notion that the Curse sucks no matter who you are, just in different ways. While this isn't strictly in accordance with Dave's intentions, I think it's mostly compatible with them.

                                                    Note that I haven't addressed the power differential between mages and hunters. Once coping mechanisms for the Curse are in play so that a hunter cell can deal as effectively with mages as with, say, changelings or werewolves, you still have to deal with the versatility and power that a mage commands. That's where hunter determination and ingenuity are really put to the test.

                                                    As for witches: I'm hoping that if Hunter 2e actually has witches in it (as opposed to relying on WoD2e to supply approximations of the various major gamelines), witches will be flexible enough in their definition to approximate Second Sight thaumaturges to Awakened mages (complete with power, versatility, and even the Sleeping Curse) and everything in between.
