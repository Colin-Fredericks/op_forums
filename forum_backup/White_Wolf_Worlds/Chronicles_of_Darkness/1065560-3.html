                        Basically, the Deathless more or less broke Egypt towards the end of the First Sothic Turn (it's implied that a couple hundred to a couple thousand gods tossing around earth-shaking magic with impunity in service to their own agendas more or less gutted Kemet and caused the First Intermediate Period) at which point the Guildmasters got together and collectively decided to ensure that this many Deathless were never packed into such a small geographic area ever again, and established laws governing what the Deathless could, or could not, do to each other or to mortal populations in the future. The Prince of Glass, signature character for the Sesha-Hebsu, was one of the architects of this plan and the Diasporic Code that is written in Guildhalls of the Deathless.

                        The Diasporic Code is as follows
                        • The Heart Law: "Interfere with the cults of other Deathless if you would fertilize the sands with the blood of mortals under the gods' silent gaze."
                        • The Spirit Law: "When desires clash, submit to arbitration or suffer judgement."
                        • The Essence Law: "Do not break the seal of a holy tomb unless you wish to offer its treasures sevenfold."
                        • The Name Law: "Make no alliance with the Lifeless or usurper's of Irem's lore, or you will worship at the altar of 42 Torments."
                        • The Shadow Law: "Grasp the sunlit flail of rule and you shall be condemned to the dark sarcophagus."
                        Firstly, mess with your fellow Deathless and it will cause unnecessary mortal death and the irritating process of rebuilding a cult. If you do run into a situation where your interests and those of another Deathless One come into conflict, take it up with the Guilds or (preferably) go to the Scribes because that's their job anyways. Don't desecrate the dead, jerk. Besides, tombs are the closest thing to a home the Deathless have. If you betray the Guilds and the memory of Irem, especially to Sorcerers or servants of Ammut, then you're going to die. Painfully. Several times, probably. Most humans never experienced (or survived) each of the 42 Torments individually. Lastly, don't rule openly as a god-king. This makes things more complicated for your fellow Deathless, but more importantly it proves the existence of magic. If you learned how to wield it within your original mortal lifetime, so can others.

                                    So, the Amirani are pretty cool. I'm not familiar with the story alluded to in their history, so I can't tell who their original Demiurge is. But basically, they're made from the bodies of people who died violent or otherwise traumatic deaths, and re-made with metal. They retain the memory of the last thoughts of their donors, and this is seems to be the source of their prophetic ability - having seen the tiniest glimpse of whatever awaits a human soul after death. They only have one Bestowment, and it allows them to burn themselves to trigger prophetic visions, mechanically reflected by an extra bonus for spending Willpower as long as the aggravated damage from the fire remains. Their torment is related to being able to see how things could be better, but being unable to make that vision a reality. Knowing the potential for good in humans but seeing them choose to act on the bad in themselves instead, or especially being able to see their own potential and being unable to live up to it, is what triggers it. While in torment, their bodies show the signs of how their donors died.
