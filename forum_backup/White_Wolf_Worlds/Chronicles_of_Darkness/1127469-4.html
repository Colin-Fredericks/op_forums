                Throwing together some Sterotypes and Hunters for some of the unfinished factions.

                The Bookhounds of London

                • Ashwood Abbey - Lots of money and intensely curious about the occult but far too lazy to actually put effort into learning about it. The perfect clients.
                • Ride to Damascus - There is no doubt their books contain arcane power but they reads like MLK edited Chicken Soup for the Soul. There's no market for this.
                • Aegis Kai Doru - We could probably make a tidy profit selling to them if they would stop trying to stab every dealer they come across.
                • MI18 - Operator Manuals and blueprints for bio-mechanical brain parasites powered by the victim's day dreams are sort of magical. In an Arthur C. Clarke sort of way.

                The Fire Starters
                • You lost everything in the '09 crash. Your house, your car, your marriage. You aren't sure about this murder business yet but you're desperate and that wish sounds good.
                • You've been burning buildings down for years before one spoke to you. You think you might be going crazy but the fire's orders have been fun so you keep going. You're almost ready for your wish but you aren't really sure that you want the fun to stop.
                • Your brother was killed in a mugging years ago. Last week he, or something that looked like him paid you a visit. Later that night as you watched your home burn the flames thanked you for killing him and said there were more lying creatures like that thing wearing your brother's face out there.
                • The Long Night - After I burned that church preacher and all these biblethumpers took to the streets en masse looking for "the witch" responsible. I'm scared of what they'd do to me if I told them about their late-pastor's secret stash.
                • Habibti Ma - Rumor is that a bunch of these so called do gooders got an entire cell of us institutionalized in Egypt. The Hospital went up in flames before anyone could get any answers from them.
                • Lucifuge - Say "L'Enfant Diaboliques" one more time, I dare you.
                • The Hunt Club - Everyone likes to lump us in with these sick freaks. Sometimes when the fire asks me to kill I wonder if they're right to.

                                Originally posted by Flyboy254 View Post
                                So for Boogeyman, should we go for "Incorporeal" or "Functionally Invisible".?

                                I believe the answer would be "yes".

                                To elaborate, the transition from Ripper to Scourge seems to be a loss of something that keeps the Ripper grounded in humanity while empowering them with a power that makes them more supernatural in nature. Geniuses that become too enraptured in their own thoughts and ideologies transcends to a Maniac, Avengers that hunt down their quarry too efficiently become the horror stories that are Legends. For Chameleons, it seems that their core is that they are unnoticed; they are the "nobody", the blurriest face among blurry faces. However, they are still a "person", so to speak; they have a presence that is noticed in the physical realm like a home under a pseudonym or a membership among fishing enthusiasts. Despite them being beneath notice, people can still prove that the Chameleon is a person.

                                With Bogeymen (especially considering their Talent and Frailty), they go completely off the grid. They aren't A nobody, they are nobody; they leave no footprint or paper trail, they have no home to their name or a notable persona. They just aren't other than a Bogeyman, like a Legend but less embroidered in their story. If anything, I would compare them to something like an infamous name on the internet that searches for their screen name in search engines to figure out who is talking about them; they thrive in their lack of identity but can be warded off by forcibly bringing them back into reality by speaking their "true name" (playing into the Still Attached Frailty).

                                I can't name an example that follows the Bogeyman precisely. If anything, Daniella from Haunting Grounds has a tick that functions like the Still Attached Frailty. Daniella is a maid that chases down the protagonist because she (Daniella) sees herself as incomplete, wanting to harvest a part from the protagonist. Effectively, she is unstoppable; however, if she is led to a mirror or other reflective surface, she halts and shrieks in fury, effectively immobilizing her for a considerable amount of time.

                                                        Splinter (Ripper)


                                                        Sorry, I couldn’t stop myself

                                                        Splinters don’t want to kill. At least a part of them doesn’t. The same can’t be said for the other part. The other part kills like it is absolutely nothing, while the peaceful side is left to deal with the body. Splinters are usually very normal people. In fact they’re nicer than normal. Then there’s a part of them that becomes disgusted by the niceness, disgusted by the fact that people are just and kind in an unkind and unjust world. When this part of them becomes angry enough, it simply takes over.

                                                        Splinters have a short time to plan before the good side realizes what they have done, so they must plan quickly. They have to improvise. They don’t design traps, but instead look for places where the traps are already made for them. Perhaps there is a prostitute on an empty street, or a man that lives alone in an extremely flammable house. They don’t just club people to death; they look at their surroundings first. They know that no one would suspect their other side, so they get the bodies into the bathtub and buy a ton of lye, leaving their bewildered “good side” to do the rest.

                                                        Splinters usually do what they do because they suppressed a darker side of themselves. Maybe they were abused as a child, or maybe the anger management that turned their lives around somehow backfired. Maybe they were just too nice and needed to know how it felt to be something else, but suppressed the memory of all their kills because someone like them simply wouldn’t do something like that. It’s also probable that it was just some neurological problem. A hunter that became a Splinter probably started out facing something so horrifying that they began to refuse to believe in the supernatural, but there is still a part of them that can’t get enough of the killing.

                                                        Splinters would seem more redeemable than other slashers if they weren’t so insane. The good side is definitely a potential ally, seeing as he needs all the help he can get fighting the bad side. But sometimes his own psyche can be too big of an obstacle for him to handle no matter how much help he has.
                                                        Splinters always have high wits because they need to be able to think and act quickly and they almost always have high composure because killing comes so naturally to the bad side and the good side still needs to maintain some of his sanity. They rely on any attributes their good side has to kill.

                                                        Attribute: Wits

                                                        Undertaking Skills: Weaponry, Investigation, Science, Crafts, Stealth, Subterfuge, Persuasion, Athletics

                                                        Talent: Zen-like: The dark side of a Splinter is usually either irrational or evil, so either way the threat of death doesn’t faze him. His calmness makes him very accurate and gives him the ability to think on his feet better than most. He takes his time with every shot and is able to make use of what he has Splinters are always calm when murdering and think nothing of it, so they are given a +2 bonus to targeted attacks and take no penalties to mental rolls.

                                                        Frailty: Time Bomb: The dark side of the Splinter can’t come out whenever he wants. Something needs to trigger it and after it is triggered, it can only last for the rest of the scene. It could be triggered by the sight of blood or simply having someone visit his house. When this happens, the Splinter must indulge in their vice before anything else.

                                                        VASCU Case Study: “Two O’clock” Gunther
                                                        Transcript of interview between Folsom Prison guards and inmate Lito “M-16” Rodriguez

                                                        LR: CO, I’m telling you, you can’t let that fucker out!

                                                        Guard: Why Lito? We already know what you’ve done, how much worse can he make it?

                                                        LR: It’s not me I’m worried about, it’s the people outside, jefe. He’ll kill them if you don’t put him back inside.

                                                        Guard: You mean Gunther? He’s been released on good behavior, he’s served his sentence.

                                                        LR: It’s not him I’m worried about, it’s Two-O’clock.

                                                        Guard: Who?

                                                        LR: I don’t know, jefe, it’s like there a switch in him that flips around two in the morning! He’s a completely different person, like there’s a second person inside.

                                                        Guard: You mean dissociative identity disorder?

                                                        LR: Whatever, that fuck, those fucks, however many there are, they’re out now. Two-O’clock told me, he’s gonna start killing kids when he’s done.

                                                        Guard: That’s enough, get him back to his cell.

                                                        Note: Disturbingly, disappearances of minors has indeed shot up in the area where Mr. Gunther has moved. We may need to alert the Feds on this information, but we’ll wait until we get more information before we make a move.

                                                        Split (Scourge)

                                                        Kill me? That’s not happening. You need me

                                                        While a Splinter has a light side and a dark side, they’re still the same person even if they never want to acknowledge it. They share a name and they essentially look the same, but with Lunatics, the two parts become so different that they are nothing like the same man. The posture, the voice, and the facial expressions, usually coupled with an affinity for disguise make the two sides unrecognizable. At this point, they no longer think the same and the struggle between the light and dark sides becomes an all-out war.

                                                        Splits often think more about the long term than Splinters, but they still realize the importance of improvisation. Splits will often try to differentiate themselves from their good side to the point where they would go to places that their good side wouldn’t even consider going to. Maybe even places the good side despises and swore he would never set foot in again. They also enjoy seeing the fear in their victims’ eyes a lot more then Splinters, who are usually a lot more detached. Splits also realize the importance of concealed weapons and try to hide these weapons in all of the good side’s things. Maybe they’ll add a bit of weight to their walking cane or put a couple razor blades in their pockets. Splits have gotten a little cocky because they know that whenever anything gets rough, they can change into something which is nothing like them.

                                                        Splinters cross the line into becoming Splits when the two sides become completely irreconcilable. Splinters are still basically themselves when they give into the bad side, just a more murderous version. Both sides want to differentiate from the other and as the dark side become more consumed by his vice, he gets cocky. There’s no such thing as a low profile anymore, no hiding in broken down motels or towers or trying to look ordinary. Lunatics can also be created by supernatural means, such as being affected by mind spells or consuming some strange elixir.

                                                        Splinters struggle with themselves, but each side at least tries to consider the other side. The dark side believes that they are in this together and will make it easier for the good side to continue living an ordinary life, or at least try to put him in a position that isn’t so bad. The good side will believe that his other side is curable and someday the two sides may be reconcilable. Splits just stop caring or sometimes take pride in sabotaging the other’s life. If the good side can’t bring himself to turn the other side in, he will constantly leave evidence and assist in the case. The dark side will gleefully sabotage the good side’s relationships and terrorize his friends. This war keeps escalating, but no one seems to win in the end.

                                                        Attribute: Wits

                                                        Undertaking Skills: Weaponry, Investigation, Science, Crafts, Stealth, Subterfuge, Persuasion, Athletics, Intimidate, Socialize

                                                        Talent: Two-Faced: Splits have two distinct personalities and appearances, which are completely irreconcilable. They do not think the same way or have any of the same habits. Their abilities, virtues, and vices are the same, but they manifest in different ways. Any attempt to track, investigate, or see through a Lunatic’s disguise are penalized by 3.

                                                        Frailty: Self-Loathing- Each half of a Split despises the other half with a great intensity. Every time the Split seriously harms or helps someone out, he makes two resolve plus composure roles. If the first role exceeds the second, the other side of the Split gains control and can use it to sabotage his alter ego’s life.

                                                        VASCU Case Study: Michael Franklin/Victor Devaroux
                                                        Taken from the journal of Mr. Franklin

                                                        “I can’t take this anymore. Victor, that bastard, he nearly sent me to prison last night! He gave me control just as the young woman bled out over me. I tried to save her, staunch the bleeding, but he’d cut too deep. Damn him, that’s the third.

                                                        “I’ve thought about ways to kill him without him knowing, but he always takes control just at the right time. When the threat is immediate, he pops out and laughs, I can still hear it from the last time. He just dropped the coffee on the floor, and Mary just mopped it up with a smile. I have made sure to write her into my will, but Victor, he’s tried to have it changed by impersonating me. Thank the Lord that my lawyer is not as simple as Victor believes everyone else to be.

                                                        “But I think I have the solution now. Victor knows about killing, but not about saving. It sickens me to say this, but perhaps I can have him kill someone with HIV. It must be done, immediately, before he can stop himself.

                                                        “Jesus forgive me for this sin, but there is little other way. I have thought of time bombs or fires, but he would know then too. He’d leap out a window, or throw the bomb into the street. Mary would be endangered, and I will not have my wife made a victim by my own evils.

                                                        “Wait. That’s it. Something he won’t suspect. I’m the world’s safest driver. He’ll never see a car accident happen. A sudden swerve into oncoming traffic, a semi…

                                                        “I will feel sorry for the driver, but it needs to be done. He’ll be finished at last, and if I’m lucky he’ll die and himself alone. Forgive me Mary for what I must do.”

                                                        Stranger

                                                        “I… I need this. I need to be… complete.”

                                                        In his own mind, the Stranger is defined less by what he is, and more by what he is not. For starters, he is not complete. When he looks in the mirror, he does not recognize the person staring back. His skin feels like a mask, his body a cage, confining the true self within. He is a beauty, a beast, a monster, trapped in weak, fallible flesh.

                                                        The Stranger seeks to become whole. He seeks the transformative process, that which will bring forth his true self. He begins a quest for the formula, finding glances of the true self on the faces of others. He stakes his territory with a knife and a gun, seeking revelation in the fear and blood of his victims, trying to find that which will make him complete.

                                                        A young man hires prostitutes, takes them to darkened hotel rooms, then slits their throats with a straight razor. As the blood pulses into his open mouth, he chokes on the bitter taste of copper and longs for the day when he becomes one of the undead, when the blood shall taste as sweet as pure ambrosia. A woman, seeking the strength of a man, kidnaps female-to-male transsexuals and performs autopsies on them, trying to find the form of the rebis, the alchemical marriage of the two genders. A brutish young orphan reads of the nephilim, the abandoned children of treacherous angels, and defiles church altars with the bodies of their priests in an unholy black mass, hoping that the Grigori will see his deeds and assume him into the fold.

                                                        Background: The Stranger’s desire for completion is rooted first and foremost in the denial of his own self. He is not a person – at least, not as he is now. What he is is weak, and humble, and worthless. It is only through adopting the persona of another that he can come to terms with his own existence and feel like he is more than a shadow on the wall.

                                                        Usually, a Stranger reaches such depths of self-loathing through a period of great trauma. Perhaps he loses his entire family in a freak accident, and blames himself for some perceived weakness that prevented him from coming to their aid. Maybe his childhood was characterized by abuse from a guardian, wherein he was attacked for being a “freak.” He views himself as a mockery of existence, and wants to find out why other people can just be who they are so easily. The quest to get under another person’s skin may involve him slicing it off and wearing it as a mask.

                                                        A Stranger may begin their career as a hunter out of a desire to get closer to his chosen targets. His research into lycanthropy myths may prove invaluable to a cell that spends their time tracking down werewolves who feed on human flesh… at least, until his comrades find the bodies, missing pieces of skin that he’s taken to cure into a wolfskin belt of his own. Similarly, a Stranger may be a hunter who has experienced real loss at the hands of monsters. When a partner in his cell is torn to pieces at the claws of a raging vampire, he may wish to claim the heart of the creature’s power for revenge. But the more he finds himself trying to match the monster, the more he comes to empathize its point of view.

                                                        A Stranger is a versatile being, willing to do anything to accomplish his goal. Their Attributes and Skills depend as much upon their quarry as themselves. A man who hunts young women who idle into the forest after dark in an attempt to become a werewolf will favor Physical Attributes and Skills such as Survival and Animal Ken, while a pretty young thing who desires to become the lamia and drive men to ruin will focus on Social Attributes and Skills such as Expression and Persuasion. All Strangers have a strong head for Skills such as Academics, Medicine, Occult, or Science – they wish to understand their other self, inside and out.

                                                        Inspirations: Martin (Martin), Francis Dolarhyde (Red Dragon), Hedy Carlson (Single White Female), Buffalo Bill (The Silence of the Lambs)

                                                        Attributes: Intelligence, Resolve, Dexterity or Stamina

                                                        Undertaking Skills: Academics, Animal Ken, Investigation, Larceny, Medicine, Streetwise, Survival, Weaponry

                                                        Talent – Dreams of the Other: The Stranger seeks with all his will to become the persona he holds in his heart. His heart sings as he approaches the quarry, and when the blade finds home, he exults in the revelation that comes with it. When he pursues a target directly related to his goal of apotheosis, he has the option to risk Willpower on the roll, just like a hunter (see Hunter: the Vigil). Furthermore, if he is successful in his hunt and manages to “learn” from his target, he gains a +2 to all rolls to risk Willpower for the next scene. The glory of the revelation fades soon after, however, and he must begin his hunt for perfection again.

                                                        Frailty – No Heart But Another’s: At his very core, the Stranger seeks to become something else because he has no true sense of identity. His real name is an alias, his face is a mask, and his words are always lies. When the Stranger makes an Expression, Persuasion, or Socialize roll, he makes it at a -3 penalty at all times. If he is pursuing a quarry, he can spend Willpower to offset the penalty and even risk Willpower to ensure great success, but in all other cases, the Stranger’s true emptiness shines through.

                                                        VASCU Case Study: The Tithe-Keeper

                                                        From the files of VASCU forensic psychologist Sarah Kepler

                                                        When I finally had the chance to sit down for an interview with Jessica Casey, I found a woman very different from what I had expected. Her medical history had indicated a history of manic-depressive behavior, but that was nothing compared to the woman who walked like one of the condemned into the interview area. The scars of past suicide attempts marked her wrists -- one of the cuts still appeared fresh -- and her eyes... her eyes were always clouded. Like she was on the verge of tears.

                                                        "They won't come for me," was the first thing she said to me, and she didn't want to let it go. With enough poking, I began to get her to talk about her past. Her mother had always told her fairy tales before going to bed, but that had been before the accident. Afterwards, she ended up in the possession of her uncle Francis, who had subjected her to physical and emotional abuse for six years before child services had noticed. Every time I compelled a fact from her, she ended the sentence with, "They won't come for me."

                                                        The next session, I began by asking her who "they" were, and if they'd had anything to do with the children -- seven young boys and girls, all of whom had been in Casey's care, all of whose seemingly unmarred bodies were found in forests secreted away under trees and in rings of mushrooms. When I broached the topic, she began to break down crying, and said that "they" couldn't hear her here, due to the iron in the walls. She curled up on the floor and cried for her mother -- her true mother, Queen Mab of the Faeries -- until the guards came to take her back to the cell.

                                                        Jessica Casey escaped from her cell on November 1st, 2008, and her absence was not noted until cell inspections that night. Her true whereabouts are still unknown.

                                                        Transcendent

                                                        “Behold my glory, and despair.”

                                                        The only thing more dangerous than a man who kills for his ideals is one who has accomplished them and sees no reason to stop. Through the study of blood, flesh, and death, the Stranger becomes something more than human. They have unlocked the secrets of apotheosis, accomplished their dream, and freed themselves from the cage of meat and bone. They have become Transcendent, and nothing is beyond their limit.

                                                        Some green VASCU agents question why a Stranger would keep killing once he’s attained his own twisted sense of perfection. Those who deal in the cases of the Transcendent know that mere perfection is never enough. For the slasher, the body has gone from cage to altar. It is a wild fire, a mad god, an engine of destruction, something both beautiful and terrible to behold. The Transcendent demands adoration, and like an Old Testament God, is fully willing to strike down the blasphemous to secure the worthy.

                                                        The worst part of dealing with one of the Transcendent is that they serve as testament to an uncomfortable truth – they were right. What a forensic psychologist might dismiss as delusion or psychosis is proven to be dark alchemy, a precise formula that gave them all their heart’s desires. Hunters who face down Transcendents carry with them tales of a man who took a shotgun blast to his bare chest and kept charging, a woman who could burn a man’s skin with just a touch, and a delusional prophet whose very words were honey. There is something out there that is willing to reward slashers for their bitter deeds, and as long as there is murder in the world, it will always have a supplicant.

                                                        Background: Many serial killers have a superiority complex. They honestly believe that they are above the rest of humanity, that the average man means as much to them as the average cattle. The Transcendent not only follows this ethos, but adheres to it with a steely certainty; he knows for a fact that he is more than mortal.

                                                        While the Transcendent is most often the “evolved” state of a Stranger who has discovered the truth of his Undertaking, it’s possible for a slasher to instantly reach the state of accomplishment in a moment of dreadful revelation. Perhaps he finds a horrible secret of the universe written in a dusty tome, or discovers his bloodline is traced to an ancient clan of monsters. Those members of VASCU who know about the Lucifuge look upon the Children of the Seventh Generation with distrust; when you believe yourself to be a child of Hell, it’s only a matter of degrees until you’re doing the Devil’s will.

                                                        Like the Stranger, the character creation for a Transcendent is flexible and depends greatly on the focus of the rebirth. The man who desires to become a Faustian devil will focus his interests in a different direction than the woman who wishes to embody the wrath of the Furies. Another important factor in creating a Transcendent is that they are, at their very core, malleable. Their Talent lies in adopting the traits of their new self; they can be whatever they want, as long as it fits their ethos.

                                                        Inspirations: Serial Killer X (Condemned: Criminal Origins), Edgar Reese/Azazel (Fallen), The Metal Fetishist (Tetsuo the Iron Man), SID 6.7 (Virtuosity)

                                                        Attribute: Intelligence, Resolve, Dexterity or Stamina

                                                        Undertaking Skills: Academics, Animal Ken, Intimidation, Investigation, Larceny, Medicine, Occult, Streetwise, Survival, Weaponry

                                                        Talent – Malleable Self: The Transcendent appears mortal, but can call forth his true self at will. Once per scene, the Transcendent can spend a point of Willpower and roll Resolve + Composure. He cannot risk Willpower on this roll, or spend Willpower to increase the roll itself. Once the roll is made, the Transcendent can spend successes on changes to their form:

                                                        -+1 to Skill (1 success per dot)
                                                        -+1 to Attribute (3 successes per dot)
                                                        -Iron Stamina Merit(1 success per dot)
                                                        -Striking Looks Merit (1 success for 2 dots, 2 successes for 4 dots)
                                                        -Natural Weaponry (1 success per +1L; 1 extra success to make damage elemental)
                                                        -Natural Armor (2 successes per level)
                                                        -Healing damage (1 success per level of Bashing, 2 successes per level of Lethal, 4 successes per level of Aggravated)

                                                        These changes must relate to the form they have adopted; the Storyteller has final say on whether or not an adaptation is thematically related.

                                                        Frailty – Only Human: The Transcendent may believe he has grown beyond the bonds of humanity, but in many ways, he is still fettered to the corpse of his old self. His weakness lies in being reminded that he is still mortal, and his “true self” is mere decoration added to human clay. Like with his Talent, the Transcendent’s Frailty is entirely thematic. A white supremacist self-proclaimed ubermensch may find a black hunter has gotten the upper hand in a fist fight, whereas a woman who patterns herself after the Great Whore of Babylon and delights in driving holy men to ruination may find sympathy at the hands of a member of the Long Night.

                                                        When the Transcendent is confronted with evidence of his old self and human failings, the mask cracks instantly. All benefits derived from their Talent vanish automatically, leaving his with just his natural aptitudes. Furthermore, crushing doubt overtakes the Transcendent as he struggles with the knowledge that he isn’t as perfect as he believes; as a result, he takes a -2 penalty to all rolls for the remainder of the scene. Most Transcendents flee from such confrontations, usually isolating themselves until they’ve managed to suppress the crushing realization… and swear revenge on those who brought forth their flaws.

                                                        VASCU Case Study: Lilith

                                                        From the files of anthropologist Michael Ntuze

                                                        I first met Dr. Lillian Carver three years into my fellowship at the Smithsonian, when I was leading a program into the search for “Mitochondrial Eve” – the common ancestor of all humanity. She came to us with a laundry list of accolades: summa cum laude at Georgetown, graduate study at Oxford, a Pell Grant at 25… she certainly knew the territory. She had spent years on research expeditions in Africa, following the trail of anthropological studies. We did question the fact that her studies had taken her to Nigeria, Sudan, and Zimbabwe during periods of civil unrest and genocide; her only explanation was that she “studied with the best.”

                                                        From the start, there seemed something… odd about Dr. Carver. She always seemed withdrawn, and never joined us outside of work. I had known my share of eccentrics during my stay in academia, and merely attributed it to personal quirks. Then I heard stories of the men who turned up in motel rooms on I-495, subjected to extensive surgery while they were still alive. Around this time, I began to notice a change in Dr. Carver – she seemed more vivacious, more alluring, and even fervid at times.

                                                        When she failed to show up for work for three days, I took one of the slides from her worktable to the police on a whim – it had come from one of the men on I-495. They came to our lab and found a manifest on her computer, proclaiming herself to be “the outlier,” “the betrayed mother of humanity.” The letter was signed, “Lilith.”

                                                        Every few months, I receive a new slide from her. I never test it.

                                                        The Predator (Ripper)

                                                        While most slashers enjoy the hunt and stalking of their prey, for most it’s a means to an end. For the Predator the hunt is the thing. They live for the chase. The kill is almost a letdown, though they realize it’s an inevitable end to the ritual and even while they are taking their victim’s life they are looking forward to the next hunt. They often refer to their victims as “prey”, “animals”, or similar dehumanizing terms. They do this because they truly do not see those they kill as human, merely as outlets for their desires.

                                                        Many Predators share some traits with other undertakings. They are ruthless and violent like the Brute. They feel set aside and above the rest of humanity like the Genius. They even sometimes feel outcast and distant from others like the Freak (though they are rarely ugly or deformed). A few use flattery and seduction to draw their quarry near, much like a Charmer. However, in the end they are Predators, and their whole nature and reason for being revolves around this fact.

                                                        Predators more than any other slasher are likely to take trophies from their victims. For them these mementos allow them to relive the thrill of tracking their victim, tormenting them, and feeling the sense of power and joy as they end their life. Many Predators have signatures weapons and methods they use in their kills. Some even have special hunting grounds set aside.

                                                        Predators are often solitary, though they sometimes band together with other slashers to stage particularly elaborate hunts. Some Predators have even managed to join hunter cells and direct them towards their victims. In some cases these victims are truly guilty of some terrible offense, though Predators have been known to frame a target. A few rare Predators hunt hunters, supernatural creatures, or ever other slashers, considering them the most challenging prey of all.

                                                        Background:

                                                        Most Predators begin as hunters, law enforcement, or other professions who specialize in tracking, catching, or killing humans or animals. Others comes from other professions but are active sportsmen in their private lives. A few Predators have no such background, but find themselves driven to hunt and kill because of some deep-seated mental ailment.

                                                        At times a hunter will become a Predator. This happens when a hunter’s mission becomes more about the hunt and the challenge of tracking demons, monsters, and other enemies more than it does about stopping evil or helping people.

                                                        In all cases, the Predator spends time and effort to learn, refine, and perfect the hunt. They may stalk their first victims for days, or even weeks, before they escalate. They tend to refine their art over time, and often their first stalks and kills seem crude and primitive compared to the killer they will become.

                                                        Predators usually have a focus on Physical Attributes and Skills, though they often have high ratings in Wits, Resolve, or Composure to reflect the controlled and superior air so many cultivate. Many have Merits which either help them in attracting prey or dispatching them.

                                                        Inspirations: Thomas Burns (Surviving the Game), General Zaraoff (The Most Dangerous Game), Kraven the Hunter (Spider-Man), Aaron Hallum (The Hunted)

                                                        Attributes: Dexterity, Resolve, or Wits

                                                        Undertaking Skills: Animal Ken, Survival, Firearms, Weaponry, Stealth, Crafts, Investigation, Subterfuge

                                                        Talent- I Have Your Scent
                                                        A Predator fixates on his target. He learns their abilities, habits, and weaknesses. Once a Predator has picked his target he gains a +1 die bonus to all rolls involving the target, including tracking and killing them. This bonus lasts until the victim is killed or the Predator abandons the hunt. If he does so he must wait at least 24 hours before being able to apply the bonus to new prey. If he kills his prey he suffers no such limitation.

                                                        Frailty- Thrill of the Hunt
                                                        A Predator cannot simply kill his victim. He must stalk them, wear them down, and then eventually finish them off only when he has demonstrated his superiority. Every victim must be given a “fair chance” to survive. How actually fair this is varies greatly, but at the very least a victim must be given a chance to detect, fight, or otherwise escape before the Predator feels he can truly satisfy his need for sport. The ability of the prey is often a factor here: an ex-marine sniper might be brought down after he fails to spot the Predator when he reveals himself for a mere instant while a suburban housewife might be handed a knife and given a five minute head start.

                                                        VASCU Case Study: Orion
                                                        “Born Edgar Henry Wilson, the killer known as Orion was a ex-Army Ranger who was dishonorably discharged after an incident during the first Gulf War. The file’s sealed and we’re trying to get clearance but you know how that goes. Afterwards it seems Wilson spent some time in Africa as a merc, He even worked as a safari guide before he dropped off the radar a few years back. He recently resurfaced in connection with the Alaskan Bush Murders. Over a period of eighteen months Wilson captured a number of fish and wildlife guides, mostly local Inuits. He then released his victims in remote areas, apparently giving them a knife and a few days rations. Without fail, Wilson hunted and killed all of his victims. His preferred weapon of choice seems to be a hunting crossbow, though many of his victims were struck mortal wounds with some type of long knife or sword.

                                                        Wilson takes his moniker from two factors. First, he is skilled hunter who fancies himself a modern day hero in the Greek sense. Second, and more chilling, he left each of his ten victims in a pattern which when placed on a map approximates the major stars in the constellation Orion.

                                                        Authorities discovered Wilson’s identity but were unable to apprehend. He vanished shortly thereafter and there are no reliable reports of him since. However, the FBI office in Seattle has recently been tracking a number of killings of local police officers. Though preliminary reports are inconclusive, it seems that the location of the first four killings correspond with the belt and sword tip of the Orion constellation. We recommend sending agents to assist local law enforcement and investigate further."

                                                        The Beast (Scourge)

                                                        A Predator who is completely consumed by the hunt is the Beast. He sees himself not only as the ultimate hunter but the ultimate creature. He is the top of the food chain, the king of all the animals, and the lord of the jungle all rolled into one terrible package. The Beast is in control even when he is being hunted and some even prefer to let their victims play the role of pursuer for a time.

                                                        Beasts are more primal and feral than their Predator counterparts. They can speak and interact with normal humans, but rarely do so except on the most primal levels. Many are cannibals, ingesting their victim’s power with their flesh. Others are creatures of only barely restrained lust and dark desires. Beasts sometimes employ animals in their hunts- abused or twisted companions who are as ruthless and terrible as their masters. They tend to kill more and with increased savagery. Some even take to marking and maiming their foes and allowing them to linger for a time. They don’t do this solely out of sadism, but as a testament to the power and superiority they hold over their victims.

                                                        Most Beasts dwell outside society as much as they can afford. Many are wanderers or hermits, living in cabins in the deep woods or always traveling from one small town to the next. Some do come to the city, seeing it as a jungle of stone and steel in which to stalk and kill.

                                                        Predators often become Beasts after a period of boredom or dissatisfaction with their previous Undertaking. Unlike many slashers, they want to be challenged almost to the point of failure and too much success causes them to seek a new methodology. At this time, the Predator comes to the realization that they cannot be satisfied as they are. As the supreme stalker, the perfect killer, there is nothing left to do but evolve. With this realization another Beast is born.

                                                        Despite their near-feral nature, some Beasts can blend in with normal humans- at least for a time. They might join hunting parties or camping excursions, playing the role of the friendly guide or even the clueless newbie. Eventually, away from civilization, they will drop this guise and begin to prey upon their companions. Some Beasts even delight in dragging out their ruse by isolating and killing victims and then calling the others to see what some “wild animal” or “crazy killer” has done.

                                                        Beasts often embrace primitive weaponry and fighting techniques. In part this is an extension of the ever-increasing challenge they sought as Predators. However, it is also part of the supernatural aspect of their new Undertaking. They are just as much savage animal as rational man- balancing cunning and primal ferocity with ingenuity and advanced reasoning.

                                                        Background:

                                                        Most Beasts begin as Predators. However, the occasional killer becomes the Beast immediately. These slashers usually have some special connection to the wilderness or animal kingdom. A feral child might become the Beast if they are driven to prey on humans for food or sport. A desperate hunter might embrace the Undertaking to justify the horrible things he did while trapped in the wild.

                                                        Even more so than Predators, Beasts favor Physical Traits and Skills. Though it is not a favored Attribute their Strength tends to be noticeably higher than their Predator brethren. Mental Attributes and Skills are often lacking though Beasts are rarely stupid. Social Attributes and Skills tend to be focused on Intimidation, Animal Ken, and Presence. Beasts who prefer to hide among their prey also often have some aptitude with Manipulation and Subterfuge. Most Beasts possess Atavism or the Fighting Style: Frenzied Assault Merits. Many possess both.

                                                        Inspirations: Steve (Shoot to Kill), Kraven the Hunter (Kraven’s Last Hunt, an excellent example of the progression from Predator to Beast), Jean-Francois de Morangias (Brotherhood of the Wolf/Le Pacts des Loups),

                                                        Attributes: Dexterity, Resolve, or Wits

                                                        Undertaking Skills: Animal Ken, Survival, Firearms, Weaponry, Stealth, Crafts, Investigation, Subterfuge

                                                        Talent- Top of the Food Chain

                                                        Those who encounter a Beast feel the full effect of their animalistic nature. The Beast adds +1 bonus die to all attempts to cow, intimidate, or frighten. In addition, the nature of the Beast seems to exert some primitive control over the instincts of its prey. When successfully intimidating a target, the Beast can select whether the basic response follows a “fight” or a “flight” pattern. They cannot exert fine control over their target, but they can choose the basic reaction. Thus a Beast could decide that a person will engage in a fight response, but they cannot control if that person throws a punch at them, tries to go for a weapon and attack them, or any other aggressive action. Beasts use this Trait to lure their prey or to drive them from places of safety. Some even use this Trait to make others seem like the aggressor or a coward to others, which is useful to remove obstacles on their hunts.

                                                        Three circumstances increase the bonuses from this Trait. First, this bonus increases to +2 dice with animals, as they can sense the threat of the Beast with greater ease. Second, this same bonus also applies to any prey chosen by the Predator Trait I Have Your Scent. Third, when the Beast spends a Willpower to augment die rolls using this Trait he gains a 9 again on the roll in addition to the normal Willpower bonus.

                                                        Fraility- Law of the Jungle

                                                        Beasts are prisoners of their instincts and their own self. They must act in accordance with their status as supreme Beast and lord of the wild or be driven to distraction. They must answer challenges, confront those who violate their territory or lairs, and otherwise behave in ways commonly associated with a territorial, aggressive, and dangerous animal. They need not be immediate or foolish in this behavior- a Beast could decide to stalk a cop who insulted and called them out on the news to a remote locale and confront them. However, they must act accordingly or take substantial steps towards doing so. If they seek to avoid this behavior they suffer a -3 penalty on all actions due to the distraction, anger, and shame of denying their natures. This penalty disappears when the Beast engages in the appropriate behavior he has been avoiding.

                                                        A side effect of this Frailty is noteworthy if rarely a factor. Anyone who defeats the Beast soundly in combat or otherwise proves his superiority gains a level of deference and respect. This causes the Beast to generally want to avoid this person and if forced to confront them they suffer the -3 dice penalty to combat rolls to attack this character (they still defend normally). This situation only comes into play when another character bests the Beast in a “fair” one on one confrontation where there is no doubt of the victor. Note this situation is exceedingly rare, since most Beast swill fight to the bitter end rather than admit they can be bested and few hunters or would be victims who gain the upper hand are inclined to spare the slasher if they get the better of him.

                                                        VASCU Case File: The Jaguar Man

                                                        “We still don’t have a real name on this perp yet. We’re calling him the Jaguar Man because of our one reliable sighting so far.

                                                        The eyewitness report had this guy running around in nothing but spotted pelts and some sort of tribal jewelry. Our experts have identified this garb as a variation of the Aztec warriors the ocelotl, or Jaguar Warriors. They think the killer identifies with these ancient soldiers and their culture, or at least some aspects of it such as blood sacrifice and ritual murder. Despite the connection to Aztecs in methodology, our experts suspect the killer isn’t of Mesoamerican descent. I’m a bit unclear on all the reasoning myself, but eyewitness reports do describe the killer as having light skin and hair.

                                                        The Jaguar Man primary murder weapons are what forensics thinks are some sort of blades made of obsidian. Wounds on the bodies of the victims seem to suggest at least two different implements were used. Whether these are some stolen museum relic or tools the killer made himself is unclear. The killer uses these weapons to wound his victim to the point of incapacitation. He extracts the heart of his victims while they are still alive. He then leaves the body where it fell and takes the heart. We don’t know what he does with the hearts he takes.

                                                        The Jaguar Man doesn’t seem to be tied down to one particular territory. He operates in an area for a time and then migrates to a new location. He mostly stays around the American Southwest and Northern Mexico, though we have reports of possible killings in Seattle, Chicago, and Philadelphia. To date, he is suspected in the killings of at least sixty victims in the United States alone. We’re still waiting on accurate data from the Mexican authorities but we suspect to at least double that number.

                                                            Chameleon

                                                            “I’ve just got one of those faces.”

                                                            Robin Dunbar proposed a concept that the human brain only has the cognitive ability to maintain roughly 150 stable relationships. While some have criticized it the theory on the basis that this ignores the many social connections necessary to build up human civilization at all, no one can deny that they only have so many many people they can remember and care enough about to interact with on a regular basis. Everyone else is a face in the crowd, shifting through the observer’s life with hardly a thought about the anonymous beings walking about around them.

                                                            What happens to the people who manage through no fault of their own to slip between the cracks of everyone else’s sphere? Who somehow go nearly their entire lives without close friends or romances? Some just go on with their lives, quietly living as they do and suffering the loneliness that aches at their hearts. Others feel enraged by this, angered that their lives are somehow unseen and unheard by those around them, and they are willing to do anything it takes to be noticed by those around them.

                                                            A Chameleon is aptly named, able to slip in and out of any location simply by blending into the background. Their targets may think they were nothing more than an accidental bump into a shoulder on the subway, a passing nod to a stranger on the street, a shopkeepers smile to a customer before he departs never to be seen again, but to the Chameleon these run ins are the closest they have to real human contact, and it's not enough. To a Chameleon murder is a shout into the world: "I exist! I will no longer be ignored!". Some Chamelions kill because they feel slighted in some way by their victims. They seek revenge against the waiter who overlooked them for hours, or anyone who resembles the pretty girl at school who never knew he existed. Other Chameleons target a circle of friends one by one until they can't help but spend every waking moment worrying about their mysterious asailent. Or they choose targets, weapons, and a modus operandi to splash a larger than life persona across the front pages, a persona nobody would ever connect with the quiet man collecting the trash.

                                                            In either case, the chameleon is a patient predator. They slowly move themselves into the lives of those they follow without ever being noticed. They track their targets, trailing them to and from work, about their lives, and inevitably either growing envious of the fact that other people care about them, or of the others who spend time with the person who shows them the attention they so sorely lack. Wheather a quick kill or an elabourate statement, a Chameleon's murder can be as invisible as themselves, with every angle scouted and checked to make sure it's free of witnesses and evidence.

                                                            What makes a Chameleon dangerous is that they easily blend in anywhere. They’re the “killer cop”, putting flashing lights on the roof of his car and buying a prop badge to lure out the unsuspecting motorists he kidnaps and kills. He’s a lowly janitor, fired months ago but still coming in to work because no one cares enough to check his badge to see it’s out of date. All he is, is a face in the crowd that can’t be seen. It’s not that he’s a master of disguise, in fact many of the chameleons captured barely have what could be considered a “wardrobe” at all. Their “gift” is that the rest of the world simply considers them invisible.

                                                            Background: Not many Hunters wind up as Chameleons, they’re ingratiated too greatly with their cells to be unnoticed in the same way these poor souls are. But many Hunters have allies and informants outside the camandarie of the cell, when the heat is on these inside men can be left alone and isolated. Bereft of both social contact and the safety of the mundane world they sometimes find that when they finally escape and cut their ties to the supernatural they no longer belong among normal people, and they will do anything to seize a place.

                                                            Most people who wind up becoming Chameleons were never part of the vigil. They were ordinary, perhaps too ordinary, and ignored by everyone around them. They lived lives of quiet resentment or seething bitterness that grew and grew until the day they snapped. Others were ordinary and sociable people until some traumatic incident caused them to retreat from the world, or the world to retreat from them rather than confront the uncomfortable truths behind the crime. They tried and failed to return to the world, and then they decided they'd try more drastic measures to be seen again. These Chameleons inevitably work their backstory into their Undertaking and the murders they commit.

                                                            Chameleons tend to have very high Composure, with no human outlet for their emotions they have gotten very good at bottling up their feelings. Their skill in Subterfuge is also usually decent, they're not invisible so knowing how to stay in charachter when the foreman asks the nearest janitor to handle a task is vital, but few Chameleons master more than the basics of Subterfuge, they just don't have the opportunity to practice the more subtle intricacies of social interaction. Most other social abilities are non-existent, though a few Chameleons can from a nobody to a blazing Presence with terrifying Intimidation the moment they have a victim at their mercy.

                                                            Stealth is usually high, despite the fact Chameleons can rely on their Talent over more mundane tricks Stealth comes naturally to them. Many Chameleons have managed to disappear into a crowd or the shadows after VASCU agents have blown their cover. Crafts and Survival are also common skills, being invisible to society often means working menial jobs and taking care of yourself. Mental Skills and Attributes can be surprisingly high, society finds it easier to overlook the quiet kid behaving himself in the library than outgoing or misbehaving kids, and an empty social calender leaves plenty of time for study and mental pursuits.

                                                            Inspirations: Zartan (G.I. Joe: Rise of Cobra); False Face (Batman); Carlos the Jackal (Day of the Jackal); Jean-Baptiste Grenouille (Perfume)

                                                            Attribute: Composure

                                                            Undertaking Skill: Academics, Computer, Investigation, Crafts, Larceny, Stealth, Survival, Weaponry

                                                            Talent: A Thousand Masks

                                                            Frailty: Telltale Sign

                                                            VASCU Case File: “The Newsboy”

                                                            Notes from Det. Frank Bianchi, NYPD

                                                            Uniforms found another last night. Young girl, couldn’t be older than 22, no ID just like the others. Stabbed multiple times, left to bleed out, posed to look like she was homeless. Lucky for us, the spot where she was left actually belonged to a bum.

                                                            The captain’s getting on my ass about this one, but we don’t fucking know where to start. Every witness doesn’t remember anyone really standing out. They don’t remember anyone talking to the victim, her friends don’t mention anyone following her the past few days, it’s all turning up empty.

                                                            The only connection between the victims is that they all stopped at the same news stand to grab a magazine or newspaper. Owner said he didn’t see anything suspicious either, no one tracking them or acting like they were too interested.

                                                            Christ, they’re gonna have my shield if we don’t figure this one out soon. I’ll go back to the stand today, see if the guy might’ve remembered something since the last time we spoke.
