                Anyway, I had some time earlier this month, and I wrote up some crossover notes:

                Demon

                Demons can drive the hardest bargains when people are at their most desperate, and few situations are more desperate than when a leviathan is involved. An unscrupulous demon can turn any conflict spurred by the presence of a leviathan into a pacting bonanza. An enterprising unchained could offer information, resources and/or weapons to an Ahab in exchange for their signature, then shift into a different Cover and do the same thing for the leviathan(‘s cult). And this all before you include family members seeking to free cultists of the leviathan’s influence, free hybrids seeking to be rid of the Tribe’s genetic influence, or cultists seeking to become more divine. All available with the stroke of a pen.

                A leviathan can easily convince a cultist to sign a pact on their behalf, though doing so almost always provokes an Enuoia roll. And while hybrids can be rid of the tribe’s influence through demonic pacts, leviathans themselves have no such easy out: whatever mechanism that permits the unchained to interface with reality’s source code cannot alter the leviathan’s divine nature. Any pact that attempts to do so has no effect, with any resources committed to the pact wasted in the attempt.

                Leviathans and demons both retain the full effectiveness of their unique defenses against each other: the leviathan’s supernatural senses have just much trouble picking through the lies of the unchained as much as the demon struggles against the leviathan's resistance against direct manipulation.

                Is the God Machine a god?

                Short answer: No.

                Rather than a singular natural force, the God Machine is better comprehended as a series of networked intelligences, whose presence is represented in the World of Darkness by angels and infrastructure. It does not directly interact with or give blessings to humanity. What influence it does exert is done through intermediaries: for example, the Ink Endowments of the Knights of St. Adrian are the result of angelic blessings, and technically not the work of the greater God Machine.

                Anyone who worships the God Machine directly will either be ignored outright, or incorporated into the God Machine’s inscrutable plans, either as an asset or as spare parts.

                Promethean

                At first glance, Leviathans and the Created appear to be long lost cousins. Both are creatures whose supernatural nature have forced them to isolate themselves from humanity. Both wear a human guise that conceals their true, inhuman self. Both of them even share the ability to drag themselves back from Death’s doorway. Add to the fact that Leviathans are largely immune to a Promethean’s Disquiet, and you end up with the bizarre situation of a Leviathan’s cult being one of the safer places for a Promethean to be.

                Appearances can be deceiving however, and like most of the relationships the two groups have, it almost always ends in tragedy.

                The ultimate sticking point between the two is their relationship to humanity. While the Created attempt to move closer to humanity, the Leviathan finds their power pushing them away from humanity, both externally with the growth of the Wake, and internally, with their Channels making their true forms ever more monstrous.

                Even the suggestion that it’s possible become human again is often enough for desperate Leviathans to become dangerously possessive of the Created, and what the Prometheans thought was a sanctuary among the Tribes’ cultists can quickly become a prison. Adding to the friction is that while the Leviathan’s power gives them protection against the mental effects of Disquiet, it does not prevent a Wasteland from forming around a Promethean.

                Whatever measures a leviathan put in place to counter the Ripples of the Wake must now also contend with supernatural blights on their land. This is especially worrying if the Promethean happens to be a Frankenstein who creates Wastelands of fire and lightning, the Tribe’s ancestral bane; or an Osiran whose Wasteland introduces droughts and steals the moisture from the air, potentially threatening the Leviathan’s ability to utilize their two most powerful stages of transformation and their supply of divine Ichor.

                At this point, it’s up to the Leviathan whether to slay the Created and see if the secrets of the Divine Fire can be dissected from their corpse, join the countless others who have expelled the Created from their lands, or attempt to find some way to mitigate the Wasteland before Hunters come knocking.

                If you gaze into the Abyss…

                A possible exception to the above scenario are those Leviathans who belong to the School of the Abyss. The School is oriented around severing their connections to humanity and would therefore be the most likely to avoid falling into the behavior patterns described above. This does not mean that the followers of the Sunken School are in any way “safer” than those of other philosophies, rather that the nature of the threat they present to the Created is radically different.

                To those of the Sunken School, the goal of the Pilgrimage seems completely baffling: why in the world would you want to become more human? Isn’t humanity your greatest source of grief? Look at the nutbar cultists that dog my steps; why in the world would you want to be more like that?

                To a Promethean who has been frustrated and stymied by humanity's continued rejection of them, these arguments are seductive. Add to the fact that leviathans are almost completely immune to Disquiet and that members of the School of the Abyss are likely in the best position to handle the Wastelands that follow the Prometheans (the examples above notwithstanding), and the resolve of the most determined of the Created might start to waver.

                Of course, tragedy dogs the steps of such a relationship, even if it’s longer in coming. Despite being created in humanity’s image, a Promethean is not mortal and does not count as such for the purposes of preventing Erosion. Even though the Direction of the School of the Abyss significantly slows the process, any leviathan who stubbornly remains in isolation from humanity is doomed to eventually be devoured by either their bestial or divine nature, whereupon the Promethean will get a first hand example of what a lack of humanity really looks like.

                The development of the Created are similarly stymied. Attempting to analyze the behavior of a levithan’s cult as an example of humanity produces contradictory and confusing answers. Through the funhouse mirror-like distorting and twisting brought about by the Wake, no clear or consistent examples of the human mind can be distinguished.

                Mage

                Leviathans who have heard the stories of the Sky Wizards of Marduk would be very wary when dealing with the Awakened. Their worries are only reinforced when they discover that the Obrimos claim to be the agents of the divine and are able to produce the Tribe’s electric bane at will.

                This wariness is replicated on the Awakend’s side, as Mages who discover the effects of the Leviathan’s Wake can easily mistake the them for some kind of Abyssal creature. Those attempting to attack the Leviathan through Sympathetic connections would be shocked and horrified to learn that doing so made them equally as vulnerable to the Leviathan’s Knowledge based powers, cranking their natural paranoia up to 11.

                An experienced Leviathan can be one of the most tenacious foes a Cabal can face. Leviathans are all but immune to mind control and other forms of direct manipulation, their thoughts can’t be read without risking madness, attempting to kill them at a distance opens the Awakened up to Knowledge based powers, and attempting to slay a Leviathan in the past or future has no effect on the Leviathan in the prime time line, not to mention that said Leviathan is capable of the paradoxical feat of remembering what the Cabal hasn’t done yet. Add in the Leviathan’s aquatic nature, and speculating Mages can’t help but wonder if the Tribe played a role in the downfall of Atlantis.

                A Deal with The Devil

                Leviathans are objects of fascination and fear for the Awakened, and nowhere is this clearer than with the Mastigos. Attempting to read the thoughts of the Tribe brings them in direct contact with one of the most potent psychic forces they’ve ever encountered, not to mention that their hyper dimensional nature duplicates the more subtle effects of Space. In addition, any Mastigos who learns of the existence of the Rift will immediately make parallels to Pandemonium, fueling speculation over exactly who and what those Sky Wizards were, and what their relationship was to Tiamat and Marduk, and what relationship, if any, they have with the Awakened.

                If the two groups are able to overcome their incredible mutual paranoia, they would find that each has resources to offer to the other. The Key and The Gate is a simpler way to gather information about the past and future of the World of Darkness, so long as they don’t mind being restricted to the perspective of the Tribe’s descendants. The leviathan’s psychic might even grants the Awakened new avenues of attack against enemies who would otherwise provoke Paradox. Not to mention that their Cult can act on the Awakend’s behalf.

                In return, the Awakened can act as a bridge to humanity for the Tribe. By casting Mind Shield, a Mastigos can steel a mortal against the effects of the Wake: used in this way, every dot of Mind negates a -1 penalty on Breaking Points caused by the Wake, with complete negation meaning they are no longer influenced by the Wake (If a Leviathan chooses to Focus the Wake, roll Clash of Wills). The simple act of having a conversation with a normal person without the Wake battering them into submission is incredibly life affirming, and makes a remarkably potent bargaining chip in the hands of the Awakened, a fact the Seers of the Throne are all too eager to exploit...

                What do we want? 3rd Edition Sidereals!
                When do we want it? In accordance with the dictates of Fate!

                    One thing to bear in mind with Prometheans. The Divine Fire is in fact divine. If you want to outright say that the Gods powering Empyrean Theurgists and Judges are a form of the Divine Fire, or have a Qashmallim turn up in person to give a new Judge their commandment I can't see it contradicting anything in either gameline.

                    Then there's the fact that both Azoth and Ichor contain the divine power of creation in a highly unstable form. One alone is trouble, but putting them together is like trying to mix... well, fire and water. The Tribe and the Created both have a fair amount they could offer each other, sadly their own fundamental natures are opposed.


                    For Mage. The Symmetry between Sympathy and Knowledge is completely intentional. Thank PenDragon, it was his idea some of the powers (Read Not the Tomes in particular) are actually designed specifically for Storytellers using Leviathans as antagonists in a Mage game.

                    The Rift wouldn't be compared to Pandemonium though. The Rift is quite explicitly the Temenos, however it's the Tribe's Temenos not humanity's Temenos, there's no direct connection between the two Temenoi. Of course, that would be just as interesting to the inquiring Mage. Do bring a Thyrus or Moros though, you'll need gills or a supply of oxygen.

                    The Tribe can also be the patron in Patron Yantras and probably other stuff as well. Someone (who knows Mage mechanics better than me) would have to figure out the details, but it would be quire appropriate to go through Summoners and add Leviathans as another potential thing to be summoned (or at least astral projections of said Leviathan).

                    There also are ways to get around the Tribe's potent defences (especially if the Mages are PCs and the Leviathan is not). Turn a Heirloom into an Imbued Item for example (can you make an Imbued Item with a mind of it's own? If so you could just make it Beloved). So long as the Leviathan doesn't figure out what you're doing and start using the Item to target Rituals that could work. Plus, Mages have numbers on their side.

                    Finally. I'd say it's the Pentacle not the Seer's who're busy exploiting the Tribe's desire to mind shield their friends and family. Part of the Seer's core manifesto is "Enslave the Gods". IMO that includes Leviathans.


                    P.S. My favourite crossover titbit. Vampires who drink Leviathan blood turn from undead to alive for as long as the blood is in their system. Mechanically this just means they get the Blush of Life for free and fall under Life Arcana rather than Death, they're still vampires with all that implies. They age normally though, their hair grows, they find it easier to change as a person, and when it wears of their current state becomes their new baseline state.

                    They also have a risk of mutating, growing huge cancerous tumours, or spontaneously developing a Bloodline. There's probably a few Circle and Dragon aligned Bloodlines who's origins involved reckless use of Ichor.

                                This is one of my sample cults.

                                The Lorekeeper Society

                                ​If you were to compare Leviathan cults by sanity, The Lorekeeper would probably rank high on the sanity scale. Even though their god's wake does it's job on deteriorating their sanity, the members of this book collecting society has somehow managed to retain most of their sanity. Based in Washington D.C., The Lorekeepers have close ties to The Library of Congress, due to the fact that their founder was a employee of The Library. Their outside façade is just a bunch of men toiling over dusty tomes and locating more books to place in their already crowded library. However, there is a feeling of something sinister deep within the clubhouse. But, no visitor can pinpoint where exactly.

                                ​Within the clubhouse there is a secret door that leads to a subterranean tunnel. This tunnel leads to what a bookworm would call, something out of Lovecraft. The tunnel leads to a alter room where the alter is covered in blood and where there is a idol of a terrible sea serpent. The serpent is their god. whether the god is male or female is a secret only the highest ranked members of the Society know. The leader of the cult is a man named Roderick Croswell, a tall, pale skinned man with black hair and gaunt features. Roderick used to be the leader of a other cult which was wiped out by the Mardukian's, along with their Leviathan. Roderick joined The Society in order to possibly find a book that could resurrect his goddess. During that time, he took over leadership of The Society and started to restructure it to make it fitting for his goddess when she inevitably returns. Since the goddess was a Oceanead he structured it in a way where it is assured that she would be entertained by the cultist's squabbling over promotions.

                                ​It is unknow how The Society discovered their god. But, in under five minutes of the god's first arrival, the wake took it's toll and completely made all of The Society's members devoted servants to the god. Today, the Society has a strict rank system. On the bottom are potential initiates while the top consists of the high priest and the Board of Directors of the club. Between therse are a bunch of squabbles, fights and competitions for promotions and every cultist hope that one day they will get to be a Board Member. Their god surprisingly has allowed them to continue collecting books and tomes. While they manage to keep a thin façade of sanity, sometimes the insanity boils over and they do something stupid. For example, one of their larger members once beat the shit of a guy for no reason. This caused a manhunt which nearly exposed the cult. This has caused The Board to realize that the low-level cultists needed to be placed under a tighter reign. So, with approval from their god, they made it a rule that if anyone breaks a rule of the cult, they get beheaded and their body burnt on the altar. This new rule has managed to keep the cultists under control, but for how long?

                                Piety: 4

                                        Princess

                                        The nobility draw their power by painstakingly defending and uplifting humanity. By contrast, all the Tribe has to do is stand in a populated area, and the Wake will brutalize and twist the minds of everyone nearby, stripping away the veneer of civilization and leaving behind lesser creatures who eagerly submit themselves to the whims of a monstrous god.

                                        Such as it was in the time of the Old Kingdoms, and the intervening centuries haven’t done much to change the situation. This alone make leviathans priority targets for the Nobility, especially if they're sitting in or near an area they’ve chosen for their proto-kingdom. It’s up to each individual Tribe member whether they seek to avoid the notice of the Nobility, strike them pre-emptively, or flee.

                                        Mind Breaking Horrors

                                        It should be noted that the Nobles’ closest allies, the Shikigami and the Sworn, lack the full protection of the Light the Nobles do and have Integrity instead of Belief. Although they can’t become Beloved, this state does makes them vulnerable to the Leviathan's Wake, facing members of the Tribe in full Apotheosis, and any other power that has a greater effect against those with Integrity.

                                        It should be noted that while most supernatural effects treat Nobles in their Mundane and Transformed states as two different people, the two forms share the same mind and are thus equally vulnerable to effects that use the Knowledge modifier. This makes Somebody Else’s Problem, a Charm that deletes the memories of the user, one of the most effective ways of defending against the Leviathan’s rituals. Could the Queen of Mirrors have intentionally devised the Charm as a defense against a Leviathan's more insidious powers? Theories abound and The Queen certainly isn’t talking.

                                        Leviathan cults of Darkness?

                                        Given the unholy acts committed during a leviathan's rituals, and their precarious Integrity, a Leviathan’s cult appears to be prime candidates to be tainted and corrupted by the Outer Darkness. In practice, this almost never happens.

                                        As horrific as the effects of the Wake are, it does grant the Leviathan’s cult protection against competing influences, including the effects of the darkness (Treat the Outer Darkness as a Poaching Leviathan, using the dice pool of the local Taint or relevant power). Only an especially powerful taint could gain a foothold on those so claimed, and any area so thoroughly tainted often bears obvious scars of whatever sins were committed there, leaving such areas as the refuge of the truly desperate or arrogant.

                                        Even if a leviathan were aware of the existence of the darkness, most wouldn’t use it. Their divine might trumps most powers offered by the darkness, not to mention that leviathans feel a certain instinctual revulsion at the mutations inflicted by the darkness. The divine Ichor is a catalyst of life and growth, however hideous and unstable it may be. The influence of the Outer Darkness, by contrast, can only degrade and decay.

                                        Of course, there’s nothing stopping an enterprising cultist of the darkness from taking advantage of any taint left behind in a leviathan's temple after they have to skip town.

                                        Are the Queens gods?

                                        Not quite.

                                        While the Queens do promote (what they see as) virtuous behaviour and grant power to their followers, their purview does not extend to humanity as a whole, nor do they possess the mystical potency or inclination to fuel miraculous rituals. Their raison d'être is for humanity to lift themselves up out of the darkness, not for them to rely on divine assistance, however well intentioned.

                                        Which is not to say that Queens lack the power to produce wide ranging supernatural effects as can be produced by rituals, so long as it's keeping with the themes of their Invocations. It’s just they have to produce such magic personally, and can’t grant those powers to another person on their behalf.

                                        While the Queen of Storms theoretically has the power to fuel miraculous rituals, in practice, the collection of souls that make up her gestalt Empress body denies her the focus that would be available to a singular divine soul. Which is just as well, as her power would almost certainly be just as dangerous to those invoking her power as to their intended targets.

                                        Genius

                                        Where one threatens to drag humanity back to the bronze age, the other is the herald of an uncertain future. One is the scion of divine blood, the other graced with mad Inspiration. One expresses their power through monstrous transformation, the other through miraculous invention. It’d be difficult to imagine two creatures that are more different than the Inspired and Earth’s Most Wicked Tribe.

                                        Of those Inspired belonging to the Peerage, the Progenitors are the most interested in the Tribe. The biology of a hyperdimensional aquatic demigod is fascinating to them, and are attracted, perhaps to an unhealthy degree, of the mutagenic possibilities of the Divine Ichor. Their attempts to capture a Leviathan as experimental specimens tend to go badly for everyone involved.

                                        Due to their natural inclination for exploring into and beyond the dark corners of the world, the Navigators tend to encounter Leviathans the most often, especially those who have or are in the process of succumbing their inhuman natures. Seeing Leviathans at the height of their wickedness has left the Navigators with a “shoot first, ask questions later” mentality when dealing with the Tribe.

                                        The other members of the Peerage often ignore the Tribe unless their activities directly affect whatever plans or experiments they have. Though it is advised that the Tribe keep a healthy distance from the Inspired regardless, as most take it personally when their lab assistants are swept up in the Wake.

                                        “We won’t have these problems when I rule the world!”

                                        If one were to look beneath surface comparisons of the Inspired and Earth’s Most Wicked, similarities begin to emerge. Both have existed since time immemorial. Both have powers that isolate them from humanity, leaving them to struggle against an encroaching madness. And neither is entirely certain where their powers originate. Nowhere, however, are these similarities with the Tribe more pronounced than with the Bahraims of Lemuria.

                                        Like Lemuria, the Tribe believes that humanity has gone down the wrong path, i.e. any path not under the Tribe’s rule. Like the Unmada of Lemuria, the members of the Tribe project a supernatural field that bends others towards their will and deepens their isolation them from the rational world.

                                        Despite all of this, an alliance between the Tribe and Lemuria is likely to only be one of convenience. Lemurians are all, to one degree or another, insane egomaniacs, and do not take kindly towards anything they perceive as attempting to usurp their vision.

                                        Resetting the Clock

                                        For Leviathans seeking to reclaim the glory the Tribe had at it’s apex of their rule, they could ask for no better weapon than the Clockstoppers.                                                 Originally posted by Jimborg View Post
                                                Princess
                                                One thing to bear in mind. Princesses just don't have the fire power to take down a combat focused leviathan with a few points of Sheol in it's Apotheosis. Unless they can catch it on land the most effective strategy for Princesses to fight a leviathan is to find someone with bigger guns (typically the local army, they're easy to find) and start throwing around Bless and Inspire Charms.

                                                Incidently Acqua Charms just look and feel really weird to leviathans. Acqua is built on the concept that water connects; that it's easier to travel, trade, and share ideas, by sailing than by travelling over land. A bit obsolete now, but it was true for most of history: Ancient Greece, Renaissance Italy, Victorian England, all major maritime powers. (I'm not sure about golden age Arabia, it may be an exception). Leviathans are connected to water as the a source and requirement of life: The primordial ooze, drink regularly or dehydrate and die, our bodies are 70% water, etc.

                                                There's no mechanical effect for this though. It won't let a Princess of Diamonds hurt a leviathan with Uncrowned Fisher King by firing jets of water. The leviathan will simply assert that it's interpretation of water takes precedence and continue along it's way.

                                                Originally posted by Jimborg View Post
                                                It should be noted that while most supernatural effects treat Nobles in their Mundane and Transformed states as two different people
                                                A leviathan still needs to name or otherwise identify the target he wants to focus a ritual on. If he nominates Princess Gwendolyn as the the target and she's unTransformed then the Ritual cannot find her.

                                                The Adaptation Read Not the Tomes also treats Princesses as two people. So if he looks for "whoever attacked me yesterday" and she's unTransformed at the time it can't find her.

                                                On the other hand, if he looks for "any spies within ten miles of the temple" after the guards spotted a Noble sneaking around; that would likely catch whichever form she's in right now. Transformed or not she's still a spy.

                                                Originally posted by Jimborg View Post
                                                Are the Queens gods?
                                                Remember that the difference between god and not-god is in many ways like the difference between psychic powers and mind arcana. A Mind spell and a telepathic power might have identical mechanics, but even if the only difference is the fluff, they're clearly distinct entities.

                                                Most of the difference between Queens and gods comes down to that. The way a Queen's existence creates an Invocation even resembles the way a god's existence creates a power source that can empower people into Thurgists can tap into.


                                                If you step behind the curtain. Queens and Leviathan style gods serve the exact same story role: Extraordinary powerful entities that teach virtues and empower those who live up to their virtues (playable Queens are possible, but not really part of the core Princess game). And the fluff for both were written by the same person; so unsurprisingly they ended up very similar. The difference between the two mostly comes down to Mind Arcana vs telepathy like technicalities, and the fact gods are significantly stronger than Queens.


                                                P.S. The gestalt Queen of Storms has a focus that surpasses most singular souls. The reason she doesn't fuel rituals is that she's too focused on fighting Darkness to listen. Even if you try to direct her attention to the Darkness, she won't actually hear your message, listening to you takes up attention that could be spent fighting the Darkness. However when she notices the Darkness she can grant Ritual-like Miracles. There's rules for it in Princess, they're significantly less powerful than rituals; but chalk that up to gameplay and story segregation. Princess games are supposed to be lower power than leviathan games.


                                                Originally posted by Jimborg View Post
                                                Genius
                                                One important addition: Geniuses are one of the few splats that Leviathans fear. Most Geniuses wouldn't stand a chance in a fight against a Reef Leviathan. But the minority of Geniuses who have the Inspiration and resources, they can single handedly take down the largest Leviathans. The Steyotype section for Geniuses makes this clear.

                                                The reason is two fold: Firstly a quick look at Genius mechanics shows that Wonders can be more powerful than Marduk's super science. Secondly a Genius who builds an orbital death ray is unlikely to share Marduk's sane and rational viewpoint about collateral damage.

                                                Fortunately for players who want to have fun, a Genius might also lack Marduk's sane and rational view that blasting leviathans from high orbit is safer and more practical than building a giant robot with a lightning sword. (Not that Marduk could, unlike mad science, super science respects the square cube law).


                                                One last point. The most proactive group at stopping Leviathans from doing evil would be Lemuria, with the Navigators coming in second. After all the core difference between the two groups is that Lemuria thinks that Geniuses should help mortals, and the Peerage thinks that they're too insane and will only make things worse.
