    The Jotnar
    At first, the Uratha thought the tales of giants in the north to be mere fancies, or perhaps errant spirits briefly crossing into the realm of Flesh. They soon enough discovered the true nature of the horrors of the mountains. The jotnar are Hive-Claimed; they are shuddering hulks infested with gibbering parliaments of spirits, their flesh twisted and reworked into colossal form. The giants can walk between worlds with ease, have mastery of dark rites and are possessed of immense strength. They can devour wild beasts, but prefer to steal away humans to grind their bones into flour and eat their viscera. Their ravenous hunger extends to spirits; the northern Shadow has stretches of wasteland, scoured of Essence to feed the maw of the jotnar brood. The jotnar die only to violence, not age nor disease nor infirmity.

    The eldest of the giants, Ymir, made this abominable clan. How Ymir came to be is lost to memory, even amongst the surviving spirits of the north – and certainly Ymir himself. He is a cacophony of spirits of stone and mountain, ice and fire, all poured into one unstable vessel of incredible age. Craving children, of a kind, he made more Hive-Claimed through diabolical rites. In time, his children themselves sought children, and the brood grew as fat and bloated in numbers as their bodies were. It was inevitable they would clash with other powers. Their hunger cannot be contained nor limited.

                        I'm not Chris Allen, but I did some my own a bit research of Norse myths for the CoD games in Nine Realms topic. To be frank, it's idea start was inspired by Chris own stuff posted here. So I share my takes on them, as Chris is not answering for now.

                        If we go outside of Werewolf gameline, most often I would connect various Giants races with Beast's Anakim. Like in Norse myths, many times those Giants are said to be able to look like normal human - and then becoming enormous in next second. Just like Anakim can. Of course, using Changeling and, for example, Hobgoblins is totally doable also. But still, I would more stick with Anakims only because that Beasts are much more connected to the Material / Flesh World in the first place, as their Horrors strife from humans fears - Jotunheim is described in Norse myths to look more or less as Flesh World.

                        Originally posted by Khanwulf View Post

Lack of love and compassion from gods later on finally made Wolf Mother into Angerboda, that literally means 'One Who Brings Grief'. It exactly made Fenris as one of Firstborn, but also Jörmungandras cousin to werewolves, but it's only one small problem for Uratha historians saying 'human get it wrong'.

                            Which of these seems likely and what might she have done that horrified the Uratha so? I'm just presuming that being "Bale" is not enough to truly horrify.

                            The idea that the Dark Mother is an astral memory of Urfarah is an interesting tie. My knowledge of Beast is extremely thin--thinner than Mage, which is definitely "butter over too much bread". Werewolf isn't much better, which is why I pick at questions.

                            Anyway, my inclination is to try and make Angrboda into a real person, with whom PCs might interact, and then use some of the back-story beats of Chris' arc to set things up. That means trying to understand the players and their relationships better. I could handwave Angrboda away as just incorporated into the legend, but since we know human legends are going to be a warped reflection anyway it seems like a waste to not use her.

                            So basically a timeline, with speculation:

                            1. In the distant past, Ymir is born of a hive-claiming and is oppressed by Fenrir, who earns its enduring enmity. Alternatively, a great and cunningly malevolent spirit escapes (at least in part) from Fenrir, and flees to a forbidding land where it "goes native" by creating a hive-claimed with powerful allies it recruits--ancient spirits that remember Urfarah, and hate him and his children nearly as much as the spirit hates Fenrir. [For the sake of linking, let's say the side-effects of this claiming, or perhaps a very early attempt to eliminate Ymir (by Fenrir?), resulted in the Storegga Slide.]

                            2. By the time of Rome's height (which means, not later than 2nd century AD) Ymir has embedded himself, hidden, in the far North.

                            2. Angrboda (who might also be a hive-claimed giantess, or she achieves this reputation later) begets Hel from Loki in the forest to the east of the Baltic. Hel is wolf-blooded and suffers the Change, joining the Uratha but either falling prey to the Maeljin or carrying with her the seeds of their influence from an early age. She is driven out, to Utgard (the forest, outside) and wanders.

                            3. Hel encounters Ymir, or is introduced to him.

                            4. Fenrir is drawn in and imprisoned. The Blood Talons start going mad, first with attempts to figure out what's happening, then to save him, and to gather resources for such. ["Viking" settlement movement? Which pre-dates the raids.]

                            5. Circa 793, the Blood Talon seers proclaim that imitation of Fenrir will strengthen him and enable his freedom. The Viking Age is launched. Blood Talons spur the human kingdoms to greed and war that quickly turns into more convenient measures such as settlement to facilitate the process, and then more settlement. The response from Europe includes redoubled efforts to Christianize the North, and by the 11th and 12th centuries going a-viking is essentially done, [which implies that Fenrir was freed?]

                            Since I'm working forward from the 5th century with my own players, penciling in more will be helpful.

And etc. What could draw them back toward the real problem, which is Fenrir and more or less the end of the world, from the Uratha/Blood Talon perspective?
