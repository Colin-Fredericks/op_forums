                As for the thread topic, I vacillate. I feel like every ChronD game can generally coexist reasonably well with the others, especially if used sparingly to spice up your currently-being-played game with hints of a wider world, but I also feel like a lot of needless complexity is avoided if you run the other monsters/splats as more archetypal versions of themselves. If I'm running Hunter and there's a vampire in the story, I'm just as likely to make him a random vampire bro with fangs and a cape and a dodgy accent than to make him a Kindred™ of the Gangrel™ clan. If you want to use a mummy as a generic monster, there's nothing wrong with having him leap out of the tomb going BOOGITY BOOGITY BOO instead of painstakingly describing his Judge and figuring out his Sekhem and et cetera et cetera.

                All that said, I think Mage and Hunter are probably most dependent on the presence of other gamelines - it would be weird if Hunters had nothing to hunt, after all, and it would be almost as weird if Mages existed in a world of limitless magical possibilities -except- for vampires and werewolves and Frankensteins - and I think the games that can stand most independently are probably Demon and Vampire. Vampire because it was the first and prototypical urban horror fiction and doesn't automatically suggest the existence of other supernaturals, and Demon because it is so utterly sui generis and comes complete with an internally-consistent cosmology that explains everything Demons are and can encounter.

                                                Honestly, it might be because my game table has members of 4 splats (Vampire, Hunter, Werewolf and Mage, going on 5 since there's the possibility that the Hunter will become a Sin Eater) that I don't see how one splat could be the "omg, this doesn't work with anything else!". Afterall, the game is called "Chronicles of Darkness", not "Separate Universes of Darkness". Each splat should be a part of the overall Chronicles, not an isolated story.

                                                    Unless I'm building a Beast or Hunter setting I tend to only include one game's content at first. I try to really narrow in on a particular game's themes so I can get the most out of that. That way if I do include crossover content it ends up being a curve ball that nobody was expecting to have to deal with, and it hits that much harder.

                                                    If I'm running Hunter then I downplay the societal world-building for the monsters and focus on small groups and individuals. That way the players can get a really good challenge in, but not get overwhelmed by some sort of coordinated retaliation by a court of faeries or corporation of the damned. I haven't gotten to play or run Beast yet, but when I do I'd like to play up the societal world-building and nudge people to form connections that blur all the boundaries and make a mess of stuff.

                                                    So I guess my answer is 'it depends.'

                                                    The only game I really feel much need to run by itself though is Mummy. I feel like Mummy gets more mileage out of itself by being the main focus. Maybe throw a vampire or two in there as examples of unconventional Lifeless, but not the whole society or whatever.
