        Taking a glance at Alignments:

        I'd like to see Genius' powers moved away from "make a Wonder for everything" - the lack of spontaneity with Wonder creation kinda takes away from them in my mind.

        I kinda want to see Geniuses as being to investigation/crafting stuff what Werewolves are to combat or what Vampires are to social interaction.

        What'd be hilarious would be if their powers are predicated on them being failures. They get a big ol' penalty to building equipment and researching things, but can salvage "failed" experiments with a little Mania.

        A normal guy screws up at fixing his car's engine, gives up, and calls a mechanic.
        A Genius screws up at fixing his engine, keeps going, and ends up with a car that gets 80 miles to the gallon. Of blood, but still.

        And your powers get stronger if you dramatically failed the roll.

        (And a big discount if you incorporate a Lemure. Sure, a normal person would think twice about sticking a human heart in their fuel tank, but normal people aren't saving SO MUCH MONEY on gas.)

        Maybe (incidental thought after looking at the Beholden condition) world-view could be a mechanical thing?

        Like building new Wonders by failing rolls adds a new "hook" to your world-view. Making stuff that falls within your world-view is flipped (so you can make Wonders by actually succeeding at something), doesn't take the penalties, and all that other fun stuff...

        The one thing is that you need to have consistency in your world-view. If you, say, have figured out that machinery works better when fuelled by blood and that brass works better for wiring than copper... you'd better have some way to link those. You've got your Grand Unified Theory about how the world really works... it'd be a shame if it all turns out to be as much of a sham as the scientific consensus.

        One way to do this mechanically is come up with a list, a la Touchstones or the different archetype-type replacements that a few of the splats get instead of Virtue/Vice. Then you could do stuff like letting Geniuses that share Fragments (sounds like a good term) of their world-view work better together, mad-science-wise.

        (Joke-y idea: there's a social penalty for not sharing world-views. It's biggest when the world-view is not shared at all and when it is mostly (but not completely) shared.)

        I have decided, after some thought, that I don't really feel happy on these forums. I might decide to come back to post. Who knows - but right now, I'm gone.

        So good bye, good luck, and have a nice day.

                                The Clue mechanics are uniquely bad for bat deduction like powers, as the Uncovering the Truth rules provides things like plausible-deniability if you're short on clues.

                                Given that bat deductions cannot stand up in any reasonable Court, it doesn't make sense to remove plausible deniability and you can't make it a power to use fewer Clues when that option is already there for everyone.

                                The best solution would be to just say that Geniuses tend to Uncover the Truth early, which is why Genius related criminal investigations are full of Batmobile chases, rescuing hostages from bond-villian death traps, and the university dean complaining that there's no place in a respectable university for tenured professors having fisticuffs with captains of industry while dressed up in animal costumes.
