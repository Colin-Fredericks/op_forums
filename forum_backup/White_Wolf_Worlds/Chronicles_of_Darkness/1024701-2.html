            Ok, I think this is a major point of miscommunication. I'm going to try to express this as simply as I can, please feel free to ask me to rephrase anything the translation doesn't make sense. The Integrity system doesn't represent characters going insane. It represents a character's response to traumatic events and actions, and for most people, killing others is traumatic. But, if that rule doesn't suit your chronicle, you can change it. Removing murder as a Breaking Point or swapping Integrity for a different system, that's fine. But saying that there's a problem with the Integrity system because some people don't go insane from committing murder doesn't make sense, because Integrity doesn't measure sanity. Besides that, murder doesn't make anyone go insane, it's just traumatic for most people. If your chronicle is about playing characters for whom murder is not traumatic, that's fine. But tying that to anti-social personality disorder (the proper diagnostic term for what has been referred to as psychopathy in this thread), or to sadism (not an actual mental condition, but a preference for certain behaviors in sexual situations) is problematic because it implies very negative, and very inaccurate things about people who have those conditions, especially when so connected with talk of "insanity".

            The use of Integrity in the normal rules is to create drama and emulate tropes of the supernatural horror genre. Often in supernatural horror media, characters who experience the supernatural, the traumatic, or both, often have extreme reactions, such as becoming shaken and unable to focus on a task that would normally be easy, becoming spooked and making stupid mistakes like "splitting up to cover more ground", or feeling survivor's guilt for having lived through an encounter with a Thing That Should Not Be. In some extreme examples they might go catatonic or start to question reality. The Integrity system is primarily about modeling those reactions to trauma, in a more nuanced way than just having a pool of "sanity points". It also models similar reactions to personal trauma, such as experiencing something related to the worst thing a character has ever done, or taking another human life. At no point is it meant to punish players for their character's actions. One of the big issues with 1st edition's Morality system is that it was often perceived as a system of punishing wrong character actions, and Integrity was specifically designed to correct that issue with Morality.
