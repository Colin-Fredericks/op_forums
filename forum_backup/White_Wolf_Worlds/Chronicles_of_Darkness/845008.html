    What Are Durances Usually Like In Your Games?

    03-10-2016, 07:20 AM
    I feel like something important is missing when a character's time in Arcadia amounts to little more than an unrelenting, hellish nightmare. I also like to describe the relationship between changeling and Keeper in more ways than the generic victim and abuser. Sergeant and soldier, teacher and student, king and prince, and so on. I believe that encourages an open mind and leads to more varied antagonists.

    If I had to give an example, I'd say that Hayao Miyazaki's "Spirited Away" is my favorite kind of Durance. Wonder and terror in equal measure, with a slight tilt towards wonder. I don't have a problem with darker Durances. I just think Arcadia should be supremely malleable. Not only is the contrast helpful in illustrating the diversity of changelings, it can generate meaningful drama if handled right. Imagine the difficulty that an Ogre with a Splatterhouse-like Durance would have with connecting emotionally to those with less horrific experiences.

    Am I making sense? I guess I just have an aversion to excessive angst.

                      I'm tending towards the idea that many Durances are full of wonder, but there's no downtime. Even if you enjoy having magical adventures, you'll eventually wear yourself out physically and mentally. Imagine being Superman day in and day out, and there's never a time where you can take off the cape and take a nap or enjoy a quiet night out as Clark Kent. Even worse, many keep yanking you between the extremes, where you can never feel safe. You've saved the city of Townsville, got the key to the city, and the praise of the hapless citizens, but here comes yet another super villain out to destroy everything you care about.

                      "No matter how many times you save the day, it always ends up in peril again. Sometimes, I feel like the maid. 'I just cleaned up in here! Can't we just keep it clean for five minutes?!'" -Mr. Incredible
                      There's a reason why stories tend to stop at 'happily ever after.' No conflict, no story.

                          In general, I tend to gloss over Durances, mostly because the changeling themselves tend to forget the details as they're escaping through the Hedge. Durances tend to become old, half-remembered dreams, and the really important thing is the emotional association. The pang of cold fear that the changeling feels when he sees a woman with long elegant gloves is a lot more important to me than the exact details of the changeling's relationship with the Lady of the Thousand Masques. He probably doesn't really remember why she was so frightening in the first place, but he trusts that reflexive fear.

                                                          Originally posted by Master Aquatosic View Post
                                                          What's Dream Warping?
                                                          A set of oneiromancy techniques exclusive to the True Fae in 1ED. They require a dream phial to be placed next to the dreamer, or the presence of the True Fae. These techniques use Persuasion (strange beauty) or Intimidation (pure terror) instead of the Empathy used by changelings. These dreams can Ensorcell the dreamer (i.e. give them the ability to see past the Mask), cause many derangements over months, be brutally harvested for Glamour and any other sleep-related awfulness you can imagine.

                                                          I'm guessing Me!Me!Me! was a mix of Persuasion and Intimidation.
