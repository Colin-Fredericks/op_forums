                    Originally posted by malonkey1 View Post

                    Thoughts regarding Postulate structure?

                    Name • to •••••
                    Prerequisites: [if any]
                    Effect:
                    Notes: [if any]

                    What about adding a [] type to Postulates, where the [] signifies that the Postulate has a kind of fill-in-the-blank designator? So Incident Detection [Vampires] •• would be an Apokalypsi Postulate that lets you detect the supernatural powers of vampires (like a Vampire-only Peripheral Mage Sight). Energy Projectile [Electricity] • would be a Katastrofi Postulate that enables electrical energy to be projected at a distance. By using the [] designator, you let individual players and storytellers opt into whatever phenomena or sub classifications they wish, without them having to be spelled out in the rule-book.

                                About "Postulates" - why is it desirable to put hard limits on what a Genius can do? Mad scientists, as a trope, are all about overreaching - they go beyond the limits of morality and sanity, and are destroyed by the dangers they in their hubris dismissed. The Genius powerset should not, therefore, have predictable ladders of techniques to be mastered in order; instead it should be wide open from the start, but be balanced by risk to the Genius. Building Wonders you don't really understand should be not just possible, but basically routine - with a very real possibility of blowing yourself up every time you do it. Progress in studying Axioms shouldn't expand what you can do, but reduce the risks involved in doing it.

                                                Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                                How would be represented mechanically? Because there still needs to be guidelines. Even mage has guidelines.
                                                As it happens I was thinking of Mage's Paradox/Reach system. Adding Reach to a spell makes it more potent, but it also increases a pool of Paradox dice that has to be rolled before the spell is cast. Every success on that roll changes the spell's effect in ways contrary to the mage's intent. This fits for Genii even better than for mages - the question when a Genius builds a Wonder isn't whether the Wonder does anything, but whether it does what the Genius wants it to. But it doesn't fit well with mechanics that split up the possible effects into hundreds of fine categories, each of which has to be acquired independently. Ideally I'd want a Storyteller to listen to a short description of a Wonder, compare it to the Axioms, give it ratings and set a dice pool for Havoc and a cost in Resources or weird materials in just a few minutes; the more finely graded the Axiom schema is, the harder it gets to do that.
