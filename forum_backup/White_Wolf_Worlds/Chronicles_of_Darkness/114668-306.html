                                            Walking with the Sun was a rote in Mage 1e

                                            It was more along the lines of a Devotion (opposed to Discipline) for Mages: a "conjunctional" spell, I guess, that required Life 5, Mind 5, Space 4, Optional Matter 3 or 5 (I think that was it, not 100% sure)

                                            It cloned the Mage per success, teleporting the clones anywhere in the world. Virtual 1-man army, with the ability to flip between clones for the purpose of casting spells (your soul could inhabit any of the clones, but only 1 at a time. switching was reflexive), shared mana pool. With optional matter, you could copy existing equipment with 3, or create new equipment per clone with 5.

                                            It was pretty insane for a Master spell, but the requirements were sky high. Closest thing to an Archmaster spell outside being an Archmage, imo... we need more spells like this in 2e, which is why I asked for some cool examples. I'm probably thinking "conjunction" spells, though...

                                            It was pretty hilarious in a 1v1 situation: all the sudden the Big Bad is staring down 6 dudes instead of the lone Mage, and gets bum-rushed by 5 clones + you, with "you" being any of the 6. Sure, they couldn't cast except for 1, but an AoE strength buff was enough to have these Legacy-attainment, brawl 5 clones to grapple and beat down pretty much anyone in a normal game (the only real threat besides a "Greater Being" was another Multi-Master Mage).

                                            But yeah, if you've made it that far along as a Mage, you deserve that kind of power. That same Mage casting WwtS was once upon a time someone who couldn't even outheal the DPT of his Werewolf and Vampire teammates. But the smallest minnows often grow to be the biggest fish.

                                                That would definitely be a conjunctional spell. Not a combined spell. Combined spells are not one spell using effects from multiple Arcana. Combined spells are literally just two different spells that you cast simultaneously.
                                                The benefits are: less casting time, simultaneous effects, less spell control needed, less Mana spent on Spell Factors, and less Willpower spent if relinquishing the combined spell.
