    Promethean and Transhumanism Goes Here

    11-20-2014, 07:18 PM
    The intersection of these spheres is a great source of great argument, but it can derail other threads. Why not bring it in here?

    Let's start: Centimani is the transhumanist "Refinement", as it pushes the Promethean outside of human norms of being, thinking, and moralizing. Or, Centimani is not a transhumanist "Refinement" because it is in fact neutral to the borders of human experience, and further dwells on the current misery of the Saturnine Night without a means of ending it. Or, ... .

      You can (if you wanted to) actually view Promethean as a humanist metaphor for transhumanism for whatever it's worth.                   I agree with the notion that 'transhumanist' requires humanity as a base, and that the Created – as not-yet human – ought to be, ipso facto, unable to attain such a state. In First Edition, all of the Pandoran Transmutations are exclusively directed at the user's own body, but we don't yet know what sorts of things Second Edition Freaks might be capable of doing.

                                  Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                  You can, just like I pointed before - in posthuman state, Uplifted dolphines or chimpanzee weren't never humans. But still they will be posthumanity, joining the new kind of humanity with they thoughs or personas.
                                  Since you obviously missed my post addressing your misunderstanding/mudding of transhumanism in the other thread. So here it is again:

                                  Um...no. The idea of "uplifting" less developed species is a sci-fi trapping that ties into the idea of culture/technology progression an organisms development beyond its normal biological abilities (which is very similar to the transhuman ideal but not actually the same thing). Dolphins made super intelligent by cybernetics or gene-manipulation or whatever are "transpersons/transdolphines" not "transhumans". Your mixing up the concept of "person hood" with humanity, the two are separate.

                                  So to provide the definition of transhumanism:

                                  Noun - the belief or theory that the human race can evolve beyond its current physical and mental limitations, especially by means of science and technology.

                                  So, as mentioned here, for one to be transhuman, one would first need to be human. Something Prometheans are not.
                                  Uplifted animals would/could never "join" the human race. Because they are not biologically the same species as us. Even in a transhuman context where a human has heightened mental acuity due the same process/mechanism that uplifted another animal the two would still be distinct species. That's how biological distinction via species stratification works. One could argue them as a new genus/sub-genus of their respective species, but the fact they have both become "super intelligent" does not automatically enter them into the same species.

                                  Uplifted animals could engage with a human/transhuman as other PEOPLE or sentient beings of potential intellect, they could likely contribute to a transhuman society, but they themselves would not be transhuman. As I mentioned before, your mixing up biological and social constructs here and expanding the term transhuman beyond its actual definition quite a bit.

                                  Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                  Thinking of humans as "particularly smart apes" is obviously not helping.
                                  Satchel, don't waste your time man. This thread might as well be titled "Promethean and Missing the Point". People who engage in the transhuman ideal inherently have issue with the state of humanity and are going to devalue it.

                                  In real life I'm all for transhumanism and I think most people are at least intellecutally aware of the flaws present in human society, biology and psychology. Being from a social science background I'm a little more skeptical then most about the practical distribution of transhumanism in a human society. I'm also pretty aware that in some ways humanity and being human is pretty awesome as is and that people can be capable of amazing things despite their "limitations".

                                  However, in the case of NWoD and in particular Promethean I agree that humanity should be represented as "special" in some way.

                                  In the metaphysics of most NWoD game lines we have souls, no reflection in the shadow, leave ghosts, are the favoured tools of the God-Machine, are the only creatures able to Awaken, the only meat things worth dragging back to Arcadia, are the base from which (and may be required) all save one major supernatural (Demons) springs from. That starts to sound like a laundry list of things that the supernatural world seems to think we are pretty unique and special in regards to.

                                  Add to this the fact that pretty much every attempt in the NWoD to go "transhuman" is pretty much evil (yes their are exceptions, but they are exactly that, rare instances not involved with antithical amoral means/methods/aims) e.g. Mage Litches, Modified Chieron Hunters, all Mummy's, two of the three major types of immortals, the majority of Archmages ect. Hell even the majority of post-human states are pretty suckie really - Vamparism, Werewolfism, Changelinghood and Geistism. All have pretty hefty innate drawback (see Vamparism) or throw you into inhuman methods of thought (from a human perspective) and engage with inhuman societies that do break you down to a certain extent and give you an imperative that throws you in dangers way (Werewolf is perhaps the best example, Geist and Changeling both have elements of this as well though).

                                  Really, the only "transhumans" that don't get an utterly obvious drawback innately or through their social structures is Mages. Even then, being a Mage is what you make of it and is very much fraught with falls to hubris, madness, corruption (both moral and spiritual) and exposes one to a tone of supernatural threats.

                                  This is not to say that transhumanism should always be view as negative within the context of every game line in the NWoD. I'm happy having the Ordo Dracul and its "transvampirism" being portrayed as a good thing. Mage should engage with transhumanism as a sub-genre of its theme of hubris and wisdom. Hunter (at least a tier 3 game) should engage with at what point a "transhuman" hunter is just another inhuman monster. Demon should engage with the uniqueness of humanity as a contrast to the uniqueness of the Unchained and the "transdemonic" state that Hell may represent is an obvious parallel.

                                  My point is more that devaluing humanity (not human life, that something that should be devalued in inhuman/supernatural horror games I feel, particularly by morally dubious characters) I feel takes away from the NWoD in some ways. Yes you can use it to great effect if you want to run some sort of existential horror element in a Lovecraftian vain about the universe not giving a shit about humanity. That's a particular type of game and it has its place in the NWoD rosta without doubt, I just don't think its the baseline assumption of the majority of the gamelines.

                                  All this being said, the golden rules applies. If you want humanity to be nothing special and the aim of transcending the species norm to be the "big good" ideal of the setting in your games go for it.

                                                  (*is writing to this to Je N'ai Pas De Mots, so you can suffer mood whiplash as I have*)

                                                  Another point occurs to me.

                                                  Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw
                                                  The game is explicitly anti-transhumanist, in that it's always shown to cost more than it's worth; sorry if that upsets you, but we're making a horror game here.
                                                  Forget transhumanism and post humanism and humanism. Let's remember this is a horror game.

                                                  Explictly, this is a game whose thesis statement says the horror comes from discovering that the world is filled with nightmares, and now that you know you can't go back. That implies in certain ways that advancement is inherently terrifying first, with a fear of stagnation being addressed but treated as second. The sort of advancements that make modern transhumanism possible stem from technological and societal changes that have only pushed humanity deeper into their comforting lie that the monsters don't exist, exaggerating the problem.

                                                  In plainer terms, this is a game whose opening line says you were better for having not checked the noises in the alley or the colors under the bed. This isn't a fault of the game, it's a fault of the genre, and if that's part of what the genre does, the evocation of fear in the things we all have in common, I'm not sure you can call it a fault. Yes, the game(and genre) acknowledges that staying put isn't better, that things still happen in ignorance, but that comes after "The most horrible thing to happen to you is that you moved away from the familiar and the comfortable enough to find out disturbing secrets". And that's always a choice, by the way, a character can work to convince themselves to ignore even instances that are shoved on them(the movie Oculus has a great example of this, by the way).

                                                  Prometheans are unique in that they are born under the bed, but have the instincts to hide under the sheets. They can do what every other protagonist in the World, subtextually, wishes they could do-they can go back under the sheets. It's a terrible notion for progress, but again, that's the genre. It pits the ideas of a person going forth and hiding together in the Pilgramage, the character can learn and discover about humanity and earn the right to forget the most horrific aspect of the World, to assume step zero.

                                                  That grates against players, I know. It grates against me! I don't play the WoD as a horror game, I do it as a dark urban fantasy. As much as I like my external nightmares and personal horror and tragedy and such, in the end I like triumphant victories, self-actualization and growth in general, and endings that ultimately make the world a little brighter. Of course, I'm also way more of a humanist than I am of a transhumanist (I do believe you can be varying shades of both, and even be both of even more contradictory things), so the Pilgrimage still appeals to me on that front. But even if I use horror to inform but ultimately still not direct my use of the setting, I'm still side stepping and manipulating the basic fact-this is a horror game and with that comes a certain set of standards that affect the genre.

                                                  Think on any horror movie or story. Think critically about it, long and deep. Then think about Promethean.

                                                  Are you really surprised at what the horror genre has to say about transhumanism? Welcome to [part of] the buy in.
