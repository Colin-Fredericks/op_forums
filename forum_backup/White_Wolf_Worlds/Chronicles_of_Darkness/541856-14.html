            Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
            Agreed. While I do appreciate that they included the idea of wisdom through fear, I think it was too emphasized. I imagine most Beasts as embracing their role. The tone varies according to the particular Beast, of course, and even from day to day. But making the teaching of that wisdom a significant part of their culture sounds rather defensive on part of the Begotten. Looking for justifications so often is the main flaw of Heroes.
            I agree that they mention it an awful lot for an optional, but they do at least once note that some Beasts just retroactively call the assorted murders/assaults/homewrecking/etc they were going to do anyway "lessons" if anyone asks them about it, and some Beasts honestly just don't bother with the facade at all. There's room for not being a teacher, the setting's fine with that. The issue - and this is a general one - is getting a table on-board with all playing Beasts who have enough troupe cohesion to actually run the game. The guy who teaches middle-class families to appreciate their blessings by spooking them from time to time, promoting close-knit bonds of love and gratitude, and the gal who likes to play home invasion with her least favorite political demographic, leaving nothing but broken-eyed ruin behind, can't really co-exist. What I've found is that despite most other lines letting you run 'monsters,' the lack of a Morality stat in Beast somehow facilitates all kinds of ethical argument and sniping. I guess the game doesn't let you make the argument that you're "acceptable" if you're a diablerist but I feel like there isn't really an argument anyway. Still, some folks like having them.

            Back on topic, I want to run a Lovecraftian psychiatrist who terrorizes scientists, occultists, and private detectives into nervous breakdowns and then REALLY puts the screaming to them, an embodiment of Things Man Was Not Meant to Know. Those who seek knowledge beyond what they need to survive and live normal lives must be shown the limits of their kind; those who stargaze will learn never to raise their eyes again; et cetera et cetera. Someone who styles themselves as a gnostic tyrant, but is actually just bitter that they weren't smart enough to cut it before their Devouring, they only finished out their degree by supernatural compulsion and strength of fear, and searches for someone with the will and gumption to hold them off.

                            By day he works as a zookeeper, more specifically as a keeper for the tigers at the local zoo. They seem to listen to him, as if he's one of them. He seems to like them too, even staying after hours to feed and watch them. In fact, it's probably because he's there at night that the zoo hasn't had the same troubles as others in the area. There have been a rash of violent break-ins or animals escaping their cages and enclosures, even sometimes quite dangerous ones. They're never found after the escape either, seeming to vanish. Every now and again, though, someone spots a cougar drinking at a stream or something and the media frenzy starts all over again. A while ago, they even found a body that looked to ahve been stripped clean by some kind of big predator...

                            In truth, of course, the zookeeper is an Eshmaki Ravager whose Horror is a great tiger the size of a horse and who instigates the 'breakouts' as a method of destroying the complacency of the populace. His Lair is a vast jungle in which he keeps his dangerous 'pets', letting them out when they need to hunt. Sometimes he joins them.

                                Admittedly more of a methodology than a concept, but it might be an interesting setup for an antagonist.

                                Okay, first, you have to be a Tyrant. Second, you need to have the Nightmare Tabula Rasa, which can erase memories, and generally have a high Satiety. Then you either need to have a Lair that can be farmed, or have access to bulk supplies of food. Then in the real world, you hunt someone down, stalking them and taking pains to cut at the ties binding them to their life. Then, when they're all alone, you lead them somewhere you can open a Pathway and pull them into your Lair. You gloat and gleefully tell them what's going to happen and how they can't stop it (this feeds the Horror) and then hit them with Tabula Rasa, using the High Satiety effect to erase their entire life's memory and the Satiety expenditure to make it permanent; if you wish, you may set it to when they next sleep and then use You Will Never Rest, if you wish for more time to set the stage. Use the circumstances, copious amounts of supernatural force, and your social skills to convince them that the reason they can't remember anything is because you are a god who just forged them into being, and that your Lair is the whole world. Repeat as needed.

                                Congratulations, you now have a cult who depends on you for survival that you can farm Satiety from! You are also a total scumbag, and the pcs are likely to end up ruining your plan.

                                            Makara Collector
                                            You always dreamed of being a wizard. Well, technically you dreamed of being chased through a sunken castle by a massive serpent whose eyes betrayed centuries of cunning and wisdom, but what you wanted was to be a wizard, a magnus who had the knowledge to move heaven and earth. Really you figured the universe owed you it. After all, you had to work and sweat and struggle for everything while your sister, the favorite of your parents and everyone else, coasted through life on a wining smile.

                                            Eventually, you left to go to college, where you finally confronted the serpent that was you and claimed your birthright. You were upset when your realized this blocked you from your power, but you grew somewhat content, becoming as much of an expert on the sorceries of your Kin as an outsider could. Eventually you were called home to help provide for your parents (because of course the Golden Child couldn't be bothered), and for a time the world was good.

                                            But then you met your sister again, and when she flared with the light of Kinship, you grit your teeth and bore it. When her Nimbus shone with the shimmering coloration of the Primal Wild, it took all that you had not to bash her pretty perfect head into the nearest wall. Now you aid her in the investigations that plague her soul, her using you for your knowledge and skill and you using her to gain an in with her fellow Mages and the Satiety you need. But as the days go on you become more and more aware that you're nothing more than her assistant, her on-demand Yantra, her familiar! Now you grow bitterer and bitterer, and soon you fear you'll do something you regret... or worse, something you won't regret in the slightest.

                                                            Fire is the great protector, the purifier. Literal gods walk the land, blessings those they choose, destroying others on a whim. In the Neolithic Era, she's a shaman - a wise woman who sits on the fringes of society and counsels those who seek her out.

                                                            Or so they say.

                                                            Really, the People truly know the only reason why one would visit her: revenge. She hears the pleas of wronged supplicants, using the power of the great Horror within to judge the truth of their words. If their words ring true and they pay her price, she'll hunt down and punish the guilty, throwing their soul into the darkest depths of the ocean.

                                                            Basically, she's a Makara Nemesis, with her Horror a titanic sea-serpent known as the Terror of Death that swims through the Ocean Beyond Life and the Primordial Dream. Her fear - and lesson - is that you cannot escape what you have done, and even death is not a release. For when her Horror claims your life, it drowns your soul within the ever-swirling currents of its Lair, forever.
