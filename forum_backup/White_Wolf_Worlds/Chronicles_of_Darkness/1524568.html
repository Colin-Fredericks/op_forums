    Cross-over team

    10-26-2023, 11:27 PM
    Here's the question, there's a team of seven people, eight with ST, who want to play a cross-over, but would like a second opinion on how to do it. The points I would like you to focus on are the following:

    How to fit different creatures into the same team? They intend to play characters from multiple games, so a hint as to what goal/circumstance would bring them together would be welcome. Exclude hunter, these are just antagonists in this chronicle.

    What beings should be included? All games have antagonists, but I wanted opinions on which ones to use, because obviously it won't be possible to use everything and it would be good not to include a group that wouldn't exactly be a threat, it would also be good to know to what extent these threats could cooperate with each other.

    In the case of a chronicle within a large city, which groups do you believe should be included in the formation of that city? I know they intend to use Vampires and Werewolves (and maybe beasts), but some other working examples would be good too, just to not get too generic or messy.

    Basically that's it, and to make it clear, I'm not participating in the chronicle so there are things that I don't know how to explain why they are like that, maybe they just thought it is cool.​

      That is very broad.

      For antagonists, I would suggest one of the major one's, like the God-machine, or the Exarchs, or the Judges, or maybe even a Conspiracy from Deviant, something which is a large threat.

      Possibly two of these (one as a potential ally and future antagonists) or if you want deeper politics, use a least three more minor factions (not antagonists, but like the Lancea, Guardians of the Veil and Storm Lords all trying to get the players to side with them over a number of resources/events/missions).

      So everyone is either focussed on one big undefeatable evil (which can be undermined in some way, but ultimately it is a very big threat to everyone) or everyone is approached by a powerful (but not cosmically so) group which they can choose to work for or against (or you can do both).

      Including vampires can be more difficult, unless they can work through ghouled proxies during the day, or something similar. But that is something to discuss with the vampire players. Including a Mummy can be very messy given their unusual relationship with time.

      Lastly 7 players is a lot.

                      What to include or exclude is an interesting question. One of the answers to that will depend upon how big of a gap in power are you comfortable with and how much stepping on toes is okay. Mummies are much stronger than pretty much everything except experienced Mages, on the bottom end you have mortals (including hunters, not including mages). Deviants can fall virtually anywhere on the power chart depending on how they are treated, one thing I would recommend is taking away the aggravated damage lash, easy agg to everything is something Chronicles chose to avoid most of its lifespan.

                      For antagonists you need something strong enough to be a massive threat, the God Machine, Conspiracies, Judges, and Exarchs are great options but I don't think it has to go that high if you, a rank 6+ epheral entity is plenty strong to force everyone to come together, but so is an elder vampire or a particularly powerful Mummy or Mage. Heck, you could even do a more exotic threat like a Pangean. You probably want at least 2 as was mentioned above but I think 3 is better, a third antagonist faction brings a lot of depth and options, but I think more than that can get too muddy.

                          I'm surprised no one brought up The Contagion Chronicle yet, so here's me advising you check that and the Player Guide for good directions. Beast is controversial, but it's storytelling chapter and Crossover discussion in Chapter 5 is also useful.

                          You can come up with just about any argument that you want for how the group gets together-while each splat's metaphysics usually does a lot to immerse them in their corner of reality, monsters of various sorts do collide and run into each other reasonably a fair amount, and those bumps and stumbles can lead to odd friendships and communal goals-but it's worth noting that the reason your player characters get together is gonna set a lot of the tone for the chronicle. A group of monsters who gathered in the same apartment because they didn't run into anyone else from their corner and just rely and trust in each other is gonna be out for a different Chronicle set from a group of monsters on a military drive to kill God(whatever they think that is).

                          As for what to include, it again depends on what the overarching goal and gameplay style for the game is going to be, but a good thing to keep in mind for designing opposition is that you need scaled opposition that plays into the strengths of the characters. I primarily bring this up because it can be intimidating to bring in the Arisen, the Awakened, and the Unchained into a crossover game, (and depending on your players, you might not have to), but by giving them issues and opponents that can really hamper everyone else if they're not on the ball with it, you can allow them to act like giant trees in rainstorms for their group, handling issues that they feel rewarded for wrestling with while creating a space for smaller scaled players and opposition to work. Put another way, if you imagine everyone else as chess pieces, Mummies, mages, and demons work to make sure the board doesn't swallow them or change on them.

                                  Originally posted by Psicose View Post
                                  Also, can anyone give a tip on using mummy as a rival?
                                  As an antagonist that eventually needs to be beaten sort of rival, or as a sometimes pain in the ass, sometimes ally sort of rival?

                                  In either case, a mummy (be they Arisen, Deceived, or Shuankhsen) are pretty good mid-chronicle twists for a narrative, and generally for a narrative on the whole with early bits of a chronicle conflicting against a mummy's cult, which is already a good opportunity to introduce unique antagonists and future supportive characters between sorcerers, relic-wielding specialists, ghosts and spirits(Particularly if you keep some of the kickstarter text on Utterances that kept more of an eye on spirits), Imbued individuals, Deviants, and the odd other splat to come in-the Judge(/temakh/Ammut) and Guild of the mummy in question gives you a direction and way to understand they're interacting with the chronicle without outright defining what they're about, and that variety makes for a great excuse to have a diverse, stand out cast of antagonists who can change over the game.

                                  The middle of a chronicle becomes a great time to then summon the said Mummy, because the height of the mummy's power lets them radically change the nature of the game with their farther reaching powers, and their individual power can lead to a lot of tense conflict-a mummy awakening is great fodder for a Knight of Cerebus moment, and can become a wrecking ball to any complacency or reliance in the setting and developing narrative, and that in turn can incentivize the other major players of the setting to accelerate their projects or shift goals. By this point, you can reasonably figure out what the mummy's and cult's goals and main modus operandi are, so you can firm up all the details you left lying around into a direction you need to to make the game what you need it to be. And, of course, you don't need to worry too much about what happens if they knock out the mummy (or their Sadikh/Fasad, if you're employing one). Awakening a mummy is, in short, just a great time to really start the climax and to make a memorable shift in the story, the sort of thing players are likely to be talking about for years.

                                  And in the close of the game, the Mummy's waning power and increased interest in Memory allows for you get a catharsis in a wide variety of ways. If you want to go with a pure antagonist, the cult's weariness from running this entire time is likely in place, they are a lot easier to put down, if not in a position to go down for an extended, taken out of the chronicle death cycle, and the increase in Memory also means that your players have more avenues to get at them. All of those factors also lead well into diplomatic or change of heart sort of answers as well, too, and in a game that's leading up to it's final conflict, getting the long experience and occult knowledge (and even occult power, just because they can't destroy a city anymore doesn't mean that low tier Utterances and Affinities aren't potent tools all the same) can be a massive boon against final antagonists.

                                  Mummies, of all the varieties, make for good rivals primarily because they have so many ways they can come and get into the fray, and the nature of working through cults and the way Memory can change the game means they have a lot of versatility in how they can complicate or drive conflicts. Their Power is always a great way to shock the game, and whether you introduce it in the middle, beginning, or end, it's bound to be a really rewarding way to set the story.

                                  The biggest problem with them, and the reason why I opt for mid-chronicle, is that it can be easy for a Mummy to be overwhelming-you want to pay attention to what the cast of player characters have and make sure you don't build your mummy in such a way that they can't escape it and live to fight it another day. That's true of any antagonist you design to come up multiple times in a game, but because mummies do start off with an assumption of power that is reasonably part of the reason you'd want to use them for, you want to be particularly careful on that. By the middle of a chronicle, the PC's should be empowered enough to have plenty of answers, and certainly by the end of a Chronicle that'll be the case, so you don't have to worry about it, but if you start the chronicle with a mummy awakening and doing their shit, it pays to give that attention.

                                  Anything more might need more details.

                                                      The last game i ran i required one thing of my players (during character creation they all had to be anti-war) and then I let them come up with any background they wanted to explain why they were anti-war (though one ended up as an arms dealer) it worked because this one thing in common brought the characters together, but they had control over the details...

And it is more of a collaborative storytelling approach).
