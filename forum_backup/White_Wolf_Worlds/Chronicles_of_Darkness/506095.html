    The Unaligned.

    05-06-2015, 05:44 PM
    I personally enjoy being unaligned. My current game the unaligned are pretty much just dead corpses (hehe) walking who will die of. They're are no unaligned elders or any of kind interaction with them. I'm well aware this is just the story tellers preference and am not complaining but i am just curious how everyone else views and treats them in thier own games. Hopefully we can have a nice long talk about them since not much is really written about them.

      Generally the unaligned become marginalized unless they have some kind of individual edge. If they do have an edge it basically just lets them keep existing without joining one of the big organizations, and they're well advised to stay off the radar. They'll never have any real clout or power, and if they try to tangle with those who do, it won't end well for them.

      Maybe they have good reasons not to get involved in covenant politics, but the price you pay for that autonomy is lack of support.

      ETA: Another way you could run them is by having a large number of unaligned come together to form a sort of local mini-covenant, separate from the big ones.

                  As far as I've been able to tell from experience, there are two kinds of unaligned.

                  The first is the undecided, who just is biding their time before joining, stalling for various reasons, or hasn't figured out the point yet. They usually join one eventually. Not much to say about them other than they're usually neonates who haven't seen how vicious the world is yet.

                  The second kind are the rejects. Either no one wants them, or they're the ones turning their back on the covenants. While the former would be happy to be part of a new organization (if they survive that long), the latter are more keen on going it alone - they often don't want to be part of an organization, the whole reason they went unaligned in the first place!

                  Unaligned, of the second kind, are generally lone powers (or a cotorie of lone powers) that set up a little kingdom and have turned their back on the All Night Society. They got their herd, their ghouls, their sires and childer and sisters and brothers, and let the rest of the world burn so long as it doesn't cross their territory's borders.

                  There are exceptions, of course, but that's been my experience.

                          I think the Covenants might actually see some benefit in keeping a few unaligned vampires around. Sure they'll be marginalized and mistrusted for the most part, but that could also translate into a certain degree of invisibility. If one covenant can blackmail, threaten, or otherwise cajole one (or more) of the unaligned into acting against their rivals, then they've effectively got a secret weapon that no one is likely to see coming. Better yet, there's little to link them back to the party instigating the attack, particularly if they've concealed their true identity from the unaligned (which would also be an effective means of avoiding betrayal or retribution).

                          Of course, pulling something like this would probably only work once or twice (at least with any given unaligned individual), since the target of their attack is likely to flag them as an enemy and pay closer attention from that point on. The unaligned would therefore be pretty much expendable, and things aren't likely to end well for them regardless of how successful they are. Figuring out how to escape this kind of situation could make for a pretty cool scenario.

                                          Some bloodlines have their own organizational structure as well, making them technically like mini-covenants unto themselves. One example that comes to mind (since I've spent an inordinate amount of time trying to update them for use in 2nd ed) are the California Xiao from Bloodlines: The Chosen, who are organized as a cult, though one more similar to Heaven's Gate or the Peoples Temple than one of the sects making up the Circle of the Crone. The Kuufukuji are another example, carrying on a communal, monastic existence mostly apart from kindred politics.

                                          Speaking of monastic sects, the Sakadagami are sort of a mini-Ordo Dracul built around a vampire-focused school of Buddhism, who achieve results similar to the Coils of the Dragon through specialized exercises and intense meditation. As a group, they only really seem to exist in Thailand (and possibly only in Bangkok, at least in any significant numbers), but I could easily see re-imagining the Kuufukuji as a similar sect inspired by the teachings of Thai masters.

                                                          Some people just aren't that much into networking, others may want to socialize but resent authority or overly strict rules and so on - how many people are actively political in day-to-day life?

                                                          Yeah, kindred might have a greater active need of those things than your average human being due to their peculiarities but also suffer greater pressure to resent some aspects of it - so overall i actually treat unaligned as pretty common, invisible, mostly silent when not manipulated but ever present "kindred masses", undead monsters that interact among each other in a coterie or convenient neighborhoods basis, sometimes surprisingly human in their behaviour due to involving themselves with far more mortals (or the occasional small cliques of other supernaturals) than the than kindred, specially those of the "big parties" in their city.

                                                          How many districts of your own city do you know very well or are very invested in on a daily (or nightly) basis?
