                                              I'll admit, we can't lean too heavily on in-character explanations to evaluate the subject, given some of the external context. "Atlantis" as a choice for the magical precursor probably ought to be considered against the real world sentiments that led to it being popularised as such a thing in both fiction and pseudohistory in the first place. And the Pentacle Orders might also still slant a bit in that direction in some of their ideas and general iconography.

                                              But I'd also say that this was the sort of thing the game was aware about in itself from quite early on, and a lot of efforts were made towards actively redressing that. I do think that in some early books like Tome of the Mysteries, there's a certain tone of "okay, I know how this looks, but just hear me out". A comprehensive look does give a more rounded out picture as well as the tools to go even further on one's own initiative, but I wouldn't blame (or downvote) anybody for looking at some of the stuff presented at the surface level and coming around with questions.

                                                  I find it interesting that no one has mentioned how the clarification of 2nd Edition came in parallel to the Dark Eras of To The Strongest, at least overtly. The big reason for the mention is that it was properly conceiving of the origin of the Diamond Sect as both the oldest still standing sect for the main game, and in tying it's foundation to the Hellenistic World, a lot of the game design and terminology came into focus.

                                                  TO be particular about this, while the unified Greek nation has it's influences on the Diamond in design, and consequently for much of the game as a design flow from there, the majority of it's initial foundation is rooted in the Middle East and the Indian sub-continent and the peoples thereof, represented by the Persian Karpani, the Egyptian Weret-Hekau Hemka, and the Indian Mantra Sadhaki-from the evolution and methodology from this determination and how it affected 2nd Edition's development, that alone would incline the game towards more a Middle Eastern/Eastern influence.

                                                  To compound matters is the fairly quick addition post Dark Eras's initial pitches and Awakening 2nd's release was the stretch goal for Princes of the Conquered Lands, centering on the Shona Awakened of South Africa at the beginning of Portuguese colonialism in the area. The goal was an excuse to challenge and reframe the text and set a standard moving forward for the franchise by taking a fairly different and an initially unplanned on worldview and seeing how well the model constructed for Awakening worked with what we know of the Shona people's drastically different approach-Shakespeare in the bush but with wizards-but then allowing what came out of the clashes to reshape Awakening going forward by letting the Shona warp what was constructed for Awakening, rather than try and cram the Shona into Awakening's model. Awakening's archetypal model with it's already non-Eurocentric outlook actually handled that pretty well, but the experiment compounded on the design decisions made from the non-Eurocentric approach of the core, and curved idle details further away from such models, which would see expression in the nitty gritty in the gameplay mechanics of the Signs of Sorcery(rooted in decisions connected to Tome of the Pentacle, about to be discussed), and in the in-game history by increasing the cultivation of the Diamond (again, used as the the main reason for most of the dialect Awakening due to it's age and prominence) to make the next major layers of Great Cults to contribute to that core ideology and vocabulary being the Chinese Fang Shih, Eblaite(Syrian) Maskim, and the Judean Synhedrion in, as promised, the Tome of the Pentacle.

                                                  Tl;dr up to this point, it would be more accurate to call Awakening Middle Eastern-centric, with it's in game decisions reflecting choice to move away from eurocentric models.

                                                  That said, there is a point where there's no denying that the Grecian infleunce can't be ignored-Brookshaw himself once described the core of the game as Neo-Platonism by way of Theosophy-and a lot of Eurocentrism roots itself in the Grecian and Italian Pennisulas. But on the other hand, the game is designed for a mostly "Western" audience in mind, on account that White Wolf and then Onyx Path having a lot of their reach limited to those regions, and that audience is inclined to better understand Neo-Platonic thought rather than the workings of Hindi religious and occult practices. And to be honest, to have even the core shy away from it and to have it's ongoing path be rooted in distinctly non-European beliefs and occult practices really pays out.

                                                      Chronicles of Darkness, and pretty much every Storyteller derived game, is Americentric even down to the presumed availability of firearms. 2e is much better than 1e was, and 1e was nowhere near WW's worst example (fuck the Scion 1e weapon table only listing specific models of forearm), but it's still there. Anything suggesting vampires can't easily move between cities probably doesn't take into account how densely populated Europe can be.

                                                      Which is fine, the developers are primarily pulling from their own cultures and trying to avoid stereotyping others. The game will never be truly universal even if only due to a limited number of pages.

                                                      As to Atlantis itself being a Eurocentric element, yes but as has been stated it's been massively de-emphasised and turned into the idea of 'the time before' and 'the awakened city' with less specific ties. The idea's still there, but the gnostic elements have taken centre stage. Which I'll point out is still a relatively Eurocentric element, and arguably a bigger deal than Atlantis as it's entwined with the setting's metaphysics and cosmology.

                                                      I'm just hesitant to say that gnosticism is a strictly Eurocentric element as I don't know enough about most religions or philosophies to say if similar ideas crop up regularly all over the place or not.
