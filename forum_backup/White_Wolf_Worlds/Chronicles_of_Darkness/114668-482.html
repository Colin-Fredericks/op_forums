                I'll note that per old statements from Dave Brookshaw as developer, there are two kinds of ghost mages.

                In most cases, it's just the ghost of a mage; they might be unusually cogent and get Influences and Numina reminiscent of their rotes and praxes, but are otherwise nothing special and cannot use Supernal magic.

                In some cases, mages undergo processes to basically transform their living selves into ghosts; they become ephemeral beings and I think still get tied to anchors and the Underworld, but retain the entirety of their consciousness and full range of their powers. This constitutes a form of lich.

                        1, 2 & 5- They have never been officially defined, so you would need to apply some creative thaumaturgy.

                        3- Pneuma is the pure, undiluted energy of the Supernal, before being sublimated into the Fallen World. This is what archmages build their Golden Roads out of. Its not perfected mana, but rather the most purified and raw expression of Supernal energy that exists. Its also one of the rarest, most valuable, nearly legendarily rare magical materials a normal mage could ever get their hands on.

                        4- Since no mage normally has the perspective of all the Watchtowers, that would normally be impossible. Creating an Aedes, especially one that exists in all the known Supernal Realms, would require either one of the most powerful archmages in the modern day or a incredibly rare and dangerous Atlantean device, bleeding edge even by their standards. Its punching way above the weight class of what a final Attainment should be able to accomplish.

                        4.5- You would require very high mastery of both Arcana of that Realm. The spell would be of the Practice of Dominions, which means the caster would need at least 8 dots in both Arcana and the required Quintessence. All that is squarely in the domain of archmages.

                                                            Minor Disclaimer: I haven't played the game yet, so I don't know if this works yet.
                                                            Wisdom is the measure of control a mage has over their magic. It is threatened by acts that show lack of control (duh). A mage actually loses Wisdom by justifying their actions, telling themselves that what they are doing is Necessary, or Right, or Just. " I did _____ because it ________ and that makes it ok" . You probably know this. The reason I bring it up is because I think the time to make the roll is variable. A saintly mage doing their chores by magic in order to save time for study probably makes the roll right away. They have already justified their action. A warrior that makes a few bad calls during a battle probably makes the roll at the end of the Scene. A mage so driven by Obsession that they are hanging by a thread that finally snaps should roll Immediately. The examples go on. You can pick on or the other, but I think a hard and fast rule here makes you lose something. It's when the mage has time and the opportunity to think about their actions that Wisdom falters. Premeditated actions should always roll Immediately in my opinion, they have had time to think about it before hand.
