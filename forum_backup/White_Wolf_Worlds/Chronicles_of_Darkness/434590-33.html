            Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
            Lots to reply too here


            While the primary themes of Beast and Leviathan are very different (and honestly. I have no idea what Beast's primary themes are meant to be) both include themes of family, and themes of "the primordial part of the human mind".

            But Beast's idea of family are people you choose, people you like. Leviathan's are people you're stuck with because you've got no one else. Beasts are trying to help people reconnect with the primordial knowledge they've lost. Leviathan's are primordial instincts trying to modernise.

            It seems obvious that rather than trying to focus on Biblical Tragedy and whatever Beast's core theme is one should focus on those shared points and the contrasts between them. I think you could do something interesting with the idea that Beasts run around trying to get monsters to embrace their monsterousness and help them hunt but take one look at Leviathans and go "okay... maybe it's best for everyone if you keep on rejecting your monsterousness". I'd consider writing rules for "overfeeding", where a Beast's Saitity goes past 10 and they have to somehow "vomit it up" for their own health. The sort of thing you might get if a Ravager witnesses a Leviathan forcing an entire town to kill each other.

            BTW. I've previously mentioned that Ocean Minds affects Beast's abilitiy to force a good first impression. That was a rule that assumed you're playing a game of Leviathan: the Tempest that includes Beasts. In a true Crossover game.... I've got no clue how you'd handle the conflicting ideas of family.
            Actually, one of Beast primary themes is that You Don't Choose Your Family. Even their Kin mechanics works wether the Beast and their Kin are friends or foes, as long as they're important to each other. Also, Beasts are great team players thanks to Mother's Kiss.

            Another theme is about being true to themselves, and not neglect part of their nature wether it is their monstrous or human side, especially since both kinds of neglect are likely to get Heroes on the Beast's trail. Beasts aren't so much about pushing toward monstrosity than about accepting that part of themselves or of their family. Beasts tell their monstrous Kin it's ok to exist the way they are. More, they tell them that they belong to the same great family. It doesn't mean they'll get along, but the Beasts won't judge.

            Many Beasts think of themselves as the children of the Dark Mother which some associate with Tiamat, which could either bring them closer to the Tribe or bring them into conflict. Beasts are also often clannish and territorial which may lead them to either confrontation or alliance. Also, a Leviathan's Wake could generate chambers and affect the Hive dramatically, attracting the attention of the Begotten. Some Beasts, mostly Makaras, may also share the aquatic proclivities of Leviathans and share this aspect of their existences as few can. An aquatic lair might also be a good place for a Leviathan to retreat from the world for a while. The Makara's Vampire Stereotype comes to mind:
            "You don't drown. My place, then?"
            A Begotten's Hunger could also either create links or frictions: A Leviathan would have no troble keeping a Beast well fed thanks to Family Banquet, but a Beast could also decide to feed from an Atoll, a Beloved or even from a Leviathan.


            When will you rage?

                                                        Changing topic. Here's The Wake, based on Vent0's ideas. And a picture of a Beloved Priestess I found on DeviantArt because it seems fitting.





                                                        The Wake

                                                        The Wake is the term used by Leviathans for what is, in effect, the sensation that their divine nature produces on normal humans. It is a psychic pressure front that humans feel on an instinctual level - it is the dread majesty of a Leviathan's birthright. Tribal scholars claim that it is the "shadow" cast by the Leviathan's true form, and it affects a region around him, which grows as the Leviathan's Sheol increases.

                                                        Mortals affected by a Leviathan's Wake are infused with a mixture of awe, terror, and deference. For all that this seems attractive, it is a deeply isolating effect. A Leviathan can't enter a room without people lowering their voices, or go to the grocery store without people self-consciously apologizing for getting in your way (even if they weren't) - the Wake functions as a psychological battering ram, preventing the Tribe from blending in with a crowd. A Leviathan is always, willingly or not, intimidating every mortal in his presence. The Wake is a beacon, a message from the days of the Primordial that impresses itself on human minds - "You're less important than me; everything here is mine if I want it."

                                                        The allure of the Wake, especially for Tribe members who might have been socially awkward or bullied before they awoke to their true natures, is obvious. However, the Wake is also extremely dangerous - taking advantage of it means taking advantage of humans, further alienating the Leviathan from the world he has to live in. Many Leviathans have reveled in the Wake until it Eroded their own minds or they created so many enemies that word reached the Marduk Society.

                                                        Mortals subject to the Wake are aware that they are cowed but will, without training or knowledge, put it down to the Leviathan having unusual charisma or presence. They get the sense that the Leviathan just happens to be the sort of person who the room stops for. The Wake also does not alter a subject's memory, only their current ability to act. Their recollection of events during their exposure will therefore be tinted by their own nature and desires, and how the Leviathan acted towards them. If the Leviathan used intimidation to browbeat them into doing what he wanted, they will recall being intimidated and will attempt to justify their response in a fashion that seems consistent. A timid person, for instance, will assume that they gave way as would be expected, while one that resents being pushed around might formulate reasons why they would permit it. This tendency can be a blessing or a curse - if a person's mental scenario elevates the Leviathan's actions to a criminal offense, they may very well get in touch with the appropriate authorities. A member of the Tribe could breeze into a high-security facility, but the consequences may yet follow them home.

                                                        Some mortals are more drastically affected by the Wake - these unfortunates are struck with a lingering obsession with the Leviathan, hovering between a dangerous mixture of strong emotions. Unaware of the truth of what they are experiencing, they find ways of explaining it - some outlets more constructive than others. The only unifying element of this obsession is that the mortal is certain that there is something different - something special and unique and equal parts horrible and wondrous - about the Leviathan. If they have not met the Leviathan, they instead become convinced that there is something unique, horrible, and wondrous nearby. In isolation these Beloved end in one of two ways - either the mortal breaks free of the Wake, or the mortal takes ever larger risks as their obsession grows until their complete self-destruction.

                                                        It is only in a collective that Beloved can find some measure of stability and satisfy their unique needs. While a canny Beloved might attract followers of their own and create an outlet in the sharing of ideas and speculation; perhaps learning genuine occult lore and becoming a danger to themselves and the Leviathan they obsess over. Most long term Beloved either group up with others like themselves or enter the Leviathan's service. However the group forms, the psychology of Beloved ensures that its internal culture will be markable unhealthy. With their obsession over a divine Leviathan, the only appropriate word is a Cult.

                                                        A Cult serves as extensions of the Leviathan, his agents in the human world, permitting him to avoid the strain of everyday life. It can infuse the Leviathan with Ichor through reverence and the enactment of mystical rites - their subservience reaffirming his divine nature. Beloved are also prime hosts for the generation of Lahmasu, as they are willing, perhaps even eager, to be touched by the bloodline of the Tribe.

                                                        With the psychological isolation of the Wake, the lure of staying secluded and meeting only with one's Cult is strong, especially for older, more world-weary Leviathans. This is a potential trap, as interacting with the Beloved is not a substitute for interacting with normal humans, and it is often the ultimate fate of the hidden Cult leader to fall into degeneration, isolated from his own human side.

                                                        Mechanics of the Wake

                                                        The Eye of the Storm

                                                        Not everyone is affected by the Wake.
                                                        • Leviathans, other divine beings, Atolls, and Ahabs are always immune to the Wake.
                                                        • Characters whose Supernatural Tolerance is two or more lower than the Sheol of the Leviathan are vulnerable to that Leviathan's Wake, but cannot become Beloved. They are otherwise immune.
                                                        • For resisting the Wake, Marduk agents and Empyrean Theurgists have an effective Supernatural Tolerance equal to twice their highest Virtue.
                                                        • Characters who have powers that defend against mental effects may use those powers in place of their Supernatural Tolerance.
                                                        • All others are fully affected.

                                                        The Storm

                                                        People within the wake and able to perceive the Leviathan with their natural or psychic senses face a heightened sense of dread, anticipation, but also eager curiosity. Those who spend a significant amount of time within the Storm, or have a conversation more than a quick hello remain within the Storm until they next wake from sleep.
                                                        In the storm all Integrity Breaking Point rolls take a penalty equal to Leviathan’s Sheol. Mortals, Hybrids and Lahmasu who roll a Dramatic Failure on a Breaking Point roll may become Beloved. If their Integrity is less than (4 + half Sheol) before rolling they may become Beloved on a regular Breaking Point Failure

                                                        Sidebar: Mad Oracles

                                                        When a psychic has prophetic dreams, visions, or some other out of body experience they count as though they were physically standing at their viewpoint for the purposes of the Wake.

                                                        This means that a lucky, or unlucky, Leviathan could turn someone Beloved without ever going near them. A psychic only has to have a vision of where the Leviathan is, was, or might be (the future in the World of Darkness is never certain).
                                                        A Leviathan’s Ripples are as effective at catching the attention of psychics as anything else.

                                                        The Fringes

                                                        People within the Leviathan's Wake but unable to perceive the Leviathan through natural or psychic senses are deemed to be within the Fringes. They feel a vague sense of dread, anticipation, and eager curiosity.

                                                        In the Fringes all Integrity Breaking Point rolls take a penalty equal to half the Leviathan’s Sheol (round up). Mortals, Hybrids and Lahmasu who roll a Dramatic Failure on a Breaking Point roll may become Beloved.

                                                        Divine Rights

                                                        When interacting with those affected by the Wake, a Leviathan cannot hold back from intimidating everyone around them anymore than an eagle could avoid intimidating a rabbit. Any social interaction the Leviathan makes automatically counts as Forcing Doors.

                                                        If the Leviathan spends a point of Ichor to intentionally focus the Wake they gain Hard Leverage and open a number of Doors equal to half Sheol (round up), however barring unusual circumstances this automatically inflicts a Breaking Point roll.

                                                        A Leviathan may focus the Wake and use other Hard Leverage in combination to open additional doors, or they may focus the Wake to cause a Breaking Point without social interaction or even speaking to the victim. It’s not that rare for Leviathans to simply hammer a person’s Integrity until they become Beloved, are lost to madness, or turn into an Ahab.
