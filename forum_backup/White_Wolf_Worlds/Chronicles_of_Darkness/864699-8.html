            Originally posted by Diggs View Post
            A story should have a beginning, a middle, and an end.
            Good advice for writing, but for RPGs specifically, I find it more useful to say that a story/adventure should have a motivation, a structure, and a resolution. The beginning, middle, and end are what happen when player characters interact with the afformentioned elements.

            Originally posted by Diggs View Post
            A chronicle is the whole thing so it ends when you're done with the characters. It begins and ends with the chronicle being everything between. To use a movie example, The Lord of the Rings trilogy is a story that is part of a chronicle including the Hobbit movies.
            I disagree. You can run multiple chronicles/campaigns in the same setting and/or involving the same characters, as long as they have different overarching narratives. To use another beloved film series as an example, look at Star Wars. Each Star Wars film is a compete story, including a beginning, a middle, and an end, and each stands on its own as a narrative experience. They had to, with three years between each film - if New Hope hadn't been satisfying outside the context of the trilogy, no one would have come back for Empire, especially not after such a long wait. The trilogy overall is a complete chronicle. It tells a series of stories about the same set of characters in the same setting, connected by a common narrative - the Rebels' struggle against the Empire.

            The prequel trilogy is another Chronicle in the same setting, involving many of the same characters, but with a different narrative - Anakin's fall to the Dark Side. The individual films were complete stories, as well. Less well-written stories than their predecessors, but complete stories nonetheless. The new trilogy is yet another chronicle set in the same universe, which brings back many of the characters from the previous two chronicles, but features another new narrative. And then there are the side films that are coming out. Standalone stories in the same setting, but starring different characters and featuring different narratives.

                                Originally posted by ChroniclesOfDarkness2e
                                • Story — A story tells an entire tale, following the dramatic arc of a related series of events. It might be comprised of several chapters or be completed in just one. It has an introduction, rising tension, a number of twists, and a climax that brings things to a conclusion.
                                • Chronicle — The big picture; a chronicle is the collection of interlinked stories that involve your characters. They might be linked by a common theme or overarching plotline, or they may only share characters and locations. As your story progresses, the players and Storyteller work together to create an ongoing chronicle.
                                A rigid definition won't fit all possibilities. Technically, the Tolkien stuff is one chronicle and all the Star Wars movies are one chronicle and the MCU is one chronicle. They are so massive they likely would never be completed as a gaming experience so that is a logical problem with the example. Not possible to define a subjective experience in objective terms. You give an example, I give an example. I don't know if that creates less confusion or more, but the point should still be clear that a Chronicle is what a group makes of it. No rule requires a Chronicle to have more than one story or less than a 1000 stories. Your D&D example was fine but I don't know who is familiar. Beyond that, imprecision is a certainty.
