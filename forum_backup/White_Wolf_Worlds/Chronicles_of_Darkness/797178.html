    Oneiromancy

    12-31-2015, 12:19 AM
    Suppose a changeling is unlucky enough to get targeted by a rampaging horror one night and gets his dream self pulled into a lair. Also, suppose this changeling is skilled in Oneiromancy/dream manipulation. Naturally the changeling is going to decide he doesn't want to have a nightmare tonight and tries to make it a more pleasing dream. What happens at this point? My storyteller and I have been pondering this for a while, just some theory crafting while we wait for mage, and we can't really come up with a clear answer. We're pretty sure the horror should be able to resist any attempts at modifying its lair but other than that we've got nothing.

                  The Horror leaves her Lair to visit other's dreams, not pull others into her Lair. Furthermore, the way that Changelings deal with dreams is idfferent than visiting through the Astral. Mundane dreams aren't part of the Temnos / Primordial Dream, despite the name. Two completely different regions. Furthermore, proper dreams are kind of a border area that touch on a lot of different regions, and are not quite inside individual Onerios either.

                  In short, what you're doing simply doesn't have rules or expectations with it. The Astral has never been shaped like a dream, and I don't expect it to change with 2e rules. As things stand, the Fae cannot use their oneriomancy inside the Astral proper. They can only do it in the areas where dreams touch on the Hedge / Faerie. If a changeling is pulled entirely into the Temnos, then they cannot dreamshape.


                  Even in Mage, there was a clear demarkation between dreams and the Astral. You can use a simple Mind 3 spell ( I think it was 3) to enter a dream. You need other ways to enter the Astral proper, such as a Hallow or Artifact. Its a common misunderstanding to assume they're linked. They're not. Dreams are kind of a borderland that touch on so many different regions.

                                      Changelings don't see the Oneiros. They see the [Redacted] connected in the Skein.

                                      As Brookshaw has pointed out, the Skein in general is this high floating level that blends between simple dreams and the first layer of the Astral. Changelings regularly walk around the Skein, this amalgamation of the two, and regularly futz around with it, pulling off deep tricks that mages and other creature would simply have to go deeper for, and also have greater mobility than most in that sphere-but a changeling's power extends to the powerful inner worlds of sentiment in each person, not in the that weird mass where people lose individuality called the Temenos. The Dark Dream beyond that, where humanity as a species on the whole interacts with the world, is something kind of beyond them.

                                      As it stands now, as far as canon goes, changelings just lack the infrastructure to effectively move what Beast's deal with. if their Horror is appropriating a dream to get their feeding done, a changeling wandering that dream is going to find it too heavy to shift. Heaven help them if they end up in the Lair.

                                      Now, as house rules go, I'd still let them try, and they even have a sort of edge, but we'll come back to that. Oneiromancy can be done, but with penalties, equal to Satiety if it's a Beast in another person's dream, Lair if they're in the Lair. This is just a quick and fast thing, and I wouldn't keep to that. The advantage that changelings have, though, is that they can actively leverage things, they can act and change. A Beast is too bound to their Horror and Lair to do so, so they never get to contest.

                                      One other thing? A changeling, while dreamwalking, is as powerful as they always are. The Beast, in the meantime, has moments of power and moments of weakness. They flux in ways changelings don't. That can be an advantage or a disadvantage for the changeling, depending where they are at.
