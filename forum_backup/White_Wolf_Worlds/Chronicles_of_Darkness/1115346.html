    Can a mage cast a spell while being possessed by a Spirit?

    07-12-2017, 11:15 PM
    Hey everyone,

    I was wondering. I have a mage that got possessed by a spirit, for the sake a roleplay I let the mage be conscious and talk to the spirit.

    Now the Mage is asking if he is able to cast a spell while possessed, more specifically exorcism or something similar.

    I am inclined to say yes... but perhaps with major penalties. What do you think? It worries me this might set a precedent that Possession is easy to circumvent...

      With Possession, 'Living hosts are put into a coma-like state while being possessed.' Mages can't cast spells in comas.

      Obviously you're free to do what you want, but allowing mages to cast spells like exorcism while possessed is going to severely weaken what possession does.

          Aaaaactually, even in a coma, the situation is not quite as certain as all that. If the mage's soul can engage, it can still cast a spell. You are generally in some deep weirdness and storyteller territory, of course, but for mages who surf alternate states of consciousness regularly, it starts to become more likely that they are able to function than not.

                  Since you've already houseruled possession we'd have to do an evaluation of the situation. Since spells come into being from imagos, and imagos are formed mentally, a mage that is conscious should always be able to cast a spell (unless something is specifically messing around with the mage's ability to cast spells). However, the only Yantra the mage could reasonably employ are those that doesn't require physical control of your body. Only places, concentration (though the spirit might be able to interrupt the concentration by a contested roll of some kind) and Persona could be used as Yantras. Additionally, I happen to recall that casting a spell in a grapple will levy a -3 dice penalty to the spellcasting roll if the mage loses the grappling roll due to being overpowered by the opponent. Since the mage is quite clearly overpowered by the spirit that penalty could apply in this situation.

                  I'm assuming the mage is a PC so I get not wanting to remove all agency from a player, however the mind is the most powerful weapon a mage has so it was a bit brash. But you could solve that problem by allowing the spirit to control whether or not the victim is conscious. If the spirit realises the mage is starting to cast a spell and can reflexively put the mage in a coma-like state as a response then you functionally allow the player some agency while removing the huge problem it'd otherwise cause.

                      I'd think the answer is usually no, but with a certain caveat. A mage typically needs to be able to think to cast magic, so in general, a mage who is possessed cannot think and therefore can't cast. But, IIRC in a previous discussions about cutting mages off from thinking/using their magic, it was stated that while it might work for a while, because magic is just as much an expression of the soul you'd more likely than not turn them into something like one of the Mad. Without the ability to consciously control their magic, their magic would instead eventually become completely unshackled and act purely in accordance with their Obsessions.

                      I would assume that eventually, without a guiding consciousness to control their use of magic, they would begin using magic without restraint in order to fulfill the mages Obsessions, without regard for what the spirit possessing them would want and beyond that spirits ability to control. It would almost certainly end horribly for everyone involved.

                                  If a mage can think, the mage can cast spells. That said?

                                  Originally posted by Casting During a Grapple
                                  Mages can cast spells in a single turn without making any outward sign. A mage in a grapple (whether he initiated it or not) can cast a spell whether he wins the grapple check for the turn or not, but suffers a –3 penalty to the spellcasting roll if he lost. Mages in a grapple cannot employ any Yantras involving movement, but if the grapple was initiated part-way through casting a spell, any Yantras the caster has already spent turns incorporating into the spell have their full effect.
                                  Given we're talking about possession, and not just grappling, I'd inflict a full -5 penalty. Couple that with the spirit's Withstand and the almost definite lack of Yantra (I'd allow a rote and concentration, but no mantra, mudra, place or tools. Or ritual bonus from long time casting). See if the spell can go through. If yes, the mage "fights off the possession." If not? The magus is still trapped. Then, if the mage fails, have the spirit "punish" the mage in some way for fighting back and see if that discourges them. Maybe do the drop-unconcious thing.

                                          I don't think the mage can cast spells in the Material world while unconscious... Whether they can spells while within their own Oneiros* while possessed would be a judgement call if that type of unconsciousness allows dreams. If so, then interesting weirdness, if not then frightening helplessness.

                                          As a ST I'd make the call based on the themes of the game;

                                          A Protagonist Empowerment Fantasy style would justify choosing that the possessed do indeed dream and thus doesn't rob all power, just adding a layer to the hero's journey which includes delving within.

                                          Darker Lovecraftian cosmic horror and existential dread would totally make choosing helplessness the best choice.

                                          Both are valid but once the judgement call is made it should remain consistent, they don't mix well unless you want players to feel betrayed about the loss of their Empowerment Fantasy they bought into.

                                          *=And thus have a chance to either face the possessing entity within the Oneiros, or escape their dream via Astral travel and magic shenanigans to otherwise get at their possessor, perhaps possessing another, find a way to manifest in the material from the Astral, or other out of the box solutions.

                                                  Is the mage permitting the use of his body, or is the spirit possessing him without the will of the mage? The reason I ask, is because if the mage is the willing participant, he can utilize shielding spells to keep his mind conscious when his body is possessed by another's will - among other things. Utilizing such a spell, I would grant no penalties to spellcasting from things like grappling and other things. Now - I totally understand they are -already- possessed, but I figured I'd mention it just in case for your player (as if it's a case of 'I didn't take precautions' there is a lesson to be learned! :P)

                                                  If, however, the mage is not a willing individual then I would probably not allow the casting. There is nothing in particular stopping a mage from casting in the Onieros - except, that if I'm not mistaken such an endeavor would require external factors (a hallow, demesne, iris) to allow the mage to be there. Since a mage is not supposed to be conscious to begin with, and they were not in such an environment - the mage may need to rely on his allies to bail him out... or, since this is slightly houseruled, convince the spirit to go to a suitable location where they can reach their onieros - which could be good for story

                                                          From my understanding, it depends on the mage's consiousness, subconsious (oneiros) and the spell's range of effect. All the cases assume that the mage was unprepared of the possession
                                                          a) The possessed mage looses consiousness but the possessing entity has extra powers to block even his subconsious. Such a mage has effectively no oneiros (or a "frozen" oneiros) and is completelly helpless for the duration of the possession (usually a scene and remember it costs the entity 3 Essence each time), unable to cast any spells of his own. This is a rare/powerful kind of possession fiting for the aforementioned Lovercraftian horrors and other God-like entities (the Kings Above).
                                                          b) The possessed mage looses consiousness but he has access to his subconsious and thus his oneiros (under my understanting all mages have access to their Oneira and only need places of power to start Astral Paths beyond them. Alternatively, assume the entity happened to stay for some time in a place alowing Astral Travel). Upon entering his oneiros the mage can either target himself or (as mentioned above) search the repressentation of the possessing force in his mind and target that instead. Both with the right combination of spells (target self to teleport near creature's bane, mark own flesh with letters asking for help, trap the entity into an oneiric prison, oreiricly blast it out, etc) can help with the situation directly or otherwise.
                                                          Note: I don't remember where I read it, but it was mentioned that while inside your Oneiros you can cast spells on your physical self and anything inside your mind (and anything inside your mind has a representation in your oneiros)
                                                          c) The possessed mage somehow manages to remain consious. Though rare, people with Legacies or experienced Mind casters can manage it. In such a weird case, not only can they cast in the physical world regardless of the entity's possession but are possibly entitled to a clash of wills for each action against their will.
