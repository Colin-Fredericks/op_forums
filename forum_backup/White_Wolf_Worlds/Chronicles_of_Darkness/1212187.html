    Brainstorming for a Dark Era about the birth of Psychoanalysis

    04-27-2018, 05:47 AM
    Hey there.

    I've been thinking for a while about a Dark Era focused on the birth of psychoanalysis. Namely, one that's mostly about Freud, Jung, the revolution their theories caused and that plays with monsters, mental health, dreams and the clash between supernatural and science, all seasoned with the mood and events of late 19th-early 20th century Central Europe. Mostly focused on Austria and Switzerland for obvious reasons, though mention of other places would appear when appropriate. After all, the Era is more based around a scientific movement rather than a series of localized events.

    Like all ideas that won't leave my mind, I made peace with the fact that I'm eventually going to try and write it. It's gonna be really far in the future, since I have a long list of things to do before that (including finishing another Dark Era when Geist comes out) and I have no intention whatsoever to dedicate myself to a lengthy project like that right now, but experience taught me these things require careful planning and research, so I'm starting to gather some toughts. Besides, I have plenty of books about Freud, Jung and psychoanalysis, but I'm gonna need more than that for a Dark Era.

    This brings me here asking for ideas, suggestions and opinions. Truth be told, it will be a Beast Dark Era at the very least, because that's from where the idea came and I think I can do something decent and interesting with the tools and hooks the gameline provides in this context, But, pitch for Beast or not, I'm posting this in the general forum because I could use any sort of comment and because the template for Dark Eras now generally includes more than one gameline. So, I'm open to suggestions.

    What you think this Era should cover? Any sort of elements that can't be missing? Others that should? Which gamelines you'd like for it to include? Basically anything you think can be useful to discuss is welcome. As the title says, this is a brainstorming thread, so there are no wrong replies.

                                  I'd vote for Changeling and Beast as the primary game lines to explore. If you wanted to include more than two, maybe Geist or Mage. I lean in the direction of Changeling and Beast mostly because of their strong connection to Jungian concepts like universal archetypes, dream interpretation and the "shadow self."

                                  There's an aspect of the birth of psychoanalysis that isn't often talked about: When Freud started practicing his talking cure, he was working primarily with one specific group of patients: young women from wealthy families who were experiencing "hysteria" (what's now called "conversion disorder"): the presence of physical symptoms without a physical cause. Through talking with these women and exploring the nature and genesis of their symptoms, he came to a conclusion: that in the overwhelming majority of these cases, the young woman in question was being sexually abused (generally by her father). When he went to publish these findings, he was rebuked by his publisher and told that his conclusion must be faulty, as it was impossible that the well-to-do men of Vienna might sexually abuse their daughters. So, rather than standing his ground, he went back to his office and worked come up with some theory for why young women might -think- they had been sexually abused by their fathers. What he came up with were some of the concepts he is now best known for: ideas like penis envy and the Oedipus complex. So, to sum up, the dark history of psychoanalysis includes a compromise that covered up for sexual abusers and did real harm to psychological practice with women for decades.

                                  When constructing your setting, you might put some thought into what to make of this aspect of the period. It would be easy enough to say that Freud learned something else through his work with symptomatic young women and had to cover it up. Personally, I would say the respectful thing to do would be to leave the history as it is: a chilling example of an educated man covering up the real causes of women's trauma.

                                          As for giving supernatural explanation to human behaviors, I'm generally more of the idea of leaving them as is, so we do agree on that. Not saying in not gonna intertwine things here and there, that's the whole point, but I do believe that real screw-ups and crimes are better done if the responsibilty remains mostly untouched from the supernatural. Reality is already scary and chilling as it is, to deny the truth of the events would be a disservice to both history and readers.

                                          I tend to prefer letting monsters do their own thing and be the one influenced by men (and just then influence in return). And remember that CotD monsters are people before the supernatural template happens. It's a method that served me well when writing about that minefield of horror that's Belgian Congo, and I doubt I'm gonna shy away from Freud's (or anyone else) mistakes and the fault of the society of the time here either.
