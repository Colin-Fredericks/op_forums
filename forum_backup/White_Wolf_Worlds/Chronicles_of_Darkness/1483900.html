    Are Vampires WEAK?

    06-13-2022, 03:50 PM
    There's a thread in the VtM forums about crossovers using V20/Revised editions and someone there mentioned this:

    Vampire PCs are expected to start the game way too "lame" for their kind, as one friend once put it to me "You can't even FEEL like a TRUE vampire with 3 dots in disciplines and everybody around you and their mother is more powerful than you" and I'd say he's not really wrong. The perception is that the PCs in Vampire are so vulnerable and weak that it's really hard not to feel damned while you can't see the sun anymore and still you can barely overcome a normal human in overall strength. Just imagine if Mage PC's started with only 2-3 dots in spheres and/or you had mostly only access to perception powers, it sure would feel different, but instead, Mages start with 6 dots in spheres, they're probably not that impressive for their kind, but still, they can definitely FEEL like real mages, the same thing is true for Werewolves, to start they begin with the powers associated to werewolves from legends, (I'd say even much more so, as werewolves in WoD are clearly superior to the general werewolf from legends, as WoD is the only universe I know where werewolves are the "best fighters in the world") they can change form at will, etc, so they don't really depend on Gifts, Gifts for werewolves are just pluses, now imagine if the only feature starting werewolves PCs had was being vulnerable to silver and then they'd be closer to what the average PC feels when starting a vampire game.
    I know now he's referring to OWoD, but because I'm working on a VtR chronicle where Vampire PCs may (well, eventually will) bump into other monsters, I want to make sure; are Vampires weak? It's one thing to not be a combat train like Uratha or to not have the sheer versatility of Willworkers, but does that really make them "lame?" If I throw the Coterie at Werewolves, Magi, Changelings, etc, is there a likely chance they'll feel outclassed and want to play another splat?

          First or second edition?

          First edition Requiem came at the very beginning of nWoD feeling out where to balance supernatural powers in the context of trying to avoid oWoD's moments of excess and plan ahead to put the main three splats in a comparable ballpark. The end result is that compared to where the gamelines were later on, Requiem vampires felt a little muted and conservatively designed, aside from the customary strengths of White Wolf vampires (the Vinculum was always a potent tool in your pocket) and a few balancing blips that didn't reflect where later rules went (1e Protean gave access to aggravated damage output much more easily than other 1e powersets doled it out).

          Second edition Requiem does a pretty good job of spreading out useful Discipline powers so that even the first dot of a core Discipline feels meaningful and transforms what your character can do, I would say. Stuff like Iron Edict and Face in the Crowd is pretty versatile and effective, and neither of them eat up all three of your starting dots to get.

              I speak mostly from theory here. I only storyguided Mummy, Hunter and Mage. I read as many books I can put my hands on and a lot of Storyteller Vault, I also have SOME experience with Demon, Changeling, Deviant, Werewolf and Vampire from using them as NPCs is the Mage Chronicle.

              With all this information, I really suspect that Vampires are at the bottom of Supernatural Food Chain. At same level of Changelings on my opinion.

              This not make them lame, but make crossover interaction more complex. Mages can do things others splats dream about and Werewolves in combat form are nearly immortal recovering all non-agg wounds per round.

              Vampires thrive on other things and crossover interaction need to highlight this. Mages can't do magic in front of mortals without consequences and Acts of Hubris when a Vampire can use Disciplines in front of a crowd without fear of destroying the space-time. Werewolves on combat form are savages capable of killing friends and can't use it's full capacity outside of combat. Vampires are more reliable and capable of work with fewer limitations.

              This have narrative highlight also. Vampires have more connection with humanity to the point they miss being human. Werewolves, Mages, Changelings and Mummies are less capable to interact because human feel so weird to their understanding and experiences. Mages know the truth and can't care less about questions of souls and logical functions, Werewolves have more understanding of spirits than human society, even Changelings need to be aware of Fae creatures and Clarity, keeping the Freehold working also, instead of create community with other humans. Vampires go to parties, have sex and don't have any other great social impediment than being pseudo-incapable of working at day.

              This, still, is a opinion from theory and not so much experience. Seems like creatures like Geist also have the upsides of Vampire without the downsides, but I don't finish that books yet. But my analysis let me to this conclusion.

                              Lets see, they have the inherent ability to degrade lethal and aggravated damage one tier from most non-supernatural sources. They get a passive bonus to Defense with Celerity and can defend from firearms, this is when they are not using it to interrupt the opponent's flow with precise actions.

                              Then we have Resilience, which is ridiculously powerful. It degrades damage from EVERYTHING for the entire turn for a mere point of vitae. The mage's aggravated damage spell, the werewolf's claws, the 200 people firing squad and the rocket launcher. Plus with enough skill in Vigor, a peak human condition vampire can throw something with enough force that even a vampire will take lethal damage (size 10+ objects) and with more force than being hit by lightning. That will certainly make enough of an impression that foes will reconsider their chances of survival.

                              This is all provided that the vampire even choses to entertain their....guests. Dominate, Nightmare and Majesty can simply shut down targets in combat and are very useful outside of it. The its all a matter of feeding them vitae to start forging a Vinculum. One drink makes "diplomacy" much easier and sets up the second drink, placing a powerful supernatural under their wing.

                              This does not mean they have carte blanche. Sure, mages are much less deadly when taken by surprise. But mages are also dirty cheats and masters of asymmetrical combat. A veteran one will probably have prepared more than a few contingencies on hand at all times or can come up with a spell that seizes the advantage for them. Werewolves may be just as resilient as vampires, but they can be even more ferocious and, unlike vampires, they are designed to master pack tactics, which is a significant force multiplier to an already powerful foe.

                              Vampires still need to strategize and prepare, but they are by no means weak.

                                  There's a thing that bears knowing about vampires that's present in other lines to some degree without as much of an explicit metaphysical focus toward it, which is this: the Requiem is about finding, building, and exploiting a niche. In between struggling with Humanity and hiding from the sun, the thing that vampires do is carve out a space in the local ecosystem (literal or metaphorical) and use the tools they have as unliving feedback loops for distilled Life to allow themselves to carry on in as safe, comfortable, and rewarding a fashion as they prefer.

                                  That means extending their reach, painting as much of the town as they can in their particular shade of red, and generally getting to the point where you have a territory under control enough that where your next meal is coming from isn't a primary concern. From there, you can step up the Maslow hierarchy to pursue more personal goals like communion with Old Gods, or safeguarding cursed relics, or transcending the limits of vampirism, or twisting new ideas from the living to serve the purposes of dead, or just making yourself politically irreplaceable from all the things you bring to the table.

                                  That also means being out of juice has a markedly more direct impact on the Kindred than it does on, say, werewolves or mages or the Created. The downside to having a theoretically infinite ceiling on what you can get under your control with enough blood and servants and allies is that you're the cardinal point of vulnerability in the whole machine you've built, which necessarily requires that vampires be vulnerable in a way mummies and changelings and the Bound are not.

                                  Vampire is arguably the monster game most concerned with inhabiting and twisting the mortal sphere as an entity, and its primary driving impetus for doing so is that undercurrent of hunger and desperation that the Kindred carry through their nights.

                                  (It's also worth noting that a lot of things in ChroD that cause a monster to more closely resemble its legendary basis are failstates — the easiest point of comparison I can provide is that Uratha who tilt their Harmony too high look more like movie werewolves than werewolves who belong to packs and tribes and engage with the Shadow do. The Kindred partake of iconic vampiric strengths and engage in dramatic emotional setpieces because that's the type of game this is, but maintaining Humanity specifically helps keep you from being something physically incapable of crossing a line of salt or routinely gets pincushioned by foot-long wooden spikes instead of being able to credibly pretend to be a a person with vampirism, and the slow slide down the ladder with age is as much a matter of compromise as fatigue. You start out not looking like a legendary vampire because being a legendary vampire is exhausting or overwhelming for people who grew up as people.)

                                          This is also something where you can't just look at single starting level PCs. The CofD (and 2e even more so) reduced the stress on werewolves as the physical powerhouse measuring stick for the game-line. What makes the Uratha scary is their exceptional teamwork boosting. A coterie of vampires vs. a pack of werewolves is much more stacked in the werewolves' favor because they have a lot more stuff that's oriented around boosting their effectiveness as pack hunters. A coterie can, of course, be a well trained combat unit, but that takes a lot of specific training compared to what a pack gets just for being a pack of werewolves.

                                                  2ed vampires are all but weak. On raw numbers are probably the best major splat (probably not from the beginning though). Let's analyze a bit more in depth:

                                                  - at low BP (1, 2 or 3) probably - on a physical point of view only - werewolves can have an edge on vampires, and yet it's the only splat which is outperforming kindred on raw physical power. Non the less when talking about mental discipline kindred outperform any other splat. Plus a good use of Animalism can compensate the gap in terms of dice/damage

                                                  - at BP 5 the gap goes away: maximizing the physical stats lead both werewolves and kindred to a pool of 28 dice (more or less) without counting devotions like force of nature, sudden strength etc.

                                                  - Above BP 5 kindred have the edge on any other splat in terms of raw numbers - due to physical intensity and frenzy.

                                                  And again, this is only if you talk about physical stats. Kindred are very versatile and can easily turns their enemies against each other: Majesty, Dominate, Animalism 4.

                                                  Any encounter is very situational but I would say that, no, kindred are not weak at all.
