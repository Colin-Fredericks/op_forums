            In theory. The themes of Mage the Awakening are "phenomenal cosmic power and crippling hubris"; in a Mage game (or a crossover game), complications arise with actually trying that, like spell sympathy, the massive amounts of time, and narrowing in on the right moments - not to mention the very dramatic consequences trying this might have.

            In a Werewolf game, I would not allow this avenue to answer the questions the players want. The mages who can or would research that kind of thing very much have their own agendas at heart, and you can bet that if they're asking about the origins of Uratha, those aren't very pleasant agendas for the characters. It'd become a moral temptation between "do you want the answers" and "they will re-write your template".

                                                SHADOWHISTORY
                                                What makes a werewolf a werewolf? Why are the
                                                blessings of animal power and the curse of Rage transmitted by blood, rather than a disease as the movies would
                                                have you believe? How can these creatures even exist?
                                                The answers may lie deep in the distant past, before history began. Everything a werewolf is — the source of his
                                                strength and speed, the curse of silver and the bloodstain
                                                that speaks the word “Forsaken” — is all said to tie back
                                                to the legend of Father Wolf.
                                                Once, as the legend goes, the world’s werewolves were
                                                its guardians. They were loyal servants of Father Wolf,
                                                a mighty spirit that hunted the worlds and meted out
                                                punishment to those that broke the ancient bans. Then
                                                the first Uratha, the mothers and fathers of the race, committed an unforgivable crime. The other lords of the spirit
                                                world cast the Uratha aside, forsaking them.
                                                Now the werewolves have lost almost half their
                                                heritage. The pacts that were struck between them and
                                                their spirit cousins are all but severed. They walk among
                                                humans like wolves among sheep, disguising themselves in
                                                the skins of men and women, denied the hunting grounds

                                                Not in-character and neither are texts like those from Skinchangers (which in its Chapter about spirits tacitly assumes the father wolf story is true a number of times).

                                                It is the part where the game describes itrself, so to say, where I think it closes possibilities instead of creating them.
                                                The corebook alone talks mostly as a objective / omniscient narrator, I think that removes a lot of the fun for such a game.

                                                It seems like the creators were content to make the characters face a situation where everything is stacked against them, they are "guilty" by heredity for an unfathomable but necessary crime and all the world hates you...
                                                That is surely a very bleak world, but not really a mysterious one. Vampires get VII, an antagonist they know preciously little about and similar, but the host, the Bale hounds, the Pure, non of them are very mysterious.

                                                I think that "absolute history" which is at least in most books assumed to be completely true does not help the game. Of course, you can just ignore it and make your own story, but if many people feel they need to, it is a bad strategy.

                                                I do not think it works out and it should from the start.
                                                It could have cut a great deal of the metaplot and instead started out with blasphemies of all sorts about how werewolves came to be.
                                                I think it is especially unfortunate that the book basically tells you and keeps telling you that the Forsaken are right and the Pure are wrong.
                                                That sets the mood quite a bti against the interesting dilemma of making the characters wonder if, despite all the extremism and such, maybe their enemies have a valid point...
                                                Why give dark fantasy / horror protagonists so much moral certainty about the core of what they are?
                                                I think that is one of the rather unfortunate leftovers of Apocalypse which they actually SHOULD have dropped.
