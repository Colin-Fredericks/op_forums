        I'll start with a welcome back to Pandalfino, and an apology for letting the project sit fallow. I'm still not ready to return to the project, but I could have been more punctual in responding to some of these ideas.

        Second, I'd like to mention that we're still waiting on write-ups for the Savants, the Viziers, and the Caitiffs. If anybody wants to take a shot at developing a Y-Splat, look to the two that are already in the half-book to get an idea about what questions the Philosophy needs to answer.

        And now, to respond to Iceblade.
        Originally posted by Iceblade44 View Post
        Philosophy of the Devils
        Celebrants of sin
        Hmmm... The first paragraph of this section gives me an intriguing idea.
        What if the egg isn't some force of evil, but Huang Long herself? A remnant fragment of her that has hatched back into life...
        Here's the problem, though. Huang Long's body is the entire universe. So as this infant fragment grows, she does so by absorbing everything she touches (It could be perceived as a metaphor for the Big Crunch, if you're into cosmological models). She is contracting the Treasury and consequently all of reality as she grows. Before the first collapse, she was kept in check by the Nidhoggr, but now she grows unbounded. In fact, the stories of Jormungandr might actually be about Huang Long.
        This creates a wonderful dilemma for the Oroboroi: Huang Long sacrificed everything to create the universe. Does she deserve to exist, if it means everything else has to be consumed? Some dragons believe she does. They hate the corruption of the world they live in and yearn for the simplicity of uniting with this "great mother" and becoming a single being. These dragons call themselves the Phoeni (Plural, since Phoenixes just sounds... wrong), but others call them the Celebrants of Unity or, sometimes more shortly, the Saintly Devils.
        Dragons that subscribe to the philosophy of unity work to disassemble the forces of order in the world, thereby weakening the Idols of the Treasury and making them easier for Huang Long to absorb. These Phoeni take on the forms of great birds, rather than great lizards, to symbolize the rebirth of the great mother (Actually in some dragon mythology, Phoenix is the female of the breed with a 66% Yin composition, so this is appropriate). Obviously the Oroboroi are adamantly opposed to these Phoeni because they pose the greatest threat to their reign imaginable: the surface of Jormungandr/Huang Long and the end of the universe.

        I like the idea of a villain like this, because it will force players to pause and consider whether their actions are justifiable.


        Originally posted by Iceblade44 View Post
        Idols
        Idols are the hearts of business just as the Treasury is the heart of the world (well, universe, but world sounds more profound) and Hearts are the heart of a dragon's reign. I like the idea of an Idol possessing its composite parts, but I feel this should be less a force of invasion and more an order of hive-mind structure. When someone joins an organization, they give up a piece of who they are to the function and control of the organization. These pieces are the Anima you describe, and the Idool uses them as it uses its own limbs, sending commands through a mental connection to get something done in the physical world, which they are unable to interact with directly. In part this is why the Oroboroi find them so useful, if an Oroboros descends into the deep perceptions of the treasury and entreats an Idol, she can cause cascading change in the physical organization it treats as its extremities. This is often easier than actually enacting change in person.

        I like the idea that Idols can hold grudges. It certainly sets them apart from spirits. I also agree that Idols would have grown in both population and size after the first collapse, but as a result of trophic cascade rather than slavery. Elder dragons (Nidhoggr) hunted Idols for sustenance as a wolf might hunt a stag; Idols exist in part due to delicious Breath running throughout their body. This fact is part of why humanity was in check before the Elders were usurped; any organization that would try to grow would spawn an Idol which would subsequently be hunted for sustenance.
        With the Nidhoggr that turned Zmei hiding in the Material world, the Idols found roles reversed, becoming predators with terrifying reach. This too was a hunt of substenance; by killing a Zmeu the Idol could absorb its Assets into its organization extremities, growing in power as a result.
        Zmeu being overrun by the Oroboroi simply made this hunt easier, since Oroboroi rarely delve into the depths of the Treasury.
        This creates a terrifying concept, that those companies that treat themselves as legal entities have a malevolent force behind them actively working to destroy you (if you're a dragon, that is).
        I like this too, including the connection to the Einherjar. I wouldn't include a first Idol though, or restrict Einherjar to a single idol. Rather, it is an extension of the hive mind that any Idol may apply to any of its extremities: it can convert any anima in its organization into a vessel for the vengeful Einherjar and then sick the resulting monster on its enemy. This is another scary thought; being part of an organization can turn you into a monster for the purpose of destroying a rival company? That's some great horror fuel.
        No need to compare or connect Idols to Spirits. The Treasury has overlap with several realms invisible from other lines, but it is best to develop as if it exists in a vacuum and let Storytellers make their own assumptions about how realms are related.
        So it sounds like we're dividing the Treasury into three grades of perception:
        0: Sternum (Physical World)
        1: Pericardium (Almost Physical world, people are dressed in a way that reflects their intentions and they speak in surface thoughts)
        2: Myocardium (Abstract world, home of Idols that represent organizations. Spatial and Temporal Distortions of the Treasury begin here)
        3: Endocardium (World before the collapse, most commonly known as Jormungandr's Treasury. Home of the Shayatin, Huang Long, and the place where dragons go to seek resurrection)


        Originally posted by Iceblade44 View Post
        Terminology
        I like the distinction between Nidhoggr and Zmei. It also makes explaining the stages of a collapse more clear, particularly the first collapse. I would say that all elders called themselves Nidhoggr until some removed their hearts, and were redubbed Zmei by the remaining Nidhoggr as a derision of their cowardice.
        As such, there would be very few Zmei (if any) in a modern Dragon the Embers game, as they would have been ripe for usurpation during the second and any subsequent collapse.


        Originally posted by Iceblade44 View Post
        Sovereignty
        Who is more powerful, the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom or the President of France?
        Each would tell you they are more important, and the Oroboroi are no different. There is no status rank system because that would mean they'd have to admit that someone has more authority than they do, and that would be against everything that defines them.
        There are some subtle measures of political hierarchy, though, which can be measured by a dragon's Furnace and more importantly by her Assets. I would say that the Assets mechanic covers what you're trying to do here, particularly the Province asset.

        As for pulling rank in the Treasury, again that's a matter for the dragon's assets. I could see perhaps having assets adorn an Oroboros the further she descends into the perception of the Treasury, and that adornment granting her an advantage when dealing with Idols and bargaining with Shayatin. When I get back to writing and produce a rough draft of the Treasury Appendix, this is something I'd like to discuss in more detail.

I could see perhaps changing Nightmares into something a little more general: broken dreams turned to monsters when potential failed to coalesce into a victory in the real world. For example, the unpublished book sitting on the counter might trigger the birth of a Djinn (Nightmare) in the Treasury's Myocardium. The potential of a whirlwind booksigning tour and two sequels collapses into a black mass of horror which seeks to collapse other dreams and thereby increase in number. It's a powerful symbol for the contagious nature of depression and it would give Oroboroi a reason to hunt them (if a Maiden is spawning Djinn in the Treasury, they could turn their misery onto the Province and cause a lot of damage).
        The key is shattered dreams. Djinn thereby attack by raising the spirits of a victim only to knock them down when they're ecstatic. They need the hope to collapse in order to create another Djinn.

            Here is a little bit of the Philosophy of Savants,as I picture it.Hope you like it(feel free to enrich,modify,point out what you think needy of change and why!)

            "What you fail to understand,you fear.In what you fear,lies salvation.Break the chains of logic,and you shall be set free."

            Humanity has always been a phobic race.Clinging on to religion,society and self-made guilt,they resemble larvas at the edge of a leaf,staring into the chaos underneath.Those few that make the transition to a butterfly and fly off,are the ones that are truly free.According to a Savant,ignorance is not bliss.To be complete one must embrace the unknown,for even if your logic is not enough,your emotions are.Chaos is a part of all of us-the very part that gave birth to the world itself.To disdain it,is to disdain yourself.Only through chaos can there be creation,and to be caged by self-imposed prejudism against the very law of nature is to exclaim pure hubris in the face of the universe.Order,is after all,the foolish dream of mankind,the very reason humanity is degrading minute by minute,second by second,striving against its real purpose.Chaos is not the end,it is the means,and in accepting that you're pretty much accepting the self,and your part in the vast scheme of the cosmos.A savant thus,is the key the world needs to unlock the shackles of denial and turn the tide.

            Mannerisms:Savants try to embody their philosophy tenet through unpredictability.They form wide arrays of behaviors,seeming emotionally unstable to those that would hardly know them,and on rare occasions even a little crazy.Their constant esoterique monologue is a trait that defines them,as they often shun themselves to break the stereotyic leader people expect to see.That way,they encourage people to act accordingly and be the first that takes the plunge.They are most often enthusiastic about their ideas,to the point of contradicting themselves,never settling down to one opinion or the other.This may get them across as opportunists,but becomes appealing as well.A savant is most likely the mysterious,liberated kind of person we 'd all love to be but lack the will to try.Not surprisingly,they draw audiences through attitude alone,being the indirect voice on the back of people's heads.Where there's a riot or a disagreement,a Savant has most likely been.'Doubt everything'they say,often creating tension just to impart a taste of how important 'living' actually is,not simply surviving.Taboos and rules are meant to be broken,and in what is forbidden lies the sweetest sin of them all.All worth living for a single moment,even if this moment never comes.Had the world started paying attention to the greater picture and not on details,none of this would have been the way they are,Savants agree.

            Assets:Savants most often find value in the living,as people tend to reflect that so cherished unpredictability(an object would not do that well anyways...!).Objects after all,are not meant to last forever,and may one day fade like words on the wind;their philosophy on the other hand,will not.Therefore,Maidens are the most usual means of replenishing breath.Savants tend to have an even more obsessive relationship with them than other Oroboroi do,pushing themselves to the extreme in effort of making the seeds of their beliefs bloom in the hearts of their ''dearest''.This leads to self-destructive tendencies some of the times,but again that is the thrill of the moment according to a Savant.In what concerns Provinces,Savants tend to enjoy loose-organisation circles,accepting anyone who does not pose a threat to free will,leaving the people act and think on their own;that is what they strive for,in the end.They seldom take action(usually supervising the constant uproar of mortals),and when they do they usually strike like thunder;wild and sudden.The geographical preferences for their Province,is usually random as well,though some tend to prefer rural or suburban areas where they can do more as they please,without having the authoritarian background of a vast metropolis on their mind.

            Den Formation:Relationships with others of their kind tend to be tense most of the times,especially with Imperators as they lie on the opposite sides of a coin.Savants are often portrayed as non-caring individuals by other dragons,unfit for royalty or leading.For all the disadvantages their rebellious nature brings,the constant judgement they ensue to others and themselves as well is honest and well-based.They most often function as the ''extra eye'' in a den,searching through words and actions to find flaws in other Dragons in order to get their own philosophy as infallible.That zeal works for the den as well,as many times has a phantom menace been noticed firstly by a Savant,and thus prevented.

            Character Creation:Savants base their strengths in speed of thought and action;the first to question the self is the first to access the unknown.Most useful Attributes fall between Wits,Dexterity and even Manipulation(indirect approach often helps people believe more in themselves and less in another,which is exactly what Savants envision).Talents that help Savants convey their principles range from mental to social,and include skills as Occult and Academics,as to set hardly countered arguments on the table,as well as expression or even athletics to demonstrate their point,socially or physically.

                You had a great start, Pandalfino, thank you for working so hard on this for everybody.
                I made a couple changes, which you will find below.

                I wasn't really on board with the butterfly motif, sorry.

                ==================================
                Savants (Modified from Pandalfino's Take)
                Humanity has always been a phobic race. Clinging to wise words that have long shed their meaning, they are like cattle perched on the precipice of a cliff, one crack of thunder away from stampeding to their own destruction. The safety of the familiar is a lie, a tradition of stagnation that leaves them rubbing sticks for a flame as the Embers before them wither and die. Man and monster share this ignorance and to a Savant there is no greater threat. Pursuit of the new is ingrained in all creatures--it is the very thing that gave birth to the world. To shun it is to cut away at the very definition of life. Safety is the foolish dream of humankind; the notion that a force can be culled, defeated, trapped in prisons of iron and stone. To learn from the world is an act of change, and change is painful. In that way, ignorance is a bliss the Savant must tear away from the world so that it can grow.

                Mannerisms: Savants try to embody their philosophy's tenets through unpredictability: they are the wild card at the poker table and the devil's advocate in the courtroom. They form fractal behavior arrays, often appearing unstable to the casual observer. There is method in this madness, however, each social peculiarity is explicitly planned to provoke thought in the other party. They are often found narrating an epic monologue, even stepping away from a conversation to carry on an apparent aside with themselves (often to the confusion and dismay of others). They view humans as comrades rather than cattle, acting as a voice to a crowd. They encourage others to follow in their footsteps, taking up arms and spurring on the cause. Savants are forcefully enthusiastic about their ideals, mainly because they cannot conceive the notion that they might be wrong. This can make them difficult to negotiate with, particularly when interacting with other Oroboroi. It also gives them the air of opportunists, as any conversation can and will be redirected to their cause. The key to working with a Savant is to convince them that your goals and their goals lead to the same destination (though perhaps not on the same route). Savants are shockingly charismatic; this comes from their confidence in their beliefs. They draw audiences through attitude alone, planting subliminal messages in the minds of everyone that crosses their path. Riots follow in their wake.

                Assets: Savants find more value in the animate than the inanimate. They tend to take on disciples and set them to work fetching data that can be plied towards the cause. These disciples therefore end up cloistered in the various dusty libraries and evidence lockers, serving as Maidens for the Savant and her cause. Savants tend to have an even more obsessive relationship with them than other Oroboroi, pushing themselves to the extreme in the effort of making the seeds of their beliefs bloom in the hearts of their ''dearest''. When a Savant takes a province, it comes in the form of loosely-knit circles, the kind that can splinter into other organizations. A Savant maintains the province mainly by convincing its constituents that all of their problems can be solved in the pursuit of her goal and her justice.

                Den Formation: Relationships with other Oroboroi tend to be tense, mainly because the Savant is such a driven individual and her rebellious nature contradicts much of what a Dragon seeks to gain with her power. It is her knowledge of rebellion, however, that makes a Savant an excellent resource in an alliance. When the Province is in an uproar; when the Maidens cry for freedom, it is the Den's Savant who knows how to set everything right again. She is, despite her quirks, a people pleaser. Who better to quell a rebellion than somebody who knows how to start one?

                Character Creation: Savants base their strengths in speed of thought and action; the first to question the self is the first to access the unknown. Most useful Attributes fall between Wits, Dexterity and Manipulation; the indirect approach is highly effective as someone who comes to a conclusion on their own is more likely to trust it (even if given a push). Talents that help Savants convey their principles range from mental to social and include skills such as Occult, Academics, Expression, and Athletics (to outrun the backlash when they get people rioting).
                New Oroboroi drawn to the Philosophy of the Savant find the withering fate of their new brethren distasteful, and so seek to break the rules of man so that they might challenge the rules of nature itself; the Philosophy gives them license to stir the pot and change the world, violently if necessary.
                Savants begin play with one dot in the Water Edict, in honor of their founder.

                Concepts: Modern revolutionary, punk rocker, associate professor, professional gambler, priest, IT guy, park ranger
                ==================================

                And, here to remind myself: I need to clean up the reason why a new Oroboros might turn to the Paragon philosophy, and specify that Paragons begin with a dot of the Fire Edict.

                            No, there hasn't been any development on Chimeras... or any development at all, actually.

                            In previous instances of lulls like this I spent the break writing new material for the book, but this time I was waiting for write-ups on the remaining two Y-Splats. Eventually I lost interest, and now it seems that the project has stalled into its grave.


                            If you like the project and you want to resurrect it, I have the following recommendations:

                            1.) Ditch the "Dragon" term
                            The problem with the name Dragon is one of exposure; it's too familiar and so prospective contributors enter the project with too many expectations of what they want to see. As such, they are frequently driven off when they do not encounter what they were looking for. If you revive the project in a new form, I recommend using a more obscure term, like Zmeu (Plural Zmei) [sz+MAY-OOO, plural sz+MAY-EEE]. This would of course mean reworking the lexicon of the content that already exists but it would be worth the break from the cloud hanging around the term "Dragon".

                            2.) Make a new "The X Chronicles" thread
                            Dragon began its development as a nWoD fan template and it has not been able to keep pace with the changes that accompanied the God Machine Chronicle and the second edition new World of Darkness. As such, any hope for a new phase of the project will hinge on bringing the content that already exists into compatibility with the God Machine Chronicle. I tried to have my cake and eat it too by making the book compatible with both versions of the nWoD. It doesn't work. If you crack open the half-book, you'll find that the Ablutions, Edicts, and even some of the secondary characteristics (like Furnace and Breath) are at a midpoint between the old edition and new edition rules compatibility. Take out the gooey parts that are already partially compatible with the God Machine, solidify them, and then build from there.
                            Possible titles for the chronicles:
                            Zmeu: The Myocardium Chronicles
                            Zmeu: The Ignition Chronicles
                            Zmeu: The ChimeriChronicles
                            If you really want to reignite the project, I'm sure you can come up with something better than that, it's just to get your brain storming.

                            3.) Start from Scratch
                            With the half book and the second-stage development PDF, it might look like you can harvest a great deal of the written text and continue from there. You can't. While the mechanical bits are viable, the so-called "fluff" has consistently been the most detested portion of the document since its first PDF; it cannot be salvaged.

                            4.) If you're looking to produce a PDF, start looking for artwork early
                            A big mistake I made was waiting to finish the text before starting to seek artwork. See, while you're looking for artwork to make the book look nice, everyone else is losing interest because there's no new content to read. It's better to break the project into tiny pieces and work on them all simultaneously (possibly allocating certain contributors to certain tasks) so that the people following your project consistently have content to keep them hungry. If you don't do this, when you encounter a task that you cannot perform yourself (like I did with the factions), there will be no one around to help you.

                            5.) I won't be part of the new project
                            One of the problems with Dragon's third-stage development are the ghosts in its rafters, and I am the big one. When I'm around it looks like somebody is stitching together all of the pieces of the project and I just don't have the chops to do it anymore. Another lead developer shouldn't have to fret me looming overhead. Therefore if a new iteration of the project is to succeed, I cannot be involved.

                            Best of luck. I do love this project and I hope it gets completed some day. But I'm not the guy to do it.
