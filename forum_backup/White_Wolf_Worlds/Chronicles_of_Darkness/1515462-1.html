                        It could be, even though it is likely to meant to represent the fact that they are dealing with the unknown. I still like my theory about it being intentionally nameless, however :P

                        Anyway, moving to the next conspiracy-

                        Operation: Nebula

                        Mo older brother is an Ancient Astronaut Theorist. He doesn't just believe in aliens, or watch the History channel unironically, or even "just" believe that the aliens were the ones to build the pyramids or Stonehenge. He has a computer which is detached from the internet on which he saves all of the data he has about aliens and the research he does about them, including his evidence, because he claims that Google intentionally censures information about aliens. When I started my bachelor degree, his words to me were "don't forget to always seek after the truth- don't let those guys in the academy tell you what is truth and what isn't. Don't let them lie to you". He is also a very conservative person which makes conversations with him very difficult, but that generally works together. Also, he totally believes in a left winged LGBTQ+ conspiracy which stops democracy and destroy our values and all of those nice things.

                        In short, it is hard to live with a conspiracy theorists.

                        I mean, I am sure that not all Ancient Astronaut Theorists are radical conservative fellows- but there is a reason that it is a common trait. The idea that the institution tries to control you, hide the truth from you, use all kinds of substances and experiments on you without your consent. My other sibling is also a conspiracy theorist, even though he goes much more to the religious aspects of it, letting religious figures tell him about the chips in COVID vaccines and all of that nice things. What I am trying to say that it could be easy to poke fun and such people- but those are really frustrating personally traits to deal with, and which means that I have too much knowledge about how such people think, and as such I probably understand too well how the average ON argent think- that the truth is out there, that the institute hides it, and that insidious entities use it to control our society. And the reason those people may have the tendency to become very conservative is because you can just substitute "aliens" with some minority group that you hate or some "pure race" theory and you get to the same result. Still, regardless of the political opinions that conspiracy theorists may hold, they generally have a common trait- you can't trust institutes. They are all corrupted, trying to hide the truth, and seek to control your life.

                        And in the context of Hunter- they do have a point. But in that case, the question I had after first hearing about ON was "how can they be a part of a conspiracy, when it represents everything that they are against?"

                        The actual writeup actually found a good way to answer that question- they don't understand that they are a conspiracy.

                        ON is a yet another very young conspiracy. Considering that the organization have started through online communities, they have probably being formed during the late 90's at best, and most likely in the early 2000's. So like CWS, ON have around 20 years of existence, formed when someone discovered that what he believed to be a conspiracy theory was, in fact, real- or at least, part of it. In order to find the truth, he started communicating online with other conspiracy theorists, creating a society dedicated for discovering the secrets of aliens and their agenda, as well as developing methods to extract special "alien cores" from either alien technology or corpses, later being used to develop the so called Xenotechnology. Here we already see a number of red flags, which become even clearer when we look at the Endowments section- as only members of ON are able to extract alien cores, that the gizmos all look very sci-fi in appearance, and that in the hands of people who aren't part of the conspiracy, the gizmos start to fail and degrade. That makes it sound almost like a... dream? Fantasy? Shared hallucination? This is better reinforced by the fact that there isn't any description of what "aliens" are- which is in a strong contrast to Lucifuge's definition of "demons". There is not "Infernal Dread Power" which distinguish a alien from a non-alien. All monsters are aliens. At least- all monsters that the hunter believes that they are.

                        On the surface, the jump between online forums to conspiracy to Endowments seems like a very huge leap- but by taking into account how their Endowments work, things start falling in place. The members of ON all have a conspiracy theory- they truly believe that aliens are real, and that they control the world. The funny thing is, that in the CofD, they are not wrong. The God Machine could very well be an alien computer seeded upon Earth. Planetary Angels invade Earth's Shadow, while Void Leviathans are draw to the life it offers and then try to crush into its depths. The Gentry kidnap and mutilate people in a manner which is very similar to aliens. The Visitors outright claim that they are a part of an interstellar struggle between different alien factions, while hosting the brains of their vessels. Fears of the Other often manifest themselves as alien entities, allowing those Begotten to take their form. Heck, even Cheiron are potentially controlled by aliens. There are alien-like entities everywhere in the CofD- and they are all equivalently irrelevant. Why? Because when you can extract a vampire core to power your stealth cloak, either you need to admit that vampires are also aliens, or that this is whole mess simply does not make sense. Like, sure, Marcel Foster's dead apparently have been experimented upon by something, and someone did stole the journal- but is that related to the aliens described by the conspiracy? There are so many different conspiracies in the CofD- either the aliens control them all, or that the conspiracy is much more complicated than that.

                        So basically what we have is that Xenotechnology works a lot more like a shared hallucination than some actual technology- but counterpoint: Xenotechnology is real. The alien cores are physical entities that can be interacted with. The gizmos are objects that have physical, direct effects. In this case, let's give you a counter-counter point- Xenotechnology works a lot more like an act of sorcery than as an item Endowments. It has a certain amount of Successes needed in order to produce a certain object, with Xenotechnology being a 1-5 Endowment which provides schematics (rituals?) in order to produce gizmos. It even has a cost- not in the form of Willpower, but in the form of the alien core. Counter-counter-counterpoint- the Clash of Wills for gizmos does not use Occult, unlike the rest of the Endowments. Instead, it use Crafts. That leans to the idea that there isn't much "magic" in the creation process, but that the act is technological, to a degree. It depends on the skills of the hunter in understanding how technology and engineering work, not in how to manipulate the occult energies which are embedded in the very fabric of the universe. As such, there is a technological side to Xenotechnology. Counter-counter-counter-counterpoint, artifice is also a medium for magic, and if Xenotechnology is relying on some shared hallucination, it should depend on the ability to perceive the object as real, and how well the hunter can define it as an actual technology. The strength of the gizmo depends on how well the hunter understands technology, because the better they understand it, the more "real" the gizmo becomes. A conspiracy theorist could make a gizmo that is based on half baked theories, but reality is quick to deny it. One who has a degree in engineering, however, can make a much more convincing technology instead.

                        Ok, so Xenotechnology is some short of a shared hallucination that can be crafted into form by those who take part in it and subscribe to its theories, basically a form of technomagic. In a way, it works a lot like the Technocracy from M:tAs, yet more wacky. However, magic does not come out of nowhere- something have created that shared hallucination. Something created the conspiracy. People don't just develop the ability to extract alien cores and build special equipment out of discussing it with others online. Something organized the conspiracy to a degree it is functional, without the conspiracy theories actually believing that they are a part of a shadowy organization. This is the problem with online communities- you can never know who is on the other side of the screen. Is LostLight an actual person? Or am I just a highly advanced AI produced by the aliens who control the government? Obviously you assume that I am a real person with too much free time (I actually don't have that much free time, and yes it means that the little bit that I have I use to write wacky theories about a RPG, but nevermind that). However, if I was an advance alien AI, could you have noticed it? Or if, perhaps, I was an intelligent ape? Three dwarves in a trench coat? Am I vegetarian? Vegan? Am I trans? Gay? Bi? Am I conservative or leftist? Is my brother even exist, or did I just made him up just to make me feel relatable, or give me some sense of expertise about the subject? Am I, LostLight, even real?

                        The internet is a shared hallucinations, my friends. We are all part of the conspiracy.

                        Ok, going back from the highly philosophical argument (too much Philosophy Tube), the point that was made is that under the cover anonymity, no one can actually who they are talking to online- and it is really unclear how ON actually makes sure that "aliens" do not infiltrate their networks. Probably they are doing some security measures, but considering that most monsters who do use the internet know well enough how to act "human", it is likely that they won't really be able to stop them. However, here comes the actual defense mechanism of the conspiracy- they are crackpots. Monsters will not take them seriously. Oh, sure, there is likely a Guardian or an Invictus member who spend a few hours a day rolling through their feeds in the case that the conspiracy has managed to somehow uncover something they shouldn't, but the amount of misdirection that the conspiracy leads itself to believe in, and the fact that no one will take them seriously because of their Ancient Astronaut theories means that 99% of the time, they don't actually need to do anything. Even if, let's say, they manage to uncover some instance of them meeting with a vampire, they are likely to claim it was some sort of a "parasite alien", and when they'll go around telling people about "parasite aliens", no one will believe in them. Because they are crackpots. Because they make no sense. They are a living "masquerade breach", yet they are also a living masquerade by themselves. They are a walking, living paradox- people who know the truth, yet their truth is denied by the world, for it is not true, even though it is.

                        Wait- isn't there a whole gameline about people who "know the truth" that the rest of the world denies because it lives in a lie, and that there are some ancient entities which control the population in an attempt to stop the truth from being spread, and that if they'll try to spread the truth to others people will deny it because it is true and basically leave us with a living paradox?

                        Where was I? Ah, right.

                        Operation Nebula is controlled by an Abyssal Intruder.

                        (I can almost hear Arc yelling at me "those are the Gentry! Those are obviously the Gentry! Or at least the God Machine!". Alas, almost)

                        I mean- think about it. Something allows those people to create impossible technology by manifesting alien cores. Sure, the God Machine could be behind of it. The Gentry could be behind of it. The Onachus could be behind of it. The Contagion may even be behind of that- the shared hallucination spreading like a plague and causing people to make reality bleed and produce alien technology from their subconscious. But there is something with the Abyss that fits- the Abyss has been known to infest souls and replace the truths of the world with products of its own. It is known to be drawn to those with a less stable mind and seep through the cracks. It is known to be related to the world of dreams. It is known to be related to madness and denial of reality. The Abyss, the dark ocean behind the world, is also often called the Void- and alien entities do creep into reality from it. The Abyss works narratively as a metaphor to the Great Beyond and eldritch gods, while also working thematically to explain Xenotechnology. In that case, however, we need to understand how this Intruder looks like, and how it operates- after all, all Intruders are based around replacing aspects of reality with themselves- and in that case, how the Intruder which is behind this whole mess actually operates?

                        Probably, by the internet.

                        I mean, Intruders generally leak into the world by accident, when strange patterns are arranged in strange ways- and nothing is better at producing such patterns than the internet. Random combinations of words, numbers and topics, openly discussed transgressions and sharing of radical ideas. All of that serving as a rich ground for the Abyss to open up in the wrong place and the wrong time. It could be that this Intruder has started small- substituting some parts of the internet, creating a literal "dark web". Creating false personas that mimic humans, bots made out of eldritch terror, propagating misinformation in an attempt to hide the truth, or conspiracy theories in order to lure agents in. Some of those conspiracy theories are more radical than the others, but all are meant to propagate and invest radical narratives which would open up those who believe in them to the Abyss- basically, taking the "dead internet" theory and putting it on steroids. And as this alien internet grows through the internet, it is met with a bunch of conspiracy theories who try to give context to the madness of the CofD. It mimics their opinions and reflects them- yes, you are correct. There are aliens everywhere. They control the government, the media, the pharmaceutical companies. They have controlled humanity since its inception. Yes, there are aliens. Yes, they have advanced technology, you are absolutely right. Yes, you can harvest that technology. Yes, you can make advanced weapons out of it. You want proof? Very well. Why don't you try to harvest one? Oh, you found this object inside? What if you will build something out of it. I found this schematics- it is completely true. Believe in it. Believe, and it will be true.

                        And they believed. And it became true.

                        So yeah, it would be very, very tempting to call this Intruder the Echo Chamber- and I think I will use this term, as it is fitting. The Echo Chamber's strength is by the fact that it can take misinformation and make it real, disrupting reality in the process. Its weakness is that it is not independent- it must mimic the thoughts of others, which means that those affected by it have a lot of power and control. The Echo Chamber is only passive, and the more people are engaged with it, the more it is able to allow them to manifest their fantasies into reality. In that case, you may ask, what is actually the endgame of the Echo Chamber? What it actually plans on doing? The answer i very simple- to simply satisfy the wills of those engaged with it. Those conspiracy theorists want aliens? Then they are getting aliens.

                        An whole invasion of aliens.

                        That's the peak of the shared hallucination that ON have- the creation of a full blown alien invasion. Spaceships, tripodes, lizardmen, the grey- everything you want, all at once. Conspiracy theories will become true- an alien base will be uncovered in Antarctica, Earth is hollow and full of dinosaurs, whatever you can think about- it is real now. The truth will be revealed to the world- no longer they will be laughed upon as crackpots. No longer their truth will be denied. The invasion will come- and they will be there to stop it. Or, perhaps, to join it. Depending on who is more popular on their social media. After all, the Echo Chamber will reward those who are the most engaged within its dark platforms.

                        So don't forget to like and subscribe, everyone! Who knows? Maybe it is your only way to stop the aliens from coming to you.

I considered the God Machine, some Gentry manipulation, or even have the Visitors as actual aliens that manipulate the conspiracy from beneath the tinfoil hats of their members and that the alien cores are some form of concentrated Sekhem or something along those lines, or even just some Astral bs, so let me know what do you think and which other candidates you would have used to explain their Endowments. Anyway, next will be the Survivors Club- both among my favorite groups and one which I think needs some recontextualization in order to close what I think are some of the logical gaps in the writeup. Anyway, see you next time!

                                I still have an Ascending Ones rebuttal I gotta get out that's more important than this, but I will confess this one strikes me as one of your wildest ones out there, and one that gets me yelling Occam's Razor. I think you got a little too hung up on the conspiracy theorist angle of it all and the overall relationship of conspiracy to truth and data and the consequences of a post-truth world-which, hey, makes for fun theories and gets into grousing about the fact we are dealing with a post-truth era and how that sucks, but it does feel like reaching beyond what you need to grasp.

                                So, tackling points:

                                1)"How can they be a part of a conspiracy, when it represents everything that they are against?"

                                I don't think this is as much of a trip up as you think it is, particularly since we're this side of the January 6th Insurrection. Conspiracy theorist's problems with conspiracies tend to not actually be about the existence of such so much as what they think they're doing-they're quite happy to imagine themselves as part of a conspiracy if it's counteracting those malevolent ones. QAnon, for whatever else can be said about it, is a very illustrative example, as it's directly establishing it's adherents into a militarizable conspiracy even as it lays out the conspiracy acting against them. It's about being on the wrong side of power, and I imagine many people are just as happy with playing as dirty as the opposition as to have a dirty opposition in the first place.

                                Anyways, I feel like the bit where Foster makes connections and unifies them under the codename Nebula is sufficent enough to validate that, yes, ON agents know they're in a conspiracy.

                                2) The Cores and the Aliens Themselves

                                I feel like the reading that ON can draw cores where no one else can, and how they can draw it from any kind of monster, is another one of those things that run right past where the assumptions start, including a tangential on how every monster has a heart, so there's your core right there-but we're running ahead of ourselves.

                                ON's interests are in aliens, and while on this side of the fog of war it can be hard to discern the distinguish the difference between "monster" and "alien", the write-up is allusive enough about a distinction that I think it's fair to say ON has lines between the Aliens, who are engineering the invasion and domination of the world, and the monsters they create in that pursuit. This is doubly supported by the fact that while the appelation of alien can apply to a great deal of monsters, some games are definitely more alien-y than others-in particular, Changeling, Demon, and Deviant all more readily deal with the themes and tropes of alien stories more than others, starting from alien abduction and ending on governmental conspiracies regarding the manipulation of such. This is supported by a lot of the text having allusions towards those first two games, with the high-tech facilities folded in space pretty readily sounding like Descent's Facilities, and the mention of the thorny homeworld in the Cyberglass endowment.

                                Part of what makes that particularly compelling is the fact that fairies and demons (and deviants) are among the splats more likely to have something on them that is likely to be "core-like"-a Mannikin's hearts, the Summer Courtier's Sword Manifestation, the Processes of any given demon, the Coactive growths or Invasive's prosthetics. These are all things that feels toyable.

                                Obviously I don't to leave it at that, since part of the fun of having the target be aliens is that it allows for a breadth of experience more specific enemies would deny (in particular, it keeps the Promtheans on the table, and if you think I'm gonna do my Stargate peeps dirty by suggesting there's no way ON would go after the Arisen, you are wrong), but again, core experiences for definition and all that.

                                3) Xenotechnology

                                THe simplest way to go at this one is the question of "mass shared hallucination" versus "arcane physics", which one seems more likely in the more objectively-driven Chronicles, and in particular if we accept that ON's operations are intended to be a lot more confined in terms of targets than every monster out there. The biggest argument lies in if we're also engaging with the fairy side of things, how does that work. The obvious answer of "like magnets" asides, the hallucination appraoch would be more approachable if it had a model more akin towards spellcasting factors in Mage or Realms in Changeling the Dreaming, but that it makes the case that schematics are more fixated, and are shareable for any of their actual mutability strikes me that it hews away from that model (Masterworks are more the crafting thing that leans into mass shared hallucination).

                                Also, I don't wanna bother with this in talking about ON on the other thread but it's admittedly only tangential here, but whatever: Xenotechnology continues the illusion of a march of things that continues to make Genius less and less relevant, and I am somewhere between very okay and very meh with that.

                                4) Roy Elliot

                                For whatever else can be said about the overall wibbliness of the conspiracy in terms of of what's going on under the hood for it, one thing stands out pretty strongly-Roy Elliot is a very convienent actor. Foster spends all these years running from pursuants and never getting a clean lead, and then when he meets Elliot, who's as dubious as Marcel's father, all of a sudden the man has compelling enough evidence of an alien invasion with multiple competitors and several high tech facilities that Foster then commits to forging Nebula? That, as the kids like to say, is sus-and more than that, it's shockingly coherent for being so convenient, and it doesn't carry the stink of nihilism that usually surrounds the presence of the Abyss.

                                And I admit, I have run out of steam, so I'll have to get back to this point later.
                                But to be fair to the theory, let's square the obvious with the theory-the God-Machine and it's shit does have more in common with the Abyss than any other peer in the conventional monstersphere as both have this element of contradictory invader from outside of reality, so.

Having the source of it being, let's ay, a exiled True Fae or something like that should work just fine- but I still feel like it is the most thematic explanation, even if not the simplest one.

                                        I still like Echo Chamber as a concept. It's definitely gonna show up in my game, with or without ON.


                                        I don't think this is as much of a trip up as you think it is, particularly since we're this side of the January 6th Insurrection. Conspiracy theorist's problems with conspiracies tend to not actually be about the existence of such so much as what they think they're doing-they're quite happy to imagine themselves as part of a conspiracy if it's counteracting those malevolent ones. QAnon, for whatever else can be said about it, is a very illustrative example, as it's directly establishing it's adherents into a militarizable conspiracy even as it lays out the conspiracy acting against them. It's about being on the wrong side of power, and I imagine many people are just as happy with playing as dirty as the opposition as to have a dirty opposition in the first place.

                                        Anyways, I feel like the bit where Foster makes connections and unifies them under the codename Nebula is sufficent enough to validate that, yes, ON agents know they're in a conspiracy.
                                        If it wasn't clear, I do think that the writeup did a good job in explaining this part. I personally think that QAnon, for example, also don't really understand what they are- some do, probably, but did the people who got on the Capitol actually understood that they were manipulated? I think they didn't, but on the other hand I am not American. My brothers are a good example for it- they say that "the media forces progressive agenda" upon them, and that they "see reality clearly", but that's because they themselves don't understand that some people with power and money made sure to construct the conspiracy theories they believe in. Again, it is a subject I had the "delightful experience" to live with such people, which obviously colours how I perceive this conspiracy, but I guess that's just part of being a human or something like that.


                                        I feel like the reading that ON can draw cores where no one else can, and how they can draw it from any kind of monster, is another one of those things that run right past where the assumptions start, including a tangential on how every monster has a heart, so there's your core right there-but we're running ahead of ourselves.

                                        ON's interests are in aliens, and while on this side of the fog of war it can be hard to discern the distinguish the difference between "monster" and "alien", the write-up is allusive enough about a distinction that I think it's fair to say ON has lines between the Aliens, who are engineering the invasion and domination of the world, and the monsters they create in that pursuit. This is doubly supported by the fact that while the appelation of alien can apply to a great deal of monsters, some games are definitely more alien-y than others-in particular, Changeling, Demon, and Deviant all more readily deal with the themes and tropes of alien stories more than others, starting from alien abduction and ending on governmental conspiracies regarding the manipulation of such. This is supported by a lot of the text having allusions towards those first two games, with the high-tech facilities folded in space pretty readily sounding like Descent's Facilities, and the mention of the thorny homeworld in the Cyberglass endowment.

                                        Part of what makes that particularly compelling is the fact that fairies and demons (and deviants) are among the splats more likely to have something on them that is likely to be "core-like"-a Mannikin's hearts, the Summer Courtier's Sword Manifestation, the Processes of any given demon, the Coactive growths or Invasive's prosthetics. These are all things that feels toyable.

                                        Obviously I don't to leave it at that, since part of the fun of having the target be aliens is that it allows for a breadth of experience more specific enemies would deny (in particular, it keeps the Promtheans on the table, and if you think I'm gonna do my Stargate peeps dirty by suggesting there's no way ON would go after the Arisen, you are wrong), but again, core experiences for definition and all that.
                                        In the Discord, the writer have stated that in their intentions, ON treat all monsters as aliens, so while you could describe it as "only certain monsters have cores", I think that assumption is "all monsters have cores". It also seems that I misread that ON agents lose the ability to extract cores from "aliens" without the conspiracy, even though the text does seem to suggest it (namely, them extracting an extraterrestrial fuel cell out of a cryptid. Sure, you could say that they just know where to look, but... I don't know, sounds fishy). Even if they can still find cores out of nowhere, the fact that gizmos break apart after one scene still makes it suspicious- but that already leads to your last point.

                                        THe simplest way to go at this one is the question of "mass shared hallucination" versus "arcane physics", which one seems more likely in the more objectively-driven Chronicles, and in particular if we accept that ON's operations are intended to be a lot more confined in terms of targets than every monster out there. The biggest argument lies in if we're also engaging with the fairy side of things, how does that work. The obvious answer of "like magnets" asides, the hallucination appraoch would be more approachable if it had a model more akin towards spellcasting factors in Mage or Realms in Changeling the Dreaming, but that it makes the case that schematics are more fixated, and are shareable for any of their actual mutability strikes me that it hews away from that model (Masterworks are more the crafting thing that leans into mass shared hallucination).
                                        I would personally argue that there isn't much of a difference between the two, because obviously the mass shared hallucinations doesn't manifest into reality through the power of faith alone. There is a reason I added the Abyss in there, even the Echo Chamber could easily be replaced by some sort of internet based Infrastructure of some kind, or the titles of a True Fae being embodied by the ON agents as long as they don't violate the Terms and Conditions they clocked on without reading them. I used that angle because I thought it actually gives more power to the hunters compared to the other options. Echo Chamber is, in essence a passive and reactive mechanism- the hunters desire a reality, it works to manifest it under the condition it is not true, and will generate more misinformation. Compare that to a Gentry or a Demon manipulating the conspiracy, and they obviously hold more control in that regard. I also considered some sort of Sekhem Sorcery as well in that regards (again, Visitors), yet I just did the Enigmatics so it felt like to repetitive.

                                        Also, I would be lying if I were to say that Genius wasn't there at the back of my mind, but I have never actually fully read the game, neither have I played it nor am I a huge fan of it. If anything, as mentioned, the Technocracy was more dominant in my mind in this section (but really, what is the difference :P)


                                        For whatever else can be said about the overall wibbliness of the conspiracy in terms of of what's going on under the hood for it, one thing stands out pretty strongly-Roy Elliot is a very convienent actor. Foster spends all these years running from pursuants and never getting a clean lead, and then when he meets Elliot, who's as dubious as Marcel's father, all of a sudden the man has compelling enough evidence of an alien invasion with multiple competitors and several high tech facilities that Foster then commits to forging Nebula? That, as the kids like to say, is sus-and more than that, it's shockingly coherent for being so convenient, and it doesn't carry the stink of nihilism that usually surrounds the presence of the Abyss.
                                        I honestly mostly disregarded Roy Elliot as a significant factor. He's just, well, there. Obviously will be interested in seeing a cool twist including him (yes, I know you are going with either a Demon or a Changeling, but I'll always vote Visitor if Echo Chamber is not around).

                                        And yeah, the Abyss and the God Machine do share more in common than they would like to admit. A strong hate towards Hell, for example (and being eldritch entities that contradict reality and all of that jazz)
