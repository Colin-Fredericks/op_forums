      • Goodness - moral excellence or admirableness

        I know virtues and vices are supposed to be single words that serve as shorthand for a character's defining ideal and character flaw, but the most interesting personality I played was something that came into being over the course of a game. It didn't quite fit the virtue and vice I picked initially and I never figured out quite the right words to sum it up.

        My character was bitten in the middle of a zombie siege on an isolated cabin in the woods. Real standard tropey horror movie stuff, but what was not standard was that through some insanely good rolls he survived long enough to be cured and went on to participate in a sequel chronicle.

        But between the bite and the cure there was a stretch where in and out of character I wrestled with the specter of a terminal illness and the absolute certainty that he had at most a few days to live. And there I found a weird sort of clarity, an understanding of the self preservation instinct as a sort of shackles that he was now free of. He was, in effect, already dead and that meant he didn't have to waste any thought or effort on his own survival. No question on who would go into the basement first, send the dead guy! Any subsequent bites won't matter! He could take all of the necessary risks because for him there was nothing left to lose, it was already lost, and further risk was meaningless!

        It wasn't quite suicidal, because he considered himself an asset to everyone else's survival and was damned determined to milk his failing body for its maximum utility. It was however, a profoundly strange lack of self regard, lack of self care. A weirdly third person utilitarian view that said "Don't waste any resources on yourself because you're dying soon anyway, give it to those who might yet live."

        And then... he lived, except he wasn't really living because the fatalism had worked so well that he never left that mindset behind. He wasn't living he was just... failing to die, indefinitely. The lack of self regard became a vice as he allowed himself to suffer ongoing emotional trauma on the theory that you never have to deal with the consequences if you just die first. At the same time, it also fueled his virtue as he poured all of that displaced energy into trying to solve the problems of everyone around him before he burnt out for good.
