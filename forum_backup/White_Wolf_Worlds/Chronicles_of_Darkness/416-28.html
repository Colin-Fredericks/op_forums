                            If people want wolves, let them stay wolves and not cover up as a hyena or panther or such.

                            I think it will for sure not fit into the core as rules, but I think it would be great if the new Idigam chronicles gave story hooks or openings to connect to a possible later "Fera" book.

                                Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                In Forsaken, it's harder to justify the entire "Other 'Weres' do the same thing you do, but better" because the culture and history of the Uratha is singularly tied into itself(Which, no, is not the same as saying Father Wolf is definitive truth, in case anyone is thinking that). Much of the Forsaken story goes that way. So why do the other 'Weres' exist, and how do you balance essentially probably ten other major templates that share characteristics with your main thing without stepping all over it? Forsaken doesn't offer an easy answer(never read War Against the Pure, so who knows, I might be missing something) because of how diverse it is to begin with.
                                Not that I'm trying to keep this argument going, but I'd like to disagree. If you're willing to put a little work into it, this isn't as much of an issue as you assume. I've included other Changing Breeds in the werewolf game I'm running, with original mythology, and no one has felt their roles "stepped on" or any other issues of redundancy or too deep a schism in the cosmology (though Doubt and Blasphemy are two of the themes I'm using....)

                                To give some examples of Fera with other purposes that I've used:
                                -Were-foxes purpose is to help spirits enter and influence the material, and humans to shape the Shadow. They decide whose wise enough to be the exception to Father Wolf's law, and pushing those that abuse the other realm too greatly. They do this without the knowledge or agreement of werewolves, of course.
                                -Were-lions claim a heritage of kingship and stewardship over humankind, becoming leaders, warriors, and judges; they also prey upon humans which fall out of balance with nature.
                                -Were-rats were given the purpose of making sure humanity's waste didn't poison the world. Natural processes return resources to nature, but humans haven't learned that yet, so rats clean up after them; they also try to reconcile artificial and natural spirits, put into conflict by human expansion. The Beshilu (and their own selfishness) has undermined them, though.
                                -Were-bears are drawn to the deepest parts of the wilds, being natural wanderers, and come across anomalies which they guard as a consequence - verges, ancient horrors, forgotten Wounds.

                                These aren't roles which the Uratha or others can't fulfill, and like I said, I play around with Doubt by giving alternate creation myths and different roles - I tried to give options to my players - but they're purposes the same way "keep humans and spirits separate" is the Forsaken's purpose without stepping on toes. (Lions may toes-step a little, but I don't put any wolves in Africa, and designed lions to be their foils in several ways - like being descended of Helios). From my players' feedback, I've subverted the snowflake syndrome and worked them into Forsaken's setting well enough to be interesting, not upsetting.


                                I just wanted that it can be done, and in interesting ways. I would not advocate making other Changing Breeds a default part of the setting; perhaps another supplement that revises the creation rules and makes clear how they can be used, but I don't feel a strong need for it. War Against the Pure's rule set (though it could benefit from revision and expansion) has been ample enough for me; I'd rather see more along the lines of Predators and Skinchangers.

                                To me, there's already room for other Fera in Forsaken; ie, there's nothing which contradicts doing what you want with your own story. It may take some work, but the best uses of them in Werewolf are going to be fan projects personal to the Storyteller and chronicle.
                                If there was to be a re-imagined Fera template, I'd actually enjoy something along the lines of Lost Light's fan project more; their own unique game setting, which, in true WoD tradition, is compatible enough for crossover.

                                /my two cents

                                        That's what I'm running by in my game's WTF overhaul; Wolf Blooded is just a name; they're the offspring of spiritually potent/Shadow-touched entities. They have the potential to become werewolves if triggered; sometimes strong enough Resonance is left from their parents to make them into werewolves, but more often it's a bite, an attack, something that alters the Resonance of their essence to be conducive. Unfortunately for werewolves, it also means -other- entities find these spiritually potent people just as interesting. Not only as wonderful prey (extra Essence beyond a normal human, sometimes odd abilities due to it; see Second Sight and Book of Spirits etc. merit wise among other ways to up their untapped potential), certains others find them more hospitable to possess or achieve synthesis with. And that's beyond weighing in what their relevance is to possible other shapeshifters out there and Idigam.

                                        Werewolves call their offspring that bear that wolf-blooded; really, they're spirit-blooded. It's also meant to make them precious; you -want- to protect your family. Spirits and similar entities like Shartha are very, very likely to take an interest in them, I wanted to reinforce that family was not only important like it is for wolves, but there's some paranoia and inherent horror to be descended from a werewolf, and a reason for them to pay close attention or keep a tight family; things tend to go strange among them even if they never turn into a werewolf themselves.

                                        I also wanted to present the idea of poaching; that is, individuals of other packs, tribes etc. taking an interest in the descendants of other werewolves as prime breeding stock -and- werewolf recruits if forcibly changed in course of the story. Nothing like having your daughter be taken when your vigil slipped, and turned by a Pure to bolster their pack numbers when you're a Forsaken who was hoping to hell she'd be free of horrors of werewolf existence. Things like that that hit home hard. Even if something isn't introduced in Idigam chronicles in that vein, I'll be keeping that with the overhaul we're using. Not suffering from Lunacy is... well, irrelevant. Not using that. Their importance is their potential, and how readily someone close to you can be compromised or twisted. "Cursed" family lines? 7th sons of 7th sons and the like turning into Werewolves? Other ideas in that vein? Hell yeah.

                                        Werewolf: the Forsaken Overhaul: A W:tF Shard and House Rules, Pre-Idigam Chronicles and taking different route. Constantly being Updated.
                                        New Bloodlines and Blood and Smoke Updates: Homebrew Bloodlines and updates to prior ones.
                                        Belial's Brood Blood and Smoke: An updated take on the Forsworn.

                                                            I'd certainly like to see the Idigam as -threats- to the world. They aren't necessarily interested in, say, wrecking the world as we know it, but they should have the capacity to do so if the mood strikes them. So they'd be more than just another spirit to deal with, so to speak. Not all of them, of course, but at least a couple.

                                                            I really enjoyed the stuff from the Night Horrors book about them being able to just pluck souls from bodies and replace them with spirits. The book suggested that only the (I forget how to spell its name) one from the Rockies defeated by Max Roman was able to do that, but it might be possible for others to do. Things like that, that go against the entire system, would be good. Being able to subvert rules of the World of Darkness that people take for granted like we do the laws of physics. Being able to rip templates from people and put them onto other things, even stacking templates in some mad amalgam of things, that sort of thing too.

                                                            I liked how the Strix chronicle gave some example Strix, with a wide variety of motives and strangenesses (one of them that hunts other strix, others that prey on humans, others on vampires, etc). So it would be nice to see a wide variety of sample Idigam. I think there's a hard-to-resist tendency among player to look at books and think, 'okay, that's it, that is all there is,' and getting rid of that inside the box thinking is an important part of these games.

                                                            Lastly I think it would be interesting to show what happens to those who decide to willingly follow the Idigam, as it doesn't matter how dangerous stuff is, if people (even Uratha) can see it as a path to power or fulfilling some other need, they'll take the risk.
