    Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
    In terms of "what doesn't work", we desperately need better reasons why Geniuses don't break the Masquerade. In 1e as its written, there's nothing stopping a Genius from, say, building a giant robot, having it smash a bunch of buildings, then filming it with his iPhone and throwing it on YouTube. I actually had the idea that Wonders interfere with mundane technology, meaning that they always look blurry and fake if you try to film them. And, fitting with the theme of futility and the struggle to actually change the world, I had the idea that people can see wonders just fine, but their memories cover them up.

    So, say a giant robot rampages through a city- people try to film it, but most cameras just glitch out. A few capture blurry images of a giant shape towering over the city. After it's over, most people swear that it was just an earthquake. The few people who do remember probably keep it to themselves, realizing they have no proof and no one would believe them. And that's how you can get away with giant robots rampaging through cities and still have a masquerade.

    On an earlier thread, I also mentioned having Manes be Twilight entities similar to Spirits, as another Masquerade measure, and I believe someone actually wrote out rules for this. To keep Mane Geniuses plausible, maybe we could say that Catalyzing gives a Mane the power to manifest?

    And, finally, I think Genius is rich and complete enough that it doesn't need to be a ChroD fangame. I would completely support creating an original set of mechanics and releasing it as an independent product.
    I was the one who made those rules you mentioned, but I'm not satisfied with them. Giving normal Manifestations to Manes, even if tweaked and powered by Mania instead of Essence, makes them too close to spirits. Conceptually, they're closer to hobgoblins - a big aggregate of weirdness that is quite corporeal and shaped by ideas/stories. Perhaps they would be capable of shifting into the same frequency of Twilight as Goetia and Astral projections, but it would not be a universal ability. The more advanced or stranger Manes might have additional ways of interacting with the material world and the Mania within, but that would be rare.

    The Illuminated, however, are strong candidates for being modeled with an adapted ephemeral being template, like the Strix and Idigam. Remember how the Strix have Embodiments that alter their pseudo-ephemeral forms and allow different types of possession? And how the Idigam can mutilate and mutate spirits with their Essence-Shaping? The Illuminated should have something like that, but focused on the direct manipulation, generation and dissipation of Mania. Heck, they might not even need to build Wonders: their mastery of Mania would be such that they bring Wonders, Manes and even Bardos into existence simply by willing it. Like how Athena sprang fully formed and armed from Zeus' brow.

    In my new vision for the Illuminated, the notion that they are former Geniuses who had all Obligation burned away by Inspiration is a rumor and cautionary tale, rather than a fact. No one knows for sure how they come to be, or if they always were. Even figuring out what they want in most cases is very hard, as they are exceptionally alien. I need to think of ways to make them more distinct from True Fae as well.

        Being the antagonist fanatic I am, may I suggest a little of both?

        Some Illuminated are almost definitely Geniuses who lost all Sympathy and no longer see the world as anything more than raw materials and a testing field for their dreams. These Illuminated often have a touch of tragedy about them, as their Magnum Opuses are often altruistic in nature; the overall theme (which I think should be something like Big Dreams, Little Dreamers-the passion and ambition is there, the capability is not, not without compromise and realistic expectations) is magnified and examined, showing exactly what happens when the dream makes no accommodations for anyone or anything else. Illumination is the light of pure knowledge and a higher truth, certainly, but the thing about light? It's blinding, and the Illuminated themselves are the most blind of all. I'm thinking something like Dr. Moreau; on some level, he wants desperately to perfect potentially revolutionary medical techniques, but he doesn't comprehend that turning a bunch of innocent animals into beings whose very existence is torture is causing more problems than it solves-and the sad part is, the very fact his beastmen live is proof he's on to something. On a less sympathetic note, Yohji Amada from Binary Domain creating a truly sentient AI-by, and I quote "instilling the concept of suffering into [it]." Grand dream, awful methods (Larvae on crack). Perhaps something more akin to super-Lemurians, but the possibility should be there, if only to show why Sympathy is needed.

        Others, though? Others seem to have come out of nowhere. They may be (hopefully) particularly unempathetic posthumans from a distant future, Mania elementals given a body, Manes embodying disproven subconscious theories (and thus not possessing a recognizable ego, only the urge to create), real no-shit extraterrestrials, all of these, none of these...Those Illuminated embody the mood (which is something like Endless Possibility, Endless Terror-a truly open world is also one truly open to things that can go wrong). Their past, what they are, is pointless-what they do is the entirety of what anybody should be concerned with. Much as how VII is the vampire's vampire story, these Illuminated are the mad scientist's mad scientist story-they appear from nowhere, set up shop, and unleash a tide of wonders and horrors into the world that no one is able to truly explain, only hypothesize.

        The difference from the True Fae? Gentry have obvious motives for alien actions. Illuminated? They can explain perfectly well what they're doing, and anybody who studies their black miracles can quickly grasp it. But their motives? Even madness seems too mundane for the experiments of the Awful Ones. Just as a complete lack of Sympathy prevents them from understanding you, so does it prevent you from being privy to their natural-disaster minds-only the aftershocks.

        (Don't think they should have ephemeral rules, though-not entirely. Perhaps they have full mental stats but P-F-R for everything else, like how Embodied Strix have physical and spirit stats. The core, soul, and heart of an Awful One is the nuclear engine she calls her brain. Everything else is but meaningless tools of it, and can be recycled and retrofitted as needed).

                Sympathy was the new term for the Integrity meter in the document. I actually like Obligation a little better, so using that from now on.

                Love the Conflagration idea for pseudo-rank. We could also create a similar structure of Dread Powers for Illuminated, with each dot of Conflagration providing a different power related to the direct manipulation of Mania (we could call them Plasmas to keep the "celestial fire" theme, or it could be the different powers of the long-fabled Axiom of Pankosmoi, if we really want to drive in the "alien knowledge" thing-Lemuria's obsession would then be not so much discovering the Final Axiom but being somehow able to learn it while sane-by Lemurian standards), and different families of pure Dread Power to synergize with their Axioms. I figure that the groupings are based around the narrative qualities of Illuminated (with my working names based off of Greek philosophies-something the Lemurians came up with to try and quantify the unquantifiable): incomprehensible knowledge (Eleatic), the rapid reshaping of the world (Sophist), and the consumption of all things to fuel ambition (Pythagorean). Most are based more around buffing their Axioms and Wonders or messing with the abilities of others.

                As for the God-Machine, I'd say Illuminated are the closest thing you can get to actual reliable theorists on how occult physics work-they seem to have an instinctive understanding of a truth beyond paradigm that may be occult physics. Then again, could be something completely different, and in any case, the Awful Ones are, if anything, even stranger than the Machine (at least you can guess at the mindset and motives of angels). For its part, the Machine would love it if the Illuminated would leave, as it regards them as living natural disasters-at most, it can point them in the direction away from itself, but when one's dealing with the maddest of the mad, Infrastructure they find tends to be poked.

It is virtually impossible that their gimmicks and secrecy could be so effective that it would be enough to keep their existence a secret.

                    The only reasonable answer is that the people of the WoD KNOW, but they prefer to keep their head down and not get involved in anything. It's a survival trait that develops in a society where the supernatural tends to cull hard anyone who is too nosy and sticks her head out.
                    THe majority of WoD people are almost certainly very superstitious and paranoid, but try to only think of the good things to protect their necks and sanity.

                    (I did once initiate a hypothetical fan template called "Agent", which was essentially MIB mixed with Paranoia the RPG: people who aren't sure that their own memories are real or that they are even still human and who maintain a sense of paranoia and secrecy even with each other hunt down supernaturals who have broken the Masquerade and witnesses who got away, all in the service of an alleged worldwide conspiracy and world government.
                    Morality stat was called Paranoia and compelled you to do things like never using your real name (Mr Brown and Mr Smith), never admitting everything you know about something, and so on.
                    Renown stat was called Influence which you could swap for all kinds of social merits or equipment, to the level of having a hotline to the President to order a nuclear strike at the very top.)

                    It could be easy to rework it for Genius, since Genius is good at modelling stuff like this anyway.

                        Hello/welcome Firanai! Glad you like Genius.
                        Do you maybe want to play-by-post it?
                        I might be willing to GM a forum game for a sufficiently MATUREbunch of players.

                        Moochava isn't making a new version soon methinks - he said updating all the tangly details (starting with the bookmarks) was already too much of a bother in the 1.1 version.
                        He does respond to fan letters and questions though! (via his email on his site)

                        We've had discussions over at rpg.net as well as this board before - really too much to collect all of the good/bad ideas.

                        Me, I'm against any so called "new edition" that starts to tinker with the parts that work. You do that, you fail as an amateur game developer.
                        GMC version of Genius - I'm glad if some folks try to do that, (personally I don't give a sh#@, I think GMC is a crap system )

                        What I'd want to do for a 2nd edition would be a minimalistic change list for things that just plain don't work.
                        Here are the things I prepared hypothetical changes for:

                        - Made Mania recovery more dynamic

                        The current default method is "research for X hours", which is a time investment that players skip anyway, and all it is good for is to holding up the game when PCs are on the clock or need to do stuff together. What I wanted to do was make players able to do "research" while on the move, and interact with the enviroment more - for example, disassembling technology to see how it works would net Mania but also destroy said equipment in the process (they'd net Mania based on their estimated Respurces value). So if you pick the TV apart and rebuild it as a death ray, you still have to answer to the landlord about where the TV had gone...Something like that, maybe. (remember this is all hypothetical)

                        I also wanted to make the Resources requirement for Wonders more dynamic: you don't have to have the Resources MERIT to cover your expenses, you can just steal or otherwise acquire the materials for a one-time Resources bonus.


                        - Switched up Metatropi rank order (and made minor adjustments to some other Axioms too)

                        Metaptropi users currently gains the incredibly powerful Sorcerer's Stone effect (turning one material into another), which can do stuff like insta-kill enemies, at the early 2nd level.
                        Yet changing size is somehow a Rank 3 effect. I switched all that up, so that now growing/shrinking stuff and attacking Size is a more basic effect (this is also in line with how the Collapsible variable works), so that there is a clearer progression from small cosmetic changes to more dramatic alterations


                        - Limited what a single individual wonder can do

                        Geniuses are incredibly powerful; each new Axiom rank gives them access to a host of new powers.
                        What's MORE surprising though is that there is very little limit to how much of this they can exploit at once.
                        Let me give you an example. If you want to make a Wonder that can do EVERYTHING at Apokalypsi 2 - you can!
                        All you need is a successful skill roll. There is an attempt made to make the Genius' rank in science skills act as a limit on this, but it's not too difficult to bypass with a lucky roll. (Plus, any Genius can near-max out only 5 science skills easily, even if also need points for other things, say fighting and social skills.)
                        There are variables that reward you for limited focus, but the tiny bonus from them is hardly worth the loss of flexibility they come with.

                        I intended to make it so that there is a bigger incentive to create specialized Wonders, to cull down on Genius power a bit and encourage players to make more unique ones.

                        (I recall when I was making a robot character and wondered

                        - Made "separate wonder" rules clearer.

                        This is really a formatting issue: some Wonders (like telephatic ones) are separated from others at the same rank in the same Axiom, but this is often unclear.
                        You don't need a "graph" or something like Moochava suggested; you just need to make the borderline clear in the text.


                        - Small updates to some variables
                        eg: "Assembly Line" is now a variable, not a merit.
                        (I could explain why/how it works but next time k?)


                        Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                        And, finally, I think Genius is rich and complete enough that it doesn't need to be a ChroD fangame. I would completely support creating an original set of mechanics and releasing it as an independent product.
                        I tinkered with my own "WoD alternative" systems before (to fuel all the fansplats that are being dreamed up if Onyx Path ever breaks off the permission - it is already impossible to storytell fansplat with GMC as you need THREE books (the core rules, the GMC update, and the fansplat book, very unpractical to the new player).
                        I could work on one if there's interest.

                        Though frankly, at the end of the day, you just want to play, and in most games 99% of the rules does not even actually matter.
                        All I'd really want is a better die resolution mechanic. (Success/Fail options do not an interesting story make)
                        Then there is the matter of years of skill mattering more, a better targeted shot system etc. ...actually, I may want a ot.
                        But frankly you only need the basics for a good game. You can already take the best of oWoD/nWoD/GMC/other RPG system of preference to create a good not-so-original system that does just fine for a game, and playing is the whole point.

                                                            Originally posted by Firanai View Post

                                                            I would really love to play. The mad genius trope is one of my favourtie ones from all time. It's such a tragic an amazing figure. The super intelligent scientist who can create amazing things capable of changin the world, but it's misunderstood and rejected by society. From the optimism of Tom strong to the pessimism of Rick Sanchez, both aspects of how well or bad can it go.
                                                            By the way, I'm curious, what do you think about the proposals I posted? what do you think about them?
                                                            Which comment would that be, is it #7 on the first page?

                                                            I think that a Genius who is trying to cure herself of her Inspired condition can be a fascinating character. Certainly a good story in that.

                                                            That said I'm not sure if I agree with the way you went about it.
                                                            (Now, you can do in your own campaigns what you want, of course, you don't need to follow the book.
                                                            Still, these are some pretty crucial elements.)

                                                            When you say:

                                                            "In this process, the genius takes one of his or her wonders, and proceeds to turn it into something that regular people can use"

                                                            This isn't possible and this is why.

                                                            Behind real technological advances there is research. Ie. an attempt to understand how natural processes work, then trying to see if any of what that allows for that could be beneficial for humans.
                                                            How the real laws work is not up to the researcher. Sometimes you get unexpected or unwanted results. Sometimes it turns nature simply does not allow for something, or that the cost is too high to afford.

                                                            Wonders definitely DO give you what you wish for. But, although the laws that hold them together may SEEM real, they are really a product of the Genius' imagination plus the mystical mumbo-jumbo wecall Mania and Inspiration.
                                                            Remove Mania and the laws the Genius made up also disappear.
                                                            (Of course some Genii may argue that these laws are also true and real on some meta-level of a "higher" reality, but it does not make much difference from the POV of science in our "mundane" reality.)

                                                            Making a true, mass reproducable scientific equivalent of a Wondernot as simple as taking a Wonder and substituting the "real" laws. There may not even be an equivalent real law, or the same result may only be achievable through a completely different process.
                                                            You can't force the laws you need on reality, you can only learn more about its set laws. (And that's what Mania is all about, forcing the laws you want on nature.)

                                                            A genius who gets it into her head that she can make a Wonder "real", what she's actually doing is replacing make-believe laws with other make-believe laws, still all held together by Mania.

                                                            I'm not saying Wonders can never be turned into "real" machines. All Wonders by their very nature have at least a little real science in them. Replace the Aether Mechanism in a clock with a regular mechanism, you've got a clock. It may not be able to show you the Universal Countdown anymore, but it will still tell the time. For a Genius who's trying to reconnect with the munande scientist she used to be, rebuilding her Wonders in this manner can certainly offer some solace and clarity.

                                                            But this is very different from actual scientific advances, which can only be achieved by regular, mudane research, research and research.
                                                            If a Genius can somehow make a recovery from her Maniac state she may be able to resume doing this. She may spend decades trying to remake the Quantum Mechanical Butterfly she once could create from just fantasy and air, but for real this time. She may find that natural laws do not allow it, or allow only for a much weaker version
                                                            She may find that even if it is possible in theory, research will still need decades or even centuries to get there. That is the lot of a mundane scientist.

                                                            (On the bright side, she may realize once again that reality can be more creative than human imagination and that there are unexpected, unexplored possibilities in this type of world too.
                                                            Maybe she'll even end up preferring things being like this to what humans (her included) can dream up for themselves.)

                                                            IF SHE CAN ACCEPT ALL THAT, then she may be able to make a recovery.
                                                            If she is still trying to make Wonders turn real though, still chasing dreams, then actual recovery can't even begin yet.

                                                            What a Genius who wants to be a real researcher has to do is this:

                                                            First, she has to try to become a non-Genius. At least temporarily. To overcome a madness that clouds her eyes and think critically once more.
                                                            The obvious problem is that any laws that a Genius can dream up BECOME real. It's not so easy to find criteria by which one can separate fact from fantasy.
                                                            Of course, many of them ARE real too. Many Genii have a good theory which has ONE critical flaw. Sometimes it is a real flaw - the numbers don't add up, the logical process followed misses some required step. And sometimes the flaw is simply not being backed up by evidence.
                                                            But the Genius can convince herself quite easily that thing ARE being backed up by evidence. How will she know it's not? People have accepted various scientific theories for centuries because they mostly fit and anything that didn't was not confirmed to be (un)true.
                                                            But even if the Genius did try to confirm everything: Mania conspires to pull the wool over her eyes.
                                                            Whole BARDOS spring up to seemingly support one theory or another.

                                                            So here's what I think a Genius could do.

                                                            1. force herself to become aware of the flaws in her own theory.

                                                            It's really a self-seeking process.
                                                            Do any results seem to come to her too fast, too easily? Those MAY well be a product of her madness. (A bigger problem is if they aren't, yet the Genius still can't trust them. See how this is not easy?)

                                                            What happened during her Catalysation? When she thought she discovered something, it probably FELT real. Like she stumbled upon actual
                                                            Yes, there probably all sorts of small bits that did NOT fit but she could not see them.
                                                            Was there any time when she was going too fast, was too obsessed to pay attention?
                                                            Is there anything that would have seemed surprising or unbelievable, before she became a Genius?

                                                            The more successful she is at finding the tangly bits in her own theories, the better chances she has.

                                                            2. seek therapy

                                                            Madness is often routed in some sort of trauma for the Genius, often related to her Catalyst.
                                                            If she realizes what ulterior desires keep her in the madness place and solve them, that could "cure" her too.
                                                            It may actually be more sensible to take this approach.

                                                            Many Geniuses have the desire to prove themselves feel wronged by their peers and society, and so on.
                                                            So this COULD be where Inspiration comes from, too.

                                                            3. surgery
                                                            Well, not neccessarily real surgery. The idea here is that you identify the physical or mystical source of Inspiration and remove it by force.
                                                            Of course, one problem is, nobody's really sure what the source of Inspiration IS.
                                                            One option is to simply destroy random parts of oneself one by one until one is no longer a Genius..
                                                            Another option is to try this on somebody else first. Number 3 is definitely the preferred option of many mad scientists. Genii who study Inspiration are dangerous and feared for this reason - they need OTHER Genii to be their test subjects.)

                                                            IF the Genius completed the process (and nobody says any of these even really work), then he/she has to deal with the after-effects.
                                                            Like an addiction, Inspiration tends to leave behind a void.

                                                            Some Genii relapse or become Beholden, no longer able to believe in their own ideas but desiring the comforting feeling of there being a higher meaning, or organizedness, or a sense of morality, to nature. They need that certainty with which somebody declares the laws of nature to be so, and the only one they can get this from is another Genius.

                                                            A rare unlucky few turn Clockstopper, or a similar creature. Having lost faith in ALL science, Inspiration takes a 180 turn and what previously warped the world to contain MORE science now forces it to hold LESS science, STILL proving the former Genius right.

                                                            All of this is they make it in the first place. But here is a better question:
                                                            If somebody believed in REAL science and was content with it this much, how did they become an Inspired in the first place?

                                                            Every Genius transgresses at some point, venturing into the unknown driven by...something. They all sacrifice something, physically or inside themselves.
                                                            Faust sold his soul to the devil. Frankenstein stole corpses. Pretorius was excommunicated by his peers.
                                                            Inspiration is really a sort of madness. Not something you get out of the blue and definitely not something you get against your will.
                                                            If that spark is not lit, if the Genius can not put his knowledge and work before what other humans hold dear in order of importance, then he will not Catalyze.
                                                            Every genius once throws away conventions - including scientific conventions - in exchange for the secrets of the universe.

                                                            I don't think it's impossible to try to go back on this choice - a Genius' path is fraught with disappointment and dissatisfaction.

                                                            (Though this also has to do with their unsatiable hunger - a Genius may not neccessarily think his condition is WRONG or not better than the mundane method, they just still don't feel like it's ENOUGH.)

                                                            But if you go by how mad scientists normally operate, would they drop a tear for their colleagues or the public, for the cowards who were too WEAK to see the truth?
                                                            No way! You, alone, a human superior than the rest of humankind, can truly understand its meaning, truly appreciate its beauty.
                                                            Without madness there is no "mad" science. If you are a scientist, but you're not mad. If you're not mad, how could you ever catalyze?

If you crush an evildoer with a giant robot, even if the robot will only exist as long as the Genius does, they are still crushed.
