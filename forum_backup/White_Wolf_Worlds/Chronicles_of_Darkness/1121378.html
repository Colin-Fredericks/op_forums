    Let's Brainstorm: Cooking a Chronicle w/ xiongrey

    07-27-2017, 01:34 PM
    Sorry for the cheesy title ^.^;

    So i'm creating a chronicle for my fiancé, My brother, and his wife. None of them frequent these forums and are not likely to so I feel pretty safe posting this.

    I'm brainstorming some ideas for the Chronicle and I feel like it would benefit from other people piping in ideas if they so chose so I'm posting on the forums. If no one posts, that is ok, I will continue to brainstorm this until it's all hammered out.

    So what is the game about?:
    In very Broad strokes, It's an overarching story that starts with innocents of the neighborhood kids and their brush with the supernatural and moves onto a campy "horror camping trip" story that ends with the story moving into one of several game lines: V:tR, W:tF, M:tA, P:tC, C:tL, G:tS, D:tD, B:tP (notably absent are the lines that will not likely see a 2nd Ed by the time the second Story is concluded (so in the next year and a half. Also absent is Hunter because my sister-in-law does not seem to want to play Hunter.)

    The players do not and will not know at the time that I'm working towards one of the game lines and by the time they find out, it shouldn't look as if it was going anywhere else. How will I choose which to really pursue? Well I'll watch the players and the characters and see what they're enjoying about and through out the first story (if they're really enjoying RPing the fear and the running than that's a hint they might enjoy C:tL w/ these characters, but if they choose to fix things with a passion then that's a hint they might enjoy a W:tF game. And if they try to use circumstances to better benefit their characters in someway than that's a pretty clear analogue to V:tR. These are just examples, it'll be subjective.). Secondly, the first act of the second story (them in the woods) will introduce quite a few plot hooks/foreshadowings/Chekhov's guns. These are analogous to a play style or modus operandi of a game line. Following one or taking much interest in one chooses how the rest of the story goes (imagine Cabin in the Woods and how the "horror" was chosen a third of the way into the movie).

    Examples of this would be: if the characters immediately go to the power station higher in the mountain, their need to fix things first and foremost would indicate a W:tF plot (werewolves like to fix things more spiritual but it would be good). If they immediately attempt to follow the random symbols on a few of the trees near the campsite, convinced that they must mean something and start following the clues then the story gets colored w/ M:tA tones. If they explore a little more despite the warning than it becomes a C:tL game. You get the idea.

    The chosen direction will color the weird things that happened in Act 1 and it will be a fairly straightforward campy horror story from there on with it ending in whichever story line.

    tl:dr: First story is an Innocents game a la Stranger Things/Super 8/Goonies/ect. Second story is campy college kids horror in the woods story w/ diverging pathways each ending in one of eight game lines.

    The Process:
    Trust me, this is less ambitious than it sounds. I originally started this out with it becoming a C:tL game solely and mostly fleshed what that would look like out but then got to thinking that I could do the first two stories and have them end in almost any Game Line that could want. So here we are. A lot of this is going to be subjective to the players. So the main three things I need to do are:
    1. Identify, in broad strokes, what about each of the eight game lines the players would enjoy (or players in general) and then seed that into the stories.
    2. Provide foreshadowings appropriate to each theme that clearly attract the players in that direction if that's the kind of direction they like playing with.
    3. Fit the trappings of the chosen Game Line into the plot and feel of the Second Story so that each choices addresses at least some of the foreshadowings.
