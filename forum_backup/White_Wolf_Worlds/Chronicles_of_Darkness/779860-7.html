        The closest to concrete that we have so far are these things.

        Conclusions are attached to an Attribute's dots.

        Strength, Dexterity and Stamina: Destroy, Travel and Protect respectively.
        Intelligence, Wits and Resolve: Sense, Transform and Restore respectively.
        Presence, Manipulation and Composure: Invoke, Control and Calm respectively.

        Disturb is a special case in that it's addition to rolls is graded by Serenity - 10.

        So for example, if a Siren wanted to Travel to the Underworld, she could use "Self-Death-Travel" and roll Akasha + Dexterity for it when doing it A capella.

        I have no idea what to do for Stanzas, but I believe that it would be like casting a Mage Rote (Attribute + Skill + Akasha), although in this case the Attribute MUST be the attribute used by the Conclusion (So in this case Dexterity + Skill + Akasha) and the skill could perhaps be anything the Siren wanted it to be? For example, if "Self-Death-Travel" was to go to the underworld, then logically "Occult" would be a skill for that. If she wanted to use it to make a dead body move to a location, then it would perhaps be the "Athletics" skill being used instead. In short, depending on what the Siren wants to do with the Stanza would dictate what Skill she wanted to use.

        I don't know whether we should use the "Rote skills" that Mage uses. What do you all think?

                Ooh, boy. By complete, I assume that you mean "complete with each definition". I think we have a working list, at least. I'm gonna try to add definitions on the fly.

                [Introductions]
                These parts of the Verses dictate what the Verse is targeting.

                [Self] targets the Siren's self and body while [Object] targets an inanimate object with a Size that is equal or less than the Siren's Size. Because this Introductions have a supremely limited scope on what they may affect, they add a -1 Pneuma modifier on the overall cost of the Verse

                [Target] targets a specific creature that is within the range of the Siren's Verse as determined by the value of her Akasha. This Introduction has a cost modifier of +0 Pneuma due to how subjective the Verse may be in affecting the Target. This Introduction will also be affected by Supernatural Tolerance if the [Target] is supernatural.

                [Location] targets a location with defined boundaries, such as a park lined with a fence or a piece of property. This Introduction has a cost modifier +1 Pneuma due to the fact that the Verse only affects the location itself, not the creatures within the location's boundaries.

                [Area] targets creatures with a location defined by the range of the Siren's Verse as determined by the value of her Akasha. This Introduction has a cost modifier of +2 Pneuma as it allows the Verse to become innately ingrained into a location for a set amount of time. When a creature enters the [Area], they are affected by the Verse as if the Siren targeted them using the [Target] Introduction.

                [Global] targets an area that extends beyond the feasible range of the Siren's Akasha but may still be considered a region, such as an entire city or the expanse of a river or even an underground cavern. Because of the nature, this Introduction has a cost modifier of +4 Pneuma; the sheer scale of this Introduction covers a great amount of targets,

                [Opuses]
                Opuses are the elements that the Verse is attempting to affect or contact in context within the [Introduction]. Each type of Water provides selection of Favored Opuses that the Sirens of said Waters are able to obtain at a discount. Each Opus has a selection of interpretations that would affect what the Verse is capable of affecting in relation to the Opus. Literal interpretations of the Opus will allow the Verse to behave at its strongest and without question. Conceptual interpretations stretch the definition of the Opus but could be feasibly related to the literal interpretation. Opuses used as their conceptual interpretation provide a penalty to the Verse roll, as the Sirens are attempting to wrap the Verse around a stretch, kind of like creating a metaphor that could be taken in a different way. Symbolic interpretations relate to the connotative implications of the Opus rather than the denotative definition. Because some Opuses are based on elements that are not always corporeal, some of them would need to rely on a symbolic interpretation in order for the Verse to make sense. Like with conceptual interpretations, symbolic interpretations also provide a penalty to the Verse roll as the connotative implications of the Opuses may range depending on language or culture.

                Opuses may also be used in conjunction with one another to specify what element is being affected, sort of like a Venn Diagram that focuses on what overlaps. However, additional Opuses add +1 Pneuma cost for each additional Opus used beyond the first Opus.

                Of the Opuses that are not favored by any Waters, there is [Water] itself, [Sound] due to a Siren's connection with The Song, and [Beauty] due to the association between water spirits and attractiveness in most myths and folk tales.

                The Water of Polaria favors: [Darkness] for shadows and the absence of light or even nightmares, [Death] for dead bodies and ghosts, and [Freeze/Frost] for things that are literally cold or are on "cool-down"

                The Water of Tropicana favors : [Light] for sunlight or light sources and fire, [Passion] for emotions and love, and [Temper] for things that are hot or are being heated up like a debate

                The Water of Gardenia favors: [Earth] for soil or stones and unrefined metals, [Animal] for creatures that aren't capable of free will, and [Growth] for vegetation or things that are capable of growing like mold or a movement

                The Water of Vaporia favors: [Wind] for air currents and breezes, [Weather/Sky] for things that are naturally occurring events like sandstorms or rain, and [Pressure] for air pressure or things that have a heavy metaphorical weight like decisions or guilt

                The Water of Freshwater favors: [Human] for mortals untouched by the supernatural, [Technology] for electronics or devices created out of refined metal such as cars, and [Fate] for fortune or things that are related to the idea of destiny

                The Water of Discordia favors: [Blood] as in blood or possibly even flesh, [Taint] for impurities or decay like rust or oil spills, and [Chaos] for junk piles or situations that can be described as chaotic

                [Conclusions]
                Conclusions define to what extent the Siren is able to manipulate the chosen [Opus]. Each Current provides a Conclusion for the Siren to utilize. The Pneuma cost of each Conclusion varies depending on what the Conclusion is meant to do. The Conclusions also provide the other half of the Dice Pool for the Verse.

                Conflict: [Protect] (Stamina) allows the Siren to preemptively [Protect] the chosen [Opus] from being affected by outside sources, keeping the [Opus] in the state that is was when the Siren sang the Verse. The protective capabilities of this Conclusion provides a +2 Pneuma Cost.

                Healing: [Restore] (Resolve) allows the Siren to [Restore] the chosen [Opus] to a previous state in which the [Opus] was not damaged, polluted, or otherwise affected by outside sources. It may also refine or purify the chosen [Opus]. The ability to restore what was broken provides this Conclusion with a +1 Pneuma cost.

                Harmony: [Calm] (Composure) allows the Siren to gradually slow the chosen [Opus] until it comes to a rest. It may also temporarily stop the [Opus] for a duration before allowing it to continue. While the abilities of this Conclusion are predominantly for utility or defensive purposes, the ability to stop certain events means that this Conclusion provides a +2 Pneuma cost.

                Buoyancy: [Travel] (Dexterity) allows the Siren or whoever she targets to freely move through the chosen [Opus] as if it were water. In terms of using a Verse upon a [Location], it connects instances of the [Opus] like portals within the [Location]. This Conclusion's abilities would provide a +1 Pneuma cost.

                Wisdom: [Sense] (Intelligence) allows the Siren to [Sense] an instance of her chosen [Opus] within the area she targets. The utility of this Conclusion and the lack of an offensive/defensive application means that it provides a +0 Pneuma cost.

                Acheron (Void): [Disturb] (10-Serenity bonus to Verse Rolls) allows the Siren to affect the chosen [Opus] by jostling its stability, causing it to react violently and chaotically. While this definition is vague, it allows for a variety of events to occur simply because of one chaotic event, providing a +2 Pneuma cost.

                Cocytus (Dark Wisdom): [Transform] (Presence) allows the Siren to change and manipulate the appearance of the chosen [Opus]. The components and aspects of the [Opus] become malleable and bend to the Siren's command like clay. The illusive nature of the Conclusion provides a +1 Pneuma cost.

                Lethe (Mankind's Victory): [Control] (Manipulation) allows the Siren to direct the chosen [Opus] and influence it on a localized level. The [Opus] moves under the Siren's commands and flow in a way that would defy logic. The range of command provides a +3 Pneuma cost.

                Phlegethon (Dark Passions): [Invoke] (Wits) allows the Siren to summon the chosen [Opus] into the area, even if it is not present or would logically not exist in the area. It is a Conclusion that causes things to appear at an instant and within the Siren's command, providing it a +3 Pneuma cost.

                Styx (Primordial Darkness): [Destroy] (Strength) allows the Siren to command the destruction of the chosen [Opus], causing utter devastation in its wake. The level of destruction does vary depending on the [Introduction] and [Opus] but it is a swift action, akin to a sudden revelation. The sheer power of this Conclusion provides a +4 Pneuma cost.

                                                            I think it would be a good idea to look at Demon's Demonic Form, considering that most of the mechanics would work similarly. Effectively, a Siren's Aquatic Form is akin to the Demon's Demonic Form in that it allows them to use all of their Alterations. However, a Siren can partially transform her body and only use some of her Alterations. For Demons, they have Modifications, Technologies, Propulsions, and Processes. They gain a fixed number when they are created and gradually develop more once they hit a certain amount of Primum. They may also lose and gain an amount of abilities when they gain Primum.

                                                            However, most to all of the Siren's Alterations are passive with some allowing the Siren to perform special actions tied into her Alterations, like an ink pouch that allows the Siren to fire out smoky clouds of ink into the air. In this instance, the Alterations are more like Beast's Atavisms as they are something that is fundamental to the Siren. I feel that the main difference is that while supernatural creatures can see a glimpse of the Beast's true form when they activate their Atavism, the Siren's true form is only present when she becomes submerged in water. Another difference is that while a Beast's Atavisms are free action-based abilities that are free to use unless the Beast wants to enhance them using their Satiety, a Siren's Alterations are ingrained trait-based abilities that she may awaken with her Pneuma and keep awakened for a period of time.

                                                            However, the longer a Siren has her Alteration active, the weaker the Refraction that protects the Siren from being exposed grows. A few minutes of having her Alteration up is no issue; allowing her Alteration to last for hours causes her true nature to rise up every now and then, with scales causing her skin to reflect in the sun or her teeth sharpening to that of piranhas. I would say that every scene beyond the scene the Siren activates her Alteration necessitates a roll by the Siren to keep a hold of the Refraction effect, with a penalty per scene equal to the amount of Alteration the Siren has active and allowing the penalty to be cumulative and gradually growing the longer the Siren has the Alterations active.

                                                            As for what her Aquatic Form allows the Siren to have, the most-obvious abilities would be to have the Siren breathe underwater and swim, possibly at twice her Speed. She would also have all her Alterations active and able to be used. The Siren's vulnerability to losing limbs also disappears; while the Siren is in her Aquatic Form, her limbs do not dissolve into sea foam. If the Siren loses a body part while she is in her Aquatic Form, she may retrieve her limb and attempt to reattach it as long as the Siren and her limb are in the same body of water; if her limb is thrown onto land, the Siren may attempt to bring it back into the water. The internal logic is that, because the Siren is connected to The Deluge and the waters of time, the Aquatic Form prevents the limb from dissolving by halting time for the limb and turning time back to the moment when the limb was removed. However, if the limb dissolves into sea foam or if the Siren leaves the water and regresses into her human form, the limb will not attach without actual medical assistance. Effectively, the Siren's Aquatic Form is in-and-of-itself an Alteration that is only awakened when the Siren is submerged in water.
