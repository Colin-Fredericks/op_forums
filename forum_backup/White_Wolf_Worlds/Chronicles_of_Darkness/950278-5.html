                                                Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                                It does call out though that most Genii never get off the ground. They lack funding, or just plain common sense, enough to never really amount to anything other than quacks. A Mage can resculpt reality with only their mind - a Genius actually needs to build something to be anything other than a delusional mortal (in most cases).
                                                It's not just this though. Let's start with the Genius numbers.
                                                Originally posted by Genius Pg. 38
                                                Estimates of the genius population before 1990 or so guessed that one person in a million was a genius, giving a total world genius population of a few thousand. That was wrong: once the Inspired got online, superior communication allowed for a revised estimate: there were, probably, at least a million geniuses out there, and many of them wanted to talk to one-another. The Internet was a revolution, and it continues to be so: there are now a million Inspired online (though many probably have no idea what they are), with estimates of the total genius population being anywhere from twice to ten times that.
                                                So (just doing crude estimates) that puts the Genius population at 1/3 per 1000 to 5/3 per 1,000 (If we round down to six billion). It might just be an idiosyncracy of mine, but when I hear those numbers can't help but apply that to other types of supernaturals. Vampires, werewolves, changelings, mages - Putting them at those numbers I have a hard time wrapping my head around either a setting where the supernatural hides from an unknowing mundane or one where the mundanes willingly hide the supernatural that they know from themselves. And Genius is on shakier grounds because 1) The mechanics Genius uses to keep Geniuses under a masquerade are iffy, being mostly societal as opposed to innate and 2) Most Geniuses by their very nature are not disposed to stay in the shadows. This might sound unfair to Genius, but let me put it this way; weird as it sounds, I'm okay with a setting that has two to ten million Geniuses. I'm a lot less okay with the setting saying explicitly that there are two to ten million Geniuses, because that invites me to start thinking too hard about that.

                                                I like a lot about Genius, but this has always been one of the things that bothered me about it.

                                                        mp122984 I dug out an old email chain between Moochava and AlgaeNymph on this for you - and it appears that the numbers you're quoting are basically a bug. Moochava intended Geniuses to be less common. That's in addition to what Vent0 said. Also Geniuses are intended to be an usually populous group of supernaturals so I wouldn't apply the numbers to other groups directly.

                                                        Here's the full email chain:

                                                        Originally posted by AlgaeNymph
                                                        The high estimate for Inspired in the world (p.27) is 1:5000 (which is /a lot/, fanon population estimates for metanormals are ~1:50K-100K), but what are the typical and the low estimates?
                                                        Originally posted by Moochava
                                                        The typical estimate is around 1:15,000, while lowball drops down to 1:40,000. There are almost certainly more geniuses than other types of metanormals. However, many of these are lonesomes. Many of *those* in turn don't even have any Axioms; they just have a dot of Inspiration and ten points of Mania: they're intuitive code-breakers, gonzo hackers, car mechanics who can work miracles, and other people whose abilities are so close to baseline humans that they're even less strange, mechanically, than the psychics and cultists that inhabit the World of Darkness. Other lonesomes have figured out how to build a few wonders but don't really understand what Inspiration is or can do. They haven't read the "game rules," basically. Geniuses who are as aware and experienced as the typical starter vampire are comparatively rarer.
                                                        Originally posted by AlgaeNymph
                                                        Ooo, interesting. Percentage estimates?
                                                        Originally posted by Moochava
                                                        Up to 50% of the genius population are entirely unconnected. This number is dropping fast, though; probably by 1% per year--it's just an increasingly connected world. About half of these lonesomes, maybe a little less--according to estimates--are below starting PC genius level, mostly just people with a few tricks. Even among known geniuses, there's strong clustering on the lower end of the scale. The more unremarkable mad scientists will go their whole lives at Inspiration 1 or 2 and a few dots in one narrowly-focused Axiom, repeating the same experiments over and over. These pathological types aren't encountered too often because they aren't such great PCs, and they tend to sequester themselves, working alone or in small, tight groups of like-minded folks.
                                                        Originally posted by AlgaeNymph
                                                        A comment on genius population statistics -- according to p.32, [Note, this is referring to the 1.0 pdf. It's the same text as the p38 bit you're referring too] the genius/mortal ration is explicitly ~1:6707 at minimum. You may want to emphasize how most of them have only a dot of Inspiration, don't know what they are, and so forth.
                                                        Originally posted by Moochava
                                                        Yeah, I'll consider making the population statistics more explicit, though I really want to avoid anything that'll close off people's ideas of the games.
                                                        Sadly that bit never got patched.

                                                            To put the issue into a bit of perspective:

                                                            10 million Genii, if they were their own country, would be around the ~90th by population (they're almost the population of Portugal!) depending on which list of recognized countries you use (keeping in mind that there are ~200 recognized sovereign nations on Earth, again with some variation depending on recognition status and such). If it was an independent religion it would fall between Judaism and Bahai in population.

                                                            Obviously the idea is that they're not organized by a single location or a common ethnic/social structure like those, but that's the kind of numbers we're talking about here. The idea they're supposed to be unknown to most people just doesn't work at those levels.

                                                            If it was a game that didn't bother with a "hidden shadow world that most people don't recognize exists all around them," and just went with them being a thing? OK. Sure. 10 million works as large enough to be a thing most people know exists, but rare enough that you might not know one if there just aren't any near you, and a "quiet" one could blend in fairly easily without anything to explicitly single them out to normal people.
