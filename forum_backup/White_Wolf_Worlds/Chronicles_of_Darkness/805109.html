    A little question about Vampiric Buddhism in the West

    01-10-2016, 06:08 AM
    I know that the title seems strange, but bear with me. Just before going to sleep yesterday, I suddenly thought : "What would I become/do if I were to be Embraced ?". It's a strange question, and the strangest part was possibly the answer that I got : being mostly atheistic-agnostic in life, but with some interest in Buddhism and animist religions/faith, I was left with some serious questioning, especially for character concepts. Some years ago, I would have probably envisioned myself going toward the Carthians as an obvious choice, but as time passed, I've grown disillusioned with human parties and politics, so I've now a hard time seeing me joining any Covenant (the two "political" because I've lost faith in Politic, the two "Faithful" because I don't believe in God and I don't like the idea of strict "churches"/"worship cults", and the mystical Ordo Dracul, while very attractive, would probably be abhorrent for me in the early Requiem).

    But I still believe that people Embraced need some semblance of "solace", or at least something to rely upon, and I think that people like myself could turn toward whatever a "proto-faith" they had in their life. the problem is : what would be Vampiric Buddhism ? Especially here in the West ? As of today, there is many, if not temples, at least place of prayers for this religion in most Western Countries but I've no knowledge of Buddhism being portrayed in the WofD and the CofD; the other main faiths in the West (Judaism and the various branches of Islam), I've some knowledge of how they are portrayed from the basic Lancea et Sanctum, and anyway, being all abrahamic faiths, I could see how to tweak the Lancea the right way to have true Covenants specifically for the Judaism and Islam. But Buddhism resting on very different basis, with even less unity between its sects than in other great religions, I really can't begin to know from where to work on it.

    As a bonus question : would a neonate as I could see myself being could feasibly try to take control or at least ingrain himself/herself into the Bouddhist community of his town ? After all, it often seems to be a part of the population mostly left alone by the western Vampires.

                  Originally posted by MCN View Post
                  Officially, Buddhism has shown up in two books - the first was incorporated as part of the Ordo Dracul methodology as a way of achieving Transcendance. The second was as its own covenant in Tibet led by the Naga King, the vampire equivalent of the Dalai Lama.
                  Not Tibet, actually; Thailand. The Naga King was the ruler of the Kindred in the Sukhothai Kingdom, a Nosferatu representing himself as a sort of demon within the framework of the pre-Buddhist, Hinduism-influenced folk religion of the Thai people. He was converted to Buddhism after encountering an itinerant holy woman known only as the "Mae Ji" (more typical current transliteration would be Maechi). The Mae Ji seems to have been the originator of a system of beliefs and practices resembling some of the teachings of the Ordo Dracul, and these teachings have been passed down and perhaps refined by Buddhist vampires in Thailand ever since.

                  It's not clear if the sort of Kindred Buddhism practiced in Thailand has spread very far outside of Thai lands. Assuming that the fortunes of the Thai Kindred followed those of their mortal relations, their culture and institutions may have made some inroads into what are now Burma and Cambodia. The Mae Ji herself is said to have ultimately gone away to the west, leaving open the possibility that he teachings reached other kindred, and possibly even influenced the development of the Ordo Dracul in Europe.

                  This is all from Ancient Mysteries, by the way.

                  In the Beijing chapter of the 2nd edition book, it's apparent that the vampires of China have their on philosophical can mystical schools that bear some resemblance to the Ordo Dracul, and have been influenced by the Rites of the Dragon since it became available in China. In Beijing itself, these schools are the Way of the Dragon and the Dragon's Path, but there may be other, similar institutions elsewhere in China. Like mortal Chinese Philosophy and Religion, I suspect that these groups would draw upon a variety of influences, including China's various schools of Buddhism.

                          Basically? The Ordo Dracul is the Transcendence covenant, which is where (for the standard western covenants) any kind of ascension, transformation, or even Golconda seeking tends to end up. Seeking out a state of Nirvana is thematically close to that idea of Transcendence that its inevitable that the Ordo Dracul would absorb at least some of the practices.

                          There's some question on how, of if, buddhist vampires would develop other advantages along with the Coils. Blood alchemy (key for the development of Scales), despite everything, seems to be the main Modus Operandus of the Ordo Dracul, followed by geomancy (wyrm nests). In the past, you could justify buying up the Mind of the Unblinking Serpent style merits through buddhist style learning, but there's no equivalent in 2e. Even the Rites of the Impailed tend to be more scientific. So, do keep in mind that this isn't a perfect fit. And, if we go by the Ancient books, there is actually a kind of rivalry between the Ordo and the Naga, sparking over into violence.

                          How all this manifests is going to be up to you to discuss with your ST.
