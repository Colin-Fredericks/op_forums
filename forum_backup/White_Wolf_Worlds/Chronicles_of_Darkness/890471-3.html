        Here are my two cents:

        1)

        The God-Machine is the remains and magnum opus of the First Alchemist, the first man to ever make contact with the principle and sacrifice to it in order to gain power. As the years went by and he gave up more and more of himself - itself-, he only became more hungry for power and sought to drag the Divine Fire itself down into his clutches, becoming the Fire itself. In the end, though, he realised that binding the Fire to himself would be impossible and such an apotheosis might as well be obliteration. In his fury and rage at the final thwarting of his plans, he made one last sacrifice, forging himself and what remained of his soul into a mockery of the angels of the Fire. Thus, was the first Angel born, a being whose singular mission was to find a way to capture and command the Principle. This first, nameless, Angel built the first piece of Infrastructure to support itself, drawing upon the Essence of the universe to sustain its existence in the absence of the soul which had allowed its creator to pull down the Fire. The Angel determined that it alone was not and would never be powerful enough to achieve its goal and set about forging the chains which would enable it to bind the Fire, first by weakening the influence of Its eternal change upon the world by rendering the world utterly stable and unchanging and then by reaching up into the realms from where the Fire issued and cooling it. The God-Machine's ultimate goal is to make the world into a beautiful, perfect, clockwork nirvana where nothing ever changes and everything is predictable.

        2)

        The God-Machine was built in some far-off corner of the universe by an ancient race and its sole purpose is to exist and impose order upon reality, counteracting entropy and thereby slowing the universe's descent into the cold oblivion of the Big Freeze.

                                                Originally posted by ajf115 View Post
                                                Of course, by doing so you are (if you're a demon) writing yourself out of reality because the thing that created you never existed. If not, you're still creating a temporal paradox, because if the GM never existed, how would you know to go back in time and destroy it?
                                                Explicitly according to Mage, temporal paradoxes aren't a thing. Think Corridors of Time for a moment - every time you use it, it ought to cause a "temporal paradox" because you are going back in time to change your own history, which negates the need to go back in time.

                                                And technically from a logical standpoint, temporal paradoxes should never be a thing anyway. Any universe where alterations to the past are possible means temporal paradoxes are impossible:

                                                A time traveler from Timeline 1 goes back in time to meet himself and convince himself to change a decision he made/will be going to make. In Timeline 1 they never had this conversation, hence the need to go back in time. Timeline 1, there is only one instance of this time traveler, so we'll call him Iteration A.

                                                Iteration A enters Timeline 2 and meets his previous self (who we will call The Precedent). Under a "closed loop/prevent paradox" model, after talking to The Precedent he would have to also inform The Precedent of the need to go back in time and when to do so to "close the loop" and have the conversation with himself. However, none of this conversation happened in Timeline 1 and there was only one instance of himself so clearly Timeline 2 is either a different universe or a universe that is already coping with alterations.

                                                So instead of "closing the loop" Iteration A has his conversation with The Precedent to change his mind over that important decision he was going to make, tells him to keep up the good work and then Iteration A decides to go back in time again to 30 seconds before he originally landed in Timeline 2 to go meet himself again and say hi.

                                                At this point, Iteration A is now entering a distinct new timeline - Timeline 3. At this point because there may be confusion, let's change his tag to "Iteration B".

                                                Timeline 3 creates a binary scenario - either Iteration A shows up 30 seconds after Iteration B lands in Timeline 3, or else Iteration A does not appear at all.

                                                The first scenario indicates the universe is endlessly rewriteable - it can causally fold back on itself endlessly based on the actions of a prime time traveling actor.

                                                The second scenario indicates that time travel is multiversal - that a time travel jump simply inserts energy backwards in time, causing a universe that diverges from its parent-timeline and infinite such divergences are possible but they are non-interactive. That energy is lost in the original timeline's universe and gained in the second timeline's universe.

                                                Quod Erat Demonstrandum: There is no universe where both (1) time travel is possible and (2) the past can be altered that also have "temporal paradoxes" which require you to "close the loop". If time travel exists and you can change the past, you can alter your history and thwart your own existence as much as you like, time traveler you won't have to care.

                                                        The short version, from the logic side at least, is that a closed loop scenario only works in a universe where the past isn't changeable - in any other model not only do you not need to close a loop, it's actually not physically possible (you wind up with there being two yous in the time period you originally left).

                                                        While you can't discount memory manipulation in the original timeline (well, in our universe you basically can, but let's ignore that) sequential time jumps can easily prove or disprove what scenario is actually occurring regardless.

                                                        Oh, also as a subpoint - because closed loops only work in universes where the past isn't alterable, you don't need to stress about them because the loop will always close... because you didn't change the past.
