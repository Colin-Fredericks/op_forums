                        Well, it also depends on the "Skill" level of how optimized the Mage uses his magic as well as the thematic scenario he accompanies within the game.

                        I mean, really, a crying widow in a funeral home is probably not going to put up much of a fight most of the time in that scenario. Likewise, the corporate overlord of Chicago who regularly uses his magic to tilt the odds in his favor with the focus of a surgeon is probably going to be hard to take down.

                        Really, I think we should establish the general competency level of the mage in question. Because while I get that mages are powerful as a template, not ever mage is going to have the same "go to" spells, nor will they think to use them the same way. I mean, face it, some mages are genius level intellects who can creatively manipulate spells in all sorts of whacky ways, but they probably aren't representative of "all mages".

                        Another thing to consider is the relative... difference in experience points between mage and hunter PCs. Objectively, we can determine that Mages receive about... 18+ exp worth of stuff as part of the template just counting the starting Arcana dots (the cost of the 6 default arcana dots, while I am unsure of their price in 2e probably does go to around 20 and that's not factoring the other things). Hunters meanwhile don't get as much free stuff, just the option to spend merit dots on Endowments, which I suppose to be fair tend to be rather overpowered for merit dots. Still, probably a good idea to factor that in when designing an encounter.

                                                    That, and the notion that Sleeper vs. Sleepwalker is strictly a binary thing also bothers me: either you’re completely and absolutely subject to the Sleeping Curse and always forget any encounter you have with Supernal Magic, or the Sleeping Curse has no hold on you and you have no more trouble remembering Supernal Magic than anything else. Never anything in between.

                                                            Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                                            So you'd prefer stages of repression where the sleeper sort of remembers but dismisses it as something mundane a la Lunacy?
                                                            This is, in fact, how it works. You only get total amnesia if misremembering something as a mundane event is absolutely impossible.

                                                            The kicker for Sleeper Hunters is "witnessing obvious magic", as well as being the thing that gives mages Paradox, inflicts an automatic Breaking Point on the Sleeper doing the observing. And supernaturally recovering the memory of a spell when Quiescence has taken it provokes another one.

                                                            Also oft-forgot in these kind of conversations is that Sleeper presence doesn't cause spells to fizzle out - it wears them down eventually, but being a Sleeper will not prevent a mage from fireballing you in the face.

                                                            The most important thing's the Breaking Point, though. Not only is the Paradox inflicted not that big an inconvieniance to the mage (it's only a point of Mana to buy it off, after all) but being a Sleeper Mage-Hunter means your career will be short, sad, and pissing Integrity.


                                                            Sleepwalker is a one dot Merit. Most Tier-3 Hunters are automatically Sleepwalkers anyway. If you want to use the Awakened as enemies, use them.
