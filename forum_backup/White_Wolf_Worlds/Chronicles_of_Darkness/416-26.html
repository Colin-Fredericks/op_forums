    I believe, Zap, that you might be confused about a fundamental premise of the nWoD line. In cWoD, there was a metaplot, there was canonical history, there was specific information about variations across time and geography; in that sense, it was more static. Sure, variety was accounted for and explained, but it was like having dozens of setting books, and because of the meta-plot, it felt more like the setting was dependent on using that published stuff.

    For nWoD, they wanted to abandon that more or less entirely. Every line is introduced as a "toolbox". You can take what you want, toss what you don't, and ultimately there's very little to contradict what you do in your stories; there's no "right way to play". This is one reason the Tribes (or any organization splat) seems broad - so that you have room to come up with regional variations on your own. It is static in the sense you're talking about - 8 Tribes, all over the world, all throughout time, and the published information only really shows the general information about them, giving the impression that it's always been the same. (this is kind of subverted in Tribes of the Moon, where they do a lot of discussion about the tribes in different times and places.)

    They haven't always been the same; that's a silly thing to assume. Like you said, there are thousands of reasons why they wouldn't be. The difference is they didn't publish much about that, so that you can come up with your own. it's a feature, not a bug.

    If you compare the various settings which have been published, like Shadows of the UK to Hunting Grounds: The Rockies, you will find the diversity you are looking for. They just aren't overt about it because the assumption for the whole game series is "make your own". That assumption does make it harder to find examples of diversity, but that's what the setting books showcase. Which is appropriate for supplements; I don't think its something that needs to be put into the new corebook, personally.

            But that is exactly it, Papa Bear, tribes do form around usually more than one of those focus points.
            Forsaken tribes do exclude the idea of family as a tribe basis as it is done in Apocalypse (some families can end up always being in one tribe, but that is more like every one from family x being in the local chess club), they are not regional but global in a game that explicitely cut down on the higher level of werewolf society (no septs anymore, just individual packs and how they interact with their neighbours).

            They really do focus on ideology and belief, that is, at least the corebook gives not much more than that.



            Also, I want to say that I think that criticism about just people who have not read all the books clamouring about stuff they want to see changed is a bit hypocritical.

            People, like me, who do apparently like the idea of a werewolf game (otherwise I would not have bought the corebook or anything in the first place), but who do not really like Forsaken as it is will not be likely to buy more and more books if the ones we do buy do not satisfy us - and especially when we cannot get anyone interested to actually PLAY them...

            I think no one in my group was too thrilled about Forsaken, I was not too thrilled, I bought Blasphemies (because it might fix some of my problems) and Skinchangers (which does broaden what kind of shapeshifting myth you can incorporate. And I read a bit of the chapter on other Shapeshifters from War against the Pure and the Pure in a bookstore when I had to wait for someone anyway.
            I later bought the chroniclers guides (which had some interesting stuff) and I browsed through a number of other Forsaken books, but my incentive to buy any more was not too great.

            I think as there is in general a lot of RPG books to buy and as I did invest a lot in both the Dark Eye and GURPS as well as in a number of World of Darkness books, I did not really end up going for more with Forsaken.

            ...but I think exactly those who like Werewolves, but do NOT like Forsaken as it is now are the ones who might be won over with an Update.

            Many have said before that the new World of Darkness Lines ARE toolboxes, not prepackaged toys you use as is.

            I think Werewolf shoudl emphasis this more from the start.

            Maybe the problem is that unlike other lines, the concept of Werewolf is quite narrow.

            Vampires get to form their Clans from general stereotypes of vampires, both from fiction and mythology, the Stryx chronicle adds the focus on the more Wiedergänger Nachzehrer like Vampires as well.

            But while stories of animal transformation / animal spirits are extremely common, werewolf, both lines though, do focus on a subset, namely both werewolves as a "race" (you are born werewolf, you are not a normal human ever), not on ideas like "magical" shapeshiftings or bargains with suupernatural entities or curses (ll of which Skinchanger did offer) as well as, of course, wolves.

            It is an extreme contrast to lines like, say, Changeling, which are extremely diverse in what they can be.

            I think the fact that people do not like Tribes as much is because without ethnic background, there just is not as much to base differentiation on, so the overlap with Auspices might be hard to avoid completely.
