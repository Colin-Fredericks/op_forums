        The scientist fills in for the witch at the edge of the woods, with her knowledge of herbs and weather patterns, her baleful distrust of the elders and her wisdom reaching far ahead. The scientist fills in for the priest, his invocations telling the populace how the world is, his miracles pushing forth the promises of the gods. Likewise, the mad scientist, with his radiation and anthrax and mind-control radio waves, is nothing more than the witch with blood sacrifices and poisons, the priest who evokes the gods who stand for the end of civilization.

        I admit, I've been playing a faulty game.

        Truthfully, Genius is untenable by going on the 'visionary'/artist route, because it will eventually crash into that Mage problem time and time again-and ironically, It will also crash the fuck into Mummy, sooner or later.

        Realistically, what I think Ge-no, not Genius, mad scientists-what I think Mad Scientists should be is a minor template or Hunter Conspiracy, a collection of merits that offers possibilities within the confines that best fit mad scientists in the ChroD game. It should really surrender this notion of cosmic affecting/traversing shit-or at the very least, the entire thing needs to be treated on a smaller level for what is there to really magnify. If we have to play mad scientists as a major template, then dig really fucking deep into the archetype and what has emerged from it since it's inception, work on making it more and more distinct and more more solidly mad scientist, while spreading the skirt and exploring what that means without crossing the lines back into the Mages. Fuck, that's what should be done even if you went the "collection of merits" route.

        This is why the visionary argument is bullshit, because it's a way to justify Genius as is, but in such a way that it's even more superficial and superfluous, makes it seem more pathetically like a Mage ripoff. Even if you wanted to go the artists/sociologists/etc., Genius'd have to play that card very carefully, and honestly it's succeed more by downsizing the reach of the character if it wanted to go that way.

        My main point is that, while I don't agree with this idea that the visionary/artist route is the way to agree, I do agree with the feeling at the core of it-that Genius is anemic, that it lacks substance of it it's own, that it has no meat worth investing in. The heart of the argument is that Genius is lacking, and that's fundamentally true. I'll entertain the messenger, the visionary/artist route, because it carries that message.

            I'm not sure I agree completely, but I admit to seeing major similarities between Axioms and Arcanum, and between Wonder Creation and Creative Thamaturgy.
            There are similarities - thats pretty inevitable since the WoD does mechanics in fairly broad strokes.

            I'd say the key difference between them is how much a charachter can do when their devices are removed. In Awakening Mage who looses is staff has a small disadvantage, but if he still has full access to his Arcana. In Genius a charachter who looses his jetpack cannot fly at all until he reclaims it or a replacement.

            This means that Awakening would find it rather hard to mimic the very common plot line where a mad scientist or a science hero's devices are taken away and they have to solve the challenge without them.

            But fair's fair. Genius has it's limitations for charachters who fit the themes but are not device builders too.


            Still. I don't think it really matters that much how different or similar Axioms and Arcana are. The thematic differences between the two games are what matters. As I said above origonal Doctor Frankenstein was an alchemist and Mary Shelly drew on Shakespear's Prospero when writing him. Mages and mad scientists have a lot in common so there will of course be plenty of overlap in both directions: Both games are about people who live and think apart from regular society and weild strange powers normal society cannot touch.

            But Mage is about how that power corrupts*. Genius is about how that power** (and all other power) is ultimately an illusion. That's the difference, all else is elaboration.

            * Especially in 1E where it was stated as the theme in the introduction.

            ** I use the word power, but it might be more accurate to call it "control".
