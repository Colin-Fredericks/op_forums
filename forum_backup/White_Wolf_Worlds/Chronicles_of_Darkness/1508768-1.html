    I personally find it very worthwhile to see the perspective on what they sell their philosophy as when becoming Hollow isn't a basic point of initiation and when they have to fit into the Diamond sect, which is defined by the fact that each Order is seen as a component of an idealised magical society. That latter one was actually a notable sticking point for me, and I feel as though this write-up does make the reconciliation fairly central (including with acknowledgement that it's not exactly a smooth fit).

    Plus, it might have to write from the perspective of not assuming everybody reading has access to Nameless and Accursed. Tome of the Pentacle is about the history of the Orders, so the entire exiled Diamond Order has to be significant in that context in its own right, even if it turns out that the philosophy is recognisable to the modern version (that has no qualms about describing itself as part of Atlantis).

    Still and all, it does seem to suggest that Arc had a point about this not being the most novel preview for people to clamour for.

                Originally posted by Michael View Post
                Although it raises the question that it's still a rewrite, it is different. Are we meant to take away that the Tremere have meaningfully changed?
                I can't bring the new text right in front of me for the moment, but putting the best I can recall of it against the Night Horrors write-up, I find a notable distinction between the original idea of Hunger primarily in a more abstract form of an actively pursued and cultivated ambition, a sense that enlightenment is there for the taking and must be yearned for without reservation, and the version of the Order in which all are Hollow and the Hunger is a much more literal thing of "you have converted yourself into a void through which the vitality of souls can be fed as sustenance for the Seventh Dragon". Indeed, there's quite an emphasis in the write-up for the modern Tremere that they escew the abstract in many regards, that they place a greater value in the lived experience, the direct action. I think that's a foundation for why they view themselves as superior to the rest, that their philosophy expresses itself very directly upon their physical and metaphysical forms compared to other Orders who might be regarded as applying their philosophies as more aspirational or allegorical.

                That, I think, would be why the shift in magical symbolism works. The former is for when most Tremere are experiencing Hunger as an idea, the latter for when that gaping is experienced bone deep in a fashion that is considered most revelatory and validating to their ideas about the relationship between subtle and gross. A tenth century Tremere might leave their yearning alone for a while with no ill effect, but a modern one viscerally carries it with them every single day.

                So I see these things and I do indeed believe that there's a transition that runs deeper than just "they're all Hollow now and don't align with the Diamond sect".

                                            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                            From write-up, I do not understand them and their place in Medieval Diamond.                                             BTW: Do we have date when Tremere were expelled from Diamond alliance?
                                            If the continued usage of the Dragon symbology wasn't already a tip off, the short answer is "they brought increased knowledge of magic and implementations thereof to the Diamond, and even after their explusion, their inclusion had a marked benefit to the Diamond."

                                            In some of the specifics, the Tremere are explicitly spelled out as the most concerned (in their time) in the mysteries of the soul and the mastery of the Subtle Arcana, intent on utilizing the lot as the whole fruit for cider as opposed to a more sliced up version for juice (as it were), and tackled the harshest ends of getting wisdom and secrets from gods and monsters. Their ambition and dedication to shaking the boundaries challenged what was at that point more than a millenia worth of established traditions, which was useful running all the practices and ideologies of the Diamond through Chestertons' Fence.

                                            In an era where humanism had yet to come into formal philosophy, the Tremere undoubtedly sowed the seeds for the future shape of the Silver Ladder (and by extension the Free Council) by emphasizing the power and right of humanity to grasp, wield and consume magic as opposed to being inclined towards systems of intercession. Their interest in the unification of the soul and the effects of the Watchtowers upon wizards, for better and for worse, would supplement and bolster the Alethian traditions of the Guardians of the Veil, and also uncomfortably the secret center of the Tremere would be a valuable set of lessons learned by the Guardians in terms of their Esoteric Tenets and the practices emergent from them. THe Adamantine Arrows would likewise gain valuable insight into warefare-as-magical-enlightment from their god and monster hunting ways, and the Mysterium are arguably only a phrasing of precept away from being Tremere.

                                            And of course, the Tremere brought in the draconic symbolism that has come to be a significant form of each Order's Shadow Name since then. None of this is a small thing.

                                            Originally posted by Michael View Post

                                            Sure, but my point is it's already happened. The point of no return has already been crossed, it's simply a matter of how long it takes the Diamond to notice.
                                            The Tome of the Pentacle is there to explore, among other things, the history of the Orders of the Pentacle Alliance and how they ended up the way they are today. The Tremere's public facing Order contributed a lot to the shape of the modern day Diamond in terms of just sheer contribution , both from what the orders took positively from their inclusion and the ways that the various orders filled in the hole with the Tremere's expulsion-it wasn't that long ago you brought it to our attention that the Silver Ladder did have ambitions against vampires, and that probably has it's roots in the way the Tremere helped them define their relationship to monsters and in taking on the role of paternal humanist priests, as mentioned above.

                                            It's also significant because the betrayal of the Tremere has left wounds on the Diamond that persist to the modern day as taboos, particularly as deep study of the soul goes and ambitious exploitations of gods and other supernaturals go.

                                            And finally, it's important to understand how the Tremere themselves evolved. Arguably, if the Tremere never joined the DIamond and then suffered a civil war, the Tremere would likely be operating (more) openly (I don't think they'd ever be out and out open) as a major Sect of magic. The ability to reliably manipulate the other orders is also a likely reason why some of the Major Houses grew to the prominence they did in the wake of the expulsion.

                                            The Tremere, alongside the Keepers of the Word and the Pancryptiates, had an impact on the Diamond, and even the Pentacle Alliance, that lasts into the modern day of wizardry. Understanding that history not only helps to fill out the setting, it also sets up story seeds for yonks and even suggests tools that players can explore and pick up that might not be obvious if you just take the modern Pentacle ALliance's word for everything.

                                            The past is power, in a setting where power is the point. Why would this be ignored?

                                                    I would say the argument for entry into the Diamond would be some combination of presenting a compelling thesis for how they could fit into the Atlantean model, initially presenting a greater focus on the hunting of monsters and Reapers in a way that managed to appeal, and perhaps no small amount of bribery with compelling secrets that they had acquired through their methods.

                                                    It should be considered that at this point in history, the Adamantine Arrow are not focused on being martial defenders of the Awakened; they primarily express their thesis through attaching themselves to the conflicts and causes of Sleepers. If the Awakened Nation is concerned with being safeguarded from monsters and active destruction of Reapers, there's a niche available for that.

                                                    As far as their crimes not being known yet, like... I'd presume the Tremere hid them. People conceal their crimes, it's not some massive leap that it took time and a greater degree of exposure and scrutiny for anybody to collect the data to form a more thorough picture of what the Tremere were actually about. If some Bound goes missing, there isn't an Amber Alert to notify every mage in the vicinity immediately.

                                                    And I'd say for souls they could very well be doing what is the norm for modern Tremere, i.e. "they don't consume those things to non-existence, they swap out ragged souls for fresh ones for the sake of discretion and sustainability".
