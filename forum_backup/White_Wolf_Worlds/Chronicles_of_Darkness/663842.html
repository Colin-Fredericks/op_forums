    Can True Fae be benevolent in a human way?

    09-14-2015, 12:42 AM
    I'm asking this because in my game there are both benevolent Gentry, and the normal kind (i.e. amoral, solipsistic, fickle, etc). The former are a minority among the ones that have been personally encountered by the characters. I am worried that perhaps it's somehow wrong to have benevolent Gentry who don't turn into Charlatans. Here are my questions:
    • What exactly prevents Gentry from feeling empathy and forming genuine connections with people, positive or negative?
    • Why do they necessarily have to become Charlatans if they become more human in mindset?
    • How does an ST reconcile Feuds for Titles and developing closer bonds with people? Could it be interesting to tell stories about conflicted True Fae that don't suffer Dwindling or Charlatanism?
    • Is it somehow "overpowered" for a True Fae to be capable of creativity and compassion, while retaining their characteristic powers of dream warping, Immortal Flesh and the like? For example, when I imagine the Doctor from Doctor Who as a True Fae, this is how I imagine he differs from other True Fae.
    • How could Wyrd Transcendence relate to all of this? I'm not talking about headcanons about Mage Arcadia in relation to Changeling Arcadia. I would like to know how you think Wyrd Transcendence affects the personality and metaphysics of the Gentry. They are instinctively drawn to it, according to Equinox Road. Perhaps the Gentry who are ex-changelings, born from compassionate dreams or otherwise coming from a background of closeness to humanity can have an easier time empathizing with people. This is reflected in what Titles and Feuds they seek, which might affect what Wyrd Transcendence gives them. I believe even the mad and capricious Gentry have free will and are accountable for their actions.
    • How does one establish a clear definition of what the Wyrd even is? It's related to Fate, both in game terms and etymologically. Is the Wyrd preventing or making it harder for most Gentry to approach humans as equals? If so, why? Can it be convinced to do make an exception?
    You don't have to answer all of that, of course. A simple sentence consisting of "I agree/disagree, and here's why" is enough.

      To my understanding, and with the caveat that I have no issue with games moving away from what I see presented in the books, as long as fun is had, and also that I haven't completely kept up with 2e spoilers so I wouldn't know if any of this is changing...

      The Gentry don't become Charlatans because they grow more human in mindset. They grow more human in mindset because they are becoming Charlatans. Or, in the specific cases you're thinking of, the two processes run in inextricable parallel. They are the same process.

      The Good Folk aren't people, really. They have certain qualities of people, narrative aspects and defining threads, you might say. They're descriptors, thriving their greatest in a world which is stripped down to description and narrative threads alone. They're not held down by the concrete human experience and attachments that we ascribe to personhood, and that frees them to do the most horrible and wondrous things. It's also what keeps them bound to their kingdoms in Faerie, parasitizing the human world to bring richness of meaning into their world of signifiers without signified.

      One of the Others which finds itself developing some ember of that inner life of personhood, the ability to connect with others and form genuine relationships, simultaneously gathers the strength to survive in the mortal world without constantly stealing from it, and surrenders its fateful might by extracting itself, bit by bit, from total union with the Wyrd.

      There's no question of overpowered or underpowered. That's only relevant when you look at specific game roles.* The importance of this idea is to ensure the True Fae retain their teeth as figures of horror and foreboding. The Lords and Ladies of Faerie can never be relied upon to have your best interests at heart; the greatest they are capable of is affection like you would show a favorite toy or heirloom. Something that might actually come to form a bond with you is more human than that, more understandable – and, in the parlance of horror, it loses power. Emotionally, at the base of it, and to keep that expressed in the game, mechanically as well. You could find a friend who will stand with you against the old gods, or direct another horror of equivalent power at it and hope you emerge from the crossfire unharmed. But you can't call upon God to fight beside you as a friend. You can only hope. You can win, but the uncertainty is essential.

      As for Wyrd Transcendence? Think of that as changelings going in the opposite direction.

      * The most obvious game role to consider, of course, is player characters. If you're asking about that, yeah, probably don't run an empathetic True Fae PC together with PC changelings. Too easily overshadowed, even accidentally.

              Can the Gentry be benevolant in a human way? No, not really Why not? Because they're not human. They're biologically, psychologically, and spiritually Other. Alien bodies, alien mindsets. There is a reason we call the True Fae forces of nature instead of evil creatures - because they adhere to Blue-Orange morality instead of shades of grey. Making them anything in a human way kind of undermines a good chunk of who and what They are. While you can do whatever you want at your table, making them less-than-alien is not something I recommend.

              What prevents Gentry from feeling empathy? Their alien mindset. There are no basis of comparison - you can't sympathize with someone if you don't think anything alike. Forming genuine connections? To the Gentry, they think that the Titles and pacts they make ARE genuine connections, and the human connections are an odd aberation. Again, part of how the Others are naturally predisposed to view the world.

              Do they necessarily become Charlatans for becoming human-like in mindset? Acting human is grounds for banishment from Faerie, which gets them kicked into reality and turned into a Charlatan. So, not directly, but yes.

              Why does the ST need to reconsile the two at all? Gentry act on whim all the time, and they can have a soft spot for certain people. Doesn't mean they won't go to war over titles. If you mean why can't the Gentry avoid battle? That's a different discussion altogether.

              Its not overpowered, no, but its not exactly thematic. Gentry feed on human dreams, desires, and wishes. They become the embodiment of said desires, and are often restricted by it. This helps explain parts of their nature. That said, its not like crerative sterility is really that critical to the game. And the Gentry are certainly inventive when it comes to their entertainment, so its not like they have zero creativity at all. Showing compassion on a whim is certainly within character as well. Being outright compassionate all the time, however, is thematically inconsistant, and might make your players lose some suspension of disblief over the matter. That latter is the largest concern with the issue. It all comes down to what story you're telling, though. If you've got a good reason for it, then go for it.

              Wyrd Transcendence undermines the idea of "human compassion." It requires Clarity 0 for a reason - the new True Fae is unable to tell friend from foe, right from wrong, completely divorced from reality. Characters at Clarity 0 are completely unable to tell that they're hurting someone.

              "I believe even the mad and capricious Gentry have free will and are accountable for their actions." Fair enough. No one said they didn't. That said, the True Fae live by completely different standards than humans. Its an impossibility to measure them against human rubrics. Are you familar with the legal phrase "not guilty by reason of insanity?" Legally speaking, that only applies when you are completely unable to tell that an action you take is wrong and that there would be legal repercussions for it. You could completely feel that shooting Sam is the morally just and right thing, but, if you know that others would punish you for it, you're not legally insane. If a True Fae shot Sam? They'd be completely baffled as to why people were making a big deal out of the issue. But wearing white after Labor Day would be tanamount to genocide to this Fae, and would be horrified that no one else cared. What barbarians!

              How does one establish a clear definition of what the Wyrd even is? Generally speaking, you don't want to clearly define what the Wyrd is until its critical to the story. Keep it flexible, and then it won't get in the way of the story you want to tell. You're completely justified in saying its the narrative force of stories. Another can say its the collective sentience of all human desires and dreams. Is the Wyrd making it harder or preventing Gentry from seeing humans as equals? Maybe, considering that its doubtful if the Wyrd is even sentient or aware. There's too much debate. Can it be convinced? Well, you'd have to actually FIND the embodiement of the Wyrd first. Good luck with that. If you want to make a story of it, and your players would have fun, go ahead.

                                      Same way a Fae pretends to be a human. You take the basic premise and follow it's internal logic.

                                      Also remember, it's an RPG. It's already imagination and storytelling not real life. The relationship between RPG players and their PCs and NPCs is quite like the relationship the Fae feel to themselves and the rest of the world. (I'm assuming it's not passed many people's notice that the descriptions of Fae attacking each other sounds not a million miles from an RPG)

                                      You may care about an NPC as they exist in the story, but once the game is over they stop existing and most people don't give it much thought. You don't keep an RPG going just to extend the life of a character (or you can, but much like becoming a charlatan, other people/fae will look at you oddly).

                                                  If a Fae does act benevolently by human standards, they probably aren't aware they're doing it.                                                   It's much more likely a benevolent Fae is only kind because that is what they represent / aspire to, rather than have any real compassion towards mortals.
