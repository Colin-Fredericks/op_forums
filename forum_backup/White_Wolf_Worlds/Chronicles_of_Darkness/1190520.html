    [2E] Solo Focused CoD gamelines

    02-28-2018, 09:41 PM
    So there is subtopic discussion of mine in Beast: The Primordial forum I think is showing much boarder problem some STs have with few of CoD games. Nominally, it's that some of those games are written as for Solo Games - 1 on 1 player and ST sessions - in mind more than group activity. Will focus here on 2E versions of gamelines, for having more clear picture, as some of them were under big rewrite. Here are my and Wormwood's points on this topic.

    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post

    You see, it's a problem with Beast: The Primodial me and few other STs I talked to have. It's all about 'my Beast', not the group at large - ironically to Family theme of the game. Werewolves hunt together in packs. Mages uncover mystical secret in cabals. Even vampires are doing their politics by pulling coteries resources together. In Beast you sate YOUR OWN Hunger. YOU are aiming to become Apex. Go YOURSELF for Inheritance. All those actions are done by ONE PC, not whole group. BtP, like Promethean the Created, seems to be made for solo games - the 'snowflake issue' - and this is problem for many STs wanting to run it as multi-players group. It's a MUCH less problem than PtC - but it's there. So this is why I need Chronicles Seeds - to see possibility for chronicle for few PCs as one story in BtP - not collection of solo games mixed together.
    Originally posted by Wormwood View Post

    I grant you that Werewolves have a very special bond as a Pack In-Setting. But Mages uncover mystical secrets in Cabals because they cannot do it by themselves, or a majority (In-setting) would. Coteries, likewise, break apart once they don't need each other anymore. Demon Rings (?) are more of an uneasy truce than anything else. Changelings and Hunters at least share similar traumata, but the idea of "you have come together as people who went through the same supernatural event" is the basic premise of the whole gig.
    A special bond that is shared by the members beyond that basic premise IMO only applies to Werewolf, Promethean and Beast. As a Werewolf player, I don't need to tell you about werewolves. Prometheans are united by a common goal, the desire to keep each other in check and on the right path, mitigate inherent drawbacks of their existance and metaphysically bind themselves to each other. Beasts see below.
    Regarding the 'Sate YOUR HUNGER': same is true for every other splat. Vampires sate their hunger, not their friends'. You cannot eat for a friend. Even the 'hunting as a Pack' thing is a desire of singular werewolves.

    I don't think Beast is made for Solo-Adventures (nor do I think Promethean is) - they work solo, yes, but so do most splats. However, they shine with a small group. Also, "Snowflaking" is more of a problem with Z-Splats etc, when people need to be "the only/first/last of their kind", feel the need to be more rare and powerful/useful/special than their fellow players or otherwise cannot be satisfied with a 'normal' template to breath life into. Beasts are merely quite customizable (though, at least mechanically, not to the same degree as Changelings and Demons). Not bashing z-splats here - I love them, they can add a great layer of meaning or depth to a character, but shouldn't completely define the character.
    Futhermore, the moment you play a one-person-game, (and as I said, that works JUST as well with every other splat as it does with Beast, with the possible exception of W:tF, imo) everything becomes 'Special Snowflake-y' very quickly (unless you specifically grognard it VtM style): Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Changeling, Demon, Beast, Mummy, Hunter, Geist - doesn't matter.

    Some more points on Beasts being about "My Beast":
    - One way of sating a Beast's Hunger is, literally, to help their Family members feed themselves (help Mages hunt for Artifacts, participate in a Siskur-Dah, help a Changeling get that Glamour...). This comes especially in handy for those Beasts that take issue with their Horror's Hunger. That is plenty of incentive to not work alone, and even go out of your way to help others. There is even similarity to how Uratha accept other Supernaturals if they help them Hunt - there is a purpose to the action, and usually mutually beneficial.
    - One major way of expanding their Lair (a.k.a. grow as a Monster/person) is to seek out places where your supernatural relatives left a lasting impression. The more the Beast learns about what took place, the more people and supernaturals involved they meet, the better their chances of finding and claiming a chamber. This is a very strong incentive to learn more about the occult history of the area, get to know the local things that go bump in the night (not just splats, but also horrors, classic antagonists, ghosts, spirits, whatever - the more you get to know, the better!). This is even mechanically encouraged by giving the same amount of dice for being the supernatural that caused the impact in question and having encountered the supernatural in question (+3). Even more dice are given if the Beast has Family Ties with this supernatural being (+5!).
    - Broodmates can draw on each other's Lair Traits to call down their own - literally exponentially making them stronger than lone wolves; a very literal "Power of Friendship". The Mother's Kiss even underlines this by giving Beasts a Mentor-Stance - the more powerful a Beast, the better it can support WEAKER Family members. This makes the most sense if both have a different focus and thus compliment each other. Furthermore, being strongly acquainted with certain splat grants you access to new powers: Nightmares are literally reflections of how other supernaturals (or even specific individuals) spread Fear - a Beast looking to increase its toolset needs to interact meaningfully with other supernaturals.
    - Like all the other splats, Beasts have unique antagonists trying to hunt them down - unlike in most other cases, though, their enemies get special powers that only work against the Children - plenty of incentive to try and invite other supernaturals into their Brood, to better defend themselves.
    - "Go YOURSELF for Inheritance". Well, first of all 2 out of 3 Inheritances are more like alternate game-overs/suicides than winning. Next, a Beast going for an Inheritance is very similar to a Mage going for Archmage. That is also one Mage going for it, not a whole Cabal having the goal of making that one dude Archmage. And if you say "well, they can support him and he relies on them" - well, duh, same for Broods.
    - Beasts get nice little set-ups to pretend to be part of another splat. This is to encourage IC exploration of other supernatural societies. Plenty of story-hooks here: Beast new in town, follows the first vamp into their society, becomes friends and boom, fudges up and their cover is blown. How will their alleged 'friends' react? Or: Beast went undercover in a Cabal for some time, but cannot keep lying to their Family any longer. But they might decide it makes a more interesting test subject than friend, or consider it a threat. On the other hand, they might welcome the options the Beast brings to the table. But then how will others react to them indulging a 'pet'? etc etc.
    - Not every Beast wants to be Apex, same as not every Kindred wants to be Prince. It's An endgame, not the only one. Plus Broods might like to have the Apex in their Brood, because it would give THEM ALL quite the substantial powerboost (one of our Lair Traits is in EVERY Chamber? AWESOME! LET'S DO IT!). So they might actually work together to make one amongst them Apex.
    - Core literally states that most Beasts have an inherent desire for companionship and do not want to be left to their own devices. That isn't too tough to roleplay, imo.
    - Beasts need to act physically, but also mentally and socially. They have the same needs as all other splats to cover the Heroic Groups basis (Be that Tank, Brawler, Smart Guy, Silver Tongue, Thief or whatever other template you want to use).
    And, most importantly imo for non-mechanical reasons:
    - Broods literally connect their 'Souls' and can take a hike in each other's soul. They even invite their Cousin Broodmates into their 'Souls'. You think this sort of connection will be as haphazard and instable as most Coteries? On the other hand, you doubt it would be as strong as a Promethean Branded Throng or Werewolf Pack?

    Honestly, the things you mentioned don't make sense to me. You see lack of focus; I see wealth of opportunities. You see "Hunting together" in Werewolf, but don't see "Family" in Beast?
    The way I see it, a Werewolf grows strong alone, to work primarily as part of a Pack - a Beast might (might! not does!) work alone, but mostly grows thanks to others.
    I don't see how that is, at all, problematic. Different, sure. It's supposed to be, it's a different gameline.
    Maybe it's just not your cup of tea.

      Now, my answering point's risen by Wormwood.

      1. There is very pressing narrative force in Mage to form cabals - i.e. other mages will get's the secrets you cannot get yourself. Not only this that stat starting mages are VERY weak and easily to be eaten by other supernatural forces of CoD universe. The only real narrative force for Beasts to connect is oppression from Heroes - and even this seems a bit stretch without some very powerful antagonistic individuals. About coteries in Vampire and circles in Demon - maybe I do not run this as games because of this in build selfishness? Maybe. Promethean is THE MOST 'My PC' game in CoD pond - it's whole reason to be is 'my character wants to be human - IN THIS WAY'. If character goals clashes, it's very little narrative force for them to be together - ST need to extensively sew Milestones for each PC to depend on other players only to said to first player ‘no, you did not marked Milestone with this awesome action – you need more of it… Or maybe other one for working on it…’. Changelings and especially Hunters are really good on narrative forcing players to cooperate - in Changeling you have whole aspect of 'you come from the same strange insane realm of torture together' ( shared PTSD, in reality ). And hunters are all about 'we MUST cooperate to survive the night, or the monster will kill us'. I do not feel the same in Beast - it's basically 'I like you guys, but...' - and there is then start of solo scenes like Hunger. Which leads us to...

      2. Hungers in Beast are VERY different. In Vampire - all PCs are craving blood. So joined scenes of hunting can be made. Even if not - seeking blood is very simple to devise on foot - so ST derail time for each PC on session is very little. Hunting in Werewolf is shared from logical reasons, it's a premise of game in fact. Getting Mana in Mage is complicated thing - but's there other PCs shine in a way 'we can make it work together'. Changeling works more or less the same as Vampire - as emotions are 'getting extra' for doing slightly more specific way of gathering, but whole motley can feed from the same powerful emotional scene. Promethean is once again ‘it’s my way of getting Pyros’ here.

      3. Points risen on the Beasts, as counterarguments – I will ponder on them more. Sounds a bit more convincing to work in game. But best would be to point where in rules of BtP core there is intensification of collaboration on Begotten groups parts.

                                                      Again, after reading through that thread, I have to say I heavily disagree with your point of view, Wyrd.
