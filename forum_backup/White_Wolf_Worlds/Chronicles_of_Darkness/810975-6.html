        This. This is where the tiers really fall apart.

        Splats aren't classes with focused roles. If Vampires truly were "the social splat", Nosferatu would be pretty well fucked. And calling all Demons master infiltrators discredits the entirely valid "cover shredding war machine". Perfect in game example: I ran a game about two years back, mixed party: Purified Immortal, Changing Breed (Bees!), Promethean and Changeling. Tiers would inform you that the Changeling's social acumen would put him at the forefront as the cunning, pledge-crafting illusionist, the Prommie and Shapeshifter were meat shields, and the Immortal struggled to hold his own. The actuality was that the Immortal was the gregarious, dominant face. Shapeshifter their intel specialist (and hacker), Prommie a quesing mystic with bokor tendancies, and the changeling, Giff Muldoon, a rock stupid ogre with nil for social skills and a habit of flinging himself fists first into gunfights.

        Long story short: The tiers were wrong, and if I'd planned around them the game would have been a disaster.

                            Originally posted by stylanski View Post
                            My distaste for the tier system stems from the reason it snuck into CofD in the first place. I will not expand on that, but it didn't do the fourth wall any favors in my games. I never understood why Vampires or Werewolves cannot get Cosmic level powers, and why it is considered taboo (in a toolbox system) to get a book dealing with global and cosmic chronicles.
                            I'm not sure really what the first part meant. Tiers was a Hunter concept originally and all it is really is just a matter of what scope the player group is dealing with in the storyline. It's not a measure of the individual potency of a splat or if it can transcend it's nature and become something more. You can have a cosmic "tier" Hunter story, mortals story, Werewolf story,etc. As for the latter part I think the important thing is asking "Why can't Vampires or Werewolves cannot get Cosmic level powers?" is honestly asking the wrong question from a development point of view. Each development team has a vision for the game world and those visions lend themselves to a default level of play, a so called vanilla chronicle. Mage doesn't dial itself all the way up to the cosmic level of interaction as a forward thrust part of the game because the Mage developer wants mages to be "better" than other splats any more than the street level average chronicle of Vampire or Werewolf. It's just a how some of the visions of the game have played out. In addition with stuff like the upcoming elders book for Vampire, the crazy stuff going on with that Uratha king who might not even be a Uratha at this point anymore(I don't remember it's name sorry) a lot of the games are starting to have material in that scope to play with.

                            Word count has more to do with why we haven't seen certain things I suspect, than anything else.

                                        Originally posted by ShadowKnight1224 View Post
                                        That seems like a wording problem, not an informational/content problem.
                                        You are talking to two people who have experience dealing with crossover games who are telling you that using these lists to plan have the potential for more harm than any good to a new ST. I provided a list of different supernaturals with relevant information that I actually use as guidelines, and have suggested to people running the game in the past. Do you have any actual experience in this matter, or are you just guessing?

                                        Do you know who actually needs special attention from STs in full crossover games? Mages - their magic rules are complicated as hell, and you need to understand how they work, and how drawbacks like Spell Tolerance, Spell Limit, Paradox, Unraveling, Disbelief, and Improbable work, as well as when spells need to be ritual cast instead of allowing Instant magic. Its necessary for a big part of the splat's themes, or they stop feeling like mages. Do you know who else? Sin-Eaters and wolves. They get Essence / Plasm from dealing with spirits and ghosts, or tied to a locus/haunt. So, you're either tied to a specific location, or need dedicated "feeding scenes." Or have a player that designs a PC based on essence / plasm efficency - I ran a SE once that never actually needed to deal with ghosts or haunts, and could recharge plasm through Virtue/Vice; it wasn't easy, but doable. Do you know who doesn't need special attention? Ordo Dracul vampires. But would you know any of this from any tier ratings? No.

                                        Actually, I'm fairly sure that the tier system was thrown out because it actually did more harm than good, iirc. I think there was a blog post that mentioned it. People started thinking in terms of straight jackets and defined blocks that couldn't be budged, when the tiers were supposed to be meant as an aid. If tier 2 is city-wide, and tier 3 is global, what do you call a game that spans three or so US states and a bit of Canada? What about when when tier 1 hunters join together to form a power bloc to protect their city? Is a Lucifuge not allowed to set up shop and worry just about his few bloody blocks he protects? Games needed to be more flexible than the tier system allowed.

                                        Honestly, the tier system existed when the first three games came out. It seems like, to me, that Werewolf, with its focus on pack over any larger organization, is a tier 1 game, city-focused Vampire was tier 2, and Mage with its global scale concerns is a tier 3 game. And yet, each of those games shift and change a lot. Mage, despite having global connections, is often hyper focused on individual Obsessions or what the local Seers are doing than some grand world wide chess game. Vampires are fairly mobile, and its not unheard of for covenants between cities to organize and talk and plan together - Carthians are renowned for it in some places, especially for their trouble makers who start a rebellion, then move on. Werewolves form protectorates.

                                        So, it really came down to the question of asking how beneficial the tier system was, and how much it helped stories versus hindering them. Combined with the fact that I'm fairly sure that the system itself kept being redefined. Fairly sure that Mage used a different system than everyone else, which led to confusion. Why keep something that only causes confusion?

We can say "vampires are tier 3 stealth characters", but unless you've invested in Obfuscate (or maybe Protean) that's not gonna hold true. If even a white room brawler's tiers are dubious how on earth can anyone expect CofD tiers to be accurate?

                                                            Tier lists are only true (and debatable even then) for the abstract concept of the splat, the idealized Promethean with all Transmutations and Bestowments. But they invoke in people assumptions about how a splat is supposed to perform that is, IMO, far more detrimental to crossover than any power disparity.
