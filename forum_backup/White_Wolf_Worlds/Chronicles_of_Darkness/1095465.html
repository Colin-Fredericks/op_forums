    New Covenant : The Harbingers of Skulls

    05-22-2017, 09:47 AM
    So I'm doing a mash up of owod, nwod and CoD. The Harbingers of Skulls are a Covenant that takes parts of the True Black Hand, Harbingers of Skulls, Giest the Sin Eaters (used as foundation for ghost rules). this will be used in a CoD game where I've done away with Bloodlines and made some of them Covenants. The Harbingers of Skulls use the Discipline of Mortis to interact and live in the Underworld. I would like feedback what to include in the Morits Discipline and the concept as a whole. As you can see it is not finished yet.

    HARBINGERS OF SKULLS
    You want to join the Harbingers of Skulls because:
    You have an affinity to death that surpasses that shared by most other vampires.Perhaps you were like this even before the embrace, all fucked up inside. Maybe your existence as a walking corpse has left you wanting to know more, even if it means going down to the Underworld to find the answers.
    While it is possible to come across the existence of the Harbingers by mistake, the most common method is to be invited by them.
    The big picture:
    The Harbingers are a secretive cult that lives in the Underworld in a city they call Enoch. A river of blood flows through the city and most of inhabits are ghosts.
    They have a large crypt that houses three sarcophagus.
    None has dared to disturbed them since inscriptions state that they are the resting place of the Daeva, Nosferatu and Ventrue Antediluvians. Theory persist that they are in Torpor.
    Where we came from:
    Eons ago a group of mages and vampires discovered a city in the Underworld. This group found evidence that the city was the spectral remnants of the first city of mankind, Enoch.
    Our practices:
    We watch over the Crypt of the Antediluvians and await their awakening. All other practices stem from this.
    Chatterlings
    Chatterlings are humans taken as infants from the Living World and raised in Enoch, who, if they are worthy, will then be embraced in adulthood to help usher in the Antediluvians awakening.
    Nicknames:
    Harbingers,The Boys Downstairs
    Concepts:
    Chatterling, Envoy to the Living World,Mortician, Funeral Home Director, Serial Killer,Seraph, Shakar,
    Wazir
    When we are in power:
    In the land of the living the Harbingers have very little power. What power they do have is behind the scenes as they manipulate kindred governments to usher in the awakening of the Antediluvians. The Harbingers of Skulls deploy seraph and Shakari across the world to perform various acts in secret.
    When we are in trouble:


    Lexicon
    Aralu: three mysterious beings whose crypts rest in the heart of the catacombs below Enoch. The majority consensus holds them to be the torpid bodies of Antediluvians.
    Cain: The first vampire. The Guarded Rubrics stat that it is the first murderer Cain, being marked by God as being the First Vampire.
    chatterling: the mortal servants of the Hand, whose name grew out of an insult derived from their propensity to tremble when speaking to their masters. Chatterlings provide a ready supply of slave labor and nourishment for the vampires of the Sect. A small percentage are abducted during infancy, then trained from a young age to be ghouls or prospective childer.
    Del’Roh: The title of the supreme governor of the Harbingers. Anadja, the current Del’Roh, earned it by being the sole vampire capable of approaching the Basalt Throne since the rediscovery of Enoch in the lands of the dead. Her lack of need for rest, and evident immunity to the negative effects of the Underworld upon other Kindred, are attributed to mystical properties granted by the Throne.
    Enoch: (alias: The First City) a city purportedly built by
    Caine, the progenitor of all vampires. Thought destroyed, its dark reflection was discovered in the Underworld and subsequently requisitioned as the capitol of the Harbingers. One unpopular theory posits that the city’s current purpose is to act as a prison for the Aralu, placing them as far away from the mortal world as possible.
    Guarded Rubrics: an ancient library entrusted to theHarbingers by the being called Inauhatan. The Rubrics is the most complete collection of vampiric history in existence.
    rawi:( plural: ruwat) a scholar of the Harbingers who, with other ruwat, protect and study of the Guarded Rubrics. When capitalized, the Rawi refers to the entire group as a whole.
    seraph: the generals of the Harbingers who answeronly to the Del’Roh. they oversee entire continents, enemy Sects, and regions of particular interest to the Harbingers.
    shakar: (plural: shakari) the professional assassins, guardians, and hunters of the Harbingers.
    Underworld: the spirit plane of the dead on the edge of nothingness, composed of ghostly echoes from the material world.
    wazir: (plural: wuzara) The wuzara determine the Sect’s direction and monitor the development of its long-term goals. When capitalized, the Wazir refers to the entire group as a whole.

    Nagaraja
    Staying in the Underworld for centuries and using necromantic disciplines has horrifying results. The most ancient of Harbingers develop into what is called Nagaraja, eaters of flesh. Three requirements must be meet for the condition of the Nagaraja to take place.
    First the vampire must be no less then 200 years old. Second, The vampire must have lived in the Underworld for a period of 100 years without leaving it.
    Third, The vampire has attained at least the second level of the Mortis discipline.
    The vampire develops shark-like teeth and must eat human flesh. Note that the vampire still must drink blood equal to it’s Blood Potency.

    Mortis
    Mortis concerns itself with Ghosts and the Underworld. Vampires that learn Mortis incur a major drawback. They find it harder to invoke the Blush of Life. Having one or two dots in Mortis causes the vampire to have to spend two vitae to invoke the Blush. At levels three and four, three vitae is needed. And at the fifth dot, four vitae are needed to invoke the Blush of Life.

    • Dead Like Me
    This level of the discipline requires no roll.
    The vampire can see,hear and talk to Ghosts that are within visual and audible distance. If the vampire spends a point of vitae, they are able to interact and feed from ghost as if they have become manifested for the scene.

    •• Storming the Avernian Gate
    With this discipline the vampire can see and enter an existing Avernian Gate, also called a Low Place or Cenote.
    Cost: 1 vitae
    Dice Pool: Wits+Occult+ Mortis
    Action: Instant
    Opening an Avernian Gate is technically as simple
    as reaching out and touching the door. That’s in
    theory, of course, but in practice it isn’t always so
    easy. Those who are not so powerful, however, can do things to coerce the Underworld into recognizing their morbid natures (“morbid” meaning the character’s intimate connection to death energy, not the character’s demeanor). Sacrifices in particular go a long way toward helping open an Avernian Gate; sacrifices of varying degrees can be found under “Suggested Modifiers,” below.
    Roll Results
    Dramatic Failure: Failing to open a gate,the vampire becomes marked by this failure: he may suffer death marks (gray or red striations like those sometimes found on a corpse), stigmata, deep bruising, or other forms of apparent morbidity. In addition, the character may not attempt to enter the Underworld again for 24 hours.
    Failure: The door fails to open, and the Underworld re-
    mains shut. The character may make repeated attempts, but each successive attempt suffers a cumulative –1 penalty.
    Success: The gate opens, and access to the Underworld is granted. Most often, this appears to occur as the pieces of the blocked path slide silently out of place — bricks tumble away with nary a sound, mortar fizzles to dust and whispers to the ground, concrete cracks and parts with only a faint cough. Once inside the door, the Underworld is initially clouded entirely in impenetrable darkness, and characters must feel their way along the rough-hewn tunnels or skull-laden walls for a minute or two — after that, the Great Below
    reveals itself in its melancholic splendor.
    Exceptional Success: The character’s expenditure of
    vitae gives him an intimate connection on this trip into
    the dark recesses of the Great Below. As a result, he is
    blessed for a time, gaining +2 to all Perception-based rolls made within the Underworld. This bonus lasts until he leaves the realm; upon returning to the living world, the bonus ends.
    Modifier /Situation
    +5 Sacrifice of a human life
    +3 Sacrifice of a living animal (must be
    bigger than a rat, and be able to bleed)

                  1. If they aren't player options then you have wiggle-room as to what they can do. Results matter more than methods.
                  2. This is critical -- if everything revolves on waking the antedeluvians, then either there is a plan they are working towards or they need to find something else to do. What I read suggests that they have this cool ability to see ghosts and go into the Underworld, but that's just window dressing... because they are sitting around some coffins... or else they're messing with governments to.. do something to do with these coffins...

                  If it were me, I'd look into other aspirations and goals, and things that might make them relevant to the game. They can glean secrets from the spiritual echoes in bones. They can fetter ghosts to havens to act as guardians, or to people they want to be haunted into insanity.

                  Here's a similar example:
                  1. So I'm in Medieval Europe. We have monks, who pray to God day and night in the monastery. Okay, so? How is this relevant to my lord and his retinue?
                  2. Well, in addition to praying alot, they also write books. Okay, that might be useful; the lord can commission a bible, a book of poetry, or a very friendly history.
                  3. And the abbot is politically connected and controls a goodly chunk of land. That is relevant to the king, and can prove useful or detrimental to the lord.
                  4. Sometimes monks will leave the monastery to use their considerable esoteric knowledge in the service of others. The lord needs a letter written, or his taxes counted and documented. His steward has come down with a fever and needs healing. There is a dispute and they need someone wise, impartial, and honest to adjudicate.

                  Monks have gone from "some guys who do stay behind walls talking to their God" to "someone worth our time to be nice to." I'm guessing most of the really cool Harbinger stuff will never realistically come to the coterie's attention (it sounds like super-secret, and likely incredible, stuff). But some of their more down-to-earth activities could be actually relevant and visible. They can be useful or they could be adversaries. And you could make it even more interesting if they could be both depending on the particular topic and day.
