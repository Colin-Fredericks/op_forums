        Millennium, for hunter games, especially for those focusing on the VASCU, Precinct 13 or other cop heavy games.

        Persona 4 (not a movie or tv, I know) was instrumental in getting me to protray the communities that chronicles take place in as places that other people live in, and that makes them worth protecting.

        Twin peaks - a small, isolated community that's slightly... off.

        Blue Bloods - not a show I found perfect, but the dynamic between the father (police commissioner) the two brothers (a cowboy cop detective and idealistic patrol cop) and the daughter (the DA). Whenever an investigation comes up the characters often have wildly different vantage points and concerns, which helped me form the organizational politics of the campaigns I run.

        True Detectives

        Orphan Black

            General WoD

            Games - GTA Series, Legacy of Kain, Resident Evil, Silent Hill
            Film - Blade, Blair Witch Project, The Crow, Dark City, Hellboy, the Matrix, Pan's Labyrinth, Pulp Fiction, Seven, Snatch, and a lifetimes worth of other horror films.
            Television - Buffy TVS, Constantine [#saveconstantine], Dead Like Me, Master's of Horror, Fear Itself, Night Gallery, The Outer Limit, Supernatural, Tales from the Crypt, Tales from the Darkside, The Twilight Zone.

            Films like the Blade or the Crow feel like movies where someone on the production staff was was White Wolf gamer. Perhaps more so in the Classic WoD, but the general look and feel of these films present a world that is a somewhat dingier version of the world outside your window.
            Horror Anthology series give me ideas for the multitude of strange occurrences and terrors (both mundane and otherwise). And shows like Buffy or Supernatural have so many interesting big bads, mythologies, and monsters of the week that it is hard not to snatch an idea or two. and the films of Guy Ritchie and Quentin Tarantino both teach the lesson that Style Matters.

            Vampire

            Film - Casino, Dracula (Bela Lugosi), Fright Night, the Godfather Trilogy, Goodfellas, Interview with a Vampire, Near Dark, New Jack City, Reservoir Dogs, The Usual Suspects
            Television - Breaking Bad, Gangland, Kindred:the Embraced, Law and Order: SVU, the Sopranos, True Blood

            My Vampire games owe much to the Crime genre. I see parallels between the All-Night Society and the inner workings of organized crime. I layer those ideas with a nice helping of actual Vampire Movies/TV, with personal favorites being Fright Night, Near Dark, and of course, Bela Lugosi in Dracula.

            Werewolf

            Film - An American Werewolf in London, Colors, Dog Soldiers, Gangs of New York, Glory, Ginger Snaps, Full Metal Jacket,the Howling, Platoon, Spaghetti Westerns, Tombstone, the Warriors

            My werewolf inspirations come mostly from film [I don't see many werewolf themes on tv, or at least on anything I have seen]. The I like war films for the valor and fatalism displayed [great for WtA], while I prefer films about gangs for WtF, for the violence, fast life, and "us against the world" mentality. Both of these genres place a great importance of the unit over the individual, important when running a game about packs. The westerns have these wonderfully majestic, yet somewhat stark and lonely backdrops that always feel appropriate for werewolf games.

            The Artificer's Workshop - A collection of Exalted Artifacts
            Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)
            The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
            Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
            Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities
