    Ideas for an Obrimos Death Legacy

    07-20-2024, 06:47 AM
    This is a just-for-fun thought exercise, but I've always found it very hard to come up with a good Death-Primary Legacy that actually fits the Obrimos themes and isn't just arbitrarily tacking on the Path. A few Legacies in general don't really resonate enough with their Parent Path and could work just as well (if not outright better) if they originated with another Path entirely (looking at you, Reality Makers and Whipping Boys). The problem is further compounded in this case because, firstly, Death is so strongly thematically associated with the Moros, making it hard to come up with a non-Moros Death Legacy that actually feels thematically proprietary to its Parent Path without going for soul stuff, which has kind of been done to death (lol?). Secondly, compared to other Inferior Arcana, Death seems the most thematically distant from the Path that has it as Inferior. Even write-ups in books like the 2e Core, Signs of Sorcery, and Summoners don't give much inspiration for a workaround, and in fact seem to reinforce this thematic divide between the Obrimos and Death.

    I've come up with a few ideas myself, but never ones that really satisfied me. One was a Legacy focused on the destructive aspects of Death and Forces, but this again didn't really feel like it resonated the most or even enough with the Obrimos and was basically a lame Masters of Destruction rehash. Another more promising concept involved the idea of "ghosts of dead stars" in link with the Black Huriyah from Mummy 1e's Book of the Deceived, with this Legacy seeking the black angels as the only remnants of cosmic embodiments of power, or maybe to appease them in the hopes of rebirthing dead stars or something. This idea fell flat due to it better working with Spirit rather than Death, especially since the Black Huriyah seem a lot like void spirits, albeit Duat-adjacent. I also couldn't think of a less nebulous goal/Theory for the Legacy. I've also seen some other fan-made Obrimos Death Legacies, but never felt any avoided the problems I outlined above enough. However, I'd be very interested in hearing other ideas from the good folks on the forum.

                  What comes to mind for me is admittedly not as broadly useful, but an Abyssal Legacy taking "inspiration" from the Deluge Obrimos Nasnasi align themselves with seems apropos.

                  The tricky thing with Death and the Obrimos, to not put too fine a point on it, is that thematically the Path of the Mighty relates to stuff that generally signifies sustained positive existence — light, fire, sound, presence, the Essence of the fivefold soul — and Death is chiefly used for messing with the end product of a given state of existence. There's no small number of Life-based Obrimos Legacies for the same reason the Path resonates with enduring and "perfected" materials like gold and steel, but even without the Death Arcanum being tightly linked to the Moros it's hard to find a Path-related basis for the category of wizard that has a radiant Nimbus and a hefty symbolic overlap with secular and sectarian power to focus on the bywords for the taboo that are darkness and the dead.

                                                      Ghosts are a big part of the Death Arcanum because they're a big thing resulting from death and revolving around death and caught in the tangle of the world's death flavoured metaphysics. Moros see all that in terms of transition and finality and the friction against those things, but that's not the only way of seeing ghosts and their big part of the Arcanum.

                                                          Originally posted by Michael View Post
                                                          To my mind, the Moros arcana symbolism is deeply anti-ghost. Like, yeah, their Path gives them power over them, but it's all about change and moving on, the thing that definitionally ghosts don't do.
                                                          I wouldn't go that far. I think there's an empathy for ghosts, even if there's a concern for ghosts resolving their anchors and moving on. In fairness, that's a concern ghosts often have as well.

                                                          I think Moros perspective on ghosts could well just be not that they are people who died but can't move on but people created by somebody else's death made up of what they were attached to in life, and often with a driving existential goal of seeing those anchors to their conclusion.

                                                          Sometimes that comes from a place of thinking the ghost goes somewhere, sometimes it's just about letting the last thing they do in an unpleasant existence be fulfilling and purposeful so they slip peacefully into non-existence.

                                                          Moros can be concerned with the larger mystery of why people can be so attached to life in the face of death that it can create entire people to continue embodying those attachments, but that need not always take precedent over engaging with those people.

                                                          I'd say Obrimos may or may not be concerned with resolution of anchors (the prior suggestion of giving them more was honestly a bit hasty), but either way are probably going to see the metaphysics behind why ghosts exist as having purpose and value in its own right, rather than just a consequence of human weakness.
