                Threads stalling a bit. Lets keep fiddling.

                Darklings were motivated by Fear. They killed before they could be killed, betrayed before they could be betrayed. They ran, they hid, they took the most confusing route they could just to ensure they weren't followed.

                Fairest were motivated by Beauty. Maybe it was a fellow captive, or a memory of home. Maybe it was a dream or a glimpse of sunset. They were snapped from their fugues by these flashes of beauty and drove themselves forward, however they could, hoping to reach them.

                Ogres were motivated by Anger. One too many beatings, one too many traumas. Enough is enough. "Live free or die," isn't that the saying? They tore through their chains in their rage and cut themselves a warpath, all the way home.

                Wizened were motivated by Reason. They didn't just react. They planned. Their masters gave them powers and crafts and otherworldly knowledge, and they wouldn't let any of it go to waste.

                Beasts were motivated by Instinct. They saw an opening and they took it. It didn't matter the means, it didn't matter the cost. There were no plans or grand triggers, only action. "Don't think, just do."

                Elementals were motivated by Desperation. They could see the signs, feel the signs. Their time in Arcadia was warping them too much and they were beginning to lose what little humanity they had left. They let go of their burdens and trivialities, focused on retaining what was truly important, and trudged their way back, losing more of themselves with every step.

                I'm not entirely satisfied with the Elementals' drive, but I can't think of how to turn "Zenning Out" into a basic concept like the others. I think the major defining trait Elementals should be keep in 2.0, though, is alienation. The "Fury of Nature" is basically window dressing to a Seeming that even the other Lost agree is really, really weird.

                            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                            Okay, so I'm just going to go on a long brain barf about Elementals and see what we can get out of it.

                            Elementals in the old version were the products of being made part of the environment, of being made one with the world. This implies a direct connection to the Stranger in question more so than most, because the world is their Domains, sometimes their Realms. No wonder the bastards have humanity issues-,they've been twice infected, once from that perspective of the breathing world(as opposed to the bleeding world, ala Beasts) and once by sheer Fae effect. In a sense, this means that when a changeling uproots itself from the Domain and walks out, or about, the Elemental is more of a direct thief than any other-they literally became part of the Stranger and them ripped themselves off and out. It's like if your elbow skin just said "Welp, I'm out", and tore itself brutally from your arm and preceded to run for the nearest bar.

                            Elementals seem like kind of rude bastards that way. That's not relevant.

                            It might seem like I'm suggesting "Agency through theft", but that's not it, it doesn't parse, not with the examples handed out by Dave-burn the manor down, bury the dogs in a landslide, hold your breath and count to ten minutes(Guybrush). Still, it informs it a little.

                            One thing that strikes me is that in the varied aspects of many Domains, it seems like Elementals are consistently baser and rawer. We have places where there place is a playpen, where an Elemental might be born from the surging mass of teddy bears and thus be a teddy bear themselves, one cotton and cloth drop in a plush friend ocean. Instead we get examples of Sand and Metal and Water and Fire and Earth and Wood and Lightning and Wind-we get those because they are archetypal, they're what we expect, but it also feels rawer. In fact, along with that rawness comes the feeling of power, of might, of something barely contained-each Domain that was marked as being Elementally favored had that sort of feeling, of action and energy just waiting to leap. You never had limpid pools, you had surging oceans. You had castles of glass and wire in the middle of the storm, and the lightning running through them was leaping, struggling,seething through. It feels ready to rip everything apart, even at it's most controlled-or at least that's how I remember it.

                            And with that comes the sense of overwhelming power-not in the same way as a Ogre. Elementals were often marked as tough and power-partly because of their Blessing, sure, also largely in part of the simple and brutal elegance of the Contract of Elements(and even Communion and Wyld). But I think a larger part of it has to do with the fact that they are used to being part of the environment, used to having it be them. Admit it-when you see an Elemental in your head going nuts, the element is filling the room, the element consumes the subject matter. It floods, it snarls and grows, it eats with it's many flickering tongues, the wind pierces all, the lightning is in our roofs, our steeds, our blood, the Earth quakes, the sand and snow twists and blinds and binds. It doesn't even matter if the natural element is there, the Elementals bleed it. They become powerful because they are walking Domains. They control the place.

                            It occurs to me that, of the aspects of a Fae, it really is the Domain that makes the Elementals. You can make a (poor) argument that the aspects of a Fae all tie to the Seemings in ways-Props to Wizened, Actors to Fairest, other things where the metaphor falls apart. But Domains are very Elemental, a part of the world that is not more directly part of the fae-Elementals could but probably don't come from Realms. The main thing that marks the two apart is that the Stranger is in control of a Realm, it is not in control of a Domain, even if it is a part of them. It's a place where power like that can come unchecked, where the Stranger is most likely on the wrong foot.

                            Okay. I think I got a simple sell for the Elementals.

                            Agency through chaos, power from unity and control.

                            Elementals take the raw, unbridled power of the Fae, and in an Autumn-like twist, throw it back at them(Hi Granfather Thunder) They create chaos and destruction and problems on the large scale. They burn the house down, the cause the landslide. They bury Strangers in a sea of teddy bears and run like hell. Chaos is a great equalizer-when no one knows what's going on and no one has the power to handle it, everyone is equally unable to act. Elementals are the earthquakes the Strangers never expected, and they get the power to express that agency by dominating the world they are in-and continue to do so. THey become one with the world they are in and seek to control it's every aspect, and gain the upper hand on people by throwing their expectations out the windows. Elementals are the ones who change the game. They learn to control the board, and deny others(Others) the chance to do so.

                            How's that for a thought?
                            Very interesting, and also does explain elements of them being raw, although the Mankin and the like mess with this, but Kiths changing should fix that.

                                    Hmm...

                                    Okay, this gives me an idea.

                                    Now, we already have the association of fear, and we also have the aspect of breaking rules. Who violates traditions in mythology? Tricksters!

                                    Of course, Tricksters are something any Changeling could embody, to say the least, and Beasts, for example, could easily embody what mythology will frequently call the amorous Trickster(i.e., sexual. Example is in some myths featuring Coyote.)

                                    However, what if the Darkling is the really cruel Trickster? Sure, we have other Changelings being cruel, but its in different ways. To compare,

                                    Fairest are cruel in a social way, and in becoming like their Keepers. Sure, they'll trick you, using the tools of social edict and charisma, but that's not the only type of trickery. Ogres... well, their brute force, and later on, also have aspects, ironically, of being the one who punish rule breakers. Beasts meanwhile, if we go with them being amorphous tricksters at times, well, that's a trickster, but not really as mean spirited, if you will. Wizened meanwhile are expertise and magic. They could certainly use trickery, but they're more the type that breaks out of prison with an elaborate plan, if you will.

                                    So, where does this leave Darklings? Well, what about trickery being a bit harsher? Also, remember an origin for Fae vulnerability to iron is that they broke a contract with Iron, and so, Iron took revenge. Well, what if someone like a Darkling did that? It would make sense with their origin too, because they got sent to Arcadia from breaking an obscure rule, well, might as well do it to your Keeper. Fear can come into this too, because they also represent the fate of breaking rules society tells you not to.

                                    Consider that, in myths featuring tricksters, they frequently, in the society they appeared, represented behaviors normal citizens weren't supposed to do, such as theft and the like. Expanding that to Darklings isn't a huge leap with their origins in 1.0, and could be an interesting response to being taken to Arcadia if they try becoming scary con artists, if you will.

                                    Sorry, bit of a brain spew of my own, tell me if you guys like it.

                                        Decoupling Seeming and Kith is awesome. My group's been doing that for years. I also really like the idea of a major character component being attached to the Changeling's escape. I've always been of the opinion that it is an under-treated aspect of the game. That said, I feel like Seeming is a bad fit for it. I've always loved Seeming for its broad archetypes not just in types of physical alterations, but also in psychological effects of the Durance. For me, being an Elemental is not just about being an object, but being removed from your humanity by your durance such that you have trouble relating to people. Being a Wizened isn't just about being turning into a goblin, but being subjected to torment such that spite becomes second-nature. People of the same Seeming shared a bond of common experience. A Beast lion is going to have more in common with a Beast rabbit than a Fairest lion, the superficial similarity with the latter notwithstanding. Kith, on the other hand, was a toolkit to fine tune your Seeming's physical characteristics. I'd be fine with kiths turning into a grab bag of physical aspects like those used in Demon to define Demonic Form. I'm less interested in going back to CtD's list of kiths as the primary splat.

                                        With the changes to Court, I'd almost prefer a new "splat" be added to reflect the escape. Not having any great insights into what it should be, though.

                                                Hmm. Okay, let me see if I can help things at all by trying another Kith, this time by thinking about the Seemings through the new ideas some have posted here. Namely, Draconic.

                                                A Beast Draconic is the ultimate predator, doing whatever they want to whomever they want to or who dares to threaten their power. Everything to them is a conquest to be forcefully achieved, whether its in the physical or social arena.

                                                A Darkling Draconic embodies both darkness, but also its allure. They know what words to whisper to extract pledges from mortals, but also when to appear as their worst nightmares.

                                                A Elemental Draconic appears aloof, not engaging socially much, but when contested, become a fierce opponent, however once they smash their opponents, they once again withdraw from affairs.

                                                A Fairest Draconic is the embodiment of majesty, and a forceful presence. Their voices could make a crowd into a mob, and their enemies cower in fear.

                                                A Ogre Draconic is fierce, slashing their enemies with powerful claws, and ripping them apart with their grand teeth. Their strength makes them hard to escape as well, refusing to back down for anyone.

                                                A Wizened Draconic is truly an amazing administrator, always knowing who needs what, and builds the grandest of homes for themselves.


                                                So... yeah, essentially, tried to take different chunks of both Dragons and perhaps more violent demons to get somewhere. This probably has quite a few issues, but still, hopefully something to help discussion.
