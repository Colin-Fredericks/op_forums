      Very briefly due to lack of time…

      Lore-wise, mages and their Orders being ‘descended from the Awakened City’ is now a symbolic declaration rather than a factual statement. Well, it sorta was too starting from some latter 1e supplements iirc, but 2e makes that clearer. We even get to see, in Dark Eras, how disparate mages from the Hellenistic era grouped up into the precursors of modern Orders.

      System-wise, balancing spells/Practices is not done by hard-capping certain effects at higher Arcanum dots, but soft-capping them via Reach. Higher Arcanum dots give you more ‘free’ Reach. (Eg. You can totally try to mind-control humans at Mind 2, even to self-harm, but good luck trying that consistently if you’re a newbie who got Mind 2 just a few days ago.) Non-free Reach gives you Paradox. Speaking of which, Reach is likely your main source of Paradox, especially with the Covert-Vulgar distinction gone.

      EDIT: Reach being the main source of Paradox also implies that, if you screw up and/or blow up something, most of the time it’s your own damn fault for overestimating yourself and underestimating your challenges. Hubris, indeed.

          The central changes:

          - The Supernal Realms are more holistically depicted to match their nature in Imperial Mysteries: not a higher dimension or greater plane one can travel to, but a state of being, the symbols and meanings underlying and represented by phenomenal reality. Using the Mage Sight doesn't just reveal an understanding of the phenomenal world through knowledge of the Arcana, but literally opens your eyes to an overlay of the Supernal Realm as it is, intertwined and coexisting with the phenomenal world. A Moros looks at the world undergirded by Stygia and sees the ghosts of regrets and attachments haunting a man. The Obrimos look into the Aether and see the scepter and robes that drape those who wield authority.

          - "Spell aspect" is gone. The risk of Paradox is not connected to how subtle or explicit your spell is in breaching the natural law of the Fallen World. Instead, a Reach mechanic is introduced. The Reach of a mage's spell determines the availability of a number of spell factors, with even the ability to cast a spell without an extended ritual requiring a small modicum of your Reach. There is no limit to how far a mage can push his Reach, but a mage's safe grasp is limited by his understanding. Reaching beyond your understanding has prepared you invites the risk of a Paradox.

          - There is a more overt focus on the notion that mages seek out hidden truths and discover secrets about the world. Anomalies and supernatural mysteries are rife in the world, and a mage's drive to uncover, learn about and harness them is what fuels the accumulation of arcane experience.

                      I think this thread (http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...elopment-blogs) is most useful for finding the blogs, it has all the links on a single page and in order.

                      The big difference in mechanics is the change to spell factors. All spells are cast on a basis of single success pulls them off, exceptional success pulls them off with slight benefit, and how far they go is based on penalties to the casting pool.

                      Penalties get offset by using yantras, ritual implements with symbolism relevant to the spell you're casting. How many yantras you can use depends on Gnosis.

                      And factors default in forms of less convenience, with Reach (and thus Paradox risk at the most basic casting level) necessary to move them to the advanced table. Rituals aren't extended actions to accumulate lots of successes to factors, they're the default for how long it takes to cast a spell. (They can be pushed longer to get up to five bonus dice to the spell)

                      Rotes get a couple of good changes out of this; they become normal spell dice pools with a Skill as a Yantra, and they give you as much Reach as if you were a Master. So it's pretty useful even to have 1 dot spells as rotes, because that five free Reach can effectively cover all factors plus boosting the spell effect.

                          Rotes get a couple of good changes out of this; they become normal spell dice pools with a Skill as a Yantra,
                          This is a good improvement mechanically. It is no longer the case that the rote dice pool can be smaller than the improvised casting dice pool - it is always a (+skill dots) rote mundra. Whereas in 1e it was Gnosis replaced by attribute+skill, which might be cheaper for less experienced characters to maximise. But at higher Gnosis not so much.

                          Praxis were also introduced, which are not present in 1e, and like legacies represent some shaping of a mage's soul.

                          A number of non-legacy attainments were codified, taking numerous spell-like abilities from 1e and some other (spread through sidebars) to be more clearly laid out and with common rules (sharing some features with legacy attainments, such as not being spells).

                          All good stuff imho.

                                  I'd say that the thing that does come out as a major change is altering primary factor. In First Edition, it depended on how many successes you rolled, so you couldn't exactly fine tune it and penalties to improve other factors probably came at the expense of it. In Second Edition, primary factor defaults to a level equal to Arcanum rating, and can be lowered at will and raised still further with penalties, but you're still guaranteed a decent result and free up space to expand additional factors.

                                  There are a bunch of other things that get cleaned up. Rather than being a case of either adding another penalty to the casting pool or rolling a trait against its result (which is which being a tad arbitrary), resisting spells is a streamlined matter of "Potency needs to exceed Stamina, Composure or Resolve", sympathetic casting cuts down on the number of levels of connection and makes it a resistance to overcome rather than a penalty, as well as temporal sympathy being made more its own thing... oh, aimed spells were revised significantly, so that rather than being "sometimes spells can be used as projectiles" they're an option for when you don't Reach from touch to sensory range, being able to throw the spell whatever it is (offset by it being necessary to make it an area spell).

                                  Legacy powers were revised into something more systematized. For one thing, they get five Attainments rather than three, so there's earlier access and more ongoing progression. When Attainments are meant to have powers resembling spells, they're modelled more directly off of those spells, so not so much odd cases like the Bearers of the Eternal Voice getting their Mind dots as automatic social successes. The most significant thing is that the power of Attainments have to be fully accounted for in the Reach you'd get for an Arcanum rating, with no option to go beyond it (as well as generally set factors, since they're not rolled to activate). This can lead to cases where a high level Attainment confers a lower level of spell; if you want Psychic Domination that can be used instantly, at sensory range and provides anything goes level command, that needs to be a fifth Attainment even though it's a 2 dot spell because you need Mind 5 to provide all the Reach that needs.
