    Quick question (or two...)

    Hi.

    So, I am introducing my son to RPGs. I've asked him about the type of story he'd like to play and he's currently very into the Percy Jackson books. I liked the original Changeling for its mix of fantasy and modern times. I'd like to perhaps use C:tL (or the rules at least) to run this campaign with him, but I have a few questions:

    -- do the characters still basically perceive the world magically in the sense of me seeing a tall muscular guy with a metal bar, but they see a troll with a sword? Or perhaps I see them swing a branch in the empty air while they fight a dragon?

    --how do the various powers compare to the original? What kind of abilities are available?

    --I like the God Machine update to the rules. Would there be a lot of conflict between C:tL and these rules? Would I need to change much?

    --finally, are the high-level fae of sufficient power to replicate deities?

    Any info or advice is appreciated. Thanks!

      Changeling: the Lost is pretty different in concept and tone to Changeling: the Dreaming. The characters of Lost are humans who have been abducted to Faerie by the mad, alien Others, the True Fae. They've fought their way back to our world, but their durance in Faerie has left them transformed, physically, mentally and spiritually. They seal Contracts with aspects of the Wyrd, the weave of fate, in exchange for pieces of the Others' power. The Arthurian themes are played down a bit in Lost; there are some airs of archaic power structures and Kings and Queens of the Seasons, but not so much a big aristocracy-peasantry divide or recitations of courtly love or the like.

      Lost has no concept of chimerical reality, but it does have something similar (and simpler, really): the Mask. The physical transformations worked upon a changeling's body, that transform them into hulking ogres or glowing white angels or men of fire and candlewax, are normally concealed by an illusion cast by the Wyrd, called the Mask, which causes normal humans to perceive them as fellow humans, though possibly not quite the way they looked before they were captured by the Gentry. Tokens, the enchanted items which changelings can craft in the Hedge halfway between our world and Faerie, are similarly covered by the Mask. Dragons, not so much; the hobgoblins and fae creatures native to the Hedge can enter our world, but the Hedge is more of a liminal place than a "layer" on top of our world. Somebody present to witness one of the Lost fighting a dragon would damn well see a dragon, and if the dragon decided to attack them, they'd damn well get hurt for reals.

      The major supernatural powers you buy are called Contracts; they're like Vampire's Disciplines in that each is a scale of 1 to 5, with a different power accessed with each new dot level. Contracts are a bit more story-themed; each Contract, for example, has a Catch — a condition under which you can use the power without having to pay its activation cost — and the Catch often has more to do with what faerie logic associates with that power than anything else. For example, a supplement has a Contract of Separation clause that allows the Fairest to glide across surfaces that could not normally support their weight, and without leaving a trace. This clause is free to use if the Fairest is wearing nice shoes that would be ruined if they didn't use the clause to cross. Changelings can also form pledges, which aren't purchased with XP, but are worth mentioning. A pledge is a supernaturally binding oath sworn with another person. Pledges can have sanctions that inflict punishment on those who break them, or boons which benefit one or both parties so long as the terms are complied with. There's a relatively simple (in my opinion) system provided for putting pledges together.

      GMC rules don't generally require a lot of tinkering to port backwards into 1e nWoD. You'd just need to figure out a cost in experiences for Contract dots.

      The power of the True Fae varies depending on what realm they're in. They're pretty consistently quite powerful, but within their own domains in Faerie, they are basically local gods.

      Lost is a very good game, I'd recommend checking it out for yourself.

              Originally posted by Arnabas View Post
              I'd like to perhaps use C:tL (or the rules at least) to run this campaign with him, but I have a few questions:
              Just remember that CtL can be pretty dark. Like the WOD in general it does fall into the "urban fantasy" stuff along with things like Percy Jackson, but the tone is completely different.

              Scion might be worth a look, warty system and all, for running a lighter and more action happy game. And it is about playing the kids of gods and all that.

              -- do the characters still basically perceive the world magically in the sense of me seeing a tall muscular guy with a metal bar, but they see a troll with a sword? Or perhaps I see them swing a branch in the empty air while they fight a dragon?
              This is a bit variable, but generally speaking while in the normal world, changelings she everything normally unless it is Fae, at which point by default they see through masks.

              So unless a Fae creature is strengthening their Mask to avoid notice, if you see an Ogre, you see their fae visuals, not the real world ones (though you can look at it).

              CtL doesn't do the "fighting the invisible dragon" thing. A fae creature that is on this side of the Hedge will have some form of Mask. A non-fae on-looker is going to see "something" that fits the general shape and concept of what's being fought.

              On the variable side of things, a changelings Clarity rating alters how strongly they see things as "normal plus" (high Clarity) or blended realities (low Clarity).

              --how do the various powers compare to the original? What kind of abilities are available?
              If you mean "Dreaming" by original? The two rule sets are vastly different.

              The main powers, Contracts, work like vampire Disicplines, coming in 1-5 dot progressions. There are a lot of them offering a wide variety of powers. There are also Goblin Contracts that don't have to be 1-5 progressions, but come with drawbacks.

              You also have built in Seeming and Kith powers.

              Then there are general systems like Pledges (including expansion to Goblin Vows), Hedge crafting (making fae items with powers), and Tale-craft. These are broad and flexible systems that allow for lots of additional powers.

              --I like the God Machine update to the rules. Would there be a lot of conflict between C:tL and these rules? Would I need to change much?
              I'd personally think it is a head-ache in terms of the number of things that should become Conditions. But if you're willing to ignore any of the powers imparting Conditions and just use them as written, you're mostly going to be fine. Though the combat powers and new combat rules will need adjustment.

              --finally, are the high-level fae of sufficient power to replicate deities?
              Yes

                  It does have powers similar to the Kith powers in Dreaming. But there is more customizing. You have your Seeming powers (and weakness), then you can pick a Kith (or two) which adds an additional special ability to the Seeming innate Abilities. If you have a Court, that gives you a few minor abilities depending on how in tune with it you are. You can also get an Entitlement, though they are less common than the above three that almost all PCs have, sort of a cross between a CtD Society and Noble House, which has some innate ability or treasure or something. The end result is blending these you create a sort of unique Faerie creature.

                  Contracts, the actual purchaseable magic system, work differently than Dreaming. They are more straightforward 1-5 dot abilities, but with some twists, like "catches" that allow free activation if you meet certain qualifications. Less Bunks though more "Have spent an hour in a room with no natural light" or "the object you are affecting belongs to a sworn enemy"

                          The power level of things is Dreaming is hard to compare. The Kith bonuses are a bit more potent in CtD, though in CtL you get a Seeming and a Kith bonus. The Arts & Realms system is also very hard to compare directly to the Contracts system. Bunks vs. Catches for example is hard to put any "numbers" to. Bunks are freeform, giving player creativity more punch but it slams into the ST's judgement. Catches are set tricks, which means you can be creative in trying to ensure a power you plan to use a lot is one you can satisfy the Catch for, but you don't have to worry about coming up with a new Catch on the fly all the time.

                              It depends? Their powers work around normal people all the time, which is an advantage compared to Dreaming. Normal Humans don't inhibit them at all. From there it depends on who you are comparing. One Kith in Dreaming had the sole power of being able to, alone, create a traditional Hawiian structure overnight. All Lost Kith/Seeming combos are more potent than that. One just gets an extra dot of Dexterity and that is it. Again, most if not all combos for Lost are better. However, by and large they are weaker, though someone with Seeming, Duel Kith, and high Mantle in their Court are pretty close to the same power level, and can be better customized.


                              Once you work other stuff all Lost get it evens out. Hedge Abilities, Orrniromancy, Talecrafting, Pledgecrafting add those in and combine them with the ability to not give a fuck less if someone believes in faeries and they come out ahead overall, plus Dreaming Changelings are kinda fragile (they are implied to often only really exist as such for a decade or two). But yea, just comparing innate abilities, they come out a little weaker, but with better customization.

                              ETA: In terms of Arts vs. Contracts, Arts do within the Art have more potential flexibility (due to the nature of Realms) in that a healing power could repair objects, for example. It tended to make for a bigger investment, though, for that flexibility. Arts also had a higher peak in terms of power level, but Contracts tend to be more broadly useful. In Dreaming I usually only bothered with a couple Arts, while in Lost I tend to buy a lot of Contracts. I've observed similar behavior in my players. Some Arts, like Chronos and Naming, are much stronger. Most just have a more potent top level power, and a few are even weaker compared to Contracts, though like the much stronger ones they tend to not be main book powers.

                                      Nothing has been confirmed — officially, the stance is that the writing team would love to do further chronicle books if Strix, Idigam and Fallen World prove successful enough to make it look viable. Unofficially, there are strong hints that Promethean already has one in early development, and I'd put money on Changeling being next in line after that.

                                      Though honestly, I think Changeling is the limited line that needs the benefits of a chronicle book the least; streamlined rules are nice, and the powers could probably be tweaked up with the lessons learned about the game in the intervening years, but the biggest strength of a chronicle book is to be able to take a second stab at boiling down the essentials of the gameline into a single new core that does a good job of painting a vivid picture of why you would want to play the game, and to reconcoct the rules to better support those things. Changeling is arguably the line that best hit this stuff out of the park already (you can make a very good case for Hunter too).
