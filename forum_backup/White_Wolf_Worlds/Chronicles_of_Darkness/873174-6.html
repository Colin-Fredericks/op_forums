            I want to clarify something, because people have asked:

            Just because something hasn't been touched on yet, doesn't mean it's been removed.
            Like the Knights of the Bloody Chalice, there are alot of things we haven't had time to go through yet. We want to keep everything that made the 2nd and 3rd editions of Embers what they were, but we want to add more flavor, structure, and darker elements to them, while expanding the possibilities of what Dragon can be.

            The "Transitional Rulebook" was just that: a set of new ideas and mechanics which would carry over Dragon into 2nd Edition. It's neither the full book, nor really a finalized game. It's more like the GMC Rules Update which White Wolf released a few years ago: it's there so you can adapt old material for new games. It doesn't have all the content because that sort of thing is an entirely different Dragon. The version of the book we are working on right now, Dragon Rekindled: The Oroboroi Chronicles is the one which will have all the content.

            Examples of things which we are NOT taking out, but haven't gotten around to yet
            • The Treasury, which is being placed on the Far Shore of the Dreamtide
            • More Antecedents for more Heartstring, going back to the original Dragon at Heartstring 10
            • Dynasties, which are being written as conspiracies / occult-groups
            • Provinces, Maidens, and Hoards, which are in there now, but will see more refinement.
            • Dreams, Nightmares, Dream Worlds, and all the other Dreamtide stuff, because the Appendix only touched on them briefly (Nightmares are actually our next planned "glimpse", after the Knights)
            • Heart Removal and all the things that come with it, including methods of keeping the Heart and how far you can go from it.
            • Pilot Lights and Brood Bonded. To those out there who have lamented their removal, you have no idea how much work is going into the entire chapter on them. We want them to have their own Character Sheets and everything, just like the "Wolf Blooded" in Werewolf 2nd Edition.
            • Unlisted Edicts, which we're going to rebuild into it.

            Examples of the very few things which ARE being removed
            • Einherjar: they're interesting, but they didn't really relate to anything else in the book.
            • Grafts: once again, they're cool, and we like them, but they just don't fit in with anything else.
            • Ominous Drums: we like it, but it needs to be rewritten, and I've got a different idea for what the story should look like...

            Note also that just because we're removing something now, doesn't mean you can't use it, nor does it mean we won't change our minds.

            A(nother) Plea for People
            If you want to give input on something, join the project: we're really not being picky about anyone who wants to brainstorm and develop ideas with us, just with people who want to directly add the final content. Darinas, for example, is playing a huge part in fleshing out the Knights of the Bloody Chalice (expect the release on that by Thursday night: we're excited!), because it was one of his favorite things in the Embers books.

            Once again, joining is as simple as applying, so if anyone's interested, apply ASAP!

A large chunk of Embers' appeal was the wide variety of hacks and mechanics it presented, which gave Storytellers and other Fan Projects a huge amount of fodder.

                                    That said, taking old mechanics and rewriting them is a major part of what we're doing, and using variations on the ones added in GMC is going to be a HUGE help: the Tilts are great for replacing alot of the old rules, the Clue and Doors systems will really help with the Purviews which deal with investigation and persuasion, respectively, (actually, one of the Knowledge Impositions is specifically geared towards slipping behind the doors and changing a belief while leaving them still shut tight for the next guy, and the new mechanic is making that alot easier to write.) and the Item Concealment rules which were clarified recently have outright REPLACED what used to be a relatively complex system for Ablution Tells.

For example, Dynasties, where the Dragons of St George are made to pretty much WALLOW in Guilt without consequence, while the Delvers simply know what the Reimagining is on a deeper level than the others do, so they just will it not to happen. It only really comes up if you rise in power way too quickly, which is part of the point of the mechanic, thematically, or if you have characters who are really old.

                                    On the other claw though, if you have such a bad group that you see a mechanic with potential as potential for infighting, then that's not really our problem: we're going to have advice on running a group in the book, but if your group just infights then you can't expect us to hold back mechanics intended for groups who don't to save your group for another session or two. You don't HAVE to use it if you REALLY don't like it, but we're writing a small chunk of the book's concept around it, and it's fitting nicely so far, so we haven't seen a need to write it out.

                                                Ok, so, last night I got a chance to read through someone's copy of Beast... And the more I read the more it seems like a completely different game.                                                 Originally posted by Michael Brazier View Post
                                                The thing is, Beast: the Primordial basically is a take on dragons as PCs. The Horrors aren't necessarily reptilian monsters, but the whole game is built around the scene in fantasy stories where the hero descends into a great monster's lair to kill it, told from the monster's viewpoint; and dragons are probably the most common kind of monster in that scene.                                                 But it's such a different take on it. Beasts aren't supposed to be "real dragons", they're supposed to be avatars of Humanity's fear, and the Dragon thing is incidental / metaphorical. The Orobroroi are real Dragons, period, even if they're different from the initial thought, and when they're not Dragons, they're Ubermencsch / Gods. Go try and make that criticism of the similarities between Beast and Werewolf or Vampire: they're all scary, and Beasts can be made like Werewolves or Vampires, because Beast is supposed to be a versatile book like that. That doesn't make the one book eliminate the merit of the other.

                                                If I'm reading it correctly, the biggest difference between Beast and Oroboroi is that the latter get to play both roles in the iconic scene, at different times in their lives: an Oroboros begins as the dragon slayer, claims its power by eating its heart, then returns home to rule, becoming dragon-like; eventually he grows so monstrous that he has to be slain, becoming the dragon whose power will be claimed by his murderers.
                                                There are several other major differences though.

                                                The Oroboroi have transformation powers, which Beast has nothing like, that mean that they end up with this really powerful Dragon Form to use in this very hands-on visceral manner. Beasts don't have anything like that, instead they have their Lair and their Atavisms, which aren't as immediately powerful or visceral as the Oroboroi.

                                                The tone is entirely different: whereas Dragon takes this very power-centric theme and uses alot of dark-fantasy elements, Beast takes on this very dark/goth theme that uses elements of traditionalism. Beast takes all this time to bemoan the lack of fear in our civilization, that we believe we've risen above the harsh realities, and need a horror to teach us an important lesson about what we SHOULD fear. Dragon is about punching the Beast's viewpoint in the face, grabbing a shitload of power, and carrying out some sort of godlike undertaking.

                                                They are literally night and day as both good guys and bad guys: Beasts are like Batman, Dragons are like Superman. I can see a Dragon punching meteors in the face, or fighting interplanetary horrors, or attempting to solve some global political crisis, but I don't see the reclusive Beast doing that. Meanwhile, I can much more easily see a Beast lurking in the shadows, waiting for criminals to strike, then dealing out some good old fashioned justice, either by terrifying them, or by just beating them senseless. On the flipside, a Dragon gone bad hoards power and resources to dominates humanity as a whole. A Beast gone bad becomes an obligate sadist, preying on people at random on an individual level. Their scope, power type, and interest are completely different.

                                                As a book, Beast doesn't have the same volume of potential as Dragon. Don't get me wrong, it's a good book, but there's only one character I can see myself interested in, there's only one plot I can think of running, and there's only one real undertaking presented. Hell, the only major antagonist I see in Beast is the Hero, where as we're making up random things to throw into any campaign. Don't get me wrong, I think that the idea of Beast is great and I can totally see myself playing it... But only once. Like Promethian, it's creating this very well crafted and detailed view on what is essentially a single kind of story, and it's great at what it does, but it's not what Dragon is. Dragon has a much broader scope and usage, with material that can bleed over into any other setting, and that's one of the things that's so great about it.

                                                Incidentally, why isn't it possible for his Heart to be divided among several people when an Oroboros is killed?
                                                The Heart is a single entity: all parts have to be there to function. Think about it this way, if I were to give you a new component for your computer, like a new motherboard, would you be able to run the computer without a complete motherboard? When doctors do Heart Transplants, they can't just divide a single Heart up between multiple patients, because each chamber has a function. In this case, you need the power of a full Heart to become an Oroboroi. We've been getting into this with our discussions of Pilot Lights, who are going to grow Hearts one chamber at a time, but like I said, we haven't gotten to the point of writing a release on stuff like that yet.

                                                Does the game really need the number of dragons to be slowly dwindling with no possibility of recovering?
                                                Well, that was a theme that was played up in the original, with the idea that there may be a way out. We're not seeing a need right now to play it up. I will say though, there aren't any other books with that theme as far as I know, and it does add a bit of melancholy and tension to it all...

                                                do dragons need the theme of a lost paradise?
                                                It was in the original Embers, and we're trying to be true to that. It's working for us so far too, so we haven't felt a need to change that. The Knights of the Chalice have their origin in it, and we're actually basing a chunk of the Dynasty stuff on that once we get there.

                                                I suspect the PC origin (you ate a monster's heart to steal its power!) already does enough along those lines - Oroboroi chose to exile themselves from humanity, their lives before eating the Heart are an age of innocence that they deliberately abandoned and can never return to.
                                                True, the whole "First Sin" thing is used in two different ways. Like I said though, I think what you just said should show you why I'm saying the games are completely different.
