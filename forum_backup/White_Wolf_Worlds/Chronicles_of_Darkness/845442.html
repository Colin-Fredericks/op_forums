    Vampires and Body Armor

    03-11-2016, 12:28 AM
    Let's talk about body armor. I heard some talk a while back about people preferring their vampires in cocktail dresses and tuxedos over riot gear and flak jackets, saying it didn't really fit the mood. I can see where they're coming from, but on the other hand armor really is pretty handy to have around. It isn't even that expensive in many parts of the world, certainly nothing that your average vampire couldn't manage.

    Now, let's put aside the Legion of the Dead back in medieval and Imperial Roman times, Sanctified going around wearing lorica, and the occasional Invictus knight in ceremonial armor at a formal event.

    Do vampires in your games typically go around using body armor? A ballistic vest under your coat? Heavy biker leathers? Heavier stuff, still? Not just your player characters who want to get an edge, but your NPCs too?

    In my own game I saw, at most, one of the older vampire PCs wear a coat with some kevlar plating sewn in - because he wanted to supplement his Resilience.

                  Armor... depends a lot on the character. I would fully expect an Invictus Knight to come in armor. Whereas I expect someone trying to appear as a social butterfly to not. Vampire is, above all else, a Social game. The main struggles are going to be social before physical. So, when we come to Elysium looking to make an impression, I expect to see vampires prioritizing what impression they want to make. The knight is going to want to project the image of a guard prepared for shit to hit the fan. The socialite is going to come in outfits that provide social bonuses. Each wears clothes that make the most impact for what they're trying to accomplish.

                  And here's the thing. Most modern armors are designed with the idea of saving you from bullets. Vampires are already bullet resistant, so that already cuts a large chunk of worry off. Armor can be big, bulky, and obvious. With Vigor in the equation, and how weapons deal auto-damage equal to their equipment modifier, you'll want to focus on just getting one success and let the auto-damage modifiers take care of the bulk of it. Claws of the Unholy shred armor like butter with a hot knife, and the social powers are both effective for putting an end to combat and not taking armor into account. Biting deals lethal to vampires, and most armor doesn't protect the neck - its worthless against such a common tactic. So, the actual benefits of mundane armor for the average vampire is actually lower than you'd think.

                  That's not to say that armor doesn't have its uses. Armor does provide an edge in combat, and if you're expecting a fight, every little bit helped. Reinforced clothing is a general 1/0 armor that's widely applicable and pretty cheap - its an easy to get, covers most of your body, and doesn't restrict movement. So, why not wear it if you don't need the social bonuses from other clothes? Against an opponent trying to stake you, that armor can mean the difference between unlife and torpor.

                  To me, vampires are ultimately practical. They don't eschew armor because it doesn't fit the theme. They eschew armor because while violence is part of their lives, their image is generally more important. Is it more advantagous to show up in armor and expecting a fight, or show up in an outfit that gives you an edge socially? In Elysium, physical violence is banned, so most Kindred likely won't come prepared for a fight. They'll come prepared for a social conflict. Meanwhile, the Sheriff and her Hounds are expected to come prepared for physical trouble all the time, so them NOT wearing armor is more of a cause for eyebrows being raised, imho. Going out to expect a fight? Wear the armor.

                  Its all simply a matter of priorities. As the Social game, vampires tend to prioritize social advantages. But its not absolute. That is all.

                          I'm one that doesn't like vampires wearing armor in a modern setting. More savvy players tend to see the advantages of armor and pick up the item since the benefits almost always outweigh the negatives without some ST fiat. Newer players wouldn't go right to kevlar. I hate the escalation of armor, as well. Either having to give all the NPCs the same thing because every PC has it, or in PvP situations where it just seemed like a noob tax if you didn't have it.

                          For my peace of mind and story visuals, we ended up houseruling a new inherent ability for all Kindred - blood armor. Spend 1 Vitae for a scene, and get the benefits of Kevlar. Doesn't stack with other armor equipment or powers. Players still get the bonus, I don't have to deal with a swat team every scene.
