                    Currently, the only Dragon character I have in play is a Slayer named Jean, who got his heart by killing a baron in the French Revolution. It's a mixed-party campaign involving Vampires, so I did this interesting thing where he was originally just a bulky red dragon with an axe, but after hanging out with the Ordo Dracul for too long, he's slowly been altered by the perception of what a "Dragon" looks like, so he's now basically the dragon from the Ordo Dracul logo. They've had him on retainer as a monster hunter for a few decades. I like playing him because it's like having a mini-garou on standby that WON'T kill the rest of the party, it's an immortal creature that ISN'T Vampires but is still pretty open-ended, and just the general aesthetics are really nice. Also he makes a good troll on the rest of the party, especially their Primogen benefactor.

                    I also had the PCs encounter a Deep One in their back yard, offering to trade with them, in the last campaign I ran: we'd just gotten Werewolf: Second Edition and I wanted to throw a curve-ball that wasn't being hostile to them. Dragon had a lot of good ideas sort of floating around in there.

                    It's hard for me to really remember much about the others, because I've had a ton of characters over the years and I really didn't get enough time with them, and it's hard to find GMs who have read Dragon in the first place. I'm probably going to end up having to run a game on some forum with a transitional ruleset...

                    The two elements that I've enjoyed coming into play are the Dreamwalking ability and the simple fact that Dragons are basically creatures of what you think about them, and therefore, it stands that you can really screw with them by just believing things about them, like "he kinda looks like the logo". That was something that we figured out which had a lot of potential, which I wanted to expand upon: forget about being petrified by insults, what if people's opinions of you could alter your very form? There were alot of things like that that, when we we thought through them further, had these really interesting implications. Oh! I also like the thing where they eat to get back power, that's made them fun, especially Jean, because he's obsessed with food, and the other players didn't know what he was, and hadn't read the book, so they thought he was just obsessed with food for some other odd reason like "he took gluttony as a vice". (come on guys, Gluttony hasn't been a "vice" for over a year.)

                    Also, there's something pleasing about CJCs little doodles in the first and third addition...

                    Dislikes I've had are the morality system pushing that you have to "rule" something, and making us have to stretch the limits to make any campaign that isn't all about them, or that has them doing anything other than trying to "rule" a city. Stuff like that and the simple way it's written were things that have made it hard to actually get a lot of use out of the book, as it turns off the other main GM of my RP group: he's the kind of guy who insists that Mage is "broken" or "overpowered", but has very obviously never even read it. I also (as you've probably guessed) didn't like a lot of the changes in 3rd edition: the Treasury has no precedent in World of Darkness and the Dreamtide stuff was really interesting to play around with, and a lot of the nerfing in general felt like they'd been pandering to the people who thought it was "broken" or "overpowered" when they hadn't actually read it. Mage took the route of doubling down on those people with the 2nd edition.

                    So... My plan is to take a mix of 2nd and 3rd edition, keep that feeling of the raw power that comes with stolen divinity, and inject that dark, visceral nature that Vampire and Werewolf seem to have going for them. For the morality thing, I was thinking of making it more generalized: you got this heart by killing someone, and it was originally acquired by murdering a god, so you teeter between the Hubris of being too confident about the power, and the Guilt that you're not living up to the potential of the Heart. For the writing thing, I was thinking of taking more cues from Vampire, which has this pervasive darkness to it, but isn't so dark that it ruins the fun and interesting setting. (I'm one of those people who thinks WoD settings are best when you can justify really inane stuff, like werewolf tea-parties between the viscous and traumatic fight sequences, because just picture a garou in a frilly pink dress, daintily sipping tea)

                        I felt like popping in, because I'm actually the one running the game with the above dragon as a PC. Honestly, there are a lot of things I like about the rules and plenty of things I would want to work on to alter or fix.

                        The newer GMC morality systems could improve Dragon much like they did for the other splats (providing a Hunger system for Beast, and Werewolf Harmony in particular are my favorite uses).

                        I also loved the concept of the Dreamtide (having already been a fan of dream-space in RPGs in general...including in Changeling and to a lesser degree in Mage), and since Beast recently arrived and gave information on concepts like the Primordial Dream, it made so much sense to me that Dragons could inhabit a similar space.

                        One of the reasons I like Dragons is that they represent the raw power, both in the mental and the physical worlds, that has the ability to dominate all. Even though I've only used Dragon twice [Once for NPCs in a game I have on hiatus and once for the ongoing game above], It became a favorite of mine that I want to continue to experiment with. I love that dragons are so powerful, but they have no way to protect themselves, like Werewolves do, from mortal observation.

                        The one idea that I had recently was that if I was to change something about the book, it would actually be how Breath is regained. While I can see interesting ideas when it comes to eating food or strange materials to regain energy, it seemed really mundane and strange in-play. It ended up boiling down to, when we played it, something like "Oh, I had some food, can I get Breath back?" and to me that felt very uninteresting. I realized that I liked the concept of eating to regain energy, and unlike in Werewolf it would be something that was encouraged and not as negative. Being someone who read Fairy Tail I can't help but think it would be cooler to have Dragons eat physical properties (Ie: Lightning, Steel, Fire, ect.) and perhaps have an option to devour spiritual material (Essence, Souls, ect.) and perhaps even concepts if it doesn't step on Changeling too much. Eating essence was actually something that came up in our game as a potential option, and to make things interesting I even allowed Jean to develop a Vampiric Ablution so that he could feed on blood (though it was more painful and brutal than the subtle vampiric kiss)

                        Otherwise I don't really have any further suggestions, but I had been interested in reviving the game for a long time after watching the development stall.

                                        Originally posted by RockstarRaccoon View Post
                                        SHOCK! Do you have any notes I might use for this endeavor? Would it be possible to chatter?
                                        All the notes should be on this board. I had a few PMs from the other board that didn't make it due to the change over from WW to OP forums. Basically my advice is simply understand the themes and mood at play. The main mood of Dragon is entering into a species is hovering near it's end. Empires in disrepair, enemies hounding at the gates, etc. That being said, there is hope. All is not lost, and embers can set the fire anew. I believe we pounded the point too hard of the first part of the sentence while not enough effort was put into the second part.

                                        The theme is divine right. The empire maybe in disrepair but it is still an empire. An empire with assets, levers, and buttons to change the face of the world and you're now in charge of dealing with it. You forcefully made that choice as well. Others splats can tell rightfully tell themselves that they were dragged kicking and screaming into their current state. A dragon in Embers can not (I see this as the main dividing line between Ember and Inheritance as well.)

                                        Originally posted by RockstarRaccoon View Post
                                        Dislikes I've had are the morality system pushing that you have to "rule" something, and making us have to stretch the limits to make any campaign that isn't all about them, or that has them doing anything other than trying to "rule" a city. Stuff like that and the simple way it's written were things that have made it hard to actually get a lot of use out of the book, as it turns off the other main GM of my RP group: he's the kind of guy who insists that Mage is "broken" or "overpowered", but has very obviously never even read it. I also (as you've probably guessed) didn't like a lot of the changes in 3rd edition: the Treasury has no precedent in World of Darkness and the Dreamtide stuff was really interesting to play around with, and a lot of the nerfing in general felt like they'd been pandering to the people who thought it was "broken" or "overpowered" when they hadn't actually read it. Mage took the route of doubling down on those people with the 2nd edition.
                                        on the morality system:

                                        For context, we went with "A dragon king" due to pinning down the game's concept back when it was still in it's conception phase. We took a look at the WoD like that was out at the time and asked our selves, what do these splats do.

                                        For vampire, they politic

                                        For werewolf, they hunt

                                        For mage, they explore,

                                        For the created, they journey.

                                        For changeling, they dream.

                                        For hunter, they stand watch.

                                        For giest, They guide.

                                        and you can continue this line of thought for the others as well.

                                        So with that line of thought, what does a game about Dragons do? How does a game about powerful creatures who hoard resources with tempers work in a game of horror/urban fantasy? In the end, we went with "For dragon, they govern." due seeing how the mandate of heaven worked in Chinese thought and how easily playable the concept was.

                                        now back to the complaint: yes, that has been an issue has been brought up and I believe that stuff like Maidens have been suggested. I don't remember if it made it into the new ed but territory is viewed less as a brick and mortar and rather control over a person, group and/or physical place. This should be more adjustable for players.

                                        As for the Treasury: This is been an attempt to get away from saying point blank that "Yes this is the Astral." and more to hammer home the Dragon's access to resources and right to rule. If it helps any, think of it as a Dragon themed part of the Astral where Dragons can change things in accordance to their wishes.

                                        As for the Deep ones: I don't know how CJC intends to see them but I viewed them as competitors and mirror/foils to Dragons. They are a force that knows the Dragons time is ending and are starting to close in to take control of the role that Dragons have. They are more deeply tyrannical than Dragons and willing to engage in the worst abuses and atrocities without a second thought. Also planned on using deep sea creatures for inspiration. This sadly, didn't get hammered out and was cut out.

                                                        Well, since people are asking, I think I'll share some of the thoughts I had right before I resigned.

                                                        I did consider trying to finish Dragon the Embers on my own, as I had in the gaps between both the collapse of the first-stage development (when I unintentionally drove Gepetto off) and the collapse of the second-stage development (wherein I produced the document which became the Phase Two PDF). However, I was and continue to be woefully unable to wrap my head around the second edition; I was comfortable with the idea of hacks when Mirrors launched, but God Machine Chronicles swiftly transitioning into Second Edition and then the Chronicles of Darkness... it was too much for me. I couldn't keep pace, and it drove me mad (you can see my breakdown if you read the whole thread; I snapped and lashed out at everybody... unprovoked. I still regret it). I found that I could no longer shape the game to my vision. With other projects on my plate, I gave up on Dragon the Embers and crossed my fingers somebody else could take up the torch.
                                                        To put this into perspective, I'd like to mention that I joined the project (which was already in motion) back when White Wolf started advertising Geist as a new game line. That was the summer of 2009; that should give you an idea of how long people have been working on this game.

                                                        I was unhappy about the loss of the Dreamtide; I rather liked the idea and abandoned it from pressure of splat identity; one of the biggest criticisms at the time was overlap, and the Dreamtide shared much with Changeling even if the similarity was unintentional. The Treasury was a frantic attempt to restore some semblance of mysticism to the game. That said, I rather like the story with the five dragons and the birth of the universe, and it's equally viable as an explanation for the Dreamtide (since the Ocean Oroboros in the Astral Realm would essentially be a pool of ancient dragon blood). There was a suggestion that Dragons would enter the Treasury by circling (flying or running in concentric circles); perhaps this could be added as an entrance to the Dreamtide.

                                                        Fossilization was an attempt to make the Oroboroi more fragile and it was in its first iteration when the project ended; this too was a reaction to balance criticisms. It needn't have been as integrated as it was; instead of being a reaction to insults it would work better as a reaction to the loss of Heart, and an explanation as to why the Oroboroi don't leave monstrous bodies littering the world when they die. They do, but they're statues.
                                                        The unfinished revision: For each day an Oroboros does not make physical contact with her Heart, a piece of her body fossilizes (erase one Health dot). Fossilization can be halted by making contact with her Heart or by claiming a new Heart. It is reversed by tearing away the fossilized flesh (inflict Aggravated damage to restore dots; costs experience). This is what kills Oroboroi who have had their Heart stolen (in lieu of immediate death), which would give players the option to recover their Heart or find a new Heart if that plot plays out.

                                                        As for the problem with gorging to recover Breath; that was being addressed with the new Assets system for Breath; an Oroboros stores her faith in her possessions (people [maidens], places [provinces], and things [hoards]) and draws from them when she needs to muster supernatural power. The ways to replenish (interact with the object over the course of a scene; eat people; or set back the Asset for a quick fix) were in need of some fine tuning, but I believe this to be one of the best systems introduced into the game.

                                                        There were other promising changes; such as the new Y-Splats (though the 5th one wasn't shaping up and should have been pruned), the Edicts (which replaced the unwieldly "power" philosophies but needed more design synergy [and possibly the pruning of the elemental Edicts] ), and the more in-depth presentation of the Shayatin (formerly Deep Ones) and Nidhoggr (formerly "old" Dragons) as spearheaded by ExplodingFrogs. Still, the sticking points were prominent and unresolved, such as the issues with Mandate as a Morality substitute. It was easy to see where problems existed, but the solutions were not clear.
                                                        I had some other ideas to improve the game that were in their early stages; I considered breaking Furnace into two separate Potency stats "Earth" and "Sky"; one for Ablutions and one for Edicts. When that didn't pan out in my head, I considered using "Earth" as a replacement for Mandate with breaking points referring to the psychological corruption of power distancing the Oroboros from mortals (a superiority complex). My troubles with the second edition changeover got in the way, though, and I couldn't resolve them.

                                                        I urge you all to take what you like from the second and third edition and use it as a baseline for a new effort. There are things which need to be thrown away. There are things which need to be changed. For instance, Chronicles of Darkness books have a completely different format from 1st edition nWoD. Take what's here and craft it into the game you would want to play, and I will be happy that something came from the effort of all of these great people. Remember; there are ghosts in the attic and I'm one of them; I can't take an active role in development if a revival is to succeed.
                                                        If I can give one last bit of advice, it would be this: read an official splat and try to answer the questions it asks within the framework of the Oroboroi. This is how I wrote the first edition, basing it off the questions which were asked (and answered) in Werewolf the Forsaken and Mage the Awakening. It really is quite helpful in structuring your approach to the fan work.

Having the perceptions of others control that appearance, though, is something Rockstar came up with and is an awesome idea.[/Ninja'd]
