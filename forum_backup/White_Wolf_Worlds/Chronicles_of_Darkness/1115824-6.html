    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    Do I understand it correctly that Al-Amin fill the similar place in Caliphates Covenants that Invictus do on the West? They follow law, they resolve conflicts and sustaining the peace?
    Broadly speaking, yes. When al-Amin first appeared in Ancient Mysteries, they were the Islamic version of Invictus. Since 2e allows for covenants that aren't part of the core 5, we've allowed al-Amin to diverge from those roots a bit more, but they still evolved into the same general niche even if their methods and history are very different. It's close enough time that European Kindred mistake al-Amin for Invictus.

    With that said (and by no means is this official), I would say that modern Crones would also be very interested in researching al-Amin's history (and regions where they survive in modern nights). While al-Amin are strictly monotheistic and Islamic where the Crone are pagan, they find common ground when celebrating the power and importance of women.

                    Originally posted by Draconis View Post
                    I suppose this is a fair place to ask: what exactly does al-'Amin mean? All I've been able to find is that it's one of the names of the Prophet, and thus also a not-uncommon personal name for men.
                    I've seen it translated both as "The Trusted" and "The Faithful." When it's used as a name, it would just be "Amin" although "al-Amin" (al is the in Arabic) can also be used as a title or a regnal name. An example would be Muhammad al-Amin ibn Harun al-Rashid, the sixth Abassid caliph, who is usually just refered to as al-Amin, even though his given name Muhammad ibn Harun. Al-Rashid is similar, it means "The Just" or "The Rightly Guided," which can be used as a title or can drop the al to be used as a proper name. In the case of Prophet Muhammad, my understanding has been that al-Amin was more of a reverent nickname he was given by the people of Mecca for his skill at conflict resolution.

                    And while we are in the topic of names and Harun al-Rashid, Harun is the Arabic form of Aaron, so his name if anglicized is "Aaron the Just"

                            As we get Backers PDFs of the Dark Eras 2, time to return to topic, based on the final text of ‘1001 Nightmares’ Era.

                            Here is one of interesting previews into it – Glossary for monsters of the 1001 Nightmares. Vampires are called here ‘div’ ( because ‘vampire’ is Western term ). ‘Ifrits’ is name for divs Methuselahs, but also other powerful supernatural creatures, like Beast’s Horrors, Strix, powerful spirits, other ephemeral beings, changelings, sometimes others. Term ‘qutrub’ is name for Kindred ghoul, but also Wolf-Blooded – or even werewolves.
