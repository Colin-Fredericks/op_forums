                        Originally posted by Wraith_Magus View Post

                        Which is more interesting?

                        A story where there is a coherent narrative, heroes and villains working towards their own respective purposes, and a climax and conclusion that matches the themes of the story and where the audience is built up in anticipation for this conclusion...

                        ... Or a story with completely random actors popping up out of nowhere, unannounced, full of sound and fury, signifying nothing, that involves nothing so much as a bunch of unintelligible STUFF happening with no rhyme, meaning, plot, climax, or resolution?
                        1) You've clearly never read Strangeness in the Proportion.

                        2) Stories don't happen on the scale we're discussing. Stories are people level. They can be about people doing things that pertain to these large scales, but in the end it's people stories are about.

                        3) I enjoy stories with coherent narrative, protagonists and antagonists working towards their own respective purposes, with a climax and conclusion that matches the themes of the story and where the audience receives catharsis from anticipation in a world where random actors popping up out of nowhere, full of sound and fury, signifying nothing, that involves unintelligible stuff happening with no rhyme, meaning, plot, climax, or resolution is actually not so out of place. Hell, I have that happen all the time. Life is (semi-)like that-we tell stories that matter to us in a world that just can't make sense because there's so much unrelated stuff in it.

                        The World of Darkness evokes a great sense of verisimilitude because it honestly feels like how a real world of supernatural forces that go up to the cosmic level would act. THere's no answers for the world, and in the end it doesn't matter anyways, because my life-my stupid, tiny, significant, brilliant life- exists down here in the dust and mire and not in the eyes in the dark we call a starry night sky. I favor stories that look the nihilism of cosmic horror in the eye and punch it until it pays attention, thereby robbing it of power-but that's meaningless if the universe actually has enough coherent meaning to actually amount to something.

                                            Old thread that went off topic into the meta debate about whether people should try to reconcile things or not. (Spoiler - I'm on the side that thinks it's fun to do so and that players enjoy knowing there's something behind the curtain). But Xenu's Paradox said something earlier about how the CofD setting almost appeared to be multiple timelines shattered through some catastrophic event on the part of the splat responsible. What if that were literally true and the only way to put things together again were to line up the biggest overlapping parts as your starting point. I'll try to explain what I mean.

                                            Imagine a tapestry. Maybe it's five tapestries, maybe it's one. Maybe they're stories, not tapestries. But whatever the number and whatever they are, you're trying to stich it back together into a coherent whole. Like unifying Roman and Greek gods or trying to reconcile two accounts of an ancient war, you start with the big points of similarity. Here is a Beowulf like figure that appears very similar in both tapestries - in fact, the way they're posed, the colours used - they look almost the same. Stitch here - sew them together. There is an island. The writing above one says 'Mu', the other says 'Atlantis'. But there's a giant wave sewn in gold and azure depicted rising over both. That looks almost good enough to join. There's a hole here, something was ripped out. Large, there's a bit of grey remaining. It could be a wolf or it could be a watchtower. Okay - leave that, we need to find the tear. Ah - here it is! What did this? A fire? An animal? Doesn't matter, this is the edge or it. Of them. This is where the tapestry was torn (if it ever was a single tapestry). An event, a cataclysmic event, caused by some mortal sin - pride, ignorance, jealousy... It's huge and it's common to all of the stories. That's where we put our first thread. It's the overlap in the two accounts of the war, it's the Zeus equals Jupiter. We stictch. And we keep on stiching.

                                            Nothing quite lines up perfectly - particularly not people's accounts of events - because those events are a construct of trying to reconcile different worlds. That's what the God Machine is. It's the weaver that is trying to repair the damage. Blindly and experimentally and without reference to morality or happiness or what the picture was originally intended to be. But it sees two edges that look like they can be joined and it does so. People may argue over what "really happened" but the God Machine has already put its own version in place and moved on. That's why Demons can slip between personas swapping and retaining pieces of people's lives - because they're the needles of the God Machine. Misplaced ones perhaps, broken ones probably. But they're needles that sew the threads of the world. Is the Dark Mother an ancient mage who ascended to the Supernal? Maybe she was once or maybe she wasn't, or maybe there were two similar figures that have become one figure now that history has been crudely joined. This is why sometimes the God Machine will create what people think are alternate timelines. It joins two sections together and for a little while the picture is different. But then it realizes this doesn't work. Maybe there's a better fit over there, maybe the edges didn't line up as well as it intended putting a wolf's head on that man over there. So it unpicks and tries again. The God Machine isn't intelligent, it is smart. It cannot design, it can only see. And it feels its way along blindly, trying and trying to repair.

                                            Was it once a single, bright tapestry that tore apart? Pieces missing so that even when joined together at last you can still see the damage, still characters and events missing from the tale? Was it always five different tales now being clumsily but so skillfully joined into one? What is the Abyss - things that have yet to be woven in? Loose threads and torn fragments of the picture that can never be found a place? What is the Supernal? Another part of the picture or the picture on the jigsaw box that the God Machine or the Exarchs look at as they try to assemble the world to look like it. Do the Exarchs know they are missing pieces? Does the God Machine care that it is?

                                            To pull back from the description back to game level stuff, the thing I like about this idea is that the inconsistencies and different takes on things have an in-game explanation. It's not just a storyteller being okay with the fact that things don't quite line up, it's an explanation you could give to characters in the game, it's an explanation that you could provide to your players at the end of a three year epic cross-over game and they would be satisfied. I have never been and never will be satisfied with the approach that there need be nothing behind the curtain. Once a player or GM acknowledges that it's all sound and fury signifying nothing, then exploring of mysteries becomes devalued. We might never find Moby Dick, but knowing that Moby Dick isn't out there makes searching for him much less satisfying. We do not need to know what is down in the depths to enjoy searching, but we cannot know that they are empty. Players expect their storyteller to know the truth in any mystery and are less satisfied if she does not.

                                            Fnord!
