    I'd like it if the "multiple attacks/targets" maneuvers were harmonized to use a common-ish system; I think having a single guideline for "How this sort of thing works" would save a lot of book-checking at the table. We already have Autofire as our rules example of multiple targets, so I'd be inclined to make it the standard - you can attack X targets and suffer a -1 penalty for each target after the first.

    I think this is what we have listed for multiple targets:
    Combat Archer 3: Uses medium burst autofire rules.
    Iajutsu 3: Attack up to [Dexterity] targets, no apparent penalty. (Could be a "medium burst autofire melee attack.")
    Staff Fighting 4: Attack all opponents "in melee range", -1 penalty per target, but autohit any foe that attempts(? attempted?) a hand-to-hand attack that turn.
    Alternate Thrown Weapons 3: Attack up to [Dexterity] targets(?), -1 penalty per target. The penalty is "per projectile" but the text reads "multiple targets" so I assume this is one strike per target (as I think we're not returning to the multiple attacks on a single target scenario.)
    Two-Weapon Fighting 4: Split your attack pool between two targets and suffer a -2 penalty to each attack.

        Originally posted by StSword View Post


        So you can combine grappling maneuvers with non grappling maneuvers, but not other grappling maneuvers?

        That's a little odd. Why is it easier to combine a boxing maneuver with wrestling than another wrestling maneuver?
        The term "maneuver" does not mean the same thing in the context of the list of things you can do while grappling as it does when describing the benefits of a Fighting Style Merit. The exceptions are those Merits that *explicitly* add an additional maneuver to the repertoire, which then become additional possible grappling results. The combat rules are clear about how many grappling maneuvers you can inflict on an opponent.

        Keep in mind that Storytelling is not a d20-style system where words have arbitrary meanings based on game system templates. Unless the text calls out something and labels it as a tag/template its meanings are in normal English and context-driven, not hard-coded.

        As for combining boxing and wrestling there are many cases where it works (such as MMA clinch work) but for the most part, boxing is hitting with Brawl and grappling is applying grappling maneuvers with Brawl, so they don't usually work together.

                        It's been talked about a little bit, but I feel like Punch Drunk is really worrying from a design stand point. As was mentioned, it allows for a normal human to take four hellfire missiles before going down, and works much more effectively and convincingly if limited to only bashing attacks as proposed. This in turn fits with the general shift in GMC to Lethal damage being a bit of a bigger deal.

                        However my bigger concern, as the author of that merit said himself, is that it was balanced without supernaturals in mind... which is another point of contention for me. All of the supernaturals will draw from this book if the storytellers allow it, as all of them are at heart humans. It may be impossible to think of all the permutations for abuse from every supernatural splat there is, but a drawback as weak as "doubled healing times" is effortlessly overcome by even the base abilities of pretty much every supernatural I can think of - except maybe changelings.

                        I like many of the merits here, but worry that the more you scale up everyone and everything the less it feels like a world of darkness and the more it feels like angsty superheroes.

                        ________

                        That worry aside, my other comment is that At Any Cost feels a bit overpriced to me. Especially when compared to things like Meditative Mind and Support Network, which can both easily make back the experience spent on them due to saved integrity.

                        The number of times I've been in a combat that inflicted more than one or two tilts on a character and lasted more than three rounds are nonexistent. Maybe scale the cost back to 2 dots and make the requirements 2 Resolve and 2 Composure respectively? That way there's more room to grow into the merit if the character chooses to invest further experience into those traits.

                        I'd also remove the mention of an "opponent" from it, as that makes it feel more fighty than a merit that could just as easily serve any of the emergency first responders.

                            A few thoughts on Punch Drunk.

                            - If your ST is using hellfire missiles (or tank rounds) against human characters and not inflicting the damage as aggravated, your game is running in some kind of extreme action hero mode that WoD really doesn't cover.

                            - Punch Drunk is not for supernatural creatures. Humans only. I'll amend the text to clarify this. If STs decide to allow supernatural characters to take the merit despite this, the ST assumes responsibility for the problems this causes her chronicle.

                            - Punch Drunk's design space is intended to in some way emulate the action movie element where the Stallones and Schwarzneggers take bullets and blades and somehow, beyond the point where anyone else would have died, he somehow just keeps coming. The mooks keep shooting, but the action hero just refuses to die.

                            - All things being equal, Punch Drunk extends the character's life by up to 4 turns. 4 turns in which her actions are degraded (she still suffers dice penalties), but she can still act. This is the point in the action movie where the bad guys call for the flamethrowers, or high explosives, or whatever. They try to take the fight to aggravated, because she will. not. die. At this point, smart characters either use their few extra turns to get to cover, or hope that their allies are close enough to find them.

                                        So one thing I haven't seen people talk about much is how the new styles interact with the existing ones. Given that hybrid styles are intended to be a possibility I felt this was worth exploring so I did some research
                                        These are organized first by tag, then by dot level. I’m just going to name and give my assessment, since everything is either in the Fighting Style section of the GMC rules update or Hurt Locker Styles doc I won’t bother with a more comprehensive cross reference atm. Will attempt to keep them in the order listed, no promises.


                                        Striking Styles
                                        One Dot
                                        Martial Arts: Focused Attack: Solid foundational one dot. Reducing penalty to hit specified target by 1 and armor by 1 potentially useful for almost any follow up
                                        Unarmed Defense: Like a Book: Too situational. +2 defense is great if your character is a professional martial artist in a ring fight. Falls apart as soon as somebody pulls a knife or facing multiple opponents. Or multiple opponents with knives. A bonus you lose when things get *more* dangerous isn’t helpful.
                                        Boxing: Head Protection: Much like previous maneuver, not a huge fan. Not bad, but not great.
                                        Martial Arts(HLA): Leg Kick: Great 1 dot move. Probably my favorite
                                        Clinch Strike: Listed as a Striking Merit, wouldn’t use it as one. *Would* use as grappling merit, which it also is. Will return to in grappling section.

                                        Two Dot
                                        Martial Arts: Defensive Strike: Good, not great. Useful for sizing up an opponent or when stalling
                                        Unarmed Defense: Studied Style: Meh. Only really relevant if facing more than one opponent. Still too situational
                                        Boxing: Defensive Jab: Great move. Might be *too* powerful if abilities that modify unarmed damage include it
                                        Martial Arts(HLA): Cutting Elbow: Interesting move! I like it for flavor, mechanics seem solid
                                        Boot Party: I see why this is a Striking maneuver but I don’t see much use for it since there aren’t any striking maneuvers that make inflicting Knockdown particularly easy. I could see it having and being used in Street and/or Grappling tags however.
                                        Headbutt: Interesting idea, mechanics are a little weird. I know stun locking is a concern but I feel like this could be handled with a normal attack roll and some modifiers. Might take this for fun if I had extra points on a street fighting type, but not as a core skill.

                                        Three Dot
                                        Martial Arts: Whirlwind Strike: Similar to Defensive Jab. Would be interesting to have a character with both
                                        Unarmed Defense: Redirect: This is where unarmed defense finally gets good. This skill is cool and fun, albeit not much use 1v1.
                                        Boxing: Knockout Artist: This is a solid ability. Stun is a powerful tilt and making it slightly easier to inflict is nice. Still requires a 4 damage hit though, so while good it wouldn’t be my first pick.
                                        Martial Arts(HLA): Trapping: This is a strange one and I’m trying to understand where I would want to use it. Against an armored opponent, or as a setup for trying to Stun? It feels like the sort of thing you could use really effectively once and then opponents would learn to break contact before the other shoe dropped.
                                        Ground and Pound: Much like Boot Party and Clinch Strike I don’t see this making much sense in a Striking Style but Grappling and Street have great use for it
                                        Hip Drop: Hip Drop is a really interesting case. Under normal circumstances it doesn’t offer much, but it gets incredibly powerful if you have Touch of Death.

                                        Four Dot
                                        Martial Arts: The Hand as Weapon: This would be a really good merit if The Touch of Death didn’t exist. However, in the new system there’s very little reason to make your fists 0 lethal weapons when you can use this slot for another skill and make them +2 lethal as the capstone anyway.
                                        Unarmed Defense: Joint Strike: A contested Strength + Brawl roll from a style that emphasizes Dex isn’t the greatest idea, especially since failure means you take damage like normal.
                                        Boxing: Combination Blows: This is interesting and potentially fairly powerful. Boxers will always have at least two dice, and may have as many as four at character creation. Typically this averages out to a +1 damage but I kind of like the variable damage aspect. A good pick.
                                        Martial Arts(HLA): Inch Force: This is a solid defense against enemy grapplers, which are potentially quite dangerous under the new rules. Situational, but potentially a life-saver.

                                        Five Dot
                                        Martial Arts: The Touch of Death: Incredibly hard to justify not taking this. Very powerful merit.
                                        Unarmed Defense: Like The Breeze: I was wrong, there is a good way to set up for those “if opponent is prone” moves. Cool Maneuver, satisfying pacifist type move.
                                        Boxing: Out for the Count: Dealing enough damage to stun is still tough, but if you do the fight is over.
                                        Martial Arts(HLA): High Momentum Strike: Another possible source of Knockdown, or bonus damage. This isn’t a bad move but others provide easier/more reliable means of achieving what it provides. Probably the weakest overall capstone.

                                        My overall thoughts on a hybrid Striking Style: Focused attack or Leg Kick; Defensive Jab; Hip Drop; Combination Blows; and The Touch of Death. Defensive Combat: Brawl is a must, and Fighting Finesse: Striking allows me to make a str 2 dex 4 sta 2 martial artist who can wreak havoc with only 8 merit dots invested. Granted that’s 1 more than a character gen mortal gets but still, not a bad investment if we’re playing a slightly higher powered game. Alternatively, str 3 dex 3. Spare merit dots would go to inch force, knockout artist and out for the count, in that order.

                                        Grappling! I’m just going to list these by name, Grappling and Grappling Alt are easy to find but there are a ton of loose ones all over the place, sorry.

                                        One Dot
                                        Sprawl: Solid, but kind of worrying. So many grappling merits are based on going prone that this means anyone with a one dot merit can make large sections of the style much harder to use. Do two characters with Sprawl remain standing during a grapple until one of them passes out or breaks free? This might need some weird house ruling.
                                        Standing Throw: Hell yeah. This is a set up for most of what follows. Great one dot.
                                        Tuska Ate: Pretty sure that bonus applies to the roll, not as bonus successes. My only issue with this is that it means the player and ST has to track a bunch of modifiers. Grappling is different from normal combat, grappling with a weapon has special sub rules, and inflicting tilts is another layer of mechanics.
                                        Clinch Strike: Great one dot, my favorite of the options.

                                        Two Dot
                                        Takedown: I’m not super clear on how this is different than Standing Throw? It deals damage and doesn’t require you to establish a grapple first, but otherwise it seems exactly the same.
                                        Small Joint Manipulation: Don’t know what the Agony Tilt is yet but this is cool
                                        ChokeHold: A great way to non lethally dispatch weak opponents but it scales poorly against high stamina ones.
                                        Boot Party: Not technically a grappling move according to the tags. Really should be since it can work off Standing Throw/Takedown so well.
                                        Headbutt: Everything I said about this before still stands
                                        Trunk Squeeze: Similar concept to Choke Hold but I like the execution better. Combine with Positional Dominance and Clinch Strike for massive bashing in short order.

                                        Three Dot
                                        Joint Lock: Basically an upgrade to the Hold move, and a good one at that. Solid, and a prereq for some of the upper tier abilities. My feeling is that it effectively creates a grappling sub style, which I like. You can be really good at joint locks, or you can do other grappling moves.
                                        Ippon: Upgrades Takedown. Good if you’re using Takedown, not so much if you aren’t. Takedown is a good move though.
                                        Ground and Pound: A great way to follow up Takedown.
                                        Ground Fighter: Probably a good idea if you’re going to be elbow dropping on prone opponents.

                                        Four Dot
                                        Dynamic Guard: Solid, not a whole lot to say about this. It does tend to reinforce my notion that going dex four with fighting finesse in the chosen style is the best way to get an edge in unarmed combat
                                        Lock Flow: Makes Joint Lock better.

                                        Five Dot
                                        Tap or Snap: This is awesome
                                        Positional Dominance: This is a nice damage bonus that works with any other grappling maneuver. As such it’s fairly powerful but for a style capstone I think it’s appropriate.

                                        My overall feeling on grappling is that there are a lot of viable options. Going for Tap or Snap, a Restrain move, simply wearing the person out with tons of damage, or trying to stun them by slamming on the ground are all possible. Of all the Fighting Styles Grappling is now the one that I feel has the most interesting choices.

                                        Light Weapons: I’m not going to do the same style writeup as I did for Grappling and Striking. I just don’t see much in the way of possible synergies between the styles that currently use the tag. If anybody disagrees I would love to be proved wrong! That said I like Kiriage as a possible replacement third dot for Light Weapons. Feint is an interesting move but somewhat tricky to understand, and as near as I can tell is only really worth it if the opponent is heavily armored.

                                        Heavy Weapons: This style needs some love. Most of the other existing styles have gotten a fair bit in the way of new options, but Heavy Weapons got almost nothing, with one notable exception that I will get to later. Yes, Armed Defense is tagged as a Heavy Melee style as well as a Light Melee style, but the two don’t actually mix well at all. To use techniques from both a character needs strength, dex *and* stamina all at three, a literal impossibility at character creation and an expensive sink even for more advanced characters. What Heavy Weapons did get was Hip Drop, a terrifying prospect when combined with a Fireaxe. Just hope you don’t miss. Speaking of missing, could we get an alternate first dot for the style? Sacrificing three dice for a +1 weapon bonus is not remotely a good trade. In a system where attack and defense values are usually pretty close, that represents a massive drop in accuracy, and since successes add to damage that +1 just balances out the average damage lost to the missing dice. It’s a net loss every time.

                                        I may get to Street Fighting later. For now I think this wall of text is already pretty massive

                                                    Originally posted by Bunyip View Post
                                                    - Punch Drunk is not for supernatural creatures. Humans only. I'll amend the text to clarify this. If STs decide to allow supernatural characters to take the merit despite this, the ST assumes responsibility for the problems this causes her chronicle.
                                                    I just don't think that mundane merits should be locked away from any of the templates. Part of the point of the templates is that they are still at heart or origin, human. Making mundane merits that the supernaturals can't take separates them mechanically from that and then also feels really weird when apparently normal humans have access to some sort of in-born heroism that can allow them to take leagues more punishment than the most durable promethean or resilient vampire.

                                                    Particularly for such a low buy-in cost. Resolve 3, Composure 3 is pretty cheap for such a supposedly "hero-locked" ability, and only slightly above average, assuming humans average 2 in any stat.

                                                    Originally posted by Bunyip View Post
                                                    - If your ST is using hellfire missiles (or tank rounds) against human characters and not inflicting the damage as aggravated, your game is running in some kind of extreme action hero mode that WoD really doesn't cover.
                                                    As for the Hellfire missile example not being appropriate, I discuss Rules As Written when discussing design, not Rules As Intended. These rules are being put up explicitly for feedback and revision, so discussions on written rules are important I think. Sure, you could change it so that missiles deal Agg, but per RAW the following all do Lethal damage and would be protected against, including 2000 pound bombs (Armory p. 111), Rank 5 Entity blasts (GMC p.229), supernatural meteor strikes (Mummy, p. 130), and fires of all intensity (NWoD Core p.180). Changing the way that Lethal damage is applied as opposed to Aggravated damage would require a comprehensive overhaul nearly all of NWoD and NWoD2e combat mechanics.

                                                    If you wanted a more relevant example I could have used a werewolf's talons, an eldritch creature's mauling, four consecutive car crashes. Take your pick, but singling out the fact that missiles are an absurd example is missing the entire point of the example.

                                                            I have to say, I am disturbed by the change in tone I am seeing here.

                                                            I feel the current merits do not feel amenable to a horror game. I love the idea of giving players more options to how to play their characters, but I have two major issues: Some of these seem are too powerful. They appear to give the player more than just options, but a serious edge that removes some of the constrained nature of the game. These merits are empowering, and that is not a good thing. (I can "project" a field that makes people fight more fairly? That seems a little much."


                                                            2. The way this is written worries me. The idea that having friends around to "validate you" will just make things better. The idea that if you watch a truly horrific (your friend getting eviscerated as you are mind raped) scene you can brush it off with some hugs and tea. The trauma and sanity loss are meant to be serious parts of this game handled maturely, and they seem to be getting downplayed. I think the power being given to the players sends the wrong message about what this game is and how to handle issues in your own life. You don't get better and you aren't supposed to. Things stick with you and take effort to deal with. Friends and loved ones are not there to validate you. They challenge you, support you, and lead by example. This is more than the stresses of a work day, these are horrors that threaten life and sanity.

                                                            Now of course I do not want to be needlessly negative. I am just confused about what the overriding goal of this is. Honestly I think much of it could be fixed with phrasing, emphasizing that being a well rounded person can help ameliorate these issues though not eliminate them entirely. And isn't that what the mechanics are going for in the first place?
