    Between Serial Killers and Slashers

    06-16-2016, 11:50 PM
    Okay, so we all agree that slashers are fun to use once in a session. But where do you guys consider the line to be between a "typical" serial killers that we're used to, and the slasher killers that CofD introduced?
    Tags: None

      To me it comes down to what you want them to be able to do. If they're "mundane" serial killers, then I use the Ripper level Undertakings. They get a mechanical boost but are overall still pretty grounded. If you want them to be able to do things that are explicitly supernatural, then you've crossed into Scourge territory.

          Yeah. If you're actually using a serial killer, as in a mundane mortal who has an uncontrollable urge to kill people, you should just use a ripper. They don't have to all be a super-competent one but that's what the ripper type is there for.

          If you want someone who has killed people but isn't a serial killer, for instance many mortal characters at the end of a chronicle or most action heroes, then you're just dealing with a mortal who's taken some Integrity Breaking Points.

                      Pretty much this! In my mind there is a bit of a difference in serial killers and Slashers though. I feel like a serial killer is, pardon the term for such a monster, but more mundane than a Slasher. Typically a mortal with mental disorders and terrible compulsions. I think crossing over to a Slasher represents on the Ripper level a touch of the supernatural, and on the Scourge level a slathering of it.

                          The book is pretty clear that rippers are not magical at all, this is represented by their Talent, which is 'abnormal, eerie, even awe-inspiring, but they aren't magical.' And their Frailty, which 'logical extensions of their madness and shortcomings, not arcane secrets that hunters can use to bring them down.'

                          If you don't want to use rippers for 'mundane' serial killers, that's fine, but that is what they are for.
