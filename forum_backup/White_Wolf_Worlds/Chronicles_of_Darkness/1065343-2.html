    The best argument I've seen for Beast is that in many ways it's going back to the idea that kicked off this entire franchise.

    &quot;A beast I am, lest a beast I become.&quot;

    Yeah, you have to hurt people to live, what's new about that? The trick is to do it in a way that causes minimum harm (or just target the assholes who deserve it, there's enough to go around) and maybe gives something back in return, and then use your powers to do some good in the world.
    Yeah, I really don't think Beast's "you have to hurt people to live" is all that new for CofD. Vampires have to drink human blood, werewolves have to hunt, etc. Nor is their justification of their own behavior an unusual thing. I think what bugs some folks about it is that Beast has stripped away some of the layers of metaphor, making it too real for some folks' comfort.

        Well, its also no small part in that the hurting is differently from vampire, werewolf or changeling. Vampire blood dolls can be compared to a blood donor at Red Cross, and the feeding can take any form from mystical rite to sex to loyalty. And it feels good for all involved. Werewolves... well, eating humans is actually rare; most hunts against humans don't involve death and injury, and even when it does, the suffering is over quickly. Changelings snack on willpower, something that is so ephemeral you might not even realize it, through any kind of emotions. Sex, sports, and working in funeral homes are all valid and pretty harmless ways of feeding. Hells, I have Enchanting Performance on my current 'ling, and he can basically give out Inspired at will. Take glamour from lots of people, they all lose one point of willpower, but they get it back from Inspired, ending up in a net gain of glamour for the changeling and no loss for the humans. Demons have people willingly sign the dotted line, and neither mages nor prommies feed off people at all.

        Beasts feed on suffering and pain and loss. They're naturally sadistic, and the nature of that sadistic Hunger makes it hard to figure out ways to balance it out with some kind of positive event. You have to make people -hurt-. Not just physically, but on an emotional level, which, by many, is considered far worse than just a physical hurt you might not even feel. Beasts want to pull people off the hill and enjoy the thrill of their loss. In a very real way, its a game about indulging sadism, and that's not something many people are comfortable doing. Hells, most people feel guilty even imagining it*. And the closest thing you have to not indulging is feeding voyeristically while someone else hunts.

        So, yeah. Saying "you have to hurt people to live is not new" completely misses the entire point. Beast's take is vastly different than any other gameline, and not just because of metaphors. Its the very underlying reasoning and assumptions that make up the hurt that makes all the difference.


        * as per Psychology Today, which I think is a fairly reputable site. Feel free to disagree.

                Beast just takes the romanticized aspects away from hurting people, by not letting you cover up what you are really doing.

                Blood dolls being like blood donors? I don't recall blood donors being addicted to the experience and the person drawing the blood. The varied methods of drinking someone's blood does not change the fact that you're still drinking blood. Feels good for everyone... well, drugs certainly feel good.
                Yes, hunts don't have to target humans as the prey, nor do hunts end in killing the prey... But collateral damage is a thing. Humans simply get in the way. Lunacy from witnessing werewolves in action, organizations wrecked in the more abstract hunts, cultivating Resonance that affects people's minds and behaviors, or just plain ol' Death Rage ripping apart people unlucky enough to pass by.
                Don't remember word-for-word how the previewed Changeling rules work, but if changelings do drain Willpower... how can the victims not realize it? Willpower isn't just meta-narrative fuel for players to spend on behalf of their characters. It reflects how mentally and emotionally energetic a person is. Spend too much Willpower, and you feel down in the dumps, so you take a nap to rest or indulge in potentially unhealthy habitual behaviors (ie. Vices) Feeding on emotions is generally subtle, but changelings have an incentive to aggravate and exaggerate the emotions people feel... and with example emotions like Desire, Wrath, Fear, and Sorrow, I wouldn't want a changeling running around near me.
                Demons have people willingly sign the dotted line, yeah. So do loan sharks. And those elaborate contracts that technically tell you everything you need to know in the fine print.
                Mage's don't feed on people, but they can screw up Sleeper lives around them in a million ways. If the leaking Nimbuses, Quiescence, unsafely relinquished spells, and Paradoxes don't do it, then the mages treating Sleepers as pawns on the way to satisfy their Obsessions will.
                Prometheans... really? The guys who poison the world and peoples' minds by slipping up in some journey that they aren't clear on the details?

                Now of course, all the above supernaturals grant ways to mitigate the damage you do like you said. Which I find worse, because they're lying to themselves how they aren't monsters, when they're responsible or disciplined monsters at best. Beast, on the other hand, throws that veneer outside the window.

                                                        Originally posted by Ekorren View Post
                                                        All very interesting. So when you play Beast, are you basically a human with the monster's powers, or do you have the ability to become the actual monster (i.e. shapeshifting), or are you not in human form at all?

                                                        Does the Demon book allow you to play as Angels as well?
                                                        For Beast, the answer is generally no. There are a couple of exceptions, but usually the only way to take monstrous form is to either retreat into your Lair or impose your Lair on reality.

                                                        For Demon, there are no rules for playing Angels. Angels with the amount of free will to be player characters by definition have fallen and become Demons, or have become cut off from the God Machine and become Exiles. Angels are really suited for narrative role because they lack free will and because they use the ephemeral entity rules (which are not used for player characters. Now with that said, if you really wanted to, you could come up with some rule hackssince there is a 2e Werewolf supplement that provide some very basic rules for using spirits as characters. Also, Geist 2e is supposedly going to feature rules for playing bonified ghosts as their "minor" splat (aka not the book's main focus, but a related "lesser" character type. Vampire's minor splat are Ghouls, and Werewolf's minor splat is the Wolf-Blooded, both of which are featured in their respective book's 1st appendix. Something similar to minor splats are also going to pop up in Hurt Locker, but I won't spoil the details for you). If we do get hard and fast rules for playing ghosts as PCs, they would most likely be useable to play angels as well.
