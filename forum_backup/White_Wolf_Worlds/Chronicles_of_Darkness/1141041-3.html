    Originally posted by ajf115 View Post
    There are multiple types available and they will be more like Spirit Influences in that you make your own, although we will be making example Spheres. We know that the potential number of Spheres would be huge, but as a rule of thumb Spheres have to be defined to a single, easily-conceptualised thing, and you have to be able to tightly and intuitively define it - this is an actual part of the Sphere creation process.
    Are you planning on just creating rules for players and STs to create powers, while giving a few examples?
    This is simple, but it sounds difficult to play.
    Many players in my group had trouble playing Mage the Ascension back in the day because Spheres were so open to interpretation.
    Players had so many unknown options, they had no idea what to do. So, they only used the few example spells.
    It is possible that many in my group didn't have the imagination for playing MtA, but they did much better when Awakening came out because there were well defined power levels and 10-20 times more spells written out.
    I personally love to make things up in or out of game, but many players do not. In fact, my groups ST has been very "book-centric" and is reluctant to allow anything that isn't written in an official book. I just recently got him to let me use a custom Vampire Discipline after nearly 20 years of gaming.
    Maybe your experience has been different, but I've found that most people (players) like things to be well defined.

    So, my opinion would be to make as many examples as possible, but don't call them examples.
    Then, supply the rules for players and STs to create their own, if they want.

    Don't get me wrong, giving players the ability to create things in game (or during character creation) is always good.
    I just think a game can be too dynamic for most players, if they have to create everything themselves.
    I tried writing up my own game to play spirits years ago (Spirit: The something or other cool name). Spirit Influences are either too wide or oddly specific, I eventually gave up on it.
    If you go too specific, the power is week. If you go wide and general, it can be over powered. Middle ground was difficult to find.

    Spheres: My guess is that around a dozen Spheres would be a good "final" number, while giving players guidelines for the creation of others. If I understand correctly, Spheres don't have any ability themselves (besides Authority). They function as a partial definition of the character while supplying dice for Designs and Revelations. Is that right?
    Designs: These sound open, which works for creating long term effects. Rules for creation should include power levels, effects, duration, etc. can set the number of successes (or dice modifiers) required for a new Design. Dice pools built by method (social manipulations, physical changes, etc) to decide Attribute and Skill combinations.
    The rules should give an example of Design creation, then have 5-10 "generic" Designs already made (I've already got an idea for one called "Cupid").
    Revelations:
    Originally posted by ajf115 View Post
    Revelations have a rating much like Designs and are exactly the kind of immediate effect/combat use powers you're talking about. Lightning strikes, temporary enthrallment, healing and so on. We're intending to write a number of 'Sphere-agnostic' Revelations (Smite, enthrall, command environment etc) with guidelines for the magnitude which each Sphere rating should allow, along with tables etc for freely designing Revelations.
    I like this. Sounds like you create a list of generic game rule effects, then allow a player to "flavor" them with the Sphere of their choice.

                        Originally posted by Nicolas Milioni View Post
                        Can you give me examples of what sorts of things a empyrean remembers where he has flashbacKS to his former estate?
                        What an Empyrean remembers of their former live is often a source of pain and confusion since, although the human and Celestial are merged, the minds and thoughts of a tiny and temporary human don't quite line up with an eternal entity from beyond time and space. The memories of their former life often come in visions in an Empyrean's quiet moments, and nightmares in their sleep. They're almost always unpleasant, and even then, they're very often inconsistent--the inhuman experience, thoughts and emotions of a Celestial consciousness squeezed through the tiny human mind spurt and come out in poorly translated pieces. The Empyrean remembers an event one way during a vision, while in their sleep they may remember an entirely different event, just to think back one day and remember the first event but with shifted details.

                        It's a mess, but, even with that certain patterns emerge--though the story and images may change, the overarching theme of the visions persist.

                        The Empyrean remembers that they became too passive and humanity overwhelmed them, or they became too greedy and just started taking more and more from the world until something collapsed, or perhaps they lost touch with themselves and for that they became ineffective in their duties. The overall failing remains the same, even if the visions and nightmares can be completely different from one another.

                        That's actually why the Creeds were formed--by squeezing their thoughts through the symbolism and allegorical images of real-world religions, the visions and memories start to become more cohesive, allowing the Empyrean to gain better control over them, and form a baseline to start their work in fixing what they created. An Empyrean may pick up from their visions that they didn't possess the personal strength to manage their incredible power and so they ended up destroying themselves--thus they choose to join the Titanes, and over time they may genuinely start to remember themselves as an Olympian in ancient Greece, whose petty and negligent abuses of their power led to the revolt of the world and the fall from Mount Olympus. Or, though they may not think of themselves as a literal Olympian, their memories are close enough that they might as well be. Much like real-world religions, some people interpret the stories more literally than others. While those without clearer visions tend to also be less rigid, it also grants less control over the visions, which can lead to a build up of Choshek.

                        Now, what's Choshek? It's a dark and corruptive force within an Empyrean born from the crushing guilt and sorrow of their past life memories. Similar to a Promethean's Torment, if it is not regulated it can overwhelm an Empyrean and induce a state of Chashak, or darkening, wherein they lose access to the bulk of their powers and become vulnerable to attack.

                        Sorry if this was a bit of a ramble...
