    Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
    You know, I’ve never really thought about summoning removing the concept from memory in the context of the anima mundi. Can somebody remove the idea of themselves from greater humanity? Would that make people forget or just not think of them until the duration expires?
    It's an interesting idea, but I don't know if it's going to have any tangible effects. Removing something from an Oneiros is pretty dramatic for that individual because you've literally removed part of them. Removing something from the Temenos will likewise remove it from the collective consciousness, but every individual will still remember it because it's still part of their Oneiros; at most they'll just have a little harder communicating the removed concept with other humans because it's gone from the space that bridges the idea between two individuals. We even have a system modelling how difficult it is to make any significant change to an idea in the Temenos, further implying thst just changing a single Goetic entity just doesn't do that much. I.e. remove the walker of an actor from the Temenos and people will still relate to that person through their personal memories of watching movies and maybe interviews of that actor.
    The Anima Mundi isn't connected directly to humanity (personally or collectively) so it's a lot more difficult to wrap your head around the effects of changing or removing a Goetic entity from there, but I think it's still not that noticable. Humans won't be directly affected and most stuff that is directly connected to the Anima Mundi (like wildlife, celestial objects, the Arcana) can't really react to any changes, unlike humans who can still react to changes in the Temenos.
    I've also heard that the Aeon of Fate tends to not stick around without that causing any known problems, but I genuinely don't know if it's something written in the books or just part of a campaign I heard about or someone's personal head canon. Regardless, the Arcanum of Fate should still exist independently since it doesn't come from the Aeon, and the Astral doesn't (afaik) follow the model of As Above, So Below.

    I'd gladly read more about this if I'm wrong in my interpretation, but maybe it's not the right thread for that.

                    The Aeon of Fate often looks like Medraut, and conventional wisdom is that the Aeons are confined to their Citadels in the deepest part of the Astral; Dancers in the Dusk, a Changeling supplement taking a broader look at how the Lost interact with dreams, presents several dream-entities that are basically Astral entities reflected into the shallows of ordinary dreams, among them a couple of Aeon-equivalents (called Morpheans, which makes things confusing when talking crossover), including the Ruinous Prince, who is Mordred and who offers a Vow of All-Consuming Fire that basically fills the Ruin's M.O. by letting someone achieve greatness that will not last beyond their lifetime.
