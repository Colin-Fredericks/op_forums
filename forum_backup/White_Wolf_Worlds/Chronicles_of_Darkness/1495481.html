    Disappointing Derangements

    10-23-2022, 12:43 PM
    So, while reading up on derangements, I noticed, there were ten of them, and thinking, cool, I can simply roll a d10 to determine which derangement they develop. I could randomly roll and if they rolled that one again, it would move to severe. Alas, there are nine-derangements. So maybe if I roll a 10, they luck out and don't get one or, if they roll a one, they roll again and it's severe; I like randomly rolling stuff because it, at times, takes the decision-making away from me so I can focus on other things. Yet, there is fun in picking the player's derangement considering their action and the degree of their dramatic-failure.

    How do you handle derangements?

                              Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
                              Actually, I like derangements; it provides the possibility of consequences for a player's action.
                              Besides all the problems with how derangements were implemented, they don't really add much here Even dropping from Morality 2 to 1 is slightly favoring not gaining a derangement. The odds are that most characters aren't going to get a derangement from Morality (or equivalent) loss, because they're not going to be trying to drop their Morality into the toilet. If you have five PCs and they all manage to keep to Morality 4+, you might see one or two derangements between the group.

                              For non-mortals, the mechanical consequences of losing their Morality equivalent was far more important than the potential of gaining a derangement.

                              Also, there were something like 40 derangement pairs printed over the course of 1e; though this includes a number of variants that interact with things like Frenzy, as well as ones that are exclusive to specific supernaturals.

                              Whatever your feelings about 2e are here, the Integrity system ensures there are always consequences for actions, as every result on a breaking point check has an impact of the PC. Even an Exceptional Success, which doesn't impart a Condition to resolve like all the other do, refills 1 WP and gives you an extra Beat as your character comes through the trying event more resolute and experienced. Consequences don't have to be bad after all.

                                      Well, for the most part, buying any CofD book 1e or 2e (I know it's a bit confusing, the old books are titled "World of Darkness" but there was a branding change as the 20th anniversary editions of the classic WoD books started selling a lot), you can get them via DriveThruRPG as either pdfs for Print-on-Demand. The shipping costs aren't great depending on where you live relative to where DTRPG has a printer, but if you want physical copies that's probably the most reliable way to go. You can always point out that the books just changed printers and aren't out of print since you can get fresh printed copies whenever you want if you think someone's trying to gouge you by calling one of the books a collector's item.

                                      Since you already have the 1e core book, you can get a free file from DTRPG called the "God-Machine Rule Update," which is a guide to updating the 1e core rules to the 2e ones (obviously as an early thing, it's not 100% the same as the full 2e books) to get a feel of the changes to the baseline rules.

                                      It's also worth considering that three of the supernatural games (Demon, Beast, and Deviant) only run on the 2e rules, so there's another reason to consider looking into the 2e stuff before you go too deep on the older book.
