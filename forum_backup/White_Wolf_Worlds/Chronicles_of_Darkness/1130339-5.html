            Many gamers believe the phrase "caveat emptor" is lazy, but the whole Chronicles of Darkness line is full of options to include or remove. If I ran a chronicle of Requiem with every bloodline, Discipline, and sub-faction of each covenant present, it would be a mess. You pick and choose.

            I've said it before, but my design style if utility. I prefer to give players and Storytellers options and gripping chronicle hooks over hard-and-fast rules for play. If I write a Discipline, I want every level of that Discipline to have a story behind it. One night there may come a coterie who wish to study the genesis of Dominate, and behold, each level offers a tease about the first recorded user, or one of its most famed implementations, or the way certain vampires use it. The same goes with the Contagion. Every paragraph must give options, story hooks, or simply spark imagination. I want to make chronicles ever more exciting and fun.

                    Spoilers for Three Shades of Night following:

                    The contagion in the book was the "muskegon virus" (which seems to be fictional) that was mainly transmitted by mosquitos, crows and ... vampires. Within the fiction, the regular version of the muskegon virus mostly affected the young and elderly but the big contagion they are fighting was supernaturally amplified, leading to almost certain death no matter the physical fitness of whoever caught it. I am a bit hazy on the details, but I think the cause for how the muskegon virus got out of hand was the shitty spiritual landscape of Chicago (might be a Wound there somewhere?), which also led to many catastrophic accidents and other weirdness. In the past, this was in fact the cause for the Great Chicago Fire, which the Big Three back in that day could not prevent because a vampire flaked out on being a sacrifice for a Crúac rite meant to fix the situation. In the book they managed to prevent a second major catastrophe coming to the city, the Cronies actually could go through with the rite this time, easing the overall situation, while the werewolves and mages battled the sick spirit that was the cause of it.

                    So as far as participation goes, the vampires are the first to notice something is awry because of it affecting them first and they provided the Crúac rite. Werewolves had the spirit expertise to figure out what was the base cause of all the shit that was happening and the Awakened were there to put all the disparate supernatural elements together.

                    Also the Unholy shows up at one point.


                    It is unlikely that this particular contagion is the one talked about in the brochure, but it seems possible that the general idea was inspired by the book.
                    Loved that book ^.^. It's what made the Unholy so interesting to me.

                                        So, what would make the Contagion something that tends to facilitate crossover play? One theory: we've been told that the Contagion infects each gameline's antagonists, presumably enhancing them in some way. You might get super-strix or super-Pandorans or super-goblins, etc. (where, by “super”, I mean “harder for the gameline's protagonists to deal with). But what if this comes coupled with a general vulnerability to the other gamelines?

                                        These enhanced Hosts might be nightmares for the Uratha to deal with; but the Kindred are able to discover a fairly straightforward way to put them down. Conversely, the Contagion-infected Strix might make the Kindreds' Unlives even more of a living hell than usual; but the Uratha can tear them to shreds without too much trouble. So an alliance between vampires and werewolves would be able to deal with the Contagion far more effectively than either one on their own.
