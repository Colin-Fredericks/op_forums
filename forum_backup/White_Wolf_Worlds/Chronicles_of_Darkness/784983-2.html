    It's just hit me - If Elements from Clues are used to get new Clues quicker, it means that Uncovering Clue roll is not at all about what characters find, but where they know to find it.

    From mine example:

    If our previous Clue was "Mystery in question is Abyssal Intruder that Guardians of Veil call the Yellow Sign or Corrupted Mandala. First name comes from the occult symbol that start to spread in the settlement where Intruder has shown."

    Then Element from it can be used to point "In 1895 there was macabre and bizarre theatrical play written, called King in Yellow, from unknown author. It’s full of Yellow Sign imagery and connected to “Lost City of Carcosa”, located on the shores of Lake Hali in the Hyades."

    BUT Uncovering the Clue gives us not the info itself, but rather where to find it. So our PCs could get from Element to look in "Strange Symbolism" darknet database on net or to go to Guardians Symbols Expert in Consilium ( based on previous Clue context). STILL, PC will not know anything about 'Lost City of Carcosa' until they solve a problem in getting it - like going in to weird darknet forums or Social Maneuvering the Guardian NPC to let him talk it. From Uncovering the Clue they know where to look, not the info itself.

    Or at least it's how interpret this quotes from corebook:

    Originally posted by CoD
    Don’t be afraid to add story framing to the search for a Clue. In our previous examples, maybe the monster left behind a dangerous contaminant the characters have to overcome to case the scene. Or for our wrecked ship, perhaps the characters have to traverse shark-infested waters to get where they need to be. If the characters perform particularly well in these efforts, consider offering bonuses to the effort to find the Clue.

            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
            I think the intention is actually that you do need to have the minimum number of clues to uncover the truth, but the complications arise based on how many elements you have left by the time you get all of those clues. I also like the risk/reward dynamic that this interpretation creates. You can spend Elements to help you get more Clues (or to help on other rolls related to the Clue), but doing so means that you might have to face future consequences when you finally uncover the truth, unless you make up for it by finding some clues with extra elements.
            Yeah I'm a little uncertain here.
            A.) Pg 79 states that Uncovering the Truth without further rolls requires the expenditure of elements equal to the number of Clues required.
            B.) Pg 81 states that Uncovering the Truth requires a certain number of Clues.

            So, considering a "three-clue mystery":

            A.) Page 79 'wins'
            * Spending Clue elements on rolls reduces how many are available to Uncover the Truth.
            * Three "empty" Clues (their elements spent finding the other clues or during play) can't solve a three-Clue mystery.
            * A single Clue with 3 elements to spend can solve a three-Clue mystery.
            (Would they still suffer two complications, because they're two Clues short?)

            B.) Page 81 is correct
            * Spending Clue elements doesn't matter because 0-remaining-element Clues still count as Clues
            * Three "empty" Clues (their elements spent finding the other clues or during play) can solve a three-Clue mystery.
            (Would they still suffer three complications, because they're three elements short?)
            * A single Clue with 3 elements to spend can't solve a three-Clue mystery. (It's just more useful for finding other Clues.)

            C.) Both:
            * Spending Clue elements leads to complications during Uncovering the Truth
            * You need three Clues and three elements to "cleanly" solve a three-clue mystery.
            * Three "empty" Clues (their elements spent finding the other clues or during play) can solve a three-Clue mystery, but you suffer three complications.
            * A single Clue with 3 elements to spend can't solve a three-Clue mystery. (Not enough Clues.)
            * A single clue with 3 elements, and two other "empty" Clues, can solve a three-Clue mystery "cleanly"

            What do folks think? C seems to be the most game-useful as it keeps multiple-element Clues from trivializing whole investigations.

                        By-the-book I think you would roll for the Clue at that point, as things like the character's personal stakes in the investigation and so on could be factors.

                        Like, it's possible to get the guy to talk (succeed on Social maneuvers) and then have the investigator's questioning produce bad results (for example, they get hit by the suggested penalty to the Clue roll for being emotionally invested, and so when the councilor talks they hear what they want to hear or they have to cut the interview short because the investigator gets too agitated and the councilor gets threatened.)

                        My gut instinct would be that I wouldn't want to call for a clue roll after at least two rolls for Social Maneuvering and maybe a third to just get the meeting. Just have the force-doors roll "work as" the Uncover Clues roll.

                        Offhand I'd have handled that scene like this. The parenthetical stuff is analogies to similar plot structures.
                        1.) Action to secure meeting
                        (Chase scene to catch fleeing accomplice, larceny roll to break into someone's office)

                        2.) Go to meeting, make Uncover Clue roll using a Social Skill to model the interview/interrogation process. Even if the guy won't give you the information you're looking for, he might accidentally reveal something or compromise and point you in a useful direction. On a dramatic failure, he misleads you. This avoids "he won't talk at all", the least useful possibility, as a potential outcome.
                        (Interrogate the suspect in the interview room, search the desk for incriminating documents)

                            I agree with Five Eyes, I would consolidate the Forcing Doors attempt and the Uncover Clues attempt into a single roll. So it would suffer penalties both from the number of doors remaining and the number of times the character had used that dice pool to uncover clues in this investigation, and could benefit both from Hard Leverage and from spending Elements, as well as any Merits the character may have that interact with either subsystem. Then the possible results would be:

                            - Dramatic Failure: The Councilor resents the character's attempts to draw information out of him. He offers false information (a Tainted Clue) and is immune to further Social Maneuvering attempts from that character. The character may gain a negative Condition, like Guilty or Notoriety.


                            - Failure: The Councilor is resistant to the character's attempts to draw information out of him. They're still able to draw some insight from his terse responses, but not as much as they had hoped (an Incomplete Clue). He is immune to further Social Maneuvering attempts from that character.

                            - Success: The character manages to tease the information they're looking for out of the Councilor (a Clue). He will still be susceptible to further Social Maneuvering attempts by that character.

                            - Exceptional Success: The Councilor is especially responsive to the character's attempts. He spills everything he knows (a Clue with an extra Element) and is still susceptible to future Sociap Maneuvering attempts by that character. The character may gain a beneficial Condition like Informed or Connected.

                                                        Reading the whole rules once again I think you can Uncover the Truth with less than needed Clues - you just need to have <base Clues number> of Elements. See those quotes:

                                                        Originally posted by Chronicles of Darkness, p. 79
                                                        Once characters reach the required number of Clues, they’re able to Uncover the Truth if that’s their goal. They can do so without any additional rolls if they spend a number of Clue elements (divided however they like) equal to the
                                                        required Clues for the investigation. See below for more on Uncovering the Truth.
                                                        And this:

                                                        Originally posted by Chronicles of Darkness, p. 81
                                                        Once characters have the requisite Clues, they may Uncover the Truth. So long as they have the required number of Clues, this doesn’t require a roll; they have pieced together the puzzle. However, for every Clue short of the required
                                                        total, the Storyteller adds one significant complication to the investigation.
                                                        So in mine Yellow Sign Investigation example - if character has only 5 Clues and 5 Elements from them, they cannot Uncover the Truth. ( They could make Castle Theory Roll by mine own House Rule, but it still be on -8 penalty ). But if they have 5 Clues and 13 Elements - the same number of Elements that base Clues need to Solve the Mystery - they can Uncover the Truth with Complications, based on 8 Clues missing.

                                                        This is how I understand the system for now.
