    Core CofD has breaking points occur whenever someone encounters something (typically violence or supernatural phenomena) that outstrips her ability to handle it. Baselines who have little or no experience with the supernatural could be set off by simply seeing a Deviant use an Overt Variation (or merely meeting one with an Overt Scar). Granted, ones who haven't seen a Deviant grow violent might well still suffer a breaking point if the Remade turns that violence on them.

                The limiting factors for conspiracy actions are that they have to affect the cohort in some way, they have to be within the scope of their available Nodes, they can only be safely undertaken a number of times per chapter based on Standing (which is further cut into by Deviants attacking their Nodes and sabotage by other conspiracies), and that using them stops a conspiracy from building (as much) Effort for its projects without suffering the same disorganization as they would from exceeding their safe limit. A conspiracy in conflict with a cohort or other conspiracies doesn't need to make a purge at all if it can go a couple of chapters without taking damage, and doing that is both easier to manage by using conspiracy actions to stymie Deviant activity against their Nodes and less costly to the conspiracy's long-term assets both organizationally and in terms of retention.

                Pushing its limits means a conspiracy is courting disintegration, and Resistance only helps "more" with that in the same sense that Stamina keeps you from dying as rapidly in an armed conflict. The system is not made to model its mechanical elements existing in a vacuum for their own sake.

                                    I haven’t finished the chapter but I am of two minds on the subject.

                                    On the one hand, if there is an easily accessed exploit in the rules or some other kind of weirdness about them, it’s a good idea to bring it to the author’s attention. Whether it goes against the spirit of the rules or not, it’s worth bringing it to the attention of the people writing the book so they can decide if it is important to fix it or whatever.

                                    On the other hand, the conspiracy mechanics are generally pretty ST facing and, to my understanding, are more about helping STs develop cool storylines and to give some mechanical weight to fighting against the Man, than they are about optimizing numbers and creating a cool character sheet. Like, I get that urge. I understand that urge. However, when building an NPC organization as an ST, one should probably fight that urge, much like you should fight the urge to put inescapable death traps in your dungeons. Sure, you can just say “Rocks Fall, Everybody Dies,” but ...why would you do that to your friends?

                                    Similarly, you might be able to exploit the rules to build an unstoppable Conspiracy and turn your game into an unfun slog where the players never manage to win... but why would you?

                                    So.... It feels like the answer to any exploits in the Conspiracy rules might be to include a couple notes or reminders about what the point of the rules is? Or something?

                                    At the end of the day, any “build your own” game is going to have some exploits and hacks in it because you can’t make everything perfect and, even if you could, that doesn’t mean it would have the right atmosphere. Especially since anyone looking for a serious exploit is probably gonna find one eventually. Which turns it into an Out of Game issue and those are usually better handled by encouraging people to talk and try to get on the same page.

                                        From what I understand, the intent of the Conspiracy mechanics is ultimately for them to be available both to the Storyteller and to the players: in Deviant, we're eventually going to get a book dedicated to the Devoted, which will presumably feature Conspiracies and cohorts working hand in hand; and outside of Deviant, rumor has it that this system is also going to be seen in Mummy 2e to represent the Cults that the Arisen tend to run.

                                        So with that in mind, if there are any blatant exploits that could be abused by players running a conspiracy, that's definitely something to look at.

                                        All that said, the reason I said “blatant” is because I definitely prefer Onyx Path's approach of assuming good faith in the part of its players over other game systems that seem to assume that the players will try to break the rules and so they need to be made ironclad to withstand such abuse. In this regard, my thought is that if an exploit exists that can occur even if the players are acting in good faith, then patch it.

                                        But if it only occurs when they're blatantly meta-gaming the system, then only patch it if doing so is a simple fix and/or it's so tempting of an exploit that it can't be fixed through Storyteller intervention with the players (i.e., a figurative slap on the wrist and/or an admonishment).

I believe a dice pool of 7 is the point with the smallest chance of any result but normal success, and Standing 4 is just where an Attribute of 7 becomes practical.

                                            In a word, then, if the ST is regularly throwing more than 9 dice on conspiracy actions, he's being very much a jerk to the players. The normal way for conspiracies to develop new leads on Renegade cohorts is from the surveillance roll - which is based on Standing and the cohort's recent activities, not any Attributes.

                                            Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
                                            On the other hand, the conspiracy mechanics are generally pretty ST facing and, to my understanding, are more about helping STs develop cool storylines and to give some mechanical weight to fighting against the Man, than they are about optimizing numbers and creating a cool character sheet.
                                            Well, yes, that's true. But here's the thing. For the same amount of Effort that goes into adding one Attribute dot, a conspiracy can add one new Node and gain both a new important NPC and a new avenue of attack; or it can develop three new Icons, and give its agents in the field a significant power boost; or it can make a drastic change in society at large. All of those are much more interesting and cool than increasing an Attribute ... and increasing Attributes doesn't help a conspiracy do any of them in the future.

                                            So, from both the conspiracy's viewpoint and the ST's, what's the point of higher Attributes?                                                 As always, I'm watching this conversation closely, even if I'm not saying much.
