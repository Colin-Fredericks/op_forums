    GMC Virtue/Vice

    01-11-2014, 06:50 PM
    Sorry if this has been said before.

    My idea is that Prometheans could have a Nature and a Aspiration (trying to come up with another name for the latter).
    The first one is how he is while the second is a trait in humanity he admires and tries to emulate.
    The biggest difference is of course that both can be something positive (or awful) and that the nature (at least initially) probably is something simple. They'll both most likely change during play until his journey is at its end and he's happy with himself, at which point he faces his new dawn.

    The aspiration could be tied to the refinements as well, considering most of them emulates different parts of humanity. Studying Mercurius the character might strive to emulate a trait of his progenitor or as a Centimani trying to be the perfect monster.
    The nature could be childlike traits early on and when his humanity has increased (low starting humanity seems to be a popular idea here) or he's simply lived long enough it'll change into something more mature, could probably just use the Masks/Dirges from vampire.

    A lot more could be done with it but what do you think about the basic idea?

      It's certainly an idea that has been discussed. I don't know if there's a need for "maturity" in terms of Virtue or Vice, since you could argue Vices are simplistic and pretty low-minded. I'm working on a framework that makes Virtues and Vices a reward for certain advancements. Prometheans instead begin with a Remnant of their body/bodies' original inhabitants, and also the ability to gain Willpower by means of their Refinement. The latter is in a back-to-the-drawing-board, because I'm finding the Lineage to be a better indication of the Promethean's emotional responses. Refinements are a better source of Aspirations/Milestones.

      The context for this framework is that Prometheans possess only the barest sense of self at their "birth", and it takes some experience and suffering to draw strength from oneself.

                                          Minor nitpick: as I view it, the whole point of the Pilgrimage is that the Promethean doesn’t have a Self: it is an organic machine with behaviors that arise out of its Humour and the residual “programming” (e.g., muscle memory, language facility, etc.) of the body. That lack of Self is what humans instinctively sense when they meet a Promethean, and is the root cause of Disquiet. Gold and Lead aren’t “Self” and “what weighs them down”; they’re “what is my Pilgrimage’s destination?” and “what was my Pilgrimage’s origin?” For those who like the notion of Purpose-built Prometheans, Lead is their original Purpose; and Gold is the “Self” tat they’re striving to attain.

                                          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                      So, here are a couple of the discussions that have touched on the topic, since cobalus said he didn't find them.
                                                      • House-Ruled GMC Patches
                                                      • Stolen Lives
                                                      But Lead and Gold...

                                                      So, my hitch here is that they're tied to the Pilgrimage. Great! But. So is everything else about the Promethean, to the point where it can be a stubborn point of pride not to Pilgrimagize. If you take what is the basic game psychology of the Promethean, and make that also a part of the Great Work, then what of Man is there in the Promethean? "Oh, she's a Refuser, she lets her Lead drag her down." You need a better anchor to hang it on than alchemical motifs that are already used as Refinements. Lead & Gold does two disservices to the Promethean: first, it displaces Virtue and Vice, robbing them of possible human connections. Second, it reduces the extremes of their personality to facets of the Pilgrimage, not even allowing them the illusion of self-determination.

                                                      So, come at Lead and Gold from a different angle. It needs to be different enough from existing traits to justify its own existence, else it requires a re-imagining of other traits. As an intended replacement of Virtue and Vice, it needs to do the job thereof, while meeting the previous stipulation, and conveying certain themes and natures of the Promethean psyche. Finally, it needs to be a matter of choice for the character, a statement of who he wishes to be (or not be), with real rewards in order spur the player's investment in that character.

                                                      In my Stolen Lives setup, one of these was the Remnant, a shred of the Promethean's host body's original inhabitant. "Stolen Lives" is in the game's subtitle, but not a lot calls it out in the game book. That would be "Lead" in my take, because the Remnant is something to overcome in order to gain Mortality, but it offers such a comforting sense of identity and belonging that it's easy to fall into its trap and lose the plot of the Pilgrimage. "Gold" would be something else; Refinement, perhaps, since it represents the chosen "true" path to the New Dawn, and when the Promethean achieves a Refinement-specific Milestone, he also gains a sense of the reality of Mortality, and the correctness of his choices.

                                                      I'm not saying this to pimp my ideas or put down others', just trying to inject more confusion into the topic.

                                                          Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                                          Minor nitpick: as I view it, the whole point of the Pilgrimage is that the Promethean doesn’t have a Self: it is an organic machine with behaviors that arise out of its Humour and the residual “programming” (e.g., muscle memory, language facility, etc.) of the body.                                                           I thought about this for a few days, and I have to disagree.
                                                          I suppose it depends on what your definition of "self" is, but coming at it from a psychological/sociological viewpoint:

                                                          I disagree because of the existential difficulty of not having a "self." People who do not have "selves," as far as psychologists and sociologists are concerned, are those raised feral, by animals or by terrible vial parents. Both of these are recorded, and the poor children (I tend to think the later is the worse) in question still have rudimentary understanding of the world, but not of who they are.
                                                          Granted, you need a society to interact with to forge a self, but your basic starting Promethean character is going to be much more PC-able then the real-world examples of "people without a self."

                                                          The Promethean condition is a lot similar to Borderline Personality Disorder than this, which is basically what it says on the tin. I think that's a more accurate way to describe a Promethean's state-of-being. It also allows characters which are playable, since you can't relate to someone without a self.
                                                          Seriously, look it up with Torment, Refinements and Disquiet in mind. Very similar.
                                                          If you can't relate to your own character, that's a problem. I'd hate for a game to tell me "your character doesn't have a self!" That basically means they have nothing for me to cling to.
                                                          Sure, you can pitch that they're an organic machine, but I find the idea that Prometheans have all these important qualities that make up a person, they just have to uncover them within to become that person. They have to make choices and decide what is moral and what isn't and fall in and out of love and lead a life before they get to have a life: just like the person(s) they were made from.

                                                          The core book is pretty clear that Prometheans still feel emotions, want things besides food and water, get bored, and are curious. Those indicate a self, to me.

                                                          For what it's worth, I'm really not a fan of any of the suggested alternate Anchors (not that my opinion matters for your game, but still). They just seem, inaccurate.
                                                          None of them really seem to express what makes a Promethean's core being. Verge has a lot of good ideas, and I agree that over-placing emphasis on the Pilgrimage robs the Promethean of what little tie to humanity they already have: that's actually my biggest issue with alternate Anchors.

                                                          Look: Pandorans get Vices, and only Vices, while Prometheans get Virtues and Vice. The biggest difference - from an existential/philosophical viewpoint - between a Promethean and a Pandoran is the Created's Virtue and it's Azoth. Your bastard children hate you because they can never be like you, what with your proto-soul and your positive qualities.

                                                          If you need to change the names of the Promethean Anchors to Lead and Gold, ok, but how those are different from Vice and Virtue (respectively), I'm not sure. I think Refinement provides enough space that you could, as Verge has already done, write up some Willpower-resotration Conditions for them similar to Mask/Dirge. I think you could do some cool stuff with the idea of weight, still, since Weight is the Pilgrim's natural enemy: guys, you want to travel light, but the Promethean is carrying an awful lot of baggage.

                                                          I dunno if you need to change Virtue and Vice, though. I still haven't been convinced it's as necessary as changing Milestones and Humanity.

                                                          Tangentially, there's "Simulcra" as a Humanity alternate, and Verge has his own (which I will not reveal for him); this thread makes me want to make one called "Self" and see how that plays out, since it's much easier to frame the Pilgrimage here as "here are the little bits of your Self you start with, now go make more. When you're done you get a shot at the real thing."
                                                          I suppose that "Self" is what all the alternate Humanities have been about, though. Hrm.
