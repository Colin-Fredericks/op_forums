    The fact that humanity is the basis for so many things in the WoD makes perfect sense, being as they're some of the only sentient things on the planet, which is why the obligate focus on humanity doesn't really strain my suspension of disbelief in other lines.

    Promethean starts with something that is a *person* but isn't a *human*, which makes it nearly unique (until demon came along), and then posits that well, of course, the best thing they could do would be to turn into us.

    Which is a sentence that leaves me scratching my head. It's such a bizarre and arrogant assumption. It hits me the same as white people rolling up on a foreign shore and saying "these poor people, they haven't even got a fiat economy or organized religion, how willl they ever be happy unless they become like us?"

    And I mean, the standard argument is "well, that's part of the buy-in" which is why, the first time I brought this up, it was in the context of shrugging and rejecting the buy-in. This time, I'm just popping up to say there's nothing particularly transhuman about that, transhumanism is something else that involves humans transcending the limits of humanity. This would just be...things that were never human, deciding that it's possible to be a fully realized person without being human. And trying to find that sense of fulfillment and identity in a world that actively and violently rejects all other ways of being.

    I think that could be a good story, but it's not particularly transhuman, it's just also not one the line creators are interested in telling.

                                                        OK so to undermine my own post... I think I totally missed the point of several arguments that are being made. Also this post is almost stream of consciousness for me to try and work out some general thoughts of Promethean I have. So apologies beforehand for this possibly kinda being besides the point.

                                                        It also kinda took a while to write, so I missed a few peoples updates that I think kinda make this a little redundant...oh well.

                                                        Originally posted by Gabby View Post
                                                        Whether demonizing transhumanism, or a certain variety of transhumanism, is the right decision thematically is another issue. I just definitely don't think it's an issue of transhumanists looking around, going "that thing has positive stuff to say about the human condition! FUCK THAT!" Assuming their issue is with the former will probably lead to you misunderstanding them most of the time.
                                                        Though Arc has beat me to this point. I think it bears repeating:

                                                        Considering the base assumption of the horror genre and the WoD is that getting involved and progressing is actually a worse state of affairs then leaving well alone the statement that:

                                                        "X if engaged with (almost) always ends badly and will/has negative consequences for you."
                                                        If we take this as a simple genre truth that is applied as a metaphysical truth for 9/10 situations in the WoD then the later argument you suggest Gabby is actually pretty damn valid. Because its A FACT of reality/the setting that its pretty much the case. This being said their are examples (I've detailed some in a previous post) that show their are exceptions to this rule.

                                                        However, 9 times out of 10 its a bad idea. Its fun to tell a story about that 1 in 10 time, makes for an interesting game, but its the exception, not the rule. Its not something I think any WoD game should really support as a default assumption (as such an assumption would go against he default horror tone that all the games engage with to different degrees). Its fine for a sidebar somewhere in a core book and a reminder about the customization quality of the games, but it does not need to be a major emphasis and certainly not for Promethean (the most humanist of all the game lines).

                                                        I think their is an additional wrinkle to the argument however. Namely, that their is the element of a "quality of life" so to speak. This is why humanism (which is progressive itself) is kinda "safe" even in the WoD. Humans can have pretty awesome lives and do pretty awesome things. Yes we get sick, are physically fragile, die eventually and are subject to all the nasty human evils we inflict on one another. However, overall people get to live their lives to a reasonable level of free-will, contentment and progression. Its only when the idea of having "more" then you need for these basic "human" quality of life e.g. Immortality that the idea I posit above and that Arc talked about previously in regards to the horror genre a bit kicks in.

                                                        Prometheans have a terrible quality of life, easily the worst of any of the major splats of the NWoD (Changelings come close I guess) the point of their journey is to reach the point where they "belong" to the world in a native way. Hence why theirs no "punishment" for them trying to have "more" then there lot.

                                                        On to the point about defining your own identity you brought up Gabby in response to Satchel's point.

                                                        I think equating the Promethean condition and its drive towards a humanitarian (as opposed to a transhumanist/post-humanist/anti-humanist) end with something like a gender identity issue is not really a great comparison. I can see the parallels for sure (as sure that some of them are even purposefully engaged with), but I think equating the entirety of identity with gender is too narrow and simplistic a focus.

                                                        Prometheans are constructing an identity wholesale, they try and create a human viewpoint that will facilitate their transformation into real human beings. Part (likely a huge part) of that is deciding a gender for sure, but there is more to it then that. If anything I'd say Prometheans have "species dysmorphia". Not something we have in the real world obviously, but theoretically I see their problem as being the fish that wants to be, wish it was and feels a drive to be a bird, rather then a man feels he is truly a woman in the wrong body.

                                                        So leading on from this is of course the argument of:

                                                        "Why can't the fish want to be a (insert animal X) instead of a bird?"

                                                        Well, that's when we get more into the in-game stuff that Promethean has involved with itself.

                                                        The Divine Fire for reasons no one understand or knows considers humanity special. Prometheans being creature of the Divine Fire share this "metaphysical bias" as we might call it. Hence the innate drive towards the Pilgrimage and the New Dawn. Hence the Azothic Memories within them stoking them forward. I think of it like a sort of "migration instinct" you see in birds, its an innate, natural, instinctual and semi-conscious drive. Going back to the fact hat Prometheans form identity whole sale over their pilgrimage, this innate drive helps them and guides them towards that end.

                                                        Of course Prometheans are free-willed beings, so they can reject that instinct decide they would rather be "X instead of a bird", but that means actively going against their nature and denying the animating force they have in place of a soul. It would be the equivalent of a migrating bird staying in a climate that would result in its starvation.

                                                        Given all this, I don't really see a reason why a Promethean could sustain a transhumanist viewpoint for very long. I get that they could and likely would come across the idea at some point on the Pilgrimage. Some of the braver ones might even try an embrace it. However, they would notice pretty quickly surely that they have stalled on the Pilgrimage and that something likely feels "off" or "not right". I think some would turn back then, embrace what feels "right" or "natural" to them, if only because its a comfort to feel that you are progressing again. A few might hang on anyway, but stagnation is going to be the result, which leads to despair and Flux. Of course, the Hundred Handed are always waiting.

                                                        Which is sorta a visions cycle isn't it? If the die hard transhumanist Prometheans end up as Centimani then it acts as a sort of social warning for later Prometheans to not dabble in that particular philosophy. Though I am sure many might still study transhumanism to understand humans and the "flaws" they see in themselves that Prometheans are likely just trying to understand as "features" of humanity.

                                                        The second point to consider is consequence. I've already touched on possible consequences for a Promethean who "abandons" the New Dawn for a "better ending" but there is another point I'll bring up here. Deciding that you as a person are non-binary and don't fit into either gender but are unique is I imagine a difficult thing to work out and deal with. Society is largely gender binary in nature, so obviously deciding to not engage with the social construct of binary gender is going to have some social consequences (something you may be more experienced with then I give the tone of your previous post).

                                                        For Prometheans the consequences of trying to "not become human" are pretty much all consuming. Even taking the fall to the Hundred Handed off the table that I talked about earlier, there is the simple fact that being a Promethean kinda sucks. Disquiet, Wasteland, Firestorms and activating Pandorans are the bread and butter of you existence. As you progress on the Pilgrimage these things are toned down a bit and you learn transmutations that can help with some of these aspects. Though its important to note that no matter how much supernatural power you emass you will never complete negate them.

                                                        Prometheans who decide to "reach for their own ending away from humanity" or "try to inform an identity removed from humanity" (basically attempt not to be human but not be Promethean either) are going to continuously suffer from the consequences I mentioned above. They can cultivate the supernatural powers to take the edge of some of these, but its never going to mitigate them completely. Never really make it bearable. Never going to make them feel like the world does not hate them and reject them (and I do mean THE WORLD on a metaphysical level here, not just human social rejection).

                                                        I can't imagine what's that like really, to know deep in yourself that metaphysically you "don't belong" (its ultimate rejection, beyond anything humans can actually experience) must do terrible things to a persons self. Hang in there all you want with your "own definition", it never going to get better for you then what you have at that moment. Is it any wonder then that a Promethean would "conform" or "compromise" to being "merely human" if even that "limited state" promised relief and belonging from such feelings? I don't find it hard to imagine almost every person exposed to that capitulating to that drive in the face of such terrible suffering.

                                                        As a final point:

                                                        Yes I know this does not really address your issue with the fact that the game has a metaphysics that leads to all the details and ends I talked about above. Personally, I feel all of it is necessary to really sell the uniqueness of the game that is Promethean. I can see how it could be seen as limiting by some people. I can't say I could see it as hurtful or dangerous in its intent or message (then again I'm a white, upper middle class, heterosexual male so I'm about as "target audience" as you can get for the majority of societal norms anyway) However, I'll strongly stress that the games message is one to be taken within the context of the game and the NWoD as a whole and not necessarily a philosophical statement on how people should view humanity in the real world (not saying you can't differentiate between the two, just as a general statement).

                                                        Anyway, I've rambled and this is post is very convoluted and I'm not sure it got the points across I wanted it too, but at least I tried.

                                                            Originally posted by Gabby View Post

                                                            I've implied no analogy. "Becoming this one specific thing instead of something else" presumes there is something else that one can reasonably become that possesses the qualities one is attempting to gain and retain — an assumption that doesn't bear out when the best fits for that either directly draw from that one specific thing or are the result of something becoming more like that one specific thing.

                                                            The examples we have on the books of Prometheans becoming other things include, in the form of rumors:

                                                            • Spirits - for which the keys to the process are specifically mentioned as a cautionary tale about the Ulgans.

                                                            Originally posted by Strange Alchemies p104, When did the Promethean first learn about the Pilgrimage?
                                                            Some Prometheans hear about the Great Work from the moment they are able to understand the concept, or even before. Others hear about it only as they are about to undertake their independence from their creators. Still others only hear their creator talk of it occasionally, or to other people, and they are never given any context for what it means. Some hear absolutely nothing about the Pilgrimage from their creators, and have to discover its existence from other Prometheans. The circumstances under which the Promethean hears of it, and from whom he hears it, can set the tone for his Pilgrimage. Those who hear of it from a cruel creator may walk it with hesitation, while others may consider the Pilgrimage the thing that took their beloved creators from them.

                                                            Other Prometheans simply find themselves on the Pilgrimage. For some, they’ve somehow always known that their goal was to be come human; it simply took someone else putting it into words for them. Others seek to be near humans, to emulate them, but do not understand why they do these things. The Pilgrimage is an innate part of the Promethean existence — the Divine Fire is transformative, and rare is the Promethean who is simply content as he is. Prometheans are all driven by the desire for change, to become something else, and most of them find their role models in humanity.
