        The Weimar Republic (Interregnum Germany) - Social liberties, crushing debt, and the rise of Fascism. (Werewolf/Hunter)

        World War I just happened - due to some bad choices made by Kaiser Wilhelm II, it's officially Germany's fault. And the rest of Europe won't let them forget it.

        The traditional leadership is gone, France is occupying the industrial centres along the Rhine, and the population is facing crippling debt and inflation due to the rest of Europe demanding reparations. Germany's national pride is gone, but...

        There's no censorship. Prostitution for both sexes is legal, homosexuality isn't criminalized, and women had the vote.Germany took a massive step forward for the arts and film. But...

        No one wanted a democracy - it had a built-in "go back to autocracy" clause in the constitution. The government was divided and weak; there were paramilitary gangs of young men wandering the countryside, since the military didn't have a place for them. Near the end, you had Communists and National Socialists getting into bloody skirmishes in the streets.

        It was a disaster waiting to happen - and we all know how it ends.

        But they didn't.

            If I ever get a few days off work, I'd love to actually develop my 1960s America Mage setting, All Along The Watchtowers. It plays off similar themes as Mage Noir, with the idea that America as a nation has Awakened - and possibly broken in the face of the truth. Vietnam is a war with incredibly low public support, a source of dead brothers and horrific images on the television rather than the patriotic glories of the past. Social conservatism and mainstream religion ring utterly false to the young and the famous, who increasingly toy with free love, hard drugs, and fringe religions (that in the Chronicles of Darkness world may well be genuinely supernatural). Race relations and generational gaps threaten to boil over into blood in the streets. Sleepers breach the heavens and walk on the face of the moon, something well beyond even the greatest magic of Masters.

            The reality of the Fallen World rings increasingly hollow, but it seems like the Sleepers would sooner spiral into madness than find the Supernal lurking underneath. It's Cold War paranoia and Hollywood cult murders and acid trips bringing fragile minds into contact with alien intelligences. It's magic being increasingly invalidated by technology and new symbols overtaking the old faster than anyone can comprehend.

            It may well be the apocalypse.

            Remi. she/her. game designer.

                Anyone have any ideas for non-Egyptian pre-Colonial African Eras? I like the idea of the Mansa Musa Pilgrimage as a focal point, but I'm not sure what to do with it.
                The Fulani Jihad, and rise of the Sokoto Caliphate, works for several lines. In the early 19th Century, members of the Fulani ethnic group conquered a series of Hausa city-states in (what's now) Nigeria. History remembers the Jihad as springing from a mix of ethnic tension, religious* intolerance, and a desire for social reform; sparked by the exile of the religious scholar Uthman dan Fodio. But who says history is right?

                The Fulani Jihad could easily have been cover for a feud between vampires. Or mages. Perhaps the Hausa city-states were strongholds of the Diamond, and the jihadis pawns of the Exarchs (possibly Paternoster). Or maybe it was the other way 'round, with abusive Seers in charge, and the Jihad was a revolt by African chapters of the Adamantite Arrow and various Nameless groups (one of the frequent charges was that the Hausa leaders had lost touch with their spiritual beliefs . . . )

                Not hard to see werewolves, or Beasts, or hunters getting in on the action, either. Or maybe the truth was that the Unchained cut one too many deals with the Hausa rulers, and caught the eye of the God-Machine . .




                * Despite technically being Muslim, Hausa rulers allowed (or at least turned a blind eye towards) the practice of traditional African religions, which outraged the more orthodox revolutionaries .

                                Hunter: Manifest Destiny

                                Hunters in the Post-Bellum United States. Taking on Victorian Vampires and geniuses in the choked and polluted cities, werewolves and fae in the expansive plains, and all the human magics in between.

                                With a little more detail, the 1860s-1890s was when the US really went into overdrive on taking the plains and united the coasts. With the completion of the Transcontinental Railroad and the advancements in ship building technology the United States was taking the continent by storm. Hunters would inevitably spring up when facing the werewolves trying to destroy the railroads and mines springing up, the strange and secretive esoteric societies rising among the middle and upper classes in the cities, mad scientists trying to force their inventions of the unwilling and unknowing public, along with any fearsome critters found during the expansion. Firearms technology was advanced to the point of repeating fire with self-contained cartridges, and the hunter concepts stretch from Native Americans trying to protect their ways of life, to immigrants carrying with them the secrets of their homelands, to American-born citizens finding out about what lurks in their cities and settlements. The game can take place in the classic "Old West" boom town or ghost town, across the Great Plains, the bustling coastal cities, the lumber camps of the Northwest, isolated reservations and Federal forts, and the few remaining tribal settlements left. Perhaps even a mission to Alaska and Hawai'i?

                                    Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                                    Anyone have any ideas for non-Egyptian pre-Colonial African Eras? I like the idea of the Mansa Musa Pilgrimage as a focal point, but I'm not sure what to do with it.
                                    Some more options, besides the Fulani Jihad:

                                    The history (and mythological baggage) of Ethiopia lends itself to a Mage & Changeling setting. Medieval Europeans sort of knew it was there, telling stories of the kingdom of Prester John, a Christian civilization lost somewhere in Africa. Its just that sometimes they seem to be talking about a real place, and sometimes a fantasy land. Home to one of the oldest forms of Christianity in the world (Ethiopia's overwhelmingly Coptic Christian), and yet cut off from the rest of Christendom, it could well be a magnet for medieval mages looking for "lost" mysteries. And some might find more than they expected, stumbling upon the faerie kingdom of the "real" Prester John.

                                    For more recent events, you could look at the invasion of the Galla, or Ethiopia's defeat of Italy. That's right, during the colonial era, Ethiopia actually defeated the European power seeking to colonize it.

                                    Or, if you want more of a blank slate, there's Meroe (pronounced "mare-oh-way"). Located in what is now northern and South Sudan, it started out as an ancient Egyptian colony known as "Kush", and later gained de facto independence during internal strife in ancient Egypt. However, almost overnight, for no obvious reason, it turns into the kingdom of Meroe. The language shifts dramatically, to the point that modern scholars are still struggling to translate it, and lion imagery suddenly becomes very prominent. Meroe survived into the Fourth Century AD . . . when it just sort of disappeared.

                                    Obviously, that gives you a lot of flexibility. A civilization that radically changes, for no good reason, flourishes for a thousand years, and then just . . . stops. Again, for no obvious reason. There are a thousand cool things you could do with any of the splats, regarding the rise and fall of Meroe.
