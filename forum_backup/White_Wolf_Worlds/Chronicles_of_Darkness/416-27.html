    Since Vampires got a few thematic fighting styles, I sure hope werewolves will too.
    One to emphasise the wolf / near wolf like tooth and claw and one that makes use of having class and increased strength in warform (or near human form too) would be good.

    But I do hope that this will include making the warform a bit less disadvantageous, I did like the unlimited war-form from apocalypse and the Sumer chapter in the chroniclers guide.


    I also really would like to see other shapeshifters worked in in some form, and if it is only to give them more of a space in the werewolf canon.
    Stories all over the world have shapeshifters, many different kinds. I never really liked that Forsaken did away with that to have a clearer focus on werewolves opposed to other shapeshifters.

    If I could wish for any kind of revision, I would love to see the game making "origin" one of the five type axises.
    It might be difficult to work, it would be a completely different game where werewolves of quite different types band together out of necessity, but it would be fun to really explore the different stories for how werecreatures came to be, as victims of a curse, born from a uniuon between god / spirit and man, by means of magic (akin to skinchangers, including pacts with supernatural beings), as an animal that turns into a human form or as werewolf deals with it, as an inherited trait.

    ...this is not really feasible for Forsaken, but it still would be fun to see the variety incorporated in some fashion.

    Nevertheless, it would be nice to at least have the mythologically prominent werecreatures appearing in the areas where legends about them are found in some fashion that makes them playable (meaning, not a clear cut enemy from the start).

                Originally posted by Gurkhal View Post
                How is the present War-form disadvantageous? My players can usually kill almost anything they like and scare off all humans nearby in the rounds of war-form that they get.
                I can only really comment on that from the perspective of a NWOD rules apocalypse game (which does lack the main drawbacks of Gauru form), but even there, I did end up choosing Hispo / Urshul form quite a number of times for the benefit of speed alone.
                In essence, Gauru has +1 ST +1 size and health and 1/1 armor over Urshul, which gets better DX, initiative and speed.

                For pure combat, that of course gives Gauru the edge, but the I think the disadvantages of having to roll or enter death rage to do anything but attack, attack, attack! and the limits on what kind of merits / tools you can use is rather significant.

                Also, do not forget, you can run around in the other forms with only possible witness holding you back, but if you know you might be walking into an ambush or similar, you cannot simply shift to Gauru and be ready.
                Additionally, if you run out of time, you have to spend a turn or a point of essence to shift to the next best form.
                Thus, in many fights, that is two points of essence / two turns lost for picking Gauru.

                It of course depends on your style, but I at least thought it more advantageous to build your comat capabilities around Urshul as a Forsaken.


                @Darksider: I think I should first state, that I agree with you on two issues:
                1. Changing Breeds was bad, I tried to like it, I wanted to like it and I really could not. Aside from the idea to have a sort of "built your own werecritter" toolbox approach, loads of hunky guys showing of their bodies and a smidgeon of the idea that werecreatures could also be unbalanced in the direction of losing the human side, their was pretty much nothing good about it...
                2. Fera suffered from special snowflake syndrome, the fact that "rarity" based balance does not work in rpg groups and the fact that they annnoyed the people who did want to play werewolf and not second string to someone with cooler powers...


                I still think though that it takes away from the fun not to deal with non-lupine shapeshifter legends.
                If not as somethting directly appearing in the core, there should be, well, room for them, it should be easy to implement them as something that could be playable (maybe with its own chronicle book) and which fits in the werewolf universe.
                As I much hope that the idigam chronicle will be open and easy to modify into what you want to play, it should be optional to include them, but I rather would have them in the core than the idea that werewolves ae everywhere, even in places where wolf shifters (or wolves themselves) are not to be found.

                I rather would bend a werewolf game's premise to real world myths and legends than try to press real world legends into a narrow, made up comsology.
                Then again I am generally not too much of a fan of the "this is the real truth about (insert name of supernatural entity or other here), all legends just got it wrong, those stupid humans".

                I always found that to some extend to be an excuse not to do research or trying to force one's ideas down other people's throat.

                                    Originally posted by Darksider View Post
                                    Like I said, I have no problem with Fera being part of (or the main focus of) a later supplement that you can take or leave, but if it does get made I want it to be mechanically sound and it has to avoid the "special snowflake" approach. Changing Breeds failed on both accounts. Sorry, tigers are just not physically stronger than elephants and shouldn't confer better physical stat bonuses.
                                    Honestly.... The problem wasn't even just Changing Breeds, though that book had issues. Its that it was one of four books that ran into issues with bringing alternative were critters into werewolf mainstream.

                                    At first, we had the Hosts - Rat and Spider, mainly. While not necessarily shifters like the werewolves, they're still kinda part human, part spirit-animal, so I'm counting them here. They got a blurb in Predators, then just kinda... fell flat as a thing. Some one shot entries in Night Horrors, but still not much detail or page count.

                                    Then, we had Skin Changers. Skin Thief half-template that could have been a werewolf half-template like ghoul or proximi, but kinda fell to the side; a shame, since a wolf blood using a fetish as the agent could pull it off without being so cheesy. And there are also other kinds of half-human, half-animal critters. Kitsune, a snake lady, and more.

                                    War Against the Pure had ANOTHER chapter on animal stuff. Better mechanics, but still all over the place

                                    And finally, there was Changing Breeds. Not only was this a mechanical nightmare, not only with existing ones but the creation rules, but also a thematic disaster with the whole return of the eco-terrorist vibe, and a complete lack of ability to really be integrated into the Werewolf cosmology, a key reason for wanting the book for many.


                                    Really, if you're going to have alternative shifters.... and, yes, its clearly an issue since we had no fewer than FOUR distinct books on the matter, then you need to make an effort to have some kind of entry into the main core. Want the focus to be on the wolves still? Okay, that's fine. I don't mind. Make them all antagonistic, fine. But there needs to be a set of rules and considerations for alternative were critters from the word go. About one quarter of the books deal with the issue in some manner. There's a clear need for it to be addressed.

                                                You know, one thing that occurs to me is that, at least with the Apocalypse, you could just heavily rewrite the initial War of Rage to make sense of things within the continuity. Each 'Were' was supposed to fill a slot in the needs of Gaia, a role to fulfill. It made sense that the wolves weren't as good as the others in the role, although they took it as best they could, and if you just dd some revisions, bam, you now have the Fera filling in the roles of tribes, so long as you carved out a spot for the wolves still. That may create problems as far as people wanting to play [insert tribe here] goes, but you can at least play two versions of apocalypse and have it sort of work.

                                                In Forsaken, it's harder to justify the entire "Other 'Weres' do the same thing you do, but better" because the culture and history of the Uratha is singularly tied into itself(Which, no, is not the same as saying Father Wolf is definitive truth, in case anyone is thinking that). Much of the Forsaken story goes that way. So why do the other 'Weres' exist, and how do you balance essentially probably ten other major templates that share characteristics with your main thing without stepping all over it? Forsaken doesn't offer an easy answer(never read War Against the Pure, so who knows, I might be missing something) because of how diverse it is to begin with.

                                                I've always been of the opinion that you can either make a Wereanimals game or a Werewolf game, but you can't really have both unless you're okay with some things looking a lot like the others, which most people aren't.

                                                            Including Fera in the core book will exponentially increase the probability of me not buying it, as I am obviously in the camp of "a game about werewolves should be about playing werewolves." I want all 8 Tribes plus Ghost Wolves, along with a proper setting update due to the reappearance of the Idigam and solid rules reflecting that. I'd like to see the Hisil given a much more detailed write up, with things like "places that aren't" and the Firstborn's den realms given word count. I'd also like a separate book covering new rules for Maeljin (and the Bale Hounds) since they were previously heavily tied to the 7 Deadly Sins and the GMC revisions have moved away from that particular conceit. I like the "werewolves are cursed by the Devil/demons" trope, which the Bale Hounds certainly fill, but it really doesn't belong in the core rules. As an addendum I'd also like to see the wolf-blooded beefed up in power and status similar to ghouls in Requiem. They deserve at least minor template status (biological family also shouldn't be considered part of the Herd) and it never made sense to me that they were susceptible to Lunacy but ghouls weren't.

                                                            Fera just don't bring anything worthwhile to the base setting and its themes IMO, the argument that there are other shapeshifter myths doesn't hold water for me because there are plenty of variations of the vampire myth, yet Requiem/B&S doesn't go out of its way to include those as playable sub splats, I'd like Werewolf to share that degree of focus, and I sure as shit don't want to see Werewolf turned into Exalted. The core book will have a limited word count and I want that focused on the stars of the game line, not wasted on bit players that will never see use in one of my games. It's fine if you want something different, it just means we don't want the same thing out of a game based on werewolves. I can at least appreciate that Changing Breeds tried to give Fera their own theme to play with, independent of Forsaken's.
