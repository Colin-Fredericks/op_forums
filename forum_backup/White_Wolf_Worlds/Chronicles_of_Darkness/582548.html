    Micro Dark Era - Counter Cultural USA

    07-07-2015, 02:37 PM
    The countercultural revolution was a turbulent time for the vigil. A young generation eager to escape the stifling culture of their parents unearthed monsters and occult lore as they explored new scientific, philosophical and spiritual ideas. To historians of the Vigil, such that they are, the countercultural revolution is often seen as a continuation or final resurgence of The War. Once again powerful Conspiracies were shaken and new groups formed to fill the vacuum.

    If the stormy sixties were a continuation of The War than they were a continuation in more than spirit. The Shadow of World War II still loomed large over the Vigil. Without the destructions of many established Conspiracies during the War (and European Colonisation centuries before) the events of the decade simply could not have taken place. Ideas and discoveries that gave birth to Cells and Compacts in the 1960s would have been prevented or appropriated a few decades earlier by Conspiracies eager to protect their monopoly or destroy whatever foul monster a well meaning student might blunder into.

    Other historians say that the countercultural revolution was not a continuation. It was the pendulum swinging back towards the centre as a young generation explored new philosophical and spiritual ideas; bringing a sense of mysticism and a spirit of revolution to counterbalance the predominantly technological and government backed organisations born in the fires of World War II. Many of the groups were overtly political, with strong ties to civilian ideologies.

    Whether it was an echo or the returning pendulum; the stormy sixties were but a puff of air before the howling fury of The War, the alliances forged were mere Compacts compared to the grand Conspiracies backed by the might of empires that arose in previous decades. Indeed, by the decade's end most of them had been consumed by larger groups eager to plunder their knowledge, censor unAmerican activities, or assert their monopolies on certain areas of occult lore.


    Peace and Love For Sale

    The hippy movement preached peace, free love and returning to nature. In the world of Darkness any Shaman, Werewolf, or Vampire crone could tell you one of those three things was not like the others.

    Which is not to say the hippies were doomed by their ignorance. Some retreated back to the cities and never again attempted to reconnect with nature after a Werewolf pack firmly told them to get off their territory. Other hippies ended up recruited into werewolf packs or a shaman's following, which is not always a bad fate, or a Vampire's blood-and-nature cult, is pretty much always a bad thing.

    But not every hippy sold out to monsters or capitalism as soon as they realised their ideology was wrong about Mother Earth. Many returned safely from the wilderness, disillusioned about nature but still confident in the moral principles of the hippy movement. As they had accepted the existence of the supernatural they were Hunters, even if they did not actually Hunt anything. These Hunters lived among their fellow hippies, and when someone had an encounter with the supernatural they were there with a supportive ear.

    It was the presence of Hunters that allowed the hippy movement to develop a supernatural base of it's own. Hardcore psychonaughts were starting to uncover some very effective concoctions, as they peered into the Shadow on an acid trip or unlocked latent psychic abilities though mushrooms Hunters in the movement were there to gently suggest they accept what they saw no matter how easy it would be to write it off as a bad trip. As their products got more reliable (though not safer) a radical splinter of hippy hunters began to form. If nature was red in tooth and claw they would tame the spiritscape and create the green land of peace and free love they wanted.

    Towards the mid sixties dozens of communes were formed. Stocked heavily with spirit seeing drugs these communes began to create spiritual landscapes of peace and cooperation through, well just trial and error. And there was a lot of error. In some commune the presence of so many people trying for spiritual awareness weakened the Gauntlet allowing possessions and manifestations, in others attempts to nurture the right sort of spirits led to a feedback loop that overran the local spiritual ecosystem and the minds of any humans nearby. More than once hippies went back to the Werewolves or Shaman who'd disgusted them years ago to beg for help. Others were destroyed by enemies, usually Werewolves, who objected to mortals tampering with the spirit world.

    But most of the communes lasted for a while. A few even succeeded at their aims and the rest were simply yet another commune with an ineffectual occult communal works project. They survived until they met an enemy more powerful than any they had faced before. The Federal Drug Administration.

    Some say it was an operation by Task Force: VALKYRIE that was infiltrated by The Cheiron Group. Others say Cheiron was running the show and VALKYRIE were infiltrators. In the back channels of Network Zero there are claims that it was actually Last Dysterty International and rumours that the Russians or international drug runners were involved. Outside the upper echelons of the Conspiracies all that can be known for sure is that somebody pushed the FDA into a massive series of raids. Entire communes were arrested and drug labs burned; then during a media blackout the chemists, the most experienced psychonaughts, the psychics, in short anyone with valuable knowledge, just vanished into thin air.

    The last surviving communes, even a couple of successful spirit scapes, survived because they exclusively imported their drugs from other communes and because they got damn lucky. But cut off from their suppliers their days were numbered. Some continued their work blindly, attempting to manipulate a spirit ecology they could no longer see or measure. The rest advertised for a shaman to take over and lead the project in the hope that their past successes might attract someone who agreed with their ideals. Most simply gave up, and the dream of a home grown occult hippy movement creating a loving natural world died with them.


    The Feminine Mystics

    Feminism had less of an effect on the Vigil than one might expect. All Hunters ignore social conventions, starting with the one about pretending the supernatural doesn't exist, and many take it futher. If a millennia old Conspiracy such as the Ageis Kai Doru takes a contrarian position on gender essentialism the tutting neighbours are unlikely to notice and would be unable to do much if they did.

    The actual connection between the feminist movement and the Vigil is a little more indirect, forming through related areas like archeology, literature criticism, and neo-paganism. Attempts to create a new mythology that understands and exults the feminine perspective. Most of these projects had no interest in genuine occult lore, and those that did showed the typical accuracy of a rookie hunter with no institutional knowledge to draw upon, which is to say they were near universally wrong on all accounts.

    But it is a willingness to explore beyond social conventions that leads one to the supernatural, accuracy has nothing to do with it. During the decade there were several notable encounters between feminists and the supernatural that forged a solid, if short lived, connection between feminism and the Vigil.

    The largest single point of contact came through Wiccia, and to a lesser extent other neo-pagan faiths. By the 1960s Wiccians had undertaken fifteen years of enthusiastic occult investigations during the turbulent, and thus fruitful, war and postwar periods. In addition to their own modest discoveries many covens had attracted patronage from a sometimes benevolent witch or ghostwalker, or a rarely benevolent vampire curious to see if there was any substance to wiccian “witchcraft”. By the time the first public Dianic Wiccian coven was officially formed in 1971 neo-pagan influenced Cells dedicated to fighting or learning from the supernatural had been growing within the feminist movement for years.

    When their exposure from the supernatural didn't come from neo-paganism it came from the abundance of low hanging fruit during the countercultural period. Just a few decades earlier several Conspiracies were destroyed, their lore and occult tomes were scattered, and often left where a student critiquing classical mythology for her thesis could stumble upon it. Archeological sites that were once quietly guarded now offered their secrets freely. For feminist students, indeed for any students, it was either the best or worst time to be interested in myths.

    [picture: An archeologist holds something that looks like Venus of Willendorf, she screams in pain from contact with the supernatural object. If you play Darkest Dungeon, the Vestel's affliction animation would be a good model]

    Almost from the beginning the burgeoning feminist hunter community had a strained relationship with the regular feminist community. It is far harder to reinterpret a text on the worship of an ancient goddess through a feminist lens when your patron is a witch who'll tell you if your interpretation makes theurgy more or less effective (neo-pagans, incidentally, dealt with similar problems a decade ago by mostly deciding they didn't mind what regular society thought).

    This ideological turmoil would simmer through the Cells and occasional Compacts for the duration of the countercultural revolution, many feminist hunters found themselves returning to academia or activism after a short Vigil. Others found that building their Vigil atop a shaky ideological foundation made them easy recruits for established hunter societies, or worse. By the end of the countercultural period, when Conspiracies began to absorb or squash the newer societies, there was scarcely anyone left to resist them.

    Sidebar: Survivors

    Not all the feminist organisations born during the countercultural period were destroyed, assimilated or faded away. Among the successful survivors are the Maiden's Blood Sisterhood and the Athenic Church.

    The Sisterhood, who might be more accurately described as a compact full of feminists rather than a feminist compact, adopted a fairly typical goal of protecting humans from monsters. Their straightforward mission shielded them from ideological turmoil while their tendency to recruit from respectable upper class families and for their alumni to take positions at more powerful Conspiracies gave them protection from the backlashes at the end of the countercultural period.

    As for the Athenic Church, they share some responsibility for why so few feminist Compacts survived beyond the countercultural period. Their flashy and impressive blessings enabled the Church to poach members, and mount the occasional crusade, against feminist and neo-pagan organisations. Even hunters who weren't directly affected often found the Church's actions disillusioning. The publication of When God Was a Woman in 1976 is generally understood as the moment when feminist hunters abandoned all attempts of reconciling their personal experiences with mainstream feminism theory.


    The Man is Sucking Me Dry

    For almost as long as the student protesters imagined some old white guy in a suit as The Man countercultural Hunters of all stripes and backgrounds were picturing The Man as a very white guy in an opera cape. To understand how Vampires became the prime evil for a generation of Hunters you must go back in time.

    Two decades before the summer of love Task Force: VALKYRIE had a problem, that problem was communism, and also Witches. The Task Force's increasing competence at getting information about Witches had unearthed a recent and growing vein of socialism. To say the Men in Black were terrified would be putting it mildly but between their regular monster hunting duties and Ivan VALKYRIE just didn't have the resources to open a third front, and so they made a Faustian bargain that still haunts them them to this day. They funded vampires to fight a proxy war against the witches.

    But to get the full story you must go back even earlier, to America's shameful history of slavery. Vampires are timeless creatures, and rather predictable in their way. They need blood, and slaves were an almost limitless supply. In turn the Cainite Heresy, who only see race as “human, vampire and everyone else”, did as they always had done and recruited the vampire's victims, slaves and abolitionists alike.

    In the 1960s these two stories combined in a perfect storm that would define a generation of the American Vigil.

    By the time the countercultural era came around centuries of predation had created an enormous concentration of Canites among African Americans; a young, angry, heavily armed, black man in the 60s could be explain himself in one of two ways: Civil rights activist or vampire hunter. Unsurprisingly the Canites chose the former and quickly became vocal and well placed members of the civil rights movement; despite their unworkable vampire-centric theory of race relations.

    The Canites, or Hunters in general, embedding themselves in revolutionary movements is nothing new. The experienced Vampire knows when to fade away and let mortals figure it out, and that's just what would have happened if the experienced vampires hadn't been usurped two decades earlier by younger monsters with equipment, funding, and most importantly, cover ups courtesy of Uncle Sam. What's more, many of the new Vampire elite were slave owners in life and took the civil rights movement rather personally.

    And so the Canites dragged counterculture hunters into their conflict with vampires, the vampires brought in the Men in Black, and for a generation of hunters The Man had Fangs.

    Rite of Denial: Antiquate

    The Canite writes a list of the Vampire’s crimes, real or imagined, on a sheet of paper using blood for ink. Then, tucking the paper under his tongue he insults the Vampire. If the Vampire hears the insult, including through technological or supernatural means, the Rite takes effect.
    Cost: 1 Willpower, 3 thimbles.
    Action: Instant
    Roll Results
    Dramatic Failure: The Canite fills their mind with memories of Canites from antiquity, they cannot hold more than flashes and suffer a Breaking Point.
    Failure: Nothing happens
    Success: The Vampire’s mind is filled with the fog of ages. They forget everything they have learned to adapt since their mortal life. Any attempt to use Allies, Contacts, Mentor, Retainer, Staff, Status, or similar merits which represent ties to modern thinking people have their Successes reduced by the Canite’s dots in Rites of Denial. Negative successes automatically become a Dramatic Failure. If using the Merit does not require a roll, and does require interacting with other people, the Storyteller should come up with a roll.
    Exceptional Success: As above, but the Canite’s dots in Rites of Denial are treated as one dot higher.

                          I have no particular plans to write up any Compacts from this era at the moment. I've got lots of, I wouldn't call them concepts or ideas, but I know how the groups would form. But if I wrote them up I can't think of anything they'd say that isn't mostly in the first post. Where's Glamourweaver when you need him?


                          The Hippies would have two main compacts: The spirit scapers and the psychonaughts; the two groups would be strong allies with some dual memberships and shared communes. The Psychonaughts would mostly be attempting to explore the Astral, the Shadow, and have less reliable ways to perceive some other realities like ghosts in Twilight. The spirit scapers would mostly stick to basic proven techniques to see the Shadow. Ignoring the riskier experimental drugs that let you explore the Shadow or do anything with other realms in favour of a somewhat safe Two World Eyes.

                          Also note that the Illuminated Brotherhood was formed from an attempt to recreate the Marsh Chapel experiment, which took place in 1962. Its almost certain that the psychonaughts were aware of this experiment. It's possible that whoever's responsible for the Illuminated Brotherhood might have been trying to retrace the steps of the psychonaughts and rediscover their techniques. Marsh Chapel would be one of the clues not locked away in some Conspiracy's archive.


                          Feminist Hunters would typically advance from Cell to Compact when they discover something that can provide an actual ideology for a Compact. The Hippy goal of forsaking the modern world for a simpler and more spiritual life has a built in place for Hunters who get genuine Spirtuality. The feminist goal of changing society doesn't really have much room for occult specalists since the occult is more dangerous and unless you're really really good or a major template, less effective than activism.

                          However the odds of finding something that you can build a Compacts ideology around; and that something being comparable with your own ideology is pretty low. Just look at the examples - the Sisterhood believe in defending mortals from supernatural predators - mostly tangential to feminism. The Athenic Church call themselves feminists, but read some classical mythology about Athena. There'd be one or two genuinely feminist Compacts, but between Hunters being sucked off into non-feminist Compacts on one side and Hunters giving up the Vigil to go back to regular feminism on the other side; there wouldn't be many.


                          Finally the Civil Rights movement. Hippy and Feminism is new territory full of unaligned hunters looking to climb the Tiers. Defending African Americans is old territory and solidly Cainite territory. Any time a Cell of hunters in the Civil Rights movement reaches the point where it starts recruiting and attempts grow into a Compact; the Cainites will hear. They'll turn up with the recruitment pitch and the promise of blood magic. Either the new guys end up Cainites. Or they've said no to a Conspiracy of fanatics who're starting to wonder what motives turned them away from the light. Are they perhaps being manipulated by vampires? And of course, you have Klansmen, police and other mortal threats against African American groups arming up.

                          So IMO it's either Cell or Cainite in the civil rights movement. Not one Civil Rights Compact managed to form. You might get other Compacts or Conspiracies recruiting from the African American population but unlike the Canites they aren't actually part of the Civil Rights Movement.

                                              The Easlen Humans (who are renamed in 1979 to be the One Earth Battalion) are a compact of hunters that sprung out of the combined mindset of martial arts practitioners, Human Potential, and Non-violent retaliation. The principles of non-violence were understood to add no violence to any situation but to manipulate the violence that others bring away from the innocent and on to the creators of the violence, as violence always begets violence and harms the initiator (usually after it harms many others) The Easlen's Understood the best recourse was to cut out the middle-men and any other victims than the perpetrator of the violence from the way of harm. they generally follow the Ethos of the "Warrior Monk", Testing the limits of their potential with a mix of meditation, sparring, and heavy psychedelic use to push the boundaries of their consciousness. As part of the ethos of Human Potential, The Easlen's will work to undo or mitigate supernatural influence on humanity, while treating supernatural monsters as the humans and consciousnesses that they understand them to be.
