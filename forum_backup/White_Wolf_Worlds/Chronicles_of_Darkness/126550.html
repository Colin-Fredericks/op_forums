    Help Me Build a Character!

    05-06-2014, 04:55 AM
    I'm about to join my first ever game of Changeling, but I'm having some trouble working out a few kinks with my character. He's the Sun's Tongue (Summer Court Diplomat) and a Draconic Fairest focused primarily on Social stats and Physical secondary. My character spent his time in Arcadia as a great dragon, forced by his keeper's influence to consume innocent people and burn the countryside, all while his keeper would invade his cave home every evening to slay him (he would rise the next day as though the fight had never happened). My problems are as follows

    1) Contracts. I can't really figure out what Contracts I should put my dots in. So far, I have Eternal Summer 2, Stone 3, Vainglory 2, Elements (Fire) 1, and Fleeting Summer 1 (I had extra experience to spend since I'm joining the game a few sessions after it's started). Should I shift some dots around? Am I spreading myself too thin contract-wise.
    2) Mortal life, I can't really come up with one. My character was taken when he was twelve and returned looking like he was twenty-six (though he has no idea how old he really is), even though only fifteen minutes had passed in real life. He hasn't been to college, and while he actually managed to get something of an education by reading some of the great books that were amongst his considerable horde in Faerie. I can't really come up with an appropriate job that he could do to make money or really create a mortal life. The only connection to the "mortal" world I've come up with so far is his fetch, whom he keeps an eye on from a distance, hoping it can have the childhood he had stolen from him.

    Anyone have any ideas? I'd really appreciate the help!

      think that after a childhood spent mostly in Arcadia, life in the human world may seem weird yet fascinating. There are all kind of customs that your character has to re-learn that most people learn between the ages of 12-21 - while of course, he looks much older physically. What'd a first date be like, where he's treated like a mature partner but has to resort to a cheat sheet on his sleeve that says things like "Don't forget to smile" "Forks on the left" " 'Commitment' means she is serious (do NOT attempt a Pledge!)" and such. Same goes for a job interview - even something like a handshake may seem strange and mysterious. On the other hand, he'll likely seem oddly knowledgeable about certain areas that were part of everyday life in Arcadia. That could prove a perk when landing a job, for example. (Especially if he covertly uses his Contracts to help him out a bit.)

      Maybe he talks briefly about his life as a monster in a cave occassionally to dates and they think he's talking figuratively.

      He may well have friends in the Motley or Court who are in the same shoes, and become reclusive and keep only to the company of other Changelings believing they are too strange to fit into human society, but he thinks he's better than that.

          Job and all that? The number of people abducted as children returning as adults seems pretty high in Changeling. Grab up a mentor and have it be a Changeling that puts effort into helping those abducted as children learn to grow up. Make the Mentor some sort of Businessman and you even have a job, though you probably need to make it a job where you work out of the sight of the public. This actually allows you to role play all of that ignorance, and have a 'safe' place to make mistakes. For mortal social life you could always have mortal coworkers and your story can be that you've been in a coma since you were twelve and your mentor was nice enough to help you adjust. The Coma story also helps out if your start talking about your life as a dragon around mortals. You can then say that you dreamed while in your coma, and spent more of your life dreaming than awake so the dreams seem to be more like your life than this reality. To explain why your family doesn't help out you can tell them that they tragically died in a car accident while you were comatose.

              In terms of mechanics I would use suggest possibly doing the following:

              -use your merit points to give yourself Wyrd 3 (a lot of your abilities will be affected by your wyrd score, it affects the amount of glamour you can have/spend at one time, and it's cheaper to buy a high wyrd with merit points than with exp than the other way around)


              -get vainglory 3-4 (Vainglory 3 makes it so mortals can't attack you [and sometimes supernaturals] and boosts your social stats a bit. Vainglory 4 makes people run away. Considering you used to be a rampaging dragon provoking enough awe/terror to have people not want to attack you and/or run away might prove appropriate.)


              -get element (fire) 2-3 (Element 2 gives you armor, makes people not want to hit you in melee, and provides a pseudo fire breathe attack. Element 3 let's you more convincingly attack people with fire and control fire in general. Again with the whole dragon thing seems fairly appropriate to have.)

              (Personally, if it was me, I would say go ahead and get Element [fire] 5. It wouldn't make a lot of RP sense for a dragon to turn into fire, but Element 5 is a pretty much a game changer. It makes you more or less immune to damage while active, provides a cheap method of making your brawl attacks deal lethal damage, and has incredible synergy with royal oil)


              -possibly get the goblin contract royal oil (4 dots) if it's allowed (it's expensive, requires you to touch your target, and doesn't make a whole lot of sense RP wise, but causes all current and/or future fire damage to become agg. With the ability to control and attack with fire it becomes really destructive really quick)


              -get at least striking looks 1 if you can (striking looks 1 provides a small bonus to many social rolls. With pledges you can upgrade yourself to striking looks 2 basically for free. With Vainglory 3 we can double this bonus, giving your character a +4 to these social rolls.)


              -get the enchanting performance merit if you can and if it's allowed (it reduces any social penalties based on your wyrd, allows you to spend glamour to turn persuasion and expression rolls into rote actions, and creates an emotion for you to feed on. Practically pays for itself)


              -possibly get Stone 1-2, instead of Stone 3, to save on exp if a high non-combat strength is not essential to your character RP wise.


              -get 2 dots within a fighting merit of some kind. (Not really essential, but can make your character fairly dangerous in combat with pledges, especially if it provides extra attacks.

              (For example say you get Kung Fu 2. With a pledge you can upgrade it to Kung Fu 4. You have brawl 4. With a kung fu specialty you get a +1 bonus to brawl [dice pool 5]. With that same pledge that upgraded your Kung Fu fighting style merit you can give yourself another +1 bonus to brawl [dice pool 6]. You have strength 3. That's a dice pool of 9. With Stone 1, with the suggested above wyrd, you will get an average of +2 to your strength. That's a dice pool of 11. If he can apply the bonus for an all out attack while using Kung Fu 4 [can't remember] that's another +2 for a dice pool total of 13.

              The average defense is 2. You get to attack twice because of Kung Fu 4 with your current dex [dex 3]. That's on average 8 damage. With the Lethal Mein merit we can make that lethal instead of bashing.

              That's just a basic example. I've actually seen much higher damage [16-18 damage], but those involved hedgespun weapons/guns and may be a bit excessive for a normal game.)


              With the above you have a maximum dice pool of 19 on persuasion and expression rolls, with rerolls on failed dice, if you use all your vainglory and fairest powers, but it will burn through a lot of glamour without the use of catches and take 3 turns. At minimum use of powers (1 power) you're still looking at a dice pool of 15 with the ability to reroll. (also requires a specialty and use of a willpower point)

              In terms of combat you can beat someone senseless with a fighting style of your choice, opt to go the elemental route and light them on fire at a distance with element 3, with effective invulnerability under most situations if you choose to get element 5, or opt to not have to fight at all with vainglory 3 or 4.


              I know that covers more than just contracts, but I wanted to show you some options if you didn't know they were available.


              In terms of fluff your character can actually use pledges to provide whatever material need he may need (resources, allies, contacts, retainers, etc.) so he actually doesn't need to work, though he may want to get himself better attached to the mortal world or maybe he can use the fact that he can do this to get himself more involved with others like himself.


              I personally had my Changeling use a pledge to give a mortal resources 4 that required her to give my changeling most of it. Literally became a millionaire through a random conversation with a mortal and a pledge.


              If your character does want to work think of what he may be good at. He is incredibly good at persuasion so he could become an activist against corporations destroying villages/towns for profit, he could become a diplomat or mediator for powerful organizations, or maybe a spokesman for a college, or the military, etc. He is also good at brawl so he could become a MMA fighter, specializing in kung fu, a martial arts instructor, a protector of the community, etc.

              Also kudos for actually wanting to protect your fetch. Never met a changeling character that wanted to do that, but that sounds friggen awesome.

              Edit:
              Also you can be a rock star or other form of musician. All your stats line up beautifully to be one of the most talented musicians out there and it requires no formal education, if of course your character desires such a thing.

                  I agree that Vainglory should be higher, a social-based character can get a lot of mileage out of the 3rd and 4th clauses. The character you've described doesn't really seem a good fit for Stone though since it is an Ogre's Contract, but the argument can be made out of your character's Durance. If you planned on using Stone as self-defense, I think you can find something more fitting for him, like a Token weapon; a few points in the Merit can get you a pretty effective tool that fits the character much better. I'm envisioning a dragon-motif antique pistol that instead of bullets fires gouts of flame; not only would this provide a replacement for the fiery breath he might remember fondly from his Durance, but it also means the pistol won't take 15 minutes to reload. Also at least Elements (Fire) 2, a flaming aura can definitely put off a few attackers and being near-immune to fire himself plays up the dragon very well.

                  Something else about the Durance troubles me; you said he became a dragon, but that sounds more like a Beast's durance to me. If that is the case, and it can be (flexibility in character creation is my favorite feature of Changeling), it would be a good argument for a Dual Kith, Possibly a Fairest Draconic/Elemental, Draconic/Hunterheart (the dragon you described was very much a predator, and it would give automatic double-boosts to Brawl attacks), something along those lines. Also you said he aged 14yrs in 15 minutes, but that seems a but too much of a stretch to me. My average rule of thumb as a ST is about 1yr to 1 month of time between Earth and Arcadia, and it can work in either direction (a month on Earth = 1yr in Arcadia or vice-versa). I would recommend putting him away from Earth for a little longer to better fit that out-of-touch feeling he should have from missing 14yrs of growth in Arcadia.

                  As far as the mortal life is concerned, sometimes a Changeling has to kinda cut his losses and build a new one, but that can be tough. The New Identity Merit can represent fake Driver's licences, SSI cards, and Birth Certificates necessary to get a job, even 2 dots would get past most casual background checks that employers use. I don't recommend relying on Pledges to cover material necessities like Resources because Pledges can be cut off in a moment, and you have to have a lot of trust in the other party if they are going to be a major pillar in such a vital part of your mortal life. I ESPECIALLY don't recommend following Kingneo's recommendation of manipulating a Pledge with a mortal that forces them to give you all their wealth, because as a ST I would either 1) not allow that at all as it exploits the ideas of mortals getting better benefits from Pledges than Changelings, or 2) find the swiftest and cruelest way to show you that it was a bad idea, such as having him do something stupid that breaks the pledge and leaving your character destitute.

                  All of this is based entirely on my experiences though, and I haven't seen how the GMC rules update affects Changelings (just downloaded the Rules Update a few days ago).

                  Edit:
                  For an actual job, you might have him look into something where his natural (non-Contract and non-Pledge) abilities would be useful. For a social character you might look into sales, Customer Service, Bartending (allows access to lots of gossip and tips at a busy place can really add up), etc..

                              Originally posted by jhornb02 View Post
                              I ESPECIALLY don't recommend following Kingneo's recommendation of manipulating a Pledge with a mortal that forces them to give you all their wealth, because as a ST I would either 1) not allow that at all as it exploits the ideas of mortals getting better benefits from Pledges than Changelings, or 2) find the swiftest and cruelest way to show you that it was a bad idea, such as having him do something stupid that breaks the pledge and leaving your character destitute.
                              Honestly modifying how Pledges work so that Changelings could only ask so much without offering a favor or boon of some kind may work as a better balance if you thought something like this might become a problem.

                              As Pledges currently work, to my understanding, you can create a Pledge that doesn't hurt a Changeling in any way other than limiting the number of Pledges he or she can still make/have up, and create a scenario where the mortal in question actually wants to obey the Changeling.

                              An example of that is in the spoiler

                              Easiest way would be to have the pledge have a short duration (mostly used in combat pledges), even works to our benefit to do so, the shortest of which can be a day.

                              The mortal in question pays a Changeling for advice (to be paid after the mortal gains the bonus to resources). The Changeling's advice is the task for the pledge.

                              The pledge, in addition to giving resources 4, grants the mortal bonuses to several skills (persuasion, expression, whatever). The mortal, following the advice of the Changeling, finds his or her life much easier, easier to make friends, easier to do his or her job, possibly more status as a result, and, of course, undreamt of wealth. When the pledge ends the mortal will want to come back to the Changeling for "advice", wanting that spark of inspiration again because of how successful the Changeling's advice seemed to make him or her.

                              The Changeling, as before, gives "advice" and asks for payment after the mortal gains his or her bonuses from the pledge.

                              Eventually the Changeling's "advice" will become so valued that the mortal in question will come back of his or her own accord, possibly daily, no longer capable or willing to perform his or her work without it (bonuses to skills and resources when the advice is being followed and the loss of these bonuses, and the gain of poisoned boons to skills the mortal highly prizes, when not following the Changeling's advice).


                              If the mortal in question were to break the Pledge for whatever reason the biggest detriment a Changeling would suffer is limiting the maximum pledges he or she could have up at a time and that's negated through a short duration, which could be as short as 24 hours.

                              Once the Pledge wears off he or she could simply set it up again with the mortal or with someone else, possibly with a rival of the mortal, and have the original mortal see how he or she screwed up for not following the Changeling's advice and watching as another profits.

                              Eventually the Changeling could come to the original mortal again, or not, and the original mortal would most likely be grateful because as soon as he or she follows the Changeling's advice he or she sees an instant boost to skills, resources, etc.




                              This is not to say that True Prep should do what I did with that particular character (it can make certain situations complicated). I was simply trying to show True Prep options he could use for his character to get by (regular day job options, ambitious career options, a pledge to himself for resources, a creative pledge to a mortal for more resources). Any option is fine. I just want him to enjoy himself.


                              True Prep, Jhorn's (hope neither of you mind me calling you that) advice about having your character having the Beast Seeming is quite good on an RP level and probably matches for character idea best. They spent most of their Durance as animals, even mythical ones, after all.

                              If you're worried about social bonuses Beasts can raise their Presence with Glamour so they are on par with Fairest in terms of raw charisma, though manipulation could become a problem, and getting vainglory dots would be much harder to get due to the increased exp cost. Fang and Claw is a pretty good contract to have, with good information gathering and additional senses (like being able to see in infrared)

                              With that option your character could see heat, breathe fire, possibly control fire, talk to reptiles, burn people that attempt to hit you, and talk his way out of a lot of situations. (That's Fang and Claw 2, Vainglory 3, Element 2-3 [3rd dot may be too expensive to get], and Token 3-4 to allow you to more convincingly breathe fire [that was an incredibly brilliant idea Jhorn]). Add in Dual Kith to give yourself the Dragonic kith, and Stone 1 to simulate more of that raw power of a dragon, and you're all set.

                              If you want to enhance it further you can give yourself Lethal Mein to make your attacks lethal, instead of bashing, to simulate some of the raw power of being a dragon, and Enchanting Performance to make you a social powerhouse. Striking Looks is also a good option for it's passive bonus and your bonus from Vainglory.

                              In case you don't know Lethal Mein, Enchanting Performance, and Token are merits, the first two in the Rites of Spring book (I think) the last in the core.


                              If you still want to go the Fairest route, still a very good selection (a prized dragon of a gentry who was "groomed" and "pampered" to become a great, terrifying, beast to rampage across the countryside only to be killed by his or her lord every night to make him more terrifying fits a Fairest styled Durance), you could even try the Shadowsoul Kith in addition to the Dragonic Kith if you wanted to experiment with Dual Kith. It will give you an affinity to night time and make you absolutely terrifying to others with it's bonuses to intimidation.

                              With that option your character could talk himself out of practically everything, make mortals incapable of harming him, cause mortals and supernaturals alike to run in fear in situations where your motley is in trouble, breathe fire, possibly control fire, burn people that attempt to hit you, and be a fairly competent fighter (that's Vainglory 4, Element 2-3 [fire], Token 3-4, Stone 1)

                              As before Lethal Mein, Enchanting Performance, and Striking Looks remain good options for this character.


                              Ogre is also another good choice if you are looking for that pure, unyielding, strength (and your character seems to have been abused enough, being made to fight and be killed over and over again, to make it seem Ogreish). For that there is the Troll Kith which allows you add your strength to manipulation rolls which covers quite a few social rolls. With that Kith you will actually have a higher manipulation based persuasion dice pool than a Fairest.

                              To simulate raw charisma instead of manipulation, possibly more fitting for your type of dragon, you could ask your ST to let you add your str to presence instead of manipulation.

                              After that you could go Stone 5. Covers armor, gives you a big boost to str, grants a gluttonous based healing, allows you rip through walls like it was cardboard, and even provides a scene long boost to some of your social rolls thanks to your Kith with Stone 3. Add in the Token suggested by Jhorn and you have your fire breathe. Add in Element [fire] and you get whatever additional fire stuff you may like. Very dragonic in my opinion.

                              If you want to enhance that further Lethal Mein remains a good option as well as Enchanting Performance. Striking Looks won't as good as the above without Vainglory, but still quite solid for a social character and a way to distinguish himself from most Ogres.

                              I apologize for the long post. I know that's a lot to read.

                                      Three easy ways for Changelings to breathe fire.

                                      Armour of Elements Fury, the second Clause of Elements (Fire), and just say that the Dexterity + Brawl + half-Wyrd attack is you breathing fire at close range.

                                      Lord's Dread Gaze, the fifth Clause of Eternal Summer, and just say that the beam of heat is fire from your mouth (does require you to be Mantle Summer 4+ though).

                                      Finally, a Goblin Pledge with an appropriate purview (bonefires, torches, flammable alcohol, the act of fire-breathing, your choice) with a boon that lets you make a fire-breathing attack. It would presumably be a medial boon (purview helps you injure a target). The precise details would need to be worked out with your ST.

                                          To answer your question #2, some ideas come immediately to mind:
                                          On having a mortal life: it would be pretty rare for a 12 year old to not have friends or family. Sure, with this weird event, you can't go home again. But no matter how strange my life gets, I have a sister I would confide in that I could convince was me, even if I looked like an alligator, because we grew up together. If your connection with a friend, it would actually be easier to convince a child that a bizarre miracle had happened than an adult. You would have to be careful about your kid-friend, but I think that working out how to stay in touch would be interesting. I really believe that having a realistic character means having a connection to someone mortal from pre-Durance. Some may disagree, I'm sure.

                                          Career ideas: I think your first question should be, why does this guy have such high social skills? Sure, he was maybe a natural as a kid, but as he has grown up, those abilities have really increased quite a bit. Something social must have been happening in Faerie. Maybe your horde included a group of ghosts of sacrificed maidens or a talking harp or some lizard men that worshiped you. If you can decide (or if the GM decides) what social thing happened, it might lead you in a direction.

                                          Easiest job to get for a high social-- commission sales. For most sales jobs, you need no education qualifications. Many will give you a chance if you look and sound charismatic-- make a few quick sales and you're in. If you have few morals, start your own ponzi scheme.

                                          Career Counseling for Dragons: perhaps base a career on dragon skills? Appraiser-- known for greed, maybe your great at accessing value. Jeweler-- you developed an advanced knowledge of gold and jewels. Herpetologist/ snake handler: you have a knowledge of/ a way with snakes. Pilot: sure, you need to take some lessons to get a license, but you really understand flying in your bones. Steel Mill worker: maybe as a fire-breather, you have little fear of and like to be around high temps.
