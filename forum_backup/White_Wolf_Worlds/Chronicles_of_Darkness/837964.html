    How do you Hack your Setting?

    02-27-2016, 02:52 AM
    Everybody knows that, whether you call yourself a games master, a story teller, a dungeon keeper, whatever, if you're playing an RPG, you're likely to customize it. And with fluff and themes that can often be highly contentious, the Chronicles of Darkness are no exception to that rule. Since there's a whole bunch of threads scattered over these boards discussing different ways of making specific setting hacks work, I was wondering if anyone would be willing to share the ways they've tweaked and changed the Chronicles of Darkness to make them truly theirs?


    Hopefully not being rude here, my own answer to my question...

    For starters, I run my Chronicles of Darkness closer to Dark Urban Fantasy than pure Horror. There's still some horror in it, hence the "dark" part of that descriptor, but exactly what is horrifying and how much so depends on lot on where you're standing.

    The general approach to the setting is inspired by Unknown Armies, and can be summed up in two words: Occult Underground. The world humanity thinks it lives in is but the tip of the iceberg, and things get darker and scarier the deeper you dare to descend into the shadows. Past the Requiem Sanguine of the vampires, down through the hellish worlds of the spirits, the fae and the dead, past the resentful fragments of old gods and new, deeper and deeper until you reach the layer where beings of divine might conduct a subtly fought eternal war that ever threatens to tear the fabric of reality itself apart.

    From a mortal perspective, the setting has something of a Cosmic Horror motif, for a simple reason: Humanity Does Not Matter. I agree that the Old World of Darkness went too far with its description of the power and influence of supernaturals, but at the same time, I feel that the New World pulls too many of their fangs for my liking. My Chronicles have it that monsters aren't as a general rule, controlling everything - but that's because, as a general rule, they have better things to do. Mages examine mysteries that could redefine reality as humans know it. Vampires whirl away the eternity with feeding and intercine strife. Werewolves hunt ceaseless, as incapable of conceiving of an alternative as they are of physically stopping. Whatever problems monsters have with humanity is on a localized, individual level; across the world as a whole, what happens to humanity bothers supernaturals not a whit.

    This, then, gives Hunters a somewhat darker motif. Yes, the Vigil is a candle in the dark... but the thing about candles? They burn out. Pyrrhic victories, bittersweet endings, and the nagging query of "is this really worth it?" are the order of the day. Of course, a world is only dark if there is light to contrast it; many hunters do decide that, yes, this is worth it, and taking whatever victories you can is part of the theme as well.

    As you might guess, I make a significant use of Imperial Mysteries as a basis for "the god tier" of the Chronicles of Darkness, painting the higher worlds and the manifold powers who must meddle subtly, lest they unmake the world in their enthusiasm.

    Something inspired by a setting hack actually here in the Mage forum now, my setting has more Watchtowers than the core five. The core five are just the most common; every imaginable tripartite combination has its representative Watchtower. This alters the effect of False Awakening (Night Horrors: The Unbidden) slightly, with those rare scholars of the arcane who know of the Archmages wondering if it mightn't have been a failed gambit by an Archmage... or, worse, if it mightn't have somehow retroactively created the "lesser watchtowers" as equivalent exchange for burning itself into creation.

    There are a few other bits and pieces, but these are the main hacks I apply to my setting. So, what're yours?

              I hesitate because it was quite a few years back. Plus, I generally used basic guidelines in my head without actually creating any coherent cosmology to go with it. I broadly speaking knew what I wanted and just kinda went with that.

                  Well, first off, everything here. Especially the setting hack docs for the assorted lines.

                  Beyond that I have some general setting rules I tend to go by:
                  • I've downplayed the X-Splats in every game I could. Vampires use Bloodlines, Werewolves shift Auspice, a Mage's Path only serves to describe his general world view, etc. Same with Y-Splats, though to a lesser degree. It's not a fair assumption to assume all cities have the big five Covenants represented (indeed, it's very rare), or that Tribes are world-spanning groups. Basically all changes are intended to reduce the sense of a homogenous global community, and encourage distinct regional settings and quirks.
                  • I removed most of the "formerly human, now pure evil" counter-splats. Belial's Brood, The Pure, The Exarchs (and by extension the seers). That's not to say I removed asshole villains, only that those villains tend to come from within, and be individuals as opposed to groups. Individuals is a big deal when I run, all characters are themselves first and members of their respective groups second.
                    • Wholly alien threats, like the God-Machine, Gentry or Abyssal entities remain horrific, but in a "they aren't evil, just completely different" sort of way.
                  • Attached to the previous two points, Supernatural populations are kept very small. Mini-splats: Revenants, Wolf-Blooded, Fae Touched, etc. play a bigger role and greatly outnumber their major counterpart upwards of 100:1 (Sleepwalkers to Mages). This is primarily to keep the 'magic level' more down to earth, and make any character with a Major splat clearly, a big player. It also help manage NPCs on the fly, and prevent over-saturation of high-magical characters
                  • I downplay otherworldly travel in all games where it's relevant. When it does occur, it is dangerous and significant. Werewolves about to embark on a hunt in the shadow? Shit is going down. Astral journey to discover a lost truth? Story climax. All-in-all the aim is to keep the game here, on earth, and not strolling out into otherworldly superspace (which is something I associate more with cWoD).
                  • Mage, Demon and Geist see some fairly considerable changes.
                    • Mage: Paths define little more than the Mage's version of Mage Sight, and color their view of reality. Mages have a harder time slinging Improvised spells (no Ruling Arcana), but Rotes are considerably stronger and easier to learn. I forwent Legacies for Arts, which serve much the same purpose but don't lock the Mage in as hard, so a Mage who wanted to spread out (and spend the experience) could learn several different "styles" of magic. All-in-all the aim is more style, less common, but more impactful spellcasting and an attempt to remove some of the sameyness between Mages of the same path.
                    • Demon: Very little difference mechanically, but the God-Machine plays a much less active role. Demons are more free to act bizarre and use their powers, not quite openly, but with less fear of being hunted down and dragged back into the machine. Glitches now make up the primary repercussion for Cover loss, and most Demons are expected to have several Glitches.
                    • Geist: Is hugely different, inspired by things like Persona, Jojo's Bizarre Adventure and (most recently) Ajin, Geister can manifest as ghostly forms and act on their host Sin-Eater's wishes. Manifestations are entirely custom to every character, evolving and developing into weird and unique abilities as they grow. Sin-Eater's labour under their Geist's passions, forced to enact them or fade from the memory as though they had actually died.

                      The mood is drawn from Delta Green and Unknown Armies; pursuit of the supernatural is something undertaken by the people determined/stupid/crazy enough not to keep their heads down like the rest of humanity, and they're punished for it. Hunter is particular is basically one big exercise in destroying your life in order to uphold the Vigil. Moral degredation is a big deal in Vampire and Hunter (what lengths will you go to in order to do what you have to - either to survive, or to fight back, respectively), and pretty much every game hinges on the horror of losing oneself, either to alien urges and ways of thinking, or jusy straight up sanity slippage. There's a sense of a secret history, though never in the form of "Hitler was manipulated by Werewolves," instead focusing on the supernatural as a mirror to what the mortals are experiencing.

                      Personal Horror, written in big letters on the front of every gamebook. I want to steer as clear from Urbam Fantasy as possible.

                      As for more specific tweaks, my Mage antagonists are almost always drawn from the Pentacle itself, my God-Machine is either a work of Cold War occult science or else the Fallen mirror of the Principle, I've made some firm connections between Geist and Mummy, and I don't recognize Beast as part of my World of Darkness.

                              The last campaing I ran was Hunter: the Vigil, and usually when I run it, I modify the world heavily. I make full use of Dread Powers and great majority of my monsters are homebrewed. Even the "official" ones are heavily modified and do not exist as they do in their own books. I had silver-fearing daywalker vampires, sorcerous werefoxes, cultists and servant creatures of Alien God, cursed werewolf, strange cryptids formed by magical surges, witches of all sorts, ancient forest god/spirit, angels of Machine God and other sources, djinns, immortal relic hunter and many more. None of the creatures belonged to organisations described in books and most worked quite differently and with greatly reduced strength.

                              I also didn't have Supernal Realms and spirits of Shadow Realm had much harder time affecting the world of flesh. "God level" creatures pretty much didn't exist or were unable to directly affect the world, and had to use mortal or supernatural agents. Mood was far closer to Urban Fantasy than Horror but terrible things happened and characters suffered from their calling (and from stupid choices). Still, hope burned bright and torch was kept lit, with some monsters being allies and others enemies. Supernatural world was far more splintered and varied than the one in books; surprisingly common relics, spontaneous magic surges and alternate universes & other worlds creating unexplained, frightening and intriguing phenomena around the world.

                                  My biggest changes, mechanically, have been to Vampire, both Requiem and Masquerade. Thanks to the Translation guide, it started me off on a cool meld idea,and it just kind of snowballed.

                                  The initial hack was to use Generation and Blood Potency at the same time. Generation acts as a starting minimum and maximum potential boundary setter. Blood Potency increases with age, up to the maximum allowed by Generation. Then, remembering a line from the old Masquerade game about vampires hitting a thousand years old underoing a kind of change, I decided that any vampire who hits 1,000 can raise his upper limit by one for every thousand years he's alive. So if a 9th - 13th gen actually made it to 2,000 years old, he'd effectively be 7th Gen (because the maximum for those gens are all 5)

                                  While working on that, I realized, watching some related media, that I was never very fond of "The Beast". So I replaced it with "The Hunger." Instead of having to constantly worry about frenzy and rotshreck, a vampire instead has to make an effort of will to pull off a victim when feeding. They also gain a new stated called "Hungry"if their Blood Pool is less than half full. Normally, in my games, vamps all have Blush of life when sated, and when they get Hungry, they start to degrade.At Hungry, they look like classic WoD vamps, pale, cold to the touch, no pulse. At 1/4 or less, they gain "Starving", and start to look more like Cappadocians. Each state has penalties to the Hunger checks when feeding. A botch means they go on a feeding rampage.

                                  Without rotschreck, I ditched sunlight and fire as Aggravated damage. Oh it'll still KILL them, they can just heal it. It also allows vampires, under certain conditions, to move about in daylight, something most can do in RL lore (as opposed to in-game lore). Aggravated damage is now caused solely by supernatural abilities.

                                  After doing this, I realized the last great inequality of vampires versus every other supernatural. They have very few passive abilities, and those are tied to the three physical disciplines. So, I gave them a mild regenerative effect. As long as they're not "Hungry", a vampire slowly repairs Bashed and Lethal damaged over time. Every turn, the highest level of damage downgrades. Lethal becomes Bashing. Bashing becomes healed. Aggravated damage can ONLY be cured by actively spending blood, and it's still limited to one a day. Remember, in my verse, a vampire is functionally "alive" as long as they're well-fed. Makes it harder for hunters to find them, too.

                                  Admittedly, this can downplay the whole "personal horror" aspect, but I like to think it's preserved with the following conundrum. Whenever a vampire feeds, he risks killing. Period. No more "i'm a noble vampire who never kills when I feed" stuff. To stay alive, you have to eat. OFTEN. So more risk of killing. You can not eat as often, but you'll lose that warmth of humanity, and gain a higher risk of losing control when feeding. So which is less monstrous?

                                  I'm currently working on a homebrew setting,where I'm making it my own, partially by taking ideas from other shows, movies, and fiction, filing the serial numbers off, and merging them together into their own world.

                                  * Vampire - modified to be more like The Originals, with a bit of Blade. Clans are gone, in favor of player-created Bloodlines, reflecting regional lineages that hold sway in an area.
                                  * Werewolf - half Forsaken, half inspired by Bitten; the Forsaken are bitten, cursed werewolves, used as pawns by the Pure, who are natural born , aristocratic werewolves who breed true. Both guard the spirit world, but for different reasons.
                                  * Mage - thanks to the Dresden Files, I chucked the lore entirely, and replaced the factions with magical styles. The Council still exists, but as a kind of judicial body, mixed with dispute management. Their arch enemy is the fact that other supernaturals keep wanting to use them as pawns in their own schemes, and many of their own kind let them.
                                  * Demon - actually covers Fallen and Angels, in a sub-setting inspired by the old In Nomine RPG, with more than a few nods and winks to Supernatural, just like...
                                  * Hunter - basically the same, just with my own custom groups.
                                  * Beast - gutted almost completely, and being reworked into a system where they're the descendants of the old gods, who awaken to a gift which is powered by adoration or fear (worship). Unfortunately, they also gain power from killing each other and absorbing their divine essence. So two parts Highlander, one part Hancock, one part Sylar from Heroes.
                                  * Changeling - Still reading, not sure yet.

                                  Unlike CofD and WoD, my games are designed for characters of multiple factions, though I leave enough variety for a species-specific chronicle.

                                      It might be a flaw in my style but I do a hack for each of my Chronicles. Usually to add a gimmick that the chronicle will revolve around.

                                      My first game: Circle of Five was a large Cross-over chronicle with the following setting and rules hacks:
                                      - The five Supernal Realms were ruled by a major entity called "Rayvul Krishisho"
                                      - The five players played children whose souls were taken to the Supernal by the Entity to be trained in becoming the avatars for 5 pieces of his soul.
                                      - the ultimate goal of the Entity was to dilute it's essence in the fallen Realm so that The Abyss doesn't destroy it and therefor everything, in this way, the 5 pieces of it's soul acted as "Pillars" holding off the Abyss.
                                      - When the souls returned to their bodies (with the 5 pieces of the souls), they needed to find 10 artifacts that linked to one of the arcana in order for the pillars to work.
                                      - There were 5 guardians that were reincarnated (but didn't know it) to protect the pillars and the Circle of Five.

                                      Rules Hack:
                                      - The players picked 2 Arcana at their leisure, one gross, the other subtle.
                                      - Every so often they would get a dream and in the morning they would have access to any spell in one arcana of one mastery level as rote and they would look older.
                                      - The characters would have a compulsion to gain power from the artifacts, doing so would weaken or kill the guardian but would give that player mastery over that arcana as rote in total (and they would have serious seeds of the Abyss in them corrupting them). This basically breaks the pillar. (killing both artifacts, would mean the loss of character control as they go insane. the players of these characters nearing this were made aware and a few continued the path of darkness willingly because the story was awesome. One attacked the other characters and guardians with the false hope that he could gain their power as well, the other became so saddened by what he had done to the guardian that served as his romantic interest that he left for arcadia to search for her.)

                                      ~~
                                      The other notable examples of Hacks I've done were mostly for prequel stories to the Circle of Five like:
                                      - A town that there are nodes everywhere where demons and angels (of the inferno variety) come out in droves and possess people. The more they possess the less likely anyone can leave the town. These nodes were new and their mechanics were different but similar to verges.
                                      - A silent hill story where i added a mechanic for one of the character to behave in similar ways to Lisa from SH2.


                                      There are others but the Circle of Five remains the biggest hack i've ever done (and likely why I don't hack many of my later stories as a way to "try something else")


                                      Frequent Story Teller for the Circle of Five gaming group.

                                          Thematically, I like the theme that humans are important and can change things, just that it's not always a good thing that they are or a good idea when they try to. And, ultimately, to accomplish some things you might have to lose some of what we consider humanity, which isn't always the same as morality. It's not necessarily evil to do so, to break a rule or transgress a boundary of law or nature, but it's often frightening, and difficult, and of course the loss of innocence always comes with some sadness. But that doesn't mean it isn't worth doing. It's like growing up- it hurts, it can screw you up, it can screw other people up, but that doesn't mean it isn't ultimately worth it.

                                          I tend to downplay the various thematic and mechanical aspects that draw supernatural beings towards corruption (ie, the Beast). Why? Because humans can corrupt themselves quite well on their own, and often it's the human part of a supernatural creature that drives them to evil.

                                          Also, things are grey, but that doesn't mean that everyone is equally right or equally at fault. Some things are just wrong, and fighting against evil doesn't always make you evil.

                                          Another thing: you'd be surprised what people can get used to. Do something long enough, it becomes your new normal. A vampire that's spent twenty years as a human and 150 as a vampire doesn't necessarily think of the Danse Macabre as anything more romantic than "the thing I do all night to pass the time". And said vampire doesn't spend all his time brooding or backstabbing or hunting or whatever. He wakes up in the evening, gets a drink, goes to Elysium, goes home, and logs on to the Internet to argue about baseball with night owls and friends in different time zones. He sits and watches the game, sees the sunlight on the screen and can almost feel it on his neck. And then he wonders, hey, what about that Curse of the Bambino? It sounds ridiculous, but hey, if vampires are real...

                                          So he texts his coterie-mate, who's been studying the supernatural for the Ordo Dracul, and he asks her, and before you know it they're being chased through an abandoned quarry by a giant golem made from stitched-together Red Sox corpses. That's the world of darkness.

                                          That's for the thematic side. In terms of mechanics, I can't really use Promethean and Geist without major tweaks. Promethean as it is far too limiting and Geist is just...very broken in a mechanical sense and requires a ton of house ruling to use.

                                                  The majority of my hacks have been to update 1e content to 2e. Vampire and Werewolf are relatively unchanged (except that I still run both bloodlines and lodges like they're clan and tribe junior). I have global hacks to the main Chronicles of Darkness rules concerning skill specialties and when their required (e.g., need motocycles specialty to drive a motorcycle, need a neurosurgery speciality to do brain surgery, etc.) and species factor (for calculating speed). No hacks to 2e Werewolf at all. Cosmetic hacks for 2e Vampire (renaming Revenants to Ghasts, more closely associating them with Belial's Brood from 1e, etc.). I did a bunch of hacks to various embeds for Demon (many of which were based on or copied Pendragon's Demon hacks). Once the finished product is finally released I'm likely to do a number of hacks to Beast that will make it possible to have kinship with mortals, have spawn in one's lair, and solidify the dream world's mechanics.

                                                  Fluff wise I don't do very many. To discourage metagaming I do tell my players that various parts of the fluff they read may not be true (especially concerning the origins of things). Depending on how Mage and Promethean play out I'll likely be doing some hacks for them. Mage is likely to see some toughening up of paradox and a general refluffing of the X splats from ways of seeing the "true world" to extradimensional fonts that mages draw their eldritch powers from (think Dr. Strange and his invocations), or in other words I'll be rejecting the whole "world is fallen" schtick and promoting the idea that mages are all crazy people that meddle with forces they barely understand and can't really control. I'll definitely be rebranding Promethean as "Monster: the Created" as it's going to let me elevator pitch the game to prospective players with a minimum amount of fuss (who wouldn't want to play Frankenstein's monster?). I might also nerf disquiet and wasteland if the 2e doesn't nerf them adequately to my liking.

                                                  My biggest hacks have always been to Hunter and Geist. In the case of the former, I added a power stat, Initiation, to hang endowments and the risk willpower mechanic from. This is simply a matter of personal preference. I like my Hunters to slowly become something inhuman. As it increases, Hunters slowly begin to risk becoming Slashers more and more (as they learn the truth - without humans there would be no monsters). In the case of the latter I pretty much jettison the mardi gras atmopshere and play it more of a straight up old school Revenant type game (if folks remember that Wraith spin-off from the oWoD). Its the most substantial fluff hack.

                                                      Also, crossovers and theories, in the sense of how much we imagine them as being a part of our personal "worlds/chronicles of darkness" problem merit mentioning as well. I personally like them, but I can understand the objections people have. I tend to like some degree of order, structure, and, for lack of a better word, "ecology". The books make it clear that Mages and Changelings exist in the same world, so honestly it would be sort of ridiculous to assume that there are two completely separate Arcadias that just happen to be eerily similar. I think of the cosmology of ChroD as a sort of ecosystem- the God Machine, Qashmallim, the spirit choirs, all those things have their own "ecological niche" and interact in various ways. I actually like the "evolutionary" perspective Beast has, because it fits in with this.

                                                      I map out the various types of supernatural phenomena roughly to the Watchtowers/Paths, and think it would actually make sense to house rule that a Mage could specialize in using forms of magic related to their path's "area"- such as an Acanthus learning to harvest glamour and make Contracts.

                                                      Hell, I've even sometimes thought it would be fun to consider the separate additions parallel universes that someone emerged from the end of the oWoD, like an extremely meta goth version of sburb.

                                                          I tend to make my games less full of horror and darkness, creating a World of Dimness instead (because I and my players like humor and whimsy, and get burnt out with too many successive dark/emotionally draining plots). There is some horror and strife, but less than the setting-as-written, and sometimes things just exist (like friendly squid-men anthropologists, or Team Rocket, or jovial werewolf frat bros). Plus, there's something very satisfying flipping the script and turning a light plot dark or dark plot light.

                                                          I also trend against a unifying cosmology; plenty of characters have theories about the interconnectedness of all things, but the "truth" (from an OOC/GM perspective) is that there's a bunch of overlapping realities, some of which bleed into others, some of which lead independent existences, some of which connect, and some of which are fictional. This truth is mutable depending on what other GMs are running the game with me.

                                                          Oh, and the Realm of Blackness is an in-universe RPG originally written by Black Dog, now taken over by Rocky Road, and is otherwise indistinguishable from the oWoD. (And I call myself a GM rather than an ST.)
