            Gonna try to get more organized and add in a few extra ideas.

            1st: Prime: Spend a point of mana and pinpoint the location of the nearest and largest source of mana in sensory Range. Dots in the Prime arcana count as Potency towards piercing magical concealment effects.

            Life: Add Life to your Mage Sight for free. In addition the location of creatures within sensory Range that can be sacrificed for mana are also revealed. This doesn't reveal what type of creature they are, but does reveal the amount(s) of mana they can be sacrificed for.

            2nd: Prime: Spend a point of mana and raise a Veil with a Potency equal to your Dots in Prime that helps to conceal your true nature. The Veil hides the mana in your Pattern and also activation of further powers such as other attainments or the casting of spells.

            Life: Spend the point of mana and take control of a target in a fashion similar to Trigger the Lizard Brain from 1e.

            3rd: Arcane Soul's often find themselves needing to stockpile stores of mana to continue to fuel their existence. This attainment lets them create Tass (Use the spell Dave spoiled as the template).

            Life: I haven't had a good idea for this yet. Maybe something like he is more efficient at committing a sacrifice and receives double the usual mana from it.

            4th: By spending a point of mana the Mage may turn himself into a hybrid of pure mana and his living form for a scene. This purified form does not need to eat, sleep, breath and does not age. Any mundane action that is undertaken by the Mage (I.e. requires a die roll) costs the Mage either a point of mana or causes a point of Bashing damage (Mage's choice). This attainment is considered by many members of the legacy to be the goal they truly seek. Unfortunately, the fact that the effect is short-lived necessitates a constant supply of mana to fuel it.

            Life: When the Mage turns into his purified mana form he also has the option of paying a point of mana to take the form of a creature he has sacrificed instead. This includes people if the Mage has turned to human sacrifice.

            5th: Haven't had a good idea here yet. Maybe allow the Arcane Soul to extend his time in purified form while in a Hallow at the cost of Unmaking it. Seems sort of lame though.

            Life: The Mage may create comatose duplicate of himself. This is a living body and so requires further magic or mundane life support to maintain or it won't survive on its own. The body is usually a copy of the Mage's own, but there's no restriction on this. If the Mage places a soulstone inside his duplicate then if he should die his soul will anchor to the duplicate. He may then activate it to come back to life in the duplicate body. Because of the high level of Gnosis needed to access this attainment no Arcane Soul maintains more than two of these duplicates at a time. The soulstone from this process may be reused.

            I think overall this is a really good mix of investigator for what the AS are specifically interested in, some stealth and control to make it easier to sneak or manipulate themselves towards what they want, a way to ensure their future and to achieve actual short lived immortality and then finally a much longer lasting form of immortality. I've got a couple attainments in there that are basically dud space right now unfortunately. I need to think about them more.

            Anyways, the weaknesses of the legacy should be pretty readily apparent. The 4th attainment requires a lot of mana to maintain while also obviously being one of the most desired effects of the legacy. If they've managed to stockpile a lot of Tass they can keep it up for a while but eventually they'll run out unless they stay constantly on the search for more sources of mana. Hopefully, that puts some pressure on them to make ethically dubious choices, but you could also play a pretty lax AS who exalts in his purified form but stays in it for much more reasonable periods of time. Aside from that is also the temptation to commit sacrifices for increased breadth of power. Sacrifices are usually considered in poor taste, but they're allowed. Switch up to humans for the bigger amount of mana though and you will get hunted down (Especially since you can sacrifice a Mage for even more mana). The last attainment adds an additional cost to immortality in the form of soulstones. I actually really like this one because at Gnosis 10 a Mage might be able to manifest the final attainment of his legacy and ascend. So by seeking immortality in the Fallen the Mage is deliberately preventing himself from realizing his actual, true potential.

                My apologies for not responding sooner. Anyway, I've read the suggestions and feedback on the framework for my Arcane Soul Legacy and I've been using it to adjust the concept and consider it's attainments, history and culture. I won't be posting it all just yet as I'm still working on it. As for Mrmdubois's ideas, the idea of attaining a "Pure Mana Form" does not fit my original intention for the Legacy, but I did consider that it could be it's own Legacy, possibly with links to the Arcane Soul legacy either as something vaguely related or something encountered by the Arcane Souls or as a divergent path of it. I like the "Mana Leech" idea, and considering the Pure Mana Form idea, what would a Mana Leech/Vampire see when they encounter a being made purely of mana? A mana Form Legacy could potentially come into conflict with a more Left Handed divergence of the Arcane Soul who steals and leeches mana.

                The most important thing I've concluded based on the input in this thread is different "Tiers" of Immortality and feeding through the Arcane Soul Legacy. Basically, a low-maintenance practice of the Arcane Soul Legacy merely stops ageing, simply sipping feeding the soul just enough to get by does not impart any additional effects, and such a Mage could potentially live as an immortal hermit if they have their own Hallow and Sanctum/Demesne are are careful and self reliant.

                The more they feed their soul at each feeding, the more benefits they gain, and therein lies the possibility for hubris and corruption, as an Arcane Soul who drinks deeply could become superhuman or almost god-like. I figure at it's greatest extreme, an Arcane Soul could drain Hallows dry, absorbing truly vast amounts of mana in a single feeding and becoming very powerful and allowing them to stave off death for extended periods before required to feed again. I've roughly brainstormed a few Tiers of feeding.

                1. Minimal feeding. Ageing stops but otherwise require food, water and oxygen and sleep. Minor resistance to disease. Must feed lightly every few days.
                2. Second tier of feeding. Ageing stops and become immune to disease. Food, water, oxygen and sleep are still required. Must feed moderately every week.
                3. Third tier of feeding. Ageing stops, immunity to disease and physical needs no longer apply.(food, water, oxygen, sleep). Must feed on large amounts of mana every year.
                4. Fourth tier of feeding. Ageing stops, immunity to disease and physical needs. Gain enhanced spellcasting. Must feed every 2 years. Inordinate amounts of mana required for feeding. Risks disrupting or depleting Sanctum defences. At this Tier onward the Arcane Soul gains "Soul Luminosity", causing their soul to shine brighter than others and imparting some weaknesses.
                5. Fifth tier of feeding. Ageing stops, immunity to disease and physical needs, enhanced spellcasting and physical and mental attributes. Must feed every 5 years. Vast amounts of mana required for feeding. A Hallow or Sanctum risks being destroyed completely.
                6. Final Tier of feeding. Ageing stops, immunity to disease and physical needs, enhanced spellcasting and greatly enhanced physical/mental attributes, resistance to damage. Must feed every decade. Requires vast amounts of mana, such as from draining a Hallow dry.

                Besides these benefits based on feeding, Arcane Souls could gain abilities that allow them to potentially absorb some or all of a spells power, or in some cases, to drain the mana from living things that possess it, like a Vampire. I plan on writing up details on divergent forms of the Arcane Soul and a lot of drama in the Legacies history. Long story short, the original creator of the Legacy intended for it to be used for good, but over the years some have abused and distorted it, and the entire Arcane Soul spectrum suffers for it.

                Weaknesses from Soul Luminosity could involve things like being easier to see and track with spells and mage sight, making it harder to resist certain spells due to sympathy aquired by "Locking on" to the Arcane Soul's brightly shining soul.

                I have also considered the Tremere Liches as the arch-enemies of the Legacy, who find an Arcane Souls soul especially nourishing.

                        I wrote up a couple 1e legacies (which I thought I posted on here or the last forum, but who knows?) that relate to that theme.

                        Mine 'em for ideas, use 'em, ignore 'em. Whatever.


                        The Famished

                        Their souls strive ever upward, but they find their body literally requires the touch of the supernal to survive.

                        Path: None
                        Order: Mysterium or Guardians of the Veil                         The Free Council has a branch, as well. Investigations by the Guardians have no explanation; No breach of security or link to any known member ever surfaced. Apparently some Councilor spontaneously joined this legacy. That didn't stop the Guardians from attempting to contain that branch. FC Famished can expect to be hunted by even the Guardians who accept the legacy as valid.
                        Oblations: Performing acts of refined creation, inspiring others through art or personal council; Helping someone find True love; Teaching the Mysteries

                        1st: Sublimation

                        Req.: Gnosis 3, Prime 2, Dream 2

                        The need for higher things makes the famished soul lean on mana as a source of higher power. The Famished may, at any time, spend a mana to reconnect, momentarily, to the supernal. This expenditure normally lasts for a single action, but that can be an extended action. The act is known as "refining" the act, or "empowering." Refining acts mundane or opposed to the supernal realms can still work, as it reveals them to be such. For each level of Dream that the Famished bears, the Famished must refine an act throughout the course of the day. Failure to do so causes breathingto fade in significance, requiring mana or suffocation. Refining breathing only lasts for a scene, until the requirement has been met. The character's personal deadline can be defined by the storyteller or the player, but remains fixed. Midnight is a reasonable choice. So are dawn & noon.

                        The Famished must refine the act of eating, or it provides no nourishment. Any scene in which the Famished eats without refining his consumption, the food passes on without being absorbed. The famished excretes as if they digested that meal while starving, but all nutritional value is lost. This does, obviously, count towards refinement in a day.

                        To anyone who has had contact with the supernal, contact with the Famished invokes a lesser longing, as well. Essentially any use of the skill Empathy, for example, on one of the Famished automatically causes memories of the supernal to resurface in that user. Famished using Expression trigger such things as well. The Famished always feels this; such contact shares it in a lighter form.

                        The cost of any supernal magic directed at the Famished increases by one mana. The caster is intuitively aware of the increased cost as part of forming their Imago, and may pay it, if they have the capacity to do so. If not, the spell automatically fails, and the caster takes a distraction penalty equal to the Dream rating of the target Famished for the rest of the scene as they are assaulted by scenes from their awakening or any other supernal experience. If a caster does pay the surcharge, the Famished is briefly refreshed, momentarily connected to supernal. This restores a willpower point or,if that pool is full, a point of mana, as a result.

                        Optional : Mind 1

                        If a refined action fulfills the Famished's virtue, the result restores a mana per point of Dream or rank of Prime, whichever is lower, as well as the normal willpower.

                        2nd: Osmosis

                        Req.: Gnosis 5, Prime 3

                        Attempts to contact the Famished Soul shares the need for supernal power before any other contact may be made. This strong draw means that any attempts to touch the Famished soul with active magic, which even includes scrutiny with Mage Sight, requires an increased mana cost. The cost increases by two mana or, when the Famished's soul itself is the target, by the Dream rating of the Famished.

                        Furthermore, whether due to some form of scarring or exercise, the Famished Soul is far sturdier than normal. Attempts to manipulate it are not merely more expensive, they are harder to accomplish. Any activation rolls made to target the Famished Soul are penalized by the soul's Dream rating.

                        Non-supernal contact transfers a similar need, sympathetically, as well. This is interpreted differently by different types of souls. The common theme is that the psyche of the affected one suddenly shares a need for a concrete connection to a higher nature or purpose. Prometheans associate this longing to the Magnum Opus; Vampires usually associate it to blood, because that is stolen life, though some of the more mystically minded may feel it as part of their magic. Others associate it with all the noble things that they lost, such as the sun. Werewolves generally link it to a patron spirit. The effect hits hard, though. Without success finding something that excites the draw to the Supernal, these characters temporarily gain or exacerbate the derangement Depression, or other effect appropriate for needing to find a higher purpose. This is not an attack and is not perceived as one, but rather a sort of common feeling. If this impulse can be acted upon within a scene per dot of Composure, this effect never takes place.

                        Optional : Mind 3

                        Any supernatural contact with the Famished's mind, soul, or emotions leads those who made the contact stuck with a lingering longing for higher things. This lasts a day per level of the Famished's Mind + the interloper's Intelligence. The longing represents a need to feel higher things, beyond normal considerations. Failure to achieve success in touching the supernal costs a point of Composure each day, which heals as resistant Aggravated wound. Failure to even attempt to contact also deals a loss of Stamina. These are cumulative and lasting, but only a single event is required to allow healing. The problem is that the need is for a thing that may well be unrecognized by the one feeling it. Seeking is enough to avoid the worst of this, it's true, but success requires that a character find some sublime truth. For Sleepers and creatures unfamiliar with higher concepts, merely seeking out sublime art or religious experience can be enough, but it must be profound. Most is insufficient. True love certainly functions. Awakening is a distinct possibility from the search. For creatures with souls or spirits bound apart from the supernal in some way, such as werewolves, this requires that they seek insight from higher things. Quests deep into shadow to speak with celestial creatures certainly qualifies. For magi, this is actually simultaneously easier and harsher. For their partial knowledge of the truth leaves them only allowed to use quests for supernal truth, but they know exactly what it is that they crave. (Alternately: The mind is hardened, as well, requiring the extra mana be spent to manipulate the mind or emotions. Those that cannot spend mana are less affected, and may overcome the need instilled by spending a willpower.)

                        3rd: Parched Soul

                        Req.: Gnosis 7, Prime 4

                        Unless the Famished has full mana, his soul draws it from around itself every round. This literally is no action on the part of the character and cannot be suppressed. The least resistant source is drawn from first. The Famished's Mana capacity is also increased by Prime + Willpower rating.

                        Resistances are defined as follows: (Use this as a guideline to define any types missed.)
                        The effective resistance increases by one if not in direct contact, and then by a further one per yard away.                         Free mana is the most available. Entering the area of a Hallow drains a point of mana and deactivates that ambient mana reduction when this effect is active. Tass and vessels made for sharing tass are next. These both count as having a resistance of 0, however. Artifact resistances are their rating -5, but this can be modified by the function of the artifact. After this is mana stored as part of patterns. Objects use their Structure as their Resistance, while Mages use their Gnosis. Ravens with this second attainment add their Dream to their Resistance. Sleepers with mana injected into their patterns have no resistance. Spells cast use their potency, but only mana spend funding them (and not the cost from this attainment or that used to suppress Paradox) can be drawn.

                        Optional Mind 4

                        Mana and willpower are interchangeable when mana rating is full. The limits on which types can be spent per round and per action, but the a mana spent can be used as a willpower or vice versa. Furthermore, whenever willpower is fully refreshed through a virtue, the Famished recovers a mana, and has the option of transforming up to his current (before recovery) pool of willpower into mana. (Limited, of course, by maximum mana capacity.)


                        The Ravenous

                        The Ravens seek to align their souls to the Far Shores. By doing so, they pick at a scab on their souls—their thirst for higher things. To quench this thirst requires that they use magic and mana in a way other magi cannot. This provides a form of power that most cannot duplicate, either, but it is dangerous..

                        Oblations: Gathering mana, transcendental meditations to seek higher truth. Literally, the act of finding links to higher truth and even gathering mana can qualify as a legacy oblation. The requirement is that the search is active and should reveal sublime truth, mana, or both. This means, among other things, that magi practicing their path oblations can roll for their legacy at the same time.

                        1st: Pangs

                        Reqs. Prime 2, Dream 1, Resolve 3

                        The hunger begins. The new raven must face their inner needs head-on before beginning this quest, which requires a strong Resolve. The hunger is for higher truth, and those needs are of a vastly higher priority than the mundane. The soul of the raven absolutely requires the touch of the supernal as a mortal requirement. This means certain mundane actions lose their significance unless refined by some tie to higher truth. As a result, the Raven must perform a refined action per level of Dream, each day, or they begin to suffer from a loss of their connection with the supernal, ceasing to benefit from the merely mundane. Each day of deprivation increases the severity, removing the benefit from a widening array of actions. Eating is the first act to cease providing benefit without being refined. Then social contact. After that, sleep. Next, water. Finally, breathing. Should they somehow manage to survive, they degrade ever farther, losing mundane senses, and heat from anything but the sun. The raven may still refine an act to gain benefit from it, even in a degraded state. Fulfilling the requirements for a day lead to the status walking back in severity by one step each day. A Raven thus finds their Dream a mixed blessing, given the rarity of opportunities to engage in sufficiently refined activities.
                        (Option: Required refinements don't count against the daily total?)

                        Refining an act requires a tie to a sublime concept, but needn't require anything mystical. Eating provides a great example. Holiday feasts often qualify as something sufficiently profound, though many do not. That depends on the family and attitudes involved. If the Raven cannot achieve the required refinement any other way, a mana can be spent to sanctify it. Those with insight will notice the difference. If one spends the mana and achieves sufficient profundity, the mana is recovered.

                        The sight of a raven is keen to the Supernal, which functions as Supernal Vision with a potency equal to the Raven's Prime. When the dream of a higher reality is brought into the dream of the present, there is a keen moment of connection for anyone touched by it. The raven can grant a glimpse of his need to any other caster through a spell targeting his own pattern. The casting of a spell onto a raven requires that caster to reflexively pay a mana per level of attainment that the raven has achieved. (1 at 1st, 2 at 2nd, 3 at 3rd) The raven can suppress this effect as a reflexive action for a round, but not if the effect targets their mind or soul. It is unclear what effect this recollection would have on beings without memory of the higher plane or with the ability to actually visit it. Some posit those without a memory would simply be unaffected, while those who could visit would find themselves either affected as normal or affected with a strong urge to go back. The final answer is, of course, up to the storyteller. Or, see the "Famished" above for some other thoughts.

                        2nd: Gaunt

                        Reqs. Prime 3, Dream 3, Composure 3

                        The raven's soul is tougher for its clear sight of its own nature and the longstanding trials it endures. The effect is a Soul Shield, but uses the raven's Dream rating as the penalty applied towards the die roll in question. The potency is equal to the mage's Prime. Attempts to draw mana from the mage's reserve must overcome this penalty, as well, given the strong need the raven has for this connection above. This applies to all mana inside the mage's pattern, and cannot be ignored or suppressed, even when the raven is the party drawing it out. If the mage uses magic to draw and transfer from an external source to an external target, the penalty can be avoided, since the mana never enters the Raven's pattern.

                        Opt. Mind 3

                        The raven's mind is protected as well as his soul. An effect equivalent to Mental Shield, except that it uses Mind as its potency, and Dream as the penalty to such attacks.

                        3rd: Vortex

                        Reqs. Prime 4

                        When the raven lacks mana in their pattern, their pattern drains it automatically each round. One point per dot of Dream is drained from any given mana containers each round. This does not require an action, but is a standard part of their attainment. Mana is drawn from simplest source to most difficult. The difficulty is based on several factors. Tass and vessels for mana such as artifacts tend to give their mana up the most freely. The resistance of other mages is based on their Gnosis, while objects resist with their Structures. Ravens with the second attainment count as having Gnosis + Dream resistance. The raven may suppress this effect with concentration when their mana is below maximum, or activate it as a reflexive action even when it is full. This is good for drawing mana into other objects without a spell required to pull the mana free or spending a penalty for combined spell casting. The raven may also perform a number of Legacy oblations each day equal to Dream. All such oblations must, of course, be refined actions.

                        Opt Mind 4

                        A raven can no longer regain willpower through any unrefined action. Fulfilling one's virtue provides a point of mana in addition to the refilling of willpower. On refined actions, the raven can spend willpower and mana interchangeably. The raven remains limited to the normal mana limits per round and only one willpower can be spent per action, but the points can come from either pool. (A Raven with this attainment, a temporary willpower pool of 9, and the ability to spend 15 mana per round could take 9 of it from his temporary willpower pool. Only one of those could go towards a standard willpower benefit.)

                            Right, it's been a few months, but since the core came out I figure people are going to want to dust this topic off. Speaking of which, here are a few Left-Handed ideas I had for antagonists:

                            •The Correctionalists: A well-intentioned (but still utterly monstrous) Mastigos Reaper Legacy that, uniquely enough, is less interested in the souls themselves and more the people they've reaped. More specifically, they use Death to both take the soul and then freeze their target's degradation, exploiting both a quirk of soul loss and their optional Mind Attainments to render people exhibiting "unacceptable" or self-destructive behaviors into model members of society.

                            •The Dorian Brotherhood: A group of Reapers who can actually use photography, video recordings, and other representational media to steal souls, using Death and Space in order to pull off a "Dorian Grey" sort of immortality, pushing off age, negative Conditions, and breaking points off to the decaying soul-pictures.

                            •The Arbiters of Death: A Reaper Legacy of insane "advocates for the dead" who hunt down people who have wronged or dead or who are "unworthy" of life, ripping out their soul, leaving the victim vulnerable to the Possession or Claiming attempts of ghosts the Arbiter has deemed to deserve a second chance at flesh. They then reforge these souls into "living ghosts", enslaving them as punishment for their "crimes".

                            •The Incarnate Will: A heretical Lich offshoot of the Silver Ladder, members of this Legacy believe that Mages are the nascent divine aspect of humanity, and use Mind not only to direct their worshippers as is their "right", but also to further their goal to ascend into a Awakened soul clad in nothing but Astral power, a functional deity that "guides" humanity from inside their collective soul.

                            •The Cult of a Thousand Eyes: A Left-Handed Legacy that is none the less popular amongst Panopticon (and can pop up in the Guardians from time to time) that forms cults and then use Space to turn the act of worship into a sympathetic link, allowing them to use their followers as anchors and targets for their magic with ease, especially when borrowing their senses.

                                                    Patrons of the Palace: A Fate/Mind Mastigos Legacy, Guardians of the Veil or Silver Ladder. Members of this Legacy, colloquially referred to as 'Greys', per Gris Eminence, take the view that the best way to ensure a stable and productive society is not to solve others' Vices, as a Concord of Serpents might, but instead to ensure that everyone is sufficiently competent to meet the tasks ahead of them, whether they wish their help or not. Their attainments enable them to see the incompetence of others with respect to important upcoming tasks, and they tend to pick a protegé that is woefully mismatched with the task requirements ahead of them. They gain proficiency with respect to their protegé's task and have the ability to temporarily endow them with skills (or in a Left-Handed variant, strip them of skills). Of course, the ability to constantly see the failings of others around them tends to lead to an extreme bitterness or a controlling demeanor, and it's a much more self-sacrificing Legacy than initiates might believe. While a burgeoning Grey will swiftly be bombarded with favours they can easily spin into political clout, truthfully the senior members of the Legacy tend to become more apolitical, often dropping out of Orders all together to become an independent adviser, the classic hermit mentor archetype. Their initiation consists of entering the Oneiros of someone they hate or consider worthless, and reshaping it without magic to improve them.
