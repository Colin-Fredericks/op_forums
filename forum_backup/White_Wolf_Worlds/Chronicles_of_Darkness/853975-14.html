    Originally posted by SaulottheGentle View Post
    If I may suggest a small idea, what if Followings covered mainly the five aspects most WoD games follow? Leadership/Social, Warriors/Fighters, Lore and Secret Keepers, Tricksters and Spies, and finally those who follow human development and the like? I know it may be a little cliche, but most of the other gamelines seem to have the pattern for either X or Y splats. I can also admit Siren sort of does the same for the "Good" currents.

    Ideas on this
    The Leader/Social Following could be the somewhat blatant religion that you know from the old world. They take the forms of lost gods from dead religions, adopting the forms that people know of and can look up to. For example, in Scandanavia, most Leader Following Demigods could take the form of the Aesir or Vanir in Viking Mythology. I can see this being a mix of the Followings of Heirs and Sparks.

    The Warriors and Fighters are both the gods of conflict and war as much as harmony and peace. Their Following is perhaps the most involved in the human state of matters, as they're the ones who are most likely to try and keep the state of affairs of their cults to be peaceful and smiting down enemies to the cult with great prejudice. This could be a mix of the Following of Banners and Descendants.

    The Lore and Secret Keepers are the Gods you imagine when it comes to hierarchal societies. The Gods are always up top, and the members usually seek enlightenment through the god. Those with greater amounts of enlightenment tend to be amongst the Priests or greater levels of Halysis. However, their thirst for knowledge and enlightenment aren't quenchable and it always goes back up to the god up top too. In a sense, think of them like a less malignant Hermaus Mora from the Elder Scrolls. This could be a mix of the Followings of Vassals and Eidolon.

    The Trickster and spies are the Gods that you automatically think of when it comes to secret societies or cults. They're the gods who hold large amounts of influence and exert it on others.Another idea I had was that these kinds of gods do decieve and lie to their own followers, but those who manage to work their way past the lies and tricks the god puts up, they tend to be able to move up to greater levels of importance. They could be a mix of the Followings of the Morning-Star and Ascendants.

    And finally, the Human Development Gods. These gods tend to be less "God" and more like forces of nature, lines of psychology, they embody the way the human world tries to grasp on natural and unnatural phenomena, and trying to change themselves in turn to human development and belief of the phenomena. Heck, maybe even the Following tries to lead the development. Afterall, a man of science reads hundreds of books and believe that there's more to learn, which in turn can give more potentia to a Demigod. The ideas I have atm is that this could be a mix of the Followings of Annunaki and Embodiments.


    Of course, these are just ideas, feel free to disregard them. I'm just stating some suggestions on the number of followings. If you do take these ideas into account though, perhaps some of the ideas for Followings could still be used for Z-Splats?
    As much as I like the stuff that you've said here (and I do, the religion/following ideas are great) I kind of want demigodly society to be among the most fractious of any supernaturals in the CoD. My vision of it is that they although they have these big, organised Followings, unlike Mage's Orders or Werewolf's Tribes, demigodly Followings fight against each other more than with each other. They really do launch crusades in the Drasil against other Followings to steal their Icons and Gardr because each one believes that they are right to do so. Even within FOllowings you get factions which declare other factions 'heretical' and try to wipe them out. That's not to say that there aren't demigods who object to that, but when faith and belief are such big things in your life that they literally empower you to work miracles, they become things that all too many demigods live by. Their faith is what keeps a lot of them going through the decades and possibly centuries, when all their power does nothing to help those around them and only indoctrinates mortals into their service, their faith is what they cling to, as people do. Their faith that I will return to the service of God, I will become transcendent, I will be freed from my human failings. (Those are the followings of Vassals, Ascendants and Embodiments respectively)

    As I say, I think that mood could definitely be achieved with fixed factions and then splinter-factions within them, but I'm not sure it would be so easy to get the 'warring religions' thing. I don't know. I'll consider.

                    Here's the first finished Aspect: Creation. Any thoughs on it would be greatly appreciated. Don't hesitate to point out flaws if you see them though.

                    Creation
                    The Aspect of the Mother
                    The Progenitors
                    The Builders



                    [e.g. Gaea, Amun, Ra, Izanagi, Baldur, Tiamat, Odin, Pan Gu, Brahma]

                    Introduction

                    Throughout history, every faith, mythology and religion has recognised the divinity of creation. All of them recognise the miracle of birth and every faith has some kind of creator god or fertility deity. Even the ancient animistic faiths had spirits which brought plenty and aided in childbirth. Creation is the greatest miracle allotted to all life: the ability to create more of its kind, to build something that will last beyond itself.

                    Other than birth, another creative event present in every mythology is the very creation of the world, almost always carried out by powerful and intelligent forces. Pan Gu, having hatched from his world-egg, held the sky and earth apart long enough for the heavy elements of earth and water to sink to form land and oceans and for the world to become stable enough to stand without him. Ra created himself from the primordial waters and, by will, raised the first mountain from the sea of chaos.

                    That said, one need only look at Gaea in Greek mythology, Tiamat in the Mesopotamian tales or at the sacrifices and rites performed in honour of countless fertility gods to see that creation need not be a benevolent force.

                    Ascension

                    Those who Ascend through the Aspect of Creation are most often artistic and driven souls beforehand, and the days and weeks leading up to their metamorphosis are almost always characterised by a lack of inspiration and success and a growing frustration with themselves and the world around them. Perhaps they are an artist who                     find that they can’t come up with anything to paint. Maybe they’re a businessman whose investments keep falling through. They might even be a highschooler whose significant other keeps standing them up.

                    All this leads up to a breaking point, where the proto-demigod feels utterly crushed by his frustrations and failings. And then a light comes. They feel something ignite inside. Their will and resolve return and the lightning-fire of                     Potentia first burns through them, re-shaping their body even as inspiration strikes them. Their faith in themselves is re-kindled, but the world still remains flawed, to their sight.

                    Drive

                    The drive of the Progenitors is to create. They cannot help but see the world in terms of what could be made or built upon, beside or in place of that which already exists. Although they are inherently creative, they find it difficult to help but see the flaws in what is and not think that they could build better, regardless of the truth of the matter (altogether too many Builders fancy themselves master architects when they are, at best, mediocre, if that).

                    The Progenitors are often found in positions where they can exercise their creative urges. Architecture and the arts are perennial favourites, but many take up careers in politics or business, where they can build the closest things to empires possible in this modern age. It is rare to find a Builder without means, as even those in professions which would normally see them living hand-to-mouth such as a commissions artist tend to prosper, using Miracles or the effects of Halysis to sell their work. In general, whether by innate talent or supernatural power, Progenitors tend to be trend-setters rather than followers and, like all demigods, chafe at working under others.

                    Some of the Aspect of Creation aim far higher than others, seeking to shape not only the                     present, but the world for years, decades and centuries to come. Some take up causes which they believe in - perhaps animal welfare, perhaps humanitarian causes such as AquAid - and make themselves the face of them (or at least their local branch). They try to make sweeping changes, hoping to make the adjustments in the collective consciousness that are required for lasting change.

                    Not all Builders are so benevolent, though. In fact, they are the Aspect most likely to build Cults (although not necessarily under that name) in the open. More than a few third-world dictators have Ascended to this Aspect and it is rare to find a Builder without a powerful vision of how this world should be. And where such visions exist, it is all too common to find someone who is willing to go to extraordinary - and often monstrous - lengths to see them through.

                    Followings

                    The chosen Followings of the Demigods of Creation are most often those which look towards the future and see the Empyrean state as being a blessing of some kind. Of the most common Followings, the Followings of Ascendants, Embodiments, Sparks, Banners and the Eidolon are the ones most often chosen, although a Progenitor following the creed of the Annunaki, the Vassals or the Heirs is rarely enough to raise eyebrows.

                    Cults

                    The Cults of the Builders tend to be some of the largest, as it’s far easier to convince someone that you are good when you are trying to build something great than when you are tearing down anything that you disagree with. The Builders often try to recruit from those who have (and can lend) the power to build something new. They often count business leaders, politicians and similar among their followings, or at least more so than the other Aspects.

                    Cults to the Aspect of Creation are often some of the most structured, as the demigod at their heart tries to build them as best they can. They often end up with complex hierarchies of rank and holiness, with equally complex games of power as cultists compete for the favour of the demigod. In this way, the cults of the Progenitors are at once both rigidly structured and chaotic, as cultists rise and fall.

                    More commonly than in other cults (perhaps save those of the demigods of Order), the cults of the Builders tend to have rich and deep mythologies, in which the demigod is, if not the principal actor, then at least a major character. These are not necessarily traditional mythologies, as they are most often based on the demigod’s Following and incorporate significant figures from its mythology, but they are universally compelling. Many Builder cults will gather on sacred holidays and recite tales of their deeds and those of their patron. More than a few archive these stories, turning them into a growing mythos of the God and his Blessed Followers.

                    As the Builders themselves are - generally speaking - divided between those who hold dominion over nature and those who rule the creations of humankind, their cults tend to be similarly aligned. Many of the cults of the Progenitors revere the demigod as a manifestation of the power of nature, but just as many see him as something more approaching a messiah, come to deliver them and the rest of humanity from its own sin and evil and to create a better tomorrow.

                    Domains

                    Perhaps unsurprisingly, the Builders typically manifest Domains which are creative in nature, with a not-insignificant number being patrons of the arts and sciences. However, there is a demographic split in the Aspect between the demigods who mostly hold Domains concerned with human endeavours and those who hold Domains over the growth and flourishing of nature and the natural world.

                    Common Domains include plants, animals, inspiration, light, health, earth, crafts, metal and water. Relatively few Builders manifest the powers of the ephemeral elements of fire and air and tend not to have power over the dead, although power over the denizens of the Shadow is not unheard of.

                    Concepts

                    Charismatic cult leader, corporate empire-builder, master sculptor, visionary terrorist, eco-warrior, famous architect
                    Stereotypes
                    • Destruction: Our natural opposites, and yet how can one make art without a clean canvas?
                    • Chaos: A stagnant civilization is destined for destruction. That’s what these guys are for.
                    • Order: Now these guys are like publishers. You need them to sort out your stuff but they always edit.
                    • Vampires: Not in my empire.
                    • Werewolves: These guys understand the importance of territory. And every king needs hunting dogs.
                    • Mages: Every emperor needs advisors and philosophers. Just make sure you know what they’re up to behind your back.
                    • Beasts: Our opposite number, us gods to their monsters. It’s always good to have a stick, though, for when the carrot isn’t enough. They're good for that.
                    • Mummies: Undead servants of dead gods, keeping alive the memory of a dead empire, when they can. I wonder if mine will be remembered as long.
                    • Mortals: How can you stand living in something you didn’t build with your own hands? I don’t know how I ever managed.
