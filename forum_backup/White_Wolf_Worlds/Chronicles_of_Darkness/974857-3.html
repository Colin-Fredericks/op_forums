    Originally posted by Strill View Post
    It's not enough to have a psychic power. You also have to know what you're targeting, and have a power that can make a mage give up. Plenty of spirits or goetia can make you angry, or want to do drugs or something, but that's not going to accomplish anything useful in this situation. Most importantly, they still can't injure you without a very specific numina or influence that precisely counters your spell. Also, vampires are out because they have to make eye contact.
    You have to see the target, but only mages I believe really need to know what they're targeting, and not entirely with them. Eye contact is necessary only for Dominate, and possibly Face of the Beast, a Nightmare power. One could just use Majesty to lure the mage back into physicality. Regarding the ephemerals, for instance with Anger you could invoke the Berserk Condition, which makes it significantly harder for a mage to cast spells.

    A player with Death 4 can shift into ghost twilight, and turn into living shadow. Spirits can't see him. Goetia can't see him. Most supernal/abyssal beings can't see him. Most mages can't see him. Vampires can't see him. Werewolves can't see him. Changelings can't see him. Prometheans can't see him. Any other monster that's not some kind of ghost can't see him. Anything that IS a ghost still might not be able to see him if he's in a patch of darkness, still can't physically hurt him, and needs specific mental or soul-based numina to even so much as interact with him.
    A character can, otherwise this book would be selling a lot more. Death-attuned ephemerals can see him, this includes death spirits, and there is a nice ability that allows werewolves to see such beings. But we're back to this. Your character is hiding. They aren't hiding out of bravery, are they? They're hiding because they're scared of whatever they're facing.

    That's way too specific to build your campaign around.

    And because the player's abilities are so powerful, the only way to challenge them is to hard-counter their defenses.
    It really isn't. Your necromancer is dealing with this problem for a reason, it's something they are interested in, it's something involved with them and is likely to have abilities in line with theirs, otherwise how did they get to this point? If not, and said necromancer is for some reason investigating a lightning demon, now that the necromancer has run away, a lightning demon can sizzle off and continue zapping people in peace, because running away doesn't defeat antagonists. Or alternatively we go back to the Freddy Krueger situation, where it doesn't matter if you turn into a shadow and slip into Twilight, because it's already done its damage while you were asleep. You're going to have to track them down through the clues in the dead they've left behind.

    You referred to 'good horror movies' in your original post. You know these do not start with the main character in a room with the monster and about to end it. Unless it's about to start a flashback 'how did we get here' sort of story. There's a lead-up. There's a reason a character finds themselves in that position. There's build-up, 'rising action.' When the monster is hidden in the shadows, it doesn't stick around waiting to be identified. It's hidden in the shadows for reasons, and for other reasons the protagonist finds themselves poking at those shadows. For instance in the movie Cloverfield, which popped up in a search for monster movies. The monster isn't seen fully at first, only the devastation it brings as the characters try to find a way to escape and survive. They see the smaller creatures that spawn from it, infecting others. Knowing what the monster looks like does not change the need to survive, to run from angry citizens or protect yourself from weird bugs. And yes, you won't be hurt if you turn into a shadow or shift into Twilight, but you'll be watching it all happen around you. Skipping over Pacific Rim, you have Descent. The horror there isn't just from the morlock-like creatures. Their sudden appearance is certainly a fright, but the rest of the terror involves the cramped conditions of the location, their knowledge and experience hunting in the caves compared to the protagonists, who are lost, surrounded and outnumbered, and beset by problems within the group.

    Again, these are things one does for all the games. These are things writers have done throughout fiction, in all manner of media. Running mage is no different. You, the storyteller, are not running blind. You should know what would count as obstacles for the player characters. If you choose not to use them, that is not the game's fault.

        One must be careful to not fall into the common trap when discussing Mage where one assumes that one mage can do everything in the book. Even assuming that the average group is going to have Arcana at 4 or higher is a stretch. Advancement isn't very fast in these games.

        Creative Thaumaturgy is also pretty restricted when it comes to supernatural things that aren't Supernal. It's limited to both Practices and Purviews. Knowing can't really give you information on supernatural powers of any creature that isn't under an appropriate Purview. So basically just Goetia, Ghosts, and Spirits. Or, more accurately, the book leaves it up to the Storyteller as to whether it can or can't. But even given that, most Horrors don't fall neatly into any particular Arcana.

        So in many ways, this isn't quite the issue that the OP suggests that it is.

        However, any group of PCs that manages to cover all or most of the Arcana at two dots (pretty easy when you can start with 2 dots in 3 Arcana; it doesn't really fall into the trap my first paragraph addresses) can basically make themselves effectively (barring Clash of Wills) untouchable with Shielding spells. And untouchable people are fairly immune to external Horror like this thread talks about.

        And hiding really has nothing to do with being scared, especially when you can magically tear the target apart while you do it. Snipers and assassins and predatory monsters aren't cowards. They're just being effective.

                            Active Spells for scene: 1 Indefinite Ghost Shield (which I ruled would give a clash of wills against disciplines; it was a mage game, after all. Would not always rule this way. As indefinitely up, would offer +4 in a clash of wills.)
                            1 Potency six Alter Life on self (giving +6 bonuses to stealth/camoflage and all relevant senses, and in another fairly generous ruling, to initiative.)
                            Shadow Form - or whatever that was called - that turns her into invulnerable shadow.

                            She had, for various reasons, kidnapped a young ghoul (mage custody battle - whoah). The vampire coming in had the following stats.
                            Obfuscate 3.
                            Brawl 2, Stealth 2, strength 2, BP3.
                            I ghoul retainer with Obfuscate 3.

                            Now, this is how it fell out.
                            The mage expected a backlash - so at night hung out at her sanctum protected as above.
                            In comes the Obfuscated Ghoul first. Mage wins clash of wills easy, blasts them from shadow form, taking 1 Pdox, but whatever. Body of the ghoul is now lying outside sanctum, Obfuscate broken.
                            Passer by notices it, calls an ambulance. Mage, quick thinking, changes into human form and comes out to try to move the body.
                            At which point, late arriving vampire sees mage. Narrowly misses Obfuscate clash of wills, but massively wins a mundane Stealth check. Attacks, goes into frenzy near dead ghoul, and entirely drains the mage in two rounds. She stays conscious due to death armour, but with 2 health left, gives in, and feeling generous, I let her wake at the start of next session in a garage somewhere, drugged and with an IV drip in her arm of Mekhet vitae.

                            But here's the thing: yes, we assumed the battle would be easy; with so much health (through nuDeath armour) and an easy spell to slip back into shadow form should it all go down. But even with weak stats, with blood pumping and willpower and frenzy, she was lucky enough to escape. We rerolled the battle afterwards a few times, but it kept coming up similar; either the mage put the vamp down quickly, or if the vamp got his hands on her - it was over pretty soon. Perhaps she shouldn't have left shadow form; might have gone down very differently, but she would have had to leave it at some point; and the way things fell out, she played the hand dealt her.

                            I don't know. Yes - a mage with all the cards in clean sight and loads of time will know everything. But right now, in front of you, in game - I don't show the players the sheets of the other characters. They know what their characters see - they don't read the script.

                                Originally posted by Strill View Post
                                It goes without saying that mages are going to encounter all kinds of monsters, but that kind of horror doesn't really work in Mage. Horror movie monsters rely on the unknown. Good horror movies keep the monster hidden in the shadows, and never reveal what it actually looks like until the end. Our imaginations are much scarier than anything a costume artist could come up with. But Mages have this covered.

                                Mages can see everything, pinpoint the nature and weaknesses of any given monster, and have all sorts of ways to become nigh-invulnerable, like turning into shadow, or shifting into twilight.
                                Mages can't see everything. Not all at once, anyway. They're still human. You can only have so much focus, only so much magic active without requiring Reach. A mage with half a dozen spells active is going to be risking serious consequences every time she casts a spell. Also, from this and your other responses, it seems you've fallen into the White Room Theory Master trap: assuming any given mage has any given spell and access to all kinds of knowledge. That doesn't happen. No one mage knows everything. Heck, even with Occult 5 and a grip of specialties, a character still doesn't know everything. Mages need to study the supernatural, but that can be difficult when that inhuman force is currently trying to eat you. In-character knowledge is different from the numbers on the sheet, and even the most well-learned mages don't know it all. They might pretend they do, but that's probably why some mages don't live to become archmasters.

                                Shadows and Twilight-forms are not invulnerable. They might be to a particular threat, yes, but it's okay to just let the mage win in that case. It becomes less about the horror of the monster in that scene and more about the clash of magical powers. And even then there's plenty of room to surprise the mage. Shift into shadow form? Great, but the problem is that shadowy owl-thing you're dealing with is much more at home in that state. The spirit's Influence still reaches you. Maybe you use Know Spirit to learn its Ban and Bane, and now you know its weaknesses. That doesn't save you from the streams of chemical fire it's belching your way right now. With time and preparation, yes, you can handily deal with this pack of ghouls, but you don't know where it's coming from, and it's hard to study them properly when they're trying to kill you and eat you, and your neighbors.

                                Also don't forget that "horror" can come from revelation as much as the unknown. Let's take the first Alien as an example. A mage would have a much easier time seeing into the dark and in general fending off the creature, right? Now add in that director's cut scene where Ripley encounters Dallas and Brett cocooned by the Alien and being slowly transformed into eggs. That's where your horror is. The mage will find those unfortunate souls, but the revelation of what this thing is capable of, even if it's not quite capable of doing it to her, is horrific. Like I said in one of my responses, attack the things a mage holds dear if she herself can't be challenged. To what lengths will the character go? What if that makes her the monster?

                                Things can still be scary even if you know a lot about them. You might be an expert in lions, but having one attacking you is still pretty terrifying. But don't fall into the trap of assuming the mage is an instant expert and has every answer needed on hand. You might have great power over shadows, and so you have a powerful weapon against that creature made of smoke and shadows, but its ability to manipulate the same can directly challenge yours. Some Mage Sight will help you understand the threat a little better, but it's not a guaranteed win. And when you do finally persevere, but you didn't kill your foe because it possessed a corpse and fled, or you destroyed it only temporarily, or realize there are others....

                                Horror can be a little more difficult to pull off, yes, since mages have power and insight. However, that also means they can see the teeth in the shadows, and all the danger that entails. They know that the parasites eating a man's insides came from some half-flesh, half-spirit abomination, and that their magic can't cure it. At best, the mage can keep him alive with an indefinite spell of regeneration, but it may be too late. Or even her magic can't help the man's husband, who has been cocooned and transformed into another of those wasp-like things. And now the mage must kill or trap (for study, of course, totally humane experimentation...) something with the eyes and voice of a tortured human soul but the hideous body of a wasp.

                                    Fun addendum to what I said before: You can also prey on the fact you will have multiple Mages with different Sights, Gnosis, and knowledge.                                             Making monsters horrifying is without a doubt harder when dealing with Mages than Vanilla mortals, after all being trapped in a dead end alley with six flesh eating zombies foot dragging their way towards you and nothing but your fists and teeth to protect you is terrifying, being trapped in that same alley with a working mini gun and the skills to use it...not so much.
                                            ​You can try to tailor the monsters to your characters or design the conditions and environment to inhibit their strengths, techniques which may come across as too contrived or frustrating to enjoy, but my recommendation is to focus more on the investigative approach with your story. Focus not on fighting the monster but make most of the work about identifying and finding it. I also recommend more than one monster to give players opportunities to fight and win without ending the story, while using lesser threats to emphasize the final, greater monster waiting at the end.
                                            ​I also recommend skipping the axe wielding stereotype only interested in chopping up any teenagers who step into its path, instead use monsters who have plans and agendas of their own that are already works in progress when the Mages cross paths. The initial horror comes from what the monster has already done, quickly followed by the realization of what it is working to achieve, only at the end does it jump out of the shadows (or the Mages tread lightly into those shadows) and fall back on its ugly face to terrify.
                                            ​To me Mages are about investigations, combat is just the occasional distraction and the last act and if violence alone can't save the day all the better.
