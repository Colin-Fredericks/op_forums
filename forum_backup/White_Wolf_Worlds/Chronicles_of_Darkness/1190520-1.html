        Okay wyrdhamster, I'll try again.

        There is nothing inherently wrong with just a Thyrsus being able to perceive the Mystery.
        This time, it is a Mystery in Pandemonium, next time, it will be one in Stygia. Everyone gets their time to shine. All's well.

        You can still make it so that you need an Obrimos to get them to the spot from which they can perceive it. You can make it so that the gained knowledge is in code: your Thyrsus can perceive it, yes, but not understand it. Make it a schematic, a map, a crypto-key the group's Mastigos deciphers and copies down. You can make it so that the gained knowledge requires the party's Moros to create a specific perfected material to create the object of the schematic, which could be just a sort of compass leading you to the next mystery, where your Acanthus can open a time-portal to acquire a relic or Grimoir.

        You can make it so that the Mystery within Pandemonium is veiled with Spirit magic - your Mastigos needs the Thyrsus' help taking that down first, despite the latter being unable to see it. However, in an effort of teamwork, he can lend the Mastigos his understanding of Spirit, guiding him much as Tower Control guides the untrained person that takes over the plane as the pilots collapsed for whatever reason. The Thyrsus tries to guide his Cabal mate through symbols and meanings that the latter doesn't understand at all. Let them roll teamwork, at -2 or -3, to give the Scrutinizing Mage a dice bonus. This is hard work, an uphill battle. That makes success all the sweeter.


        As long as seeing the Supernal Mystery that only one path can perceive isn't the absolute core or pinnacle of the story, there isn't a single problem with it from my point of view.
        And since you're the one creating the Mystery in the first place, that should be entirely in your hands.

        And if you don't like it: fine, don't use it. Houserule it that having access to Mind 1 means you can view the Pandemonium Mystery as if you were Mastigos. Houserule it so that with Mind 3, you can share your Mind-Sight with a Themed Cabal.
        Ditch what doesn't work for your table. That's always been the golden rule of ChroD.
        You're the ST. It's your job to give every player a chance to shine. You control what they can find, what that means, what they need to do to piece together everything to figure out what was hidden. Do that in such a way everyone has a great experience. Everything else is secondary.



        RE your claims of Prometheans being simply able to make an Alchemical Pact, I am afraid that is wrong. Only Prometheans on the Path of Lead are capable of Branding a Throng. So yes, you DO need special powers. Or access to someone with those special powers. And Prometheans are rare...

        If every Werewolf can learn the Rite from the beginning, but effectively has to buy it, that doesn't make it free, now does it? It's a 2-dot rite, too. Someone within the group has to give up on a 2-dot Rite so the group can participate in the "Game's main group's activity". How is that any different from getting an Imbued Item?

        Furthermore, I don't see how or why exclusive access to certain Mysteries denies the 'Games's main group's activity'. In fact, I would claim it underlinesthe need for cooperation to uncover secrets - after all, a Cabal exists so that they can uncover Supernal Mysteries, and why would you need others if you could do it all on your own? This is exactlythe kind of mechanical system supporting/encouraging/requiring team cooperation that you claimed Beast was lacking, and praised in e.g. Werewolf.

        Also: you don't need to include those very specific Mysteries, now do you? There are still plenty other options. You're literally getting stuck on an option, not the 'most basic game plot' to make it work. Plus as I tried to show above, there are plenty of ways to make it work, from a spot-lighting experience to make one player stand out for a while, to outright teamworking experiences.
