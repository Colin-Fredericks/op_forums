    [Flavor] How to do a Mixed Werewolf/Wolf-Blooded/Human pack

    05-19-2014, 04:55 PM
    So first of all, sorry if this is posted earlier in the forum, I haven't checked OP forums for nearly 3 months.

    So my question is mostly just flavor and how to set up and describe a chronicle which features a pack with players playing more than just werewolves.

    So to frame my question is a description for what I planned to do for a Werewolf game set in Western Washington:

    -The players would play as werewolves from whatever tribe and none of them would know each other because they were each picked up by a different pack in different areas.

    - The Players would be put together in the middle of a forest, dropped off by the pack that found them and are expected to form a pack.

    - The story would be about surviving together and starting a pack and all the challenges that that would be about, while the following stories would be about the challenges of a pack carving out their place in the world and about the Hunt.


    With an update from Onyx Path about how packs can and often do have both wolf-blooded and human pack mates (just often without either of them being in the inner "family" circle of the pack, I was considering offering the players a choice if they wanted to play either a wolf-blooded or a Human at the character creation session. The one caveat would be that at least half would be Werewolves (with six players that would be 3 but I would prefer at least 4).


    So my question would be: would that work since wolf-Blooded are rarely allowed in the inner circle and as far as I could tell nearly never the human. The main theme that I am building for this chronicle the many challenges of the pack with pack life being the main feature for many of the following stories.

    My second question would be how to include Wolf-Blooded and Human Player Characters in a pack because I have a feeling W-B and Human PCs are a lot different than NPCs in how both the player's and I deal with them.


    Frequent Story Teller for the Circle of Five gaming group.

                                                  Based on what has been teased so far, humans would be the ones "maintaining the camp" and doing the back up research, wolf blooded directing the camp's activities and being the intermediaries between human and Uratha (when the situation required) and possibly some light field support/reserve action. What I think could be interesting would be to have your players play both a werewolf character and a second human or wolf-blooded for when you want to shift Points of View and show what support work goes into any giver hunt. I've had success with that in past games.

                                                  Here's something else you could consider. In my first Forsaken game the ST would let me play the NPC's in a scene where my character wasn't present or was in the background (He'd play my character based on the way I had been playing unless I said to do something differently). It worked really well (got me extra acting XP, that was nice) and made the game more fun. He gave a brief description and I'd improvise away, it's kind of disturbing at how much fun I had acting as the scumbag characters but it was fun seeing the other players have specific reasons to dislike the various "bad guys". The other players liked it because it gave some diversity of voices to the antagonists, though none of them wanted to do it when offered the chance. I find if there's only one person playing all the bad guys they start to sound alike when they shouldn't (something I catch even myself doing as an ST, makes me feel like I'm shortchanging the players ).

                                                  I have seen ST's that didn't do that, all had some theatre/acting background, but a really active and vivid imagination should be enough. Let the players try that if you don't want them playing multiple full characters, I think it may help them feel more vested in the story if they have a direct hand in personalizing NPC's, it definitely worked that way for me.
