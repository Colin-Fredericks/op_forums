    I admit I'm hesitant to use Ego because that's what I'd use for a psychic morality trait if I ever did a Psychic splat. But lets bat that idea around a bit; if we used Ego how do you think the loss of Ego would manifest in gameplay?
    Because I think it sounds cooler, I'm going to use Ahamkara instead of Ego for this example, as Pendragon suggested.

    Ahamkara

    Mankind loves their emotional material ties. He loves that brand new company car, that watch his wife gave him, that homemade card crafted by his daughter. Because, to this mortal mind, these objects have meaning outside their usage, He subscribes qualities to them they do not exist in reality. The Wicked Tribe have a clearer vision, any recognizable emotions a Leviathan feels comes from their human or beastial nature. A Leviathan's divine mind is profoundly alien, devoid of any emotions as we might recognize them, perceiving only that which is true. Ahamkara is the talent of drawing forth that mortal deception, so that a Leviathan might sympathize with their subjects. Otherwise their behaviour appears inscrutable or worse, mindless as any beast, leading to the assumption that perhaps any divine wisdom or godlike intellect the Tribe possessed died with the Progenitors. Alien as they might be they are the gods. What gives mere mortals the right to see difference and call it lesser?

    (Yes I borrowed liberally from here. That's not a bad thing. It also allows us to keep Upwellings as presented in that post.)

    It's not quite Empathy, because no matter what that piece of yourself no longer exists. It's too far away, you've changed too much. But you can feel a higher calling, Upwelling within your blood. If you were ever truely human you have long since passed that point. But sometimes you can fool yourself, that you haven't changed, that all those small, petty, mortal things matter. That the new iphone is exciting, that your daughter's birthday is not another Tuesday and that faith is a matter of more than obedience to a greater power.
    But somewhere, deep down, you know that it's a lie. Nothing ever really changes.

        As for TvTropes - you'll have to be more specific.

        Sapience

        It is a cruel irony that divine beings should fear being reduced to mindless beasts, but The Tribe do fear. As the Tempest breaks against a Leviathan it drags him down with a terrible Undertow, a current that drags them down into the mindless depth. Swimming with a current is always easier than fighting it and a Leviathan who goes with the flow feels an instinctive satisfaction, but acting like a mindless beast has it's costs. Too much indulgence and a Leviathan may forget how to think like a man or a god, becoming a Typhon. A savage mindless monster.

        Sapience measures a Leviathan's descent. A leviathan with high Sapience can think as well as any human, perhaps even better. As Sapience falls a Leviathan becomes more minimalistic, at first they are easily overwhelmed by instincts and emotions but by the end thought and reason wash away.


        Undertows

        Every Leviathan has an Undertow deep within their psyche. It is the path to beast they might become, a genetic legacy, the lizard brain of a species that was ancient when the first lizards crawled from the sea. Or perhaps it is the dying thoughts of Tiamat still alive within her descendants. Or the Tribe's psychic trauma at their Progenitor's death. In the Tribe these concepts are not mutually exclusive.

        Whenever the Leviathan solves a problem in a primal monstrous manner befitting their Undertow they gain a Beat and a point of Willpower. However if they do so they must also roll to see if they loose Sapience.

        Ahamkara

        Mankind loves their emotional ties. He loves that brand new company car, that watch his wife gave him, that homemade card crafted by his daughter. He loves his wife and daughter. Because, to this mortal mind, these objects and people have meaning outside their usage, he subscribes qualities to them they do not exist in reality and sings praises to the goddess of love and the god of cars. The Wicked Tribe are not those gods, and they have a different way of perceiving the world. A Leviathan's divine mind is profoundly alien, devoid of any emotions as we might recognize them, perceiving only the practical utility in objects, and people. Ahamkara is the talent of drawing forth that mortal viewpoint, the domimence of the two mortal minds within the Leviathan's threefold nature, so that a Leviathan might sympathize with their subjects. Otherwise their behaviour appears inscrutable or worse, mindless as any beast, leading to the assumption that perhaps any divine wisdom or godlike intellect the Tribe possessed died with the Progenitors. But no matter how Alien they might be, the Tribe are gods. What gives mere mortals the right to see difference and call it lesser?


        Upwellings

        Within a Leviathan the divine nature is represented by an Upwelling. A spring that carries the Tribe's divine legacy up form the Rift and into the material world. A Leviathan who follows their Upwelling adopts the alien tyranny of their forbearer's becoming an Ophion, but power over mortals is intellectually satisfying. Every Leviathan has their own Upwelling, it is the god they might one day be. It is a set of instincts and drives that push the Leviathan into becoming that god. It is a fragment of their Progenitor. It is a throne in the primordial waters that is theirs by birth. It is perhaps all of those, or maybe none of them.

        Whenever the Leviathan solves a problem by using people in a manner befitting their Upwelling, as is their divine birthright, they gain a Beat and a point of Willpower. However if they do so they must also roll to see if they loose Sentience.

        Schools

        A Leviathan is a being of great and terrible power, surrounded by a cult of worrying zeal and questionable sanity. Consequently a Levithan's life is one of hardship, and no matter how instinctively satisfying it may be to use the tools given to them by their Birthright, enormous claws and psychic dominance, neither is appropriate in this complex and dangerous modern world of international politics and attack helicopters. And so The Tribe swim in Schools.

        A School is something that gives the Leviathan direction; a way of solving problems that does more than just buy time until the next crisis. It provides solutions that build upon each other, until at last the Leviathan finds a stable long term solution to their life's problems. Or at least, a School provides Leviathans with the illusion that they're moving towards a long term solution.

        Whenever the Leviathan solves a problem by using people in a manner befitting their School they gain an Evolutionary Beat. In addition they may roll to see if they regain Sapience or Ahamkara, if the solution involved the Leviathan's personal supernatural abilities they may regain Sapience. If it involved working through willing allies, pawns or Rituals they may gain Ahamkara. This can result in a situation where a Leviathan risks loosing or gaining a Dot in the same roll.

                                    After doing a bit of research into Aristotle I must revise my original assessment of terms for Integrity stats. Phronesis and Eunoia are absolutely perfect.

                                    In Aristotle's (translated) words Phronesis is, "wisdom to take counsel, to judge the goods and evils and all the things in life that are desirable and to be avoided, to use all the available goods finely, to behave rightly in society, to observe due occasions, to employ both speech and action with sagacity, to have expert knowledge of all things that are useful." If that isn't an ideal term to measure how bestial/non-sapient the Leviathan has become I don't know what is.

                                    Eunoia on the other hand is semi-synonymous with benevolence or goodwill possessed between others (literally "beautiful thinking"). In reference to rhetoric Eunoia is, "Goodwill towards an audience, either perceived or real; the perception that the speaker has the audience's interest at heart." In Leviathan terms this is a measure of the Leviathan's ability to perceive the interests of others, and put put them before himself. In this sense a character with high Eunoia would be, well, like a normal person. While one whose Eunoia has fallen low takes on sociopathic tendancies, finally becoming perfectly greedy, unable to recognize that others even possess interests, and quite incapable of caring.

                                    Relevant Synonyms:
                                    • Phronesis: Sagacity, Wisdom, Foresight
                                    • Eunoia: Benevolence, Goodwill, Selflessness

                                                            The Mother
                                                            The Mother exists for her children, she has given birth to heroes and monsters and will give birth to many more until the day she is dragged up from the depths. Her only wish is to see her children triumph. No matter if they wish to build empires, defeat champions or ruin nations, she will give them a world where they can flourish.

                                                            The Judge
                                                            The Judge does not wish to create a world without sin, no his motive is far more brutal. The Judge wishes to create a world where all sin is punished and all sinners face their judgement. So long as this is so, The Judge would be satisfied if the whole world turned against the gods and drowned,

                                                            The Lawgiver
                                                            In many ways the Judge's twin, the Lawgiver emerges from the depths with bronze tablets to create a world where all follow the laws within. He punishes harshly and rewards sparingly but when faced with the choice the Lawgiver would ignore one sinner to instil his commandments within two.

                                                            The Patriarch
                                                            Parisian without limits, The Patriarch will see his people thrive. He bestows his favor freely but never spares the teacher's rod when his people stray from their own best interests. To those who threaten the chosen people or just stand in their way The Patriarch sends his storms and his plagues.

                                                            The Tyrant
                                                            The Tyrant is involved with the day to day affairs of his kingdom or church, woe betide anyone who questions or disobeys his orders no matter how cruel. The Tyrant is a close cousin of The Patriarch, both are invested in the success of their chosen people or faith. They differ in the tools they use.

                                                            The Weaver
                                                            It is said Fate binds even the gods, but the Weaver believes she can play Fate at it's own games and master the future. But no Weaver plays for the love of the game, they have goals and plans which they enact through mortal pawns.

                                                            The Devil
                                                            Even the gods must acknowledge others, even if only other gods. The Devil defines herself by opposition, another church, another nation is her enemy. But she has no wish to destroy it for where would that leave her? The Devil instead spreads her corruption, turning her enemy against it's sacred values. Leaving it alive in name only as a testament to her victory.
