    Originally posted by xenoterracide View Post
    I've got nothing against Djinn, but this still sounds like Demon is currently, in many ways... because I believe Demon's make pacts largely as a matter of survival.
    But djinn don't make pacts in a metaphysical sense. "Granting wishes" means performing a service, not altering reality - eg, when (the original) Aladdin wished for a palace, the djinn didn't conjure one up, it built one (just that it was powerful enough to do so "normally" in a single night). Likewise, twisting wishes into ending badly wasn't a big theme - djinn who offered them were more likely to play straight - if they wanted to hurt you, they'd just hurt you (djinn compelled into service might fuck with you, but that's kinda legitimate, really).

    On the other hand, a djinn granting a wish was a common subset of another kind of story, where a character lucks into something great, and then shows by their subsequent actions whether they deserve it - if they don't, it turns on them, if they do, they go on to fame and fortume. In that sense, a djinn's wish might be less a trick than a test - while in folk tales, that's a meta thing rather than the djinn's Watsonian agenda, it could be something they're aware of in-game. Maybe interacting with humans - especially doing stuff for them - exaggerates the most telling parts of their character (ie, lays some condition relating to vice, virtue, and aspirations, forces the human to "show themselves" in some way, and focusses fate on them for good or ill).

    Not sure where to go from that, though, except if wishes are a test, they have to be one for the djinn as well. It would necessarily turn into a game about morality and consequence; Vampire is already one of those, but from an internal perspective rather than an external one.
