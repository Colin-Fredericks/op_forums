    So You Want To Be An Exorcist

    07-15-2016, 01:24 AM
    Here's a little guide on your options if you want to play some kind of exorcist in a Mortals or Mortals+ game. I'm making an assumption that you've read the Mortal Interactions part of the Ephemeral Beings rules.

    Part 0 - It Takes A Certain Kind of Person
    Not everyone is cut out for kicking Ephemeral Beings out of the human world. There's a pretty short list of traits that will let you easily justify any of the merits and such you'll need.

    1) You need to meditate - I don't care how, you just need to be someone who meditates. Prayer is a form of meditation, so keep that in mind.
    2) A rock-solid mind. You generally want a high Integrity - there are ways around mind-breakage, but you're better off keeping that soul of yours nice and healthy.
    3) An excuse to buy up Occult. Granted, everyone has one after you start meeting gribblies, but you want a really good excuse.
    4) A Virtue that's generally applicable for Abjuration. Being Devout or Thorough is incredibly, stupidly useful here.

    So if "exorcist" doesn't fly as a character concept, the next best option is some kind of religious leader or occultist. I tend to prefer the religious leader, because it's very thematically appropriate, and it justifies some nice expansions to your character concept. Plus, there's going to be religious leaders everywhere, so you can fit them in pretty much any kind of Chronicle.

    A Matter of Soul
    One really, really important thing to do is not lose your soul. Don't do it. Soul Loss ruins your ability to exorcist like chopping off someone's legs destroys their chances at being a marathon runner. There are ways around this, but they don't hold a candle to having a soul. Fortunately, you aren't at risk unless you voluntarily trade it away or you face some nasty supernatural mojo, so you're mostly safe unless your ST's a jerk or it's a big dramatic thing.

    Part 1 - The Hard Numbers
    You need certain kinds of hard numbers to be a good at this kind of thing. Luckily, they happen to be the kinds of stuff you were going to be picking anyway, so it's not that harsh.

    Attributes
    You want Resolve and Composure to both have at least three dots. Seriously. You are going to be rolling Resolve+Composure so much, it isn't even funny. On top of that, Intelligence and Wits need to have at least two dots - they're less vital, but you want that Intelligence for research and banishing, and that Wits for binding and warding.

    Skills
    You have at least one dot in Occult. Note, this is not optional - if your schtick is exorcist, you've gotta know something about the stuff you're kicking around (Encyclopedic Knowledge is cute, but isn't going to replace actually knowing about Ghosts and such). I'd really advise starting with two to three dots in Occult, and to toss on some appropriate specialities. Other than Occult, taking Academics, Empathy, or Science at two dots gives you access to some very nice merits.

    Integrity
    Hoo boy, you want this to be no lower than a seven. You take a large penalty to Abjuration once you drop below that point - and all your other exorcist stuff takes those penalties too. Ideally, you want to have an effective Integrity of ten - then you have a +3 to Abjuration rolls, and can laugh off most Breaking Points. Luckily, the merits that help your exorcism stuff also tend to make your mind rock hard, so you won't have to worry about losing Integrity too much.

    Part 2 - Your Options
    There are certain Merits that you really, really want if you want to protect mankind from stuff on the other side of Twilight. Here's a list - it's not entirely comprehensive, but it should cover most of it.

    The Must Haves
    Basically, these merits are central to pulling off this concept mechanically. You can be an exorcist without them, but I wouldn't really suggest it.

    Meditative Mind (•• or ••••)
    Meditative Mind •• gives you +3 on all Resolve + Composure rolls you make in any day you meditate, and lets you ignore environmental penalties when meditating. This is really, really nice - this boosts all your Breaking Point and Abjuration rolls, as well as the rolls for a few other merits. If you decide to spring for a few more dots, Meditative Mind •••• lets you successfully meditate with one success instead of four - speeds the whole process up.

    Hardened Exorcist (••) [Hurt Locker]
    If you can convince your ST that you should have this merit, do so. Adding your Occult (your favorite skill) to your Integrity for Abjuration purposes is lovely. If you can convince them, see if you can get further bonuses for having an effective Integrity higher than 10. The secondary benefit isn't going to show up so much, if you took my advice and picked an appropriate Virtue.

    Patient (•)
    It's so cheap, and so worth it - it gives you extra time to work with when you're trying to research or banish stuff.

    Nice Stuff
    These are nice to have, but aren't, strictly speaking, must haves. You'll appreciate having them, though.

    Good Time Management (•)
    If you have the dots in Academics or Science, you can grab this to cut down on the time investment for research, summoning, and meditating. It's useful if your games are frenetic enough that taking a few hours to hit a library isn't in the cards.

    Esoteric Armory (• to •••••)
    Personally, I don't really like this merit - it basically makes any subplot about getting your hands on a Bane completely superfluous. Still, not everyone wants to worry about that kind of thing, so it's here if you want it.

    Virtuous (••)
    Having two Virtues is useful, since you have backup if your first Virtue doesn't apply for some reason. And backups are good.

    Professional Training (• - •••••)
    It was going to come up at some point - basically, any profession where you can justify Occult as an Asset Skill is a good one.

    Library, Hobbyist Clique et al
    Does it give you a bonus to a Skill? Can that skill be Occult? Then you can probably wedge this kind of Merit in.

    Turning On The Magic
    If you have access to Supernatural merits, you have a few more choices (ignore for a moment that Hardened Exorcist is technically a Supernatural merit). They also happen to be interesting than a lot of the more mundane merits.

    Unseen Senses (••)
    Yep. It's a classic, all right - pick the right variant, and you know when your chosen Ephemeral Being type is around. Really, really useful.

    Medium (•••)
    This is why you'd want Empathy 2+. It lets you talk to Ghosts and gives you the ability to summon (and kinda dismiss) Ephemeral Beings. Useful and thematic, so it'd be one of my first choices if supernatural merits are on the table.

    Clear-Sighted (••) [V:tR Second Edition]
    This is tucked away in the appendix about the living. And it's nice. Spend one Willpower, and you're effectively immune to supernatural powers that fuck with your senses for the rest of the Scene. It's niche, but you can laugh off stuff like Hallucinate, which shouldn't be sneezed at.

    Deadpan (•••) [M:tAw Second Edition]
    You need to be a Sleepwalker (which Hardened Exorcist technically does), but this merit makes you immune to supernaturally inflicted fear unless it plays off your Vice or a mental condition (in which case you just get a bonus). Helps you avoid being spooked by ghosts.

    Bless Amulet (•) [Hurt Locker]
    The amulets this lets you make are great if you deal with a lot of Ephemeral Beings. It's a nice defense which can be handed around to other players or the poor victim you might be helping.

    Consecrate Weapon (••••) [Hurt Locker]
    Honestly, I like this less than just finding out the Bane. But still, it's not like picking this up hurts you. Plus, you can use this with a pair of brass knuckles so you can punch spirits and such.

    To Tell The Truth (Wolf-Blooded)
    These merits are restricted to Wolf-Blooded (other than, technically, Tell) - they are some of the best merits for dealing with the ephemeral world, but bring quite a lot of baggage to the table. All these merits are in W:tF Second Edition.

    Tell [Piercing Eyes] (•••)
    You can see everything in Twilight all the time. Forget Unseen Senses - you just plain can see the things you're trying to deal with.

    Kamduis-Ur's Blood (••)
    Of all the "tribe" merits, this one is probably the best for exorcism purposes. You get to make short-lived sigils that trap ghosts in one place, giving you some breathing room.

    Gibbous Moon's Birth (••)
    Take it for Occult, and don't look back. If you can have this, quietly ignore Hobbyist Clique.

    Raised By Wolves (•••)
    This makes you immune to Breaking Points from observing the supernatural. If you don't want to deal with actually rolling your really big pool for Breaking Points, you might as well pick this up.

                                                  Camera Obscura is actually terrible by itself for the purposes of fighting ephemerals.

                                                  • It only affects un-manifested ephemerals, and only deals Bashing. Compare pulling out the thing's Bane or a Consecrated Weapon, which are still useful if it manifests, and which deal Lethal to gribblies in Twilight.

                                                  • Unless I'm reading it wrong, you have to spend the Willpower every turn. That's really darn expensive.

                                                  • It gives the area you're fighting in the Open condition, which is not good. Compare, again, to Bane or Consecrated Weapon, which don't do that.

                                                  The sole advantage is that you can use it to see Ephemerals in Twilight. You can get that for much cheaper by picking up the Piercing Eyes Tell, which doesn't cost nearly as much Willpower.
