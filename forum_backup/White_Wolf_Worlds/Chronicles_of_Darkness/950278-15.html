            Have you read Austin Grossman's "Soon I Will Be Invincible"? The treatment of the mad scientist character is very similar to Genius, and even more similar when it comes to what the review is complaining about. "I was going to declare war on the world, and I was going to lose." Just having the same ideas come up in a book isn't any guarantee of quality, but if Grossman independently created something so similar, there must be a coherent archetype that they're both working off.
            ​As for my overall opinion of Genius, the backstory and setting is incoherent, the whole Masquerade bit works awkwardly with the effects of Wonders, the Axioms are a little clunky but pretty decent, and the Catalysts are pure gold. If I was making a revised edition I'd say the first Genius appeared on November 8, 1895, and that Genii PCs are supposed to break any Masquerade and shake up the status quo. Genius: The Disruption.

                Actually, that discussion on how Science is way of thought gives me an idea on how to make Lemuria better;

                Make it so they're not insane. They're pseudo-scientists who operate off of their ability to twist facts to suit their pet theories. In bare bones, that isn't different from what they are now, but rather than organizing for no good reason/being people who literally can't comprehend things outside their worldview, they organize themselves around whatever One Perfect Theory is shared among the Baramin, while fundamentally agreeing on the general idea that said theories fit in the Theory of Everything they haven't found yet. There's a point to be made about crank magnetism here-they don't work together because they have some weird authorial mandate there must be a faction, they work together because everyone else thinks they're ridiculous when they're not outright repulsive-misery loves company.

                Of course, this also means Lemurians are far less organized (could be that they aren't even a proper group, it's just a term the Peerage came up with to describe the Geniuses even other mad scientists think are cranks-see the actual theory of Lemuria), but that's kind of the point.

                    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                    I've got no clue where the idea that their rulership was a good thing comes from. And there is no "humanity should be controlled" theme. There is a "humanity cannot be controlled and even trying is foolish" theme. Lemuria's control of humanity was either mostly a lie, or like Frankenstine's monster it rose up and turned against them. (Their goal was to recreate their homeland, it lasted 5 minuets before getting nuked. And it might have happened despite all their meddeling since in the origional timeline humanity didn't need to be controlled to create it).
                    I can see where the idea come from. Paired with the "humanity cannot be controlled and even trying is foolish" theme is the undercurrent about the bitterness (and yes, even bitter disappointment) Geniuses have towards that fact that occasionally runs through the text.

                    Originally posted by Genius the Transgression, pg 35-36, No One is in Charge
                    That, say many Inspired, is why we didn't get the future with the flying cars and the "televisors" and the moon bases: those things were in the works, all set for the Lemurians to hand them down to us like manna from heaven, and we were supposed to accept them and let our betters maintain them while we lived our happy, comfortable lives. But Lemuria got its ass kicked, and has your life ever been comfortable? It hasn't, say many in the Peerage, because humanity is off the rails, free from control or intellectual extortion.
                    The Peerage don't technically regret freeing Earth from what control Lemuria had (a fact better emphasized later), but they're technologists of a sort and their attitude in this paragraph can come off as "Freedom is why our level of technology (which, you'll notice, is tied into happiness and comfort by the Peerage) isn't what it 'should' be."

                    It may be foolish to control humanity, but at the same time I feel the game can easily appeal to those who want to make Geniuses who think they can "do better" or who feel they they have the right to build superscience at the expense of what it does to society. Three of the catalysts - Grimm, Hoffnung, and Neid - are all on some level about imposing your will directly on other people. And I admit that certain Axioms appealed to me on that level. I don't think it's a wrong thing to explore necessarily, and Obligation and Lemuria can help provide balance if used well, but setting a tone that "Your attempts to change mortal society won't work, mostly because mortals won't get it, and that sucks," can go into some weird places if the GM doesn't have a firm grasp on the Chronicle.

                                                        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                                        You may keep it as a mystery, but it needs to be defined somehow. If it isn't magic, and isn't science, and isn't some rainbow unicorn poop god- than what is it? If there are theories about the subject than I think its ok- things can be a mystery, because mystery creates stories.
                                                        So far so good, six possible explenations are presented. Which, if any, of them is true is left as a mystery.

                                                        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                                        But if the Genii (I think this is the plural form. I saw people in the forums use it) don't even care about how what they do work- than all it gives you is a gaping hole in the middle of the game.
                                                        Geniuses do care about it. Just like there's plenty of Werewolves who care what happened to Father Wolf and Promethians who want to know what, exactly, the Principal is.

                                                        Most Geniuses have accepted that they won't ever discover the truth, just like most Promethians accept they won't figure out the Principal. But that doesn't mean they don't care.

                                                        Incidentally if they ever did figure it out they'd probably hit the equivalent of Archmastery. There is a 9th Axiom that controls Inspiration itself. Nobody knows how to use it and most Peers think it's just a myth, but Moochava has said it's real. If anything would let a Genius learn it, discovering what exactly Inspiration is would do it.


                                                        As for the plural it's actually addressed in the game. Both Geniuses and Genii are acceptable. Geniuses is the more commonly used plural by the Inspired; while people who use correct Latin plurals like Genii are stereotypically old (being educated in a time where Latin was on the curriculum), or obsessive.


                                                        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                                        Science is not some sort of force, it is not some weird technique or way of action. Science is a way of thought- science, at its very base, is rising questions, which lead to experiments, which lead to new questions. Science, at its very core, works to disprove an existing theory by conducting an experiment.
                                                        While I mostly agree with you, I think you need to add a little something to that definition.

                                                        Lets say I wanted to disprove the theory "the earth is round" so I conduct an experiment. I take a ball, I put it on the ground, I observe that the ball is not rolling away, so I conclude the Earth is flat. I certainly did an experiment to disprove an existing theory, but I wouldn't call it "science".

                                                        I'd say that to define science you need to add a couple of things: A minimum level of competence. A spirit of trust and collaboration whereby scientists share their work and let others peer review or build upon it.

                                                        It's those additional criteria which disqualify a genius from being a scientist.


                                                        They do experiments to disprove theories and hypothesizes. But they're not particularly competent at it (a good Scholastic could be competent). A Genius jumps to conclusions, makes basic maths errors, ignores basic precautions to keep the test subjects uncontaminated. And they don't even realize they're doing it.

                                                        And that's assuming that they don't accidentally contaminate their experiment with Inspiration - it's entirely possible that a Genius trying to test the boiling point of water will accidentally come up with an innovative new heater that boils water at 80 degrees instead of 100.
                                                        Now I'm not saying a Genius doesn't do anything at all. They can't just grab whatever chemicals are close to hand and mix them and suddenly have a bomb. Building wonders requires research, testing, trial runs, etc. Dots in mundane skills like Science or Medicine are a vital component of both Wonder building and Wonder use.

                                                        I'm just saying that a Genius' experiments and the mental state of being a Genius are not good tools for disproving a hypothesis or theory; whether that theory is about mundane science or the supernatural.
