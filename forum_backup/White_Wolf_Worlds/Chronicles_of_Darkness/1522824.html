    Setting hack (Demon/Promethean/Mummy)

    09-20-2023, 09:50 AM
    [Work in Progress]

    Design goals: unify the metaphysics of these 3 games. Making for interesting interactions, fun cross-over potential, exploring the common themes.

    Themes: the themes from Promethean (searching what it is to be human) and Mummy (remembering what it was to be human) mesh fairly well.

    Demon's (escape the God-like entity trying to hunt and control you) and Mummy leaning in to (escape the control of the god-like entities) may need some poking.

    Promethean and Demon sharing inhuman experience may also mesh nicely, Pilgrimage and Breaking the Cipher/Descent to Hell.


    Setting canon:

    Osirians: produced as an attempt to recreate the rite of return using Alchemy.

    The Principle: is dead, and more than that, it was killed by the Shain'atu to transform into a weapon to fight the Judges

    The God-machine: an agent of Statis attempting to cut off the world from outside influences which it cannot control (including the Judges of Duat), it was created from the corpse of the Principle (or whatever the Principle was before it died and became the Principle).

    Metaphysics (Essence/Aether/Sekhem/Pryos): the God-machine does not run directly on Pyros, the divine fire is too dangerous, flux tends to break things. Instead it runs on a refined Essence, though they come from the same source. In order to safely use this Essence (without the same risks which Flux produces) Aether must be bleed away.

    Sekhem is a pure refined version of Dedwen, the mystical property contained in all things, this is also called the Divine Fire by Prometheans.

    Thus Pyros/Dedwen can be refined into Sekhem/Azoth/Essence, different processes but the same underlying magical life force.

    The Principle is trying to achieve its own New Dawn, which may return it to life? Or may allow it to take it's (desecrated corpse) body back from the God-machine.

    ---

    Cross-over mechanics:
    Mummys and Demons are not directly affected by Disquiet, a Mummy is affected through her Cult, a Demon's cover can be affected, but the Demon may choose to ignore the effect (this may risk exposing the cover).

    Demons treat Sybaris similarly to Disquiet, choosing whether or not a given cover acts like it was affected. Prometheans gain insight into death and mortality [insert negative effect with positive outcomes - comparable to a minor step on the pilgrimage, but not the same as the milestone of dying and drinking from the underworld].

    Cults: Prometheans don't really do too well among humans, however like forming a Throng, a Promethean and Mummy can form an Alchemical Pact, aligning Azoth with the Life Web of the Mummy, this can make the Mummy's Cult immune to Disquiet and allowing the Promethean to treat the Mummy's tomb as a Hovel [review these rules?]

    [Todo: look at any other special effects i may have missed, add a source of Aether? Do Mummy's need help in escaping the Judges control? Possibly using Occult Physics subverted from God-Machine infrastructure? Possibly despite the Demon's protestations? Is this the same level as Hell/a new Dawn? ]

    Demons storing Aether in an electrical device can create something which a Promethean is able to use for electroshock therapy, however each Pyros generated drains the device of 1 Aether (design note: this is to prevent the perpetual motion/energy of an Aether stockpile from generating infinite Pyros for the Promethean).

    ---

    Plot hooks: Hell, A New Dawn, Freedom from the Judges, some things are easier to achieve than others, but together much greater things can be achieved.

    Promethean as the linchpin in some God-Machine Infrastructure, Azothic Object as a Relic/Vessel, the God-machine as a weapon against the Judges, a New Dawn for Mummys free from the Rite of Return?, the Principle/Qashmallim working to ressurect the God-machine/resort it's body to what it was meant to be, Stealing Angels from the God-machine, a Cipher and Pilgrimage Milestone allowing different lessons from the same event?

      Well, I have thoughts, but as usual, my first one is "Why" followed by "How"-specifically, why do the metaphysics have to be more in line with each other to be more fun, and how does removing some of the obstacles that exist as vectors for interpersonal conflict and connections be more fun?

      Don't get me wrong, no less an authoritative source on the subject than the Contagion Chronicle and it's Player's Guide advocates for smoothing things out, which goes a long way for addressing the how. But first one has to assume that part of the reason why people like playing these games is wrestling with the issues that come with any given splat, and in crossover one has to assume that goes double. A person playing a vampire in crossover probably doesn't want to suddenly start daywalking and firejuggling just because they're in crossover, they want to be the nightwalking, people-bending outsider who nevertheless contributes to the gaming group, unless they a) build their character around it, which may be an invitation for b, and b)sit down with the table and explain why they don't want to have to handle being isolated by the day and don't want Jackie Flamefingers' eagerness to jump into combat with elemental glee to always cause them to go running off. THe limitations drive character action and behavior that leads to the fun of characters bouncing off of each other as they work together and around their particulars, and while it can nice to just say "Okay, we're removing these issues so we can have gameplay work faster", there's a lot of fun in dealing with the individual consequences

      And that's just handling the consequences of effects. I'm not sure what unifying the mechanics is actually supposed to do. In a game of horror mystery, the occultism of the metaphysical forces affecting individual characters is a matter that is prevalent even in single-splat games, because the particular ways the overarching metaphysic of a gameline leads to both horror of and empathy for each comrade in the group-empathy as you learn how the metaphysic is hampering or pushing your fellows on in ways they might rather wish they weren't, horror as you realize in a moment of something happening that you don't have quite a good handle on your compatriots as you thought you had. In a crossover game where players don't have the same metaphysic (often), that all gets exacerbated and honestly just offers more moments of fun from the disparity.

      Meshing the metaphysics that are Pyros and Azoth and Flux, Infrastructure, and Sekhem and the Judges together is just....kind of really odd? Particularly since some of the conversations that have demonstrated the most fun just in casual conversation is watching how the fanbase tries to square the differences and similarities between their adjacent overseer/god-alike, what it means for the overall setting to have the Principle AND the Judges AND the God-Machine and what their relationships to each other is and what it means on the grand scale.

      I would also argue that they're not that similar (though credit where it's due, it's a good set to have this sort of experiment), but that's a gripe.

      So, yeah, give me a little bit more to buy into how and why this is a fun hack to play with.
      Okay, so since that's a bit of a hammer, I do want to leave some of the things I like-further criticisms can wait till after the answer, because that'll color those, but there are some good points straight up.

      In particular, I'm glad you call out Demons handling Sybaris like they Disquiet-I'm fairly certain you mean it in the context of the hack, but it's a good thing to just have out there for crossover in general. The same holds true for Sybaris can actually benefit the Pilgramage. The rules about Electroshock Therapy and Aether Storage is also just a quick, easy, and good clarification that I'm gonna have filed in my head forever.

      Everything else, I'm gonna hold off on until I have the requested clarification.

          One, on Sybrais and Demons. I don't think there are rules there, but that one makes sense given the rules explicit for Disquiet.

          Two, on the general issue of meta-physics. I like to have an understanding of how the setting is 'supposed' to work, on a fundamental level, and build complications and interesting interactions from how the players engage with it, rather than making a game about unknow-able and unfathomable evil lurking around every corner. Each to their own i guess.

          As for the issues each splatt takes as a given. I don't know making a Mummy's Cult immune to Disquiet is too strong (i did say to review this bit). But I think it raises interesting story potential, and potential conflict especially if following one of the Judges is one of the milestones in a pilgrimage (as Cult like worship is an aspect of humanity - i think that could fit with parts of Promethean i have only skimmed) but i would rather use it as a distraction from the Pilgrimage, where the Promethean has another reason to not fulfill their potential if they get stuck serving a Judge...

          So overall i think it adds more than it takes away.

          That said, when I wrote that I was mainly thinking the bits of meta-plot i have added* rather than the specific personal piece. So thank you for questioning it.

          The main piece of conflict I have added is between the Principle, the God-machine and the Judges (without taking away from any of their uniqueness, i haven't touched the Judges at all, and barely scratched the God-machine, it may have evolved from its original design and/or never worked as intended or been broken by interference from whoever stopped it). The addition makes for potentially interesting conflicts, as it suggests a reason for Mummy's to he sent to interact with the God-machine, and a reason for Prometheans (following the Principle) to try and interact in a different way. But none of those are binding interactions, a Mummy trying to free themselves from the Judges by empowering the God-machine might come into conflict with Demons, Prometheans and Mummys working for the judges... so really the meta-plot is there as a background to encourage cross-over play.

          While adding the potential for Azoth and Sekhem to exist in the same object is the same, but by tweaking some underlying metaphysics. But to be clear, Dedwen and Pyros being the same thing does not make their refinements the same thing. It is just 'there is energy in the Universe, and it is called different things by different people'

          *I say added, but i am just choosing from some of the answers offering in the book, the 'maybe the Principle is dead but trying to have its own New Dawn, and 'what if the relationship between the Principle and the God-machine is: the God-machine is the Principle 2.0'. If you like this particular meta-plot then feel free to use it.

                      It's a good clarification, if a little divorced from the point I was making. To make myself clear, I am not arguing for (in this instance) for a whole setting filled with inscrutable and unknowable powers and entities, because a) I personally prefer the known quantities colliding, conflicting, contracting, and conflating with each other in interesting ways, but more importantly b) Chronicles....
                      Well, I can't say that Chronicles expects you to treat every presented metaphysic as a whole and comprehensive subject, because everything still has plenty of murk to them, but that said Chronicles is more interested in leaving characters with their space of knowledge. Sure, not everything about the Divine Fire and it's various forms and emanations are perfectly knowable, but it's knowable enough that a seasoned enough Promethean is able, at a minimum, to know who to go to for expertise, and enough basic knowledge to not die in the meantime. Same with all the other metaphysic, with degrees of exploration based on what's going on with it.

                      Of course, all of that is from the grounds of the player base of understanding, and while I make the point about how the system currently works, it towards the ends of what the characters experience and have to work through-namely, that they have to work through various magical bullshit to understand everything going on with their peers, and even then there's probably always some nook or cranny of magic that can still cause shock, alarm, or distress.

                      A lot of what's in the world of Chronicles of Darkness is knowable, but none of it readily apparent. Individual splats have or otherwise quickly gain an edge in understanding their particular magical bullshit's niches as far as it is easily accessible, but the Bale Hounds are more unknown to the Forsaken than they are to the Forsaken reader. That compounds when you ask a splat to understand something outside of their familiar context. Unless the setting is built a very long and very close relationship between werewolves and changelings, your average Uratha has as much insight into the regular Lost as the average person does to the regular formulas at work in thermodynamics or quantum mechanics-they might have an idea, but the instant you ask for anything hard, they're at a loss. That can be fixed, but it's a lot of work going forward, and in the meanwhile, there's a lot of everything else. So your peers in a crossover context are learnable, but it's a long process that leaves room for them to surprise you.

                      This is what I'm getting at with the idea that the differing metaphysics build into the overall gameplay experience, and why I'm curious, by contrast, what conflation offers that's interestingly playable.

                                  [Optional Rules]
                                  A New Dawn, Hell and the Rite of Return.

                                  Ok, so i think these rules might be a bit more contraversial, so optional because if you don't like them, don't use them* (not that this entire setting hack isn't optional).

                                  1) Can Demons have a New Dawn?

                                  Yes, equivalent in difficulty to them making their own Hell, infact becoming human is one definition of hell; this is likely rare specifically because Demons - in general- don't necessarily have any reason to want to become human. But if an individual Demon learns about the New Dawn and wants to try it, the 👍👍 good luck to them.

                                  How can they do it? Much like Prometheans, with a lot of work, however unlike Prometheans Demons are not being of pure Alchemy... for a Demon to become human they need to transfer their consciousness to a human body in a permanent way. This is very different from using a Cover and in many ways seems inferior, but not being a Demon anymore is the Deepest of covers one could aim for - not that the God-machine doesn't mess with humans, but there are so many humans to mess with, the chances are any given human will be left to their own devices.

                                  Do they get anything special? Maybe? I feel like keeping the ability to make Pacts might be too much, and without the need to build Covers may even seem pointless, but like the Fall in ther first place - where Demons retain the basic understanding required to perform embeds - i would like to give them some advantage over regular humans, any suggestions?

                                  2) Can Mummys become human again?

                                  Yes and No. The powers that be make the Rite of Return next to impossible to escape, however there are two possibilities. First, a Mummy who finds themselves awake before the Rite of Return created them might be able to prevent themselves from ever being chosen†. Though what actually happens to the timeline in this case is an open question.

                                  Secondly, if it were not for the Judges then yes, it would be possible to undo the Rite of Return. But getting rid of the Judges is a seperate question.

                                  †this is just reiterating what is stated in the book.

                                  3) Is it possible for Mummys to escape the control of the Judges?

                                  Yes and no. While directly fighting the Judges on their home turf seems like a terrible plan (maybe i am wrong on this, my Mummy knowledge isn't the strongest) preventing them from affecting the 'real' world would free those living their from the Judges power.

                                  As such the best option would be to alter the universe on a cosmological scale to disconnect Duat from the rest of the world/prevent souls from travelling to Duat at all.

                                  Easier said than done of course, and most supernatural entities would utterly fail in the attempt. However Mummys are not most supernatural entities.

                                  If they were in the right place at the right time, a Mummy should be able to influence history enough to do what is required (in this case, it is manipulating the God-Machine to perform the required cosmological changes). Even that is an epic achievement, without Memories or the ability to choose when they wake up, a Mummy is at the mercy of the Judges. However, since the God-Machine was pre-programmed to screw with the Judges, it is entirely reasonable that the Judges are already sending Mummys in to 'fix' the G-M.

                                  Altering the output of specific occult matrices, which in turn changes the future decision making of the god-machine may just work. And a Mummy may just think they are tasked with finding a vessel - which happens to be part of the infrastructure.

                                  Will it work? Even if it does, a Mummy may not be so lucky that they aren't stuck in Duat indefinitely. Infact even in the best case, some Mummys speculate that a part of their soulswill be trapped in Duat regardless. Not too encouraging.

                                  4) What happens to Mummys without a connection to Duat?

                                  Without Duat a Mummy can't wake up as normal, on the plus side they cankeep all the Sekhem they find for themselves, on the downside once the drop to Sekhem 0 it becomes very difficult for anything to return them to Life. Furthermore, without the connection to Duat they are no longer Timeless, merely Endless.

                                  So absent a plan, and/or a source of Sekhem and a way to use it to wake them up, this all seems pretty risky.

                                  Waking a Mummy by any means will give them a maximum starting Sekhem of 3 dots. This is severly limiting, but that is the price of freedom. From here (Hell as Demons would put it) one option is to attempt to return to life. This is comparitively easy (compared both to the Pilgrimage/Demons attempting to become human, and to what has just been achieved twarting the Judges) mainly because the Mummy was once buman to being with. All they require is a body, their memories, a soul and the proper ritual magic.

                                  5) the Principle and the God-machine?

                                  ???

                                  I think the themes here of what it means to be human and what it means to be free run deep in these three game-lines and mesh well together. So i am not worried that dilutinf tgem will be a deteiment.

                                      Creation Myths


                                      The world is broken, things don't work the way they are supposed to, depending on who you ask they are certain to give a different reason, Mages might tell of the Fall, or Spirits of the Sundering, but most likely whoever you talk to will blame everything on the one event the do know about - but this is incomplete.


                                      Among the Broken Features are the Underworld, the Principle, and the God-Machine. Each is broken in its own way, but these largely stem from the same cause. The Underworld is broken because What Would Become the Principle died (or was killed) and the Underworld was not able to handle such a Death. The God-Machine is what was made out of the What Would Become the Principle's corpse, an elaborate self-driven machine, which has nigh unlimited potential, but became wildly out of step with its creators. And the Principle is what is left, once it had a clear purpose, now it drives the creation of life from the lifeless.


                                      Who kill What Would Become the Principle? (And made the God-Machine)


                                      • The Shan’iatu, powerful Sorcerer-Kings and creators of the Nameless Empire, the pinicale of human civilisation according to some, and willing to make significant sacrifice of human life.


                                      Sacrificing the driver of life itself to create a machine that will allow them gain freedom from the Judges, that makes sense. But something clearly went wrong...


                                      • The Exarchs have been known to work with the God-Machine and its agents, perhaps they created it in the first place. Making Duat into a Lower Depth to weaken the power and influence of the Judges, in responce the Judges create the Rite of Return and have Mummy's enforce their will upon this broken world.


                                      • The Principle was a living force, and life naturally dies, the normal cycle of death and reincarnation was broken when the God-Machine was created, consuming most of the vital force of the Principle, and leaving only embers to reincarnate as the Principle we know today.


                                      • Father Wolf killed the Principle, the Great Hunter was decieved, the Spinner Hag had designs on controlling and weaving a world she could rule, so she set about changing things. First she set the Great Hunter to the task of hunting the impossible, perhaps she hoped he would die in the attempt. But while it may have weakened him, Father Wolf managed to kill the Principle. The Spinner Hag claimed the corpse and began to build a machine from it, something to protect her new world and seperate it from the predatory world of the Shadow, but before she was finished the Great Hunter realised that she had manipulated him and he attacked... The God-Machine, unfinished, now does part of its job, growing and spreading its influence in the material realm.
