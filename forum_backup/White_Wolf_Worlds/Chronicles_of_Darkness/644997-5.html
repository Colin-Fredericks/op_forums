            Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
            You're right. If we suppose you can use something like that system from 1ed Hunter where you can fiddle with the hierarchy of “sins”, but for Integrity, we can easily roll that many dice.
            “The Code”, yes. Note that the Code has never been a “free lunch”; in order to gain immunity from further degeneration checks of a particular type, you needed to saddle yourself with some psychological quirks. I'm not sure that Hunter 2e's equivalent to the Code should necessarily work the same way; but I’d prefer that the general premise be similar.

            Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
            Granted, this is a very specific scenario created with bonuses in mind, but if Sleeper Hunters are going up against Mages, they better get all the bonuses they can to keep their Integrity in place. A Beat and a Willpower every now and then also help. The ST should keep keep Breaking Point rolls caused by witnessing Supernal magic to a minimum, methinks. The Sleeper Curse has that name for a reason, however, and so Sleeper Hunters should not be immune to such rolls.
            Broadening this a bit, I think that hunters should have an easier time than regular mortals overall dealing with encounters with the supernatural. That is, after all, the whole point of the Vigil, IMHO.

            Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
            That After Action Report Tactic doesn't sound so bad, now that Charlaquin described it with more detail. Perhaps getting an Exceptional Success on said Tactic lets you re-roll a failed Breaking Point and keep the best result of the two rolls.
            Yeah. I didn't go into detail because that would have caused the minutiae to overwhelm the broader put I was going for: hunters ought to be able to find ways to cope with supernatural hurdles without resorting to becoming supernatural themselves — it's what they do. That said:

            Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
            In any case, worsening Paradox with their observation of Supernal magic should still be a risk. There could be a Tactic that increases the chance of Paradox hitting the Mage instead of the Hunters or bystanders. We already have Tactics that do borderline supernatural stuff. For example, Bait And Switch provokes a werewolf into a frenzy but makes him attack his own pack. Deprogramming lets you remove mind control. Effigy lets you trap Spirits within inanimate objects, and may work on Geister for the purposes of permanently killing Sin-Eaters, if the ST says so.
            I'd recommend thinking twice about borderline-supernatural Tactics; that way lies Excision. By the same token, a theme of Hunter (1e, at least) is that mortals are more dangerous than the various Greater Template types tend to give them credit for: if they have the occasional trick up their sleeves that leaves a mage thinking “that shouldn't have worked”, so be it.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

Is our definition of supernatural as abnormal somehow misguided? Is there a latent supernatural potential in humans as a whole, with the environment and events activating this potential like pseudo-genetics? Did humanity always have this potential, and if not, what gave it to us? Are the monsters/major splats somehow degraded or refined expressions of this potential?                         To me, this is where the horror actually lies in Hunter. Like, yeah, you hunt monsters and monsters are scary. Ho, hum. But what's really horrifying is asking at what point you become just as much of a monster as the things you hunt. Obviously slashers are one way a hunter becomes a monster. But what about the ones who claim to be descended from the honest-to-goodness Devil and use demonic powers to fight monsters? What about the scientists who don't just kill monsters, but capture and experiment on them? What about the hellfire club that goes poking into the supernatural for a cheap thrill? What about the unaffiliated hunter who has minor psychic abilities and realizes he can use them against the monsters? How much use of supernal powers is too much? Who decides who counts as a monster and who's just one of the good guys doing what needs to be done? That's the most interesting part of Hunter to me.

                                    That said. I always felt that "when are you a monster" wasn't actually that interesting of a question - my answer is mostly because when writing Hunter homebrew I have strict rules that Hunters are not pawns of "monsters" (gods are a special exception) and thus needed a definition of monster.
