    Agents of the Exarchs

    07-22-2023, 05:57 PM
    I am looking for interesting story hooks, so no wrong answers.

    Who would make good non-mage agents for each of the Exarchs? (Starting with the ones with less influential ministries)

    I'll start the ball rolling with a pack of pure werewolves unknowingly in service to the Nemesis. How would they receive dreams or portents? What would their pack philosophy looks like? How would they differ from other packs and/or relate to other supernaturals?

    Seperately, a group of Changelings, probably loyalist, serving the Gate. Making contracts with the Abyss, or with parts of a Annunkai world/Abyssal verge, as a means to coerce/bring it into the Fallen (through defining its relationship with reality). What would their Keeper want from this? How would they interact with the Scelesti/left handed seer legacies, if at all? What would the dream equivalent of acamoth be (oneirmancy and abyssal familiars?)

          The Nemesis wants you so scared of things what may be in the shadows or otherwise lurking in the unknown that you don't stop to understand the invisible, and Twilight alone accomodates that. THe Gauntlet has other features that can make it attractive or bothersome to the Ministries and stewards interested in the subject, but the Nemesis itself has no hard stance on whether the Gauntlet is useful or not.

          As for the Pure, only the Predator Kings have something of a spiritual mandate to revive Pangaea and dissolve the Gauntlet. The Fire-Touched, who I assume was the other one you were talking about, only have mandates regarding the Shadow, with no regard to whether or not the Gauntlet is a good thing or not in regards to the maintenance of the sanctity of the Shadow or an impediment to empowerment of the myriad courts of spirits within the Shadow.

          The Web of Pain on the whole may have a general interest in the commodifcation of individuals and communites (a mild but distinct difference from just mere objectification) but it's important to note that a lot of Conspiracies are just as motivated by misplaced ideals about the human condition, so while the foundation is principlely rooted in the Chancellor's ambitions, the particulars can get murky, and the Ministry of Mammon could expect a lot more stoppage than expected. Still a good call on the whole, though.

          For my take, I'd probably want to take the time later in the day to break it down by Exarch and who's likely to be of most use to them directly against who would be the most interested, but I will take the current moment to cut down the thought load for latter by at least blanket mentioning that Beasts, Deviants, and Deviant Conspiracies can be argued to have regular presence in a good number of Ministries and in partnership with any kind of steward. By contrast, I'm also going to mention a blanket discomfort that probably exists among the Unchained due to the ease of relations between the Seers and the God-Machine's angels-it kind of doesn't matter if any particular demon, ring, or Agency has designs that line up with the Seers when their major issue is also right there.

                      The Nemesis had a Major Ministry in the form of Geryon for the majority of the Seers history, who managed the idea of tyranny through secret societies and conspiracies, doing things like sponsoring organized hate groups who hide their faces, criminal enterprises serving the people with power and privilege, and generally assailing the common folk with unpunishable forces. It was in power up pretty well up until the mid 19th century, where the invention of various communication technologies started favoring The Eye and their ministry of Panopticon (tyranny through surveillance). The decline continued to shift in that manner up until Geryon was a minor ministry in 90's (I'm not clear on when it actually got demoted, though money's on late 70s/early 80s) at which point the signature Scelestus of the setting, Angrboda, systematically wiped out the remaining minsters.

                      As for werewolves and the Raptor, it pays to keep in mind that there's particular lines to these things. The Watchtower of the Singing Stone is the purview of instincts and primitive urges, while it doesn't quite step fully into werewolf mentality (no Thyrsus ultimately owes the spiritual anything, where the werewolf has to straddle the two worlds just for their own sanity and wholeness), the balancing of Bone and Blood is well in line with the revelations offered forward by the Watchtower of the Singing Stone. THe Raptor, by contrast, is interested social Darwinism and asshole-flavored evo-psychology, in justifying evil as instincts, being proponents of biotruths as justification for limitations, advocating for gender-critical TERF-ery, promoting "alpha" lifestyles, etc, etc. Certainly that works for a lot of werewolves, but it's important to remember that it's endearing to certain kinds of werewolves rather than the whole. That line does cut across the major factions of werewolves-while it's easier to imagine Predator King or a Bale Hound being appealed to it, it's easy to see how a Storm Lord or a Iron Master can fall into the mentality as well-but by contrast, there's a lot of werewolves who take a look at the Raptor and the Sycorian Ministry that directly serves it and goes "Wow, what a bunch of assholes."

                              Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post

                              Sure. However I'm only speaking what may make good "common" agents of these exarchs. I dont think any of the templates are monolithic in being something the exarchs can use

                              EDIT: thank you on the geryon info. They sound pretty cool. Wouldnt be surprised if they could be revived in some form
                              Angrboda, I should mention, explictly fed Geryon Minsters, and very explictly the last Geryon Minister, to the Abyss, and I don't imagine it was just done for funsies. Something like Geryon could resurface, but I imagine the act itself is something of metaphysically resonant "And don't come back" action.

                              As for good common agents of the Exarchs go, oh, sure, it's an easy fit to point at Nemesis and Raptor. But you know what other Exarchs a werewolf would find themselves easily serving if they had the interest?

                              Unity(pack tribalism dividing even ostensibly united fronts in the larger politics of Forsaken is the norm), Paternoster(the almost assuredly ubiquotous, if fluid, level of mystery religion operations in Totem sustainment), Eye( the ease of hunting and the ease of spiritual spy networking), and the General (violence is the Uratha's bread and butter). The Chancellor (the unfortunate ease of which wolf-blooded can get treated like cattle), Prophet (the ease in Uratha society to tie Renown and fatalistic Resonances together) and Ruin (the general entropic way of understanding the world and civilization present in Uratha society) are also fairly easy to imagine, if not as immediately intuitive. Only the Psychopomp(general overall lacking concern of afterlives), and in direct worship and practice of the Psychopomp, becomes a wee bit tricksome. Service to the Rhadamantian Ministry is also a wee bit weird to consider("earthly punishment" like the prison-industrial complex isn't exactly a subject lost on the Uratha, but it's not inherently interesting to them either).

                              If I have to form a point out of my "remember everyone's complicated", which I am slowly getting around to, it's more that the subject at hand is perhaps more interesting to consider the particulars of raising interest rather than consulting the broad spectrum. The model of "How for whom?" is where I would argue the question of the topic gets interesting, because this gets into, simply, what is Seer-esque and what is not Seer-esque in the other gamelines' factions-how these other factions can be tempted by and repulsed by tyranny as a concept, and who is the most prone to feeling defeated by reality enough to hop on the train.

                              We've been talking about werewolves a lot, but as an example of what I find interesting in this topic and is a really fascinating case to discuss about this is changelings, and I actually really appreciate the association with loyalists. Changelings joining the Seers feels like an appreciable compromise to the conflict between Loyalists and the Lost-Seer Loyalists didn't sell out to the True Fae, and thus didn't submit to their captors, and instead are loyal to entities that are anchored in "human" reality, and that's not nothing in terms of everything that is being discussed in the Lost/Loyalist divide-but on the other hand, mages, and particularly Seers, aren't really all that dissimilar from the True Fae in execution, so what's the difference? Getting into how an Exarch or a Ministry can feel like a comfortable surrender to an entity that, almost assuredly in this paradigm, overcame a world to attain their freedom, even if they are then confronted with an even larger world that they can't as readily exceed is a good question to raise, because you can immediately see how that's the case but have a lot to gain from why it works for a Lost member, and it only gets more interesting if you sharpen the question down to "How is X Exarch/Ministry appealing to a Beast/Autumn Courtier/Master of Keys/Loyalist of the Year of Plague?", as well it's contrarian question.

                              THat little proposal taps into questions about the way tyrannies can gain support against each other, and the way a person can double-think their way into operating under one on their own, which for me is more interesting than "Oh yeah, it's easy to imagine changelings serving the Gate."*

                              Or another way, I personally find it fascinating that both the Invictus and the Carthian Revolution are both equally easy and hard sells to Hegemony because the hows of that are where the differences emerge.

                              *Though on that front, I actually struggle to imagine how that would be appealing to changelings and how you could, outside of the most niche of situations, have that happen in the first place.
