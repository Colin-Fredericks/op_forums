        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
        Belated realization, but: It's fun to note that becoming Feral is less a Game Over screen and more a switch over to Hard Mode; you get no benefit from Stubborn Resolve, you can't become a Nemesis or a Guardian, and you can't heal Instability just by moving for or against the Touchstones you no longer have, but the way Stability works means you still have purely supernatural means of recovering that put the stakes a little higher.

        Ferals are locked out of healing any Instability worse than the minor sort without developing new Scars, and they don't have any means of holding back the minor Instability at the end of each chapter or the probably-multiple minor Instabilities at the start of every story, but they'll never Falter or lose more Loyalty Touchstones. What this means, then, is that a Feral who hopes to last needs to pursue Acclimation as quickly as possible and entangle their new Scars to avoid mounting difficulties from their rising minimum Magnitude and make it easier to roll exceptional successes on Scar Finesse and Scar Resistance rolls, which are the only vector for Scar-free healing you now have.

        In other words, going Feral is less of a death sentence and more of a permanent change in priorities from personal relationships to keeping various plates spinning with regards to your occult state; until you manage to hit something like an equilibrium, you pretty much have to keep scads of Experiences on hand to buy new Variations or dots of Acclimation or Attributes — maybe figure out a Stabilizer if you're lucky.

        It's still definitely not something you want to aim for, but it's interesting to see as a "soft" failure state.
        An interesting observation. The window a Feral has in which to raise Acclimation is likely to be quite short. Remember that you can't increase Acclimation if you have any medium or major Instability.

        Originally posted by Leliel View Post

        I think it just needs a "be reasonable" line in here or the Storytelling chapter, and it'll be fine.
        There's actually a short note in the Storytelling Rules (top of p. 15 of the 4th preview) about adjusting the definitions of per-chapter, per-scene, and per-story effects to accommodate this (I tried to warn people to at least closely skim that chapter! ). The sidebar focuses on Variations/Scars/Adaptations, but you can easily apply it to conspiracy actions, as well. If you want a "countdown timer" on a Project based on in-world days/weeks/months instead of narrative units like chapters, you can do that. You can set rules for how Renegade actions that sap away conspiracy actions delay the fruition of Projects - perhaps by a day or week per wasted conspiracy action (depending on context). In fact, I can probably say as much outright in the conspiracies chapter.

        I want to be a bit careful, though. "Per-chapter means per day" works, and "per-chapter means per-chapter" works, but trying to mix them too freely in the same game without adequately communicating this to the players is a recipe for needless arguments.

        Originally posted by Deinos View Post
        Wait, can I just select Suppressible Relentless Variation and have no drawbacks for all subtle scars, and can I put this up on perpetual scars? Or is there something I'm missing?
        We've had a quite spirited discussion about this exact question. Suppressible Relentless Variation has some serious problems in certain circumstances, that I'm currently working to fix.

            Originally posted by Eric Zawadzki View Post
            An interesting observation. The window a Feral has in which to raise Acclimation is likely to be quite short. Remember that you can't increase Acclimation if you have any medium or major Instability.
            And you can only raise it once per chapter, as well, and in the best of circumstances a character has about a fifty-fifty chance of healing minor Instability if they're rolling an unresisted Scar Finesse roll with a full set of five-dot components, which means a lot of the initial desperate push is going to necessitate burning through Experiences so you can pick up new Scars that aren't Persistent (which you can also only do once per chapter).

            It's also dramatically harder if you have a higher-Magnitude Variation and no head start on buying Acclimation, because taking new Scars when you can't leverage Touchstones means any given chapter where you don't roll an exceptional success sees your Scar Resistance penalty make no net reduction at best. Even if it all goes perfectly, you've still changed your main priorities to Get Used To Being Extremely Weird And Magic instead of having goals that relate to other people.

            The upshot, at least, is that buying a dot of Acclimation itself affords you a little bit of breathing room in the form of extra Stability, provided nothing else goes wrong (#ConspiratorsDoNotInteract), so if you've saved up enough Experiences and can make it through the next chapter, you can theoretically go for another increase.

            It's an unlikely situation and a lot of circumstances can make it borderline impossible to manage, but that's Divergence and the Web of Pain in general, right?


            Speaking of this approximate balancing act, though: Are the restrictions on raising Acclimation such that you can't raise Acclimation with the appropriate Experience expenditure if you've had any worse Instability in the same chapter, or is it a viable scene to purge your solitary medium Instability into a new Scar in a safe location, gasp your way through the erupting pain, and then spend the remainder of that chunk of camera-time taking stock and getting to grips with your new abilities and trauma?

            (Also, should the exceptional success results for Scar Finesse and Scar Resistance rolls be listed under the Healing Instability section in the same way that the dramatic failure results are listed under Causing Instability?)

                    Originally posted by Leliel View Post
                    Does it matter? It wouldn't change if it happened to me, since the issue is "how long is this session in game time."
                    Well, yes, because we clearly have different experiences that colour our respective takes on the system.

                    As for your answer, I guess I'm more interested in how much time your sessions usually cover, more than the anomalies.

                    Originally posted by Eric Zawadzki View Post
                    There's actually a short note in the Storytelling Rules (top of p. 15 of the 4th preview) about adjusting the definitions of per-chapter, per-scene, and per-story effects to accommodate this (I tried to warn people to at least closely skim that chapter! )..
                    I did read it, but I'm afraid it doesn't really resolve my problem. Playing fast and loose with per-scene/-chapter/-story limits is one thing, I feel, while the Conspiracy system...

                    As far as I can tell, Conspiracy Actions have an intended pacing to them, but that pacing is assumed rather than explained. This, coupled with how heavily abstracted they are, divorces them from organic play in a way I don't quite know how to resolve.
                    In a better example, perhaps, a lot of the potential complications of a failed Conspiracy Actions (Step Six, p. 67) just sound like things players are given to do on a whim. What stops the characters from trying to befriend the attacking Deviant, or shoot the diplomats, or record and disseminate the attack on them using the core gameplay system?

                    I have less of a problem with downtime actions and projects, though they hit on the same unspoken pacing issue as regular Conspiracy Actions. They feature an even greater level of abstraction, but the abstracted events are all off-screen, or would otherwise involve instances of the ST rolling against themself.

                    (One thing I had missed on my first readthrough was the Best Defense sidebar, which neatly resolves another issue I had with the system. It's actually pretty elegant.)
