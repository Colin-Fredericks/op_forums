    Playtester here. I may be able to help some.

    Originally posted by SdeSpencer View Post
    1. Inescapable seems worse in just literally every way vs Suppressible. Suppressible has all the advantages, and you always have the OPTION to just not suppress, while Inescapable you don't have an option AND have a permanent disadvantage.
    I found this very unclear as well and I talked with Eric about cleaning up the language here. (Edit: I could be wrong on this) There is an advantage to using the Inescapable deviation: it adds a magnitude to Relentless, but not to the secondary scar (which is the unclear part).
    Example: You have a Relentless mag 2 scar and you increase the mag to 3 with the Inescapable deviation. You choose a mag 2 secondary scar to attach to it. Then you choose a magnitude 3 variation (or two 2's, etc.) to entangle with Relentless.

    Originally posted by SdeSpencer View Post
    2. The bonus for having a Relentless Variation seems really lackluster, even for the Suppressible version, but ESPECIALLY CRAZY MORE for inescapable. It has basically no advantages vs just having a toggled variation of any case (as a toggled variation has no disadvantage for being toggled on, the disadvantage is the permanent scar), it does something that for certain variations is basically a HORRIBLE scar on its own (Giant and Miniaturization coming to mind, but Camouflage and basically every other Toggled variation with specific exceptions are still really bad).
    I think my answer to whether the scar itself is good vs. horrible is a more subjective thing. I for one LOVE this scar and have used it in playtesting. This scar is great at modeling characters that work oddly. The Cyclops example with Relentless Power Failure is a good one. A character concept that I considered for testing was one that used two versions of the scar, an Inescapable Relentless Gigantic with a controlled Relentless Camouflage. He's huge, and he has to concentrate to stay visible. It's a scar; it's job is to make your life complicated and make an interesting story while generating beats.
    Or consider Animal Transformation: if you want to BE a bear that can shapeshift into a woman on command, that's how to do it. The character I played in playtest sessions had (Inescapable) Relentless Animal Transformation (squirrel) attached to Conspicuous Appearance. I was for most purposes, a squirrel. I had Pheromones to communicate (mostly) normally, but ran into trouble doing things like talking on the phone. That's what I wanted. (Granted there will be a better way to model this later with the new form in the upcoming Clade Companion, but I didn't have that at the time.)
    I didn't play the invisible giant because I fell in love with the electrokinetic squirrel. Also I didn't like how you couldn't take advantage of the healing aspect when you can't change your size. This scar might be laden with more drawback than you want to deal with in many cases. But I think it has tons of uses to build interesting characters.

    Edit: added that the squirrel relentless scar was Inescapable.
    Fixed terminology.

                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                            Turning off the Variation requires a roll, costs you every time, and is still just as subject to intensifying from Instability as any other Scar. The roll suppression requires is also (barring Acclimation) a single Attribute that is penalized by any degree of Instability at all, and dramatic failure on that roll gives you more Instability.

                                            For contrast, Inescapable can pay its price by such things as not being able to speak or needing to perform maintenance to keep the Variation working or having lower effective Presence.

                                            Persistent Scars are not inherently worse than Controlled Scars.
                                            I'm not saying that Persistent scars are "inherently worse". I'm saying that it's "inherently worse" to NOT have an option vs HAVING an option.

                                            Let's consider something for a moment.

                                            You have a 5 dot Magnitude Variation and want to entangle it with Relentless Variation. You can

                                            a. Use Suppressible. You now have an (Very Dangerous and/or Costly and/or Unreliable) OPTION to turn off your power. You may always choose to not turn off your power ever (Controlled Variations will never force you to use them), and the power will have no downsides.

                                            b. Use Inescapable. You do not have any option to turn off your power. You cannot turn off your power AND you get a Major disadvantage with it.

                                            Effectively, it's not the difference between a Persistent Scar vs a Controlled Scar, it's the ability to do something vs no ability to do so, and the no ability also comes with a disadvantage. A good comparison is to think of it in reverse.

                                            Suppressible's best comparison is just having a Variation with a Controlled Scar (in this Analogy, the Variation is the ability to temporarily turn off your Relentless Variation)

                                            In this analogy then, Inescapable's best comparison is LACKING that Variation, and then getting a Scar that is equally as bad as if you HAD taken the Variation.
