    Tier 3 Hunter/Deviant crossover issues

    05-08-2024, 09:48 PM
    The Advance copy of Clades Companion has been released now, which pretty much concludes the Deviant line. I hear that there's a fan who's working on a Player's Guide for it, which I'm definitely looking forward to; and I'm looking into a STV product of my own, as well, based on the Mad Science hack in my sig.

    And it's in that context that I'm making this post. I'm looking at doing the Mad Science supplement as a Hunter/Deviant crossover ruleset, drawing from both Hunter's Endowments system and Deviant's… well, just about everything. The reasoning for this is that both Hunter and Deviant share the feature of not actually having metaphysics of their own, meaning that the supernatural stuff that each game features is all over the board. Which is ideal for my take on Mad Science, since the core principle I'm using is that there is no fundamental metaphysics in the Chronicles of Darkness: the more you investigate, the deeper the rabbit hole gets; and you can never get to the bottom of it.

    Another reason for doing it as a crossover is that my scientists are conceptually very much like hunters, in that they differ from ordinary humans only in terms of how they react to encountering the weirdness of the setting: where ordinary people turn away from it and hunters confront it, these scientists seek to understand it. But the immediate results of their investigations are better represented by Deviant's system of Variations and Scars than by Hunter's Endowments.

    And that's where I'm looking for feedback: how Tier 3 Hunter compared and contrasts with Deviant, what issues come up when combining the two games, and how to address those issues.

    First, the Conspiracies. By default, Hunter and Deviant have very different concepts of what a Conspiracy is, with the primary point of similarity being that both varieties of Conspiracies have access to “weird tech”, for lack of a better term. I'll get to that part shortly; for now, the issue I'm wondering about has to do with how the two varieties of Conspiracies differ from each other. The first difference, broadly, is that Hunter Conspiracies are designed to be friendly towards the protagonists, while Deviant Conspiracies are what the protagonists are fighting. But even with the Devoted Companion, which flips the Deviants from the guys who oppose the Conspiracies to the guys who support the Conspiracies, there's still a big difference between the two kinds of Conspiracies.

    So, how to address that?

    Second, the “weird tech” of each game. Deviant focuses on a system of Variations and Scars, where every power has an upside and a downside and if you have access to those powers, it's probably killing you; whereas Hunter provides Endowments which don't really have downsides per se; certainly nothing like Deviant's Scars. There's also the fact that, prior to the Clade Companion, Deviant's system of Variations and Scars was fundamentally linked to altering people; whereas most of Hunter's Endowments take the form of tools to be used by the Hunters. The Clade Companion has added Prototypes, which is its term for equipment that provides access to Variations; and Hunter has one official Conspiracy that engages in altering people, featuring the Endowment of Thaumatechnology. So the “it equips people” vs. “it changes people” distinction isn't a particularly clear-cut one.

    So how to square that, both conceptually and rules-wise?

    The one thing both games agree on is that “weird tech” is the domain of corporate powers: individuals can devise Prototypes/conduct R&D within the framework of a particular Conspiracy's specialty; an individual can't establish a new framework. And, in fact, very little is known about how new frameworks are created. On the one hand, this gives the Storyteller absolute control over which kinds of “weird tech” is out there; but on the other hand, it doesn't leave much room for Doc Brown or Dr. Jekyll or Moreau.

    That's where my Mad Science system comes in: it deals with the pushing back of the boundaries of science, with the likes of Variations/Scars and Endowments as byproducts of that process.

      So, how to address that?
      In both games, if you work for a conspiracy, your conspiracy is friendly to you (for a given value of "friendly"), but the Web of Pain is not a united front and Hunter 2e introduces a modifier to breaking point rolls for encountering supernatural powers used by another hunter for a reason.

      Second, the “weird tech” of each game. Deviant focuses on a system of Variations and Scars, where every power has an upside and a downside and if you have access to those powers, it's probably killing you; whereas Hunter provides Endowments which don't really have downsides per se; certainly nothing like Deviant's Scars.
      Endowments have costs and drawbacks for their use and Scars can be as simple as "it costs Willpower to use." They're the same concept expressed in different idioms.

      I've mentioned before that it's important to not treat all Instability as that one scene from Akira, but it's also important to not treat all Scars as the End Stage — with a not-insignificant number of Scars it's plausible to see no mechanical effects from Instability until the medium level.

      There's also the fact that, prior to the Clade Companion, Deviant's system of Variations and Scars was fundamentally linked to altering people; whereas most of Hunter's Endowments take the form of tools to be used by the Hunters. The Clade Companion has added Prototypes, which is its term for equipment that provides access to Variations; and Hunter has one official Conspiracy that engages in altering people, featuring the Endowment of Thaumatechnology. So the “it equips people” vs. “it changes people” distinction isn't a particularly clear-cut one.
      Even before Prototypes were introduced, the Devoted Companion gave more detail to the ability of conspiracy Icons to provide access to Variations. Even without the presence of non-invasive Endowments, the supernatural powers a hunter can develop do not metaphysically alter them at a major level beyond the degree having another Supernatural Merit does.

      A deviant gets their powers from having their soul cracked open, and both sorts of conspiracies can and do develop occult and scientific advantages that are not reliant upon the state of the Remade with the application of time and resources (and no small amount of implicit or explicit moral compromise). The Field Projects Division still has Integrity and — barring explicit drawbacks to the contrary for their implants that nevertheless mean something different — will not slowly die from lack of work or vengeance just because there's an alien tapeworm in their body giving them Unseen Sense: Visceraworlders.

      The one thing both games agree on is that “weird tech” is the domain of corporate powers: individuals can devise Prototypes/conduct R&D within the framework of a particular Conspiracy's specialty; an individual can't establish a new framework. And, in fact, very little is known about how new frameworks are created. On the one hand, this gives the Storyteller absolute control over which kinds of “weird tech” is out there; but on the other hand, it doesn't leave much room for Doc Brown or Dr. Jekyll or Moreau.
      Establishing a new framework is largely within the realm of plot device. If you need it to happen, it happens, and if you need a mechanical determinant for whether it can be successfully made to happen there are a multitude of core systems that can be used to arbitrate how long it takes or how big a set of complications comes with the result.

      New frameworks are created by varying combinations of time, serendipitous finds, supernatural backing, and the kind of iteration for which a social/logistical machine is needed. Oftentimes the work of a singular obsessive is the thing on which they are the subject matter expert through spending most of their life on the research and/or building the framework as they discover it in action.

              The difference between Endowments and Variations isn't just meta/mechanical; it is metaphysical, at least in universe.

              Endowments don't break the soul, don't cause Divergence.
              Hunter Conspiracies, by luck or design, have a lighter touch on their agents. They can/do toe the line between having special abilities and having functional people.
              Deviants are broken people, fundamentally. That is the point of them. They careen from one disaster to another, only finding stability through destruction of Touchstones.

              Conspiracies don't necessarily distinguish between "favours Hunters" and "favours Devoted". They might self identify based on the number of Renegades they create though. Consistency is king and knowing that your agents aren't going to breakdown or start hunting you to stop the pain can be a big selling point.
              Hunter Conspiracies generally have stabilized enough that they don't need to actively participate in the Web of Pain. They have enough 'umph!' that the risks outweigh the gains, generally. They can (and should) be tempted on occasion.

              I'd argue that the Lucifuge actively recruits potential Genetypals Deviants before they Diverge. I'm trying to find my copy of Inferno so I can write up L'Enfants Diabloque as Genetypal Conspiracy; it includes the Lucifuge as not only people who hunt them, (including those who go Renegade from the Chaldeans) but implies that the limit of 666 is basically how many members they can have without undergoing Divergence.
              Not something for the "Mad Science" book, but I thought it might be relevant.
