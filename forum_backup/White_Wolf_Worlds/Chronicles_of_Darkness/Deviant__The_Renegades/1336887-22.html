Perpetual Camouflage 5 may complicate your life in some ways, but you're still the Invisible Man, and that means your stealth capabilities beyond what any Baseline could achieve.

            The sort of general rule I have with Scars is:

            Mag 1: These only come into play when the player roleplays them for a Beat.
            Mag 2: These have a couple mechanical teeth but either don’t come into play very often or carry costs that seldom carry beyond the scope of the current scene.
            Mag 3: The standard price of power we expect to see in game lines. They clearly carry some cost or risk, but they aren’t so problematic that you would hesitate to use them in a scene where they’re useful.
            Mag 4: This is where the price of power begins to get a bit steep. You think carefully before bringing these out (or taking them as Persistent Scars), because the cost is high enough to “follow” you into other scenes or to severely impede you in the current scene.
            Mag 5: These are doozies with the potential to come into play constantly, reshaping the way the Broken’s player interacts with the game.

                                Re Relentless conversation
                                SdeSpencer , I certainly understand your point, but I'm not sure that the other point is coming through to you.
                                I accept that Suppressible is inherently more advantageous; you have been successful in making that point. Having that option to bail out of your power is better from an "I don't want to get screwed" standpoint. But (more advantageous) better does not equal (more interesting story) better. Playing a character who can't just stop being a squirrel when my best friend is dying in the next room because I can't turn this damn doorknob might be the story I want to tell. Having the choice to turn it off changes the dynamic significantly.

                                I get that a drawback "should" have a corresponding benefit, because that is how we players think of these things. However, I'd encourage you to think of your scar/variation entanglements as holistic storytelling devices rather than a separate list of powers and flaws. Those two things might not seem like they have anything to do with each other, but consider what those bastards DID to you. It makes your life really hard. They're STILL after you. They need to pay for it. That's the core of the story these mechanics want to tell. Figuring out how to avoid getting screwed is kinda headed in the wrong direction. Setting yourself up to get screwed on terms you think is interesting is more on point. It's not about the beats you get when it happens; it's about what kind of squirrel do you want to be. Are you the one who changes into human form and opens that door or the one who swears revenge as your friend takes their last breath?
                                That's my take.

                                Re Hybrid Form
                                Dataweaver and Satchel Did you realize that you don't "pay" separately for the variations nested under Hybrid too? For example, a starting character with Hybrid 5 could put two 4s or three 3s under it for 5 points of variation.

                                Sidenote: I'm not sure on this, but you could probably entangle hybrid form with a mag 4 scar if you used your scar-free point. That's a lot of bang for your buck if that's what you're after. (And yes, I'm crapping all over my above "but the story!" comments with this note. I just want to make sure you all don't make the same assumptions about this variation that I did. I'm a dirty hypocrite. You're welcome.)

                                Relentless Mimicry allows you to hide other scars and the entangled variations, which is awesome, but purchasing the variation doesn't buy you those powers. It also has its own scar in addition to the hidden variations' scars, as opposed to Hybrid's variations' being scar free. Mimicry's scar also is "on" when the powers are off rather than hybrid's which is "on" when the powers are on.

                                                (I’m tired and this is just at the general collective world at this point)

                                                You know what really simple, direct example. You have 3 magnitude Camouflage (A Subtle, Easy Example Variation).

                                                Option 1: You pick Relentless Variation (Suppressible [Unstable Variation]), and then never Suppress it. You get all the advantages and disadvantages of Perpetual Camouflage, and No Downsides besides those already innate to Perpetual Camouflage.
                                                (You also, if you really need to, have the option to take a medium instability for getting rid of it for a scene, which is a steep price to pay, but the option being there is nice)

                                                Option 2: You pick Relentless Variation (Inescapable [Sensory Deprivation]). You get all the advantages and disadvantages of Perpetual Camouflage, but also are permanently Deaf.
                                                (You also don't have the Nice, if costly option to bail out, though you were never going to use it)

                                                (Other options, for Posterity’s Sake)

                                                Option 3: You pick Unstable Variation. You get none of the advantages or disadvantages of even a persistent variation, but you can choose to activate Camouflage for the scene for a medium instability.

                                                Option 4: You pick Sensory Deprivation. You can turn Camouflage on for the scene at no penalty, but you are Deaf. However, you do not get the advantages and disadvantages of Perpetual Camouflage.

                                                All of these are different in character concept of course, and I can’t really say if 3 or 4 are better, and I honestly can’t say if 2 is better or worse than them either. However, I can say with fairly large personal certainty that 1 is better than 2 or 3 (and presumably 4, though it’s hard to compare them).
