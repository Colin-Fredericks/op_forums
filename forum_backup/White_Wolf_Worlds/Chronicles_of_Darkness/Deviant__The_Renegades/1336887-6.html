    Because every game needs a Princess link, especially half-shonen ones, here's some stereotypes I made on the Discord, plus the Clade views:

    Clubs: The unnatural procedure of what was done to you is matched only by its pointlessness. I so sincerely wish I could say I understood you, but I can't - I can only help this never happen again.

    Diamonds: Once, I thought that "things man was not meant to know" was a contradiction in terms. Then you show up. Any question that forces someone else to be the answer doesn't deserve to be asked...

    Hearts: When we're done, the people who did this won't leave a legacy. That's my promise.

    Spades: ...I'm sorry. There's nothing funny about this. Except schadenfreude; I'm planning to have a lot of that, wanna help me set it up?

    Swords: Normally, I would tell you that passion dedicated to revenge never solved anything. Normally, I'm not dealing with abusers on a cosmic level. I'll help you pick up the pieces when we're done.

    Tears: There is no end to this; the Rebellious Provinces are in your enemies' thrall. Take who you need, and come with me, away from the conspiracies, away from this rotted world, where it's safe - we just need a few favors, time to time.

    Storms: You get me.

    Mirrors: I...wha...People did this? I...no, no, it's okay, we just have to get those bad guys too, it'll be better, I promise...it has to be...

    ----

    Cephalic: Your "Darkness" needs more than strength to beat. Thankfully, I can make its enablers stop.

    Chimeric: Protip of nature; the foundation of any hope is a full stomach and a safe den.

    Coactive: Your power talks back to mine. That's honestly creepy, but it's friendlier than your natural enemy.

    Invasive: Tools made from yourself are still ones you can't put down.

    Mutant: Everything is a survival adaptation. Altruism's one that's proven its worth.

            I have a concern about two of the Clades: the Cephalists and the Mutants. The other three Clades are described in terms of what is done to you. These two are described in terms of how you react to it. In particular, both are defined in terms of how you reject what they try to do to you: Cephalists reject it on a mental, emotional, or perhaps spiritual level, whole Mutants reject it on a physical level. Both imply that your Progenitor was going for something else than what he ended up with.

            But I've seen too many stories featuring what amount to Deviants with powers of the mind or soul who were engineered to be what they are to buy that: for the Cephalists in particular, I think there ought to be at alternate explanation for how you become one that doesn't have rejection of the intended change built into it. It might involve brain surgery, psychoactive drugs, a flatliner process, a hypnosis technique, or a device that messes with your mind. The commonality would be that the process that triggers your Deviation is one that's designed to warp your mind (or soul).

            That said, if it's too late to get something like this into Deviant: the Renegades, perhaps it could be addressed in Deviant: the Devoted?

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                You want a Princess link? Replace the human conspirators in Deviant with a race of cute but callous aliens, and you have Puella Magi Madoka Magica.
                And going beyond that, there's a lot of other magical girl anime and other such media that works well with the format(specifics in spoilers, and then the format overall)-

                Spoilers for Symphogear, Princess Tutu, Yuki Yuna is a Hero, DNAngel, Kill La Kill, Tokyo Mew Mew, and Sailor Nothing.

                EDIT: Double checking how to spoiler, section will be back soon.
                Symphogear Season 1 an, to a lesser extent, 2 features body horror that revolves around how the use of the magical girl artifact will invariably kill the lead character, and in other instances features how innocent people can get roped into and used by evil organization, and while the Magical Girl element is not ingrained in their bodies, it has a lot of that good Devoted angles to read regardless.

                Princess Tutu is effectively a magical girl story with multiple Deviant types created to fulfill the twisted desire of their repsective elements, with the eponymous character effectively being what would happen if you a Manticores Variations making them a sort of Invasive Deviant and Princess Krahe being a subtly portrayed Chimeric, and to cap it all off the lead character has to confront the fact that her story ends with her power causing her to vanish.

                Yuki Yuna is a Hero has it's magical girls operate normally until they need to boost up for a major crisis, wherein their second stage of magical girlness causes them to develop crippling disabilities each time it's unleashed-and not only do the powers that be know this, they eventually turn girls too crippled to continue in living idols for the state's faith.

                D.N. Angel is basically the story of a Genotypal Deviant with a multiple personality Scar and a lot of tension in how each of those personalities have different Loyalty Touchstones, all while dealing with another Genotypal Deviant going after them. While not getting into the powers betraying you and dehumanizing you as much, the conflict of interests in the lead characters as entities in the same body feel very on brand, particularly in early episodes.

                I want to mention Earth Maiden Arjuna, but I haven't seen that since I was a freshman in High school, so this is all we get.

                Kill La Kill centers around three Invasive Deviants, given power with consequences when taken to far from Life Fibers, two of which spend a lot of time trying to fuck each other up until they finally get over themselves and go after the third one. Mankanshoku is best Loyalty Touchstone. No explanation for Senketsu or Junketsu, I'll admit.

                Tokyo Mew Mew literally starts with the protagonist getting injected with genes from an endangered cat species, and the rest of the group fleshes out from there. Sometimes they inconveniently turn into animals. I'd comment more, but it has similar problems to Arjuna Not a Magical Girl series, but if you think by that token, Fruits Basket has some decent basis, you'd be correct.

                Sailor Nothing may not burn bodies out directly per se, but it features a whole lot of Volunteers who, as far as they know, have to put up with the physical ailments that denote the presence of their enemies until they die at an inevitably young age, and once the favor of the patron is removed, the titular character finds transformation to just kind of be an terrible experience. It can also radically grow wildly out of control, and can do a lot of damage as a result.


                And there's more asides

                At it's core, there are a lot of magical girl stories out there where in the protagonist either literally doesn't get a choice or doesn't fully grasp what it means to take on the mantle, and that with the power comes conditions that tend to mess with their lives and often lead to fatality or severe crippling of the ability to live at all, who are often balanced between their growing relationships to the people around them as they become friendships and their conflict with the series bad guys, with a common point, if not long running arc, being that the characters having to make the decision to abandon the former in order to fight the latter, where in fact keeping with them often has real metaphysical results for them. Bonus points include that for every supernatural entity offering it, there's often a story of a human offering it, often to exploit the empowered, there's a good sized number of "science"-based magical girls, and one of the most common ways to subvert the genre even before Madoka was a thing was to introduce some kind of body horror element to it.

                Now granted, a lot of this is because magical girls share the same spaces as superheroes and tokusatsu shows-but yeah, the genre actually has a wide range of material you could draw from for Deviant if you wanted to, from very lightly associated to Might As Well Have Been a Deviant Story.

                [/magicalgirlgenrenerd](for now)

                                                        Gotta say, I have an appreciation for how organically you can slip a Devoted Deviant into the antagonists of other games as a way of diversifying them.*

                                                        Theoretically that can be said of any of the splats(particularly Beast), of course, but as a point of comparison-If I'm running a game of non-crossover game of Geist but want to spice up the world by having elements of the other lines pop up once in a while(as I do all the time), I could add a werewolf or a changeling to an Eaters of the Dead or Tyrant Krewe, but I've got some things to answer there in order to get it a little more lined up. By contrast, if the players come across a cult of Eaters of the Dead, both mostly human and more inhuman entities, and they have a Deviant with them, I don't have to wonder long how that happened. Games like Mage, Promethean, and Demon almost beg for them to slip in with the rest.

                                                        It's a versatile splat, even if you don't wanna run a Deviant game, is what I'm saying.

                                                        (That said, "Rejected" and "Ain't No Rest for the Wicked" beg to be Deviant games.)

                                                        EDIT: Also, I do like how that sort of mentality works well for complicating personal organizations. Your krewe might decide it's worth chasing, or it might not-but either way, it complicates things when you get to a day when you find a Deviant in your own group.
