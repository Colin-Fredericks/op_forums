    This week, I'm showcasing Chapter Three, which is all about Variations. Not only does Shallow Graves have 35 new Variations, but it also devotes several pages to the process of designing new Variations, whether they directly borrow mechanical or conceptual bits from existing Variations as a starting point or are entirely new. Today I'm showing off one example of each: Sticky Filaments and Cryokinesis. Two more examples (Dream Invader and Extra Appendages) are available for free at the link in my signature (and as always, patrons have access to the full text of the first half of the chapter).

    Let's get going!

    Sticky Filaments (• to •••••)

    Overt

    Tiered, Toggled

    The Deviant can fire long, sticky filaments that serve several functions.

    At Magnitude •, choose one:

    • Anchor: As a Directed effect, the Remade shoots one end of the filament at a solid surface, which it sticks to. It can serve as a rope until the end of the scene, after which it disintegrates. The Broken (and others) can use the filament to climb, rappel, employ it as a belay, or even pull an object off a shelf or ledge. It is Size 1 and has Durability equal to half Scar Power (rounded up) for purposes of attempts to break or cut it. It can support the weight of most objects with a Size no greater than three times Scar Power.

    • Tangler: The Deviant can use the filament to entangle enemies. This is a Gentle Athletics Lash (Disabling) 1 or imposes the Viscous Tilt (Deviant, p. 320) on a 10-yard/meter radius area within range.

    At Magnitude ••, the Remade enjoys both Magnitude • effects.

    At Magnitude •••, as Magnitude ••, and choose one:

    • Cocoon: Used offensively, the filament is either a Gentle Athletics Lash (Disabling, Immobilizing) 3 or imposes the severe Viscous Tilt on a 10-yard/meter radius area within range.

    • Swing: As long as his environment includes high ceilings, buildings, or other objects higher than his head, the Broken can use the Anchor effect to jump and swing from one point to another, mimicking Brachiation (Leaper) 2. This effect has the Reflexive keyword. In addition, when the Remade successfully targets one object or solid surface with Anchor, she may attempt to connect the other end of the filament to another object or solid surface in range as a reflexive action. If the secondary Scar Finesse roll succeeds, this connection is as slack or taut as the Broken wishes it to be; if unsuccessful, she chooses whether the second end of the filament hangs loose or is attached to her.

    At Magnitude ••••, the Deviant enjoys both Magnitude ••• effects.

    Deviations

    • Steely (+1 Magnitude): The filament is particularly strong. When used with Anchor or Cocoon, its Durability is instead equal to Scar Power, and it can support the weight of objects with a Size no greater than six times Scar Power.

    The Chimeric can produce strands of material that resemble spider silk but are much more durable, but other arachnids view her as either a rival or prey that they must attack and devour (Animal Enmity; Persistent).

    The Invasive’s cybernetic arm can fire various grappling hooks and nets, but it’s not exactly subtle (Conspicuous Appearance; Persistent).

    The Mutant’s hair consists of extremely stretchy, sticky threads that he pulls out and deploys in various ways, but no one envies the extensive haircare routine he requires to keep it from becoming a useless tangle (Maintenance; Persistent).

    Inspiration: Brachiation, Lash

    Cryokinesis (• to •••••)

    Overt

    Directed, Tiered, Toggled

    The Deviant commands powers of cold and ice.

    At Magnitude •, choose one:

    • Chill: As an Aura effect, the Deviant may reduce the ambient temperature — but not below freezing. This creates a level 1 Extreme Environment (a moderate Environmental Tilt such as Blizzard or Slick, in action scenes) or reduces the level of a heat-based Extreme Environment by one. The Remade is immune to the effects of the Extreme Environments and Environmental Tilts he produces with this Variation. Outdoors, the radius of the cold aura is 40 yards/meters instead of 10 yards/meters.

    • Conjure: The Remade can conjure objects made of snow and ice with a maximum Size equal to Variation Magnitude times Scar Power and Durability equal to half Scar Power (rounded up). These objects cannot have moving parts and are treated as transparent for the purpose of calculating their resistance to damage when used as cover. They must appear within one yard/meter of a stable surface capable of bearing their weight, so this cannot be used to drop huge balls of ice on one’s opponents. Especially detailed or aesthetically pleasing ice constructs — or prisons of ice conjured around the transformed’s opponents — may require appropriate Scar Finesse or Skill rolls, at the Storyteller’s discretion.

    • Frost: The Remade saps away the heat in a target with a Size no greater than the Variation’s Magnitude times Scar Power — cooling (but not freezing) liquids or machinery. Used against a living target, it inflicts bashing damage with a weapon bonus equal to half Scar Power (rounded up) and inflicts one of the following Tilts on an exceptional success: Arm Wrack, Blinded, Knocked Down, or Leg Wrack.

    At Magnitude ••, as Magnitude •, but choose two Magnitude • effects.

    At Magnitude •••, as Magnitude ••, but the Broken enjoys all three Magnitude • effects.

    At Magnitude ••••, as Magnitude •••, and choose one:

    • Freeze: As Frost (including the effects of an exceptional success), but attacks with this effect inflict lethal damage with a weapon bonus equal to Scar Power and impose the Immobilized Tilt, with the bonds considered to have a Durability of half Scar Power (rounded up). Further, this can freeze liquids solid in an instant or inflict Structure damage on objects, halving Durability (rounded up) unless the object is designed to endure extremely cold temperatures.

    • Whiteout: As Chill, but the ambient temperature falls well below freezing, creating a level 3 Extreme Environment (the severe Blizzard and moderate Slick Tilts, in action scenes).

    At Magnitude •••••, as Magnitude ••••, but the Deviant enjoys both effects.

    Connecting himself with the principles of entropy, the Cephalist draws heat out of the Universe forever to produce localized areas of cold. He is painfully aware that every use of his power shortens the lifespan of the Universe by a few fractions of a second (Shame; Persistent).

    The Coactive made a pact with ice spirits, but they are cruel and force her to share their power with those who hurt her (Asset; Involuntary; Composure).

    The Mutant’s very presence is cold, but any environment warmer than –10 Celsius is hostile to their anatomy (Native Environment; Persistent).

    Onyx Path Freelancer and former Deviant line developer.

        Time for some more Variations. Today I'm showing off Opener of Ways and Cursed Flesh. By going to the link in my signature, you can also see Traumavore and Clairvoyance for free (or full text of the chapter, for patrons). Enjoy!

        Opener of Ways (• to •••••)

        Overt

        Directed, Tiered, Toggled

        The Deviant wields power over doors and other portals.

        For the purpose of this Variation, a door is any opening that can be opened and closed, through which the Remade can pass. It can therefore include anything from an airplane hangar door to a panel covering the entrance to an access tunnel.

        At Magnitude •, the Broken may open or close a target door as long as nothing prevents it from being opened or closed (including a mundane lock). She can also sense all doors within 10 yards/meters, which is not a Directed effect.

        At Magnitude ••, as Magnitude •, and the Deviant may lock or unlock a target door, whether the lock is mechanical or electronic.

        At Magnitude •••, as Magnitude ••, and choose one:

        • Erase: The Broken may erase an existing door, causing a continuation of the wall, floor, or ceiling to replace it. The door reappears at the end of the scene.

        • Force: The Remade may create a door where there wasn’t one before, provided the solid surface on which the door appears is no more than one yard/meter thick. This door vanishes at the end of the scene.

        At Magnitude ••••, the Deviant benefits from both Magnitude ••• effects.

        Deviation

        • Occult (+1 Magnitude): The Variation also works on magical doors, including portals that lead to alien otherworlds or distant places. A supernatural door opened or closed by this Variation remains so until the end of the scene unless the Deviant is defeated in a Clash of Wills. A supernatural door is considered locked if it will only open to someone who holds a special key or meets specific criteria, while one that can be opened by a password or physical manipulation of the existing space is considered unlocked. If erased, a magical door remains closed until at least the end of the chapter (and likely stays closed longer or permanently). The Broken has no control over the destination of a forced supernatural door; it leads wherever the Storyteller needs it to lead. Forcing a supernatural door more often than once per story inflicts a point of medium Instability each time after the first the Deviant does so.

        Living things find their way into all sorts of places they shouldn’t be able to get, but none can exceed the Chimeric’s ability to do so when he also has Out of Phase active, although he never remembers quite how he got in (Tabula Rasa; Controlled; Wits; Deviations: Dependent Variation [Out of Phase]).

        The Coactive’s spirit companion opens many doors to her, but it demands regular ritual sacrifices to coax it into continuing to do so (Maintenance; Persistent).

        The Mutant’s biology interacts strangely with what Baselines describe as “doors”. They shut others out, but they somehow never bar his passage. He is, however, bound to respond to some passphrases in ways that rarely do him any favors (Subliminal Conditioning; Persistent).

        Mutant Variation: Cursed Flesh

        Subtle

        Discrete, Toggled

        The Broken can impose an unwelcome burden on others.

        This Variation allows the Deviant to impart a single Persistent Scar (including Deviations) chosen at the time she develops this Variation (the secondary Scar). This is otherwise built like Sacred Flesh (Deviant, p. 151), complete with options for Boons, Secretions, Tokens, and Fragments (although it does not impart Variations). The Remade may purchase this Variation multiple times, and each time allows the Broken to impose a different Scar.

        At Magnitude •, choose a Magnitude •• Scar.

        At Magnitude ••, choose a Magnitude ••• Scar.

        At Magnitude •••, choose a Magnitude •••• Scar.

        At Magnitude ••••, choose a Magnitude ••••• Scar.

        Deviations

        • Aura (+1 Magnitude): Must be a Boon. The Scar affects all viable targets within short range, whether friend or foe.

        • Parasite (+0 Magnitude): The secondary Scar is Controlled or Involuntary, instead, but it can only affect other Remade, adding an additional drawback to all the Variations entangled to one of the victim’s Scars. The Remade imposing this curse may choose a known Scar (or stipulate that it affects whichever Scar is entangled with a Variation known to her); otherwise, it affects a Scar at random. This cannot affect Persistent Perpetual Variations.

        • Infection (+1 Magnitude): As Parasite, but the Scar may target supernatural beings other than Deviants, punishing them whenever they use an occult ability that requires an action and/or roll to activate. This affects a single supernatural ability — either a known capability or one chosen at random.

        • Infestation (+1 Magnitude): As Parasite, but the Scar instead imposes a new burden on all the target’s Variations, regardless of the Scars entangled with them. If paired with Infection and used against a supernatural being who isn’t a Remade, it affects all supernatural abilities that require an action and/or a roll to activate.

        • Long-Lasting (+1 Magnitude): The target instead suffers the Scar until the end of the current chapter (instead of scene, as Sacred Flesh). If this Scar is applied as a Boon, the Deviant may spend a point of Willpower to extend its effects until the end of the next chapter, potentially extending its duration from chapter to chapter.

        • Randomized (+0 Magnitude): Choose additional secondary Scars equal to Scar Power. Each time the Broken activates the Variation, it imposes one of these Scars chosen at random.

        • Versatile (+1 Magnitude): As Randomized and exclusive with it, but the Deviant decides which Scar to impose each time she activates this Variation.

        The Chimeric’s bite carries a curse that will bring out a bestial side not long after (a Boon with Alternate Persona with the Bestial Deviation), but she hates using it and can only deploy it when she has Inhuman Digestion active (Shame; Persistent; Deviations: Dependent Variation, Intimate).

        The Invasive synthesizes a toxin in an artificial gland in their right shoulder that, if it enters a Deviant’s bloodstream, causes her to destabilize if she uses her powers (a Secretion with the Parasite Deviation that imposes Unstable Variation), but the gland’s waste products thin their blood (Hemophilia; Persistent).

        The Mutant’s hair, when braided into a bracelet, circlet, or necklace, immobilizes the arms and legs of anyone bound with them — a Fragment that imposes the Missing Limb Scar. However, the process of growing and crafting such accessories requires substantial time and effort (Preparation; Controlled; Dexterity).

            One thing I'm looking forward to is the chapter where you address how to use Variations creatively. I have a concept for a Deviant who was a man who got infected by a technovirus that turned him into something not unlike a T-1000 Terminator (the “liquid metal” one from the second movie); and I've been trying to figure out how to do that without it being some complex mesh of multiple Variations, where I have to anticipate each and every use that a liquid metal body could be put to; and if I overlook something or don't have enough Variations available to cover everything, tough.

            What I'd prefer to do would be to take one Variation that says something like “you can give your body the properties of a liquid”, and perhaps a second Variation that says something like “you can give your body the properties of metal”, and then have all of the ways that those two can be used individually or in combination flow from there.

            What I don't want to do is say “the liquid part lets him reshape his arms into the form of blades, and the metal part lets him harden them into actual blades; so I need to take the Lash Variation so that he can turn his arms into blades”, and “the liquid part lets him move through bars, so I need to give him a Variation that lets him phase through solid objects”, and so on. Just one or two broadly defined Variations, and a way to get creative with how they're applied.

                    In polling the patrons, the consensus is that rather than share another Variation or two, I should share the guidelines for making Inspired Variations. So, here is this week's bonus content:

                    Variation Principles

                    Designing Variations is more art than science, but there are methods that make it easier.

                    Nearly all the Variations in Deviant: The Renegades and its supplements started with a high concept — the main thing the Variation is meant to do. Most of the time it ends up being the name of the Variation. Flight is about moving through the air, for example, and Pyrokinesis is about manipulating fire.

                    Sometimes the high concept suggests several related effects. Electrokinesis, for example, is a whole toolkit in itself; its possible uses simply pile up in the mind. Other times, the high concept is quite straightforward. Carapace is armor, reducing the damage the Deviant suffers from attacks, for example.

                    From the high concept, there are two directions you can go: You can either borrow and modify effects from existing Variations, or you can create a new Variation from scratch. You can also mix the two approaches — borrowing for some effects while starting fresh when an effect doesn’t match anything in existence.

                    Borrowing

                    Variations in Deviant are meant to be customizable. Every published Variation offers three examples of ways it can be flavored to fit Remade of different Clades and character concepts. This works the other way around, too. If your character concept has powers that aren’t covered by any existing Variation, but you see parts of multiple Variations that do things they should be able to do, it’s perfectly fine to combine from each of them to achieve the concept.

                    This is easiest when the new Variation is essentially two existing Tiered Variations twined together — a choice between two Magnitude • Variations at Magnitude •, with higher-Magnitude effects building on each of the two Magnitude • effects. Even in cases where one, some, or all the source Variations are Discrete or skip around, you can think of the whole as though it were multiple Variations entangled with the same Scar.

                    A Magnitude ••• combined Variation, for example, can be built to emulate up to two Magnitude •• Variations, three Magnitude • Variations, or a single Magnitude ••• Variation. One of Magnitude •••••, on the other hand, can mimic up to five Magnitude • Variations, four Magnitude •• Variations, three Magnitude ••• Variations, two Magnitude •••• Variations, or a single Magnitude ••••• — together with all the relevant prerequisites.

                    This means that Variations that are purely a combination of two different Variations’ effects won’t have a Magnitude 5 effect on par with either of the parent Variations. Adding a twist to such twined Variations can be a good way to make it greater than the sum of its halves. It’s also okay to have a slightly more potent effect with an inherent disadvantage beyond its Scar, such as a Magnitude ••••• Variation that grants a Magnitude •••• and three Magnitude • effects — not a legal entanglement but far from an optimal one for a Magnitude ••••• Scar.

                    Variations are limited to five distinct effects — no more than one for each Magnitude. However, nothing prevents a Deviant from having two different Variations that act as two halves of a whole. With the Storyteller’s permission, a player whose character has reached the limits of a twined Variation might be allowed to split it into two separate Variations of lower Magnitude and entangled to the same Scar — following the spirit (although not the letter) of the Fused and Shifting Scars rule (Deviant, p. 111).

                    Example of Variation Creation #1

                    Julia wants to create a character who can manipulate local gravity. She wants it to allow her character to fall safely, jump higher, climb more easily, and maybe eventually fly a little bit. But she also wants it to be able to make objects and people lighter or heavier, slowing them down or even immobilizing them. Her Storyteller points out that that is probably too much for a single Variation. Julia agrees and decides to start with a Variation that simply weakens gravity, twining together elements of Brachiation and Flight. She’ll make one that strengthens gravity later and probably from scratch. The following Variation is the result:

                    Gravity Redu​ction (• to •••••)

                    Overt

                    Reflexive, Tiered, Toggled

                    The Deviant can loosen the grip of gravity on herself.

                    At Magnitude •, choose one:

                    • Slow Fall: The Remade enjoys the benefits of Flight 1 (Deviant, p. 119).

                    • Sprint: The Broken enjoys the benefits of Enhanced Speed 1 (Deviant, p. 117).

                    At Magnitude ••, choose one:

                    • Balanced: The Deviant benefits from both Magnitude • effects.

                    • Drifting Leaf: This is only available if the Remade chose Slow Fall as her Magnitude • effect. The Broken enjoys the benefits of Flight 2.

                    • Wall-Run: This is only available if the Deviant chose Sprint as her Magnitude • effect. The Remade enjoys the benefits of Brachiation (Balanced) 2 (Deviant, p. 114).

                    Nothing too strange so far. The Remade could take both Variations at Magnitude • with a single Magnitude •• Scar entangling them both, or either of the Magnitude •• Variations with a Magnitude •• Scar entangled with them.

                    At Magnitude •••, choose one:

                    • Diverse: The Broken enjoys the benefits of all Magnitude •• effects.

                    • Ceiling Run: This is only available if the Deviant chose Wall-Run as her Magnitude •• effect. The Remade enjoys the benefits of Brachiation 3.

                    • Controlled Negation: This is only available if the Broken chose Drifting Leaf as her Magnitude •• effect. The Deviant enjoys the benefits of Flight 3.

                    At Magnitude ••••, the Remade enjoys the benefits of all Magnitude ••• effects.

                    Notice what we’re doing here. By building the Variation as a combination of Brachiation and Flight, requiring the purchase of every level of effect along the way, we wind up with a custom Variation that is effectively two Magnitude 3 Variations treated as one Magnitude 4 Variation. This is really as simple as it gets. Because a Magnitude 5 Scar can entangle up to three Magnitude 3 Variations, we could borrow a Magnitude 3 effect from a completely different Variation or improve upon Controlled Negation so that it emulates one of the versions of Flight 4. However, in this case we’re going a slightly different direction.

                    Deviations

                    • Targeting: The Broken can reduce the effect of gravity on a target of a Size no greater than the Variation’s Magnitude + Scar Power + 2. As a Directed effect, the Deviant grants one of the Variation’s effects to a target in range until the end of the scene. Remember that targeting a Directed effect always requires an action. Inanimate objects determine their effective weight by dividing their normal weight by the Variation’s Magnitude times Scar Power. A Deviant with Gravity Reduction (Wall Run; Sprint) 2 and Scar Power 5, for example could allow a 1,000-pound object of up to Size 9 to be pushed, lifted, carried, and thrown as though it weighed only 100 pounds.

                    This is the only effect that is not wholly borrowed from Brachiation and Flight, instead introducing an option reminiscent of Sacred Flesh but also very much its own thing — and one that is a potential game-changer if used cleverly. This is really all it takes to make a combined Variation feel less like two lower-Magnitude Variations entangled with the same Scar, and more like a new Variation.

                                    Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post
                                    While both offer their own set of challenges, you believe the storytellers guide will be the more difficult book to write for? And as a fun question, whats been your favorite part of writing the players guide?
                                    They're very different books.

                                    With Shallow Graves, I had the advantage of years of accumulated draft Variations, Scars, Merits, and Forms to work with. Some of those were just the usual constant tinkering I do on creative projects with no specific deadline, but a lot of them came as the result of "granting wishes" on the Discords. That gave a me a de facto platform for workshopping them. Even a long Variation or Form is sharable in that venue. By the time I decided to move forward with a STV Player's Guide, I already knew exactly what I was including and how many words I would likely need to flesh them out. It wasn't too difficult to stay on task or meet my self-imposed deadlines, and the ideas flowed pretty easily into the neat little jars I had prepared for them.

                                    Black Vans (the Storyteller's Guide) has always been a more nebulous and difficult to share project.

                                    First, the audience is smaller. At a guess, maybe 1 in 4 or 1 in 5 players is also a Storyteller, so the number of people who are going to be excited about material from a STG is much lower than those who would read and provide feedback on a Variation thrown together in 30 minutes and posted in the Deviant channel.

                                    Second, most STG material is much longer than is reasonably sharable on a Discord channel or even a web forum - at least not without drowning out other voices and conversations with whom I'm sharing space. The reason I ultimately decided to set up a Patreon page as part of the promotional push on Shallow Graves had less to do with establishing a revenue stream and more a matter of creating a venue where I can share long excerpts of works in progress. For now, it's all Shallow Graves all the time, but once the Player's Guide is fully live, I expect to shift the Patreon's focus to Black Vans to seek feedback that will help me refine my ideas.

                                    Third, Black Vans is more ambitious in its scope than any other CofD Storyteller's Guide that I'm aware of. I originally outlined it as being about as long as Shallow Graves (~210 pages excluding index), but I'm finding that a lot of the topics in it are taking more words to unpack than I anticipated. While this might seem like no big deal because it isn't a print book and doesn't require outside writers, I still have to pay for layout, editing, and art; every page of text costs around $30 to produce. That's likely to mean that either I must make significant cuts, or I need to find a way to raise more money so I can keep more of the words going into layout. How many sales Shallow Graves has (especially in its first three months) will have a significant influence on this, and patrons can also help make this a reality by continuing to support my ambitions even after the Player's Guide is available for sale.

                                    As far as what my favorite part of writing Shallow Graves was, you realize you're asking a parent to choose between their children, don't you? Or a book-lover to choose their favorite book? But I think I can honestly say that the best part of writing this book has been the outpouring of support I've had from the game's fans stretching all the way back to the Kickstarter. Without their outspoken enthusiasm for the game, their eagerness to welcome and help people who are new to Deviant, and their palpable excitement over the prospect of this project, Shallow Graves would not exist.

                                    Whenever I was feeling a little dry while writing a section and needed something to stir up my brain, fans were always willing to shout suggestions and prompts at me until my ideas were flowing smoothly again. Whenever I asked for examples of play so that I had a better idea of how Deviant is played in the wild, fans told their stories, posted links to APs, or invited me to observe (or join) their PBP Deviant games. When I needed beta readers, fans stepped up and gave me well over 100 pages of notes to help improve it.                                             Persistent Scar: Lumbering (• to •••••)

                                            Keywords: Physical; Subtle

                                            The Deviant is slow and heavy, making it easy for enemies to run circles around him.

                                            At Magnitude •, the Remade suffers a penalty to Initiative and Speed equal to the Magnitude of this Scar. In addition, the Broken’s player may accept a Beat whenever being slow and heavy creates a complication for him or his cohort. This can include choosing to fail rolls related to stealth, jumping, or running, as well as those related to climbing or balancing on objects that are not strong enough to bear his weight.

                                            At Magnitude ••, as Magnitude •, and choose one:

                                            • Heavy: The Deviant is significantly heavier and denser than a human being. He sinks like a stone in water, and ordinary ropes, ladders, scaffolding, and other supports will not bear his weight unless their Durability exceeds half the Scar’s Magnitude (rounded up). Others’ attempts to lift, carry, drag, or give a hand up to the Remade suffer a penalty equal to the Magnitude of this Scar unless using engineering principles to do so (systems of pulleys, block and tackle, counterweights, etc.); even so, relevant Build Equipment actions still suffer a reduced penalty equal to half the Scar’s Magnitude (rounded up).

                                            • Ponderous: Opponents may dodge the transformed’s attacks even if they have already taken an action during the current turn, and doing so does not take up their action for the turn. They also enjoy half their Defense (rounded up) against those of the Deviant’s attacks that would normally ignore Defense unless some other circumstance would deprive them of Defense (such as a successful ambush).

                                            • Slow: The Remade halves his Speed (rounded up) after applying the penalty levied by the Magnitude • effect and suffers a penalty to his Defense equal to half the Scar’s Magnitude (rounded up).

                                            At Magnitude •••, as Magnitude ••, but choose two of the options.

                                            At Magnitude ••••, as Magnitude •••, but all three effects apply.

                                            At Magnitude •••••, as Magnitude ••••, but the Broken is incapable of movement except by means of an apparatus or vehicle designed to lift, pull, or carry large, heavy objects — such as a heavy pickup, semi-trailer, or cargo plane. Most consumer vehicles like cars and light trucks will suffer serious damage if they haul the Deviant more than a short distance. Build Equipment extended actions meant to move the Remade suffer a penalty equal to the Scar’s Magnitude (rather than half Magnitude).

                                            Persistent Scar: Overconfident (• to •••••)

                                            Keywords: Mental, Social; Subtle

                                            The Deviant has a high opinion of his aptitude in social situations that may or may not have any basis in reality.

                                            At Magnitude •, the Remade tends to insert himself into social situations he doesn’t understand and where he may not be welcome, to interrupt or take over the role of social leader even if he is not as qualified as others in the room. Whenever this creates a complication for him or his cohort, he takes a Beat.

                                            At Magnitude ••, as Magnitude •, and choose one:

                                            • Foot-in-Mouth: The Broken tends not to stop when he’s ahead. Once per chapter, when the Deviant succeeds on a Social roll, the Storyteller may offer a Beat to force the Remade to reroll the Social roll with the same dice pool and take the second result.

                                            • Loudmouth: Once per chapter, when another character would speak first in a delicate social situation, the Storyteller may offer a Beat for the Broken to speak first, particularly if he is not the best mouthpiece in the current situation. He can only refuse by succeeding on a Scar Resistance roll. If using Cohort Static (p. XX), he must bid at least one point of Static in a decision point and may do so even if it puts him in Static debt.

                                            At Magnitude •••, as Magnitude ••, and choose one:

                                            • Balanced: The Deviant suffers both Magnitude •• effects. The Storyteller may only invoke one of the effects per chapter.

                                            • Keep Digging: The Remade must have chosen Foot-in-Mouth as his Magnitude •• effect. If the reroll imposed by Foot-in-Mouth is a failure, it is instead a dramatic failure for which the Broken does not take an additional Beat.

                                            • Blowhard: The Deviant must have chosen Loudmouth as his Magnitude •• effect. Once per chapter, when an ally succeeds on a Social roll when the Remade is involved in the scene, the Storyteller may offer a Beat for the Broken to interrupt, taking control of the interaction. The Deviant makes the Social roll instead. The Remade may only refuse this compulsion by succeeding on a Scar Resistance roll. If using Cohort Static, he must continue bidding in a decision point until the proposal he is supporting wins out or until he runs out of Static.

                                            At Magnitude ••••, the Broken suffers all Magnitude ••• effects. The Storyteller may only invoke one effect of this Scar per chapter.

                                            Deviations

                                            • Misread (+1 Magnitude): The Remade regularly misreads social situations, often believing he is in a position of greater influence than turns out to be the case. The Storyteller may roll the Broken’s Social rolls in secret, applying any bonuses and penalties without revealing what they are or announcing the result.

                                            Standard Deviation: Caveat

                                            Scar Type: Any

                                            +1 Magnitude: The entangled Variation carries a significant limitation in how it works, beyond the scope of its Scar or another Deviation. The armor provided by Carapace might afford no protection against silver-plated weapons, for example, or Telepathy might be impossible against a target wearing a hat or helmet. This restriction can often resemble those imposed by Power Failure, but this Deviation allows the Remade to add a further, automatic restriction to a Variation over and above its normal Scar. The restriction is likely to come into play approximately once per chapter, and each time it does so in a way that inconveniences her or her cohort, the Deviant takes a Beat.

                                            • Minor (−1 Magnitude): As the base effect, but the restriction is largely cosmetic or only comes into play rarely (approximately once per story). Rapid Healing might be ineffective against wounds delivered by an enchanted sword, for example, or vermin spirits might be invisible to a spirit-detecting Specialized Sense. This is exclusive with Major.

                                            • Major (+1 Magnitude): As the base effect, but the restriction prevents the Broken from benefiting from the Variation except under unusual circumstances (approximately once per story). A Carapace might only protect from silver-plated weapons, for example, or Bioluminescence might only function in the presence of the undead. This is exclusive with Minor.
