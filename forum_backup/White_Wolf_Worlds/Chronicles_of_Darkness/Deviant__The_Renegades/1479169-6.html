    The Deviant Magical Girl: The Bio-Mechanical Liberator
    Across the Chronicles of Darkness, the Remade Magical Girl directly embodies the dark-magical-girl subgenre of involuntary modification, laboratory weaponization, and corporate exploitation. Rather than receiving a wondrous celestial gift, she was chemically butchered, cybernetically grafted, or gene-spliced by a shadowy conspiracy to serve as a disposable living weapon. Her magical girl transformation is the ultimate act of bodily reclamation: using the Transformation Compact and the Spark of Virtue to master her bodily trauma, turning her agonizing mutations into a righteous shield for the vulnerable.



    1. The Transformation Sequence: The Bio-Mechanical Chassis

    The Surgical Chassis Filter: Deviant mechanics center on the brutal, disfiguring push-and-pull between Variations (powers) and Scars (crippling physiological and social drawbacks). Normally, activating a high-magnitude Variation inflicts agonizing bodily damage, flesh-bleeding, or violent physical warping. The Compact functions as an energetic Surgical Chassis: during her magical sequence, it absorbs the traumatic feedback of her Scars, venting kinetic friction into sparkling flares, hard-light seams, and polished porcelain armor plates that conceal her surgical horror behind storybook majesty.

    Scars as Magical Girl Tropes: Canonical Deviant Scars map directly onto classic magical girl dramatic constraints:
    Depletion / Cooldown: The strict "battery limit" or time-limit countdown before her magical form powers down.
    Involuntary Transformation: Transforming uncontrollably whenever intense stress, trauma, or emotional triggers spike.
    Perilous / Maintenance: Requiring a verbal incantation, dedicated focus, or an external device (her wand or compact) to lock down her unstable powers without suffering internal backlash.
    Bane: A vulnerability to specific corporate frequencies, cryogenic chemicals, or the sound of the lab sirens that accompanied her Divergence.



    2. Loyalty Touchstones & The Unbroken Will

    Virtue via Loyalty & Catharsis: The Divergence shatters the soul, eradicating native human Virtue and Vice and replacing them with **Loyalty** and **Conviction** Touchstones. A standard Deviant is consumed by Conviction—a bitter, self-destructive thirst for revenge against her creators. The Magical Girl inverts this: she anchors herself entirely to **Loyalty Touchstones** (her cohort sisters, school friends, and innocent civilians). When her Loyalty exceeds her Conviction, she enters **Catharsis**, becoming a protective **Guardian**.

    Unbroken Will in Play: Through the Starlight Compact's *Unbroken Will* dynamic, her earnest sincerity grants an automatic +2 bonus on all rolls to control or suppress **Instability** whenever she acts in direct defense of an innocent. Defending others literally keeps her broken body and soul from falling apart.

    Preventing the Feral Collapse: When an ordinary Remade runs out of Stability, she cascades into biological degradation or becomes a mindless, mutating **Feral**. The Heart-Weave provides collective emotional ballast: the shared warmth and unconditional acceptance of her magical girl team halts Erosion, allowing her to survive without losing her mind.



    3. Clade Aesthetics: From Lab Experiment to Magical Heroine

    A Remade’s Clade dictates the aesthetic and mechanical texture of her heroic persona:

    Cephalist (The Psychic Esper): The mind-altered test subject. Clad in floating prism-glass tiaras and geometric hard-light halos, she channels psychokinetic barriers, telepathic squad links, and sensory radars, neutralizing foes through pure mental grace rather than messy physical violence.

    Chimeric (The Beast/Bio-Hybrid): The spliced experiment. Rather than manifesting grotesque animal tumors, the Compact refines her beast-DNA into elegant magical girl motifs: gilded falcon wings, iridescent butterfly antennae, or prehensile floral vines that entangle foes and safely catch falling civilians.

    Coactive (The Radiant Conduit): The irradiated particle-battery. Bombarded with quantum currents or toxic fallout, she channels her unstable internal reactor into sparkling starlight ribbons and non-lethal solar beam-strikes, absorbing hazardous energy leaks to protect bystanders.

    Invasive (The Cyber-Valkyrie): The modified cyborg. Flesh fused with invasive hardware is overlaid with sleek porcelain plate, modular weapon-gauntlets, and hard-light shields, deploying starlight cannons or plasma wands directly from her prosthetic arms.

    Mutant (The Unbroken Rebel): Aberrant biology that violently rejected the intended modifications. Her costume shifts dynamically during battle, generating spontaneous metabolic immunities or crystalline bone-armor in response to enemy strikes.



    4. Narrative Engine: Liberating the "Devoted" & Smashing Labs

    The Tragedy of the Devoted: Her most emotionally fraught recurring nemeses are the **Devoted**—brainwashed, captive magical girls kept on chemical or psychological leashes by the Conspiracy. While other anti-heroes would simply execute them, the Magical Girl’s mission is pedagogical and redemptive: she uses her Spark of Virtue to break their behavioral conditioning, rescue them from the operating table, and welcome them into her Circle.

    Infiltrating the Web of Pain: Deviant campaigns revolve around dismantling the **Web of Pain**. Rather than brooding in hideouts, the Magical Girl squad conducts daring nighttime raids against corporate research facilities, clandestine black-sites, and corrupt pharmaceutical front-companies, liberating caged test subjects and shattering the Linchpins of the Conspiracy.



    5. Mascot Dynamics: The Lab-Rescue Chimera

    The Escaped Companion: While sinister corporations hunt her with tracking hounds, her Mascot is an inverted Empyrean entity that has bonded with an escaped laboratory animal—a winged lab mouse, an albino cyber-ferret, or a glowing kitten saved from the vivisection table.

    Stabilization & Electronic Jamming: The Mascot feeds on genuine acts of healing, mutual liberation, and preserved human loyalty. In combat, it acts as an encrypted communications relay and medical scout, using Numina like Beacon of Sanctuary to scramble Conspiracy telemetry and Soothing Touch to purge biological toxicity and stabilize her Scars.

    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

        The Starlight Compact: The Luminaria
        Across the Chronicles of Darkness and the framework of the Contagion Chronicle, the Starlight Compact stands as a revolutionary Sworn faction dedicated to the proposition that cynicism, emotional detachment, and isolation are the primary vectors through which cosmic decay and supernatural rot fester. Where competing factions treat the Contagion as broken machinery to be patched in secret, a moral sin to be scourged, or a military crisis to be suppressed with cold violence, the Luminaria take the opposite stance: earnest idealism and unshielded vulnerability are the ultimate metaphysical anchors capable of healing reality itself.



        Foundational Initiation & System Integration

        To operate as a unified circle, every member of the Compact integrates two essential capabilities into their existence.

        Through the Transformation Merit, operatives manifest a stylized, empowered battle regalia. Beyond granting mystical and physical enhancements, this sequence projects an identity shield that protects civilian Touchstones, schools, and families from supernatural retaliation, allowing operatives to maintain genuine, loving connections in their ordinary lives without exposing innocents to the crosshairs of predatory monsters.

        Through the Mascot Merit, the circle bonds with an enlightened, sapient ephemeral guide. The Mascot acts as a living emotional antenna, knitting the squad’s friendship into a permanent sympathetic network known as the Heart-Weave. Most crucially, the Mascot actively neutralizes ambient horror and supernatural revulsion—calming the predatory friction of the Beast, buffering the toxic rejection of Disquiet, and dulling the madness of Lunacy—so that vampires, werewolves, mages, and mortals can stand side by side in deep emotional intimacy without tearing each other apart.



        Vector: Resonance

        The unique Sworn discipline of the Compact is Vector: Resonance, a power suite that directly translates mutual trust, conviction, and vulnerability into tangible hard-light barriers, synchronized finishing maneuvers, and reality-altering sanctuaries.

        • Unvarnished Heart: By spending Willpower to speak or act with complete, unshielded emotional honesty, the operative outlines nearby illusions, disguises, and Contagious corruption in soft starlight, stripping away enemy stealth while bolstering allies with a +2 bonus to resist terror, panic, and mental Tilts. Within distinct supernatural lineages, this honesty fuels survival: it restores Glamour and Satiety, grants a +2 bonus on Risking Willpower and controlling Instability, restores Cover or Plasm, and buffers Harmony against sudden degeneration.

        •• Validation of Dreams: Whenever an ally admits a vulnerable truth, expresses an earnest ambition, or makes a personal sacrifice, the operative reflexively validates that aspiration. The ally receives a +2 bonus to all rolls made in pursuit of that ideal for the scene, and fulfilling the goal immediately awards an Experience Beat across the entire Mascot network.

        ••• Sincere Conduit: The operative radiates an expanding aura of pure starlight. Any hostile supernatural power within this field that relies on terror, despair, mind control, or emotional coercion immediately triggers a Clash of Wills against the operative’s Conviction plus Mascot rating. If the operative prevails, the hostile effect shatters completely, and allies within the field add her Conviction score to their Armor.

        •••• Harmonized Heartstrike: When at least two operatives sharing a Mascot synchronize their armaments through the hub, they channel their collective emotional bond into a devastating team finisher. This coordinated strike inflicts massive direct damage against corrupted entities while remaining entirely harmless to bystanders and civilian architecture.

        ••••• Dawn of the Unbroken Heart: The ultimate expression of hope over cosmic despair requires three or more operatives and a powerful Mascot to manifest a shimmering dome of starlight spanning hundreds of yards. Within this sanctuary, early stages of Contagion are permanently purged from mortals and allies, infected Infrastructure collapses into inert dust, and hostiles suffer continuous Aggravated damage each turn. Any member who makes a vulnerable emotional sacrifice within the dome gains the Rote Quality on all actions for the chapter, while the entire circle completely refills their Willpower pools.



        Macro-Societal Role in the Chronicles

        The Starlight Compact operates as an indispensable diplomatic bridge across the fractured, paranoid politics of the World of Darkness. In a setting defined by ancient hierarchies, territorial isolation, and cold survivalism, the Luminaria serve as a living proof of concept: that across all monster lineages, choosing to care without armor can actively drive back the horrors of the night and redeem a broken world.

        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

            On the subject of Magical Girl Heroes, the thing to remember is that Beast is problematic. It has a subtext of apologetics for predators; and part of that subtext is that in the game, anyone who might call the Beasts out get lumped in with "narcissistic low-Integrity Heroes". The thing is, the Storytelling section outright admits that they're putting their thumb on the scale: "The reasonable, caring, 'good' Heroes don't make much of an appearance in Beast, not because they don't exist, but because the narrative role for the Hero in Beast is that of a violent, narcissistic antagonist." Even in Beast as written, there are in theory Heroes who aren't narcissistic creeps; they just get ignored because they don't fit Beast's intended narrative.

            The real problem with a Magical Girl Hero isn't the low Integrity or the narcissistic portrayal, as magical girls played straight would definitely match exactly the "exceptions" that absolutely exist. The problem is that the Hero powers are too limited, in that they are all things that only involve opposing Beasts. You would have to house rule Heroes to have more broadly useful abilities, or focus the Hero Magical Girl solely on battling Beasts.

            (This narrow scope would be understandable if not for the fact that Beast is built with crossovers in mind; so it's not like the narrow scope is because the game doesn't consider how other supernaturals factor in. Rather, it explicitly excludes non-Beasts from being subject to a Hero's powers.)

                Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                On the subject of Magical Girl Heroes, the thing to remember is that Beast is problematic. It has a subtext of apologetics for predators; and part of that subtext is that in the game, anyone who might call the Beasts out get lumped in with "narcissistic low-Integrity Heroes".
                Beast does not defend what Beasts do to feed their Hunger and its moral issue with Heroes has always been that the Heroes who serve as antagonists primarily don't care if the Begotten they hunt have done anything to deserve death, with a side order of "it is extremely difficult to feel okay about someone whose primary threat is that they can put a folkloric-nonsense curse on you based on what they think you are that may directly affect your ability to behave normally, which does not go away until you are dead, depressively isolated from your magical nature in a way that requires being near death to recover from, or hungry to the point of dramatic escalation whose most expedient means of resolution is to kill the person who cursed you."

                Just as the Horror doesn't care if the Beast's victims learn anything, Heroes in the modern era do not have powers that given them extra insight into the larger picture that would make the unconscious biases that steer them have any consistent connection to reality — Heroic Tracking and the subsequent activity only cares, at root, about whether a Beast's activities (or inactivity) have disturbed the Primordial Dream, and the issues the Begotten encounter with Heroes stem from the fact that the Heroes who are trying to kill them do not recognize this and will often come up with whatever arbitrary justification they can think of to explain why the Beast has to die. They are a warped magical-social immune response attached to the kind of person whose self-image is bad enough to think their ability to perceive the ripples of supernatural trauma makes them the main character of a monster-killing narrative.

                The gameline does not "lump in anyone who might call the Beasts out with Heroes" because it does not concern itself with anyone "calling the Beasts out." The splat's primary social lubricant is only magical in the sense that it comes from shared magical qualities and goes away with the same things that sour a regular good impression and half the Satiety Conditions and Family Ties revolve their roleplaying considerations around the Beast becoming isolated or cut off from people close to them.

                Beast has no more subtext of apologetics for predators than Vampire does and I'd love if we could stop pretending otherwise ten years into the line's existence just because the Kickstarter draft fumbled major parts of its presentation.

                (This narrow scope would be understandable if not for the fact that Beast is built with crossovers in mind; so it's not like the narrow scope is because the game doesn't consider how other supernaturals factor in. Rather, it explicitly excludes non-Beasts from being subject to a Hero's powers.)
                It also explicitly presents a Heroic Gift that extends much of the benefits of their abilities as Beast-specific antagonists to characters with Family Ties to a Beast, i.e. supernatural characters for which a Hero is a reasonable antagonist to be involved with without extenuating circumstances that have little to do with Heroes in general. Considering Dark Eras 2's material on Oracles/Heroes from before the signal-to-noise ratio got quite so bad gives their powerset a number of adjustments including having a Virtue and Vice instead of a Life and Legend and effectively giving a modified version of an existing Gift to let them place Anathema in place of their ability to recruit Followers.

                The problem with magical girl Heroes is the same problem as with magical girl Huntsmen or magical girl Strix, i.e. they are designed as antagonists whose powerset exists to specifically make trouble for one type of monster first and their ability to be a threat outside of that context is dramatically more limited — setting aside conditional bonuses like Vanquisher's Strength, Champion's Endurance, and Warrior's Speed that Kinslayer can extend to conflict with all tagged monsters, Gifts mostly work by shutting down or bypassing the advantages a Beast has from having a Lair or expanding their ability to place Anathema, and the nature of how Heroes get stronger is such that for practical purposes higher-Lair Begotten treat experienced Heroes as insidious threats to their autonomy and an extra escalatory pressure valve for if they fail at operational security for feeding their Hunger rather than as direct threats, just like the Strix and the Huntsmen engender paranoia in their respective targets and the societies they inhabit by being able to shapeshift, steal bodies, make it look like someone else is possessed, skirt the boundaries of magical restrictions on their master's activity, etc.


                Beast notwithstanding, treating "magical girl" as a template to be applied to any playable monster type also sort of runs into the problem of not all gamelines' protagonist kits assuming the same sociopolitical placement as each other — a mummy and a vampire (and to a lesser extent a werewolf) both occupy a position where they are more likely to be the power source for characters who would be more appropriate to the broad idiom of mahou shoujo like ghouls, Wolf-Blooded, and Inheritors, and the isolation and potential direct fugitive status of Prometheans and the Unchained brings a degree of personal antagonism to the things that are after them that seldom directly gels with the genre's focuses. Given that this thread is still nominally about Deviant, it may be prudent to approach this as though it were one or more universal Lesser Forms.
