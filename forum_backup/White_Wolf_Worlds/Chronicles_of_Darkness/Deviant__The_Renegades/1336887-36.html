                                        On the conspiracy rules - have I missed something, or are a conspiracy's Attributes mostly worthless? Standing is important, because it sets how often a conspiracy can act. Resistance contributes to the health track. But the one thing Power and Finesse get used for is deciding whether the conspiracy pays a price when it messes with PCs. It seems to me a conspiracy that bought Resistance heavily would be much more capable than one which invested in Power and Finesse. It would be vulnerable more often, but it would have a larger safety margin, too. The marginal gain from pushing Power or Finesse past, oh, 5 or so looks a lot less than the gain from 1 Resistance dot at any point.

                                        I can see the logic behind it - the ST shouldn't be rolling dice unless the players are involved somehow, so there's no point to rolling for downtime actions; and PCs should oppose characters, not organizations, in action scenes, which leaves very little scope for a conspiracy's traits in those scenes. But then it's hard to see why conspiracies need Attributes at all...

                                            A conspiracy that buys Resistance heavily is going to be that much less effective at using Nodes that actually do anything outside the conspiracy itself, and trying to use Structural Nodes to do the work of Temporal or Exploitative Nodes is inherently less effective in a way that risks the conspiracy as a whole taking more damage (which in turn forces constant reorganization). Resistance contributes to Association, but it's the least useful for actually extending the conspiracy's reach.

                                            On the larger scale, a conspiracy that turns itself into a big brick of Resistance and Structural Nodes is pretty much begging to be subverted by other conspiracies, because it's very good at continuing to exist but doesn't accomplish a whole lot else on its own (and this goes double if it is infiltrated by other conspiracies).

                                            The goal of a conspiracy is not simply to continue to exist, and Resistance only helps it on that front rather than in acting to preserve its Principles or halt the progress of saboteurs and infiltrators.

                                                    Originally posted by Deinos View Post
                                                    As two powers can flick off if people suffer a breaking point, and deviants can be quite horrific to behold, it makes me wonder: how easily do deviants cause breaking points? Is effectively any obvious deviation of scar a breaking point in and of itself? Do conspirators take fewer breaking points from deviants?
                                                    Core CofD has breaking points occur whenever someone encounters something (typically violence or supernatural phenomena) that outstrips her ability to handle it. Baselines who have little or no experience with the supernatural could be set off by simply seeing a Deviant use an Overt Variation (or merely meeting one with an Overt Scar). Repeated exposure to the same kinds of situations will eventually dull that shock response, so a member of a conspiracy would likely grow inured to the horrors involved in creating Remade and jaded about the powers they've seen the Broken wield. Granted, ones who haven't seen a Deviant grow violent might well still suffer a breaking point if the Remade turns that violence on them.

                                                            Originally posted by Michael Brazier View Post
                                                            You would think that the dots in an Attribute correlate with the number of Nodes ties to that Attribute. But there's no such link in the text. How many Nodes are Structural has nothing to do with how many dots are in Resistance.
                                                            I made no assertion to the contrary. Using Structural Nodes, though, is what Resistance is for, and the value of putting dots into the Attribute for using logistical Nodes is comparatively low if you don't actually have at least a few high-utility Structural Nodes to lean on.

                                                            More importantly, Attributes are irrelevant to downtime actions, which are how a conspiracy extends its reach.
                                                            A conspiracy extends its reach by taking conspiracy actions and completing Projects. Conspiracy actions specifically must include things like recruiting, blackmailing, killing, or otherwise meddling with the extended social circle of a Remade and her cohort; it's a viable narrative space to draw Linchpins from, for example.

                                                            They only matter for actions against Renegades - and there, it's one roll per scene, and the conspiracy needs only one success on that roll.
                                                            Because a single success means there's no automatic way the action gets messy — and a one in six incidence of conspiracy actions getting messy is plenty of consequences when the conspiracy is big enough to be putting multiple conspiracy actions into play per chapter.

                                                            It's quite true that most actions against Renegades will use Power or Finesse; but looking at dice pool probabilities, there isn't much point to pushing either Attribute past 5, where success comes 5 out of 6 times.
                                                            …Please remember how dice pool penalties work and how the Attribute distribution breaks down, because a one- to three-dice penalty is a substantial dent at that level and a conspiracy needs Standing at least halfway up the Threat Level chart to have enough Attribute dots for a secondary 5 to be feasible without sitting on multiple Complexity 10 Projects. A dice roll is a dice roll.

                                                            Whereas Resistance directly increases a conspiracy's ability to take damage, and therefore indirectly increases its ability to take actions, which gives it more chances to increase its power, in a way that scales with the Attribute.
                                                            Again: Pretty much every time a conspiracy takes lethal damage to its Association, it has to reorganize or suffer further setbacks and can see its Principles change. Resistance and Association have no impact on the number of conspiracy actions (and therefore potential downtime Effort) granted by Standing.

                                                            "The organization exists" is not the organization's goal. A conspiracy is irrevocably gone once its Association fills with aggravated damage, and any inheritor organization is a different conspiracy; changes to policy are nontrivial alterations, so "higher Resistance means a conspiracy can, if it becomes desperate enough to throw itself into complete disarray, take more actions" is a negligible benefit if it means half its key personnel have changed departments, its public and private faces have blurred in a weird direction, and a purge has become necessary to get the group back on track while a cohort of deviants has figured out the next mutagen refinery to bomb.

                                                            If Attributes were needed in downtime actions you would be correct; high Power and Finesse would be crucial to a conspiracy's accomplishing anything. But the rules don't say that.
                                                            They also do not say "the cohort and their extended social circle are completely irrelevant to the functioning of a conspiracy that is specifically being engaged with in the mechanics because it is after them." Conspiracy actions do not have to be hostile.
