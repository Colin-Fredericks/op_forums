                            Souls are different in CofD from how they are depicted in a lot of settings. They are not necessarily associated with the psyche or self, they are interchangeable; if you lose yours, you lose your ability to self-actualize, but if you obtain another, even if it's not the one you were born with, you will still be "you".

                            What, then, do you suppose would happen if a normal human swapped souls with a Remade? We know that the Remade have damaged souls; would gaining a whole soul "fix" the Remade, or at least stop its degeneration? Would the human with the damaged soul begin to exhibit the physical peculiarities of the Remade it came from? Or would they perhaps lose their ability to define themselves, and end up with Loyalty and Conviction scores? Perhaps, in the process of cracking a Remade's soul, it becomes inextricably linked to its body and mind, and they cannot be disassociated. I seem to remember reading that Remade are very difficult to mind control as a result of their changes.

                            I think this is pretty interesting to think about. Do any of you have opinions on this?

                                                        Originally posted by Cannox View Post
                                                        So for completely new people they meet (that they don't ignore) they basically go "Either help me or get the hell out of the way"? If a person doesn't help them, do Deviants often/always consider them a hostile?
                                                        It isn't so much "help me, or get out of my way" as "if you're nice to me, I will devote my life to protecting you and furthering your interests, but if you get in my way, I will make you curse the day you were born." Anyone who does neither is pretty close to beneath the Deviant's notice. That's not a choice for the Remade. That's the consequence of living with a shattered soul.

                                                        I regard the Phantom of the Opera as a pretty good example of how some Deviants interact with a Loyalty Touchstone. Normally pretty reclusive, but on rare occasions, he reaches out to another human being. If you reciprocate, he'll do anything for you, rearrange his whole life and his corner of the world in an effort to make you happy and keep you safe. But that love is incredibly needy, jealous, always terrified that it will be betrayed, that he will lose you. If ever you betray him, his wrath will be terrible. He moves you from the "friends" column to the "enemies" column in his world, and then he redirects all the energy he spent trying to please you into effecting your utter ruin.

                                                        Not all Deviants are quite so, um, clingy and stalker-ish. But this is definitely one of my favorite examples of the difference between "healthy loyalty to one's friends and family" and "Loyalty."

                                                        For Conviction, one of my favorites is the protagonist in Memento. Having gotten his revenge against his wife's murderer but being unable to savor that victory (because he can't remember it), he intentionally tricks himself into exacting his revenge a second time against someone who was directly involved in helping him get revenge the first time. You get the sense that this cycle will never, ever end. His thirst for revenge cannot be quenched, and he will always find someone else to blame and to brutally murder.

                                                        What I'm saying is that having your physical, mental, and spiritual health utterly dependent on feeding your time and emotional energy into either protecting people or destroying them, that's probably not a recipe for maintaining healthy, long-term relationships.
