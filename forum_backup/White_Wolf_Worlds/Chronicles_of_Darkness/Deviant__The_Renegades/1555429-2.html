    Correct. Not visible in the free previews, but each allegiance get two free dots of one of the three Merits, so to get 8 dots of any given one you need to spend 6 XP/Merit dots and belong to the right team. Here's what those look like:

    Allegiance

    Each superhuman holds one of six Allegiances: Advocate, Champion, Enforcer, Rebel, Reformer or Rogue. Allegiance, along with Origin, determines the Remade's starting mix of Loyalty and Conviction Touchstones. Half are considered Devoted Allegiances for the purposes of determining how furthering Touchstones replenishes Willpower and heals Instability, while the other half are Renegade Allegiances.

    Advocate (Devoted): These superhumans subscribe to a system of beliefs that drives them to build, create, or preserve something of value. Most are religious, or at least abide by a principled code of ethics, and they tend towards altruism (and philanthropy, if they have the means). They begin play with 3 Loyalty Touchstones, no Conviction Touchstones, and two dots of the Sacred Code Merit (p. XX), which counts as a fourth Loyalty Touchstone.

    Rhetoric: For the purposes of Social Maneuvering, the Advocate counts the impression level of hostile characters as poor. She instead enjoys a +2 bonus on Social actions other than Intimidation that target hostile characters.

    Champion (Devoted): These Remade embody Loyalty and service to their communities. They are the most likely of the Allegiances to behave as traditional superheroes and take any injury suffered by those under their protection personally. They begin play with 2 Loyalty Touchstones, 1 Conviction Touchstone, and two dots of the Beloved Community Merit (p. XX), which counts as a third Loyalty Touchstone.

    Figurehead: Whenever the Champion takes an action that would improve Sentiment, she gains the Inspired Condition to take an action that would improve Sentiment.

    Enforcer (Devoted): These Diverged serve an organization that sanctions them to perform violence in its name. They might be law enforcement, military, or the Devoted servants of conspiracies. They begin play with 2 Loyalty Touchstones, 1 Conviction Touchstone, and two dots of the Victim Profile Merit (p. XX), which counts as a second Conviction Touchstone.

    Just Desserts: Whenever the Enforcer destroys a target meeting his Victim Profile, his actions during the scene never worsen Sentiment and he gains the Inspired Condition for an action against another that matches his Victim Profile.

    Rebel (Renegade): These superhumans regard themselves as protectors of a group of their choosing and do their best to eliminate anyone who threatens their chosen family. The best of them are vigilantes and folk heroes beloved by those they protect. The worst are terrorists. They begin play with 1 Loyalty Touchstone, 2 Conviction Touchstones, and two dots of the Beloved Community Merit (p. XX), which counts as a second Loyalty Touchstone.

    Friends Everywhere: Once per chapter, the Rebel's player may announce that a member of the Remade's Beloved Community is present or promptly arrives to render assistance. This is usually a Baseline (or a small group of them) but is always someone in a position to help the Broken in a small but notable way.

    Reformer (Renegade): These Diverged adhere to a system of beliefs that drives them to tear down existing systems and those who defend them. Although not all of them resort to force, they are unquestionably more interested in removing enemies than in winning over friends. They begin play with no Loyalty Touchstones, 3 Conviction Touchstones, and two dots of the Sacred Code Merit (p. XX), which counts as a Loyalty Touchstone.

    Dossier: Once per chapter, the Reformer may gain the Informed Condition regarding one of her Conviction Touchstones.

    Rogue (Renegade): These superhumans use their powers to carry out their fantasies, most of which involve hurting people. While some have legitimate grievances against their targets — such as to avenge themselves against those who murdered people close to them — many are bullies, if not serial killers. They begin play with no Loyalty Touchstones, 3 Conviction Touchstones, and two dots of the Victim Profile Merit (p. XX), which counts as a fourth Conviction Loyalty Touchstone.

    Bogeyman: Whenever the Rogue takes an action that would worsen Sentiment, she gains the Inspired Condition to take an action that would worsen Sentiment.

    Origin

    A superhuman's Origin grants one Conviction or Loyalty Touchstone as described in Deviant.

    Changing Allegiance

    Remade may change Allegiance once per story.

    • Changing from one Devoted Allegiance to another (or from one Renegade Allegiance to another) has no special requirements.
    • Changing from a Devoted Allegiance to a Renegade Allegiance requires that the character has at least as many Conviction Touchstones (including the Victim Profile Merit granted by her Allegiance) as Loyalty Touchstones.
    • Changing from a Renegade Allegiance to a Devoted Allegiance requires that the character has more Loyalty Touchstones (including the Beloved Community or Sacred Code Merit granted by her Allegiance) than Conviction Touchstones.

    The benefits of the old Allegiance (including its bonus Merit) are immediately lost and the new one's gained.

    Onyx Path Freelancer and former Deviant line developer.

        A final section at the end of Chapter Five is meant to pull together everything in Black Vans (and all the other books of the Deviant: The Renegades line) and underline what this book is meant for. It's short, so I'm sharing it all for free – the first half here and the second half in a separate post on the Patreon.

        I hope you've enjoyed the previews. Black Vans will be on sale at on the Storyteller's Vault on January 1st. I hope you'll pick up a copy once it is, but more than anything, I hope you will find it useful in running your Deviant: The Renegades chronicles!

        One Piece at a Time

        A Deviant: The Renegades chronicle isn't built on an assembly line. Every troupe has a different idea for what kind of chronicle they want to play — if not which one they want to play this time. The game's core book offered a huge variety of potential characters and stories, and four supplements of new character options, alternate setting assumptions, and system hacks have only multiplied the number of possibilities. You can't do everything in Deviant, but you can do a lot if you put your mind to it.

        DIY Ethos

        Let's talk about what goes into designing an alternate Deviant setting.

        Some Assembly Required

        Before you start tinkering with game systems, it's important to know what kind of stories you want to tell in your chronicle.

        Place

        Consider the where of your chronicle.

        Genre: The genre or sub-genre is a good starting point. Modern horror is the default, but we've presented six other options, any of which could be smudged, mixed, or modified — and a motivated Storyteller could even build a new one from scratch.

        Theme and Mood: A well-built chronicle explores central themes and conveys a certain mood. This doesn't mean every chapter rigidly adheres to these guide stars, but events should return to them again and again. Traditionally, Chronicles of Darkness games have called out two (theme and mood), while the genre hacks in this chapter each outline four, but there isn't a magical number of these as long as the list is short.

        Backdrop: The physical setting flows from its genre, themes, and mood. Pick qualities and set pieces that reinforce the overall flavor you're going for. Some of these may suggest mechanics, as well, but the focus here is on creating an interesting place for events to take place in — whether it's a single neighborhood at a defined point in time or an entire network of interdimensional outposts scattered across multiple parallel timelines.

        People

        Think about the who of your chronicle. This includes Storyteller characters but should focus on the players' characters.

        Commonalities: What's the common ground that draws (and keeps) the members of the cohort together? This can be circumstantial, such as being made by the same conspiracy or marooned on the same planet, but it could be a shared interest, such as studying ghosts or being a team of athletes.

        Differences: How do the members of the cohort distinguish themselves from their fellows? This is often as simple as having different Variations or choosing different Skills, but it might not be in a chronicle where everyone was chosen for Divergence because they shared certain skills and were given largely identical Variations. Players like their characters to have some niche, both in terms of capabilities and personality.

        Main Event: What is the central gameplay loop of the chronicle (i.e., how the characters are expected to spend most of their time)? This could easily be two to four related activities; in core Deviant, it's "elude/fight the conspiracy and further Conviction/Loyalty Touchstones".

        Sideshows: How much flexibility do the characters have to pursue activities that are outside of the main event, and what kinds of activities are most common? A chronicle in which a conspiracy pursues the cohort constantly is different from one where a conspiracy must share the spotlight with a parade of monsters of the week.

        Motivations

        Focus on the whys of the chronicle — specifically, why the cohort does the things they're meant to do. These motivations have three axes:

        Narrative/Mechanical: Narrative motivations invoke the chronicle's story and play on the players' desire for an engaging plot and interesting characters. Mechanical motivations offer rewards for some behaviors and threaten to punish others. Which axis works best at your table depends heavily on who sits at it. While some players embrace genre conventions and swallow plot hooks without question, most benefit from occasional mechanical nudges to keep them on-task. And for some, mechanical reinforcement is a primary driver of in-character behavior.

        External/Internal: Some motivations exert pressure on the characters from within, while others do so from without. The conspiracy's actions, Projects, and most of its key agents are external motivations. Aspirations and Scars are usually internal motivations. Touchstones and Instability have qualities of both internal and external motivations. Include a mix of motivations. External motivations are unifying, usually drawing in the whole cohort to address a threat or seize an opportunity, but they can feel a bit impersonal. Internal motivations, meanwhile, are extremely relevant to the players whose characters pursue them but are less likely to motivate the whole cohort.

        Carrots/Sticks: Most methods of motivation boil down to either rewarding or punishing player behavior. In Deviant, these carrots and sticks are written into the mechanics. Characters receive carrots for playing up their Scars and furthering their Touchstones. They're met with sticks when they Falter, choose not to engage with their Touchstones, or ignore the conspiracy (which unleashes nastier Projects). Both approaches work, although sticks can generate player resentment if used too liberally.

        Taking Stock

        Once you know the where, who, and why of your chronicle, make some decisions about how to render them mechanically. This can sound intimidating, but Deviant offers many systems and options that can be adapted to represent other things. You see this throughout Black Vans — taking systems from the core, The Clades Companion, The Devoted Companion, and Shallow Graves and redefining or adding to them so that they represent a non-Deviant character or convey a theme or mood appropriate to a new genre. Here are some of the tools already at your disposal:

        Adaptations: Adaptations help define different "types" (Clades) of Deviant characters. Making all of them Universal removes much of that distinction, while mandating new arrays of Adaptations multiplies the number of Clades. Disallowing reset Adaptations greatly reduces the use of limited-use abilities and should be done with caution.

        Forms: Core Forms represent a shift in capabilities (rather than a net increase or decrease in power), while Advanced Forms allow the investment of Merit dots (and potentially Experiences) to make them a hybrid of Variations, Scars, and Merits all in one customizable package. Despite their flavor, they can be reskinned to reflect other situations that match their mechanics. Symbiote, for example, could represent a high fantasy priest's powers being granted to him by a goddess who may weaken or deny them outright if his use of them isn't in line with her goals. Situational Forms like Allayed and Visitor often reflect adverse conditions and methods of compensating for them. Greater Forms and Lesser Forms (and Scar Forms) are essentially Forms nested within other Forms, and although we use them as the basis for crossover, the concept could be used to build any number of remarkable new creatures or specialized concepts.

        Merits: Most Core Merits give the character options not available to others, while the rest enhance existing capabilities in some notable way. Deviant-Only Merits interact with Remade systems, often increasing the utility of Variations in ways beyond the scope of Techniques. Most narrowly defined, nifty capabilities can be rendered as Merits. Variation-Specific Merits expand the scope of a Variation beyond the normal 5-dot range and/or create new capabilities that reflect the synergy between two Variations.

        Variations/Scars/Standard Deviations: In the core, these represent the powers (and their costs) of Broken. Although often designed as packages of related abilities, Variations are eminently customizable using the inspired Variation process in Shallow Graves. Natural Variations and Scars (and Forms that increase/decrease Size) shift the conversation from "what you can do" to "what you naturally are", allowing the introduction of extraterrestrial species and extradimensional visitors alike. Storytellers shouldn't hesitate to forbid some of these if they're wrong for the intended chronicle, or to mandate those required to make a chronicle work.

        Instability: Instability is at once a premium fuel trait and a looming existential threat. At base, it provides a mechanical carrot/stick. It reinforces a genre's tropes, which is why this chapter so often leans on it to do exactly that.

        Conspiracies: Although cohorts face individual enemies, Deviant has extensive systems for collective antagonists — especially with the expanded conspiracy rules presented in The Devoted Companion. As we've already illustrated, they need not represent literal conspiracies. They can reflect a hostile environment, collective frenemies, or even the pure narrative imperative of a Storyteller to introduce new complications to a story (especially if the cohort isn't being proactive about confronting their source).

        Rewards: While healing Instability is one way to reward desired behavior, Willpower and Beats are the main reward mechanisms. Willpower is a quick, temporary hit. Beats, on the other hand, have minimal impact right now but represent the long-term growth of a character. Conspiracy Beats in The Devoted Companion and Static in Shallow Graves offer ways to reward behavior without relying on Willpower, Instability healing, or Beats, but including too many reward mechanics can make character advancement unnecessarily complicated.

        Deviant Equipment: Although implied in the core and rendered for antagonists in The Devoted Companion, The Clades Companion presents details about equipment that interacts with Variations and Scars directly (Repressors/Boosters/Neutralizers) or emulates them (Prototypes). A character heavily invested in Prototypes might be a gadgeteer, especially if they exchange most of their starting Variation Magnitude for extra Merit dots (p. XX) or have the Inventor Merit (p. XX).

        Ephemeral Entities: These beings are naturally out of phase with the physical world. Although The Clades Companion presents them as antagonists, they could be adapted as player characters, albeit not without some caveats. First, the active conspiracy (or other collective foe) must have some means of pursuing and threatening them. Second, if they're capable of traveling to an otherworld, the rest of the cohort must also have some way of doing so.

        Techniques: As presented in Shallow Graves, Techniques resemble Variation-Specific Merits. They differ in two ways. First, they can be used (in a limited way) without needing to purchase them. Second, they all have the same cost. Although specific to Variations, the premise could be expanded to include Skills, allowing those with significant mundane training (say, 3 or more dots) to pull off stunts that leave hobbyists gawking but are still roughly equivalent to a 1-dot Merit when Mastered.

        Splats and Genres: The various pieces of Chapters Four and Five of this book are meant to work together to convey a specific setting or include the characters and themes of other games, but they can also be mixed and tweaked to create something new.

            Black Vans is now live on the Storytellers Vault.

            For any errata feedback, please fill out the errata form here until 1/31/2026.



            Broken on the run from the conspiracies quickly learn to distrust many kinds of vehicles — limousines and sedans with dark-tinted windows, police cruisers, helicopters, and a whole host of transports used by the world's militaries — but none more fully encapsulate the dread and suspicion of escaped Deviants than a handful of black vans gathered in one place. Too often these have served as a conveyance for masked, heavily armed capture crews, fanatical, wild-eyed cultists, or dangerously obsessed Devoted and their snarling Manticore companions.

            Black Vans is a Storyteller's guide to Deviant: The Renegades. This book contains:

            • Extensive advice for planning and executing a successful Deviant: The Renegades chronicle, including discussion of common narrative devices, a detailed look at the game's mechanics, and optional and alternate rules to help Storytellers finetune its systems.

            • Tools for quickly creating Baseline and Devoted antagonists, foils, and allies.

            • Systems and narrative suggestions for incorporating the other strange inhabitants of the Chronicles of Darkness into a Deviant: The Renegades chronicle, complete with guidance for crossover play.

            • Six new genres for Deviants to inhabit — Cyberpunk, Dark Eras, High Fantasy, Post-Apocalypse, Space Opera, and Superhero Emergence — complete with new and modified systems for bringing the chronicles set in them to life.
