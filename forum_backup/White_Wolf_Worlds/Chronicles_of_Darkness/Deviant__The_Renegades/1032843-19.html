    It looks like Deviant's Conspiracies are going to give us a lot more organizational goodies to play with. Geist's Krewe mechanics were noted to be the debut of the bare bones versions of these rules, and I'm rather intrigued by what "nodes" are. So, new things we know about Conspiracies:
    • Conspiracies have a Virtue and a Vice, which all members of the Conspiracy might be able to draw on (this would be in line with the Krewe mechanics)
    • Conspiracies have Principles instead of Doctrine, short sentences that are kind-of-sort-of Aspirations and outline what the Conspiracy finds important.
    • Conspiracies use the simplified Attributes Ephemeral Beings use. No change from the Krewe mechanics there.
    • Conspiracies have something called Nodes. They are separated into four categories: Hierarchal, Temporal, Exploitative, and Structural. Individual nodes appear to be connected to things like facilities, branches of government, and other resources at the disposal of the Conspiracy. Rather notably, each node is tied to a specific named character, who'll likely be fleshed out in some capacity.
    • Conspiracies have Icons. These appear to be the same sort of thing as Regalia in the Krewe mechanics, but they seem to have some more flexibility in their construction and use. One example Icon allows you to temporarily take on Variations, another simply provides the 8-again quality to a roll, while the third is possibly world-ending. Namely, allowing Santiago's Contagion strain to spread across all Chile and possibly beyond.
    • Conspiracies appear to be missing Merits. Unlike Krewes, Conspiracies don't appear to have Merits. Maybe nodes pull double duty on that end, who knows.
    • Conspiracies don't have a Power Stat. Unlike Krewes, Conspiracies don't appear to have a Power Stat that limits their growth and capabilities. Unless "Standing", is the Conspiracy power stat.
    In general? I'm really excited to see what the full Conspiracy rules are. Tinkering with mechanics for organizations in the CofD has been one of my more consistent projects lately, and having a whole bunch of new toys for the toolkit does put a smile on my face.

                It's fun to watch you folks jump on these little tidbits. You can see what I mean about Santiago, now, can't you?

                Acclimation is indeed the Deviant "power stat," rated from 0 to 5. One of the subtle ways that the Remade are less powerful than other splats is the resultant "Clash of Wills gap."

                Stability is the "health track" on which Deviants take Instability as damage (minor, medium, and major). Instability makes it harder to manage your Scars (especially, but not exclusively, Involuntary Scars) and can make your Scars worse or cause/allow you to develop new Scars ("allow" because in order to purchase a new Variation, you have to have a Scar available that is powerful enough to entangle with the new Variation). It really isn't a fuel stat so much as a gauge of your progress toward complete destabilization (with its accompanying messy death).

                Standing describes a conspiracy's power level on a scale from 1 to 10.

                Icons are the conspiracy's "superpowers," each attached to a specific Node - and yes, originally based on Regalia.

                The approach to krewes was a definite precursor to Deviant's conspiracy mechanics, and kind of also an incremental evolution of the concept of God-Machine Infrastructure - the sort of thing where I wish we had come up with this when I was explaining how Infrastructure and God-Machine projects work. Nodes indeed represent a collection of resources available to the conspiracy. Each has a Linchpin (see?), which is a character that is essential to that Node. If you take out (kill, convert, etc.) that character, you take the Node down with them. You can also destroy a Node in other ways, but it's generally more complicated, since it often involves large scale arson, mass murder, or a really effective reveal of the Node's malfeasance sufficient to get law enforcement or public opinion to shut it down. Taking out Nodes is the most obvious way of dealing damage to a conspiracy (which has a damage track nearly identical to that of Congregation).

                Conspiracy rules in the core are focused on them as antagonist organizations. I definitely want to expand on them in a supplement that features Devoted, who really should have some ability to influence and make use of the conspiracy's resources in interesting ways. However, I haven't spent a huge amount of time brainstorming what that will look like beyond knowing that I really, really, really want to expand those mechanics.

                                        Originally posted by Robert D View Post

                                        We've all gotten a great deal of joy from jumping into the Mysteries of the tidbits Thank you for confirming and correcting some of our speculations! If I might ask a few more questions:

                                        1) Does Acclimation by chance influence how large a Stability track a Remade has? (Guessing out of left field that the more acclimated one becomes to one's Divergence the more stable their physical and metaphysical state)

                                        2) I recall hints of the Magnitude (2-15) of a Deviant's (or rather the average magnitude of a cohort of deviant's) Variations is what determines the initial Standing of a conspiracy. Is there a link between Acclimation and the total Magnitude of a Deviant?

                                        3) I recall mention that Voluntary Variations can cause Instability. Do some Involuntary or Persistent Variations cause Instability as well?

                                        4) last one (for now lol) I promise. How does one heal Instability on the Stability track?
                                        1) Great guess, and correct. Not its sole effect, but an important one.

                                        2) Total Magnitude of Variations determines Threat Level, and thus the Standing of your starting conspiracy. Acclimation doesn't enter into the equation at all, and you can have dozens of dots of Variations without ever raising Acclimation. Variations come with Scars, however, which provide a different sort of limiting factor.

                                        3) Instability usually comes from either a dramatic failure on an attempt to use a Variation (or resist its Scar) or failing to act on the dictates of Conviction or Loyalty (Faltering, as it's called). There are also a handful of other circumstances that cause Instability, such as using certain very powerful Variations multiple times in a story or falling into a Variation-specific failure state (teleporting into a solid object, having your astral form killed, etc.).

                                        4) Healing Stability involves Touchstones (or being a Guardian, which sort of gives you a slow regeneration of Stability, while the condition lasts). The more severe the Instability, the more extreme the actions required to clear it. Clearing a minor Instability can involve any noteworthy progress toward defeating your Conviction Touchstones, but healing a major Instability usually involves successfully killing a Conviction Touchstone. The current version of the rules also allows you to heal some Instability by developing a new Scar, which is sort of the metaphysical equivalent of cutting off your own arm to keep from dying of gangrene.
