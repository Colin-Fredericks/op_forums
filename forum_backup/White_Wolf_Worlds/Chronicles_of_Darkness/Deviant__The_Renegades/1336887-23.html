    Taking a closer look at Force Field - it's going to be providing cover with a Durability of 2 most of the time, rarely 3 (which is the maximum it can provide, at Scar Power 5). On the surface, that's great! Bullets that do 1 (2) damage or less can't penetrate it, everything else subtracts 2 (3) from damage. But then, I found this tidbit in the Chronicles core:

    If the cover is transparent (bulletproof glass, for example), subtract half the cover’s Durability, rounding down. Both the object and the target take any remaining damage.
    Most forcefields are (presumably) going to be transparent, that means you're going to be subtracting 1 damage at most from attacks that get through, even if you have Scar Power 5. That seems a bit underwhelming, and I'm wondering if it's intentional.

            How I'm hoping this works, using a Scar Power of 4 for example:

            Turn 1: Joe spends an instant action to activate Telekinesis and uses the free target selection that comes with activation to activate a Force Field
            Turn 2: Joe's forcefield is still up, and he decides to telekinetically grapple an opponent using his instant action to direct the Variation.
            Turn 3: The forcefield is still up, and one target is still grappled. Joe uses his instant action to direct the variation to grapple another person.
            Turn 4: Forcefield up, two people grappled. A third person grappled means we are at our cap of 4 affected targets.
            Turn 5: Joe needs to reflexively direct the Variation to deflect some bullets from anti-armor weaponry directed at him that could pierce his forcefield.             The target would be the area you're targeting (unless you're using it to catch a person, in which case they get to contest). Your Cephalist Joe example is correct. To your question on the next page about transparent cover, that's something I caught late, so now it reads: "The force field provides cover as though it were a non-transparent object with Durability equal to half Scar Power (rounded up)."

            Regarding your observation about Attributes, that's intentional. Raising Attributes is a good use of Experiences for a Deviant in a way that it often isn't in other CofD games. In many ways, it's often a better option than buying new Variations, which can be a bit of a trap, since more (or more powerful) Variations mean more (and worse) Scars, and more Scars mean more (or worse) complications. Getting a ton of nasty Scars can be just as bad for your life expectancy as accumulating too much Instability. Raising Scar Attributes lets you "level up" a Variation in an often quite significant way, without any drawback - on the contrary, in fact, since you also have the benefits of an increased Attribute.
