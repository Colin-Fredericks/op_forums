    I think we do have to be careful with the virtues. The MG doesn't have to be defined by an other-directed Virtue, but a few rules must be enforced:

    1. The Virtue must be expressed in relationship with others, fostering good relations. Even if the character at first struggle with this idea and may even sacrifice relationships for performing righteousness at the player's discretion, it only counts as fulfilling the Virtue if it genuinely help others and is done for their sake, not the sake of principle.

    2. MG Virtues are about self-actualization. That is the hardest part, because the genre is subtly (and sometimes overtly) subversive. Purity in particular is not a good fit, or at least not the best way to say it.

    Otherwise I loved it.

        Self-directed and other-directed were perhaps poor choices, as that presents them as a false dichotomy. Maybe instead they should be thought of as Internal and Relational, with the understanding that there are Virtues that qualify as both.

        Some examples of magical girls and their Virtues and Vices:

        First, the Sailor Senshi:

        Sailor Moon (Usagi Tsukino)
        Virtue: Purity (her active incorruptibility and radiant power to cleanse spiritual rot)
        Vice: Sloth (her constant struggle with procrastination, laziness, and indulgence)

        Sailor Mercury (Ami Mizuno)
        Virtue: Wisdom (her analytical clarity, intellectual depth, and quiet discernment)
        Vice: Timidity (her self-doubt, social hesitation, and fear of confrontation)

        Sailor Mars (Rei Hino)
        Virtue: Conviction (her spiritual discipline, unyielding focus, and fierce passion)
        Vice: Pride (her quick temper, sharp tongue, and stubborn refusal to yield)

        Sailor Jupiter (Makoto Kino)
        Virtue: Protection (her physical fortitude, willingness to take hits, and nurturing warmth)
        Vice: Impulsiveness (her tendency to charge ahead recklessly and fall instantly for romantic ideals)

        Sailor Venus (Minako Aino)
        Virtue: Duty (her solitary burden as the original leader/decoy, carrying tragic resolve behind a smile)
        Vice: Vanity (her longing for stardom, frivolity, and vulnerability to superficial distractions)

        Next, the Holy Quintet:

        Madoka Kaname
        Virtue: Mercy (her boundless empathy and willingness to absorb the world's despair so no one suffers alone)
        Vice: Insecurity (her crippling sense of self-worthlessness and anxiety over having nothing meaningful to offer)

        Homura Akemi
        Virtue: Fidelity (her fierce, unwavering loyalty and devotion to her promise across endless timelines)
        Vice: Obsession (her hyper-fixation on a single outcome that drives her into cold, self-destructive isolation)

        Sayaka Miki
        Virtue: Justice (her earnest desire to defend the innocent and uphold an unyielding ideal of selfless heroism)
        Vice: Pride (her stubborn refusal to accept help or admit emotional vulnerability, accelerating her downfall)

        Kyoko Sakura
        Virtue: Protection (her fierce guardianship over those she lets into her heart, ultimately choosing to share Sayaka's fate)
        Vice: Cynicism (her hardened, mercenary outlook that dismisses idealism and altruism as fatal mistakes)

        Mami Tomoe
        Virtue: Duty (her veteran dedication to acting as a noble protector and guiding the next generation without complaint)
        Vice: Loneliness (her profound psychological isolation and terror of facing the dark alone behind her polished facade)

        And the primary magical girls of Maho Shoujo Lyrical Nanoha:

        Nanoha Takamachi
        Virtue: Conviction (her unyielding belief in reaching out to understand others, using absolute force of will to break through emotional and hostile barriers to establish genuine connection)
        Vice: Recklessness (her severe disregard for her own physical limits and bodily health, pushing herself past near-fatal injury to save someone else)

        Fate Testarossa
        Virtue: Devotion (her profound capacity for loyalty and unconditional love, standing unwaveringly by her family and loved ones regardless of the personal cost)
        Vice: Self-Doubt (her deep-seated insecurity surrounding her identity as a manufactured clone, leaving her vulnerable to guilt and fears of unworthiness)

        Hayate Yagami
        Virtue: Sanctuary (her extraordinary warmth and acceptance, providing an unconditional safe haven and sense of family to the outcast Wolkenritter)
        Vice: Fatalism (her quiet resignation to her own suffering and crippling illness, preferring to bear terminal burdens in secret rather than worry those who depend on her)​​​​​​​​​​​​​​

            Here's a set of six supplemental Merits:

            Here is the complete set of all six finalized supplemental Virtue Merits formatted in BBCode for easy copy-pasting:

            Supplemental Virtue Merits

            1. Vicarious Exposure (••)

            Prerequisites: Spark of Virtue (•••••), A Relational Virtue (such as Compassion, Mercy, Protection, Selflessness, Devotion, or Honor).
            Active Use Limit: Activating a standard capability below consumes the character's once-per-session active Virtue slot. Capabilities designated as a Tactical Exposure involve immediate self-sacrifice and do not consume the once-per-session active slot (and may be used even if the slot is already spent).
            Universal Constraint (Communication Requirement): None of the capabilities granted by this Merit can affect another character unless there is an active channel of communication between the character and the target (direct speech/vocal reassurance, eye contact, physical touch, or an active telepathic/empathic link). The target must be aware of the character's presence and gesture.

            Capabilities
            • Empathetic Aegis (Proactive Protection): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As a reflexive action when an ally within line of sight is targeted by a fear effect, horror, or mind-altering power, the character may spend 1 Willpower and call out to them, lock eyes, or reach for them. For the remainder of the scene, all allies with whom she maintains active communication add the character's Integrity Anchor bonus (+half Virtue rating, rounded up) to their resistance rolls and Breaking Point rolls against supernatural horror or fear.
            • Vicarious Exposure (Absorbing Trauma): [Tactical Exposure — Does Not Consume Active Slot]
              When a nearby ally or civilian is forced to make an Integrity, Clarity, or moral Breaking Point roll, the character may declare a Tactical Exposure as a reflexive action, calling out their name, grabbing their hand, or locking eyes ("Give it to me, look at me!"). The original target suffers no loss and makes no roll; the character takes the Breaking Point on her own sheet instead, rolling Resolve + Composure + Integrity Anchor Bonus.
            • De-escalating Touch (Soothing Rage): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As an instant action when confronting an enemy locked in active supernatural rage (such as Vampiric frenzy, Werewolf Death Rage, or Disquiet), the character steps into their line of sight, lowers her weapons, and directly speaks to or reaches for them. She spends 1 Willpower and rolls Presence + Empathy + (half Virtue rating, rounded up) vs. Composure + Supernatural Tolerance to soothe them for turns equal to her Virtue rating.
              • Unarmored Supercharge [Tactical Exposure — Does Not Consume Active Slot]: If she voluntarily accepts a Tactical Exposure (taking an immediate Breaking Point) during this appeal, the target cannot roll Supernatural Tolerance to resist.
            • Soothing Burden (Condition Removal): The character must make physical contact, lock eyes, or speak directly to the target ("Breathe, I'm right here").
              • Minor Relief [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]: Spend 1 Willpower as an instant action to clear a temporary mental Tilt (Shaken, Terrified, Spooked) from the target or allow them to immediately re-roll a failed resistance against an active mental Condition.
              • Trauma Transfer [Tactical Exposure — Does Not Consume Active Slot]: Accept a Tactical Exposure (taking an immediate Breaking Point) as an instant action to permanently cure and remove a severe mental Condition (Broken, Despair, Thrall) from the target, drawing the shock onto herself.

            2. Crucible of Conviction (••)

            Prerequisites: Spark of Virtue (•••••), An Internal Virtue (such as Fortitude, Pride, Purity, Autonomy, Wisdom, Perseverance, or Independence).
            Active Use Limit: Activating a standard capability below consumes the character's once-per-session active Virtue slot. Capabilities designated as a Tactical Exposure involve immediate self-sacrifice and do not consume the once-per-session active slot (and may be used even if the slot is already spent).

            Capabilities
            • Unshakeable Sovereignty (Resisting Control): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As a reflexive action when targeted by a supernatural power that would force her to act against her core nature, rewrite her memories, exert direct mind control, or corrupt her soul or flesh, the character may spend 1 Willpower. Doing so grants her complete immunity to supernatural mind-control, memory modification, or horror-induced Breaking Points for the remainder of the scene (or grants an automatic success on a Clash of Wills against direct soul or bodily alteration).
            • Crucible of Endurance (Overcoming Shock): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As a reflexive action when afflicted by severe temporary physical or mental Tilts (such as Stunned, Knocked Down, Shaken, or Blind) or heavy wound penalties, the character may spend 1 Willpower. Doing so instantly clears all temporary physical and mental Tilts and allows her to completely ignore all wound penalties and fatigue for a number of turns equal to her Virtue rating.
            • Purifying Exposure (Moral Triumph): [Tactical Exposure — Conditional Reset]
              When the character chooses to suffer severe personal loss, physical injury, or social ruin specifically to refuse a moral compromise or uphold her personal code, any Integrity roll forced by that ordeal gains her Integrity Anchor bonus (+half Virtue rating, rounded up). This action is a Tactical Exposure and does not require an available active slot. If she succeeds on the Breaking Point roll, her emotional response converts the trial into righteous moral pride, granting her a +2 bonus on all Resolve and Composure rolls for the remainder of the scene and instantly resetting her once-per-session active Virtue slot if it was spent.

            3. Eye of Revelation (••)

            Prerequisites: Spark of Virtue (•••••), A Virtue centered on Truth, Discernment, Wisdom, or Honesty.
            Active Use Limit: Activating a standard capability below consumes the character's once-per-session active Virtue slot. Capabilities designated as a Tactical Exposure involve immediate self-sacrifice and do not consume the once-per-session active slot (and may be used even if the slot is already spent).

            Capabilities
            • Piercing the Veil (Unmasking Illusions): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As a reflexive action when encountering a supernatural illusion, glamour, mind-altering concealment, memory rewrite, or shapechanged entity (such as a Changeling's Mask, a Vampire's Obfuscate, or a Demon's Cover), the character may spend 1 Willpower. For the remainder of the scene, she automatically sees through all magical disguises and illusions within line of sight, perceiving the true nature of hidden entities and reality.
            • Uncompromising Truth (Forcing Disclosure): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              When conducting an interrogation or Social Maneuvering action against a target who is actively lying, withholding vital secrets, or operating under supernatural compulsion, the character may spend 1 Willpower and speak a direct, unvarnished truth. The target cannot use supernatural abilities to conceal their intentions or lie to her for the remainder of the scene; any attempt to speak a deliberate falsehood forces the target to spend 1 Willpower or remain silent.
            • Exposing the Horror (Unveiling Concealment): [Tactical Exposure — Conditional Reset]
              When the character deliberately forces a dangerous, hidden truth into the open—such as unveiling a monster hiding in human society, exposing a conspiracy's Infrastructure, or revealing a terrible secret to save others—she may accept a Tactical Exposure (taking an immediate Integrity Breaking Point) as an instant action. Doing so strips supernatural concealment, stealth, and Cover from all entities in the scene for turns equal to her Virtue rating. If she succeeds on the Breaking Point roll, her once-per-session active Virtue slot instantly resets if it was spent.

            4. Veil of Sanctuary (••)

            Prerequisites: Spark of Virtue (•••••), A Virtue centered on Discretion, Prudence, Fidelity, Sanctuary, or Protection.
            Active Use Limit: Activating a standard capability below consumes the character's once-per-session active Virtue slot. Capabilities designated as a Tactical Exposure involve immediate self-sacrifice and do not consume the once-per-session active slot (and may be used even if the slot is already spent).

            Capabilities
            • Inviolable Confidence (Resisting Intrusive Perception): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As a reflexive action when targeted by supernatural mind-reading, memory extraction, telepathic probing, or powers that force truthful disclosure (such as a Vampire's Auspex, a Mage's Mind magic, or Eye of Revelation itself), the character may spend 1 Willpower. For the remainder of the scene, her protected thoughts, memories, and secrets are completely immune to supernatural intrusion, appearing as an absolute void or impenetrable static to the intruder.
            • Wards of Discretion (Shielding the Sanctuary): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As an instant action, the character may spend 1 Willpower to cast a shroud of discretion over a conversation, location, or group of allies. For the remainder of the scene, all supernatural scrying, magical tracking, eavesdropping, and technological surveillance directed at the warded area automatically fail, preserving the sanctuary of the space.
            • Sacred Discretion (Preserving Confidences): [Tactical Exposure — Conditional Reset]
              When the character willingly undergoes severe personal danger, physical harm, or social ruin specifically to protect a vital secret, shield an innocent's civilian life, or cover the escape of her allies, she may accept a Tactical Exposure (taking an immediate Integrity Breaking Point) as an instant action. Doing so completely erases all immediate physical, magical, and memory traces of the protected secret or missing allies from the scene. If she succeeds on the Breaking Point roll, her once-per-session active Virtue slot instantly resets if it was spent.

            5. Scales of Retribution (••)

            Prerequisites: Spark of Virtue (•••••), A Virtue centered on Justice, Retribution, Reckoning, or Righteous Rage.
            Active Use Limit: Activating a standard capability below consumes the character's once-per-session active Virtue slot. Capabilities designated as a Tactical Exposure involve immediate self-sacrifice and do not consume the once-per-session active slot (and may be used even if the slot is already spent).

            Capabilities
            • Mirror of Guilt (Reflecting Moral Trauma): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As a reflexive action when the character or an ally in line of sight suffers a physical or psychological attack from a bad actor, the character may spend 1 Willpower to force the attacker to feel the exact emotional horror and agony they just inflicted. The attacker must immediately roll Composure + Supernatural Tolerance vs. the character's Resolve + Virtue rating. On a failure, the attacker suffers the Shaken or Guilt-Ridden Condition and loses 1 Willpower as their own cruelty rebounds onto their conscience.
            • Inescapable Reckoning (Denying Emotional Escape): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As an instant action when confronting a wrongdoer, the character calls out their specific transgressions, stripping away their ability to disassociate, justify, or ignore what they have done. She spends 1 Willpower. For a number of turns equal to her Virtue rating, the target cannot use supernatural abilities to flee the scene, erase memories, or hide their emotions. Furthermore, any attempts by the target to resist social or moral pressure suffer a penalty equal to half the character's Virtue rating (rounded up).
            • Righteous Avenger (Forced Breaking Point): [Tactical Exposure — Conditional Reset]
              When the character willingly puts herself in direct physical danger—stepping into an enemy's line of fire without Defense or accepting a strike—to force a monster, slasher, or tyrant to face what they are, she may declare a Tactical Exposure as an instant action. She points at the target and speaks the unvarnished toll of their crimes. The target is immediately forced to undergo an Integrity, Clarity, or moral Breaking Point at a penalty equal to half the character's Virtue rating (rounded up). If the target fails this Breaking Point, they suffer an immediate moral collapse (gaining a severe mental Condition such as Broken or Despair and losing 1 Willpower). If the target fails the roll, the character's once-per-session active Virtue slot instantly resets if it was spent.

            6. Covenant of Redemption (••)

            Prerequisites: Spark of Virtue (•••••), A Virtue centered on Redemption, Reconciliation, Mercy, Restoration, or Forgiveness.
            Active Use Limit: Activating a standard capability below consumes the character's once-per-session active Virtue slot. Capabilities designated as a Tactical Exposure involve immediate self-sacrifice and do not consume the once-per-session active slot (and may be used even if the slot is already spent).

            Capabilities
            • Avenue of Grace (Offering Salvation): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As an instant action when confronting an antagonist acting under supernatural compulsion, ancestral curse, obsession, or corruptive blood bond, the character may spend 1 Willpower and extend an open hand or offer of peace. The target immediately receives an unprompted roll to break free of their compulsion, adding the character's Integrity Anchor bonus (+half Virtue rating, rounded up) to the roll.
            • Restorative Oath (Binding Concord): [Consumes Once-Per-Session Active Slot]
              As an instant action following a battle or truce, the character may spend 1 Willpower to seal a pledge of reform, non-aggression, or sanctuary with a former enemy who genuinely expresses remorse. Until the binding Concord is violated, the former enemy gains half the character's Virtue rating (rounded up) as a bonus on all resistance rolls against dark urges, supernatural frenzy, corruptive blood bonds, or horror-induced Breaking Points. If the former enemy voluntarily violates the Concord, they instantly lose this bonus, suffer the Guilt-Ridden Condition, and lose 1 Willpower.
            • Crucible of Reconciliation (Vulnerable Appeal): [Tactical Exposure — Conditional Reset]
              When the character steps directly into harm's way without weapons or magical shields raised—offering an enemy a genuine path to surrender and grace while taking an undefended hit or facing mortal danger—she may accept a Tactical Exposure (taking an immediate Integrity Breaking Point) as an instant action. The target must immediately roll Composure + Supernatural Tolerance vs. the character's Resolve + Virtue rating; if the target fails, they are struck with profound moral remorse, halting all hostile actions for turns equal to her Virtue rating. If the character succeeds on her forced Breaking Point roll, her once-per-session active Virtue slot instantly resets if it was spent.
            Conceptually, these Merits come in pairs: Relational/Identity, Discovery/Sanctuary, and Retribution/Redemption. None of them are strictly opposites, so with an appropriate Virtue you could conceivably have any combinations of them. However, any given MG is unlikely to have more than two or three; and that's pushing it.

            Note that these Merits could theoretical be adapted to the various Monster Mash MGs, by replacing the Virtue rating in the mechanics with whatever the MG's power stat is, and looking at the MG's Anchors (Mask/Dirge; Blood/Bone; Elpis/Torment; Needle/Thread; Root/Bloom; Life/Legend; Conviction/Loyalty) to see which ones they qualify for. (For Deviants, I'd use Loyalty as the “Power Stat” for Devoted and Guardians, and Conviction for Renegades and Nemeses.)

            In fact, an interesting observation: most of those alternate anchors form an Internal/Relational duality instead of the character strength/weakness duality that Virtue and Vice represent: Mask, Blood, Needle, Bloom, Legend, and Loyalty are all Relational “Virtues”, while Dirge, Bone, Thread, Root, Life, and Conviction are Identity “Virtues”.

            So these six Merits should be good for all Magical Girls, not just Mortal MGs.

                    I think the Virtue prerequisite could maybe be presented as an action or event, rather than a qualifier? I don't know, demanding the ST to judge a taxonomy of Virtues seems harder to implement consistently than it should, and open to confusion.

                    Some ideas:

                    Vicarious Exposure
                    -> You can fulfill your Virtue by choosing to talk when fighting would be safer and acceptable.
                    -> You witnessed a serious/dangerous mental breakdown and want to help that person.

                    Crucible of Conviction
                    -> You can fulfill your Virtue by resisting severe coercion of any nature.
                    -> You suffered physical, social or supernatural assault you could not defend against, but stood your ground.

                    Eye of Revelation
                    -> You can fulfill your Virtue by insisting on finding or revealing the truth against danger.
                    -> You saw comforting misinformation cause actual damage, as witness or participant.

                    Veil of Sanctuary
                    -> You can fulfill your Virtue by preserving the privacy of someone, protecting them with this.
                    -> You've seen someone's information revealed and used to cause undue harm.

                    Scales of Retribution
                    -> You can fulfill your Virtue by delivering the price of evil to those untouched by law.
                    -> You saw how much a bully fears when their bluff is called on and you'll deliver it to them all.

                    Covenant of Redemption
                    -> You can fulfill your Virtue by choosing to end hostilities when destruction is at hand.
                    -> You saw the cycle of misery be broken and a monster revealed into someone who can heal.

                    If nothing else, we can add a requirement that the first use of the Merit must be fulfilling the Virtue.

                    As they represent different tactics to solve a problem, each with personal costs, I'm not so sure we even need to put something there: the Merits enforce the right posture by themselves, and the player will be spending points not in a combo, but strategies they want to use in lieu of violence.

                    I think your Merits are excellent and it is a solid starting point for the other MGs. With this we have the Transformation, the power stat, and a good collection of options to give agency to a Detective MG.

                    Does the Compact of Transformation requires any further tweak for now? I think we mostly finished it, at least a prototype. Now we have the Mascot to hammer down. I think it should be a very basic critter without much capability to begin with. A single defining Perk, with the standard being to look ordinary, and the main Merit governing its ability to do its main job of Maintenance, specialized advice and measure the MG's integration into the role.

                    Other Merits can give other capabilities to the Mascot, probably similar to Social Merits as it becomes capable at support and other tasks, and one or two to give it more direct presence.

                        Here is what I currently have for Compact of Transformation, to make sure we're on the same page:
                        Merit: Compact of Transformation (• to •••••)

                        Prerequisites: None (Open to mortals and all supernatural splats alike).
                        Type: Supernatural / Physical Merit.
                        Description: The character possesses a unique mystical focus—traditionally a makeup compact, star-pendant, brooch, or pocket mirror—given to her by a Mascot. This item acts as the catalyst through which she channels her inner light, enabling her to instantly rewrite her civilian form into a high-utility, stylized magical persona.

                        Core Mechanics
                        The Identity Shield: Activating the compact provides an absolute identity shield. Even if the character’s face remains exposed, a supernatural perception filter prevents onlookers from connecting the magical persona to her civilian identity. Forcing a recognition check requires a contested Wits + Composure vs. Presence + Subterfuge (or Supernatural Tolerance) roll at a -3 penalty, which is waived only with concrete proof. Digital recordings, cameras, and facial recognition software automatically register the persona as a separate, unidentifiable individual.

                        Manifested Loadout: Upon transforming, civilian gear vanishes, replaced by a predetermined magical loadout bound to the compact. Form does not follow function. Players select standard item statistics from equipment lists but define their visual appearance entirely through flavor (e.g., a Heavy Sniper Rifle reskinned as a star-tipped wand firing starlight beams). If gear leaves the character's person, it dissolves into light and resets to the compact's transformation inventory.

                        Equipment Point Budget: The dots invested in this Merit dictate how much total magical gear can manifest alongside the transformation. A character receives an Equipment Point Budget equal to 3 + dots in Compact of Transformation. She allocates these points into her transformed loadout, creating a fixed inventory of manifested gear, which can be freely reallocated during narrative downtime between stories:

                        • (1 Dot): 4 Equipment Points
                        •• (2 Dots): 5 Equipment Points
                        ••• (3 Dots): 6 Equipment Points
                        •••• (4 Dots): 7 Equipment Points
                        ••••• (5 Dots): 8 Equipment Points

                        Follow-Up Transformation Merits

                        Shared Grace (••)
                        Prerequisites: Compact of Transformation (•)
                        Type: Supernatural / Physical Merit
                        Mechanics: Manifested gear no longer dissolves when separated from the character's person. It remains physical, retaining all statistics and aesthetics, as long as the user's Compact of Transformation remains active. The user can manifest an item, loan it to a teammate or NPC, and then manifest completely separate gear for themselves from their remaining Equipment Point Budget. Furthermore, the user can reflexively dissolve loaned gear into light at any distance, immediately reclaiming those budget points.

                        Cosmic Essence (•••)
                        Prerequisites: Compact of Transformation (•)
                        Type: Supernatural / Physical Merit
                        Mechanics: Transformed gear never runs out of mundane ammunition, battery life, charges, or internal fuel blocks. This overrides the Reload or Battery Capacity traits of underlying templates, meaning the character never spends actions t reload in combat. This benefit waives only mundane logistical costs; abilities requiring supernatural resources, such as Pure Spark abilities costing Spark, must still be paid normally.
                        And here's an idea designed to allow a mortal magical girl to get more out of the Compact of Transformation than her supernatural counterparts can:
                        Mortal Transformed Merits

                        1. Heart-Shield Aegis (••)

                        Prerequisites: Mortal status (no major supernatural splat template), Compact of Transformation (•), Vicarious Exposure (••).
                        Type: Supernatural / Physical Merit.
                        Description: While transformed, the character’s emotional empathy and willingness to absorb trauma manifest as kinetic forcefields and protective shock-absorbers. The deep empathy that soothes hearts in civilian life becomes a physical barrier that deflects bullets and cushions blows meant for her allies.

                        Transformed Capabilities
                        Kinetic Empathy (Physical Interposition): Whenever an ally within 5 yards is targeted by a physical attack, the character may spend 1 Willpower as a reflexive action to instantly leap or flash between the attacker and her ally. She becomes the target of the attack instead, and her Defense applies against the incoming blow even if she has already acted this turn.
                        Shock-Absorber Field: When taking physical damage on behalf of an ally via Kinetic Empathy or when absorbing a Breaking Point via Vicarious Exposure, the character gains a physical Armor rating equal to her Virtue rating against that specific attack or ordeal.

                        Suggested Transformed Gear: Aegis Dress / Ribbon Barrier (Manifested Armor / Shield Equipment Template: Cost 2 Points, grants Armor 2/1 or +2 Defense).
                        Gear Enhancement Note: When Empathetic Aegis or Vicarious Exposure is active, the Aegis Dress extends its Armor rating to all allies within 3 yards and converts incoming Lethal physical damage aimed at protected allies into Bashing damage absorbed by the dress's kinetic barrier.

                        2. Unyielding Bastion (••)

                        Prerequisites: Mortal status (no major supernatural splat template), Compact of Transformation (•), Crucible of Conviction (••).
                        Type: Supernatural / Physical Merit.
                        Description: While transformed, the character’s refusal to yield moral ground translates into absolute physical immovability and devastating kinetic impact. Her refusal to compromise physically anchors her to the earth, allowing her to shrug off crushing blows and strike back with the weight of her conviction.

                        Transformed Capabilities
                        Immovable Resolve (Physical Anchoring): While transformed, the character cannot be knocked Prone, forcefully moved, grapple-thrown, or displaced by physical attacks, explosions, or supernatural telekinesis unless the attacker wins a contested Strength + Athletics roll against her Resolve + Virtue rating.
                        Juggernaut Momentum: When performing a Charge, Knockdown, or Smash action against an enemy, the character adds her Virtue rating as bonus dice to the attack roll and ignores up to her Virtue rating in environmental obstacles or door Durability.

                                        Waiting for your second post to be approved, send as personal message if you can to hasten things up. I'll later give a few suggestions of my own.

                                        I like both the Hunter Compact and Conspiracy producing Deviants, but I'd add another layer:

                                        Sometimes Mascots come seeking suitable girls wiling to accept this duty. But sometimes they come with more specific needs: they have a particular girl in mind, Human or not, and the Compact of Transformation must reach her. It's another type of mission, one that puts Hunters, and not necessarily Magical Girls, in the streets to work creatively.

                                        The Conspiracy relationship with Mascots is also a matter to explore. Mascots have a noble cause and specialized knowledge, but they're not universally smart or ethical. The Deviants will need Compacts, after all, and those need a technician that's simultaneously their mentor, loyal retainer, and possible agentand spy to the Conspiracy. How crazy can this relationship be?

                                        On the plus side, and keeping with the genre, this one Conspiracy may be an exception to the norm even if still (deservedly) hated by their creations: they're genuinely not enemies to the Magical Girls that embrace the cause. They're not their owners and accept with grace to be deemed villains. They will defend their assets and work and spy on the girls, but interventions are few and genuinely well meaning, possibly offering resources to help with stability and the occasional field support.

                                        How does Deviants deal with a Conspiracy that is intrinsically ambiguous? They're not good, and the very nature of Virtue means even their most loyal Deviants can't deny their predation against innocent girls. But they are your allies for a greater cause and offer genuine selfless help you may in fact benefit greatly from.

                                        Also, the Conspiracy may bring something else to the table: failures. Attempts that became Deviants, but not Magical Girls. Misjudgment of character or the trauma caused result in a Deviant that either don't have the inclination to take up the mantle, or worse, dedicated to ruin everything the Conspiracy works for, even their cause at large.

                                        My interest is piqued because it's a core theme of the genre: the value of hope and what will happen if you give it up. As much as they may hate the Conspiracy, genuine MG Deviants (and granted, they may not all be "genuine" as far as it goes, even if they take up the cause) keep the hope and belief in others. Those who failed are now hopeless, and may become a danger not only to the Conspiracy, but to innocent people.

                                                    Should MGs with powers from other blue books, derived from splats or Hunters have access to Spark of Virtue?

                                                    My default answer is yes, but I want a second opinion. I think even those with a Minor Power Stat (the kind that goes only to 5) should be able to buy it and fuse Power Stats.

                                                    Also at Virtue 6+ the Girl should be able to buy traits above 5 like other supernaturals. Maybe it only manifests during transformation, though I am not against this level of XP commitment make her experience and exposure to magic be finally affecting her directly. Becoming greater than life in your civilian identity is also a staple of the Super Hero genre in general, and Mahō Shōjo no less at all.

                                                    We also need a clearer definition of the loadout and budget and their differences. Should the Girl be able to summon multiple copies of the same item? Is a loadout like a physical arsenal, each item only exists in one place at a time? Is it possible to expand this arsenal independent from the budget?

                                                    One source I think worth our time to look for ideas is I Don't Want To Be A Magical Girl. I'm not yet sure if it will hold as a genre example or breaker, but it does have elements to explore that especially fit Chronicles of Darkness.
