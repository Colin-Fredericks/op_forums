            First off, Clades Companion is now available for sale, so go check that out if you haven't already.

            Chapter One explored the Deviant experience in broad terms — discussing Origins, Clades, and what all Remade gain and lose as part of the Divergence. Chapter Five discusses specific Broken characters — how to create them and how to use their Variations and Scars to guide roleplaying. The sample below is from a section on the initial process of thinking about the character you want to make, whether you start from who they are (or were), what capabilities they have, their vulnerabilities, or their relationships. This excerpt only covers the first "corner" of the puzzle of making a Renegade.

            Follow the link in my signature for an excerpt from a section of the chapter about the practical considerations of character design within a roleplaying game (as well as the full text of Chapter Five for patrons).

            Starting Points

            Deviant: The Renegades offers tremendous flexibility in the kinds of characters that can be built using its systems. The sheer number of Variations, Scars, and Standard Deviations it puts into players’ hands can be a bit overwhelming, however, and the additional examples in Chapters Three and Four of this book are unlikely to reduce the potential for analysis paralysis. Add in Merits, Adaptations, and Forms to all that, and it’s a lot to take in.

            So, if you’re sitting there with a blank character sheet, the 345-page core book, possibly two Kickstarter supplements (The Clades Companion and The Devoted Companion), and now this, it’s normal to have a hard time knowing where to start. The purpose of this section is to help you get started, to resist the urge to focus on the giant pile of jigsaw puzzle pieces on your table and instead to concentrate on finding just one corner and some edges. Start from whichever of the four corners inspires you most.

            First Corner: Origin

            Start at the beginning — not with your character’s Origin but with her origin. Who was she before her first encounter with the Web of Pain?

            Childhood

            You can start at the very beginning if you want. Think about her childhood. What was her home life like, her school life? Who were her friends, and did she have any noteworthy rivals or enemies among her peers? How did she spend her free time? Was she active in sports, performance arts, or clubs? Did she have favorite books, TV shows, or movies? When the grown-ups in her life praised her, which of her personality traits did they tend to highlight? When they criticized her, what faults did they tell her were holding her back? How far did she get in school; did she drop out of high school, or did she collect post-graduate degrees like other people collect shoes?

            What did she dream her life would be like when she grew up? Did she have grand schemes or ordinary hopes? Did her vision of her future drive her constantly, or did she sort of expect that it would all come together when the time was right? Did these dreams play to her natural strengths or reflect a hope that one day she could overcome her weaknesses? Or did she have impossible dreams — fantasies of attaining superhuman abilities or irrational fears of suffering life-changing injuries or developing debilitating medical conditions?

            If she was always entangled in the Web of Pain, what was that life like and how did it shape the adult she became (or is still becoming)? While this often describes Genotypals, it’s possible for someone to grow up in a conspiracy without anyone expecting her to Diverge.

            Why We Ask: Childhood can set into motion the whole course of a person’s life, especially when the Web of Pain gets involved. A child with a rare disease may attract the attention of a conspiracy that promises a cure, while one born into a conspiracy might be enmeshed in its schemes from birth. A child with grand plans might make her winding way into an existing conspiracy or discover a means of triggering Divergence in herself or others. One who hoped for a quiet life could find those hopes dashed by conspiracy agents who lure or abduct her. A child’s caretakers could enroll her in research studies — whether because they want to support the advancement of science, because they think these treatments will save their child or make her life better, or because the conspiracy offered them something they didn’t want (or perhaps didn’t dare) to refuse. Knowing what your character had (or lacked) as a child — a home, friends, family, a dog, talents, and hopes for the future — can help inform what the Divergence will take from (or give to) her.

            Adulthood

            Consider who she was as an adult — whether as a follow-up to the questions of childhood, as a prelude to her collision with the conspiracy, or both. What was her day-to-day life like? How did she earn a living? If she didn’t live alone, who were the other members of her household — her parents (or grandparents), a spouse and/or kids, a lover, one or more roommates, or a community united by a religious or spiritual connection? Where did she live, in the sense of both city/town/wilderness and house/apartment/compound? How did she spend her non-working hours? Did she have notable hobbies, close friends, or preferred forms of entertainment? How did she unwind?

            Was her life on a general incline — with a bright future just up ahead? Or was she stuck in a slow (or steep) downward spiral? Or did it vary with the day or with what part of her life you were looking at? Maybe she had it all together at work, but her home life was a mess — or the reverse. Was she generally happy or dissatisfied with the way her life had turned out?

            Did she have any entanglements or problems that might have left her vulnerable to the conspiracy. Was she in over her head in debt? Did she have dangerous enemies (or friends)? Was she recently diagnosed with a terminal illness, or has she been living with a chronic illness for years that she would risk much to free herself from?

            Why We Ask: It isn’t unusual for people in the world of the Chronicles of Darkness to encounter conspiracy agents. It is unusual for these encounters to lead to the Divergence. The conspiracies aren’t interested in everyone. Some are picky about their subjects because it increases the odds of success. Most are picky because they don’t want to attract too much attention to themselves. Conspiracies seek out people who are vulnerable, who won’t be missed, or who are desperate. Knowing your character’s circumstances can inform potential vectors for the Web of Pain to ensnare her. Knowing what she had (or lacked) sets up opportunities to contrast that with what the Divergence took from or gave to her. Friends, family members, colleagues, and rivals often make good starting Touchstones.

            Divergence

            Consider who she became after her Divergence, including who she is now. You can treat this as a follow-up to the prior two life stages or use it as a starting point in its own right. Maybe knowing how she survives from day to day is more important to you than knowing how she got there. You can fill that in later in the character creation process or reveal it in a series of flashbacks after the chronicle begins. Nothing says you need to know everything about your character’s past before the first chapter; you would be in good company with many of the media inspirations for Deviant. A little later in this supplement, we’ll talk about how to draw Deviants together into a cohort, but for now, think of your character in isolation.

            Who is or was her Progenitor? How did they trigger her Divergence? Were there other test subjects, and what happened to them? How long did she stay with the conspiracy? Why and how did she leave? How has she avoided falling back into their hands (or those of other conspiracies)? How does she survive from day to day, meeting basic needs like food, shelter, and clothing? How does she stave off Instability? Does she hold her Loyalty Touchstones close, or does she keep them at arm’s length for their own protection?

            Why We Ask: The Divergence is the bright line drawn between Baseline and Broken. It’s natural to focus on it and its immediate aftermath. That brief period in a Deviant’s life is one where she is likely to form bonds with most of her future Conviction Touchstones, and many Remade also forge bonds with people who will become crucial Loyalty Touchstones. The Divergence brings unbreakable friendships and unquenchable rivalries and animosities. How your character responded to her newly discovered Variations and Scars reflects who she was — with all masks torn aside — and speaks volumes about who she is now.

            Bottom Line

            Starting character creation with a deep examination of their Baseline life makes it easier to think of them as a person first and a Deviant second. It tends to draw attention to how the Divergence changed them — not just by giving them Variations and Scars but by upending their lives.

                        For this week's bonus content, I'm giving you sneak peeks of a few more Scars - Scapegoat, Hubris, and Noblesse Oblige:

                        Controlled Scar: Scapegoat (• to •••••)

                        Keywords: Social; Subtle

                        The Deviant’s presence has a deleterious effect on the ones he cares about.

                        At Magnitude •, choose a Persistent Scar with a Magnitude equal to the Magnitude of Scapegoat (the secondary Scar). When the Remade activates the entangled Variation in the presence of a Loyalty Touchstone, that Touchstone suffers the effects of the secondary Scar until the end of the chapter unless the Broken succeeds on a Scar Resistance roll. The maximum number of victims the secondary Scar can affect at a time cannot exceed (6 – Scar Resistance). The Scar must be one that can meaningfully affect a Baseline. If the Scar has no viable victims to target in the current scene, Scapegoat gains the Bystander Deviation below.

                        At Magnitude ••, as Magnitude •, and choose one:

                        • Cohort: The secondary Scar also affects members of the Deviant’s cohort.

                        • Enduring: The secondary Scar plagues the victim longer, lasting until the end of the story. If paired with the Bystander Deviation, it is usually permanent.

                        • Persistent: In addition to taking effect when the entangled Variation is active, up to once per chapter, at a time of the Storyteller’s choosing, it imposes its secondary Scar even when the Variation isn’t active.

                        • Sympathetic: The secondary Scar can fall upon a target who isn’t present in the current scene.

                        At Magnitude •••, as Magnitude ••, but choose two Magnitude •• effects.

                        At Magnitude ••••, as Magnitude ••, but choose three Magnitude •• effects.

                        At Magnitude •••••, as Magnitude ••, but the Deviant suffers from all four Magnitude •• effects.

                        Deviations

                        Bystander (+0 Magnitude): The secondary Scar doesn’t affect Loyalty Touchstones. Instead, it affects innocent bystanders (Baselines who are not Conviction Touchstones, Loyalty Touchstones, or enemies). However, the Broken does not have the option to make a Scar Resistance roll to suppress this effect and Scapegoat becomes an Overt Scar. If no viable bystanders are present, the Variation works normally but no one suffers the secondary Scar.

                        Random (+0 Magnitude): Choose a number of potential secondary Scars equal to 1 + Scar Magnitude. Whenever you impose a secondary Scar due to Scapegoat, randomly select which one affects the victim at random.

                        Cruel (+1 Magnitude): As Random and exclusive with it and with Merciful, but the Storyteller chooses which secondary Scar to impose, and it seems calculated to maximize the Deviant’s anguish.

                        Merciful (–1 Magnitude): As Random and exclusive with it and with Cruel, but the Broken’s player decides which secondary Scar to impose.

                        Persistent Scar: Hubris (• to •••••)

                        Keywords: Mental, Social; Subtle

                        The Broken is certain he’s the smartest one in the room and that there is no problem that his intelligence and Variations can’t overcome if he puts his mind to it. Moreover, he wants everyone else to know it.

                        At Magnitude •, the Deviant is prone to gloat over his successes (and any enemies in his power), to brag about his intelligence and Variations, and to talk about his plans even to enemies. Whenever this causes a complication for the Remade or his cohort, he takes a Beat.

                        At Magnitude ••, as Magnitude •, and choose one:

                        • Brag: Whenever someone asks the Broken about himself, he must make a successful Scar Resistance roll to resist the urge to openly hint either at his Variations, his shocking plans for the future, or his past crimes. At this Magnitude, he need not be blunt or specific — particularly if doing so in present company is a bad idea, such as when he is being interrogated by the police — but anyone reading between the lines should be able to glean enough information from his not-quite-confession to pursue further lines of inquiry.

                        • Toy: Whenever the Deviant has an enemy in his power — whether because they surrendered or were incapacitated — he must make a successful Scar Resistance roll in order to kill them or let them die. If he lets them live, he cannot simply keep them prisoner or hand them over to someone else. He must either give them an opportunity to win their freedom (which need not be fair but must not assure their failure) or contrive a satisfying and elaborate method of execution that incidentally gives them a chance to escape. This does not affect the Broken’s ability or willingness to kill a Conviction Touchstone out of hand.

                        At Magnitude •••, as Magnitude ••, and choose one:

                        • Balanced: The Deviant suffers both Magnitude •• effects.

                        • Boast: The Remade must have chosen Brag as his Magnitude •• effect. As Brag, but the Broken can’t roll to resist at least hinting about his secrets. Moreover, if asked direct questions about any of the secret topics, he must make a successful Scar Resistance roll or lay out the truth in excruciating detail even when doing so is a terrible idea.

                        • Gloat: The Deviant must have chosen Toy as his Magnitude •• effect. The Remade’s enemies are so far beneath his notice that they don’t even warrant him killing them anymore. The Toy effect now applies to his Conviction Touchstones. Additionally, the Broken doesn’t even toy with lesser enemies, instead freeing them a short while later, usually after regaling them with lengthy monologues meant to impress upon them how intelligent and powerful he is.

                        At Magnitude ••••, the Deviant suffers from all three Magnitude ••• effects.

                        Deviations

                        • Calling Card (+1 Magnitude): The Remade feels compelled to take trophies or leave a calling card whenever he makes a significant accomplishment. Choose an appropriate type of calling card or trophy; failing to leave/take this when the opportunity arises inflicts a minor Instability on the Broken. A calling card makes it easier for the conspiracy (or the authorities) to identify his handiwork; leaving a calling card makes this Scar count as Overt for the conspiracy’s next surveillance roll. A trophy makes it easier for the Broken’s crimes to be traced to him; if investigators gain access to his trophy collection, he suffers the Hunted Condition.

                        Persistent Scar: Noblesse Oblige (• to •••••)

                        Keywords: Mental, Social; Subtle

                        The Broken suffers a supernatural compulsion to fulfill the desires of strangers.

                        At Magnitude •, the Deviant’s player may accept a Beat whenever she performs a random act of kindness for a stranger that results in a complication for her or her cohort.

                        At Magnitude ••, as Magnitude •, and up to once per chapter the Storyteller may choose a character the Remade is compelled to help. The Deviant must ensure that the target fulfills a Vice, Virtue, Aspiration, or esoteric drive (such as furthering a Touchstone). The Remade knows which person is her target but doesn’t automatically receive knowledge of his desires; she needs to learn that information on her own. Unless the target accomplishes his goal before the end of the next chapter, the Deviant suffers one of the following at the beginning of the following chapter, chosen at the time she develops this Scar:

                        • Health: The Remade suffers a point of lethal damage that cannot be healed by supernatural effects.

                        • Power: This Scar becomes Controlled instead of Persistent and gains the Repeatable keyword. The Deviant cannot activate the entangled Variations unless she has fulfilled the obligation since the beginning of the previous chapter.

                        • Stability: The Broken suffers a minor Instability.

                        • Will: The Deviant loses a point of Willpower.

                        If the Storyteller chooses a new target, it frees the Remade from any existing obligation (and potential consequences for failing to uphold it).

                        At Magnitude •••, as Magnitude ••, but the Broken has fewer avenues by which to satisfy her compulsion. Choose two of the following when the Deviant develops this Scar, and helping the target fulfill these no longer satisfies the conditions of this Scar: Esoteric, Short-Term Aspiration, Long-Term Aspiration, Vice, or Virtue.

                        At Magnitude ••••, as Magnitude •••, but only one type of desire satisfies the conditions of this Scar.

                        Deviations

                        Except for Compelled, all the below Deviations are only allowed if the base effect is Magnitude •• or higher.

                        • Guided (–1 Magnitude): The Deviant automatically learns one of the target’s relevant desires that can be used to satisfy this Scar.

                        • Aware (–2 Magnitude): Exclusive with Guided, the Remade automatically learns all the target’s relevant desires that can be used to satisfy this Scar.

                        • Compelled (+0 Magnitude): Rather than the choice of target being wholly at the Storyteller’s whim, it must be someone matching certain criteria, one who takes a certain action, or a combination of the two. For example, the Broken might be compelled to bring joy to children, perform a service for the first person to pay her fee, or grant the last wishes of the dying. Targets can include Conviction Touchstones and other enemies (but not cohort members, Loyalty Touchstones, or other close allies) as long as they meet the criteria. The Deviant’s obligation to a target persists until a new viable target presents himself, with each satisfied desire of the current target replaced with a compulsion to satisfy another until a new target is acquired.

                        • Costly (+1 Magnitude): The price of failure to satisfy this Scar is higher — aggravated damage for Health, medium Instability for Stability, and three Willpower for Will. This Deviation is not available if the Remade’s price is Power.

                        • Multitudinous (+1 Magnitude): Choose a second price of failing to satisfy this Scar (Health, Power, Stability, or Will). The Deviant suffers both.

                        • Crushing (+2 Magnitude): As Multitudinous and exclusive with it, but the transformed suffers three prices of failing to satisfy the Scar.

                        • Unyielding (+1 Magnitude): Continued failure to satisfy this Scar causes the Remade to wither away. Any damage, Instability, or Willpower lost to this Scar cannot be healed/replenished until the Broken successfully satisfies the Scar. If the price is Power, fulfilling her obligation does not allow the Deviant to activate the entangled Variation until the beginning of the next chapter.

                                            Chapter Six is about character arcs, whether they're victories in spite of seemingly insurmountable odds, poignant tragedies, or something else entirely. Today we preview some mechanical guidance around End Stage. A section about the broad arc of chronicles is available for free at the link in my signature (with the full text of the chapter available to patrons).

                                            Supernova

                                            As described in Chapter Three (p. XX), Variations have intentional limits to their range, duration, and scale. A Deviant in End Stage, however, can often break those boundaries. Most of the time, End Stage merely supercharges one aspect of each of the Broken’s Magnitude 5 Variations until the end of the chapter. This can still have a considerable impact:

                                            Control: Scar Finesse rolls to activate or target the Variation enjoy a +3 bonus.

                                            Enlarged Area: The Variation affects a larger area than usual. If it is not Directed, it grants its benefits to all allies, all non-enemies, or all logical targets within 10 yards/meters. If it is an Aura, its radius extends twice Scar Power times farther than usual (doubled at 1 Scar Power, quadrupled at 2 Scar Power, etc.). If it is Directed, the Deviant may affect multiple targets per turn (no greater than Scar Finesse) and any number of targets may fall under its sway at a time (rather than being limited by Scar Power); resolve multiple targeting as a long burst (Deviant, p. 198). Alternatively, a Directed effect’s range is increased by a factor of 10.

                                            Greater Duration: Once activated, the Variation lasts until the end of the chapter instead of until the end of the scene. If the Variation has the Perpetual keyword, once activated, it counts as being Persistent and Perpetual even if ordinarily Controlled or Involuntary.

                                            Instinctive: The Remade may activate or deactivate the Variation reflexively. If Directed, the Broken can direct its effects as a reflexive action once for each of her turns. She can do this at a time other than her own turn, but once she directs the Variation reflexively, she may not do so again until the beginning of her next turn.

                                            Limitless: Variation effects that are limited to once per scene, chapter, or story do not have this limit.

                                            Shifted Scars: The Scar entangled with the Variation changes. This could be a difference within the same Scar (which things trigger his Phobia, for example, or what behavior Subliminal Conditioning compels), or it could be a complete change in the Scar.

                                            Super-Potent: The Variation gains capabilities normally impossible for it (see below).

                                            Synergy: The Variation’s effects bleed into one or more of the Broken’s other Variations.

                                            Super-Potent Examples

                                            The below are examples of what a Variation might be capable of if made super-potent or if synergizing with similar Variations. Each lists several examples of Variations appropriate to the effect, but this is by no means an exhaustive list.

                                            • Awareness: The Broken’s senses penetrate phenomena relevant to the Variation with perfect clarity. She enjoys a two-die bonus to Wits rolls. She may also ask the Storyteller questions about anything in the Variation’s purview that she can perceive and receive accurate answers, although the Storyteller may call for Scar Finesse rolls for unusually subtle phenomena. Predator’s Cunning, Precognition, Sensor Array, Specialized Sense

                                            • Disaster: The Deviant may create a level 4 Extreme Environment relevant to the Variation. He is immune to this environment’s effects and any Environmental Tilts it causes. Bioluminescence, Electrokinesis, Environmental Adaptation, Pyrokinesis, Storm-Caller

                                            • Gargantuan: The Remade can take the shape of larger objects or creatures, increasing maximum Size by 10. Animal Transformation, Gigantic, Matter Transformation

                                            • Ghost: The Deviant is undetectable and can pass through any substance at her normal Speed. Boneless, Camouflage, Miniaturization, Out of Phase

                                            • Kill: Whether by means of a powerful Lash or telepathic power that causes victims to die of fright, the Deviant’s Variations become honed for killing. Each turn, as a reflexive action, the Remade may make a Scar Finesse roll. Each success takes a Baseline in range out of the fight. Creeping Dread, Lash, Superhuman Strength, Telekinesis

                                            • Mark: The Remade inflicts permanent damage on the target, imposing a Magnitude 3 Scar appropriate to the Variation (Magnitude 5, if the roll to target the Variation is an exceptional success). This is permanent for most Baselines; others can overcome it by devoting a story to recovering from the damage in some way. Cryokinesis, Fate’s Agent, Lash, Memory Thief, Telepathy

                                            • Narration: The Broken may introduce a convenient fact or plot twist to the scene that is relevant to the Variation. The Storyteller may veto any unreasonable suggestions but is encouraged to permit most scenarios. The first time the Deviant does this in a scene, the effect is automatic. Additional uses require successful Scar Finesse rolls with a cumulative –1 penalty, and once the Remade fails one of these rolls, she cannot attempt further plot twists in the current scene. Hidden Compartments, Pheromones, Superhuman Presence, Superhuman Wits

                                            • Perfection: The Deviant acts with perfect grace or thinks with perfect clarity. Choose Mental, Physical, or Social, as appropriate to the Variation. All the Remade’s actions of that type enjoy the rote quality, and she is treated as having three dots in each Skill of that type in which she has fewer than three dots. Blessing, Hyper-Competence, Omnicompetence, Superhuman Attribute

                                            • Portal: The Broken can open a gateway large enough for two people to walk through at a time, which remains open for a few minutes (a few turns, in action scenes). Its destination reflects the Variation used to create it. Translocation might lead to any location in the world that she has ever been, for example, Astral Travel to one of her sleeping Touchstones, and Otherworldly Connection to a random location in the otherworld connected to her. Astral Travel, Otherworldly Connection, Shadows of the Past, Translocation

                                            • Travel: The Deviant is capable of incredible speeds by air, land, or water (as appropriate), doubling the normal Speed of these forms of travel. She may also dodge without using her next action and does not lose her Defense when targeted by firearms and other attacks that ignore Defense. Aquatic, Enhanced Speed, Flight

                                            • Unkillable: The Broken is functionally invulnerable to physical harm, shrugging off damage and Tilts that reflect injury (although not ones that represent fetters or environmental factors). Anomalous Biology, Carapace, Rapid Healing, Superhuman Stamina

                                            Extinction Burst

                                            As the dramatic conclusion to End Stage draws near, the Deviant’s soul disintegrates under the intensity of her Variations. Most Remade in End Stage never experience this. It is a final surrender to the Divergence from which there is no coming back. Sometimes, the extinction burst reflects the Broken’s state of mind or goal at the time she entered End Stage, but oftentimes it is not so organized, simply a lashing out at random.

                                            Systems: When a Remade has the End Stage Condition, their player may elect to experience an extinction burst. Instead of dying at the end of the current chapter, the character dies at the end of the current scene. Even the catharsis of destroying a Conviction Touchstone cannot reverse End Stage once it reaches this point. Choose one of the following, or suggest a new one with Storyteller permission:

                                            • Anomaly: The Deviant becomes the center point of an anomaly. The area and everyone in it might be caught in a time loop, for example, or everyone is frozen in place as the Remade’s telepathic control arrests their control over their muscles. The exact radius and duration of this effect is up to the Storyteller, but it typically affects no less than a city block and ends no sooner than the end of the current chapter.

                                            • Crystalize: The Broken’s powers manifest as an object, likely one of occult power. Troupes with access to The Clades Companion may build this as a prototype.

                                            • Divergence Event: The Deviant’s soul sends out an occult shriek that cracks the souls of all nearby Baselines. Most die or suffer Scars with no Variations, but some Diverge. The exact radius of this event is up to the Storyteller, but it shouldn’t be less than a city block.

                                            • Explosion: The power within the Broken explodes out of him with enough force to topple buildings. Baselines at ground zero are killed instantly. The exact radius of this explosion is up to the Storyteller, but it shouldn’t be less than a city block.

                                            • Miracle: Instead of exploding in a wash of terrifying or destructive force, the Deviant’s soul flows out in restorative ways. It might reverse the last few minutes, undoing the damage done by an accident or natural disaster, for example, or restore all the dead in the area to life. The exact radius and limits of this are up to the Storyteller, but it shouldn’t be less than a city block.

                                            • Occult Scar: The disintegration of the Remade’s soul leaves a permanent mark on the area. It might become haunted, for example, or gravity might not work as expected. The exact radius of this is up to the Storyteller, but it shouldn’t be less than the size of a small building and could encompass an area measured in city blocks — or even miles, in rare cases.

                                                Today's preview of Shallow Graves comes from Chapter Seven, which explores the formation and dynamics of cohorts. Among the tricks a cohort often has up its sleeve are Techniques, which are "off-label" uses for their Variations. While these can enhance an individual Deviant by granting an equipment bonus or impeding an opponent, they are often used to support or protect other members of the cohort in some way. Below are the basic systems for Techniques, as well as a few of examples from the book.

                                                Access to the appendices is available for free at the link in my signature (with the full text of Shallow Graves accessible to patrons).

                                                Techniques

                                                Humans are a creative lot. Give them a problem, and they will quickly seek out tools in their environment that might solve it. They’ll wield a tool one way, and then another, and then attack the problem with it from a completely different direction until the problem is solved. A tool or technique that serves them faithfully once often finds itself applied to new problems — sometimes in ways for which the tool is not ideal. “When all you have is a hammer, everything looks like a nail,” as the old saying goes.

                                                The Remade are no exception. Once they overcome the initial shock of the Divergence, Deviants experiment with their newfound abilities — whether in a systematic way or only in moments of desperation — and discover ways to use them. The obvious applications (the ones printed in Deviant and its supplements) come naturally, of course, but trial and error yield unobvious ones, too. These crude tools occasionally prove worthless, but enough of these approaches to problem-solving through Variations succeed to encourage the transformed to apply them to new problems.

                                                Over time, if they live long enough, Broken develop favored alternate uses for their abilities. Such applications cease being ad hoc improvisations and develop into formal techniques. Most never pass beyond the transformed who developed them. After all, even the cleverest use of telekinetic powers is useless to anyone who doesn’t possess telekinesis (which is nearly everyone). But some have principles that can be applied to other Variations — and some Remade meet others who share their abilities — which allows one Deviant to teach their techniques to another. Remade who teach their techniques (or who help other Deviants develop techniques appropriate to their own Variations) are quite common among the Devoted, but a few such mentors exist among Renegades.

                                                In Deviant: The Renegades, these novel applications of the Broken’s Variations are represented by Techniques. Technique Basics


                                                No Variation permits the use of every Technique. The player must supply a narrative justification for why a Variation (or Adaptation or Form) allows a given Technique, which must be one the Storyteller finds reasonable. This will usually reflect the Variation’s mechanics as-written, but in some cases the specific manifestation of that Variation for a Deviant can serve as a rationale. Even if a Variation generally justifies a Technique, it will not work in every situation. A quill plucked from the Remade’s Deadly Ichor might work as a crude lockpick, but it will be useless against a magnetic keycard reader.

                                                To use a Technique, the Variation must be active first.

                                                Learning Techniques

                                                • Although Remade benefit from practicing a Technique until they have mastered it, any Deviant with an appropriate Variation can improvise a given Technique. A Deviant can improvise a Technique once per chapter. Adaptations can reset this limit.

                                                • Whenever the Remade increases his Acclimation, he learns one new Technique for free. He may choose this immediately or save it for a time when he needs it, purchasing it mid-scene, if needed.

                                                • The Broken may master additional Techniques by purchasing the Mastered Technique Merit (p. XX).

                                                • Whether gained by raising Acclimation or buying a Merit, a purchased (Mastered) Technique is more potent than an improvised one. However, although many Variations can express the same Technique, when purchasing a Technique, the Deviant must decide which Variation she uses to accomplish it. She must further specify its exact effects. For example, a purchased Technique based on the improvised effect of giving the Remade an equipment bonus on Skill checks must specify not just the Skills it enhances (typically one or two such Skills) but the specific applications of the Skills that benefit. It’s as narrow as a Specialty — useful when it applies but by no means applying to all situations involving its Skill.

                                                Technique Effects

                                                Most Techniques provide one of the following benefits:

                                                • If using a Variation to perform a mundane action, either replace the normal Attribute + Skill roll with a Scar Finesse roll or treat the Variation as equipment with a bonus equal to the lower of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude — chosen when invoking the Technique. This equipment lasts until the end of the scene and does not stack with the Variation’s normal bonus to rolls/resistance. Mastered: The Scar Finesse roll enjoys a +2 bonus, or the equipment bonus is instead the higher of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude.

                                                • If improving a static trait (like Defense, Speed, or armor), increase the trait by the lower of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude. This improvement requires an action and lasts until the beginning of your next turn. Mastered: This is instead the higher of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude.

                                                • If using a Variation to set up or aid an ally in the scene, add dice equal to the lower of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude to the ally’s roll, or make a Scar Finesse roll and add your successes as dice to their roll (as a teamwork action) — chosen when invoking the Technique. This affects a single roll. Mastered: The Scar Finesse roll enjoys a +2 bonus, or the bonus is instead the higher of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude.

                                                • If using a Variation to create a melee weapon, it has a damage bonus equal to half the lower of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude (rounded up), minus 1. The weapon is improvised and suffers a –1 penalty to attacks. Producing the weapon requires an action, but it lasts until the end of the scene. Mastered: The weapon does not suffer a penalty to attacks, and the damage bonus is instead half the greater of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude (rounded up), minus 1.

                                                • If imposing a penalty to an unwilling character’s mundane action, it requires a Scar Finesse roll as though it were a Directed effect, the penalty it imposes is equal to the lower of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude, and it lasts for a single roll. This requires the Deviant’s action for the turn and does not stack with the Variation’s normal penalty to rolls/resistance. Mastered: This penalty is instead the higher of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude.

                                                • If worsening another person’s static trait (like Defense, Speed, or armor), it requires a Scar Finesse roll as though it were a Directed effect. If successful, reduce the trait by the lower of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude. This requires an action and lasts until the beginning of your next turn. Mastered: The reduction is instead the higher of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude.

                                                • A Variation can grant an ally a Magnitude 1 Variation for a turn and without requiring the ally to use an action to activate it. This must be one the Variation could potentially impart. Using Holographic Projection to hide an ally (Camouflage) makes sense but using it to impart Aquatic does not. Mastered: The temporary Variation can be up to Magnitude 2.

                                                Example Techniques

                                                The following are examples of Techniques Deviants are likely to have. Each lists likely Variations that could be used to produce the effect, but these are by no means exhaustive. Each also provides the effect of mastering it, rather than improvising it. Some follow the guidelines above, while others use mechanics unique to them.

                                                Bodyguard

                                                Sample Variations: Carapace, Extra Appendages, Predator’s Cunning, Precognition

                                                As an action, the Remade blocks, deflects, or otherwise turns aside attacks on the ally against which the bodyguard would enjoy her Defense. This can also represent Variations that allow the bodyguard to give the ally advance warning of danger or to move him out of harm’s way. This increases the ally’s Defense by the lower of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude until the Broken’s next turn.

                                                Mastered: Use the higher of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude, instead.

                                                Clear Threat

                                                Sample Variations: Gigantic, Lash, Matter Emulation, Shadow Selves

                                                By making himself an obvious threat, the Remade draws enemy fire to himself, potentially sparing less battle-ready members of his cohort. As a reflexive action after taking an opponent out of the fight in a turn, make a Scar Finesse roll. If successful, a number of Baselines in the scene equal to the lower of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude — or a single non-Baseline opponent — concentrate their attacks on the Broken next turn. They remain focused on him until he is no longer a threat or until he fails to take one of their number out of the fight on his turn.

                                                This effect is cumulative with successive turns. As long as the Deviant continues eliminating opponents each turn, he attracts the attention of more and more of the remaining enemies.

                                                Mastered: Use the higher of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude, instead.

                                                Decoy

                                                Sample Variations: Beast Mastery, Bioluminescence, Pyrokinesis, Storm-Caller

                                                The Deviant uses Variations to attract attention to herself. If she remains in the scene, allies enjoy a bonus equal to the lower of her Scar Power or Variation Magnitude to their Stealth rolls and other actions to operate unnoticed for as long as the distraction lasts.

                                                She may instead provoke enemies into giving chase. For each success she achieves on a Scar Finesse roll, she draws away four Baseline (or one non-Baseline) pursuers. This can be resolved following normal chase rules (Deviant, p. XX), with the pursuit ending either when the Remade gives the hunters the slip or they catch up with her. Each turn of a chase is 30 seconds to one minute, which can give allies a clear window to overcome the remaining guards, plant the explosives, or otherwise exploit their numeric advantage.

                                                Mastered: If remaining in the scene, use the higher of Scar Power or Variation Magnitude, instead. If fleeing, each success draws away twice as many people.
