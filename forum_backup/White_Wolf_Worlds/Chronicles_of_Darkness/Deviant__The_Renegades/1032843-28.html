        For the "low-powered" thing, I wonder also if it has to do with the lack of "inherent" drawbacks that an average Remade (if there is such a thing) has. Vampires have obvious weaknesses and restrictions- you can't go out in daytime, fire deals aggravated damage, plus any specific banes you've got. Werewolves are harmed by silver, and have a physiological and spiritual need to hunt that forces them into certain behaviors. Demons can't use their powers without risking the attentions of the God-Machine. Even Mages, once you strip their spells away, are "only human."

        A Remade might be "human, but can occasionally look through a wall" or "literally a god in a human-shaped mask." So they operate on a sliding scale, one that can equal a mortals among mortals game, a minor superpowers vampires game, or a world-shredding mage game. And each step comes with increasing drawbacks to balance the scales. A low-level Deviant can't destroy a city, but they also can live a mostly normal life. And since each one is unique, the players get to design their own "inherent drawbacks" that fit the theme of their character.

                            A few questions today. I'll see if I can answer a few.

                            Renegades are not naturally equipped to form complex societies. Deviants don't have a universal aura that lets them readily identify each other, nor a special connection via something like Pyros. Unseen Sense (Deviants) can help in meeting other Deviants, as can setting your sights on the same target of vengeance (conspiracies tend to make multiple Renegades their enemies). I'm open to the possibility of larger organizations of Renegades, but I haven't come up with a good justification, yet.

                            Conspiracies in the Web of Pain aren't all good chums. Some of them oppose each other violently, and others don't have compatible interests. But yes, they do tend to be interconnected, which means Devoted are more likely to have contact with other Devoted.

                            Acclimation starts at 0 but can be increased as high as 5 during play. As with other power stats, this carries a pretty high Experiences cost. In addition, you can only increase Acclimation when your Instability is under control. Acclimation feeds into Stability, Clash of Wills, Supernatural Tolerance, activating Controlled Variations, targeting Directed Variations (and certain other Variations), and resisting Involuntary Variations. So, not quite as multifunctional as other power stats, but still pretty useful.

                            Regarding low-powered-ness, it's sort of the reverse of what Gellydog mentions. Deviants don't get a bunch of inherent advantages by virtue of being Remade - no Mage Sight, undead resistance to bullets, spending fuel to heal or rise from the dead, etc. At low Threat Level, they're just a little more potent than Blue Book mortals. At mid-to-high Threat Level, they can possess specific capabilities that are beyond what a newly created character in most other gamelines can boast. But even at a high initial Threat Level and with a good ration of Experiences, they're never going to rival the reality-shredding might of an experienced mage or demon. Nor can they easily load up on dozens of dots of Disciplines/Gifts/Contracts/Haunts/etc., because Variations come with new Scars. Additionally, there is no "fuel stat," which means Broken never get to the point where they have virtually unlimited "MP" with which to power a stream of their favorite powers. Those Scars that only carry a price when you activate one of the entangled Variations (as opposed to ones that mark you even when the Variations are inactive) burn away terribly finite resources like Willpower, health, or Stability.

                            It's true that in cases where Instability gets wildly out of control and causes the Deviant to destabilize completely, the Remade's Variations can temporarily exceed their normal limits, but this is to a demon Going Loud what a guy with a suicide vest is to a Marine lobbing a grenade at an enemy position. Going Loud is a potent weapon, deployed voluntarily, if as something of a last resort due to its risks and costs. The onset of the End Stage Condition isn't something a Deviant can carefully time to maximize damage, however. It merely makes the Broken a ticking time bomb that might yet achieve one last act before he explodes. Most of the time, the explosion happens some place where it hurts lots of innocent bystanders, instead, serving only to reinforce the game's revenge tragedy themes.

                                    07-24-2019, 10:47 AM
                                    Originally posted by Minty View Post
                                    This might not be something you can talk about at present, but I've been wondering something about entangling Variations with Scars. Let's say, for example, that you have three Magnitudes of Rapid Healing entangled with a Scar, and that Scar cannot increase in severity. If you want to increase Rapid Healing to four Magnitudes would you be able to entangle it with a different Scar, or would you be stuck at three? Or can all Scars handle up to five Magnitudes of a Variation?
                                    Great question! Worsening a Scar happens by taking on medium or major Instability in the last five boxes of your Stability track. Assuming the entangled Scar has a 4+ Magnitude version (most do, but not all), you can choose to increase the Magnitude of Rapid Healing when Instability causes its Scar to increase in Magnitude. If you do that, then the Magnitude increase of that Scar is permanent (normally, increases to a Scar's Magnitude due to Instability are temporary, fading when the Instability heals).

                                    And if the Scar doesn't normally go to Magnitude 4? In that case, there are Deviations, which are add-ons that increase (or, rarely, decrease) the effective Magnitude of a Scar (or Variation). Partially, Deviations allow you to customize or tweak a Scar so that it is more in keeping with your character concept, or to convey an effect that just isn't scalable enough to work as a standalone Scar. But they also exist to deal with the very question you're asking.

                                                        @Gellydog: No harm. Just wanted to clarify.

                                                        Stupid Loserman: CofD core has that language, yes, so I might yet get overruled on that basis (we haven't hit editing, yet), but whether or not it gets called Unseen Sense or not, "Deviants Detecting Deviants" is likely to be a low-cost Merit, rather than a Variation or universal ability.

                                                        (Personally, I've always considered that rule less about "but a vampire with Unseen Sense (God-Machine) would be too powerful!" than about "once you're a vampire, 95% of Supernatural Merits carry a huge cost relative to what you could get by purchasing Disciplines, so here's an easy excuse to refund all that wasted XP." And that differential is even more obvious for, say, mages, where 4 XP gets you the equivalent of several 3-5 dot Supernatural Merits. That gap is much smaller for the Remade. Deviants aren't likely to invest in the Telekinesis Merit when they could have its considerably more potent Variation version, but something like 1 XP for an Unseen Sense as a crude Deviant detector? Seems like a reasonable investment to me.)
