        Originally posted by yrtalien View Post
        If you have a Mag 3 scar and a Major Instability that you choose to downgrade by taking a scar... Must it be a new Mag 1 scar or can I raise my Mag 3 to 4 and follow that up by raising my variation?
        You need to take a new, unentangled Scar (which you can then immediately buy a Variation for, if you want).

        For that matter when I buy a new Variation must I have an already unentangled scar to pair it with or can you just choose to have one at any level your willing to stomach the cost for, for free when you make the variation purchase?
        You need to have an unentangled Scar beforehand, or at least a Scar capable of entangling your new Variation.

        If I'm really focused on raising some scar in order to increase its variation can I choose to turn a success into a dramatic failure so I can get instability, so I can buy it off, so I can raise my scar and variance...?
        It's allowed by the rules as written, and I see no issue with it.

                Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
                Does that mean activating the “once per chapter “ effect would let me retain the skill until I changed it?
                No. The Perpetual effects are the Magnitude 1/5 effects and that the Variation is always "on" for the purposes of answering whether you can use the per-chapter effects of the intermediate dots, which still last for one scene each.

                ​
                Originally posted by BrilliantRain View Post
                Is there something I’m missing? Are Involuntary Scars so terrible that something like this is needed to get people to use them? Is there a major benefit to Permanent Scars that doing things this way doesn’t grant?
                Involuntary Scars are necessary for a Variation to have the Involuntary activation method, both of the core Involuntary Scars make all their attached Variations conditionally Overt if their own Magnitude goes over 1, both of the core Involuntary Scars have some form of cap on their own Magnitude before applying Deviations, and the Deviation literally cannot result in a Scar with a Magnitude of less than 3 before applying other Standard Deviations.

                The way Indelible Mark works, either you have a Persistent Scar with mechanical teeth that's active whenever its Variations are (or always, with Continuous, and unlike Short Circuit it's active even when you're using the power deliberately) without the benefits of a Persistent Variation, or all the entangled Variations are Overt, you have less control over the power, and (unless you make the secondary Scar Continuous) you don't get the opportunity to earn Beats from the Persistent Scar unless you're using the now-attention-grabbing powers you have less of a leash on.

                            Originally posted by Dataweaver;

                            Could you give some examples of what you mean?

                            Sure, and if you are one the authors - please do not take this personally, the overall work is still the level of quality one can expect from Onyx Path. Just not doing it for me right now.

                            If I take the section on the Transmissible form for example (won't post it here as I'm not sure that would be ok, so I'll paraphrase):

                            Some Divergences are transmissible. Most pass through diseases. Some pass through Variations or scars. Some pass through mystical means (which could be any of several options). Some affect one Variation, other affect more.

                            In most cases they're as virulent as real diseases. In some cases, more. In other cases, barely at all.

                            In some cases the Deviant is not always contagious. It can depend on any factor. It may also have any and all kind of counter.

                            Most have an onset period. For some it's very long. For others, not at all.

                            The symptoms vary. They can look like normal diseases. They can look very bad. Or not at all.

                            The victim can fight back. The means vary. Usually if they survive they are immune, but no always.

                            If the victim fails they suffer the Divergence. In a few days or weeks. Sometimes seconds. Sometimes months.

                            Do you see what I mean? This section teaches me nothing. It's a repetition of "sometimes in the middle, sometimes more, sometimes less." There is nothing specific to the setting. It's just an elaborate way of presenting anything and everything about options. If you take Vampire for example, the properties of the Blood are key to the setting and clearly explained, with some wiggle room for stories to be told around it. Here, if someone asked me of the Divergence was contagious, I'd have to reply "Maybe. Sometimes. I don't know?"

                            I get that flexibility was the goal. But I fear here the game has got so flexible, it's lacking any unifying core.

                                                        The character creation part is done, and I need a break before I create the scenario, do I'm going to try tackling a few questions here.

                                                        Originally posted by lnodiv View Post
                                                        Revisiting Telekinesis since that's what my upcoming character in a post-Kickstarter pre-release game is going to primarily use:

                                                        If 'grappling' multiple people at once with Telekinesis, is the Deviant allowed to use a maneuver (damage, disarm, etc) on both of them on successful rolls? The answer seems to be clearly yes based on extrapolation from the earlier Cephalist Joe example, but I want to make sure I truly understand.

                                                        Also, to resurface an earlier question - does Persistent Drawback have to inherit the Attribute Category of the linked Scar? It doesn't say that it does, but 90% of the examples do. Maybe that's just because, in 90% of those cases, it made sense?
                                                        That's a good question for which I don't have an immediate answer. Force fields blocking Directed Variations is a double-edged sword. It protects you from being targeted by others, but it also prevents you from targeting them back. I kinda want it to be an either/or the player determines when buying the Variation. Physical attacks like bullets and fists (and Lash, which deals damage and so could still get through) would still be blocked either way.

                                                        As-written, Persistent Drawback allows you to choose the Attribute category. I'd considered changing it, but a couple comments after yours indicate that maybe it doesn't need changing?

                                                        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                        A handful of error-spottings for the collection, in a theme:

                                                        The Chimeric example of Electrokinesis is mechanically illegal in the previewed text, having been entangled with a Scar that explicitly cannot entangle any Directed Variations.

                                                        Creeping Dread and Shadows of the Past say they're Directed in the Variation writeups but not in the Quick Reference chart.

                                                        Odd one out spotted while checking Directed Variations: Pheromones is missing a Perpetual or Toggled keyword.
                                                        Fixing in the working draft.

                                                        Originally posted by Satchel View Post

                                                        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                        ​Involuntary Scars are necessary for a Variation to have the Involuntary activation method, both of the core Involuntary Scars make all their attached Variations conditionally Overt if their own Magnitude goes over 1, both of the core Involuntary Scars have some form of cap on their own Magnitude before applying Deviations, and the Deviation literally cannot result in a Scar with a Magnitude of less than 3 before applying other Standard Deviations.

                                                        That is indeed the intention of Indelible Mark and Short Circuit. More than anything, they're meant to reflect a trope wherein not only does the character's power manifest when they don't want it to, but it carries an additional downside for the hero personally. Bruce Banner doesn't just have Alternate Persona. He has Involuntary Stimulus + Indelible Mark (Alternate Persona).

                                                        Originally posted by yrtalien View Post
                                                        If I'm really focused on raising some scar in order to increase its variation can I choose to turn a success into a dramatic failure so I can get instability, so I can buy it off, so I can raise my scar and variance...?
                                                        Instability is an intentional Scylla and Charybdis. To increase Acclimation, you need to clear all the medium and major Instability, first, which makes it impossible (albeit temporarily) to get new Variations (unless you already have some Scars that are either unentangled or have extra "capacity"). To gain new Variations, you need new Scars (same caveats), which means you need some medium and/or major Instability to "feed" for those Scars, making it impossible (albeit temporarily) to increase your Acclimation.

                                                        I will warn that buying a bunch more Variations can become a bit of a trap, since it means more Scars, and more Scars mean more situations that f*** with you (or allow others to do so), which can lead to your demise without the End Stage Condition ever coming into play.

                                                        RE: Relentless Variation: Still pondering this one. It will likely get split into two things - a Scar for what Invariable does now, and a Persistent-Only Deviation that emulates Suppressible. But I've been working on a scenario to run as a short demo the last couple days, so I haven't had much time to think about it.
