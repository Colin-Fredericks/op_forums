                                Now, I'm largely familiar with 1e, and I don't know how often Aspirations and such are supposed to come up,
                                Forget how often they're supposed to come up for a moment: a normal player character (from the bluebook or the other gamelines) has three and a Deviant character gets one.

                                but my concern is that the per-story Instability buildup will push my players to interact with their Touchstones more because of the mounting penalties than anything else.
                                Vampire 2e's means of dealing with frenzy is that a successful roll gives you a Condition that applies a mounting penalty to subsequent rolls to resist frenzy until you fall to frenzy, kill somebody, or have a meaningful interaction with one of your Touchstones. Killing people still risks your Humanity at most levels and frenzy often escalates situations in a way that threatens similar outcomes. Losing your last Touchstone has you either scrambling for a month to find a replacement or immediately entering the sleep of the dead for weeks at minimum.

                                Changeling 2e has spending a scene interacting with one of your Touchstones as the only way to heal Clarity damage besides resolving Clarity Conditions and that track can start as low as two boxes before you go Comatose, a state of affairs which is both exactly what it sounds like and the opposite of a desirable outcome to characters struggling with their perceptions and the possibility of extradimensional re-abduction. Losing your last Touchstone heightens the risk that your character will take Clarity damage just from dreaming of their time in Arcadia and immediately inflicts them with an irrational delusion until they manage to set up a replacement relationship, on top of removing most means of healing Clarity damage beyond the lower bounds of the Trait.

                                Both of those games also have the mounting disconnect your character has with other people and/or reality as the main concern their respective stats hold at bay. This kind of upkeep is not new.

                                Deviant is built around characters whose self-concept is sufficiently fucked that they need their Touchstones to hold themselves together. You start out with multiple Touchstones for free where other gamelines have that as an extra thing you need to take a Merit for, and the game's failure states specifically include "you've completely disconnected from all your Touchstones" due to the way Loyalty and Conviction work.

                                If your players aren't invested in their characters' Touchstones then that is a different problem than their still needing to engage with them for the maintenance purposes that the mechanics provide.

                                    Minty: The relevant section got left out accidentally because I cut the example text for the blog (we try to keep them under 2k), and it was sandwiched between two examples. Instability upgrades just like Health damage does. Sorry for the confusion. And yes, it's no accident that losing a Loyalty Touchstone tends to seriously f' up a Deviant (and give her ready access to greater power to use against you right after you seriously pissed her off); there's a reason they tend to be hyper-protective of them.

                                    The MG, Satchel, and Leliel: I want to see some further playtests of Stability to make sure it's a real and constant threat, rather than a paper tiger or a completely fun-destroying PITA. We did a fair amount of tinkering on it after pre-KS playtests (Scar Resistance rolls were once further penalized by the Magnitude of the Scar ), and I'll continue tinkering with this system based on post-KS feedback and playtests until it works as intended. A couple data points that might be of interest:

                                    - Approximately 1 in 3 of Scars use Scar Resistance rolls, so the penalty to these doesn't affect every Deviant.
                                    - The Stabilizer Merit the blog mentions curbs Instability penalties on a 1-for-1 basis, making it a key purchase for Remade with a combination of a high-Magnitude Variation, low Acclimation, and Scars that call for Scar Resistance rolls.
                                    - The goal is for minor Instability to be pretty much a constant for Broken with high-Magnitude Variations, although it's fairly easy to clear with Touchstone work. Medium Instability is much less common but requires serious effort to remove - either taking a significant personal risk for the sake of a Loyalty Touchstone or permanently accepting a new Scar. Major Instability is rare and serious, and the only ways to deal with it are to accept a new Scar (which merely downgrades it) or to kill a Conviction Touchstone (which is an ugly solution to one's personal problems).

                                    I appreciate the sincere feedback offered in good faith. I always want to make the best game I can, after all!

                                                Originally posted by Deinos View Post
                                                Are Guardians/Nemeses supposed to have 2 Conviction, 2 Loyalty or max of the opposite kind (Loyalty for renegades, Conviction for devoted)?
                                                A Guardian has more Loyalty than Conviction, and a Nemesis has more Conviction than Loyalty. The sum of Conviction and Loyalty cannot exceed 5 (a Merit exists that slightly increases this). There will be more detail once we get into the KS.

                                                Originally posted by The MG View Post
                                                Alright, last note on the topic: do you have any examples of scenes and interactions that could count for Instability recovery (particularly via Conviction Touchstones)?
                                                Fulfilling Conviction is actually pretty easy. Let's say you go to the public records office and find blueprints of the conspiracy building where your Conviction Touchstone works. That's sufficient. Tailing one of her associates so that you can rough him up and convince him to cough up information that you can use against her? Also fine. Face-to-face confrontations certainly count, but the key question is whether you're taking tangible steps on the path to destroying your sworn enemies. If you are, you heal a minor Instability.
