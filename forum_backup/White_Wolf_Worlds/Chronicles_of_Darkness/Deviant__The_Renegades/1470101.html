    Making Deviant Less Renagade (Rules Hack)

    12-09-2021, 10:20 PM
    Integrated into the Variation/Scar infrastructure of Deviant: The Renegades are systems like Touchstones and Instability, which serve to enforce the intended themes and tone of the game. In my experience, they do this job well. However, the integration of these mechanics poses a problem for Chronicles that use Variations and Scars but focus on different themes. Say, for example, you want characters with minor supernatural powers and interesting limitations but that aren't also monomaniacal rebels against systemic conspiracies. My goal with the following hackery is to smooth over the mechanical hiccups that result from dropping the more "Renegade" mechanics in Deviant, hopefully resulting in a more generic and streamlined character system.

    To summarize the changes: There's no dividing line between "Deviant" and "Baseline", instead all characters use standard mortal traits found in the Chronicles of Darkness core book. Theoretically any character may acquire Scars (and hence Variations) either during character creation or from new Conditions relating to Integrity breaking points. Integrity covers most of the mechanical roles of Stability. Deviant-specific mechanics like Touchstones and Origins are dropped.

    By no means am I suggesting these hacks are a "better" way to play Deviant. But they should lightened the load of thematic and mechanical baggage if such things aren't desired. Feedback is appreciate, particularly if I've grossly unbalanced something or missed a mechanical interaction. However, I am aware that these changes will have a thematic impact, compared to standard Deviant rules, on characters and Chronicles that use them. That is, indeed, kind of the point.

    Rule Changes
    Follow the character creation steps in the Chronicles of Darkness core, rather than in Deviant: The Renegades. During step 6 (Merits), players may spend merit dots to begin play with Variations* (and Scars). The Storyteller may choose to increase the number of starting merit dots as suits the Chronicle.

    *This does modify the Variation economy during character creation significantly, but also brings it closer to how Variations are acquired in play. In a group where every character is going start with Variations, it's easy enough to use a Threat Level instead. But having Variations cost merit dots means characters with Variations are nominally balanced against those who do not. Furthermore, you can easily increase the power level of the Chronicle by increasing everyone's starting merit dots.

    Scars, Variations and their related mechanics follow all the rules in the Deviant: The Renegades, except as specified below.

    Origin
    Characters do not choose an Origin or receive a Scar-free Variation dot.

    Clade
    Characters do not choose a Clade or receive Adaptations at character creation. Adaptations may be purchased individually for three Experiences, and a character may have a maximum number of Adaptations equal to half their Acclimation rating (rounded down) plus one.

    Variations
    Variations may be acquired at character creation by spending four merit dots per Variation (which can be of any Magnitude). A character’s Variations may be distributed in any combination between Universal and Clade categories.
    Variations may be acquired in play by resolving the Unstable or Scarred Conditions.

    Scars
    Variations must be entangled with Scars as normal. A player defines his character’s initial Scar(s) as he sees fit (and as required to entangle any Variations), but a character who begins play with at least one Scar starts with an Integrity of 6 (instead of 7).
    During character creation, a character may have Scars whose Magnitude could potentially entangle additional Variations, subject to the usual limits. This excess capacity may be used to entangle new Variations during play (by spending four experiences). It may only be used to increase the Magnitude of an existing, entangled Variation during a scene in which the character suffered a breaking point, however.
    Scars can be affected or gained by the new Unstable and Scarred Conditions.
    Scar Resistance rolls are modified based on the character's Integrity, using the same modifiers as breaking points (see Chronicles of Darkness page 74). Additionally, Scar Resistance rolls are penalized by one-half the Magnitude of the highest-Magnitude Variation entangled with the Scar (rounded up).

    Forms
    Characters may possess forms, determined when a character first acquires a Scar, though not all forms have applicable mechanical effects (such as Self-Made and the Devoted).

    Loyalty and Conviction
    Characters do not have dots of Conviction or Loyalty and do not possess Conviction or Loyalty Touchstones. When a Scar has rules that affect the character’s behavior toward either type of Touchstone, the player instead designates a number of individuals equal to Scar Resistance. When a Scar affects the Willpower regained by interacting with Touchstones, it affects the character’s Virtue and/or Vice instead.

    Acclimation
    Acclimation can be purchased as normal (though a character must not currently have the Unstable or Scarred Conditions). Instead of increasing the character’s Instability boxes, it is added as a bonus to all breaking points triggered by supernatural encounters.
    Characters without Scars or Variations can still purchase dots of Acclimation, but many benefits will not be applicable unless the character later acquires Scars or Variations.

    Merits
    Characters may purchase merits from both the Chronicles of Darkness core and Deviant: The Renegades (though the Storyteller may disallow Supernatural Merits in favor of Variations).
    A character is considered to be a Deviant for the purposes of merit prerequisites if she has at least one permanent Scar.

    Stability
    Characters do not have an Stability track. When a character would acquire an Instability, he instead suffers a breaking point (at -2 for medium Instabilities, and -4 for major Instabilities, in addition to situational modifiers).
    Scar Resistance rolls are instead affected by Integrity (see above).
    Whenever a character would heal an Instability (such as an exceptional success on a Scar Finesse roll), they instead gain the Inspired Condition.
    Conditions that prevent Instability from healing instead apply a -2 penalty to breaking points.
    Conditions resolved by healing Instability instead resolve when succeeding at a breaking point or gaining a point of Integrity.
    Characters who possess at least one Scar add the Unstable Condition to the list of possible Conditions acquired by failing or succeeding on a breaking point. On a dramatic failure resulting from a supernatural encounter, any character may acquire the Scarred Condition in place of the usual options. At the Storyteller’s option, a character with at least one Scar that dramatically fails a breaking point while at Integrity 1 may gain the End Stage Condition, which can be resolved by gaining a point of Integrity.

    New Conditions

    Unstable
    A brush with otherworldly forces or psychological distress is aggravating the wounds in your character’s soul. When under the effects of this Condition, either develop a new unentangled Scar with Magnitude 1 or increase the Magnitude of an existing Scar by 1. At any point you may make this Scar or Magnitude increase permanent in exchange for purchasing a new Variation entangled with the Scar (by spending four Experiences) or to increase the Magnitude of one or more Variations already entangled with the Scar (at no cost in Experiences). Doing so resolves the Condition.
    In order to develop a new Controlled or Involuntary Scar, you must immediately purchase a new Variation to entangle with it.
    Multiple instances of this condition may stack.
    Possible Sources: A breaking point for a character with at least one Scar.
    Resolution: Resolve this condition at the end of a scene during which the new or worsened Scar causes significant difficulty for your character or her allies; make the new Scar or Magnitude increase permanent as described above.

    Scarred (Persistent)
    Something your character has experienced or witnessed has opened cracks in his soul. While under the effects of this Condition, he develops a new unentangled Scar, following the rules for unentangled Scar Magnitude on page 112 of Deviant: The Renegades. At any point, you may make this new Scar permanent in exchange for purchasing a new Variation to entangle with the Scar (by spending four Experiences). Doing so resolves the Condition.
    In order to develop a new Controlled or Involuntary Scar, you must immediately purchase a new Variation to entangle with it.
    Scars (and Variations) developed from this condition should relate in some way to the cause of the breaking point.
    Possible Sources: A dramatic failure on a breaking point caused by an encounter with the supernatural.
    Beat: Whenever the Scar causes difficulty, as normal.
    Resolution: Gain or lose a dot of Integrity; achieve an exceptional success on a breaking point; make the Scar permanent as described above.

                  I’m considering a further modification: rather than every Variation costing a flat 4 Experiences, instead price them as merits with a dot rating equal to Variation Magnitude. Increasing a Variations’s Magnitude would then cost the difference in dot rating, rather than being free.

                  The risk would be an over proliferation of cheap Variations with slap-on-the-wrist-but-still-beat-generating Scars. Keeping Scar acquisition tied to breaking points would limit that somewhat, but I’m half tempted to drop even that, and just have Scars be part of the package of acquiring a Variation. If nothing else, it’s more mechanically intuitive. (Breaking points being able to temporarily add/worsen scars is definitely something I’m keeping, it just might not be how you permanently change Scars/Variations. Unless it’s the character’s first Scar/Variation, I’d still want that to be tied to a dramatic failure and a loss of Integrity.)

                  Basically, I want players to be able to fill out a character with thematically appropriate abilities without feeling like they’re not getting the full value for their xp if they leave variations at low magnitude.

                  If I do make these changes, the end result would really be what my hacks were already leaning towards: Variations and Scars as a framework for creating custom Supernatural Merits.
