    [Black Vans] pseudo-templates for Fan-made gamelines

    02-11-2026, 04:31 PM
    With the release of Black Vans, we now have the tools to simulate, with a great deal of accuracy, other Chronicles of Darkness supernaturals using Deviant as a baseline. This is done by providing a set of Greater, Lesser, and Scar Forms designed to represent different aspects of the protagonists of a given gameline. For example, a vampire can be simulated in Deviant by the following set of Forms:
    • Greater Form: Vampire. Replaces Acclimation with Blood Potency, and Loyalty/Conviction and Instability with Mask, Dirge, and Humanity, and adds Vitae as a fuel trait. Each of the Disciplines is summarized in terms of which Variations embody it.
    • Lesser Form: Clan. Lets the player choose one of the five Clans, and specifies what Disciplines each Clan favors.
    • Lesser Form: Covenant. Gives the vampire a Mentor and Status with one Covenant, and access to one Covenant-specific "Discipline": Kindred Law for the Carthian Movement, Cruac for the Circle of the Crone, Blood Oaths for the Invictus, Theban Sorcery for the Lancea et Sanctum, and Coils of the Dragon for the Ordo Dracul.
    • Lesser Form: Properties of the Blood. Models Blood Bonds, Physical Intensity, and Fangs using Deviant Variations.
    • Lesser Form: Tricks of the Damned. Models Dead Physiology, Heightened Senses, and the Lost Visage.
    • Scar Form: the Curse. Shows how to model Hemovore, Daysleep, Fire and Sunlight, and Frenzy as Scars.
    The Lesser and Scar Forms are designed to be as independent as possible from each other, and it's stated that a given character doesn't have to take all of them; but the more he takes, the more like a Kindred he is.

    As demonstrated in the summary of the Vampire Forms, the Greater Form for a given supernatural type describes the overall dynamics of the type's intended playstyle: whatever power stat, fuel stat, Morality substitute, and Anchors it uses. The Lesser Forms almost universally include one summarizing the game's x-splats, one summarizing the game's y-stats, and at least one summarizing essential differences between that type and ordinary humans. Supernaturals with significant downsides (like vampires and Prometheans) get a Scar Form summarizing those downsides; and each supernatural type gets a number of Lesser Forms based on how powerful a typical member of that type tends to be — that is, what its Threat Level in Deviant would be. So vampires, having four Lesser Forms and one Scar Form, would typically be considered Threat level 3. The number of Lesser Forms range from one (for Hunters) to six (for Mages and Demons).

    I mention this because the book doesn't have any pseudo-spats for player-written supernatural types, for obvious reasons. So how would you go about writing up Greater, Lesser, and possibly Scar Forms for some of the more popular "fansplats" like Genius, Leviathan, or Princess? That's what I want this thread to be about: using the monster Forms from Black Vans as a guide, devise monster Forms for your favorite fansplat.
    Note that this isn't expected to precisely duplicate said monster under Deviant's rules; just provide a rough approximation of it.

      GENIUS
      Geniuses are individuals who have lost their minds in the pursuit of Science, organizing themselves into societies called Foundations that pursue different scientific fields.
      • Threat Level: ?
      • Themes: Transgression (where a little knowledge is a hideous thing that will drive you mad), Loss of Control, and the Power of Ideas.
      • Allies: Befriending a Genius is an excellent way for a Deviant to equip themselves with Prototypes. Furthermore, Geniuses can provide avenues for getting around Conspiracies, as they are often capable of performing feats that are normally restricted to them.
      • Enemies: A Genius is effectively a one-person Conspiracy. Notably, a Genius can cause Divergence, which is a major source of concern for the newly Remade.
      • Web of Pain: Genius society includes organizations known as Fellowships that assist Geniuses in making Wonders. Conspiracies in the Web of Pain can serve as Fellowships, but availing oneself of their scientific insights is risky and tends to be a Transgression. On the flip side, a Fellowship can easily find itself causing Divergences that produce Manticores or Broken; sometimes it can be difficult to distinguish between a Fellowship and a Conspiracy, and there may be no difference at all.


      [hr]

      GREATER FORM: GENIUS
      Geniuses have a Supernatural Potency called Inspiration, an Integrity trait called Enlightenment, and a Fuel trait called Mania. They do not have Variations of their own; instead, they incorporate Variations into items, animals, or people using their Axioms to create Prototypes, Manticores, or Deviants. This ability to wield Axioms is available to anyone with any Genius Form, whether it is a Greater or Lesser Form.

      In addition to empowering Divergence Projects and generating Mania, possessing the Greater Form and its Inspiration trait grants the Genius the following specific capabilities and burdens:
      • Immunity to Unfamiliar Skill Penalties: Because their minds are flooded with transcendent knowledge, a Genius never suffers dice penalties for unfamiliar applications of a Skill.
      • Breaking Mortal Limits: A Genius with an Inspiration of 6 or higher transcends the limitations of mind and body. They can use special techniques unavailable to lesser scientists to increase their Attributes and Skills past the standard five-dot maximum, becoming truly posthuman.
      • Resistance to the Supernatural: A Genius's Inspiration helps them fend off metanormal and super-scientific effects. They add their Inspiration directly to other resistance attributes (such as Stamina) when resisting these powers.
      • Deep Inspiration: As a reflexive action, a Genius can reach deep into their psyche to channel temporary Mania points equal to their per-turn Inspiration limit. These points do not add to their total Mania pool and must be spent immediately. Channelling this raw power is dangerous, however, forcing the Genius to make an unmada check with a penalty equal to the number of points channeled through Deep Inspiration during that session.
      • Technobabble (Jabir): Inspiration is both the source of a Genius's brilliance and their madness, making it the dice pool used to determine if they succumb to Jabir. When a Genius attempts to explain mad science (or even mundane technical topics) to a mortal, they risk falling into a maniacal rant and must roll half their Inspiration (rounded up). A success or exceptional success means the Genius begins throwing around phrases like "Fools! I'll destroy them all!", automatically failing whatever social action they were attempting and appearing dangerously crazy to Baseline observers.


      [hr]

      LESSER FORMS
      A character may take any of the following Lesser Forms independently. A character who possesses any Genius Lesser Form gains the ability to incorporate Variations into items, animals, or people using the Axioms they possess.

      Characters without the Greater Form do not have a Mania pool; instead, their creations and abilities are governed by standard per-scene and per-chapter limits, which they can reset using Adaptations. Characters who do possess the Greater Form: Genius may spend 1 point of Mania to reset any of these per-scene or per-chapter features.

      Lesser Form: Catalyst
      Catalysts are the driving emotional forces behind a Genius. A Catalyst provides an Adaptation, one dot in a Favored Axiom, and a Persistent Scar representing the mental cost the character has paid. The character may purchase additional dots in their Favored Axiom at a cost of 4 Experiences each.

      There are eight Catalysts:
      • Banishment (Neid): Driven by spite and rejection, favoring Control. Adaptation: Iron Will. Persistent Scar: Paranoia.
      • Curiosity (Staunen): Driven by an insatiable need to uncover the unknown, favoring Discovery. Adaptation: Obsessive. Persistent Scar: Fixation.
      • Desire (Wollen): Driven by unending greed or the thrill of the chase, favoring Travel. Adaptation: Reservoir. Persistent Scar: Philia.
      • Fear (Furcht): Driven by a desperate need to protect themselves or others from looming threats, favoring Protection. Adaptation: Stubborn Resolve. Persistent Scar: Phobia.
      • Fury (Grimm): Driven by an unquenchable, smoldering rage, favoring Destruction. Adaptation: Consume. Persistent Scar: Murderous Urge.
      • Liberation (Befreit): Driven by a pathological rejection of rules, limits, and authority, favoring Independence. Adaptation: Unpredictable. Persistent Scar: Reckless.
      • Loss (Klagen): Driven by grief, regret, and the need to prevent further tragedy, favoring Restoration. Adaptation: Redundancy. Persistent Scar: Traumatic Memories.
      • Vision (Hoffnung): Driven by a megalomaniacal desire to force the world into a utopian ideal, favoring Transformation. Adaptation: Focus. Persistent Scar: Hubris.


      Catalyst Features (The Inspired Mind):
      Because of the sheer force of their catalyst, the character gains the following capabilities:
      • The Madness Place: Once per chapter, the character may enter a manic fugue state dedicated to a specific goal (e.g., "finish this invention"). While in this state, they ignore wound penalties, fatigue, and environmental distractions, and gain a +1 bonus against mind control or persuasion attempting to dissuade them from their goal.
      • Inspired Deduction: Once per chapter, when investigating a mystery, the character may experience a flash of insight to immediately uncover a Clue with the Inspired tag.


      Lesser Form: Foundation
      Each Foundation defines the core science the character engages in. This Form grants one dot in each of the Foundation's two Favored Axioms, and the character may purchase additional dots in these Axioms at a cost of 4 Experiences each. They also receive a +1 bonus to a specific Skill related to their field of study.
      • Artificers practice Mad Engineering. They favor Independence and Protection, and gain +1 Crafts.
      • Directors practice Mad Psychology. They favor Control and Independence, and gain +1 Persuasion or Subterfuge.
      • Navigators practice Mad Physics. They favor Destruction and Travel, and gain +1 Athletics or Drive.
      • Progenitors practice Mad Biology. They favor Independence and Restoration, and gain +1 Medicine or Science.
      • Scholars practice Mad Philosophy. They favor Discovery and Transformation, and gain +1 Academics or Investigation.


      Foundation Features (Mad Science):
      Because of their scientific training, the character gains the following capabilities:
      • Enhance Output: Once per scene, the character may grant a +1 bonus to either one of their Mental Attributes (Intelligence, Wits, Resolve) OR to the equipment bonus of any mundane piece of technology they are using for one turn.
      • Understand/Dismantle Technology: Once per scene, the character may touch a device to instantly understand its function. Alternatively, they may make a Dexterity + Crafts attack against a machine or Prototype to rapidly dismantle it, dealing lethal damage that ignores Durability.
      • Kitbashing: Once per chapter, the character may rapidly jury-rig a temporary Prototype out of available materials. This Prototype falls apart at the end of the scene or after a single use.


      Lesser Form: Conspiracy of One
      This Form allows an isolated mad scientist to function mechanically as a self-contained proto-conspiracy.
      • Personal Assets: The character gains 2 Magnitude of Prototypes (assigned to a single Axiom), 3 dots of Retainer (Beholden assistants immune to Prototype Scars and mortal penalties), and a 2-dot Safe Place (a Laboratory granting a +2 to +3 equipment bonus and halting unattended Prototype degradation).
      • Institutional Traits: The operation is treated as a Standing 1 conspiracy with Power 1, Finesse 1, Resistance 1, and Association 4. The Laboratory serves as its single Node, which is appropriate for Divergence Projects but imposes a -2 penalty on standard conspiracy actions.
      • Divergence Power: When undertaking a Divergence Project, the character's effective Standing equals their Threat Level (plus half their Inspiration, rounded up, if they possess the Greater Form).
      • Action Economy: The proto-conspiracy generates 0 free conspiracy actions. To execute a conspiracy action, the character must either take an "excess action" (suffering 1 lethal damage to Association) or use their personal Resources Merit to "reverse-engineer" and purchase the equivalent action without institutional damage.
      • Subversion Immunity: Because the operation's infrastructure is built entirely on character-bound Merits, it cannot be targeted by a Subversion Project. Predators must target the character directly (via capture or coercion) to co-opt their operation.


      Conspiracy of One Feature (Unorthodox Power):
      • Power Technology: Once per scene, the character may bypass normal logistical limitations to power a mundane machine, vehicle, or facility without a conventional power source. The duration depends on the size of the object (e.g., a flashlight for an hour, a car for an hour, or a heavy vehicle for 10 minutes).


      [hr]

      FELLOWSHIPS
      Rather than taking a Lesser Form, characters who join a specialized Fellowship map their involvement to the Mystery Cult Initiation Merit. This seamlessly translates a Fellowship's localized resources, peer review, and shared lore into standard Deviant mechanics:
      • 1 Dot (Scholarship / Syllabus 1): The Merit grants a Skill Specialty or a 1-dot Merit (such as Contacts representing the group's network).
      • 2 to 4 Dots (Syllabus 2 to 4): The Merit grants progressively better 1- to 3-dot Merits. This reflects the Fellowship granting dots in the Allies Merit, as well as access to resources like discounted Procedures from the Fellowship's archives.
      • 5 Dots (Syllabus 5): The Merit grants a 3-dot Merit or a major advantage not reflected in game traits. This is the mechanical lever to grant pinnacle Syllabus benefits, such as gaining extra Sparks to spend on Wonders under the Fellowship's purview or installing unique mad science advantages.


      [hr]

      SPECIAL RULES: WORKING WONDERS AND DIVERGENCE
      A Genius is uniquely equipped to manipulate the fabric of reality, funneling their mad science into both monumental creations and portable supernatural gear. In all cases, the Genius must possess the Axioms appropriate to the project in question to incorporate specific Variations.

      Big Projects (Wonder & Divergence Projects)
      When a Genius wants to create a living Manticore, a fully sapient Deviant, or a massive, persistent Prototype (Wonder), they must undertake a "big project"—a downtime Project that explicitly makes something new.

      The core mechanic for tackling these big projects operates as follows:
      • The Genius begins with a Wonder pool of 0 dice and chooses an acceptable Project he'd like to perform.
      • Once per chapter in which the Genius did not attempt to use the Wonder pool, after spending a scene preparing for a Project, the Genius may roll an appropriate dice pool (see Foundation for some examples) and add any successes to the Wonder pool. If he does so he cannot attempt to use the Wonder pool during the current chapter.
      • Once per scene after preparing a test subject or workspace, but during which time he hasn't attempted to fill the Wonder pool, the Genius can roll the Wonder pool as the chosen Project in order to complete it.


      The acceptable Projects that fall under this category include:
      • Acquire Icon (Complexity 3, 5, or 10): The Genius develops, creates, or builds a powerful, persistent Prototype or Wonder that serves the operation as an Icon.
      • Establish Node (Complexity 10): The Genius constructs a new laboratory, autofactory, or other major facility.
      • Breed Manticore (Complexity 1-10): The Genius clones or breeds a new Manticore that is substantially similar to an existing Manticore they possess, acting as a "mold".
      • Create Manticore (Complexity 1-10): The Genius creates an entirely new kind of Manticore. This generally produces a 2-dot Manticore, leaving the creator with limited control over the specific Variations it manifests unless they achieve an exceptional success.
      • Expand Line (Complexity 1-10): The Genius uses a known, stable methodology to mass-produce a new Deviant that possesses substantially similar Variations and Scars as an existing Devoted "mold".
      • Launch Devoted Line (Complexity 1-10): The Genius develops a brand-new strain of Remade using untried methods. This is highly dangerous and unpredictable, granting the creator little control over the resulting Scars and Variations without an exceptional success.

      (Note: Projects such as Leash Devoted, Recruit Devoted, and Reprogram Devoted do not "make something" new, and so are not included in this list.)

      Smaller Projects (Prototypes)
      If a Genius is not creating a living creature or a massive Icon, but is instead building discrete, portable supernatural equipment, they undertake a smaller project. These projects use the standard Build Equipment rules modified for Prototypes as found in Clades Companion.

      Building a Prototype custom-crafts an object that grants a specific Variation but burdens the user with a design flaw (acting as a Scar). Building a Prototype takes a guaranteed toll on the Genius: successfully rolling to create one automatically inflicts a medium Instability on the creator (or a minor Instability on an exceptional success).

      The Axioms of Transgression
      Whether completing a big project or a smaller project, the Genius must possess the correct Axioms. Seven of the Axioms serve as permission to incorporate specific Variations into the resulting Prototype, Manticore, or Deviant. The Storyteller is the final arbiter of which Axiom governs a Variation, but the following are standard lists:
      • Control (Epikrato): Beast Mastery, Body Snatcher, Delude, Dream Invader, Emotion Amplification, Face Thief, Fate-Bind, Holographic Projection, Magnetic Control, Memory Thief, Pheromones, Plant Control, Psycho-Onomastics, Shadow Command, Telekinesis, Telepathy, Vocalization.
      • Destruction (Katastrofi): Aerokinesis, Cryokinesis, Deadly Ichor, Electrokinesis, Gravity Intensification, Hydrokinesis, Inhuman Digestion, Lash, Pyrokinesis, Storm-Caller, Terrakinesis.
      • Discovery (Apokalypsi): Clairvoyance, Computer-Aided Processing, Internet Acuity, Otherworldly Connection, Precognition, Predator's Cunning, Sensor Array, Shadows of the Past, Soul Seer, Specialized Sense, Traumavore.
      • Protection (Prostasia): Carapace, Environmental Adaptation, Fate's Agent, Immunity, Occult Singularity, Refuge.
      • Restoration (Exelixi): Absorb Instability, Anomalous Biology, Healer, Rapid Healing.
      • Transformation (Metaptropi): Animal Transformation, Body Modification, Boneless, Conjuration, Cursed Flesh, Extra Appendages, Gestalt, Gigantic, Hidden Compartments, Integrate Technology, Matter Control, Matter Emulation, Mechanical Transformation, Mimicry, Miniaturization, Monstrous Transformation, Omnicompetence, Out of Phase, Shadow Selves, Sticky Filaments, Stretching, Superhuman Attribute, Tool Mastery.
      • Travel (Skafoi): Aquatic, Brachiation, Chronological Alignment, Enhanced Speed, Flight, Opener of Ways, Translocation.


      The Spark of Life: The Axiom of Independence (Automata)
      Instead of granting permission to incorporate specific Variations, the Axiom of Independence grants the Genius permission to transform their test subject to another classification. It pushes the creation up the evolutionary ladder: granting permission to elevate inert matter into a Prototype, awaken a Prototype into a self-willed Manticore, or elevate a Manticore into a fully sapient Deviant.

      The Lone Progenitor (Initiating Divergence)
      Because creating a living Manticore or sapient Deviant from scratch requires institutional action rather than character-level equipment crafting, the Genius must utilize the Prototype effect of the Advanced Self-Made Form to execute Divergence Projects alone.

      Instead of building a traditional "prototype pool" to create a Deviant, the Genius adapts this process to interface directly with their Conspiracy of One. The character bypasses the pool and instead earns Conspiracy Beats that can only be used to generate Effort towards Divergence Projects. This allows the lone mad scientist to single-handedly fund and execute conspiracy-level downtime actions—like Launch Devoted Line or Create Manticore—applying the Axiom of Independence to spark true sapience in their new creations.
