    Originally posted by Alphari View Post
    How should I emulate high Magnitude powers by combining 2 or more lower Mag powers?
    I know that Mag3 can be made from 3 Mag1 variations, or 2 Mag2. But if I have Mag2 variations which are made from pairs of Mag1 - it makes things confusing.Or maybe it would be better to ask - "should I calculate each individual magnitude or whole variation magnitude"?
    I thought it would be the latter option, but now I am confused. Something like Electrokinesis is just a toolbox where each power is Mag1. And yet, we have something like Brachiation (generalist) 2 - you get both magnitude 1 effects, which should be ok. Yet it feels slightly like cheating ~ we can choose one power at Mag1, and get 2 more from Mag2 without making it a Mag3 variation.
    Brachiation 2 (Generalist) works exactly like taking Electrokinesis 2 and getting two options from the menu; you get the second power in place of upgrading Bounding or Wall Crawling. Brachiation 3 in turn functionally replaces Generalist with the Magnitude 2 versions of both of the Magnitude 1 options; the "emulate two effects from the preceding Magnitude" approach is preserved, while menu-type Variations like Electrokinesis trade for versatility (and, sometimes, synergy between the different effects).


    Another situation, wind-shaman: Mag1 enhanced speed 1 or storm-caller 1; Mag2 brachiation 2 / storm-caller 2, then Mag3 choices are telekinesis 3 or SC3; And M4 gets both M3 powers.

    Will Mag5 be a new Mag5 power, or mag3 (because M4 is made from two Mag3)? Or maybe separate mag4 power?
    This doesn't work because the non-Storm-Caller branch is triple-dipping; assuming this is meant to follow the pattern of two parallel tracks that join up at the end, the Magnitude 2 effect is providing a Magnitude 2 and Magnitude 1 effect from two different Variations, and the Magnitude 3 effect is providing a Magnitude 3 effect from a third Variation.

    That said, yes, if you were to continue the structure as a combination of existing Variations you would either make the Magnitude 5 effect by adding a Magnitude 4 effect from a different Variation, bump up one of the component Variations (e.g. increase Storm Caller to 4), or devise a new synergistic effect that builds on the existing parts.

    As Shallow Graves describes it, creating a new Variation by combining parts of existing Variations can be approached as though you were entangling multiple Tiered Variations with the same Scar.

                        Originally posted by Ipergigio View Post
                        Now, someone can give me a prewiev of the new Forms in Clade Companion, please?
                        Automatons are treated, mechanically, as objects, using Structure and Durability instead of Health. Uplifts use the statblock of an animal as the basis for their Physical actions, modified by the Physical Attribute dots they get as a starting character and purchase with Experiences. Both use their unmodified Attributes for relevant Variation calculations, and for obvious reasons the two are the only two mutually exclusive Forms in the book.

                        Outsiders are Deviants who weren't human before the Divergence and therefore have some difficulty in handling unfamiliar Social Skills and first impressions before they get some Acclimation, and the Root of whatever they used to be may mean they have non-Divergence-based abilities and difficulties based on that, though powerful abilities tend to mean they destabilize more rapidly than a similar human-based Deviant. (Automatons have a similar challenge from abilities they gain from being a haunted locket or a brain-uploaded computer, though the degree of individual power doesn't factor into it and Acclimation is used to mitigate the greater Instability.)

                        Uplifts, Automata, and Outsiders assume at the basic level that the character has all the normal capabilities of a human character unless otherwise noted, with those exceptions being modeled using Natural Variations and Natural Scars, which work as normal Variations and Scars apart from not being considered supernatural effects and therefore not being subject to effects that raise or lower Magnitude and not being capable of engaging a Clash of Wills (though Natural Scars can still entangle Variations and otherwise do not count as unentangled Scars for the purposes of gaining new Scars; Natural Variations come out of your normal starting Magnitude, although you can also spend Merit dots as part of the Form (Scar-free Natural Variations cost double).

                        All three of the above also adjust the cost of their Form(s) in Merit dots and Attribute dots based on the Size of their form and/or whether or not their form counts as a swarm (with larger bodies and more damaging swarms costing more), in addition to the ability to have Natural Variations and Scars.

                        Disconnected from the above systems on their own (though still compatible with any of the other Forms), the Myriads are functionally immune to death. When they die, they come back to life in a new body through methods set by the subcategory they belong to, which affects some of the things they can spend Merit dots on the Form to benefit from: Serials resurrect in a consistent location at the start of the next chapter, and can spend Merit dots to make this "facility" more portable and/or numerous (or gain a cost break on the Form by making their facility some level of hostile or dangerous to reincarnate in); Projections resurrect within standard Directed long range of the site of their death via the narrative justification of an appropriate Variation that they have and can spend Merit dots to reincarnate faster or not need to have a Variation that explains how they come back from the dead.

                        Both categories of Myriad default to starting every story by taking a major Instability, take a medium Instability whenever they die, reincarnate into an identical body, and have to die to change/reset their bodies; by spending Merit dots, they can reduce the degree of the per-story Instability, start an incarnation without their Physical Scars (losing access to their entangled Variations during that time but setting the timeframe in which they come back at character creation), or making their incarnations not identical (this last effect being a scaled effect called Models, with the level of potential change progressing from cosmetic to Physical Attributes to Physical Variations to Physical Scars - an additional effect that requires Models allows the Myriad to abandon their incarnation at will for a lesser Instability cost than dying); they can also get cost breaks by making it so that their incarnations start without any of their Variations until a certain amount of time has passed, making it so that they need outside help to reincarnate, or (if they have Models) leaving the relevant details of their new body up to the Storyteller rather than their player's choice.
