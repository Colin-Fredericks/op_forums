            Originally posted by Baaldam View Post
            Heroes might be closer to home in a number of ways, i guess? Must admit i'm not super-familiar with Beast at all.
            A Hero in the modern context of the Primordial Dream is someone who is driven to find a solution to the presence of Beasts. In ancient times, this meant Oracles interpreting the omens, signs and portents of Begotten activity and learning what place the Horrors had in the world society stood upon, but for thousands of years since then the increasing population and interconnection of humanity and its traumatic interactions with monsters and danger have added noise to the signal to such a degree that the typical context a Hero is encountered in is being marked as the self-aware designated main character of a storied clash with a monster that must be killed at any cost because it is a monster and they are the Hero who kills it, gaining Primordial power through this broadly ritualized murder.

            On an unrelated aside, how well or badly HtV and DtR conspiracies go together?
            They mingle. Some Web of Pain conspiracies are monster-hunting groups without necessarily being on the Vigil, and some Hunter conspiracies tilt their pursuits toward the acquisitive and have compromised the aim of defending humanity by exploiting sources of supernatural power to better fight monsters. Both are sufficiently secretive that any conflict between any given two is unlikely to escalate past the scope of cold war.

            The big distinction is that the Web of Pain is extensively interconnected and is sort of a collective human response to supernatural stimulus — they're the big brother to phenomena like Geist's suggestion to give the Obsession Condition on breaking points resulting from interactions with magical artifacts or Vampire's note that many vampire hunters have Persistent versions of the Conditions caused by the Kiss or the Assault due to particularly dramatic feedings, and the exploitation of mundane and occult resources toward their Principles comes first.

            Meanwhile, Vigil conspiracies hunt monsters to advance an agenda and make use of magic or advanced science as an emergent phenomenon of a bunch of different people who took up the dangerous work of hunting monsters gradually connecting with each other and organizing to better do that work — every active member of a Hunter conspiracy is a hunter capable of risking Willpower, engaging in Tactics, and desensitizing the Integrity of the vessel of their soul to exposure to magic and monsters and the evil they do.

            Also significantly, while Vigil conspiracy power sources may occasionally have things go wrong and produce monsters or strange phenomena and their hunters may go rogue to similar effect, the default state of affairs for a group of hunters is that they do not suffer a metaphysical blow to their human nature more significant that that developed by Merit-based psychic powers, ghouls, Wolf-Blooded, or Sleepwalkers. Web of Pain conspiracies lean on their Icons for any supernatural powers their Baseline agents can bring to bear, and those are extremely limited by the scope of the organization compared to producing, recruiting, or capturing and otherwise exploiting people whose volatile superpowers come part and parcel with burning out the part of their metaphysical anatomy that mediates between their soul and the rest of reality.

                Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                Should MGs with powers from other blue books, derived from splats or Hunters have access to Spark of Virtue?

                My default answer is yes, but I want a second opinion. I think even those with a Minor Power Stat (the kind that goes only to 5) should be able to buy it and fuse Power Stats.

                Also at Virtue 6+ the Girl should be able to buy traits above 5 like other supernaturals. Maybe it only manifests during transformation, though I am not against this level of XP commitment make her experience and exposure to magic be finally affecting her directly. Becoming greater than life in your civilian identity is also a staple of the Super Hero genre in general, and Mahō Shōjo no less at all.

                We also need a clearer definition of the loadout and budget and their differences. Should the Girl be able to summon multiple copies of the same item? Is a loadout like a physical arsenal, each item only exists in one place at a time? Is it possible to expand this arsenal independent from the budget?

                One source I think worth our time to look for ideas is I Don't Want To Be A Magical Girl. I'm not yet sure if it will hold as a genre example or breaker, but it does have elements to explore that especially fit Chronicles of Darkness.
                Sorry for the long delay; I got distracted by other projects.

                I would not fuse Spark of Virtue Virtue with a(nother) Power Stat. With that said, Spark of Virtue can be presented in a way that allows it in any character that has a Virtue; simply say that when it acts like a Power Stat, it doesn't stack: you use whichever of the two is better. Since the "fuel" of Spark of Virtue is Willpower, and everyone in the Chronicles of Darkness has Willpower, you also don't end up with more fuel stats than a supernatural would already have.

                This does mean that the new capabilities provided by Spark of Virtue can't be treated like normal fuel stats, though; because it's operating on a basis where no matter how much of the fuel you've burned, you're always potentially one action away from fully refueling it. So you don't get huge pools of Willpower points to spend; and often, the restriction in the use of a capability can't be reasonably measured through how much Willpower it costs. That's why I borrowed heavily from Deviant's "uses per scene or chapter" logic, along with resets (part of what Deviant calls Adaptations). And instead of having an escalating pool of points to spend, you get more opportunities to refresh your Willpower and to reset the uses of Spark of Virtue.

                So yeah; if you want to allow anyone with a Virtue to take Spark of Virtue, I say go for it.

                I would also say that you should get one free Virtue-based Merit when you take Spark of Virtue, singling out whichever one of them you think best represents the Virtue in question. Any others that you take represent a deeper understanding of that Virtue by the MG.

                For Spark of Virtue 6+, perhaps say that it unlocks Transformed Attributes: additional dots in an Attribute that are only available while Transformed, and which aren't counted when determining if you've hit your ceiling in the Attribute. They have their own limit (one dot per Attribute for each level in Spark of Virtue after 5, and possibly no more dots in the Transformed Attribute than in the underlying Attribute); but any Attributes above 5 without Transformation come from whatever supernature the MG has, not from the Spark of Virtue.

                In terms of the loadout, I wrote the Merit around the assumption that you get one of each thing in the loadout. That's why there are Merits for allowing you to leave a piece of gear behind, instead of it automatically disappearing when you lose it or it's taken from you.

                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                            I'd say the two conspiracy types aren't just compatible, but actually there's no meaningful distinction between them at all.

                                            What each game line does when describing their versions of a secret society including humans is not to ontologically determine that society, but it's relationship to the splat, and to an extent propose rules of interaction.

                                            So:

                                            1. Conspiracy/Cult traits don't exist. They're a means to track the status of an organization in a game whose proposal includes fighting the organization. You can use them across games, fuse them or ignore, this is not terribly relevant.

                                            2. Groups just exist individually and will have whatever fits them. Any given group can be a Mystery Cult, a Herd, a Pack, a Mummy Cult, a Krewe, a Hunter and/or Deviant Conspiracy or an Agency or others, each of those as distinct, independent aspects.

                                            It's less that they're compatible and more that they're just unrelated dimensions.

                                            3. Groups interact to whatever extent. The Web of Pain is just a framework to describe this in a game that proposes to track several such groups and their dynamics.

                                            In a sense, every single secret group in the CoD is part of the Web of Pain. It's just the name Deviants, who are regularly in big picture conflict with groups, call them.

                                            4. What makes a Conspiracy relevant for Hunter is if they track supernatural entities and send Hunters against them. What makes it be classified as a Hunter Conspiracy is to have an Endowment Merit to offer.

                                            What makes a Conspiracy relevant for Deviant is if they track Deviants, which usually means they capture Deviants. That's basically it.

                                            I'd say a Hero can be a Magical Girl, but seriously, they're the one splat where I'd say it is at the greatest effort to resist their own nature if you use them RAW. Maybe with the exception of the True Fae.

                                            And I agree with GreatHornedRatkin here, Clayton and Gaston are excellent examples of what a Hero is. Again, IIRC there's one example Hero that isn't like that, and the whole point is his constant struggle to remind himself that he is basically a fucking addict and needs control, not victories.

                                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                        3. Groups interact to whatever extent. The Web of Pain is just a framework to describe this in a game that proposes to track several such groups and their dynamics.

                                                        In a sense, every single secret group in the CoD is part of the Web of Pain. It's just the name Deviants, who are regularly in big picture conflict with groups, call them.
                                                        Where this falls apart is that "the Web of Pain" is not a diegetic term and the Web of Pain is specifically interconnected in a way that other gamelines' monster-adjacent societies are not.

                                                        Part of this is to provide a ready source of endless vengeance and/or servitude for the Remade, but Hunter conspiracies are not conventionally held to be working with the kind of logistical support that allows them to do what Divergence-related groups of similar scale can do in terms of drawing on the influence of mundane mortal organizations, do not know enough about each other for their agents to assume familiarity and working relationships regardless of how long their organizations have mutually existed, and are not intrinsically concerned with processes that require a machine to maintain — any conspiracy in the Web has the capacity to Send Agents and hypothetically field enough agents to bring Goon Combat into play because they are organizations that are recruiting Baselines and building resources that partially go into that process, and some of that resource-building ties conspiracies together by Mapping the Conspiracy having rules that require at least a few logistical Nodes that specifically come with links to other conspiracies.

                                                        Deep Dive has a section complementing the material in Black Vans for running other monsters as deviants by providing advice for running other supernatural societies using Deviant's conspiracy mechanics, and part of that advice includes a differently-framed statement of the established fact that most supernatural societies are disconnected from each other; shared philosophical origins don't change the fact that two Summer Courts in even neighboring cities don't necessarily have anything to do with each other, Agencies are typically local affairs regardless of how global the Machine is, and Vigil conspiracies are generally understaffed and paranoid enough that Hunter as a gameline has historically had plants from higher Tiers of organization as a prominent means of recruitment from the "four dopes and a shotgun" level upward. Very few supernatural societies are operating at the level of coordination of the Sects from Mage.

                                                        Different mechanical priorities are not the only reason Hunter manages the task of painting its conspiracies' sociopolitical influence through what Social Merits their Status Merit comes with, and the distinction between the international conglomerate The Cheiron Group and its chronically overquota'd Field Projects Division where all the hunters do their work is not a minor one. The Web of Pain as a structure has cross-applicability with other supernatural social systems, but just as specific pains were taken in the lead-up to Deviant's premiere to emphasize that the Remade template was not an absorptive catch-all for all the subtypes of monster the Contagion Chronicle calls "Dyads," it is important not to assume "the Web of Pain" means "every supernatural group in the Chronicles of Darkness" simply from the breadth of possible sources of Divergence and the capacity for deviants to interact with people and personlike entities.

                                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                            Where this falls apart is that "the Web of Pain" is not a diegetic term and the Web of Pain is specifically interconnected in a way that other gamelines' monster-adjacent societies are not.
                                                            True-ish and I should have been clear that I was still talking about the human-including groups, but also consider:

                                                            1. Nodes don't exist outside the abstraction. A Node that links Conspiracies actually represent anyone capable of producing the interaction rather than necessarily formal contact.

                                                            This means the bar is actually low. The two groups don't need to know about each other in any meaningful sense, they need to have someone that can transmit information to or from the other.

                                                            2. Similarly, recruiting and sending baselines can mean a helluva lot of things in Conspiracy rules because the process is just as abstract. A vampire using their pull on the mayor to cause a regulatory raid, or an Uratha tipping a pack member in the police to cause a raid are both deploying baselines en masse in Conspiracy terms.

                                                            3. Everything that happens in a Deviant game due to Conspiracy rules can happen at any other game, just not with clear management mechanics.

                                                            On most games the Conspiracy is a plot device that justify NPCs. In Deviant it IS the NPC. That is why they have the rules they have.

                                                            And their connection isn't a unique property, either, nor is it necessarily that strong. What it is is narratively necessary to track.

                                                            I'm not saying the Web of Pain means every group because of the possible sources of Divergence, even because not all Conspiracies in it produce Divergence, some only track and capture/recruit Deviants. I'm saying the Web of Pain is an abstraction that, if extended in extremis, will encompass every group because they all can be represented through Conspiracy rules if you want and come to bear in a Deviant game.

                                                            The actual extent of it is actually... A non-thing. It has no extent precisely because it's an abstraction, not an entity. It is as big as it matters for your game, not because you mapped every component, but because that was never the point.
