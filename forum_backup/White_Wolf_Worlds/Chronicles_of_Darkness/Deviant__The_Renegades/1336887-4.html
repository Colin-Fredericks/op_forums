                                    ...I'll admit some of the stuff I've been seeing the past several months on this game (and particularly what I've read in the KS preview yesterday) has me concerned. Normally, I'm all for the Chronicles of Darkness's tone of "danger's around every corner and turning into a worse monster is something characters must avoid." At the same time, though, the current tone for Deviant's at a level that even my dark-story-loving self is finding hard to digest. The organization tracking you down is everywhere, can take your life away from you at literally any minute, and any positive change you make is fleeting at best, and undone ridiculously easy by your actions at worst. Even the Integrity counterpart of Loyalty/Conviction and the Instability track seems to hammer this in. A Remade has no sense of self or internal character traits (something that sets off red flags for my inner writer), and any actions that go against pursuing completely extrinsic goals, even for ethical/pragmatic reasons, punish you with the seemingly inevitable endgame of going out violently a la Akira's Tetsuo. My friends and I tend to play our CofD games as, while crapsack, not so much that making some manner of positive change (even if just living to fight another day) is impossible. Yet from what I've read so far, Deviant's mechanics and lore go against such a concept. I hope this is just me misinterpreting what's currently available, though, because the premise of playing an experiment on the run from an organization that considers them property is otherwise a game concept I'm obsessed with.
