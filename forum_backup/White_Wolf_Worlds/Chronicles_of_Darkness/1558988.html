      The Riding The Wave Style Merit doesn't double the Strength bonus for regular attacks against non-object targets. Physical Intensity is an explosive burst of power that only adds to rolls instead of the Attribute, lasts one turn, and is less efficient than Disciplines in terms of Vitae cost when Vitae is disproportionately important to vampires compared to other monsters' fuel traits. Even with the Coil of the Wyrm, riding the wave requires rolls.

      For comparison:

      Gauru form gives werewolves +3 Strength by default and Favored Form allows that to go up by another dot (with a Rank 3 Totem giving enough of an effective Experience budget to give every member of the pack another Attribute dot plus a one-dot Merit) in addition to Primal Strength adding Purity to the Attribute. This costs no Essence and doesn't require them to accumulate successes on controlled frenzy, with the turn limit and risk of Death Rage being the primary concerns.

      The ceiling on Honing the Form from Mage is technically as high as they can offset the spell factor penalties for the cost of a point of Mana to break the Trait limit, with a maxed-out Rote Skill and a soul stone tool alone allowing +5 Strength without any net penalty to the base casting pool — a starting mage with minor investment can pull this off handily, to say nothing of a character at Gnosis 5.

      A Promethean with the Unconquered Alembic adds their Azoth to their Strength rating as a free passive effect, enabling an Azoth 10 Promethean to have a Strength of 20 before any expenditures for Transhuman Potential. This costs no Experiences unless the character wants to retain access to the effect after changing Refinements. The same Alembic also allows them to lift and throw massive objects in a way that Vigor doesn't facilitate.

      Deviants with Superhuman Strength can just outright set their minimum Strength rating to 8 if it's under 3 (or add +5 flat), and while the cost by default is a commensurate Scar there are multiple workarounds — even just going from the corebook, Cephalists and Coactives can boost the Magnitude of their Variations and Chimerics can drop their Scars' Magnitude to the level of pure roleplaying incentives, and Shallow Graves provides means of using Acclimation as the basis for further widening the gap between effect and cost.

      tl;dr The Kindred can put a lot of resources toward being able to use physical power on their environment, but it's generally wildly inefficient to do so on a prolonged basis and many other monsters can manage more stable results with less investment.

              Originally posted by DarkKing View Post


              The trade off is that while you are Frenzied, you are too delerious to use anything more complicated than physical disciplines.

              That means no mind control and no blood magic. Just full 'unga bunga,' and you're in trouble if that's not enough to get the job done.
              In my view, no: the rules do not say that in frenzy you can only use physical Disciplines. They say something else, namely that during frenzy the rational mind is no longer in command and the Beast wants to solve the immediate problem, basically by any means necessary. So the real point is not “physical yes, mental no.”

              The point is that whatever Discipline you use has to be consistent with the Beast’s impulse in that moment. If it helps you hunt, overwhelm, terrify, reach the prey, break through an obstacle, or unleash rage, then I think it fits. If it requires calm thinking, subtle manipulation, long planning, or self-control, then it does not.
              That is why I do not think mental Disciplines are automatically off the table in frenzy. They just stop being refined tools and become extensions of the Beast.

              For example, Dominate. If the vampire is in hunger frenzy and the prey has locked itself behind a door, I see no reason why the Beast could not fix on the nearest person and spit out a single brutal command: “Open.” That is not elegant mind control. It is the Beast using the shortest possible path to get to the prey.

              Same with a vampire who is being restrained, pinned down, or held in place while thrashing in frenzy. A sharp “Free me” or “Let go” feels completely in line with the state. Again, not subtle, not strategic, just raw predatory will smashing through an obstacle.

              Majesty also makes sense to me in that context, just not as a composed social performance. Imagine a frenzied vampire surrounded by people trying to corner him. He does not speak, does not negotiate, does not seduce in any human sense. He simply imposes himself. His presence becomes monstrous, almost demonic, like a succubus or some infernal predator pressing down on everyone in the room. People freeze, recoil, lose their nerve, look away, or instinctively back off. That is still Majesty, but expressed as pure supernatural presence rather than deliberate charm.

              So for me the correct reading is: mental Disciplines do not vanish in frenzy; they are only limited by the Beast’s immediate drive. If they serve that drive, they work. If they require the vampire to act against it, or with too much lucidity and finesse, then they do not.
