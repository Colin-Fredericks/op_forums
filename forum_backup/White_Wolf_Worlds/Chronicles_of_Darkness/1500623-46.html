                Really, none of the spells printed in the core book are innately rotes, and it actually is just a master list of sample (and probably common in-character) improvised spells. A person has the option to learn them as rotes in play, because the mechanical distinction between those types of spell are fairly small (if quite consequential).

                    Quick note: you don't gain the yantra bonus unless you are casting from memory (or you created the rote). Casting from a grimoire gives you the rote quality instead and doubles the casting time.

                    Basically you can either learn the rote (spend XP) and cast it from memory, gaining the mnemonic benefit. Or you can cast it from a grimoire and not from your memory and gain all the other benefits of a rote while casting a ritual speed and doubling your ritual time.

                    Only the creator of a rote can get both the rote quality and the mnemonic benefit (yantra bonus).

                                        Originally posted by Satchel
                                        It makes zero sense for casting from a grimoire to remove one of the largest benefits they offer in exchange for doubled ritual casting time.
                                        I think it creates a harder incentive to actually spend XP on learning the rote, rather than constantly needing to reference a book.

                                        Like, the mudras are described primarily as memory mnemonics, I think that by itself renders it incompatible with the process of reading the precise formula of the spell's Imago off of a reference point. The mnemonic adds the Skill rating by basis of being the foundation of how the Imago is remembered at all; your Computers Skill is helping to stand in for having a detailed description of the spell's mental image. If you actually have the detailed description in front of you, your Skill is unnecessary and irrelevant, in a way that also has a consequence of meaning that it can't really be used to fill out the Imago. But the precise formula provides the alternative way to build it that gets represented as the rote quality.

                                        I think they get set apart by virtue of the fact that a Grimoire is a means of forming an Imago completely different from all other spellcasting, the rote quality being the mechanical benefit representing the relief of that mental burden. Mudras are what you get out of knowing a rote in a way that makes casting it a lot more like the conventions of an improvised spell; it takes place fully within the mind, and hence requires the mental reference points that Yantras provide.

                                        If I was concerned with elaborating the flavour text of Scribe Grimoire, it would be in terms of how it seems like the formulae that a Grimoire's form consists of are translated out of the Imago shortcuts provided by the Mudras, but the translation is such that it still quite changes the manner in which mages can read them; like converting a tonal language into a series of Latin characters that approximate the same sounds. The formula of the Grimoire can be studied in a manner that a mage can internalise the codified Imago, but they still need the reference points of a Mudra to make that work.

                                        That the Orders know how to teach a way to translate Grimoire formulae and Yantra mnemonics back and forth is a sophisticated and proprietary piece of information that is part of the benefit of having done this for thousands of years.

                                        I would consider the description of the Create Rote Attainment not specifically stating that Mudras are a requirement to teach rotes from mage to mage to be a possible complication in this argument, but the point that Mudras must be utilised in casting a rote from memory and that Mudras are taught specifically by Orders is so clearly stated by their respective sections that I don't think an inference by omission overrides that. So for lone Nameless who become Masters, their only recourse for spreading their own rotes is to turn them into Grimoires, and any other Nameless who read those can only cast them out of the book and with the rote benefit.

                                                I think I get where the disconnect comes from: interpreting “If P, then Q” phrases.

                                                Casual reading tends to equate that with “If NotP, then NotQ” and then interprete “Is NotP, and is NotQ” instances as supporting it.

                                                But reading it via deductive reasoning, an “If P, then Q” statement implies nothing about the truthfulness of instances that include “Is NotP”, because that’s not what the statement deals with.

                                                So when the text says “If CastFromMemory, then Mudra”, an argument could be made that this text doesn’t actually say anything about whether “Is NotCastFromMemory, and Is Mudra” is true or “Is NotCastFromMemory, and Is NotMudra” is true. Judging which one is true would require one to look at other parts of the corpus……

                                                …… which kinda mixes up lore and mechanics as usual for CofD books, hence the confusion.


                                                For myself, I’m inclined to let Mudras apply to Grimoire-cast Rotes too. Less because of the logics stuff I wrote above, but more because I like the aesthetics of Rotes being this bridge between Fallen knowledge and Supernal practice.
