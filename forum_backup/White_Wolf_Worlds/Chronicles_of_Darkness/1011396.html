    Why Life is Strange is false premise on Wisdom

    12-03-2016, 03:04 AM
    It’s topic about video game that is one of best depiction of Acanthus Path and their powers. Discussion will be based on spoilers, cause you cannot talk about it without talking the plot of game. If you did not play it, go play. Right now. No, really, go right now. It will change your life. We will wait…

    Okay, you played? To the end? Great! Now we can talk about Awakened Wisdom and this game. So Max is archetypical Acanthus – she is photographer, she can rewind time, she has visions and sense Destiny over her. And at the start of the game she saves her long-not-seen best friend Chloe. From them, she saves her… a lot. And here we comes to how this game is wrong in eyes of Awakened.

    On start, Max have a vision with Lighthouse and great hurricane. Lighthouse can be obvious read as Lunargent Watchtower, especially as she gets her rewind power after this. Problem is with the storm. In final it’s uncovered it’s gigantic Paradox-like event she created with her time manipulations. Manipulations that started with saving Chloe and most of time were about rescuing her. So game say to save town from the hurricane, she must let Chloe die in their first encounter. Which any Acanthus will say is bul@#%it!

    If games was right, that meant you get your time rewind powers to save your friend – only to use them to not save her in the first place! It’s totally illogical and inappropriate. It’s the worst story ending also – much better would even that Max is running to the Chloe attacker and dies trying to save her. Or even most probably in Mage would be that Max jumps in to hurricane, finds Abyssal Demon running it and make a bargain.

    Sacrificing your loved one from the powers that let you saved her in first place must some Exarchs plot, surely!

                                  Not talking about a Mage, for a moment?
                                  The moral in the game is that there are things beyond your control, things that you cannot change. It's about accepting that while some things can be slowed, they can't be stopped. It's about valuing friendship and understanding that your loved ones could be gone any minute, right now, so you should be happy for the time you had with them and never take them as granted.
                                  It's about letting go after they are gone, and soldiering forward, instead of living in the past.

                                  In the end, this game isn't about changing the past, it's about coming to terms with loss. Time travel *isn't framed as a good thing* here. It isn't a cool superpower. Trying to change the past only bring disaster and unhappiness. "But you can go back and save the town simply by...!" is kinda missing the point.

                                  These are, by the way, very common tropes. Time travel is a literary device used exactly to impart these lessons very, very often.

                                                  You have perfect control about your choice. You can kill thousands to make one life or kill one to safe thousands. Acanthus is cursed and blessed at the same time, as they get to make this choice.
                                                  Then again note that when talking about moral and using time travel as device I was talking abuot the game in general, not in context of Mage the Awakening. Obviously, this show won't perfectly translate to Mage, mostly because it's not Mage and it was written with different stances and ideas in mind (for example, indeed for an Acanthus time travel is a tool to make their choices, while in game it represents unability to cope with events too hard to swallow).

                                                  Again, honestly, any of these ending choices can be Wise or Unwise. The key to Wisdom here is not what you choose, but seeing the bigger picture; did you decide to become a mass murderer willingly, taking full responsibility for your actions and with perfect understanding of what will it lead to?
                                                  You are Wise.

                                                  Did you do it on impulse, to save a girl, without thinking it through?
                                                  You are Unwise.

                                                  Wisdom is not about picking the morally right option, or good option, or comfortable option. It's about understanding consequences, reasons and interconnections, especially when looking at it through Acanthus Arcana. Your choice being informed is more important than the choice itself as far as Wisdom is concerned. That's why doing something labeled as unwise is not automatically a Wisdom loss; it's just harder and harder to understand and keep track of it.
