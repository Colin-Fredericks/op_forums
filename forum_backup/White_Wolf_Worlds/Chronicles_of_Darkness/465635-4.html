Humans believe fire is hot, ice is cold, gravity pulls you down, and so they are / do.                     That's how it worked in Ascension (CWoD Mage). In Awakening (NWoD Mage), it works very differently. And in fact, the Awakened take a scientific approach to understanding and explaining the universe, the supernal, and magic. Science is merely a means of studying things we observe. If scientists encounter something that should be impossible by our current theories, we develop new theories that account for and explain that thing being possible. Science is not, and will never be "finished", it is an ongoing process of expanding human knowledge. When people say "Magic is science we don't understand yet" that is the literal truth, not because the magician knows better science than us, but because anything that is understood is science. By definition, if we don't understand a phenomenon, it ain't science (and is therefore Magic) yet. The moment we understand it, it's science. Understanding it may require completely changing everything else we thought we knew, but that's just the nature of the beast.

                                Originally posted by amechra View Post

                                The quote is:

                                "Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic."
                                Yes - and what it means, in a nutshell, is that the different between "magic" and "technology" is mere context. That somehow the brains of most people are wired such that the ability to sufficiently explain a phenomena is tantamount to somehow completely disarming the phenomena.

                                As an example.

                                "You can speak to all people across the world, acquire any knowledge you desire, and summon up a band to entertain you or a philosopher to teach you with a mere command to your genie!"
                                "Dude, it's just Siri."

                                The arcane/divine split in D&D always annoyed me, too - what we're calling "wizards" is really a mutation of the Muslim/Christian alchemists/cunning folk who were busy trying finish God's work, not co-opt it. Give a cultural example of any magician and it'll almost always be "divine" magic of some sort.

                                Monks in Christian Europe in the middle ages were especially fond of building clockwork, they felt clockwork replicated the motions through which God created and maintained the universe, as an intricate series of interlocking machines. They thought everything was physical, in a way - anything mechanical was "alive," since the difference between life and not-life was some esoteric vital essence. Imagine knowing that fire burns combustible materials but not why those things are combustible, that's what we'd been living with all the way up until the 1700s. We didn't even know how fire worked until the freakin' EIGHTEENTH CENTURY. Image what it's like to construct clockwork devices that can sign names, "play chess," calculate the motions of the planets and the stars, and perform complex calculations in a time period in which you don't even know how muscles do or digestion or the very basics of chemistry. It may as well be magic. Clockwork may as well contain the same vital essence we must all use - except - wait a second - it doesn't draw in vital air! It's alive without any of the things you need to be alive! Look how it moves! Look how it perfectly replicates the motions of the stars in the firmament! Now see how the heretics are capable of building similar devices. Infernal machines. The Devil has tricked them into trying to take over God's place with devices that only mimic the vital spark.

                                Anyway, yeah. My games seem to do just fine without the God Machine - maybe that's because it was already there? With talk of orgone, John C. Lilly's Solid State Intelligence, Jewish mysticism's Kabbalah, references to divine machineworks in Hindu mythology, Chinese Mythology's enormous lean on bureaucracy (and the fallen beings that hide within/co-opt it), the stuff that people report seeing when they're high on DMT, and the Bible's straight up description of a freakin' Borg cube in Revelation as the divine city where everyone will live "as one." The Machine isn't a computer, it's a system, a system of oppression, a system of control, a system of maintenance of the status quo. Farmers building the pyramids in their off-season are a part of the system, putting together infrastructure in specific locations to disrupt or alter the flow of energy into and out of Shadow/Hedge/Twilight/Supernal. Kids in school doing pointless homework are unknowingly crunching numbers for it. Stonehenge is all that remains of an ancient infrastructure, but it's memetically important in humanity so it had the place declared a monument and set up armed guards. It built the pyramid in China to house a tiny scale model of the Empire complete with rivers of flowing mercury so that it had a back-up in case the real thing broke down. It's in stars, symbology, the paths of ancient trade routes. The cogs and gears are just the parts making noise. It's the system, man, and you rebelled and got chucked out for it. You're not looking for Lucifer because he's mysteriously gone missing, you ARE Lucifer, man. You stood up to God and got knocked down.

                                If you're itching for something Biblical, I'm sure the translation guide will be a huge help. There's a good video here about where our modern Devil concept comes from, and knowing a good deal about the mythology Fallen was trying to replicate is part of the reason why I've never been a huge fan of Fallen. But, hey, it's all about what people enjoy, you know?
