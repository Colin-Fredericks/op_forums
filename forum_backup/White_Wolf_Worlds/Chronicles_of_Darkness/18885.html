    Mummy and Magical Items from other Supernaturals

    11-26-2013, 10:28 PM
    I've been asking for this since the Preview Edition of the Core, but I would like one of the books to answer how Mummies interact with other Supernatural Items. I know some just don't want to handle anything nonMummy in their games, good for them but I am one who would like some answers for these. The major purpose of the Arisen after all was to collect Items of Power and bring them to the Judges. Relics aren't only for Mummies, they were originally supposed to be the general misc. Supernatural Item of the NWOD, Aegis Kai Doru are dedicated to them as well (though some implication is that they call all Supernatural Items Relics). Tokens mechanically are very similar to Relics, only with a different Origin. OF all the Items Tokens and Relics are mechanically the most similar. The reason why Relics are gathered is still a mystery, but we have a good indication that its mostly because of their nature as Vessels for Sekhem. But Perhaps Some Supernal Artifacts possessed by Mages would also be worthy of collection.

    I think this would be worthy of at least a sidebar in one of the books.

              I too would like an official answer at least as a sidebar, but I'll just throw out some ideas for house-ruling.

              1> For my purposes, any AKD "Relics" are interchangeably Risen "Relics" as well. I mean, all the AKD stuff has drawbacks of some sort, which aren't QUITE curses, but could be seen to be similar. I would allow Mummies to treat them as Mummy relics with a dot value equal to their Hunter Merit dot value. Likewise, I would allow (foolish) AKD Hunters to retain captured Mummy relics as AKD Merit relics at a dot cost equal to their mummy relic rating. If that made sense.

              1B> Since they kind of GAVE us WoD Reliquary as a bonus PDF with some of the pledge level for the Kickstarter, one ASSUMES that the devs intend that we should be able to use Reliquary Relics as, at least, building blocks for Mummy game relics. Basically, with Reliquary system relics you just have to judge what dot rating you want to give the item (since they don't really seem to have ratings as such) just in case your players want to eat their new toy instead of returning it to Duat.

              2> PERSONALLY, I see Geist and Mummy sharing the themes of life, death, unlife, and the underworld pretty closely. I would rule that Geist mementos and Mummy relics are different things, but share an energy. So, I'd let a Mummy drain a lesser memento for pillars, and Greater items (like Death Masks) might yield Sekhem. Similarly, I would allow a Sin-Eater to destroy a Relic and gain the plasm/healing benefit he'd get from sacrificing a Memento.

              3> Likewise, I would say that, since Sekhem is the power of eternal life/the underworld Duat, that Mage Imbued items or Artifacts that are tied to EITHER the Life or Death spheres would have an energy that resonates with/can be pulled out as Sekhem. Likewise, a Mage with an interest in either sphere might find a Risen Relic, "Interesting", to say the least. Perhaps if one were to study some we might learn a little more about who and what the Shaniatu really were...

              4> Prometheans don't seem to have any sort of magic items (other than Vitriol) and I don't see Changeling magic really "Meshing" with Mummy magic, so I'd just rule them as seperate. No real interactions going on there. Same for Vampires, No magic items that I know of.

              5> Werewolves magic items are, I believe, powered by Spirits, and spirits and ghosts are different, so unless a Fetish is powered by a spirit that would for some reason have a real resonance with the underworld or the mummies Deathless state, I doubt the Risen would even register the item's magic even if they handled it.



              So, just my personal thoughts.

                      AkD Relics: I tend to think of these as like gutter magic - a shorthand for collecting all the other gamelines' versions of supernatural gizmos into one category. What that means for Mummies is that they're case-by-case. (It would make a similar level of sense, be that great or small, for the Ascending Ones' Elixirs to be a distant descendant of Mummy's alchemy, with the poison-tolerance needed to use them a variant on the Guild tolerance to alchemical curses).

                      Reliquary Relics: I tend to think of these as a more naturally occurring version of Mummy relics, and powered by Sekhem just the same. I'd say mummies can use/sacrifice them just fine. As they weren't made via the techniques developed in Irem, no guild has a specific talent for handling them, but they aren't usually powered by such severe curses, so that's rarely a problem.

                      Sineater Mementos: Mementos are several different kinds of item. Charms and Memorabilia I'd suggest are the same as Reliquary relics, just death- rather than life-flavoured, and so mummies can at least sacrifice them. Vanitas and Fetters are too specialised to Sineaters' own powers, so I'd go with no. For Deathmasks, I'm undecided.

                      Werewolf Fetishes: I'd go with no; they are rooted specifically in the magic of spirits and werewolves.

                      Mage Artefacts: Maybe, but probably not. Supernal artefacts are highly symbolic, so mummies might be able to make some use of ones that are highly symbolic of things important to mummies - worship, civilisation, fate, the mummy's specific guild techniques. But Iremite magic is far more materialist than Supernal lore, and Artefacts might just seem empty of proper Sekhem.

                      Mage Enhanced or Enchanted items: No.

                      Changeling Tokens: I kind of think of glamour, from a human source, as dependent on that human's dSekhem/dt, but that's as close as they come. So I'd go with no.

                      Since Vitriol was mentioned: Vitriol is, to my mind, a good candidate for being the same thing as Sekhem. Which might mean mummies can take it, though they'd need different techniques from lacuna. I think it should count as a memory sin though.

                                              Excuse me while I perform the Rite of Return on this here thread...

                                              Anyway, my take on the subject goes something like this. Some forms of "magic items" are made with energies draw from human emotion or something similar. Fairy tokens certainly are, and while not all spirits are created by emotions alone the essence of the Shadow acts largely an "echo" of the real world. As such I say treat these as vestiges whenever a mummy tries to drain them. Mage artifacts and demonics Gadgets come from a sort of higher plane in terms of their power. Since Sekhem is supposed to come from the Gods and is subsequently shaped by human works (after a fashion) I would treat them as Relics for the purposes of draining. They can be part of a Lifeweb, but in terms of drawing upon their strength I'd say the "Sekhem" has been twisted too much ("these lines of fate have been unwound and stitched into something alien and impure...." or something). Under normal circumstances a mummy or a cultist cannot use it (unless it has an alternate method of use, like a Token's catch). For similar reasons I'd say a Mummy can only gain half the amount of Sekhem or Pillars from a given item (rounded up) . Sekhem flows through everything, and the resonance of Fate means it will pool in any mystical item. But an imperfect vessel intended to channel other forms of energy cannot contain the full power of Irem's ancient magics, and therefore yields less to a Mummy.

                                                      Indeed, according to the preview of the Egypt chapter of To the Strongest in Dark Eras, Sekhem is explicitly tied to the physical world. It has no particular connection to the Supernal Realms or any of the setting's other otherworlds, except for Duat and possibly the Underworld (which are, as I recall, explicitly not the same place). Since the Supernal Realms contain symbols for everything, Mages explicitly can create spells that interact with Sekhem, but it's known for being dangerous and difficult to control, and the Awakened are nowhere near as adept at manipulating it as the Shan'iatu were.

                                                      Based on that, I'd say that Artifacts from Mage do not contain Sekhem, but that a sufficiently dedicated Mage could probably learn to make at least minor Relics. That said, the process of forcing Sekhem into a particular configuration should be a difficult and dangerous process, and by no means should it be possible for a Mage to start casually manufacturing relics to help a mummy friend out.

                                                      As for tokens and the like, I'd say that anything from Arcadia or the Hedge contains no Sekhem and would probably be somewhat baffling to a mummy who came across it. Likewise, spirits don't seem to feed on or be a source of Sekhem, so Fetishes would be pretty much useless to mummies.

                                                      With AKD and Reliquary Relics, I absolutely agree with Sunless Nick that they should be dealt with on a case-by-case basis. The Aegis Kai Doru doesn't seem to be very picky when it comes to the items they collect, and Spirit Slayers, at least, suggests they even stockpile things that they can't actually use. An AKD relic could be almost anything and have almost any kind of origin, but a fair number of them are probably Sekhem-bearing Relics created by mortals, either deliberately or (probably more often) by some freak combination of genius and providence.

                                                      I don't know much about gadgets, but based on the description in the God Machine Chronicle, the God Machine seems to be pretty opportunistic about the occult resources it draws on to advance its agenda(s). I'd guess that its at least aware of Sekhem, and it's possible that it's made use of the energy in some capacity, so it may well be another case where items should be judged on an individual basis.
