            Originally posted by Tessie View Post
            I meant sources of damage that are aggravated on their own; not upgraded to aggravated based on who they hit.
            I thought about that too before posting, but I actually came to a simple conclusion:

            What kind of damage source causes Aggravated, and yet are affected by armor?

            No matter why the source causes Agg, if it is affected by Armor at all, then the minimum damage rule still indicates what you get after the Armor completely bracing the original source.

            The point being, if the Armor applies, and then if the Armor shrugs off all the damage, then the single damage that comes isn't actually due to the source, but to whatever force is still delivered through the armor itself. And I don't see many armors causing Aggravated to the wearer.

            Any source I can think of that would make sense to always cause Agg would either ignore armor completely or enough to never be completely braced by it, so damage cross over through math instead of rule.

                        This was how first edition did it. From Armory, p35.

                        Attacking with a strangle wire ﬁrst requires the wielder to succeed on a grappling roll. The following round, the attacker can apply the wire to the subject’s neck with a successful Strength + Weaponry roll (–3 for a targeted attack, but the victim’s Defense is not subtracted from the attacker’s roll). However, if the attacker makes a successful surprise attack from behind, and the opponent fails her Wits + Composure roll to notice the ambush, the attacker can grapple directly with the Strength + Weaponry roll. The opponent’s Defense is not applied.
                        The victim may attempt to break the hold every subsequent turn (per the grappling rules, pp. 157–159, the World of Darkness Rulebook). Every turn after the ﬁrst, the victim takes a cumulative –1 penalty to the escape roll, as the blood ﬂowing to her brain diminishes (maximum –5 penalty). Once the victim takes damage equal to her Stamina, she passes out. She can ﬁght unconsciousness by succeeding on a reﬂexive Stamina roll. A single success allows her to stay conscious until the following turn, when she must succeed again on another Stamina roll.

                                For simplicity's sake you can probably just use the normal grappling rules and treat applying the wire as a Hold move that applies a penalty to the victim's subsequent rolls (I would suggest -2 or -3).
                                Dealing damage is just the Damage move on a successful grapple check. Lethal if the wire is intended to cut, bashing if it's just restricting bloodflow in order to render the victim unconscious.

                                Finally, a successful surprise attack applies the wire directly.

                                                        Originally posted by Newb95 View Post
                                                        A garrote kills also by crushing the windpipe and can, with enough force, cause decapitation, if you can get leverage over a victim while using a sharp enough implement like a piano wire, you can definitely cut veins and blood vessels, sounds lethal enough to me.
                                                        But is not the same as defining the entire weapon as causing Lethal.

                                                        Crushing the windpipe is possible with specific maneuvers or by accident/enough force, and isn't more likely because of a sharp wire. Indeed, crushing the windpipe can be done with bare hands, if enough force is applied with the right movement.

                                                        If you can get leverage over a victim enough to cause decapitation with a sharp wire, you're beyond using the weapon status of the wire. In game terms you're causing damage through other means than applying the rules for a simple attack with the weapon.

                                                        We are not talking the many fancy ways one character can kill another with a wire, we're talking the weapon statistics of a garrote being used as a garrote. And on top of that, damage is an approximation of how close you are to dead. Since the garrote is already compressing one's throat and windpipe, enough force will make it lethal regardless of sharpness, because you're crushing someone's throat with all the vital nerves and vessels therein.

                                                        Upgrading the damage from Bashing to Lethal is a big deal. I'm not looking at the rules for strangling right now, you may want to make the garrote Lethal from start because it's certainly more efficient than strangling with bare hands. But you'll have two variations of the same weapon with strikingly different damage outputs. Is the difference between the two that big because one cuts? When cutting have nothing to do with the way they work?
