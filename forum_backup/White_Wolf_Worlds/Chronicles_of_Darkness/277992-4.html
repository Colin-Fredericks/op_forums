                I'd be happy if Sorcerers get rules at some point. That being said, I'm also happy using fan created material for them. Namely Octavo's Second Sight hack (Disciples of Duat), which I used in my last Mummy Campaign.

                I was thinking that when I run my next Mummy game, if Sorcerer rules are not presented in the core MtC line that Witch Finders actually lets you build some pretty awesome Necromatic/Darkness/Fate (basic themes/focuses of Iremite Magic that we've seen so far) pretty easily and with a bit more "kick" as antagonists.

                As for the matter of where we might see them, I'd place more of a bet on Sothis Ascends. For a couple of reasons.

                Firstly, Iremite Sorcerers would have come along after the fall of Irem, some of the relics described in the core talk about how later ancient cultures got hold of a bit of Irems knowledge and ran with it (and often screwed up and caused "bad stuff"tm to happen). So I'd not be surprised if Iremite/Scorpion Sorcerers have their origins in Turn 1 of the Sothic Cycle. Likewise, I would not be surprised if their origin ties into the Arisen cults of that time.

                Secondly, the Deceived, I suspect are going to require a lot of word count to cover properly. As they have their own history and culture to flesh out. Not to mention an obviously quite different rule set and maybe the basics of a semi "free form" magic system ala Mage or Blood Sorcery. On top of that we know the book contains the second part of the Avarice Chronicle, which is likely to be around 20-30 pages. Basically, even though Deceived is supposed to be around 160 pages (I think from the old preview info) that's still a bit of a squeeze for the amount of info its got to cover.

                Thirdly, if I remember correctly from the interview CAS did about a year ago, Sothis Ascends is introducing at least a single new type of antagonist. Its a big book of setting but its also a bit of a tool kit for powers, relics and new antagonists from what he hinted at. Particularly, I think it was going to look at "lesser" immortals (likely all lifeless) that more "modern" ancient cultures tried to create using incomplete and flawed knowledge of the Rite of Return. So if that sort of stuff is making it into Sothis, it would make sense to throw in the Sorcerers as an additional Antagonist type at the same time.

                Actually, in relation to that third point, if memory serves Sothis Ascends was possibly going to involve at least some word count on the other two "old school" immortals: The Purified and Blood Bathers from WoD Immortals. I seem to remember one of the writers (Malcolm???) during the Kickstarter/preview core pdf discussions talking about how their was some stuff written about how the Purified see the Arisen (sorta like a stereotype sentence that we get for Vampires, Werewolves etc in most books when the splats are presented). It was pretty horrifying apparently. They may have also been a side-box style paragraph about the topic. This was nearly 2 years ago, so I am honestly not sure how much is accurate recall and how much is my mind filling in blanks/wish fulfillment.

                Got kinda sidetracked there...anyway, roll on Deceived.

                        Originally posted by Christian A View Post
                        Besides books like Mage, Witch Finders or Second Sight, how many more rulesets for mortal sorcerers do we need? I'd prefer (and expect) Mummy wordcount to actually focus on Mummy.
                        There's a few reasons, not the least of which is that Mage is a different game with different themes than Mummy, Witch-finders is for Hunter, and...I'm not the biggest fan of Second Sight.

                        One of the bigger reasons, though, is how often mortal sorcerers are mentioned in the Mummy books as if they're expected to have their own rules:

                        There's mention of them creating greater amkhata (which, admittedly, is detailed).

                        Mortal sorcerers "wield a fragment of [power]" & "Sorcerers concentrated will and scholarship into an elementary command of Sekhem, so they deserve respect, though never friendship" & "Sorcerers know something of the Will, so their creations may contain secrets that will allow scribes to revive the old magic" (Guildhalls), and also page 86 of Guildhalls they're mentioned in a table on using talismans.

                        The core talks about how Shuankhsen get a Descent reset when they eat 'sorcerers.' The core says Mummies can learn Utterances from sorcerers - something very specific to this game's rules. It also mentions sorcerers can summon Mummies from henet without relics, and that there's certain effects when a vestige is destroyed near a sorcerer. Many relics mention sorcerers.

                        Thirdly, one of the main recommended styles of Mummy play is a pyramid-like structure with one or two Mummies, probably a Sadikh, and then some cultists all as PCs. Providing rules for Mummy-specific mortal sorcerers would be a fun addition to that. Especially with this reason, it doesn't detract 'Mummy wordcount' from 'actually focusing on Mummy' because it's still providing rules for PC play in the game, you know?

                                                    "The world is ultimately doomed" certainly wouldn't be an out of the question sentiment in a setting with as many eldritch abominations running around as there are in Mummy.

                                                    I'm less sure about the Shan'iatu making the Arisen in order to escape the Judges. The Judges are emblematic of Bronze Age justice, which the Shan'iatu imposed on their own civilisation. To me, that, more than anything, suggests a "chicken and the egg" scenario. Did the Shan'iatu impose laws on Irem because they were enslaved to creatures that thought these laws were proper, or did they "create" these Judges in the myths of the civilisation they made in order to have a divine authority to call upon when executing said laws. Given the hints in the Pale Brood's description and the section on the Shuankhsen, it sounds like the latter is a bit more likely.

                                                    Mulling on the "Shan'iatu are actually slaves to the Judges, and so created the Arisen to escape" idea did turn up an amusing little thought though. Mummies can escape the Judges themselves by going through Apotheosis. If you were to accept that the Arisen were made as subsitutes for the slavery of the Shan'iatu, what if to achieve Apotheosis you have to do something similar? Suddenly Mummy is looking an awful lot like Promethean.
