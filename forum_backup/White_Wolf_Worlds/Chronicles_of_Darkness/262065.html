    Geist Chronicles: Krewes

    09-21-2014, 08:42 AM
    Krewes

    There is a common belief among the Bound: Everyone dies, what matters is what you leave behind. Iron rusts, marble erodes, a true memorial is in people. The lives that you have changed will in turn change others. Your touch will echo down history.

    It doesn't matter if this belief is right, wrong or a transparent attempt to sound impressively wise. Sin-Eaters tell this koan, and others, to explain a simple fact. Their powers work best when they bind each other together. (The Bound also say it doesn't matter if this explanation is right or wrong, it has more heart than a chalkboard full of mystical mumbo jumbo equations)

    Most Krewes begin simply. A group of Sin-Eaters are drawn together by a common factor. It could be a goal, usually a simple one like getting rich, or quite often by being the only Sin-Eaters in town. Most begin with a constitution of some sort, it’s practically a necessity: If one prospective member is a Reaper or lets his Geist run around tormenting people and another is a Gatekeeper sworn to defend mortals from the dead. Well it’s important to have that discussion early on. Living with Geists tends to push the Bound to unhinged personalities, you can’t just throw several in a krewe without a plan.

    The Bound don’t have to take it beyond a Constitution but most do. Both more experienced Sin-Eaters and Geists encourage a newly formed Krewe to “make it official” with a Ceremony for the long term benefits.

    The Krewe Binding Ceremony

    To form a Krewe requires an Avernum Gate, knowing participants (coercion works, mind control or trickery will not), and mind altering substances. Products of the underworld are considered good luck; you can find psychedelic mushrooms growing in the Upper Reaches or take a draught from Urdabrunnr, The River of Fate. Alternatively LSD, magic mushrooms, peyote or even copious amounts of alcohol may be used.

    When the participants are well and truly under the influence each party manifests their Geists. Next they open the Gate. If all goes well no one will see the Upper Reaches, instead the Gate will open to a vast expanse that stretches into the distance. Fog banks, staircases and rivers are common. Some Sin-Eaters say they can see all the way to the bottom of the Underworld.

    Looking at their Geist the Sin-Eaters will notice a cord that stretches through the Gate and into the distance, to complete the ceremony each takes their partner’s cord and weave them together.

    That’s all it takes, though Sin-Eaters have been known to jazz up the ceremony.

    Joining an existing Krewe uses the same ceremony. One Founder or Status 4+ member is required to induct new members and the initiates weave their Geist’s cord to their sponsor’s.

    The Evolution of a Krewe

    Even a group of friends will over time develop rituals and traditions. In jokes, or an annual party. Krewes exaggerate this tendency. Firstly they do so because they have different, more extreme, pressures on them. Many groups of friends come from the same environment and so are shaped by similar forces, Krewes are part of two different worlds which cannot help but shape them differently to their mortal neighbours.

    Secondly a Krewes little rituals and cultural quirks tend to have mystical weight to them. A Krewe might use a blood sacrifice to open the way to the underworld, one day after using a black chicken for no particular reason they have an unusually successful trip. The next time they go out of their way to get a black chicken and, it’s not quite as good as the first time but it’s still pretty good. There’s high fives all round and the Krewe agree they’ve discovered a mystical principle: Sacrificing black chickens to open the Underworld is good luck.

    They’re right, too. What makes a Krewe so central to a Sin-Eater’s culture is that it’s traditions have power. Even if sacrificing black chicken’s does not provide luck, when the Sin-Eaters believed it did they created a new tradition, and following that tradition will create luck. The cause is different, but the effect is the same.

    Then sometime later the Krewe will come across another Krewe who believe sacrificing black chickens is offensive to El Pollo and brings a curse upon everyone nearby… it probably won’t be any trouble. Sin-Eaters bump into contradictory mythologies all the time.

    On the whole the Bound are aware of the mystical effects behind their Krewe’s traditions have. Few believe their rituals or mythology are the One True Way (and those guys are unpopular). Most Sin-Eaters intentionally create traditions based on what they want to be true, which is not to say they go around telling everyone that “wearing this necklace, this one around my neck, every day makes you a god”. A Krewe’s mysticism is as much a brand identity as a source of power. The Bound don’t respect anything without an air of mystery, morbidity and badassness. Also, you can’t turn yourselves into Gods with granny’s necklace, Krewes are powerful but not that powerful.

    Instead most Krewes use a hodge podge mix of funerary rituals stolen from various mortal cultures, ideas from respected older Krewes that have been around long enough to be fair game, old superstitions, and things that worked once and seem worth trying again. Krewes don’t need to be based on genuine occult principles, but they don’t hurt. Funerary rituals or odd tricks that worked before are good places to find the intersection of coolness and nuggets of genuine occult power. A few Krewes are dedicated to uncovering genuine occult truth, the advantages of using real occult principles usually balances out the loss of flexibility, usually.

    As a Krewe grows in size and age these effects increase. More Sin-Eaters weave their partner’s cord into the Krewe’s communal channel, creating a larger passage for the power of death. Discoveries and accomplishments of past Sin-Eaters remain past their death, widening the channel. Krewes have three different levels of power, which defines which Krewe Advantages they can purchase.

    Mojo 1 - Old wives tales: Even the smallest and youngest Krewe can offer some power to it’s members. Not too much, but at this level it’s easy to experiment, to replace a bad tradition with something new.

    Mojo 2 - Local traditions: As a Krewe grows it’s power increases, but it becomes more rigid. Replacing an established tradition becomes serious work and those traditions can become restraining: Not only is sacrificing the black chicken good luck, not sacrificing it becomes bad luck.

    Mojo 3 - Religious Dogma: At the apex of it’s power a Krewe’s rules become restrictive and cannot change without great effort and internal strife, but the power they offer is more than adequate compensation.

    Sidebar - Mojo and Tier

    A Krewe’s Tier, representing its general size, power and influence does not always match up with it’s Mojo. A small dedicated Krewe of who constantly study, refine and add new elements to their channel could eventually reach Religious Dogma without ever growing beyond ten members. Though it must be said such small Krewes often take generations to accomplish this feat.

    You can usually assume that Mojo will be equal or higher to a Krewe’s Tier, but a large sprawling Krewe with little interest in occult power may break this rule.

    For the record the population figures of the three Tiers look like this:
    • Tier 1, At most 10 Sin-Eaters and up to thirty mundane members.
    • Tier 2, Up to 100 Sin-Eaters maybe as much as 500 mundane members. Most Krewes this size consist of autonomous tier one sized groups but this is not compulsory.
    • Tier 3, Over 100 Sin-Eaters and thousands of mundane members spread across two or more continents.


    Krewe Benefits

    Krewes aren’t just the people who have your back in the underworld. They provide powerful benefits to their members. There are three categories of benefits.

    Founder - Only the founder of a Krewe or their successors can acquire this benefit. Some Founder Benefits can be inherited at no cost, these are labeled explicitly in the description.

    Supernatural - Any Sin-Eater who has been Ceremoniously initiated into the Krewe can acquire this benefit. However the Krewe itself must buy the Merit first to unlock the potential. Some Supernatural benefits can apply to other kinds of Supernatural members, such as Mediums or Stoners. These are clearly marked in the text.

    Temporal - Anyone, even a mortal, who is officially part of the Krewe can benefit. Sometimes people with unofficial membership can acquire the benefit. It’s not supernatural, merely the results of access to the metaphorical communal coffers.
    In addition there are three ways a benefit can be priced:

    Automatic - The benefit must be purchased by the Krew’s founders, once they do so all qualifying members get the rewards.

    Merit - The benefit is priced as a merit and each qualifying member must buy the benefit individually using experience. Most of these benefits are Founder benefits or have a Founder benefit as a prerequisite which defines how the benefit manifests in this Krewe.

    Opt-In - The benefit must be purchased by the Krew’s founders, afterwards any qualifying member can choose to gain the benefits, and it’s drawbacks, or choose to ignore it. Social pressure may “encourage” members to partake.


    Krewe Experience

    Sin-Eater take a Krewe Beat every time they do one of the following actions. These may be spent on the various Benefits that a Krewe provides:
    • Ethos - The character performed an action considered justified by the Krewe’s rules or avoided one forbidden by the collieries to the Krewe’s rules when it caused a significant problem or required a significant amount of effort.
    • Lore - The character explored the Krewe’s lore and learned something significant. For example, a Sin-Eater in a Krewe that believes you must always sacrifice to Mictlantecuhtli before entering the Underworld may experimenting with different sacrifices and record the success or failure of each expedition before concluding that Mictlantecuhtli likes black goats the best. Whether this is a valid discovery or a coincidence, or whether scholars of Aztec religion know damn well that Mictlantecuhtli prefers someone impersonating him to be sacrificed with incense does not matter. You still get the Beat and add black goats to your channel.
    • Lore (again) - Discovering an genuine occult mystery about death or the Underworld and adding it to the Krewe Channel grants you one beat. Be it a local mystery or a wide-reaching aspect of metaphysics. If you explore your Krewe’s Channel and learn something useful then you gain both beats for Lore.
    • Loyalty - A Sin-Eater who is loyal to her Krewe in the face of adversity takes a Beat.
    • Zeal - When a player fulfills an Aspiration, and that Aspiration is directly tied to the Krewe, and it doesn’t fall into one of the previous categories, take a Krewe Beat in addition to the regular Beat.

    Sidebar: Skipping to the Good Stuff
    If everyone knows that the players are going to found a Krewe, it is recommended that storytellers award Krewe Experience before the players get around to performing the Krewe Binding Ceremony. This is to avoid giving mechanical incentive to rush the character's natural development from strangers to trusted allies.

    If you’ve accumulated a significant amount of Krewe Experience before you form a Krewe. You can declare that your characters received a mystic vision when performing the Krewe Binding ceremony, or that they think they did - psychedelics are part of the ceremony, and spend the exp on what was revealed to them. Naturally you should only do this if a religious vision fits your characters.

    Mojo One

    Krewe Mythology (OOO)
    Temporal Benefit, Automatic

    Why doesn't everyone Pass On? Why is the Underworld such a pit? Why do Sin-Eaters even exist? A Krewe’s mythology is a story that explains the mysteries common to the Bound, and maybe a few more esoteric mysteries relevant to the Krewe’s interests. It is common for a Mythology to draw connections between life and death, for example a Krewe formed from the victims of a war might speak of a devil figure whose misdeeds are responsible for both the war and the state of the Underworld.

    A large part of any Krewe is it’s brand image and mythologies are the more image focused than most other Krewe Benefits. The myth’s symbols and icons serve the same role as a corporate logo or gang colours. A deity or hero figure can show the outside world how the Krewe want their members to be perceived. Lurid tales of the devil’s punishment could serve as a warning that the Krewe’s enemies should expect no mercy. The elements incorporated from mortal cultures can show the Krewe’s background and their pride in their religion, race, or community.

    Most Mythologies are designed from the ground up as branding; the elements are designed to present an image or just to be cool or badass and spread that coolness to the more symbolic elements.

    Sin-Eaters often customise their Momentos, reworking them in the styles or adorning them in the images of their mythologies. If they do so treat the Momento as one dot higher when measuring it’s effects as a social bonus. Even being a part of a Krewe shows that you’ve got Sin-Eaters watching your back, that’s worthy of respect. For particularly large Krewes (one where it’s possible for two members to not know each other) treat Momentos as two dots higher when measuring the social bonuses against fellow members.
    In addition, when it is applicable, each Momento decked out in the Krewe’s image gives a +1 to recognise your Krewe affiliation, to a maximum of +3.

    It is of course quite easy to deck out Momentos in another Krewe’s style. There’s nothing supernatural about paint. Some Krewes consider imitation the sincerest form of flattery, others are violently protective of their image.

    Sidebar: Fitting them together

    It should be obvious that there are links between Krewe Mythology, Krew Ethos, Geist Aspects and Krewe Metaphysics. A Mythology or Metaphysic can justify the Ethos. The Aspects probably have a starring role in the mythology.

    There are no mechanical rules for this. You do not have to buy Mythology before Aspects. There’s not even any rule against inconsistencies; but take some time to think how they fit together, perhaps plan all four out in advance. Designing a Krewe is part of the fun.

    Kewe Ethos (O - OOOOO)

    Temporal Benefit, Opt In

    What does the Krewe believe is moral? What do they believe is wrong? Is a medium who calls up Ghosts for grieving family offering closure, or violating the sacred border between life and death?

    Sin-Eaters can’t rely on what they heard in school, the ethics of destroying a dangerous Geist aren’t part of any curriculum. Without guidance, and usually without accurate occult information, Krewes tend to make their own moral code.

    For each dot in Krewe Ethos choose one moral rule, ideally one with a clear corollary stating something is immoral. These should also be specific actions, an ethos shouldn’t say anything goes if it makes you money or satisfies the Geist. Some examples:
    • Violence is ok to stop mortals breaching the barrier between life and death. Corollary: it is wrong to help mortals breach the barrier.
    • A clean death isn’t worse than survivable harm, we know you can pick yourself up afterwards. Corollary: A messy death that prevents you from moving on is a heinous crime.
    • Drugs should be legal, how you live is more important than how long you lived. Corollary: Drugs should be fun, pushing a bad trip just isn’t right.


    Members of the Krewe may Opt-In to the Ethos (it’s all or nothing). Every time they hit an Integrity Breaking Point they gain +3 if the Ethos said it was ok, but -3 if the Ethos said this is immoral. Mortal members get +2 or -2 instead. As always, succeeding on a Breaking Point roll can toughen you up until you no longer need to roll for that action, though it may take a few times for some of the things Krewes tend to talk about.

    Note that mortal members of a Krewe can become ok with killing, under the circumstances permitted by the Ethos. It takes a lot for a mortal to become inured to the horrors of killing, but buying into the dogma of a freaky death cult can do it.

    Geist Aspect (OO)

    Founder Benefit, Merit

    What does the Geist represent? If your Geist is the Archangel Zadkiel, the Archangel of Mercy. Why was Mercy important enough to birth a Geist? What is the role of Mercy in the cosmos? What fundamental principles of the universe revolve around Mercy?
    Perhaps Mercy is The Way To Heaven: Ghosts can escape the purgatorius underworld by showing Mercy. Perhaps mercy is the original sin, in the beginning there was no death but there was pain. The first murder was committed out of mercy to free someone from unbearable pain and that mistake cursed the universe with death for all time.

    All Aspects consist of two parts: The truth and the expression. The truth is normally a simple thing: “Hades is the benevolent lord of the dead” or “Mercy is the Original Sin”. The expression shows how the Geist relates to it, here are some possible expressions for “Mercy is the Original Sin”:
    • The Good Side of Mercy - Your Geist symbolises why mercy is not fundamentally wrong even when it caused such suffering. It is the other side of the same coin, so to speak.
    • The Bad Side of Mercy - Pick something wrong with mercy, your Geist represents that.
    • Selfishness - In the story mercy helped one person at a terrible cost to the universe. Your Geist represents selfishness or unwitting selfishness. It is ok to dip into the Krewe Mythology behind the truth in this manner.
    • The victim - Your Geist is symbolic of someone who really needs mercy, but giving it mercy will have a terrible price.

    Aspects can be a complex intersection of morality, myth and personality. There is no proof that a Geist’s Apsect actually is cosmologically significant, many Sin-Eaters think of Aspects as culturally significant rather than universal, in this case the culture is the Krewe, or think the whole thing is hogwash and only play along to keep their Geist happy. Others spend lifetimes examining the philosophical or theological implications of their Aspects, delving deeper into their Krewe’s collective channel.

    Whatever Aspects really are, the mechanical effects are simple. When your gain an Aspect you have an additional way to regain support. Depending on what you feel is appropriate an Aspect can function like a Geist’s Passion or Past, either way you can regain support by symbolically acting out it’s expression of the Aspect. For example if your Geist expresses its aspect of Mercy is the Original Sin as The Victim you could gain a point of support when somebody (including yourself) in the Geist’s presence refuses to show the Geist mercy because it would hurt somebody else.

    The Geist doesn't have to like living out it’s Aspect, you can get Support anyway and can even risk Synergy by helping the Geist go against a hated Aspect. (Presumably this represents your partner becoming less stable, even though it is grateful to you)

    Gaining an Aspect
    What happens when a Geist gains an Aspect: Sometimes, not much. The Archangel Zadkiel gains the Aspect “Mercy is the Path to Heaven” and expresses it as “The Guy Who Shows Mercy”. He looks at his Sin-Eater and says “what were you expecting?”. Nothing changed except that the player now has an extra way to gain Support points, which are mostly an out of character game system anyway.

    However a Geist taking the form of a merciless demon who gains that exact same Aspect and expression might physically transform into Zadkiel and claim the Sin-Eater redeemed him, or just deny he was a demon five minutes ago. Geists lie all the time, one reason Sin-Eaters try not to get dogmatic about Aspects. It doesn't have to be Zadkiel, he might transform into a human judge and start talking about mercy. He might not transform say that God showed up and cursed him to start showing mercy. He might claim he was always Zadkiel and merciful while fondling his cat-o-nine-tails and keep a straight face through such a blatant lie. He might claim he is an aspect of Zadkiel in the form of a demon. In short the possibilities are endless.

    It is just as likely that the demon might demonstrate a different expression of that Aspect: He might be the guy who was damned to hell by not showing mercy, or he might express the Aspect by being the guy who needs to accept mercy most of all.

    In short, gaining an aspect is an extremely flexible system. It can change everything about the Geist or nothing at all, so whatever your character concept is an Aspect should reinforce rather than clash with it.

    Sidebar: Morbid Passions

    So if you have a Geist with Morbid Passions, you can just get an Aspect and use that to keep your support up and never worry again. Right?

    Well you can, if you want to, but it’s not that easy. A Sin-Eater can’t just say “I’m an Advocate, so I’ll have my Geist express ‘The Ferryman transports souls to the afterlife’ by becoming the Ferryman and supporting me in helping Ghosts Pass On.” On some level the Geist has to accept that the Aspect reflects its role in the cosmos. It doesn't have to like it, it just needs to accept it. If you’re enjoying playing out your struggle with geist of pure evil, say it’s too stubborn to accept any aspect that isn’t as horrid as it is and keep the Morbid Passion rules in play. You can say it gets that Aspect if that makes your Krewe’s mythology cooler but a Morbid Aspect has no mechanics or cost.

    If playing the fight with your Geist isn’t as fun as you thought it would be, an Aspect makes a neat explanation for fixing it without contradicting the past. You can even say the Aspect changed your Geist’s regular Passion too. Even if the fight was fun to roleplay, convincing the Geist to accept an Aspect can be a fitting conclusion to the story.

    Inheriting Aspects

    When a founder passes on the mantle to a successor their Geist takes on the Aspect. This is non-negotiable, either the Geist takes the Aspect, the mantle goes to the next guy in the line of succession or the founder’s mantle is lost forever.

    What this means is that the successor’s Geist expresses the same truth, but it may express it differently. For example, if a Krewe has the aspect “Hades is the totally awesome lord of the dead” the founder’s Geist may express this as “I’m Hades, regal and awesome!” The founder gains Support by being an awesome leader.

    His successor gains the same Aspect, but her Geist expresses it in the opposite way: “I’m Hector, the challenger to Hades’ rule for all rulers are challenged”. The successor gains support whenever she’s in a situation where her Geist challenges Hades rule (whether she supports or opposes that challenge). By the way, as Hector is merely an expression of the underlying truth, that Hades wears the crown, he can’t actually succeed in overthrowing the lord of the dead. Though the Krewe might not survive the conflict.

    The duality between changeable expressions of unchanging truths reflects the duality of the Bound. It’s the contrast between the forward looking, changeable, living Sin-Eater and the Geist who is bound to the past and only changes with outside influence. A Krewe can change with the times as new leaders express their Aspects in contemporary ways, but it always retains a link to the past and the truths it’s founders uncovered. This may be the only real truth enshrined by Aspects.
