        How about this for the Orpheus Project:

        ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

        Sometime in the mid 17th century, with science and occultism existing in tandem, a group of German and French occult scientists and Alchemists were working tirelessly to marry the abstract concepts of newly developing science with the ancient rites of occultism and kabbalism. As time went on, and their work yet bore little fruit, members of this occult society started to grow impatient and frustrated with their lack of sucess. Each apparent clue lead them to the grave of a person who might've answeres they were seeking, if not for the fact that they died decades if not centuries ago. The groups efforts took an almost archaeological and historical tune, as they spend weeks and months trying to piece together forgotten knowledge of dead occultists and Alchemists, in search of a knowledge they were certain the long dead possessed. As time went on, some members aged terribly and other simply dropped off the project for lack of progress.
        Sometime in the late 17th century, the group recieved a package from an anonymous source, with the symbol of Metatrons Cube as the insignia seal of the large package.
        Inside, they found a letter, a map, blueprints, multiple long slender hollow rods ending in a long thin spike and a strange hedronically shaped device made from a queer dark metal unlike anything they had before seen, with openings in which tubes could be inserted. The letter spoke of a realm beneath the earth harboring rivers of eternal life, where the shades of the dead may still linger. The letter went on to explain the nature of the rods and the hedronic device, refering to the latter as a Hex-Core, to be inserted into what the letter described as The Avernian Engine. By this profane device, the letter said that the knowledge of the dead could be extracted to be readily drunk by whoever wished. The blueprints in the package explained in full detail how to construct this Avernian Engine, placing the Hex-Core within it. All the device needed were the fuel; A shade to be extracted from, and the water from this River of Life to act as the catalyst. The letter, signed by a Mr Jambres, explained that the map showed the location of a gateway into the stygian realms located in South Germany, from which this river was readily accessible. It explain to them the nature of the rods, which could suposedly trap ghosts within them, if thrust into the shades while they were material, something Mr Jambres promised would be a given in the bleak realm in which the river lie. Out of desperation and a sense of awe at the strange device, the group decided to heed this advice and pursue their goal through the shadowy portal they found, after construction of the Engine was complete.
        What the letter failed to mention was the saw-toothed, faceless horror with multi-bifurcated veiny arms and quasi-humanoid body that guarded it.

        As the remnants of the group made their narrow escape from the skittering horror and back onto the surface world, they clutched mightily their stolen price; Water needed to fuel the Avernian Engine.
        They had been in luck during their jaunt into the Underworld. They had found a shade that one of the historically inclined members recognised as a major-general of an old German-Roman army, and so they had chosen his ghostly form to experiment on.
        The Scientists found that the essence of the dead could grant them much more than merely the knowledge that Mr Jambres had promised them. Their shadowy nature coursed through the occultists and mixed with the water from The River Of Life, which Mr Jambres refered to as Anahita, gave them a renewed sense of vigor.
        The only true downside to this concoction however, was that they could no longer sneak about as living in the Great Below as long they were under the effects of their elixir. Here, the shadowy nature of what they had stolen was readily visible to all, and they were seen as abominable mixes between living and dead. Wandering through the planes of death became impossible, as all the deathless guardian horrors that lurked there saw them for the crime they had committed, and chased them relentlessly out of the shadowry domain.
        Eventually, the group recruited new members to plunder the Great Dark for knowledgeable shades and additional water, even as the exlir's warded death away from the original members.

        These days, the group has expanded heavily and branched out. The halls of their headquarters, build atop the black gate that Mr Jambres map originally pointed to, is filled with the trapped shades of hundreds - if not thousands - of historical figures, which the inner circle sups on monthly to extend their unatural life and increase their stolen knowledge. The group deploys two departments of field agents, one group who ventures into the Avernian Dominions to gather more water and opduct more shades, and another which uses the stolen powers of the dead to convince would-be interlopers and inquisitive people to look the other way.

        ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

        Whef. That became a bit more extensive than I had innitially imagined. Anyway. Let me know what you think. Will probably do something with this for my own Geist game, eventually.

                Krobeles: that’s an intriguing antagonist group for Geist; but I wouldn’t go with “Orpheus” as its name. I would like to see this done, and in the same vein as Demon’s Deva Corp or Mummy’s Last Dynasty International; just go with some other name than Orpheus.
                FallenEco: I’m sure something could be done with a corporate Sin-Eater krewe; but again, it’s not something that says “Orpheus” to me. It does say “tier three krewe”, though.
                StSword: true enough. Again, not “Orpheus”; but as you say, there’s already precedent for part-time ghosts.
                To me, “Orpheus” needs to be a corporation that seeks to turn a profit by collaborating with ghosts; and it has technologies to empower its agents to interact with ghosts via projection. It ought to be presentable as a patron for protagonists, so it can’t be too overtly exploitative; but as with any Hunter org, it ought to have a dark side that allows it to be suitable as an antagonist as well. So shades of potential exploitation are reasonable — just don’t make them overt or their primary mode of operation.
