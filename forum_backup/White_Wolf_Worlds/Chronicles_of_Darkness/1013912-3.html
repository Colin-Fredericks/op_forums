                        Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                        Punch drunk doesn't work if you're at all aggravated anyway, because you can't pay the cost of upgrading it to keep your last health box. The merit's fine.
                        Um... Did I miss a rule that says you can't spend willpower when you have all but one box filled with aggravated damage? Because otherwise you can pay for it just fine. As a hyperbolic example of what's wrong with Punch Drunk, I present McBane's typical day:

                        McBane finally has enough evidence to bring Senator Mendoza to justice. All he has to do now is testify at his trial. Unfortunately for him, Mendoza is not about to let that happen and has hired a hit-man to take McBane out before he gets there.

                        Our scene opens in the middle of the day as the McBane gets into his car, which is parked at the top of a 10 floor parking garage. McBane puts his key in the ignition, turns it, and surprise - car bomb. The bomb is +5 damage with 6 force and McBane is at ground zero. It rolls five successes on the force dice for a total of 5 aggravated and 5 lethal. Normally this would completely incinerate McBane as he has only 8 health... except McBane has Punch Drunk so the first 5 boxes are filled with aggravated, the next two are filled with lethal, two of the remaining 3 upgrades that lethal damage to aggravated, and the remaining damage just disappears as it can't fill in the last box and can't it can't upgrade anything else. This gives a total of 7 aggravated damage. McBane staggers out of his the remains of his car, or rather, there is suddenly no car around him anymore as it has been scattered over the parking garage.

                        The hit-man, a master sniper watching the car from a nearby rooftop through the scope of a 20mm anti-material rifle, is quite shocked to see McBane survive an explosion that would certainly have killed any normal human. Unfortunately for McBane, the hit-man has a backup plan: shooting McBane with a 20mm anti-material rifle. The hit-man rolls 5 successes on his attack and his rifle gives him +5 damage for a total of 10L. Normally this would be enough to completely blow someones brains out but since McBane has Punch Drunk, it does nothing (it can't fill in the last box because it is lethal and it can't upgrade anything else so it just goes away).

                        The hit-man is dumbfounded - his target somehow survived being at ground zero of a car bomb followed immediately by being hit directly in the head with a 20mm round. He decides to try again and this time gets a whopping 15 successes for 20 damage. Yet even this tremendous hit gets the exact same results: McBane completely ignores something that should have blown a gaping hole through an important bit of anatomy.

                        McBane meanwhile decides that being hit repeatedly in vital bits of his anatomy with high-powered bullets is slightly annoying so he is going get off the roof of the parking garage. But he has a dilemma as he doesn't want to walk all the way down 10 floors and the elevator is sooo slow. So he does what any rational human being would do in his situation: he jumps off the side of the parking garage. Assuming he reaches terminal velocity McBane takes 10L - and shrugs it off because of Punch Drunk.

                        McBane then picks himself up off the asphalt (he landed in the street itself which will be important in a second) and is promptly hit by a semi (size 25) going at 60 mph. The truck rolls 10 damage on its size dice for a total of 13L damage. Which McBane ignores because he is woozy from head trauma (the meaning of being punch drunk).

                        Picking himself up off the pavement. McBane realizes that he is going to be late for court! Hijacking the truck he rushes across town in a high-speed chase that is not relevant here.


                        So yeah. Punch Drunk is great if you want over the top action but it's not in keeping with the tone of the rest of the material, which is horror. As another example consider teens at a isolated camp being picked off by a slasher: the slasher is not very scary (or effective) if it takes them 6+ turns of combat to kill each teen. Also consider a hunter game: That superstrong vampire looks pretty weak if it takes them 6+ turns to kill each hunter (who meanwhile gang up and curb stomp the bastard). Or consider a military game set in the Winter War: Simo Häyhä is perhaps the greatest sniper in history, but even he wouldn't be able to take out a target with Punch Drunk in less that 6+ turns.
