                        Cities are not their tourist spots alone. You want to incorporate them, fine-I capitalized a shit ton on Paris as the city of Love and Lights in my own pursuits on the subject(And, worth noting, I consider mine the least of the four cities). But a city is not their tourist spots alone. There are deeper, weirder, more interesting settings beyond The Strip, the French Quarter, and stereotypes about Japanese culture, and we should always be willing to explore them.

                        For fuck's sake, the major reason people love the Tokyo setting as it's been developing is how much it does not play to the pop-culture conception of Japan. If we can do that with that nation, we can aspire to more for others, particularly(what I imagine is the case here) our own.

                            • Krewe of Carnivale: Court emotion - Celebration, court colors - green, Inauguration Day - Mardi Gras
                            • Krewe of Zydeco: Court emotion - Nostalgia, court colors - purple, Inauguration Day - Zydeco Festival
                            • Krewe of Hallows: Court emotion - Faith, court colors - gold, Inauguration Day - All Saint's Day
                            And here's your Bulwark:
                            At the end of the Faithful time, the New Orleans' Freehold begins its new year at Mardi Gras. It is on this night that the Freehold eschews the Quarter and the lights of the city, for their Contract has come due. The Fae come wearing graceful, wicked masques, and the Lost must put on a show. They make floats, gather beads, hone their talents, and make a parade through the Hedge, marking their territory with every throw and every shout and every footfall. This event must be perfect each time, a culmination of a year's worth of planning and practice, for if they falter in even the slightest they shall fall before the next year's end.

                            A bit rushed on the flavor-text, more than I'd like, but it's not my setting or my players that I'm trying to satisfy. At this point, it's not even the OP's.
