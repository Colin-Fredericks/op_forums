    GM new to WtF and storyteller system with a number of questions

    07-05-2018, 04:56 AM
    Hail and well met! I'm looking to start running a werewolf game when my groups current DnD 5e game finishes up, and I have a few questions to people who know the game system and the setting a little better before I start putting hard preparation into my Ideas. I don't think these questions are simple enough for the 'Ask a simple question' thread, but if i'm just being thick I'll go ask it there instead.

    1. What would be a reasonable shorthand for Poor, Average, Good for dice pools, health and such for off the cuff improvisation? E.g. if they fight a random spirit/person/etc, how big a dice pool should I give it to attack them/other actions if its a weakling, average spirit-joe, elite or godly?

    2. The group is looking to have 6 players, should i have concerns for PC niche protection given there are 5 werewolf auspices (and to a lesser extent tribes)? Should I just let them do whatever or encourage doubling on certain auspices, or any other constraints?

    3. What are some good ways to teach the system's core mechanics through play in the 1st session? This will be the groups 1st experience with the storyteller system and werewolf, so I was thinking doing a NPC guided 1st hunt and then finding their pack totem would be good to teach hunting, combat, a bit of spirit politics and such, are there any other key mechanics I should cover and good ways to teach them?

    4. How available should I make Essence to the players? I would like to let the players have fun with their cool spirit powers rather than make them be too stingy with their resources, what are the consequences for me running the game if I make essence not too difficult to acquire?

    5. in worldbuilding, how should i approach population density in my city of things like loci, enemy, other factions, etc. My setting is a capital city of the state, but a population of only a bit over 1.7 million people.

    6. again for worldbuilding, In a small city/backwater, what would the theoretical consequences of having just 1 extremely powerful but beleaguered forsaken (Rahu) claiming the territory before the PCs enter as a just after their first change? e.g. for spirits, the pure, hosts, claimed...

    Thank you in advance!

      1. You could use the Brief Nightmare rules from the CofD core for that. It's format is almost exactly the thing you're looking for.

      2. The "Faces" section of each Auspices' writeup in Werewolf 2E core has three archetypes for each Auspice, so some Auspice overlap should be fine. Add the Auspice-Tribe combinations and individual Gift rosters, and you should be fine.

      4. Broadly speaking, Essence is easier to get the more werewolf-y the characters act. Without changing existing rules, characters who want to use their spirit powers more will slowly become less human in attitude, which seems to be what the rules intend. Making Essence more common diminishes this aspect.

      Aaand I leave the rest to the more experienced.

              1. As 21C Hermit said, the Chronicles book has a quick antagonist builder. A random character has a 'Best' Dicepool of 5 for things it's really good at, a Worst of chance roll, and 2 for all other pools. A lone terror, meant to be a more powerful individual, would have more.

              2. Having multiples of the same auspice isn't bad, it can really multiply the tactics available at your disposal plus changes the dynamic when people are split up. Plus they're not guaranteed to have all the same powers at the same time. Here is an alternate Moon Gift for the Cahalith Auspice, if you do want to expand things. And obviously you're free to make your own and I'm pretty sure there's a few more homebrewed on the forums.

              3. I've found one of the better ways is to start the game with a hunt. As someone put well and I'm mangling, werewolves traditionally have great combat abilities and deal with problems that combat can't always fix. Starting them able to go 'all out' and use their biggest damage dicepools lets you see their capabilities (so you can plan for later) but also gives them some semblance of their own strength, which is impressive and can encourage players to act accordingly. Plus it's a place where Gifts get thrown about, a rite is performed, dicepools with modifiers come into play (all out combat, Willpower), and you'll have some Harmony rolls afterward. Depending on the enemy, other things come into play, with spirit targets opening up the ephemeral entity rules.

              4. Werewolves are fairly good with Essence efficiency for the most part. You might suggest they capture a locus first, or have their first hunt be about making it more accessible, either justifying the Personal Locus Merit or occasional visits to the place to charge up when necessary. Loci are key points in a territory, and having access to them would be something werewolves would do, like humans do with water and internet access.

              5. I wouldn't have werewolf population or loci number correlate to the human population (otherwise you can limit yourself, or just make a boring story because you're not allowing for variance, Elfers might have a human population of less than 100 but it's a sacred place to one of the Firstborn, who created a Secondborn there, so there's like 30 werewolves and they always keep it clear of hosts). Flesh out some starting packs that they're likely to encounter of different attitudes (having one seem antagonistic but just being socially rough is always fun for getting off on the wrong foot). Depending on the size of the area you would want a decent amount of loci, but have it so that most will require some sort of negotiation or combat to really gain access to.

              6. They would be tired and depending on things go might be overworked. A lone wolf is an easy target, but even then the things that would hunt a lone werewolf, especially an extremely powerful one, are rare. Things like a powerful host, spirit or claimed, or werewolf pack, like the Pure. It seems like it would be an easy way to introduce a first 'big' antagonist. None of them are going to care too much about the humans in the area, except for using them as potential shields or slaves. A spirit or claimed might have spirit minions it could have claim humans, a host could summon allies to take over the people, spy on the werewolf. The Pure might kidnap them and use them as leverage, or turn them against the werewolf so they can strike when he's distracted (killing people and making it look like he did it). Any of those seem like it could have things that could impact the PCs later, like one having a First Change when witnessing the werewolves fight or another new werewolf's loved one becoming puppet for a spirit assassin.

                  5. Lots of people means the gauntlet is thick, spirits are abundant and usually of the conceptual variety, any loci that form would be guarded by powerful (as in important, not necessarily as in high ranking) spirits, but background essence would also be plentiful. Enemies, such as hosts should be scattered across the city, hosts can feed on humans, spirits and uratha after all.

                  6. If the territory is claimed by just a single Uratha, he would need to have very good relations with the spirit courts of his territory, possibly allied with other supernaturals of the area, etc. (I'm assuming that by consequences you mean that the pack is there to replace him?) Consequences would probably be the distrust of local spirits, Pure spies (they don't know who the newcomers are, so they'd likely gauge them for weaknesses or chances of gaining fresh recruits), possibly a bit more cautious hosts...

                          Give them human issues and humans to interact with. Chances are, they will still need some kind of job to pay bills, so there is that. Their neighbors are human, most of their past relationships will have been human.

                          Home is Where the Bones Are is an Actual Play with newly changed Werewolves. You can see how elements of their human lives (mother in another city, football games) still tie the young wolves to their humanity.

                          I'd say - pick what you need for your story, then figure out how it is justified. A University probably has one Locus. But what if it is instead empty of them, when it should have at least one? What if it has multiple in such a small area - is there a network, or many potent spiritual phenomena? Or do they war among each other in the Shadow, using students and faculty as proxies? For Hosts - keep the numbers small and background, then ramp them up when you need a big threat.

                          Sounds fine. Some suggestions (which you can take, leave, or modify as you choose):
                          • For the Fire-touched Pastors, have them each in a similar position in their own Packs, but have them hate each other. They spend more time warring with the others and trying to poach their flock then causing trouble (usually). They lets you play them against another, and keep them as a background threat, with one attaining supremacy or there being an alliance as a Big Event if you need.
                          • For the Elder Forsaken, maybe you could pursue a story similar to the myth/legend of Urfarah? Old Wolf mentors the PCs, but it eventually becomes clear they are getting old/weaker. The PCs need to choose whether to prop them up, or depose their old mentor to take their place (socially, enforced with Spirit Rites, etc. as needed).
                          • Keep an open mind about which of the local nasties/creatures the PCs might recruit into their extended Pack - this will help cement them into the local area and power structure, and demonstrate that a new approach (their Pack) can improve things (local Spirits and monsters as allies, instead of merely in a non-aggression pact).
                          Some questions:
                          • What other supernaturals are on the council? Anything specific, or just "they exist" and you can draw on them as needed? Are you going to try and reference their specific rules, use ST fiat, or model them with CofD Dread Powers (like Hunter) or Werewolf powers (with Gifts and Rites)?
                          • Other than the Idigam, what are some other major events that have happened in/shaped the area?

                              Well, the flesh is relevant more than necessarily human. But tying werewolves to humans you have flesh Touchstones, the territory and extended pack (you generally have a number of humans in your pack) as well as the fact that many of the monstrous antagonists like to use humans as puppets in one way or another. The Hisil is influenced by what's going on in the Gurihal (flesh world) so ignoring one is just going to cause problems in another, even for the most spirit-bound wolf.

                              Following on from the locus questions, what kind of density should there be for spirit courts and hosts? Is it fair to say a University, city parkland/river system, and a large shopping district will say have a significant spiritual presence, while a given office or apartment building may have a smaller ecosystem of more minor spirits?
                              Yeah, though it might have some prime spots, a place where a family has lived since the beginning, the secret dwelling of a murderer, a 'cursed' room where something significant happened to one of their builders and its spirit lingers on, haunting current tenants.

                              Hosts also seem to be a somewhat scarce affair, with either a few individuals operating or a significant threatening infestation as a 1 off major antagonist?
                              If there's a lot of hosts, things are going to go very badly. You can see this in the fiction between the chapters. They really allow for stories at different 'levels' with just some scattered azlu or beshilu, some unique and manipulative individuals, or a whole legion of both working together alongside some juggernauts of verminous might. Like idigam they are prone to becoming very big problems, but unlike an idigam they also scale down well so you can deal with a single spider-monster or school infested with rats.

                              The plans sound good, you might check out Territories for information on some already setup areas, more fleshed out than the Hunting Grounds in the second edition core or Pack book.

                                  The Father wolf parallel is definitely something I will explore, though I'll leave hints rather than spell it out for them. He is largely neglecting the territory upkeep because he is taking care of his still alive but absolutely crippled and retired pack, and helping them keep the Idigam bound (they couldn't kill it, but can limit it's action). I would love for limited information and some paranoia leading the party to taking him down, followed by the Idigam which can retroactively erase knowledge about something to make it a secret only IT knows breaking free for them to deal with. I will give enough foreshadowing so it doesn't seem like a GM arse pull to punish them for killing gandalf.

                                  The supernatural council so far has a powerful vampire that ostensibly wants to leave the backstabbing politics of other cities and has come to this quiet city with a small brood he controls (willing to work together, but takes his pound of flesh), and a master mage in this quieter city to pursue his mysteries in peace while training 2 apprentices who are itching to prove themselves (1 apprentice will definitely be a spirit mage to cause some problems). Other than that, a few changelings, mortal occultists, a clued in police officer and such, potential allies but below the PCs weight class so will be naturally concerned about these new kids on the block. They largely view the elder uratha as the supernatural sheriff for the problems they cant solve themselves, and the elder will introduce the pack saying 'these guys deal with your problems from now on'.

                                  Regarding werewolves I want to say that after settlement there were limited werewolves in the area, where the pure (primarily fire touched, predator kings heading to the outback and there being nothing enticing for the ivory claws) and forsaken could coexist with limited deadly conflict due to ample space in territory. This ultimately changed when the Idigam landed, coalesced, and started being a problem, humans, spirits and werewolves disappearing from memory going noticed with the empty space they used to fill, both sides blaming the other and heading into conflict. By the time the Idigam was discovered most werewolves had fled the city, disappeared or been killed in pure vs forsaken clashes, with only the Elder werewolves pack and the 3 pastor types remaining. This will leave the pastor types as knowledgeable about the Idigam and history of what happened, making them enticing to speak with.

                                  Other significant events, up until the 1830s the area of Australia was unsettled by westerners (likely more like the Red Centre sample hunting ground), then was established as a non-convict colony by the British. I want to say the upheaval of an entirely new civilisation settling will have created an alien shadow that the indigenous uratha abandoned to the westerners in a 'this is your mess, deal with it' kind of way'. The only spirits to really survive this construction will be the River spirits (the Torrens and it's subsidiaries), that run through the city of Adelaide, and largely under. One problem will be the river spirits demanding tribute, threatening to burst the underground constraints of the subsidiaries that run under houses, businesses and schools.

                                  Adelaide was constructed on a grid system, very orderly, so I think that will play a significant role in the shadow with some arcane spirit laws. Recently the new hospital has been built to replace the old hospital servicing the city, and it was designed hilariously not for function causing no end of problems, so I would expect great turmoil between the spirits of the old hospital and those of the new, trying to migrate there where there are both not enough resources and new contenders, while the old hospital stands currently unused awaiting some plan for redevelopment.

                                  Regarding hosts, with few active wolves I think Azlu individuals would be happy to bide their time and develop themselves in the areas, while the Beshilu are likely somewhat active around the river (there is a sizeable rat population). However they act knowing if they make too much noise, the Elder will come down on them hard and that direct confrontation is a no go.
