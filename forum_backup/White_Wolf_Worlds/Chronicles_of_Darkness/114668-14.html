                                        Originally posted by Thorbes View Post
                                        That's a better example. If what you are aiming for is beyond normal human capacity, then I will err on not being a sin. On the other hand it is dificult to adjudicate if something is truly beyond human skill.
                                        It also depends on the action you are evaluating in the "just as well" part. Specifically, if you are viewing this in terms of raising a skill or sculpting a statue. If you view the Stat boost as part of sculpting, it could fall under being a sin, as you are you using magic for something you could do mundanely (sculpt a statur). On the other hand, if you view it as two seperate actions ("boost skills" then "sculpt statue") I would rule that there is no Wisdom penalty, as there really is no "just as well" from going to mortal dexterity and crafting skill to the peak of human ability (or higher with gnosis 6+) in a matter of minutes-hours.

                                        I also think you should seperate quality/outcome as a qualifier though, especially in a storytelling system. A mundane mortal could theoretically achieve a divine result with enough luck on a roll, just as a mage with the skills of a god fail miserably if the dice don't fall his way. While both are unlikely scenarios, I don't think it is fair to punish morality based on the outcome of a die roll (at least one that isn't for morality/derangement that is).
