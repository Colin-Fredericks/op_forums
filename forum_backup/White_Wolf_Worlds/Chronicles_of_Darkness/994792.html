    Tremere 2e

    10-28-2016, 04:40 PM
    I've been giving some thought for how to make the Tremere a Bloodline, and I've come up with some stuff. This is assuming a world where magic-users exist, but they aren't quite Awakened, but it isn't a necessity since nothing I have is particularly dependent on Mage existing or not existing.

    So, they're a universal Bloodline, but Mekhet and Ventrue are the most likely to enter in. My idea for Gift is that they can potentially learn any mortal sorcery, Theban Sorcery, or Cruac spell regardless of Covenant (they still have to pay the merit cost, though, and they can't take on minor templates, use psychic powers, or perform sorcery that requires a patron or power source without access to that patron or power source), and they have a series of Devotions related to magic. Sensing it, defending against it, etc.

    Their weaknesses are the one from Masquerade, and I'm also thinking they either need to consume human souls or commit diablerie to use their powers, and because of this they're widely hated and distrusted, meaning most Tremere have to keep their Bloodline hidden.

    Their gift sounds kind of overpowered, but I'm trying to balance it out. For one thing, they still need to shell out experience points for their spells. They have a very wide menu of things they can learn, but they don't have any more merit dots to spend than any other vampire. And then there's their weakness. The original Masquerade weakness was kind of infamous for not being that big of a drawback, and a lot of STs made up for it by emphasizing the restrictive, conspiratorial nature of the clan and how no one else liked them. I kind of codified that with the "widely hated and feared" thing, and I also added soul eating to tie them to Mage's Tremere, which helpfully gives them yet another drawback, and one that provides a good reason why other vampires don't trust them. It isn't just "they're jealous of their power" or "they're weirdos", it's that very few Princes are going to want a vampire that has to eat souls running around their city.

                                              As something worth pointing out, the OP has shown a penchant for getting super-tied to terms and ideas that are, to put it as nicely as possible, not supported by the game lines. Do please note that it makes no nevermind to me that Geckopirateship's status is 'banned'... But that said, I don't find a Tremere bloodline as useful in the game as House of Tremere as a Covenant that contrasts – and necessarily pushes to the fore – an apparent contradiction within the Ordo Dracul. That is, I'd offer up a Tremere Covenant as mystics who use (mechanically) similar tools as the more scientifically-minded Dragons.

                                              In my version, the Ordo Dracul are built on ancient notions that Dracula didn't so much INVENT as he codified and made useful to the Kindred as a means of transcending their common curse – something supported to varying degrees within came 'canon'. Around the time of Vlad's Embrace, another group of Kindred used those same principles as the departure point for exploring the latent powers hidden in the Vitae for more personal use. Whereas the Defiant seek to push the boundaries of the un-dead experience for purposes of becoming MORE that mere Kindred, these Tremere use similar applications to make themselves the GREATEST of Kindred. It's a fine point, I concede, but I see it as the former being scientists who dabble in the occult, whilst the latter are mystics who sometimes use rational study to reach their goals. For me, it removes that 'squishy' tension between the study elements (e.g. experiments, defining Coils and Scales, the veneration of Prince Vlad's own explorations) and the mystical seeking for Dragon Nests and 'magical' thinking (especially as found in the Ordo Dracul book). So, why the Tremere?

                                              First, as they were presented in old Vampire: the Masquerade, their strict hierarchy forces them to work as a group, even as they are by nature a bunch of self-serving power grubbing wanks. Maybe they keep that pyramid imagery or instead fancy themselves a 'Black Academie', but as a group of Kindred seeking personal power, such a structure is vital to keep something like peace – not to mention is fair well oozes with conspiracy theory seeds when one considers the how and why of such structures. Second, the Tremere 'paradigm' has built-in nomenclature for their works; that is, their versions of the Mysteries are called Paths, their Coils are called Ways (hat tip to Masquerade Koldunic Sorcery), and their Scales are instead Sendings. Mechanically their 'Thaumaturgy' looks like the Rites of the Dragon 'with the serial numbers scratched off', but its Paths are based more on elemental and fundamental themes – think primal sources of power akin more to elemental EFFECTS like volcanoes and floods than to the 'classical' elements themselves – that come across in allegorical forms rather than objective, 'throw a fireball' sort of expressions. Magic IS an art to them! It's Dracula's followers who wanna be scientists. Needless to say, these Tremere see the pursuit of POWER as their only core belief, leading them to regard the Sanctified and Acolytes – who practise 'lesser magics' of faith – as fledgling babes afraid to leave a safe nest of empty promises.

                                              ...I dunno if any of this makes sense to anyone else, but it's my take. I have written a lot about this version of the House of Tremere Covenant, but my troupe has scattered since Vampire: the Requiem Second Edition came out, meaning there's been no playtest or even serious discussion on the idea so far. It's a very 'niche' viewpoint, too, I know. That notwithstanding, I very much like the portrayal of Dracula-as-scientific-thinker in the game, but his bouts with mysticism as shown in Rites of the Dragon felt at odds with this. I mean, I didn't want Mage: the Awakening to be the ONLY line in this game milieu to have Tremere!

                                                  I actually liked this take of yours, Phaolan, though I would add some notes.

                                                  The mysticism mixed in the Dragons' work doesn't bother me because it clearly isn't such a clear distinction in setting. The "hermetic mysticism" is one of the first expressions of the naturalistic philosophy that created modern science, it was a natural side-effect of a scientific thinking alongside a superstitious thinking, that by its side is a natural development of the human brain, specially among too many unexplained phenomena. Such things still exist a lot today IRL, in a setting with so many mysteries it is even more likely. But at the core, the Dragons are still scientific-minded to a great degree.

                                                  About the Tremere themselves, I think there is, in fact, a lot of inspiration that can be taken from the cWoD history of Clan Tremere, including their previous time as House Tremere. Another take that could be used could be a link between this House Tremere and the Awakened, to some degree.
