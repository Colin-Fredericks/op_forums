    Books you would like to see?

    02-23-2014, 04:58 AM
    I'd bet money that Hunter: the Vigil is the most popular limited-run game, behind Changeling. While there's nothing beyond Mortal Remains on the schedule for 2014 (which is cause enough for celebration), I have my fingers crossed for it to grab enough attention to justify more books in 2015. Hunter's a very versatile game, and could easily support a further run of supplements. What would you like to see?

    At the very top of my list is a book like Ancient Mysteries/Ancient Bloodline, one that gives snapshots of the Vigil across the ages. As long as there's been humanity, monsters have preyed on man, and hunters have stood to oppose them. Throw out five or six small settings and show off some Compacts and Conspiracies that no longer exist in the modern day. A Vigil for Rome portion with a focus on the Aves Minerva and some monsters that are extinct in modern nights, and do the same for any other time that stands out.

    Mortal Remains might address this, but a small book with a focus on monster-building would be wonderful. Lots of new Dread Powers and templates for things far stranger than the usual critters that have their own games about them. This would be as good a place as any to potentially throw in aliens or more Christian demons, along with Lovecraftian beasties and their cults.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

      Most of the books I want to see I consider writing myself, however.

      I don't have anything against fighting monsters, but I've always been more interested in Vigil as an investigative / social game. There's support for this, Null Mysteriis, but I want more. So the first book I'd like to see is Hunter: Bread Winners.

      This is a book about Hunters with normal lives, and something in that normal life convinced them to see out the supernatural. Joe Average knows monsters exist but he pretends they don't, like most people in the World of Darkness, then one day he discovers the factory is about to be offshored, he stops pretending the monsters aren't real and looks for a magical solution. What will it cost, and what will the Union Cell in his factory (that he knows nothing about) think?

      A chapter on storytelling: why would an average person decide (decide is key, IMO the difference between a Hunter and a blue book mortal is that the Hunter could so easily have ignored the supernatural but at a crucial moment they decided not to) to seek out magic to solve an issue in regular life. Then advice on how to build cool stories for this. Some basic talk about various solutions, but mostly a stop-gap if you don't have other books that cover the monsters in more details.

      A chapter on mechanics, I'm thinking of diving into the Professions. I'm thinking of advice on what they can do with their contacts, skills, and maybe a Merit or Tactic that's based on their training.



      The second book I'd like to see is Witch Finders, done right. Unlike the Awakened (who are different on a metaphysical level) most Witches are just humans with magic. I'd like too really see that, for example The Union sees Witches as "immediate trouble, propsoened trouble, never friends". I want to see friends, what happens when a local witch (with no combat experience but a decent reputation) knocks on The Unions door and asks for help with a summoning gone wrong? What happens when the Union's community links includes it's witches? What do the Union think of Witches joining a cell, or hunters learning magic. I want to see questions like that answered given equal weight to "why do the unions shoot witches".

      You could do something like that for other monsters but Witches, being human, are the one where it's needed the most.



      Third and most importantly, Hunter: Cults. In fact I'm so enamoured with the concept of this book that a part of me wants to call this Hunter: The Cult Chronciles. Cults is about the following:

      Providing low level antagonists. If you look at Vampire, even a low level Vampire is a serious threat to a Cell of The Union. Cults provides smaller threats that Hunters can face on an even footing, humans with minor blessings from their gods or even no power at all - it by no means the complete picture on low level antagonists but it's a good start.

      Make organisations into organisations. It's more a feeling than a fact, but I get the impression that a typical Tier 3 Hunter game still results in PCs acting like a Tier 1 Cell. They have nifty endowments, and sometimes allies or information, but mostly they're perusing their own Vigil in their own way. Cults will contain advice and mechanics for playing part of a large organisation.

      Complete the picture on morality. Lets face it, Slasher was one of the best books in the WoD. Hunters have always had moral ambiguity and a slide to sociopathy in the corebook thanks to The Code, Slasher completed the picture by showing what's at the bottom of that slide. The moral slide of Conspiracies is also in the corebook, it's time to show what lies at the bottom there too.

      Stuff to investigate. Cults will create lots of sinister secrets and tempting immoral magic, all of which can be used by regular humans. It's one thing investigating a vampire's magic, it's another investigating a cultists magic and knowing that you could steal those secrets yourself. Plus, since cultists are human you really should investigate more than just enough to spot the fangs.

      Less importantly, Gods. Three of the six core Conspiracies are explicitly religiously themed, two of the four supplementary ones also have religious themes. Gods, mythology and religion is extremely important in Hunter, and Cults will give advice for actually including the Gods themselves in your Hunter game.

      “There are no rules. Only Principles and natural laws.” - Promethius

              I think these two are both essential.

              Regarding the first point: barring the rare zombie invasion or a truly high-power chronicle, many Hunter games take the shape of "bad thing happens, investigation, fight against individual powerful monster." Some exciting options for less asymetrical opponents would be very welcome. Cults make for an exciting dash of moral grey; Hunters are, at the heart of things, vigilantes with a cause. Does that really entitle you to kill people who are misguided, desperate, or brainwashed?

              The latter is something I really crave. Many of the Tier 3 groups are potential globe-spanning, and yet the average cell is left to their own devices other than someone passing down new Endowments every once in a while. Feeling like a part of a larger, cohesive body is often missing, and something I really enjoy in play. A focus on making sure your VALKYRIE squad has a home base to report to and debriefings to put up with, having your Lucifuge cell make yearly visits to Milan, or your Cheiron acquisitions team filing reports with their supervisor at the tail end of the week would all be most welcome. The greatest asset gained by joining a Compact or Conspiracy is organization, information, and resources; you have people to call on for help, at the cost of following orders and being held responsible for your actions. Stressing this would be really cool.

              Addendum: Something on non-Endowment assets for Tier 2 and 3 play would be really great. Sure, your VALKYRIE cell has Aetheric Rounds in their MP5s, but can they requisition a helicopter or a back-up team? The Union is bound to make friends around town; perhaps one of them is a no-questions-asked surgeon or can get them illegal firearms? This doesn't necessarily have to be enforced by mechanics, but just Storyteller guidelines for making Tier 2 and 3 play feel that much more powerful than just being a lone cell.
