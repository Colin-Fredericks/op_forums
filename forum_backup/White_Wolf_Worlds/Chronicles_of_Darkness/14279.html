    How Cover developed

    11-19-2013, 05:07 PM
    I originally posted on the KS page, but thought it might be interesting to talk about it here, as well.

    (A lot more details on the mechanics are available in the full-text preview. If you haven't checked it out, it's on the <project page on Kickstarter>.)

    One of my favorite aspects of Demon is Cover, and I’d like to talk a little bit about how we developed it. Cover is your character’s false life, the lie they tell both to the people in their lives and to reality itself.

    The idea of the double (or triple) life is a compelling one, because it leads right back to questions we all ask ourselves. Who am I, really? Am I actually the person the people who love me see? These are great questions to ask about our characters in games, too -- and in a game, we might get more definitive answers. (As a side note, I recommend Vonnegut’s Mother Night as a really interesting look at these questions, appropriate to a Demon game in a low key way.)

    Cover was a fairly early part of the Demon proposal. After years of developing Vampire, and exploring the link between the Masquerade and Humanity, I was interested in a game that essentially conflated the two -- that measured your “integrity” by how well you pulled off the big lies.
    So it entered the initial bible as a simple concept. Cover was the false life you took with you when you first escaped God. It replaced Integrity (which at this point was still called Morality). It could sometimes be used to resist supernatural effects, as the demon essentially blends too well to be affected by the uncanny.

    As we started to build the mechanics, though, we had to ask some more questions. How do you get Cover back? Can you get a new one? At the same time, we were looking for what demons got out of soul pacts. That’s where the magic started to happen.

    What if, one of the writers asked, you could build your own life out of the pieces of other people’s? Bargain with them for things they might not even want, but which are like diamonds for a fallen angel seeking a place in the world? And then... what if you could just assume another person’s identity entirely?
    What if the point wasn’t bargaining for the soul, but for the box it came in? One by one, these questions led to the pact system, a unique way in which demons both exploit and relate to human beings.

    And then, more questions. If I’m so good at pretending to be somebody, why can’t the universe back me up from time to time? Give me a little boost to pretending to be who I need to be? And from there sprang Legend.

    I like Cover because it allows us to ask some hard questions about our characters, while at the same time being immensely gameable. You don’t just ask “who am I and who do I want to be?”; the game mechanics encourage you to engage with it. And that can lead to some really cool emotional moments at the table.

                  One of the most low-key and yet poignant events in my Demon playtests had to do with Cover. Two of the characters ended up working very well together and grew somewhat close. Or as close as two characters can get over the course of two or three game sessions.

                  Anyway, some events happened in game where they had to investigate a place but didn't want to be seen to do so, so they both switched Covers, and told each other about it. That sharing of "Hey, this is the person I am when I'm not this person you usually know" turned out to be very poignant at the table.

                                      Agreed. Cover is a really fun part of the game--when they hadnt leaked much else and said the morality stat was called Cover, i wasn't sure.. but it really adds good roleplaying incentive and reigns in demon's activities, while also playing up the amoral liar aspect. Cover took center stage in my mummy crossover. The mummy had finally realized that he was seduced and brainwashed by an angel who was under the false cover of "his wife". The conflict between the wife he thought he was in love with and the idea that she was lying to him the whole time made for some extremely emotional moments (like when he remembered his wife killing him for remembering in his last life). But the Cover then became more interesting when she fell--as the cover effectively rewrites into reality as "real", by chosing to fall and be with him, all the lies she had fed him became "true", and he could enjoy all those memories with her as though she really HAD always been his wife.
