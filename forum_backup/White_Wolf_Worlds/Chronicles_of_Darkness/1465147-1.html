    Originally posted by Newb95 View Post
    The storyteller reasoning was that, rules aside, taking a point blank shot to the back of your head would basically blew the top part of your skull off and he said that a werewolf in hishu form, which is basically a human with sharper senses and a bit of regenerative power, could not come back from that.
    First up, the ST can rule as they please for their game, and if that’s the way they want to play it, sure.

    But, if we’re going by what Uratha are and the rules for them, no. A Uratha in hishu is in no way “basically a human with sharper senses”. It’s a werewolf that happens to be wearing its human-form disguise at the moment. Werewolf first and always, the form that it happens to be in doesn’t take that away.

    Werewolf regeneration isn’t pretty, either. Blow the back of a Uratha’s head off with a shotgun and it’ll fall to the ground. Then, when its Essence healing kicks in, tendrils of blood and brain will reach out to each other. Disgusting slurping sounds happen. The gore pulls fragments of skull back together and then drags them back to what’s left of the head. Weird grinding sounds occur as jagged bone bits adjust themselves like a self-assembling jigsaw, trying to work out how they fit back together. And when most of it’s done, flesh and bone start growing into the gaps, leaving some splatter on the ground.

    The player argued that werewolves are supposed to have the best regeneration of the whole CoD and even in human form, he should have just jumped up in garou form and heal everything minus the aggravated.
    Yeah, pretty much this.

    One thing thats really not clear on the core book is the regeneration of lethal wounds, the book states that &quot;Uratha heal their regeneration rate in of lethal damage every 15 minutes. By spending a point of Essence as a reflexive action, she may regenerate lethal damage instead of bashing damage for the turn.&quot;, what does it mean with their regeneration rate? does it mean that they heal all damage every 15 minutes like in garou form? also, can they spend essence while they are unconscious?
    Uratha heal a certain amount of bashing damage each turn based on the Primal Urge trait. If they spend Essence they instead regenerate this amount of lethal damage in the turn. If they just find somewhere to hide and lay low, they still regenerate bashing each turn, and every 15 minutes they regenerate 1 level of lethal damage without having to spend Essence.

    You can run a Uratha down with a truck, back up over the pieces a few times, but leave them lying in the gutter for a few hours and they’ll be back up like nothing happened. (Well, not quite like nothing happened. You now have a pissed off werewolf looking to hunt you down for payback.)

    Answering that last point - can they spend Essence while unconscious? For reflexive things like healing, yes.

        Originally posted by Tessie View Post

        Even if they fall unconscious?
        Yes.

        Let me expand on this further. The character isn’t (shouldn’t be) unconscious when they choose to spend Essence. Spending Essence on regeneration is reflexive, which can be done at any time. With injury, if the track fills with bashing you roll Stamina to remain conscious. Nothing requires the character to wait until the outcome of that roll to overcharge their regeneration. Being unconscious from massive damage is a bad state for a Uratha to be in, so the character reflexively spends the Essence before (possibly) failing the Stamina roll and falling unconscious. Then they regenerate at least one lethal box and wake up.

        Or, if they want, they spend that Essence point to reflexively shift to Gauru before the Stamina roll. They regenerate all lethal and no longer have to even bother with the Stamina roll.

                                Word of God aside, it won't get easier if you don't want to change the rules, but it isn't as impossible as you seem to think.

                                You're giving too much credit to a character that tried to off a werewolf just by sneaking alone with a shotgun. This is a loosing strategy for a hunter. A human vs splat fight isn't just hard, it is a human vs a monster. The human must compensate with teamwork, cunning and liberal exploration of the target's weaknesses.

                                If that shotgun had silver, even with reduced damage, everything would be aggravated. Even if the werewolf survived the encounter the wound would take too much time to heal. If the gunner had a good enough position to run and get a second shot, silver maybe would not be necessary. If there were three gunners, the fight would end up similarly.

                                From the hunter perspective it was a desperate, hopeless tactic, and yet was a close call. You want to compare it to a vampire? Instead of a single aggravated you would have a single lethal in the track and a pissed off predator healing with blood and seeing you as a box of juice to replenish the batteries. A Mage would solve the problem without going in to begin with.

                                Just let your players know that it isn't just a hard fight, it is a deathtrap, no matter the splat, it is to go against the very monsters of horror stories.

                                And you'll have plenty to work with for lesser opponents. If the human PCs are facing splats, they should interact much more with lesser cousins anyways. Werewolves are rarer than Wolf Blooded, and yet the later are already terrifying monsters for common people. Many encounters with"werewolves" are actually with them. But they're far more killable if push comes to shove.
