    Originally posted by Tome of the Mysteries p48-49, "Magic Versus Prometheans"
    The Created are alive — sort of. It’s an artificial life sustained by a mysterious type of energy that defies even the Prime Arcanum’s ability to fully perceive and manipulate.
    […]
    Being able to identify a Promethean by the strange fire burning within him is not the same thing as understanding that fire. Pyros eludes the scant scrutiny most mages have been able to apply to it, and so remains a curious exception to the array of forces, Essence or Mana-based energies cataloged by mages.
    […]
    The Promethean’s Azoth, the inner fire that animates him, cannot be targeted by Supernal magic, and so cannot be directly manipulated or altered by mages.
    "The Lone Watchtower" shows up as a phrase in four books, two from Promethean and two from Mage.

    It first appears in Mage 1e in the corebook as a wild theory.

    The first Promethean book it shows up in is the corebook, where it's still explicitly-theoretical and maybe the abode of the qashmallim in yet more wild theories.

    It next shows up as an incidental mention in Pandora's Book alongside all the other "what do [potent specimens of the Big Three supernaturals] see when they [finally disappear into legend]?" tidbits preceding the section on What The Principle Might Be.

    The last book it shows up in is Left-Hand Path, where it's postulated to be symbolized by the Elder Diadem.

    The only relevant places the phrase "lesson in humility" shows up are in Saturnine Night, where it's part of the writeup of Fredrich Hagen the clone-maker, and in Night Horrors: The Unbidden, where it's part of a sidebar about the False Awakening.

        I think that, combined with Wood Ingham's "New World of Gods and Monsters" article in Strange Alchemies ("A Promethean is a new challenge for the mystic. The mages think that they have access to all things, but the workings of the Pyros are completely alien to them. Try as they might, their spells won’t duplicate the feat. The power that drives the Created resists the mages’ efforts to steal and harness it.") and a handful of references to "scions of the Lone Watchtower" in connection to qashmallim, might be what I'm remembering. In particular, Tome of the Mysteries singles out Pyros as "a curious exception to the array of forces, Essence or Mana-based energies cataloged by mages." It's completely alien to them; their efforts to harness it are thwarted; the difficulty in scrutinizing it sets it apart from the other mystical energies mages have encountered. Yeah; I think that's how I got the impression that Pyros is beyond the scope of the Arcana.

        This was further reinforced years later by the curious exception in Imperial Mysteries that provided the Arcana needed to create and modify templates for every major gameline except Prometheans.

                                        What about places of power? Almost every splat has its places of power ("dragon nests", loci, hallows...) and all these places gives the "supernatural fuel" most of these splats uses (essence, mana, plasm...). What about if a group of Mages are interested in the Dragon Nest of an Ordo Dracul group because they say it is a Hallow and must be theirs, or a pack of werewolves and a group of thyrsus mages are defending a Locus from the machinations of a greedy human villain that wants to expropiate the land to build a skycraper for his corporation?

                                        It is common to see Mage vs Mage or Wolf vs Wolf fighting for Hallows and Loci, but what about stealing it from other splats (or sharing it? or defending it?). Could a place, say a graveyard, give Essense with death resonation and also Plasm? Or a peacefull forest give Mana and Essence resonating with calm spirits?

                                        Thinking about all the different types of places and fuels makes my head ache, and I am thinking of giving the world the same fuel but named different and taken on different manner depending on the splat, and not "fueling" equally everyone depending on the resonance of that fuel, each splat feeling comfortable with one place or another. It is only an idea, but since I read the concept of "resonance" I fell in love with it.

                                        It would be interesting to see this treated in the new book, and also the conflicts for places of power.
