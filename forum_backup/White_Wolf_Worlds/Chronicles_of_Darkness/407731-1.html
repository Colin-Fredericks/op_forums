    If you're going with GMC's updated social mechanics then there is ALREADY a method baked in to provide the PC's protection from social orders they don't want to comply with.

    Originally posted by God Machine Chronicles, pg 193
    Go With the Flow
    If the character does as requested and abides by the intended goal, his player takes a Beat (see p. 157).

    Offer an Alternative
    If the subject’s player chooses, he may offer a beneficial alternative and the initiator’s player can impose a Condition (see p. 180) on his character. This offer exists between players; it does not need to occur within the fiction of the game (though it can). The alternative must be beneficial and not a twist of intent. The Storyteller adjudicates.
    The initiator’s player chooses a Condition to impose on the subject. It must make sense within the context of the scenario.
    If you're using GMC opening doors -> social action complete in X doors you might as well use their "Do the thing, get a beat" mechanic as well. It's much better then "Do the thing or risk going insane."

    If you don't use Beats then just give a Willpower point instead and make offering an alternative cost a Willpower point. A net difference of 2 Willpower is enough to make players generally just go along with the request but gives the option to fight it and go for compromise when they feel strongly that their characters would never to "the thing."

            For me a roll of any sort should always have any outcome it can have be interesting and fun in some way. If winning or losing a dice roll would result in a super lame unfun outcome, don't roll.

            So, that brings us to social rolls. Same deal. Only roll if failure is a good fun possibility. For PCs influencing NPCs, it's the ST's choice, and I find it pretty easy to figure out. Just think, "could this go either way?" and "would either way be fun?" But for NPCs influencing PCs, you don't really know if the PC in question would find either result fun. So, consider it yourself, then ask. Say, "How about a roll to figure out if you buy this argument?" And if they agree, roll it and let the dice decide. If they don't ask why, and maybe all that's needed is are tweaked possible results.

                Well, if the problem is that players are trying to get back at NPCs that used social maneuvers (or even outright mind control powers) against them, then that's not really much of a problem.

                Players SHOULD be vulnerable to certain things. If a huge, scary thug intimidates them, it should have the chance to make them buckle, rather than do whatever they want. If they then feel indignant about their apparent moment of cowardice, and want to take revenge from a safe hiding place where they think they won't have to pay any consequence for doing so, then that's a pretty human reaction.

                Players might also be convinced of something by a con man, only to realize what a bill of goods they just got sold, and go for some subtle form of revenge, as well. Think of a child that thinks they have to have some random cheap toy sold at exorbitant prices, only to find it doesn't do anything they dreamed it would at the start. They throw the toy away as soon as they've played it once, and never think of it again.

                Outside of long-term brainwashing, there's good reason to just talk a player into doing something against their nature because their character is being talked into doing something against their nature, only to have them resent the action, and go back against the person who manipulated them as soon as they are outside the influence of the social pressures that forced them to crack.

                That said, it's perfectly fine to judge someone on how petty their spats of revenge are, as well, and impose clarity breaks if someone treats being talked into helping a buddy pack up their stuff and move as a personal insult that must be avenged with magical social humiliation.

                Originally posted by CasualMoth View Post
                I consider this a flaw of my GM’ing and I will change it somehow in the upcoming campaign. Having players act according to social rolls, let's say in GMC way would be quite interesting. They will know that the NPC works to open their Doors (Social Manoeuvring) and will be able to act accordingly. I will allow them to instantly fail opponents Door opening attempt, if they fall back to Clarity based denial. Depending on severity of this denial, I will add a Condition that penalizes their perception and social rolls until they role play it back to reality; AND in a serious case ask for an instant breaking point roll. If the NPC initiated some kind of negotiation that taken weeks and a few Doors were opened and the PC decides suddenly that HE is The Great Bear of the Hedge and mortal contracts do not apply to him, it will definitely cost them Integrity/Clarity (in this case it would be at least -3 BP roll).
                If the problem is one of perception then, really, why not just make your social doors the hit points of an alternative combat, thematically? If the problem is that they see combat and health boxes as the "real things to worry about", and don't see social rolls as serious because they're less structured, then just make those social doors into hit points in theme, and social combat a fully viable alternate form of combat. Talk about it with the same terms ("the insult slams into your gut, breaking another two of your doors, and the snickers of those around, enjoying watching you cut down from your lofty perch inflict a further condition that gives you a -2 to your next roll...") Describe those doors in terms of pain or desperation that mounts with each hit point they bleed out, or in the case of using honey rather than vinegar, just start outright being an unreliable narrator of events, so that the player doesn't even know about the downsides to things their character is being persuaded towards doing.

                There's no reason you can't have the party "face" have a life-or-death social duel with a rival over the fate of a Court's direction the same way they might have an honor duel with rapiers, but one of the big problems with a single Charisma stat and a "face" in many games is that it inherently means the others sit out. Organize social challenges like you organize fights, and let other players tackle different challenges. One player may be the "DPS" of a social battle, pushing your goals offensively with persuasion and manipulation, while another may be a "support", spotting the lies and tactics of the other side with empathy and wits, and the brute of the party blocks off enemy support with a swift intimidation roll to keep third parties that might support your opponents shut up in the sidelines.

                Make social maneuvers all but literal combat maneuvers, and play it up as strike and riposte, with strategies on either side. (To this end, I dislike the way that GMC treats door-opening as things that take place over weeks because of some stiff, arbitrary rules. The time scale should be whatever is appropriate to the drama.) Then, when they lose all their doors, you're justified in saying that you're taking their player out of their hands, the way that you can't play your character when unconscious or dead. They lost the fight, so they buckled, and aren't in control, anymore. Fear of that sort of loss of control should be more potent a motivator than an experience beat bribe, the way people don't screw around when the guns come out. If you make social doors apply to anyone (as it is a reflection of a more fragile state of mind) until "healed", then you should be able to complete the metaphor of hit points and combat, entirely.

                Think of it like how some RPGs have characters with high physical DEF but low magical MDF. Rather than tell your players that "they're not being serious role-players" when they don't invest in social skills, imply that they're leaving themselves vulnerable to different forms of attack or that they are going to be one-trick ponies that sit out the likely unavoidable social conflicts. You can easily frame problems for Changelings in situations where they can't simply punch the people they disagree with, like Court politics or better yet, dealing with the mundane human social power structures.

                Changelings, especially, are a type of being that thrives more on mental and social skills than the combat skills, anyway. A secret pried from your opponent might be more damaging than a cold iron spear, in the right hands. Forcing a broken pledge can be tantamount to homicide. Players should be willing to consider a stealthy spy with information-gathering skills or a tempter that gets their paramour to "willingly" hand over the knowledge of their greatest weakness just as much a fearsome combatant as a walking lion, and it's one of the few games where you honestly could just throw physical combat skills to the wind, and go completely with mental or social skills with no loss in effectiveness.

                    If you want to continue using social rolls and feel that the players are taking losing rolls as an "attack" so to speak and later seek revenge, simply take it one step further. When they take action against that person bring the weight of the Court down on them. No court monarch is going to think "but she outwitted me" or "he was a little off-putting" as an excuse to take action against another courtier. Would be wonderfully Changeling to then place your characters in the debt of the NPC.
