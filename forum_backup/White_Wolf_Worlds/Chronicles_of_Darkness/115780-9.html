            Originally posted by TheBadWolf View Post
            It's bad couse other part of group will go with target. Or don't let target go. I mean, werewolves must fight against something more than usuall Jason Voorhees' forder.
            TheBadWolf -You appear to be feeding your own concerns by focusing on white room scenarios without context.

            To use your example above - perhaps the werewolves are hunting a coordinated group that are not 'Jason Voorhees' fodder', as you you put it. So what? The Gift is still not useless, as you claim. How big is this group? Where are they located? What are they doing?

            Suppose we have a group of around 6 individuals. The Irraka uses it on one and that person tries to split off. What is the group going to do about it? Okay, they all follow him like you suggest - that means *all of them* have left whatever safe place they may have established. It also appears that all of them are now focused on trying to stay with an individual who is* clearly trying to get away from them*. That means he's not just wandering zombie-like while they slowly follow. This person wants to get away from his group, and he'll try to do that. He'll run, dart down side streets and alleys, shut doors behind him, tip over bins, make distractions. Whatever. He will try to get away and his group is now wasting its time trying to stay with him and not watching for the werewolves.

            Or the group 'doesn't let him go', as you suggest. How? He wants to go. He's not going to stop just because you ask. He will keep trying to separate. So what does the group do about it? If they're in a locked room he'll unlock it to escape. Will they restrain him? That's going to take at least one group member, maybe more, to wrestle him to the ground. Group members who are no longer watching their defences. They've got him on the ground, no what? He keeps struggling, and they have to stay on him. Or he pretends to come to his senses, and as soon as they let him go, he tries to escape again. So then what? They knock him out, or tie him up? That's one less defender for the group, and one more useless person they have to protect - if the location is compromised, someone has to carry the guy. That slows them down.

            Or let's say the group does follow the affected character and sticks together, however difficult. The Irraka targets a second character who is lured. Now the group splits in two . Both of those characters who are trying to get away from the group *will* go in different directions. They will separate the group. You now have two groups of three, and one person out of each of those three is actively trying to get away from the other two.

            And all of this assumes the Irraka *uses nothing except that one Gift*.

            You are making blanket statements about white room scenarios and not thinking through how your own examples would actually work in play. I believe that is causing your anxiety. Please don't worry, and understand that people have only seen a small slice of what it coming so far.

            Satchel - last attempt, I promise

                    Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                    Idigam is obviously themed around the idea of "the hunt" which is a great idea for getting powers more in line with what I feel is thematically "Werewolf" like. I have to ask though, with Gifts like Technology (though I do understand the idea of "removing the prey's tools" as a sorta way to tie it into hunting) and Elements how do you apply the theme of "the hunt"? As these were some of the powers I found most "un-Werewolf" like in the old book.
                    You've certainly hit on one of (many) challenges I was faced with when drafting up the new Gifts. At some point I'll try and bolt together a little design piece about some of the goals and design decisions I made on the wider level, but suffice to say that I was happy to have the lists of Facets spread their wings slightly into more shamanic or spiritual areas with some of the Gifts. This is especially true of Elemental, which is a slightly more experimental take on Gift structure than the others in Idigam.

                    At the end of the day, Technology is one of the core Gift lists, so I looked to concepts of dominance, depriving the prey of the tools they rely on and animistic interaction with the modern world. It's the Iron Masters' signature Gift and their tribal prey is the one that most relies on technology, so in many ways Technology is a Gift of exploiting the world of humans through the devices that they rely on.

                                        Originally posted by NeoTiamat View Post
                                        Color me unenthused.

                                        Well, no, that's not entirely fair. These are generally much better than the old Gifts. That said, two points/concerns:

                                        1) I'm saddened that we are still stuck with Renown, which seems like an unnecessary layer of complexity and an artificial speed bump in character development.
                                        2) The fact that the first gift I see is "perfect, unstoppable lie-detection" makes me weep. So basically... social shenanigans do not work if there's an Elodoth in the party. Lovely.
                                        On 1), I always found Renown a bit of a burden in the original W:tF system.                                                     I'm sure rituals will pick up that slack. And I wouldn't be surprised if there are shaman-y Gifts as well.                                                     Actually, Crescent Moon doesn't just let you see spirits at the first dot, it gives you quite a bit more

                                                    It's also worth noting that, by and large, Facets that are focused on the hunt work just fine against spirits too.

                                                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                                    A lot of this will come down to the expense of Gifts, Facets, and especially Renown. If the price is right, this is a minor issue, but if it's not, then we have taken a two way EXP sink and turned it into a three way, little-bit-confusing-and-yet-somehow-more-streamlined EXP sink.
                                                    I agree that this is one of the pitfalls we face and something we need to get right.

                                                    Nature weird me out a little, given how much city is in it,
                                                    I was keen to avoid Nature being a Gift that's basically pointless if your Chronicle takes place in the city. There's an awful lot of nature present in the cities and settlements of today, and it felt worth emphasising that, for example, Hunters In Darkness are just as terrifying in a city park or area of urban wasteland as they are out in the wild, dark woods.

                                                    Originally posted by Red 9 View Post
                                                    When I say specificity I'm referring to the general habit of Forsaken powers to have a "This, but not this" setup.
                                                    A fair number of old arbitrary limits are out. The Gift of Dominance no longer has the old Dominance thing about only working on werewolves and humans; detection Facets, where they exist, are generally up-gunned in what information they provide.

                                                    Originally posted by Seraph Kitty View Post
                                                    I'm concerned with how this system will work with character development, personally. By having the bulk of available Gifts be 3-dot equivalents of different applications, I foresee two related results: Werewolves are going to be really powerful out-of-the-gate, with starting characters able to pull some really nasty tricks; but also once they start gaining exp they're going to have roughly the same power-level, only diversified across a wider range of applications.
                                                    I can assure you that this will not be the case. Lots of Facets click together in nasty ways or just provide different advantages to the same area of conflict, and there is a scaling element involved as well. Your highly experienced Rahu, for example, will be scarier Facets-wise than his less-experienced Rahu compatriot.
