            Yeah, Mage has beaucoup Gnosticism flowing through it but it's not orthodox in its Gnostic leanings. My loose understanding is that most of the early Gnostics saw the world as-is as something created by the Demiurge; flawed and incomplete and ultimately illusory; something to be overcome so one might transcend and rejoin the One. Mage, on the other hand, has reality as most certainly real and directly emanating from the only thing that can come close to being called God. The Fallen World is Fallen, but it's also still the World; it's not unnatural or undesirable, merely broken.

            If you like individuality, experience, and life in general, the World is great. It's the Fallen part that buggers it up.

                        The Mysterium believes magic is alive in the sense of it being an active, self-directing force; it responds and reacts, rather than blindly acting the same way over and over. It doesn't mean that these things take the shape of creatures or entities in another dimension or realm. Which isn't to say they also couldn't, but... Eh. It's not contingent that they are, is the thing.

                        Likewise, Ascension isn't meaningless without continued self-awareness or agency - plenty of people have died trying to change the world, after all - but I don't think that is what is being said here either. Self-awareness and agency don't have to be the type or character we're familiar with for them to be there. For example, if a Supernal being is the symbol it is bound to, it can't really want or choose to be or do anything but what it is; the Exarchs will always be tyrants because there is no part of them that is anything else; everything superfluous beyond that is burned away in the light of being the very idea of tyranny. Yet the Exarchs are still ostensibly active actors; directing things according to their natures.

                        It's not easy or natural to think of a self-directing law of nature - how, after all, could one imagine gravity to be sapient? - but it's not unfathomable either.
