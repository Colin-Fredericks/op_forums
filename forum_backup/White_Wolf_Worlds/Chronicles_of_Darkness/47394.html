    Mummy power structures in cities

    01-12-2014, 11:13 AM
    I am looking forward to the DC citybook for mummy because I am curious to see how mummies maintain power structures. The reason why is that, unlike vampires, mummies spend a long time in storage. Even though PC mummies can go through multiple calls to keep the game going, the default seems to be that most mummies only receive the call every few decades or centuries. This would indicate that power structures are mostly frozen in time in a status quo and only change over long periods because a mummy wakes up and makes some noise. Given the inactive nature of mummies, how do you think they gain and maintain power over long periods of time?

                  From reading the core and Guildhalls, it seems that mummies aren't concerned with amassing power in any sort of time scale. Mummies sense of time is very different from our own. They wake up in a huge conflict between powerful sides, fall asleep, wake up and it's resolved. They've also been around for thousands of years and they would be a very stupid mummy not to try and amass some fortune or resources during that time. A mummy should also trust that its cult should be able to at least hold onto some power over the years. Especially if they have a corporation that's been around for decades (though not necessarily in the same form). It's basically, as long as I have the resources I need to function it's all good. That's why it's upon the cults to use the mummies power and influence to build something greater. Hell improving upon your gods empire is going to get you some big brownie points...if he remembers who you are that is. They can do that by gathering allies, amassing wealth through deals and hostile takeovers, or even finding a relic. Remember, this immortal being is your god. Someone you worship and love dearly (even if you've never seen her in person). The legend and the power pushes you to make the best of your situation, and maybe you can move up to the high priest of your cult. The power and the wealth at your fingertips. Who wouldn't work for that?

                  However, as made apparent many times in the books, some mummies can be vindictive. They hold grudges and crave power. These are the mummies you have to watch out for. If one comes into your territory, it's going to end with conflict of some sort. Your cult is going to be attacked, your territory ransacked, and maybe some key relics stolen. There are cults that feel invincible because they have the power of a relic or they feel like their mummy will solve any problem. But those cults can be squashed with enough time and planning. A mummy is another matter.

                  They're immortal just like you and what if they have more power? Do you run and save your cult and tomb? Or do you fight the battle so you don't look weak in the eyes of your cult and other mummies? And these battles can span decades between the two factions. Cults carrying out covert ops, political back and forth between mummy allies, or even fights using business and wealth. A fight between mummies can be a complex war on multiple fronts and who knows when it will ever end or how? The two mummies might just forget and move their cults onto another focus. Or the mummy might be rendered inert after losing her cult and everything. Or maybe it ends in peace and both are weaker but alive in the long run. A battle over territory is never short and never easy because you're dealing with immortals, they have all the time in the world and the power of gods.

                  That felt very rambley...

                      The take I'm running with in my Mummy game is as follows...

                      Most Mummies spend most of their time around Memory 3. This means that their clearest memories are of their lives in Irem and of their previous Descent. So their actions and societies are based on stagnant memories of their origins and recent events whose causes are lost to them. In the absence of any kind of personal memory, these immortals are all about institutionalizing their knowledge, encoding their own missions and agendas in their cults and Guilds. Movement within the structure of Guilds is possible - after all, your fellows will remember you being promoted or cast out, or interactions on that basis - but the reasons for the "present" structure will often be lost or obscured. Those Mummies with higher Memory have a dangerous advantage here, as they're able to better manipulate this institutional memory for their agenda, or the agenda of their cult or Judge.

                      Outside of a Sothic Turn, the chances of two Mummies randomly awaking at the same time are miniscule. But it's pretty easy for a cult to awaken a Mummy if they've got a Relic or Vestige on hand. So I've decided that "Nomes" are grand conspiracies of nominally-allied Mummies linked together by networks of cults. The secret societies that support them share enough symbolism / belief / practice / what-have-you that they tend to call up their Mummies under roughly the same circumstances, and a Mummy who needs an ally who isn't awake has their "phone number", so to speak - they can find their cult and work through the right protocols to wake their Nome-patriot for aid, judgment, teaching, or what-have-you. Even if the cult doesn't have a relic they can use, weak or poorly-aligned Vestiges aren't too rare, so they make a good "calling card". Beneath this are "Merets", Mummies with closer alliances, either due to personal feeling, memories of Irem, or especially well-aligned cults.

                      In my framework, conflicts between Mummies tend to be transitory (over Relics, for example), personal (I hate B'oboth with the unreasoning fury of ten thousand years), religious (specifically, over Judges), or structural (as above, but also including "my cult hates your cult" and other such things).
