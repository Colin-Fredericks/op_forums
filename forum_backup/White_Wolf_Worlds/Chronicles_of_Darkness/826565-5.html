                                        Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                        I like the general direction of the mechanics. Only I have one thought - Each Clarity Trigger should be both Common and Specific versions. Common should be lighter shock to the Clarity ( like -1 ) and Specific rather hard ( like -3 ). Changeling that lost husband should have Common Trigger "Love relationships" ( that's why she omits romantic movies with her buddies ) and Specific Trigger "Seeing husband". Windwing from example could have Common Trigger "Hearing amateur music" and Specific Trigger "Hearing her fleut sounds".
                                        I don't know if there's a language barrier here or something, but each Trigger does have both common and specific expressions. The unnamed character with the trigger tied to losing his husband (the husband isn't dead, by the way, the changeling lost him in the sense that they're no longer together - presumably because he's now married to the Changeling's fetch) hits a Breaking Point for seeing his husband, and he also hits a Breaking Point for being forced to confront the general concept of marriage. Aeryn the Windwing hits a Breaking Point for hearing amateur music and she also hits a breaking point for hearing flute music. There aren't varying penalties for whether a character experiences the specific expression or the general expression, but they do roll for both.

                                        Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                        It all based on idea that players can choose highly specific Trigger for her character like "seeing dead husband" or "listening to mine childhood instrumnet" and avoid Triggers - compared to players that wrote more Common Triggers and getting hit in the Clarituy all the time on game session. Or wrote those too specific and have problem with rising Clarity when they need to face some of them.
                                        The idea is supposed to be that the specific expression is an objective thing that can be used as a definite Breaking Point every time it happens, and the general expression is something more subjective, that the player can decide on a case-by-case basis if something hits the trigger or not. When the guy who lost his husband sees his ex in line at the grocery store, holding hands with the fetch that stole his life, that's undeniably going to cause a Breaking Point. But when he gets an invitation to someone else's wedding, or he passes by a bachelorette party, or his friends ask him to go see a rom com after work, it's up to the player to decide if those things hit close enough to home for her character that it's going to cause him a Breaking Point or not.

                                        Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                        When players are obliged to have both version of them in each Trigger category, it leads to more harmonious play. Also, ST does not looked so much as deliberately throwing roadblocks when much milder Common Triggers are in the world all around changelings.
                                        I don't think it's necessary to put a label on the "common trigger". That's what the specific version is for. It gives you something you can put a name to and say "that's my trigger. Every time that thing happens, it's a Breaking Point." Then the common expression is left open to interpretation. When things happen that don't match the specific thing you said was your trigger, but are related enough that you think it would mess your character up, you can say "that hits a bit too close to home for my character. It's not exactly what I said his trigger was, but it's related enough I think it should be a Breaking Point."

                                                            First off, holy crap I love Clarity so much right now I can't even encapsulate it properly. It just manages to capture the essence of mercurial and hard to comprehend fae entities with the themes and all too human struggles of the Lost. I can imagine high-Clarity Changelings pulling off the trickster archetype quite well, because a Kenning could easily allow them to see what other people in the room want to do and completely outmaneuver them.

                                                            Secondly, Embracing the Wyrd sounds horribly dangerous and powerful to me, and comparing it to Going Loud of all things intrigues me. It sounds like something you'd do if you really need something like the Gentry on your side, but don't want the ickiness of having to deal with a Stranger yourself. That being said, because of what the Wyrd is, make sure that you lock down what the narrative of that scene is, because you don't want things running haywire because your Wyrd-guided brain finds a dropped plot thread.
