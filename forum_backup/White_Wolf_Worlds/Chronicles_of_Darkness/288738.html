      I think Matt unfortunately put his finger on exactly what makes me uncomfortable with promethean, even as I like reading the books and like a lot of the trappings of the game. I just can't get behind a worldview that says that there's something metaphysically special about being human (as opposed to just having traits that humans currently have a near-monopoly on, like a developed sense of self, sapience, etc).

      The idea that they would give up power in return for completeness doesn't bother me, but especially with the addition of what are basically robots (the unfleshed), I feel like it's a little too naive and polly-anna-ish to have every nonhuman entity envying our oh-so-special-ass selves so much so that their highest aspiration is to become, not just self-actualized and empathetic and emotionally mature, but biologically and psychologically human. To use an example from another piece of fiction, it's like if the Geth from Mass Effect set out to become Quarians rather than seeking their own version of what it means to be "complete".

      It just rings false to me. Loses me at the buy-in.

      Oh well.

      Edit: On further thought, it especially bugs me because so much else in the game is geared toward showing what a shitshow humans are. Disquiet is basically a dramatic way of conveying the message "Humans are stupid, violent bullies that react in the worst way possible to things that are different". Given that, the moral of this "humanistic" tale seems to be "aspire to fit in, so we'll stop tormenting you", which is a metaphor that I find perhaps even more uncomfortable than some of Changeling's themes, which I didn't previously think possible. It looks at the tale of Frankenstein's monster, and validates the villagers with pitchforks.

          Let's put aside that I really love the humanist core of the game and focus in on the horror aspect. It's a very bleak thing that can evoke a certain sense of fatalism and nihilism, but hit the right edge and it's terrifying.

          On the one hand, you're this incomplete thing who inspires disdain, hatred, and rejection from everything. You might have super powers, but your lot in life crap. On the other hand, You can become a part of the world, be part of a community of the most terrifying, batshit insane creatures in the entire world, creatures that operate not because of any sort of ban or limitation on their choices, but because they, for one reason or another, choose to act that way. You can be hunted and left alone, or you can join a group of creatures who does everything from create small, personal hells for each other to massively killing off each other in the worst possible fashions.

          Which of the two hells is better? Deciding on that is good, tense horror.

          Now that said, I think that reading is bullshit. I think that humanity, both in and out of the World of Darkness, is pretty damn spiffy. Even with the myriad of flaws I like what we've accomplished, and I like the idea of what we can do as we continue approving. Does transhumanism have a place there? Maybe, but I think there's value to the frailties-if nothing else, we need them to unite us until we can get into the mindset of helping each other each other regardless of anything else, at which point the species is mature enough for immortality and all that other fun stuff(and even then I really don't agree with that, because I believe the neat to get sick, get hungry, and die does have it's own value, but let's not get carried away on tangents). I don't agree with the above paragraph.

          But it is a viable angle on Promethean for what it is, which is a horror game. And if that's not your slice of life, well, sorry, but...

Their reasoning goes that the only people who would perform alchemy for those reason are those who are already affluent, as anyone lacking in resources will first seek resources and thus devote themselves to a cheaper, lesser, more insulting form of alchemy. So they go after the less affluent alchemists.

                                  Everything else stems into the idea of tradition as well, that alchemy is for an elect who will work it for non-petty reasons, and that the original strictures are there for a reason, so bye bye female alchemists. While they're harder to swallow than the Guardians(because the Guardians at least look sane from certain angles), they fill a similar niche in the world and are interesting as a staple of the question "Are we sure humanity is really worth it?".

                                  I'll actually freely admit, The Pristine Order is more interesting to me than alchemists as a general concept for antagonists.
