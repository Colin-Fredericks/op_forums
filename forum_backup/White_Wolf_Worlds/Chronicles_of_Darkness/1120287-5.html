                            The Knights of the round establish and follow a Code. And it's not as if compacts controlled or formed by mages are unheard of.

'Werewolf is about dominance and violence, about a world divided into "us" and "them." It's about hunting and being hunted in turn. It's about how far you would go for one of your own.' Which fits World War 1, and many other wars, quite nicely.

                            Dark Eras don't always go for the 'easy' way. Doubting Souls was a time of witch burnings, that would have fit Mage just as well as it did Hunter. After the Fall had a large section on Dracula, but was a Demon chapter. Second Sight could have gone to mages. Aztec period for vampires. And so on.

                            I'm sure Mage will be one of the eras but I wouldn't be aghast at the thought of them going another direction. They could just have a section detailing Merlin as a mage.

                                            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post

                                            Now that I think about it, Mage/Hunter could be a good alternative to Mage/Changeling (or perhaps we could have all three with expansion, if we're lucky). The Knights of the Round Table keeping the land secure against monstrous threats does fit the era. And whether or not would the knights as Hunters have supernatural abilities that would make them Sleepwalkers could be made irrelevant if they were also a line of Proximi, perhaps made by drinking from the Grail. Or maybe each would be a different Proximus, giving each of the legendary knights a unique set of abilities distinguishing them from each other. Or perhaps only Arthur is a Proximus, with the knights being Hunters with Supernatural abilities.                                             I'm not certain if you can have Hunters who are Proximi, it might be mixing splats, but than again, Hunters are still mortal humans. Besides, a Mage/Hunter crossover where you have two groups with such different power and yet both of them are essentially the most human splats in their nature could lead to interesting storytelling opportunities, especially if they are allied against common threats.

                                            EDIT: To add Mage story hooks, we know that Ruin threw a hissy fit about the very existence of Camelot and tried to completely obliterate it, which it failed to do. But whenever an "Arthur" surfaces and tries to create a new Camelot, I can imagine Ruin attempting to eliminate him as soon as possible. Efforts of Cabals in this era could be about preventing the destruction of everything they hold dear by covert powerful supernatural forces and their villainous (proto)Seer minions.
