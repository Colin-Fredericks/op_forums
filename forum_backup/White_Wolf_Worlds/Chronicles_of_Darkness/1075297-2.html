    Chapter 4: Reaching Too Far
    Part 4


    The five guardians have backed away as Ash exited the portal, now forming a ring around him. They’re unmoved by his declaration.

    “Ha!” Marx cheers from the back. Ash fires her a grin. Annoyed, Tartarus moves in closer, blocking their line of sight.

    "Why, when I heard the phrases 'Paradox', 'abyssal manifestation', and 'experiment gone wrong' was the immediate thought in my head 'Ash'?" Tartarus asks humorlessly, looking Ash over carefully. "Set down the gear, boy. Nice and slowly. We can make this very easy for everyone." He cautions. His hands tighten on his cleavers.

    Ash stares at Tartarus. There are ways to read that statement, and when he takes a second to think about it… Ash realizes he doesn’t care. Even Tartarus and the potential threat of death can’t dampen his mood. Obediently, he drops the moon rocks to the floor.

    With a condescending grin, Ash focuses his attention on the chief Guardian. "Gone wrong?" he says in that low tone which always precedes a loud rant. "Let me tell you right now, Tartarus, what you should think of when you hear Argentum Everto. You should think of the man who dared to do something none of you had the mind or the stones for. Who called down divine fire and hit a Gulmoth so hard in the face it ran away with its tail between its legs.”

    Tartarus rolls his eyes. Of course he should’ve expected this. He doesn’t move to shut the Obrimos up, seemingly content to let Ash waste his breath as long as he wasn’t forming an imago.

    “Who saved-lordy yes," Ash cries, in the manner of a faith healer, "-who saved one of our own Masters from the jaws of damnation itself! Hallelujah! Stared out across the void between worlds and saw the Abyss, arrayed before him, and watched as they fled in terror!" His brow knits together, and his grin slowly grows wider. "I flew through the empty vacuum and I made landfall over two billion miles from where we're standing now! I walked across an alien landscape, left my assprints in the sand, and saw things that could change the way we view the membrane between all worlds!”

    One of the guardians looks pleadingly at Tartarus as Ash is busy looking up at the ceiling in triumph. Tartarus just shakes his head and mimes jerking someone off.

    “And more than all that, Councilor, you should damn well think of this moment!" Ash pounds his chest so hard it leaves a red mark on his skin. "When I, first of our kind to set foot on the outer bodies, stepped through that portal, carrying in my hands the tools for you all to do the same! And that this is the moment I announce the beginning of my grand endeavor!" he finishes, his loud voice—for once raised in triumph instead of anger—giving way to breathless panting.

    The other guardians shift and move, always at the ready, and Ash gets the distinct impression that they are all looking for the first hint of aggression; the first excuse to sink a blade in him... all except Tartarus, who is looking at him with a quirked brow. For a moment, Ash sees genuine curiousity in the man's heavily scarred face before seriousness erases that pleasant intrigue.

    Tartarus returns rather firmly to the moment. "Announce what ever you like, Ash. It is up to me whether any of it will be leaving this room." The elder declares coldly. "Lay yourself face down on the floor and set your hands behind your head."

    Ash keeps his ridiculous grin, and slowly drops to his knees, assuming the position. "Is it, councilor? I left a scarecrow on the moon. Would you use my souvenirs to retrieve it? Would you be able to ignore what it meant if you did?" Ash presses his cheek to the floor.

    Tartarus certainly doesn't look happy with that, or how Ash had made him a party to it as a witness. But in the moment, it’s not the priority. No, he waits until Ash is on the floor and then, cautiously, steps over to the young man. Ash feels a foot rest against his back and a heavy blade against the base of his neck. He’s on the executioner's block.

    Tartarus sets one of his blades into his belt, then came the chanting in High Speech.

    Ash can't see the rote mudras involved. He’s busy being pressed to the floor. He knows the words, though. All the Guardians chant them, adding their own potency to that of their master. The Mind and Spirit Arcana, forcibly conjoined, used together in a way that Ash cannot fathom with his limited mastery of one and complete lack of the other. He feels it, though. A blunt violation of the sanctity of his mind and soul are his reward for compliance, his memory explored and spirit searched. Not a painful experience, but an intensely unpleasant one that culminates in, at last, Tartarus lifting the blade from Ash's neck.

    There’s a moment of suspense as the younger Obrimos is left to wonder if he will next feel it drawn down on his spine.

    "He's clean. Clean enough, anyway," Tartarus says as he steps up and off Ash’s back. To Ash’s surprise, he then feels a hand pull him up and guide him back to his feet. "Argentum Everto, once again you prove yourself willing to take risks and put others in danger; to boldly walk where angels fear to tread." That harsh, aged voice states in a characteristic growl, yet there is a distinct lack of malice. This time, at least. Tartarus sheathes his other blade, then offers a hand to the young man to shake.

    Ash freezes when he sees it: Tartarus is smiling. He recovers his bearings quickly enough at the accusation—that he knows how to handle.

    "I didn't put anyone in danger," Ash protests. "I asked them for help in doing something and they gave it.” He takes Tartarus’s hand and shakes. It’s firm, steady, and aggressively vice-like at the end.

    “But while we're all here, if you'll consent, councilor," Ash attempts to regain some degree of formality, "I’d like to attempt to open a portal to where I just was using the rocks I brought back. While we have a team assembled for the worst. The barriers… they’re thinner out there. I'm thinking that it was our attempt to open a portal into empty space that made it possible for the Abyss to do what it did, since what we do know suggests that it otherwise shouldn't have happened."

    Tartarus’s brief appearance of levity takes a serious and dour downturn. He frowns sharply, but nods in permission. took his lip as he gave a nodding permission. "Well, we'll find out, won't we?" he says, clearly loathing the idea.

    The guardians move back into place around the portal. Marx looks to Ash, then to the rocks, not exactly looking eager herself. Still, she takes up the rock and does as she has agreed to do in the nervous excitement of the moment.

    "Hm... Weird..." Marx remarks as she holds the abrasive rock in her hand. "Feels... Easy. Like... Way easy. Easier than even opening a portal here on Earth." She tosses the stone up and down a few times. With practiced ease, she cuts another portal in the wall and, tentatively, tears it open. She’s bracing herself, bracing for a Paradox, but none come. "O... kay, that's weird... I didn't... I mean, I should've..." She ponders, scratching her head, then looking down at the strange rock in her palm. "I didn't even have to ease the casting..."

    Ash can't suppress the way his fists and arms shake in joy. "Would you do me one more favor, Marx? I'll cast some protections and take a step out with you--we don't know if enchantments can last billions of miles from its source and I won't be testing that with people--step back through, then see if you can do it without the rock." He glances over to Tartarus. "While we have a force on hand to ensure its safe."

    "Yeah!" Marx shouts eagerly at the opportunity to jump out onto another world.

    "No!" Tartarus answers swiftly. "If she can't maintain the portal and it closes - if she can't open one from the other side - then we don't have anyone here as capable to open another. I won't risk her life on that," the elder Obrimos says with the tone of a decree.

    "I didn't say close it when she was on the other side," Ash pouts. "I'm not an imbecile."

    "But... space..." Marx murmurs dejectedly, like a child who just got told they were too short to go on a roller coaster their friend had just gotten down from.

    "We will need another master of Space if this is something you re eager to test in full." Tartarus continues, while his gaze sets out towards the portal. He glances briefly to Ash, then towards the moon's surface. Walking forward slowly, Tartarus draws a bag from his belt and, ever so carefully, spoons up a handful of the silt in his hand and pours it into the bag. He sets it in his belt, and then walks back to join him. "A souvenir." He adds quietly under his own breath.

    Ash rubs his hands together before pointing at the piles of rocks and the camera. "We should probably put those in a vault." He bends over and picks up one of the rocks he'd carried in. "Except this. It is mine. I have named it Scraps." He glances over to Tartarus. "You wouldn't happen to know another master, would you?" He asks, flinging an arm around the gruff man's shoulders in a far too-friendly gesture. "By the way, I have a thought experiment I want to run by you..."

    Tartarus quirks a brow at the thought, even as growls in disapproval. Ash is flirting with disaster, and loving it.

    Before Ash can continue, he hears the regular clickity-clack of plastic on metal. Heads turned. Pathos stares in shock, looking at Ash, the portal, the Guardians... Her mouth hangs briefly open. Then closed. Then open. Then closed. Her brain clicks into first gear and she frowns. The only words she can think of come immediately to mind: "I thought you were going with the suit?"

    Ash pulls away from Tartarus and looks down at himself. He looks at Pathos. He shrugs. “Yeah, well… let’s go get fucked up!” He cheers, and is met by a hoot from Marx.

    “Pants first,” Tartarus reminds, dryly.

    As Ash, Marx, and Pathos tear down the steps of the consilium’s primary meeting place—Ash in an oversized pair of sweatpants and a Bad Girl t-shirt Marx has lent him—Ash feels happy. A genuine giddiness is bubbling up from inside that isn’t brought on by any combination of drinks or drugs. He's done it, finally, something that really, really matters, like he’s always wanted, that he held on to throughout his life, that motivated him through his mystery play.

    That feeling stays with him through the night and into the early hours of the morning. It stays with him when Pathos leaves to get some sleep, and when Marx’s circle of friends and connections show it. It stays with him through the substances they experiment with, and through the back-slaps and endless congratulations.

    Later that night, Ash locks himself in a bar bathroom and cries into the sink.

        So since I promised myself I'd do this, here's Ash's Legacy. It grew out of watching Pathos attempt to use stage magic to inspire real magic, Ash's experiments on paradox and quiescence vis-a-vis Sleeper perception, and Ash's own jump to the edges of the solar system using legwork done by mundane Sleeper technology. It deals with the Truth, the Quiescence, and the spin a Mage can put on them by skirting the edges of and exploiting the Lie. If it looks like it might be considered a Left-Handed Path based on orthodoxy and that Ash may have learned the wrong lessons, that's a valid read.

        Its major sources of inspiration are secret agent fiction and the myth of the men in black.

        For mood: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qwmk14Jj-sI




        Legacy: Technocrat

        Complex Machines: Some yantras and attainments play off the use of complex machines, which are here defined as devices composed of 3+ moving parts or that are powered by a battery.

        Ruling Arcanum: Prime

        Yantras: Use a complex machine in casting (+1, or +2 if a battery-powered device is destroyed or depleted as a sacrament); technobabble to at least 1 Sleeper, e.g. “some swamp gas reflected off Venus” (+2), expose a lie or conceal the truth (+1, or +3 if this would be a sin against Wisdom), use stimulants to stay focused (+1, or +2 if this creates an adverse Condition).

        Oblations: Repair, carefully disassemble, or maintain a complex machine; craft a significant deception or unveil a significant truth; pretend to be someone else in a social setting for an hour (Shadow Names do not count); resolve a Tilt or Condition suffered by a Sleeper from Quiescence; gaslight someone

        First: I Know You
        Initiation
        The Technocrat must first understand the world he lives in, and this is best accomplished through repeated and deductive inquiry. This attainment duplicates the effects of Supernal Vision.
        Optional: Mind *
        The Technocrat’s understanding of the world and its Truth extends just as easily to the minds of the things in it. The attainment is now also capable of answering questions as Mind Scan, though seeing what it is a rock is thinking will likely return no meaningful result.

        Second: You Don’t Know Me
        Prime **
        The Technocrat, understanding the nature of Truth, can hide it just as easily. This persistently cloaks the Nimbus of the Technocrat, similar to the spell Cloak Nimbus. It doesn’t prevent their Immediate Nimbus from being noticed with Mage Sight, but displays it as an impersonal haze.
        Optional: Mind **
        With an understanding of the human psyche, this ability extends to mundane perceptions. This attainment will also persistently duplicate the effects of the spell Incognito Presence, save that instead of being forgotten after the fact the Mage’s features are imperfectly remembered, becoming hazy and inaccurate.

        Third: Some Swamp Gas Reflected Off Venus
        Prime ***
        Understanding the importance of discretion, the Technocrat knows how to frame the Truth in ways more palatable—or obfuscate it with smoke and mirrors. The Technocrat may reflexively designate Sleepers in their immediate vicinity as Sleepwalkers for the purposes of spells cast using technocratic yantras or the Technocrat’s dedicated tool if it would provide a dice bonus. This effect lasts for a scene, after which these Sleepers will be affected by the Quiescence Curse. The primary spell factor is potency.
        Optional: Mind ***
        Provided that the Mage used a technocratic yantra or a dedicated tool that qualifies as a complex machine, at the end of a scene when a Sleeper is forced to confront the unreality of their experience they do not suffer Tilts or Conditions as a result of breaking points. This does not protect against the loss of Integrity.

        Fourth: Ready for Anything
        Prime ****
        A skilled Technocrat is dependent on his tools. An even more skilled one makes his own. The Technocrat spends 1 mana, and instantly produces a complex object no larger than Size 2 constructed as through Platonic Form. This object is suitable for use as a +1 Technocratic Yantra, and the creation of the object can count as the Yantra itself. The object vanishes after the Technocrat’s following turn. If the object’s use would take longer than this, such as hacking with a created laptop, it lasts only as long as it takes to finish the action. The primary spell factor is potency, and duration cannot be modified.
        Optional: Mind ****
        Upon creating the object, the Technocrat automatically obtains ranks in the skill associated with the use of the object he created, up to the Technocrat’s natural maximum. This skill vanishes when the object does. The primary spell factor is potency.

        Fifth: No Truth But My Truth
        Prime *****
        Optional: Mind *****



        I admit, I didn't put much thought into skill requirements, since presumably Ash would meet them. At some point I'll probably do those up properly along with a write-up on what the Legacy might be like if there was more than one person running around with it for the Legacies thread.

        This was originally done up for 1E, but it was a garbage mess. With the advent of 2E and yantras, I had the system I needed to make it work how I wanted to. Unfortunately, I can't come up with a fifth attainment I'm satisfied with. Anyone reading this with ideas, I'd be glad to hear them. It's frustrating me.

        Edit: correct a few errors and clarified that a Technocrat's dedicated tool must confer a dice bonus for the spell in question to count for the third attainment.
