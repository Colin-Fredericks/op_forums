                                      Off World Supporters needs a system connected to it - otherwise, there's no point taking more than one dot.

                                      Also, you left out the electricity resistance in the Resistances section (or left in a reference in Electrocytes), and really need to condense the Conditions. They are all pretty wordy and (especially in the case of the Paranoia conditions) are kinda piddly in terms of modifiers. Good conditions have a small number of modifiers but communicate their effects through manipulating other mechanics.

                                      In general, it's fine to leave the Beat and Resolution triggers as sentence fragments. Compare:

                                      Feverish [Condition]

                                      Your character has contracted a disease that is making it gravely ill. Any time your character engages in combat, it suffers from the Sick (Grave) Tilt. More-over, when outside of combat, it must roll its Stamina + Resolve every time a number of hours equal to its Stamina have passed. On a failed roll it suffers one point of Bashing Damage. The character cannot heal damage while it suffers from this condition. If any lethal damage is suffered because of this condition, the character’s sickness will worsen (see below). Resolution: This condition may be resolved in one of four ways:
                                      1. Gain a beat and resolve this condition if a point of lethal damage is caused by it. The character gains the Delirious Condition.
                                      2. Gain a beat and resolve this condition after a number of days equal to the character’s Stamina. The character gains the Delirious Condition.
                                      3. Gain a beat and resolve this condition if the character rolls an exceptional success on any roll to resist damage caused by the illness. The character gains the Recovering Condition.
                                      4. Gain a (single) beat if the character treats the sickness with medicine or by seeking treatment from a knowledgeable physician of some kind. Treatment requires medicinal substances of some kind which may have to be gathered (at the Storyteller’s discretion). The character treating the disease makes an extended roll using Intelligence + Medicine. They must accumulate a number of successes equal to 15 - the sick character’s Stamina. A failed roll represents an adverse reaction to the medication causing the sick character to suffer their Stamina in points of bashing damage. Once enough successes have been accumulated the Feverish Condition is resolved and sick character gains the Recovering Condition. In the event of a dramatic failure, the Feverish Condition is resolved and the sick character gains the Delirious Condition.
                                      to

                                      Feverish [Condition]

                                      Your character has contracted a disease that is making it gravely ill. Any time your character engages in combat, it suffers from the Sick (Grave) Tilt. More-over, when outside of combat, it must roll its Stamina + Resolve every time a number of hours equal to its Stamina have passed. On a failed roll it suffers one point of Bashing Damage. The character cannot heal damage while it suffers from this condition. If a number of days equal to your Stamina pass or the damage from this conditions wraps around to Lethal, resolve this Condition and gain the Delirious Condition. Otherwise, you gain the Recovering Condition upon resolving this one.
                                      Resolution: Get an Exceptional Success on the roll to prevent further damage. Someone scores [15 - your Stamina] successes on an extended Intelligence + Medicine roll.
                                      Beat: Receive medical treatment from a professional.
                                      I'd personally go even further than that and remove the need for an extended Medicine roll. I'd drop the Beat line and replace the Resolution line with:

                                      • Resolution: Get an Exceptional Success on the roll to prevent further damage. Receive professional medical treatment.

                                      Also, I wouldn't swap between second and third persons like you do in the Condition; either stick with using you or your, or use it the whole way through.

                                      Remember, brevity is a virtue. I can't think of any official Conditions that I wouldn't be able to fit on one side of an index card.

                                      I have decided, after some thought, that I don't really feel happy on these forums. I might decide to come back to post. Who knows - but right now, I'm gone.

                                      So good bye, good luck, and have a nice day.

                                                      One kind of alien that I wish was covered, but isn't — though I mostly understand why:

                                                      • “humans from the stars”: just as there are numerous alien races within each Taxon, wouldn't humanity itself be but one race within a Taxon? That is, this Taxon would involve species who could pass for human with at most a mundane disguise kit. The flip side of this is that they're unlikely to have Adaptations, and their lack of a means of Camouflage makes Exposure particularly problematic. They'd still be aliens though, with the usual susceptibility to Terran diseases, issues with Assimilation, and alien Vessels. This Taxon is the stereotypical nordic alien — though in typical Chronicles of Darkness fashion, they come in a variety of appearances rather than all conforming to a single human ethnicity.

                                                      Other thoughts:
                                                      • Shared Vessels: as written, Alien: the Stranded assumes that each alien has his, her, or its own Vessel. But what about spacecraft that had crew and/or passengers, not just a single occupant? This strikes me as a frequent occurrence, and one way to establish a tightly-knit Cluster of aliens — though the shared origin would tend to imply (but not mandate) a common Taxon and Occupātio as well. It would also tend to strain the Vessel's resources.

                                                      • You need a Glossary of all the Latin terms you've used.
