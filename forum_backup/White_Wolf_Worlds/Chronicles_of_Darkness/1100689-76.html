                    Originally posted by Omegaphallic View Post

                    I'd interesting twist if the Knights of the Round table were Changelings entitlement.
                    One of the possibilities I've imagined for portraying the period of Merlin's Enchantment of Britain is that it drew significant power from essentially overlapping the island with an Arcadian domain shared by several True Fae corresponding in part to "traditional" Celtic faerie roles. They both adopted the tropes of medieval chivalric adventure and contaminated reality of the time with it. (Keeping in mind, will you, that Arcadia is essentially free to borrow ideas from all of human history at once, thanks to it's questionable respect for linear timelines.) Later recounting by writers such as Geoffrey of Monmouth, Robert de Boron, Chrétien de Troyes, and Sir Thomas Malory all borrowed from/were inspired by parts of Arcadian "memory" and influences--as well as the Supernal symbolism of Merlin and Britain.

                    Anyway, the practical effect of the above would be a great thinning of the Hedge and significant contamination of reality with individuals of fae relation. This implies that many of the heroic knights were either Changelings that thought they escaped, ones that never realized they were abducted (because all of Britain was taken, more or less), or were in fact Fae wisps given free action. The Round Table organization was a huge prop to keep the different Fae civil and the adventuring and stories continued until it was time to turn out the lights with Camlann.

                    Meanwhile, no one inside the dance would know the difference, unless they were clued in (Hunters ala "evil Saxons") or thought it all stupid and wanted to burn it down (Morgan, Mordred?).

                    Just a few thoughts.

                    --Khanwulf

                                        Originally posted by Omegaphallic View Post

                                        I can see the area having the influences from Midevil, Christian, Pagan, Roman, Germanic, and Celtic sources among others.
                                        There are really two main ways to view the Arthurian age once you've concluded that Merlin is a thing and he did an ascension play with it:

                                        1. It went down pretty much as the writers (all of them) recorded--as impossible as that is with the cultural and technological anachronisms and mixings involved, let alone the fantastic and fae elements! Blame Merlin! (And Canada.)
                                        2. The authors re-wrote the story (legends) to correspond with what their contemporary audience could take in. The stories are not literal but allegorically correspond to Merlin's reality, complete with fantastic elements, which of course were all wiped away by Ruin.

                                        Given that Merlin is the dude of Fate and Time, either of these are perfectly valid takes.

                                        I'm pretty sure, however, that CofD will follow #2, with a strong emphasis on the period as what "went" down after Ruin got her hands on the timeline and "fixed" Merlin's shenanigans. That way if you happen to, oh, play a vamp through from RfR she won't remember things that flat-out didn't happen by the time you're hanging with Charlemagne.

                                        --Khanwulf
