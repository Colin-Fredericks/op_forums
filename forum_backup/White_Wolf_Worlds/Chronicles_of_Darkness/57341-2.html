            I mentioned perfect liars earlier. Perhaps I'll take an opportunity to clarify but first I want to make this point.

            I think that many people have already stated this in varying ways but I suppose I'll state it again: YOU CAN BE DISCOVERED AS A DEMON EVEN IF YOU ARE ACTING COMPLETELY WITHIN YOUR COVER IF SOMEONE, LIKE ANOTHER FELLOW DEMON, KNOWS WHAT TO LOOK FOR.

            -----Example time:I am role-playing a life coach (Ms. Aigh) who deals with people who desperately want to change something about themselves. They want to become more successful, lose weight, get the (gender of their choice), and so on.

            Ms. Aigh also gets her clients to sign an agreement that, among other things, gets the clients to commit 100% of their being to achieving their goals. MIND, BODY AND SOUL. They must give me their all when I help them pursue theses goals.Absolutely everything. No reservations, no hesitation.

            Right there, Ms. Aigh has got a number of poor unfortunate souls (IN PAIN! IN NEED!) to soul pact with her. And so far nothing really sticks out.

            Ms. Aigh still helps the people who don't sign (or maybe she doesn't, but let's say she does) and perhaps they succeed or not. And perhaps Ms. Aigh doesn't soul pact everyone by changing the words very slightly and reserving the soul pacts for covers she is really interested in.
            At the very worst, Ms. Aigh is perhaps considered slightly melodramatic, maybe a bit dire as a life coach, -shrug- nothing that raises much attention.

            However, to another demon (Mr. Bee) who is interested in a set-up like Ms. Aigh's (even though at this point in the example they consider her a human.) comes through her door in one of his covers to investigate Ms. Aigh and her business.

            Mr. Bee manages to meet with Ms Aigh (he still think she's human) and they have some interactions where they discuss Mr. Bee's situation (again, neither of know that the other is a demon at this point).

            Then it comes time for Mr. Bee to make the commitment, Ms. Aigh gets out the contract, (As a demon, Mr. Bee is always interested in contracts so this raises a flag and he pays close attention) and they go over terms. Mr. Bee makes excuses (perfectly executed lies) and refuses to sign the contract at that moment. Perhaps Mr. Bee questions Ms. Aigh on the contract and Ms. Aigh creates a perfectly believable explanation (which is a perfectly executed lie). Perhaps Mr. Bee makes another appointment to return, maybe he doesn't but he's met Ms. Aigh, seen what she does and seen one of her contracts.

            Mr. Bee returns to his lair/safe place/cover's house/whatever and reviews Ms. Aigh's contract thanks to his Eidetic Memory. Mr. Bee discovers that the contract contains provisions which would essentially make a human promise their soul to Ms. Aigh in exchange for her assistance to help the mortal achieve their goals.

            Mr. Bee quickly gains the strong suspicion that Ms. Aigh is a demon as well. It makes sense, He was contemplating a similar set-up.

            -----END EXAMPLE-----

            So, we have two perfect liars who have just interacted with each other and without acting out of character at all and taking reasonable steps to maintain their cover Ms. Aigh might have been discovered as a demon.


            So what does this mean?
            You are always susceptible to discovery. Especially by others who are aware of the habits of your kind (I'M SURE this point has been made before).

            In this way we also see that the ability to lie perfectly has not prevented another person from strongly suspecting the other's demonic identity.

            Yes demons are perfect liars. No argument, RAW, source material supported, etc.,.
            Your lie may still be questioned even if it appears to be the truth.

            A perfect ability to lie does not supersede, override or otherwise impair another person's ability to critically think, question or just be stubborn.

            If Ms. Aigh had perfectly explained away the process it doesn't change Mr. Bee's ability to read the contract, think on the terms and (hopefully if he's any good) recognize the terms for what they were.

            Now this example isn't perfect. Perhaps you disagree with my very rough approximation of a soul pact, I'm not trying to sell you on on the validity of my terms of the soul pact, just the idea that Ms. Aigh can use this position to get soul pacts from many people.

It is still likely that Mr. Bee considers the possibility that Ms. Aigh is a demon because he, a reasonable demon, has considered a similar operation. Mr. Bee, in this instance has less to go on but decides to do more research to get more information on another demon who may be a potential rival or ally.

                        Originally posted by genesis View Post
                        As a deviation from the most recent discussion but still on the topic of covers, does investigating a cover work if the cover is not currently in use? If so, does it still cause a compromise? And if it does do the conditions attach to the cover the demon is currently wearing or only when the demon switches back to the cover that was investigated?
                        The system set out on page 114 doesn't differentiate between active and inactive covers. It seems to me that the investigation would revolve around a specific cover.
                        If a human succeeds in their investigation then the demon must check for cover as if they "revealed a key fact about themselves" to the human.
                        Since the human is investigating a specific cover, the demon should roll that specific cover's dice on the compromise roll.
                        The conditions listed on page 118 attach to a specific cover so they should attach to the cover that was under investigation. However, some of those conditions can interfere with your demon regardless of whatever covers they're wearing; like the Hunted condition.

                        So example time, ------
                        One of your covers, Ms. Bitters, somehow draws the attention of a crazy conspiracy theorist. The conspiracy nut thinks that something is off about Ms. Bitters and starts turning his investigations toward the weird. The conspiracy nut may have already done some normal investigation but your cover stood up to that. Either way he starts investigating using the rules on page 114 and he succeeds.

                        Now, when he succeeds you are currently Mr. Christopher and have not been Ms. Bitters in days. But since the conspiracy nut was investigating Ms. Bitters, you roll Ms. Bitters cover (let's say, 5) against the compromise roll instead of Mr. Christopher's (which is, let's say... 8).
                        Unfortunately you fail and get to choose between flagged, surveilled and hunted. (or roll for permanent glitch). So let's say you choose Hunted.

                        Even though Ms. Bitters has the hunted condition. Angels have been dispatched to look for a demon in the area. Now you (as Mr. Christopher) AND any other demons in the area where you operate are put at risk even though the Ms. Bitters cover is the one that was investigated.

                        ----END----

                        It is also worth noting that if you choose to roll a glitch that glitches attach to the demon and not the cover so they persist regardless of whatever meatsuit you're wearing.

                                Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post

                                Spoofing hides the demon's true nature and allows them to pass as human. So when you say "detect me" I'm reading that as "detect my supernatural nature directly or by implication" or perhaps "detect me as anything other than human". It does not render a demon undetectable in general.

                                My reading of spoofing is that it should not prompt a clash of wills because the power as it is written already contemplates and resolves an issue in which two supernatural powers clash.
                                specifically 1) I always appear to be human even in the face of supernatural detection methods and 2) I am detecting whether this person is a human or otherwise..

                                I submit that this should not prompt a further test wherein the two supernatural powers roll against each other as that is in effect directly undermining the effect of the spoof.
                                I further submit that the wording of clash of wills contemplates embeds vs disciplines vs arcana and other such ilk. The clash of wills side-bar is placed immediately before embeds also supports this.
                                The clash of wills section is also silent regarding cover and detection because, in my view, it has already been adequately addressed by spoofing.

                                Of course, you or your story-teller may elect to house-rule that but consider the wider implication.
                                In an espionage game if every neonate vampire or neophyte mage or newbie anything could activate a low level power and tell that a demon was a demon would really throw a wrench in the whole works.
                                In a higher power game there may be more avenues open to determining that this individual is not a human without the need to resort to supernatural detection.

                                Also, spoofing is not a guaranteed thing. It has to be rolled and you must gain 1 success. It can fail.

                                In my view, further undermining the usefulness of spoofing does not contribute to better game play. That said, I could see an argument for how the weakening of spoofing could raise more tension for some especially intense espionage narratives but that's only one specific example.

                                    Simply put, I don't like how absolute spoofing is. Sure, I wouldn't mind just making it really hard to do by penalizing rolls and/or out-rolling them as with a Changelings mien, but something as hit or miss as getting just 1 success on a roll that you will usually have quite a few dice in isn't something I care for in my games. Simply put, I feel that no limit abilities such as these should remain little more than plot devices.

                                    I'll probably end up houseruling it to work much more like Changeling, where while really hard to do, can still be done given sufficient skill/effort on the part of whoever's attempting to do so.
