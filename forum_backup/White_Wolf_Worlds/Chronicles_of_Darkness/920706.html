    Who Is Your Favorite PC So Far?

    06-24-2016, 09:56 PM
    Mine was a self-exiled True Fae who used to fancy himself a cunning war god. He'd take mortals who wanted to become strong, and he'd train them to be like Ancient Greek heroes. He'd pit them against armies of monsters and many puzzles, only allowing the champions to leave. He expected his captives to not co-operate with each other, and when that happened, he was caught by surprise. Their combined might and friendship forged in blood defeated the proud giant, allowing them to escape together.

    This event did not anger the war god, as one would expect, but inspired in him a fascination with honor, valor and other virtues beyond strength. Without the possibility of learning what he desired in Feuds with other Gentry, he set out for the mortal world. Though no longer possessing Titles, he could still enter and travel through the Hedge. He took it upon himself to truly understand what truly makes one a hero, and soon developed a passion for the mythologies and religions of man.

    Styling himself after Biblical Angels, Norse Valkyries and similar figures, he became a wandering vigilante and wanna-be Good Samaritan. But reality never quite conformed to his expectations, and though he had a lot of adventures and made several friends, he was still far away from grasping all the shades between black and white. He wouldn't always be the one to save the day, either.

    I used him to explore questions of faith, justice and honor. He even diverged enough from other Gentry to actually practice religions and study history with earnesty, though he still had a bad temper and a tendency to get into trouble. Through good and bad days, he sought to expand his faith and keep his warm sense of humor.

    I did not know what name to give this character, but I knew he should have at least one Shadow Name in Hebrew. I asked someone in the SCP wiki how to say "Warrior Of God" in that language, and they said Lohemel. So that's the name I picked. Here is his character sheet, which is quite old and was written back when I didn't understand the rules very well. My vision of him has evolved significantly, and the next time I play in a CoD game, I'll probably use him (but adapted to the new gameline and my current vision).

    Can you tell which characters I used as inspiration for him?

      I've actually mentioned my favorite PC I've played on these forums before. He was a Child of Judas named Jimmy I played for about 2 years in a Requiem LARP in college. He was heavily musically inspired, drawing most heavily from Green Day's American Idiot (pretty much the whole album, which is als where I got his name from) and William Control (particularly Hate Culture), but also took cues from Aiden, Tool, A Perfect Circle, Puscifer, Panic! at the Disco, The Beatles, and others I'm probably not remembering.

      He went through a lot of character development over the course of the game, which is a big part of what made him my favorite. He started out as a stereotypical firebrand - an anarchist who just hated authority and wanted to tear down any sort of political or social structure for rebellion's own sake. He was also a hardcore drug dealer, which gave him access to people in patterns of self-destructive behavior to feed his bloodline weakness and easy victims to feed on, plus he'd get to experience the effects of any drugs in their systems, which he was addicted to himself.

      He ended up participating in a coup to overthrow the Prince (who was also a Carthian, but too liberal for his anarchic political leanings, and a target for a number of other rebellious and/or ambitious kindred in the city), which required him to play a longer game, infiltrating the system by becoming the Sheriff's hound. After overthrowing the Prince, the Sheriff took her place, and he became the Sheriff. Once he was in a position of power, he found he didn't mind political systems quite so much when they benefitted him, so his worldview shifted. He decided that while law and structure was unnecessary for capable people like himself, it was important to keep those less enlightened in check. So, he became quite the hypocrite, flagrantly disregarding the law, while also enforcing it ruthlessly.

      Cut to a few Humanity losses, a few dots in Despond, a trip to the Underworld, and a Virtue/Vice shift later, he has something of an identity crisis which is eventually resolved when he joins the Lancea Sanctum. He becomes a confessor and a secret Inquisitor who uses his position as a confidant to root out heresy and assassinates heretics by influencing them to suicide. He also believes himself to be damned beyond any possible hope of redemption. Actually, he always believed that, but early in his arc he took that as an excuse to indulge and revel in sin, cause it's not like he's getting any more damned. But at this point he's decided that even if he can't be saved, he can at least serve as a cautionary tale to others, and hopefully deter them from going down his path, or failing that, kill them before they're too far gone.

      I had planned for him to eventually kill himself, but the game got cut short when the ST had to move out of state. Anyway, he was a really, really horrible person and I love it. I'd like to revisit him some day, I think some of the changes 2e made to the game would have some interesting impacts on him and how I would play him. But, there hasn't been a good opportunity for me to play in a Requiem game, and I wouldn't want to make him an NPC in a game I ST'd because I love the horrible bastard too much and I'd have a hard time not making him a spotlight hog. Plus, being a Child of Judas was a pretty important part of the character, and they need some work to fit into the 2e Bloodline paradigm.

          Our games tend to last a long time, so I've had a long time to get attached to certain characters. My Changeling was really fun, he was a sort of opposite of myself and it was fun being a 'himbo,' who only got in one fight but helped pull everyone through situations with social skills and godlike charisma.

          But my favorite character is probably the one I'm playing now. I had been planning him for a long time before Werewolf second edition came out, and started playing maybe a month after it came out. He's still going pretty strong. Things might go bad, of course, he's come closest to death out of all the pack.

          Basically the idea was to play a bad guy. His original inspirations were things like Taking Lives, Six Degrees of Separation, DC Comics' Jane Doe. More recently I found the book Ghostman which would have likely been a less murderous version of his future self had he not become a werewolf and still gave me some ideas for him. I found it impossible to play him the way I had originally planned, as a cold and detached presence. There were no rules for Bale Hounds, and I wasn't interested in playing a player-killing psychopath, so compromises were made. He's got a personal totem that acts as an intermediary between him and his patron while he tries to explore his nature, specifically in regard to the meaning of his Embodiment of Viruhk-Ur Merit. I've taken some inspiration from the Quest of the Avatar here.

          He's a killer, but it's basically all reliant on inherent werewolf abilities and his vicious willingness to eat flesh. His combat skills are boiled down to a lone dot in Brawl, which coupled with his 2 in Strength is underwhelming until you shift into one of the larger forms. At the culmination of our last hunt we had to stake a vampire and it took him three rounds to do. All his Gifts are oriented around information and support stuff. My first werewolf character was combat-oriented and I found it too easy, so I wanted to get away from that with Malcolm. Plus as mentioned I had a ton of fun with my changeling and wanted to try it again from a different angle, Mental rather than Social.

          I think the thing that makes him the most fun is that as the intelligence gatherer of the group, and the set-up around us, I'm able to basically play him as I would an antagonist or group of antagonists as a Storyteller. Most games rely on the players reacting to the actions of villains. With his ability to get information, and for the most part act with anonymity, he's able to plot against the forces of the setting while helping the domain and power of the pack.

          Recently his 'cover' was blown. He was going by the name Malcolm, having taken the life of a guy transferring to the local college. But after the first couple chapters, between the hunters and the vampires it wasn't really useful to be Malcolm anymore. So after the hiatus when our pack met up with this paranoid protectorate we've been trying to find, I got a one-on-one session where Malcolm and a wolf-blooded went on a road trip back to California, where he's from, to pick up a new identity and try and school the wolf-blooded in the ways of the Beast. Then they spent the day at Disneyland. He's now an Army veteran named Max. I decided to stick with an M-name for familiarity's sake.

          GibberingEloquence I have no idea who the inspirations might be.

                      My favourite character that I've actually played is hard to pick, one of the top contendors is a fallen angel called Gladiel (badly hacked up name meaning sword of god) in a homebrew game universe written by a friend of mine (we've nicknamed it SHITHEDS after a joke we made in the first session run in the universe, basically a world where pretty much every mythological being is real, including gods). I wrote him based a very large amount on Michael from the Dresden Files. He was a protector sort, constantly trying to do the right thing, destroy evil, deal out justice, but at the same time trying to treat people right regardless of who they were or what they had done (he would punish and admonish a thief but in the same sentence give them sympathy for their situation) his main companion was a demon (which were distinct beings from fallen angels) who he made cry by actually treating like a person rather than assuming was the manifestation of evil just for being aligned with hell. My favourite scenes with Gladiel involve him coming across a fae who bragged about eating children and he had to come to terms with the fact that if he tried to kill her for the horrific deeds she had done he'd be needlessly wasting his life and the life of his companion.

                      The next is probably Alice Pryce, a demigod in the SHITHEDS universe who was the daughter of Enyo (Greek Goddess of the nastier side of war), we only played a couple of sessions of the game she was in but she was a very shy, reserved nerdy girl with an obsession with cute things, squirrels and was a world-class ballerina. If we had continued on further she'd have wound up the goddess of military animals (basically everything from bomb-sniffer dogs to dragons) and her uncle Ares (my friend played him as if he was Brian Blessed which was a lot of fun) actually made what he thought to be adorable war-squirrels for her, they were 12 foot tall slavering beasts of rage and death. One of my favourite scenes was one where Alice agreed to do a favour to her half-cousin Deimos who she had a crush on and discovered after some investigation that it involved going into the underworld (the realm of afterlives, everything from Hades to the christian hell) so she called Deimos up on the phone and I spent about 5 minutes in-character yelling about being sent to hell and how he was a piece of shit for tricking her.

                      Gladiel was my first foray into playing characters other than super charming manipulating sluts (and my first attempt at playing a character with a moral code) and Alice was my first attempt at roleplaying a woman, so they're both pretty special to me.

                      My favourite character that I've written but haven't played is either Gaius the vampire or an unnamed mage.

                      Gaius Octavius Tacitus (or Galen Strathclyde) is either a Greek vampire at the fall of Rome or during Elizabethan era England. Either way he is the white-haired firstborn son of a high ranking nobleman in a family with a long tradition of being soldiers. He was a soldier like tradition, favouring stealth and surprise generally and was typically used as a scout. It was on a scouting mission that he was embraced, his unit was ambushed and all of them were killed. After a few days of being a corpse lying with his men a khaibit woman came across Galen's unit and, in need of someone who knew how to handle themselves in a fight, decided to posthumously embrace one, the only person it worked on was Galen and he was quickly vinculumed to his sire. They spent a few years wandering around the region he was embraced in his only possessions being his family's ancestral broadsword, a set of leather armour and a signet ring with the family sigil on it, acting as a mercenary and hunting for strix / ephemeral things messing with people, the woman being an adherant of what she had gathered to be the original khaibit cult from studying ancient tomes. One morning Gaius/Galen woke up and discovered his vinculum had completely ended and his sire was gone, refusing to believe she was dead he searched for her but without the power of the blood bond he found his faded feelings overpowered by worry as to what had happened to his family in his absence. Upon his return he discovered his family estate had burned down in the 5 years he was gone, killing his mother and father and his twin sister had faked her death in the aftermath, leaving their uncle as the head of the house. Gaius / Galen managed to have his 'not-dead' status recognized however his uncle still held the family title and lands. Personality-wise Galen is a total white knight, a quiet, reserved man who kept his feelings close to his chest and has absolutely no sense of humour. He would be the first to jump to the aid of a woman in distress and thought of himself as the hero of his own story, the handsome knightly lord who would come to the rescue of the damsel in distress. My current intention is to play him in a Requiem for Regina game with his twin sister being played by another player where we'd have had a mildly incestuous relationship, being far too close for a normal brother and sister, both having thought the other was dead and then finding out the other was a vampire.

                      The mage is an acanthus cowboy who was shunted forwards in time from cowboy era to the modern day by her awakening. She's an adamantine arrow with a very cowboy shadow name and mage persona while also being a bit of a dork who loves western movies, mostly working as a magical mercenary that fought by using either real pistols, or firing fast spells enhanced aimed spells out of finger guns while making shooting sounds with her mouth.

                          Probably my two favorite ChroD PCs to date have been:

                          John Bradley, an Acanthus in the Guardians of the Veil. He grew up in Vegas with a single father who had a gambling problem. His dad ended up getting himself killed after a failed card counting scam ran afoul of both the card sharks he was trying to fleece and the loan sharks he owed money to, reinforcing for John at an early age that the house always wins. This strongly informed his perspective on Fate and Time magic, giving him to much reflection on both the consequences and ethical ramifications of its abuse. He worked as a police detective, trying to right the world's karmic scales a little at a time and quietly solve crimes that had a supernatural agent. Unfortunately he also took seriously the need to keep the magical world hidden from Sleepers, and he became a dirty cop by accident and association participating in the cover ups that entailed. While he saw through Vegas' transparent promises of luck and fortune he had a blind spot for its offerings of easy love and sexual commodity that he used to cope with stress. This put him into the orbit of a lust-inspired demon who's corrosive presence in his life he could never entirely shake off. He was a ruthlessly effective Guardian but pretty much always on the edge of completely falling apart.

                          Wilson Wilde, a hunter with the Cheiron Group. He was the son of wealthy family of doctors and competitive pentathletes. That he would go to an ivy league school, try out for the Olympic pentathlon, and eventually get his medical degree were all forgone conclusions in his life. He resented it, but he went along with it because it was the path of least resistance and he enjoyed the fast lifestyle of spending his family's money. It all went wrong in college though when he was caught doping in competition and using his medical connections to sell pills on campus. He was banned from sport, expelled from school, and disowned by his family. Free from the imposed structure he resented but directionless, broke, and without prospects he was approached by Cheiron Group with a high paying job offer that would exploit what few skills he genuinely had. Wilson thrived in this environment, shielded from consequences for all but his most onerous actions, indulging every drug-fueled adrenaline junkie impulse he could conceive of, gradually becoming less and less recognizably human as he detached from reality and his body was modified with ever more extreme Thaumatechnology implants. He was almost without redeeming qualities and a walking cautionary tale about the kinds of people you don't want to be hunters no matter how good at it they are.
