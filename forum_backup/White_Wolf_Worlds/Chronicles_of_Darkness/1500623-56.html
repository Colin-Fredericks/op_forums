    Originally posted by Kazorh View Post
    Yes, obviously, I know that, but how? Mechanically speaking. Reach? Spell factors?
    And obviously "How do they work?" is a hopelessly vague question to try and figure out particulars for, which means going over basics is what I would consider a reasonable part of trying to provide an answer prior to getting clarification on what you actually meant.

    The spell factors are set when you make the thing. Reach is set for the defined effects unless you use Flexible Reach, as described on the page introducing that quality. The spell is presumably not able to be made stronger than you would be able to cast it normally without Yantra bonuses, so spell factors that would drop you below a dicepool of -10 wouldn't work.

    Did I miss something? Flexible Reach only talks about reducing Reach, going from Advanced to Basic and removing options, not the reverse. Whether or not you picked Flexible Reach would change nothing to the need to have indefinite duration baked in. Right?
    Indefinite Duration costs two Reach by itself and therefore a contingent item made to produce an indefinite effect would have that extra reach locked in for the purposes of determining if it has a Paradox risk from the jump, which matters because contingent items roll to cast their spells and therefore have cause to worry about Paradox reducing their dicepool. If the item has dedicated its normally fixed Reach to producing an Indefinite effect, the question then arises as to why the item isn't persistent.

                                Fyi: in 1e they refered to these as conjunctional spells, the wording still slips in in a few spell examples in 2e. Otherwise rules for conjunctional spells don't appear.

                                Pg 111 is odd, since it deals with "the highest arcanum included in the spell", for a Mind ··, Fate ·· spell either both or neither arcanum are the highest.

                                NB, it is not your character's highest arcanum.

                                Usually I would go with "this is a Mind 2 spell with conjunctional Fate", and thus use Gnosis+Mind to cast it... Not Gnosis+Mind+Fate, or Gnosis+nothing (since neither is highest).

                                Taking the first example spell in the book Exorcism (Death Unravelling) the optional extras (Spirit/Mind 4) don't make this not a Death spell. So I would still have this cost mana if Death was not one of your Ruling Arcana.

                                Contrast that with the Spirit example spell Banishment, which has conjunctional Death/Mind 4. It would be a Spirit ruling equivalent to pretty much the exact same spell.
