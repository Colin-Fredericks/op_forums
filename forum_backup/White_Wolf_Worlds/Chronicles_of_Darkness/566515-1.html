    Originally posted by Lashet View Post
    I still think, that without this ability werewolves will be in trouble in case of high-level fight, e.g. high-PU werewolf fight high-BP vampire. On this level, Gauru regeneration stop to be so big advantage.
    At that point there are some many intervening factors, that even speculating is pointless. Just as an example: I've got two PC characters, both played by the same player but their a good fit. One is a Rahu combat monstrosity (remade from 1st ed), going at it he's got ~20 health, 25 dice worth of attack, 8 defense, between 1-3 armor and between a +9 or +10 weapon modifier with no initiative penalty. He can do quite a bit more, but that's the crux of it. The other character is a Ventrue duelist. He's rocking Light Weapons 5, Celerity 5, and a special artifact tool that treats itself as a universal bane to all things that suffer banes. 10 health, 12 dice of attack at +1 damage (aggravated), that jumps to 17 dice in Frenzy, 15 defense and two dots of Resilience.

    We were all bored, so at one point we played out the fight. The general finding being that, in the face of the Werewolf's insane attack pool and damage modifier the vampire could not possibly survive two successful attacks. Worse, against 3/3 armor he couldn't reliably deal more than two or three damage even on a successful attack, hardly enough to soldier through 20 health. Plus the Werewolf had Butchery so the vampire was only ever going to get one or two attacks before dual leg wrack spelled the end of his days. His only chance was employing Celerity interrupts to avoid attacks and feinting to reduce the Werewolf's defense, then to dump all his defense, Frenzy and stowed successes into one insane 40+ die hail mary attack. Which, even after two such assaults, was generally not enough to bring the Werewolf down (though he'd kick it the second he shifts down).

    And that's one example, between two characters without allies fighting head on. If the vampire was smart he'd just dominate a bunch of gun men to soften up the Werewolf, then ambush him with a rapier through the back of the head, or dominate the wolf himself for a convenient and extremely lethal lapdog. Countless combinations of allies, environments and circumstances render even this scenario pointless and inconclusive, to say nothing of all the other power combinations that could be at work. (Can the vampire turn into a cloud of killer mist? Can he become a locus of unholy fear and force the Werewolf to cower helplessly? Or can the Werewolf summon and control fire? Can he cripple the vampire and leave it helpless before the pack?)

    Basically, white rooms are pointless (well, not entirely, but for most of the time yes), and splats should not be compared, not least of all in a one vs one fight. The results rarely if ever translate into practical reality.

                    Well, I will say this, from experience in our Werewolf Game.

                    The Prince and several Vampires in our campaign were all capable of inflicting Aggravated Damage through their Disciplines. Generally Claws (which apparently still applies at Protean 4 in 2nd Edition yes? Someone made that statement anyways). The Werewolves were all pretty leery of them for this very reason. And rightfully so, in a few cases (specifically the Prince and his Council, whom we dealt with constantly). Additionally, many of the vampires, leery of the Werewolves also carried Silver to use against them. When our Pack started gaining power and influence, we became a threat to the Vampire community, to some extent, as we began "interfering" in their plans while we expanded our territory (there were of course several factions of vampires). When we managed to finally obtain Savage Rending, the odds shifted a bit. While we were always a physical threat to the vampiric community, they tended to regard us as something to just eliminate when the chance arose, and with Silver they were pretty good at it (and we, the PC's, were never stupid enough to just fight it out... werewolves are far better at the guerilla warfare game than vampires tend to be). Once we were able to deal direct aggravated (few werewolves carry flamethrowers around with them - though I will admit that our Irraka did carry magnesium flares with him at all times) this trend drastically changed.

                    When we dusted the first vampire that tried to ambush one of us (2 of us had Savage Rending at that point), word got out. After a bit, they discovered that the Cahalith was no longer someone to trifle with (similar to Pendragon's example character above, he could throw high teen Dice (with a Fetish allowing him 8 Again) and had 22 Health Boxes towards the middle/end of the campaign) because he was capable of taking on pretty much all comers, and when the vampires DID try to gangbang him en masse, they discovered why he was the most feared Werewolf in the city. NONE of that would have happened had he not had Savage Rending (and Spirit Pack as well).

                    Sometimes you need that threat of instant and immediate death that Aggravated Damage brings to the table. You don't need to resort to it all the time, of course (most of our fights remained non-aggravated throughout the campaign), but an armed society is a polite society as it were. Losing that core ability from 1st Edition (Savage Rending) for the ability to Eat for Aggravated was just a horrible choice, in my opinion. Yes, now anyone can do it essentially for free, but I feel that something very thematic was lost. No worries, though.

                                                        Originally posted by Lashet View Post
                                                        1) Cuz VtR is the only gameline which is comparable with Forsaken and have 2.0 ed now. Demons and Beasts are too high level.
                                                        This is simply not true. While we can argue that one gameline is "more powerful" than another (whatever that means), they never reach a point where one completely and utterly overpowers another. They all always remain competative with each other, and each gameline still remains the best at their individual focuses.

                                                        2) Cuz Fur vs Fangs is famous stable trope.
                                                        I will conceed this point. It is famous, if rather annoying. However, I find it ironic that the reverse isn't true - few vampires care about a rivalry with werewolves. No one brings up about how badass a vampire needs to be compared to a were.

                                                        3) Personaly, I don't like vamps - they arw arrognant and proud. So, if werewolves won't be strong enough they will be "second-class" as in any film, show or book when they live in one univers. Although, the last point is totally IMO.
                                                        Anyone can be arrogant - that's an aspect of being human. The Ven might be, but that's the same with the Storm Lords and Silver Fangs. Awakened mages, on the other hand? That's the game's theme.

                                                        On the other hand, look at Harry Potter or Dresden - second fiddle wolves, while vamps don't even exist. The simple fact of the matter is that, when a supernatural is the focus of the media (book, film, whatever), everyone else is second fiddle. This is even true in werewolf-focused novels - the wolves are dominant, and everyone else is second fiddle. Its just that werewolf stories are in the minority, compared to others, for a number of reasons.

                                                        Originally posted by Ephsy
                                                        Vampires can pretty much say bye-bye when the Hunter in Darkness finds their Haven.
                                                        The reverse is also true - a werewolf can pretty much say bye-bye when a Mehket finds their sleeping place. Using the Kiss on an unsuspecting sleeping target is as much of a death sentence as a silver bullet to the brain in this case. Lets be honest, attacking anyone while they're asleep is pretty much a win situation for the attacker.

                                                        This is pretty much the problem with any crossover penis measuring contests. People either give one side advantages, the other side disadvantages, or they completely ignore the inherent limitations of the other supernatural's powers. Or they try to compare specific builds of one species to a generic stereotype minus all powers or merits of another. Ephsy's example falls apart when we start looking at low Blood Potency vampire - the damage is simply spread over too much time to affect any combat. Daytime no longer reduces their dice pools, and only deals damage if we're talking about direct exposure. Sneaking into a haven without windows is little different than sneaking into anyone's house while they're asleep.

                                                        As well, it ignores bodyguards. While vampires aren't as pack oriented as werewolves, they are very much                                                         subordinate oriented - ghouls, revenants, and thralls are all very common. And, yes, that IS an advantage to being a vampire, as there's a reason there's so many covenant-based merits that grant access to such.


                                                        Werewolves are the best at hunts, yes. In fact, I would likely say that a werewolf that managed to hunt down a vampire to their haven does have the advantage. Its their specialty. I would say the same thing about a werewolf hunting down a sleeping Beast to their Lair, or a demon to their Bolthole. Actually, hunting down a demon to the bolt hole would probably have already caused their Cover to shred massively, so they're likely already long gone with angels on their tail - the mere act of hunting them has effectively defeated them, or at least massively weakened them.

                                                        Interestingly, in a direct fight, a werewolf in Garou actually has the advantage over a demon that Went Loud. Even with their high Premium, that demon doesn't have many options that can outdamage a raging werewolf's ability to heal all damage. And the demon is on a timer, with a Rank 5 hunter angel heading right for them after this particular stunt.

                                                        Meanwhile, a direct fight between a Beast and Wolf has a number of potential outcomes, depending on the individual builds. There's no way to generalize this - while the werewolf has a number of innate abilities that directly apply to combat, the Beast has none. Its all Atavasms, Nightmares, Birthrights, and merits. If we're bringing in specific stuff for the Begotten, then we can bring in Fortified Form, Living Weapon, wolf fighting styles, fetishes, and Full Moon / Strength Gifts for the Uratha. The vast majority of combat powers of the Children still rely on spending Satiety as well if they intend on going up against another supernatural, and that's a far more precious resource than essence. Hells, the most effective combat Beast is already going to be low in the first place! I actually give advantage to the wolf here. They're naturally better at hunting and fighting, imho, and I feel that a specific werewolf build has more options than a specific Beast build. Which isn't that surprising. The Wolf Must Hunt. The Beast... must pick and choose new family.

                                                        In a wolf v. vamp fight? Natural claws and fangs of the wolf already ignore the undead flesh*, reduce everything to bashing property of vampires, actually removing one of the strongest advantages vamps have in random fights. That, plus the frenzy ability, makes the best tactic for the vampire to be grappling and try to feed. They drink blood and heal, the wolf naturally heals, and they're in a stalemate without adding on powers. With powers, we start getting into things like Dominate v. Dominance, or Stealth+senses v. Obfuscate+Auspex, or using silver weapons against fire powers, and that just gets messy and overcomplicated, and pointless.


                                                        * This particular ability is actually the main reason I'm particularly dismissive of the idea of agg-claws specifically for vampires. Wolves come with an anti-vamp ability built in. And all other prey already have weaknesses that the game wants you to focus on.


                                                        EDIT - I think I should also point out that vampires, werewolves, mummies, and prometheans are the only game lines where the PC supers are naturally violent / physical characters. Vamps have blood buffs and frenzy. Wolves have shapeshifting and death rages. Prommies were super-survivors with a lesser version of the blood buff. I only remember reading that freshly awakened mummies were insanely strong, I forget the details. Something to do with pillars?

                                                        That said, mages don't have any physical powers that come naturally - they get mage sights and a mage armor, and the rest of the time they need time and preparation. Demons are natural spies and liars, but nothing says they'll ever have access to combat powers or forms. Its simple for the Begotten to never once have any physical powers. Changelings are awesome at pledges (wishes?), dreams, and illusions and making them real, but that's not the same thing as fighting prowress. Even Sin-Eaters, with their ghostly ability to survive death, had to invest in certain powers for offensive ability. Just as easily, we could have had oracles and necromancers that could only bleed plasm as they were chopped apart. Having defenses without combat ability to back it up does not make a splat adapt at physical things.


                                                        So, given all the above, I really think that wolves are naturally very powerful compared to other splats during a crossover, let alone their natural enemies. There's already a large number of advantages against six of the ten lines in combat. The vamps already have to be wary of wolf claws. That leaves prommies and mummies, neither of which have a 2e conversion yet, and frankly are rare in the first place, making such crossover an exceedingly niche case. So, no. We don't need agg-claws because crossover. Hells, in the case of Sin-Eaters, there's no point to agg claws because of how bulwarks work.
