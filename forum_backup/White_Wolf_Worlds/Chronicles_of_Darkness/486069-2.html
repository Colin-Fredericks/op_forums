        The NWoD has so much vagueness, rumour and possibility because that gives the impression of a grand unifed cosmology which is massively complicated without having to actually come up with one (which isn't really feasible for fiction). If you don't want a kitchen sink, either don't do cross-over, or ditch the idea of a coherent unified cosmology that your players can easily grasp.

                            It's worth considering that each of the different supernatural groups wants something different.

                            Vampires need blood, of course, but they're well equipped to get it for themselves under most circumstances. A more precious commodity to them is security. Vampires have perhaps the most restrictive weaknesses of any of the major supernatural types; even mortals stand a decent chance of dispatching an elder vampire if they can find the dark place where he sleeps and figure out how to let a little sunlight in. One of the main reasons that vampires tend to cultivate vast conspiracies and accumulate wealth and power is so that they can prevent just that thing from happening.

                            When vampires encounter unfamiliar supernatural beings, they may have any number of reactions, but on some level they're always going to be considering what kind of threat this creature poses to their continued existence. Mages in particular are likely to spook them, particularly if they're not aware that most mages probably can't conjure fireballs from thin air or produce sunlight underground, but the other beings with which they share the night present unique threats as well. Werewolves can track a vampire better than any human, changelings can pop up seemingly anywhere out of some kind of parallel dimension, and so on. Overall, the default reaction is probably for the vampire to avoid being noticed by the other supernatural, though desperate or arrogant bloodsuckers might consider it worth the risk to try and make some kind of deal with them, or to manipulate them toward the vampire's other goals.


                            Forsaken werewolves, on the other hand, are driven less by sheer survival instinct than by what they see as their sacred purpose. No werewolf really expects to die quietly in bed at a ripe old age, and for many werewolves dying is a small price to pay if it helps them accomplish their goals. As such, I can see them taking a more flexible approach to inter-species relations.

                            Let's say some crazy Dracula cultist is poisoning a locus with his weird experiments. The local pack will want to put him down, but if they're smart and perceptive, they may realize that he's part of a larger organization that could mean bigger trouble than one lone bloodsucker. They might try to isolate him and take him out without revealing themselves to the other vampires, or they might investigate this vampire society more closely. If they figure out that he has enemies among the other bloodsuckers, they may try to reach some sort of arrangement with them in order to take down the offending leech and perhaps even his entire rotten clique.

                                                VtM and WtA are vastly different games from VtR and WtF.

                                                Werewolves and vampires don't have universal beliefs on anything, except obvious things like hunting and blood is good, or silver and sunlight is bad. Some vampires believe they're cursed by god, some believe they're gifted by the Crone, some think it's just a disease, and the strix say they started it all.

                                                As I said, some werewolves might think vampires are the souls of dead werewolves, or spirit-claimed. And if vampires are particularly chaotic, they can bring out spirits that the werewolves might not like on their territory. What spirits werewolves won't like on their territory is going to depend on the individual pack.
