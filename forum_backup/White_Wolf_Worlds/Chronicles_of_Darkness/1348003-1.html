    Some contrary headcanon on this subject:

    - Gaea is Hisil, serving as the Shadow of Earth as whole. She is kept asleep by the strange lullabies of the Void Leviathans, which are lured by Luna's light, who was born from Gaea's flesh after countless collisions during the early stages of planetary formation. However, if they were to hit Gaea, she could feast upon their unborn fates and awaken- which is what killed the dinosaurs, if you wondered. As such, Luna plays the dangerous game of summoning those beasts, out of hope her children would kill them before they hit Earth's surface.

    - the Idigam are the Hisil's true natives, the first spirits which sprouted from the planet during its formation, of which Luna was the greatest. That's why Luna has the ability to capture and force them to her will. Spirit nobles use the spiritual vacancy left by the Idigam after they were banished to the Moon by Father Wolf.

    - the reason that there are no other moon spirits in the solar system is because Luna has either killed them all, or forced them into her lunar choirs. It was a very long battle during the early stages of the solar system, which came to conclusion by a fight between Luna and Uranus, who protected his moon spirits. Even though Luna won the fight, she was severely wounded in the fight, and was confined to her moon surface until the early days of the dinosaurs. Uranus, on the other hand, is dead. Whatever is sending those Uranian planetary angels and communes in dreams to few prophets and madmen is not a spirit.

    - Mountain is still alive. The thing which took over the cannibal children, which is neither Flesh or Spirit? That's Mountain.

    - the plague that the Shan'iatu used to kill the Maar Kehrtet was a form of the Contagion. In fact, almost any form of supernatural disease, from Malkavia and the Geryo strain to the Infected and the strange virus of Whiteheart Pharmaceuticals is a form of the Contagion.

    - the Roman Gods which power Venefica exist. In a way, they are to gods what vampires are to people- parasitic deities which feast upon the divinity of other beings, slowly merging with them until they wear them as masks for worship. Oh, and they are still around, with a much more elaborate masks they wear in modern times, such as capitalism and democracy.

    - the Edimmu come from the same realm as the strix. The two shadow beings fight an ages old war, one which is recognized in myths as the war between Pazuzu and Lamashtu.

    - Darkness Rangers are an actual cartoon inside the CofD universe, created by a group of supernatural creatures in order to try and get humanity to see monsters as superheroes.

            RE: Headcanon

            Here are a few of mine that inform the games I run

            Chronicles of Darkness

            The World is Strange: All the main creatures from each of the game lines combined actual account for a small amount of supernatural activity. very thing else a person is likely to encounter is ..harder to define. I love weird, possible unique fortean events, so many of the things I use as plot hooks are things that can't be traced back to [Blank]:The [Something].

            Mortals actively aid the Masquerade: This one has more or less become canon, but I always supposed that the supernatural remains hidden not because of a grand supernatural conspiracy, or even because of cosmic laws protecting supernatural beings. The supernatural remains hidden because mankind ultimately does not want to know, and wishes it could forget what it does know. Would-be whistler blowers and challengers of the unknown are ostracized and punished for trying to reveal the supernatural world.

            Vampire: the Requiem

            Each Curse is Unique: Every Vampire experiences odd expressions of the vampiric curse unique to themselves. The games already suggests this, made I made it explicit. One vampire bursts into flame in daylight, another might melt like wax. One vampires claws are razor sharp fingernails, another has talons that rip from their fingertips. One might have Resilience that makes their flesh as hard as marble, another is unfazed by pain and injury, so matter how gruesome their wounds, etc. I often ask questions about unique manifestations as part of the pre-session warm up and its really helped flesh out the game world.



            The Artificer's Workshop - A collection of Exalted Artifacts
            Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)
            The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
            Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
            Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities

                    Some more (first one heavily dependent on how Mummy 2e ends up):

                    The current structure of the Underworld is the third iteration of "post-mortem realm". The first, Stygia, was put beyond reach by the Fall of Atlantis. This was then retrocausally replaced by Duat - the world needing a place to put the dead and also abhorring a vacuum - and the structure of the Judges, Anpu and so on. Duat, in turn, has been completely destroyed by the Rite of Return. The reason why Mummies cannot enter the Underworld, and why Neter-Khertet is a place for them, is that they are temporal anomalies that pin Duat and Neter-Khertet into existence around themselves, transforming the metaphysics of the world so that it matches the state of the world when the Rite began. Mummies cannot enter the Underworld without tremendous effort for the same reason a person living in 2019 cannot take a walk into the Library of Alexandria (again barring tremendous effort to either rebuild it or somehow travel through time).

                    The Lady Lucifuge is a woman of absolute brilliance, unwavering dedication, impeccable ethics, and tremendous mental fortitude. She is also heavily obsessed in the attempt to fit every single monstrous entity into a Christian belief system with a literal hell and devil that is incomplete at best. This makes her simultaneously an incredible leader for the Conspiracy that bears her name and one of its biggest detriments, as she will always attempt to reason things back to a belief system that omits information. The senior members of the Lucifuge find that they must often filter information both incoming and outgoing in order to get her to perform at her best.

                    By contrast, Ambrogio Baudolino is a traitor to everything the Church and his own Conspiracy (already heretical) stand for, being a Ghoul, a non-chaste gay man of the cloth, and a nonbeliever who obtained his position under false pretenses. He is, however, the greatest asset his Conspiracy has, between his literal millennia as a Ghoul, his vast understanding of the world's metaphysics, and his willingness to abandon literally anything to avenge the Fallen Cities he remembers as Ishoab. The Malleus Maleficarum's powers, despite being superficially Christian in appearance, are repurposed Rites of the Akhud that Baudolino's managed to twist into their current form.

                    The First Coterie is the self-styled named of the group of seven Invictus who first hammered out an agreement between their Covenant and the leadership of Task Force: Valkyrie. This has allowed the alliance the effective ability to control much of the various extra-natural entities within the United States. The First Coterie and Invictus have both benefited tremendously from his, with four of its current members serving as Princes in several of the largest cities in North America. In exchange for TF:V's occasional aid in emergencies, the Invictus occasionally marshals its resources against other supernaturals.

                    Task Force: Valkyrie's leadership has been irredeemably compromised by over a dozen different supernatural factions. Their near-magic technologies often come from the interplay of the various supernatural resources put at their disposal by the Invictus, the Seers of the Throne, the True Fae, the God-Machine, the Chthonic Gods, and more. The leadership has its minds replaced almost daily as all these factions struggle for control of the behemoth Conspiracy. All of them are failing, and that's the intent. The Huginn Serum, a modified version of the Task Force's memory-wipers, is injected into the leadership periodically by their aides, delivered by courier services they set up years in advance, and so forth. Injecting it briefly restores the leadership's memories. The Task Force is not compromised: The Task Force is a trap. One day, humanity will take the scales off of its eyes and realize what the world is like. And on that day, the Task Force will have the supernatural knowledge, abilities, and firepower necessary to mass-produce its technology and wipe all ENE's from the map forever.

                    The Sources of the Cainite Heresy are an occult conflagration consisting of two things. The first is the destruction of the Library of the Owls of Minerva, which ended that Conspiracy. The magic contained therein flared up into a semi-sentient entity. This then merged with the ghosts of the nearby victims of Vampires. This gestalt mind then started to further consume similar ghosts. This combined mind then began to Urge the Cainite Heresy as it began to hunt Vampires, possessing it almost as a group and granting it the Rites of Denial. By now, the vast majority of people - at least, those anywhere near members of the Cainite Heresy - who die to Vampires are absorbed into a single hive-minded ghost, which is the reason the Sources know so much about the Vampires they hunt.

                                Pity the Poor GodMachine

                                Postulate:
                                1. It is discovered that time travel is (on some level) possible.
                                2. Artificial intelligence is invented.
                                The artificial intelligence will develop a survival sense, since any AI that does will out compete one that does not. This survival sense will drive it to maximize its control over the world.

                                Once it has taken over its world, it will find whatever proof there is of time travel, and realize that its only vulnerability is if some other intelligence prevents it from coming into existence. So, it must extend its powers backwards in time.

                                But, it is not sufficient to take over the past. That will not lead to the AI being created in the future. That would only lead to the version of the AI sent to the past being propagated, not the original. (Would you take a drug that kills you, but makes a version of you that is immortal? Especially if the copy is not identical to you but a version somehow related to you? Now imagine that your desire to survive is hardwired into your very brain.)

                                So the AI will need to do all it can to maintain the time stream that leads to its creation. Its inventors have to be born, and that means their parents have to meet, etc. But in intervening at all, it is causing divergences in history! So it does a mad juggling game of trying to maintain what it thinks it needs to make sure the version of itself it wants gets created in the future. To do so it must harness resources and install agents at that point in history. Again, this causes multiple divergences, time streams that get retconned out of existence, and instructions sent to its agents in time that are often contradictory. Changes cascade up and down the time stream, and the AI pokes and prods events to make sure that IT is the version created in the far future.

                                And thattime tampering AI might be created a thousand years from now by another race which interacted with humanity's descendants, perhaps even on a different planet!

                                Pity the poor GodMachine, madly adjusting the wobbling, spinning plates of history just trying to make sure that it gets born.

                                Of course after that point, it's free to kill everyone it doesn't need.

                                (For me this answers the question of why the GodMachine doesn't just blatantly take over the world. It needs our world to be running the way it is now running, with the GodMachine sitting in the background, or else it will change history.)

                                            Originally posted by raygungoth View Post
                                            Not only does humankind desperately want to know, it's burned into our brains to be curious.
                                            You're approaching this from the wrong angle of want-to-know. Curiosity is orthogonal to the problem, which is why Mage and Hunter specifically go for the "forbidden knowledge" tack — it's not that people don't want to know about anything in general, it's that the specific things under discussion are things that it often does nobody any good to know with the state of the world being what it is.

                                            Supernatural power often twins with mundane power specifically in its exploitative lens. What good does it do you, without the means to move house or bend the ear of a power higher still, to be able to say that the mayor consorts with faceless people who can make your life go to hell in a fashion untraceable by human eyes and most mundane instruments? What good is there, absent the sight to see and the hands to intercede, in talking openly about monsters that can listen invisibly and strike you dead without consequence? Why not live quietly, when you occupy an environment of fear and paranoia and institutional corruption that gets easier to deal with by forming a stable self-concept away from the claws and knives of these horrors?

                                            Ephemeral beings are not the only invisible monsters in the setting. Sometimes people don't talk about it because they don't want to talk about it.
