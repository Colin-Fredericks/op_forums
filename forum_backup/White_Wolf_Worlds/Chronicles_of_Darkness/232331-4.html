                It’s also possible that the Waterborn/Swimmerskin distinction will be kept, with the former being a Kith representing fae who have an affinity with the element of water while the latter is a Kith representing animal-like fae who are at home in the water. As ArcaneArts pointed out, things are still in a state of flux; while Kiths are almost certainly being generalized for broader support of multiple Seemings, the specifics of how they’re being generalized are still being hammered out.

                Elaborating on this a bit: there are only two places where I see the possibility of bestial Kiths and elemental Kiths overlapping, and thus leaving us with the predicament of “is it a beast or an elemental?” becoming vague if those two Seemings get combined: avians in the air and swimmers in the sea. No other animal kiths have such direct elemental parallels: nobody is going to mistake a Runnerswift for an elemental or a afireheart for a beast.

                                        08-18-2014, 05:05 PM
                                        Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                        Uxas: bear in mind that in this setup, a Runnerswift or Hunterheart has an “animal affinity”, regardless of which Seeming you choose to focus on. A Fairest Hunterheart might be like a lion; a Darkling Hunterheart might be some sort of nocturnal predator; and so on. Likewise, a Fireheart or Waterborn has an affinity with a force of nature — again, regardless of which Seeming it has. Likewise, the Beast and/or Elemental Seeming should be applicable to Kiths that are neither animal nor element: what would an Elemental Muse look like, or a Bestial Smith? If Beast and Elemental are to survive as separate Seemings, they need to be distinguishable from each other in some manner other than “animals” vs. “elements”.
                                        Okay, so for the purpose of this conversation, I'm going to lay out the issue as flatly as I can before I dig into it. I should mention that, starting this, I don't have a conclusion. I might have one once I reach the end of this.

                                        Are the Beast and Elemental evocative enough on their own to merit a Seeming, or should the twain become Primal(for lack of a better word)?

                                        And I'll be honest, that's a tough question. I've been spinning around in circles on that one in my head, reading the books and doing thought experiments, and I still don't have a clear answer.

                                        I think I want to approach this from two angles-what I inherently think of on the words Elemental, Beast, and Primal, versus what the books had to say on Elementals and Beasts and what Primal would mean in response to that.

                                        I'm doing it that way because when I think about it without reference, the case for Primal starts off with a disturbing amount of sense. As has been pointed out, both Elemental and Beast are seemings that seem more or less defined by the fact that they're grab bags for specific kiths, which makes the concept fall apart a little when you no longer have to be an Elemental to be a water fae or a Beast to be a Coyote-wannabe. Going from the the kithless angle, People can see what an Ogre is and what a Fairest is easy enough, a Darkling with a little work and the Wizened with some explanation. But most people( I don't my own personal results, as I'm unusual) won't come up with chimerical beasts or clashing acts of creation when you say Beast or Elemental. For Beasts you either get Beast from Beauty and the Beast(Who actually could be squared as kithless, I suppose) or the Big Bad Wolf or Aslan(Yes, I know he's a Keeper,shut up) or stuff like that. For Elementals, people will quickly think of a particular element-a human-shaped flame will probably dance into their mind first, or something like that. But that's not a seeming thing, that's a kith thing.

                                        And on a casual think, what does it mean to be a Beast or a Elemental? You, more so than others, are now made one with the world, it's just a matter of a permanence(well, more than that). That's easily compoundable.

                                        But on the opposite hand, doesn't that just make it even more vague and bland?

                                        Say what you will about Kith specificness, at least I can tell someone I'm playing a Beast or an Elemental and nothing else(save maybe Court), and people will at least have something in mind of what I'm talking about. There's an image in their head-fuzzily painted, sure, but they can picture it.

                                        What the flying fuck does Primal mean to the average joe? How do they even know what I mean?

                                        Naturally, the Kiths being frontloaded solve this issue a bit, but let's go with the two example I mentioned in an earlier post-let's assume there's a kith that is"You are fae that has to do with water"(We'll call it Aquatic) or "You are physiologically designed for ripping things apart"(We'll call it Ripperhand). Okay, so you're an Aquatic Primal. So, are you a mermaid or a water sprite? What about a Ripperhand Primal? Are you a honey badger or a razor-sharp hailstorm?As casual short hand goes, how does a ST begin planning for that?(And I've had times where I needed to start planning based on nothing but a Seeming, a Kith, and a Court for a PC). That's sort of thing works for Awakening's Thyrsus, but Thyrsus don't have to luck errant hairs out of their tails or shave their beards with hedge clips. I don't know why I brought up that last point, it was relevant earlier in the day.

                                        Without going into the books and just thinking on it, the problem comes from taking two broad grab bags and turning it into one grab bag. The two aren't strong enough to stand on their own, but together they don't stand any better. Are they easier to improve combined or on their own? I don't know. At least there's some definition with Beast and Elemental, but I don't think that's grounds for leaving them as is.

                                        I think the casual thought doesn't serve us immensely well here. I'm gonna turn to the books. What do they say are the hallmarks of the seemings?

                                        Beasts are marked by sensation and sensuality, by compressed time in the form of the eternal moment we call now, by instinct, the inherent sense of just knowing what to do, and the struggle with reason and with civilization.

                                        Elementals are marked by alienation(aren't they all?), by stretched time in the form of the fleeting eternity we call always, by harmony, the inherent sense of just being one with the world, and the struggle of empathy and human relation.

                                        Groot, by the way, is probably not actually an Elemental-that guy was human as hell, within the purview of this discussion.

                                        Okay, so what does this mean? Well, on their own, they are actually way different from each other. Of course, that conversation doesn't last too long long when we start stripping them apart. What can Darklings, Ogres, Fairest, and Wizened do that they can do also, and what's left?

                                        Beasts have a lot in common with Fairest at a glance, but in a different way given that they're moved by ecstasy rather than simple sensation-the end results look kind of similar though. The idea of sense and sensation also carries over to the Darklings, in a sense that the Darklings tend to see and hear more than most-no on pays spiders or shadows much attention, after all. the savagery of some Beasts durances can't be told apart from the brutality of Ogres a lot of the time, even though there is a reason they each get a different word(yes, there's a deliberate word choice there). The fact that a lot of Beasts are used as hunting hounds or wall-pulling oxen does not escape the Wizened's notice. And it's worth mentioning that Winter Masques touches on this very fact.

                                        By and large, it seems like Beasts aren't just beasts-a lion can be used to kill(Ogre), to impress(Fairest), to stalk(Darkling, to a certain extent), or even for labor(Wizened, and yes some African cultures used lions for labor). The only thing beasts are left with is the Wild Beasts, and even then the archetypes work against it.

                                        What about Elementals?

                                        They're a little harder to split apart, and that's because a lot of what they did was simply be. Which, funnily enough, is a point you can tie to Beasts, with their sense of compressed time-there's nothing beyond their senses, including their sense of self and now. They are what they are, and they act accordingly.

                                        Which, unfortunately for us advocates, makes a very strong and compelling case for the Primal seeming. They are the ancient fae of the world, the fae who say "Thus as it has been, so it shall always be, and today is just a reflection between". They are the voice of the forest and the ocean and the sky. It's a pretty powerful and compelling idea, with fae who might be wild and active each day, but perhaps repetitive and caught in their cycles.

                                        Yeah, I can actually warm up to that idea. I like my distinctive seemings of Beasts and Elementals, but the Primal idea, after some thought, does seem like a neat child of both.

                                        So, conclusion.....Yeah, compound them.

                                        EDIT: But come up with a better name than Primal, because seriously, what the fuck are you even talking about when you say that to people who don't know?

                                                "be a shark (or a fish or an octopus or seal" is only different from "be a mermaid or a selkie" in the seeming-related flavor. If you've decided to "be a shark", but as a Fairest, that would be like a fierce warrior mermaid with a shark tail and sharp teeth, really.

                                                I'm going to slide in with the "Don't push Beast and Elemental into the same role", because, like Heavy Arms said (which is actually a pretty good answer to Dataweaver's question? So it seems like it got missed by some people, I think), Beasts and Elemental are operating on a much different difference than just "animal" versus "human" -- Elementals are practically defined by how they can't relate to normal people, or even sentient things anymore, while Beasts are defined by how much more alive they are than the people around them.

                                                The real problem, mechanically speaking, to me is that Beasts get a bonus to interacting with living creatures -- they get 8 again and a free specialty to interacting with animals, and can spend glamour to increase dice pools based around Presence and Composure. The only people who are better than Beasts at dealing with people are Fairest, and this is actually stated in the Beast write-up for Winter Masques.

                                                Meanwhile, Elementals are actually worse at dealing with people than Wizened are. They don't get the ten again on rolls involving Manipulation, Empathy, Expression, Persuasion OR Socialize unless it's to with the elements. People are talking about how the proposal for Hunterheart Elementals are too impersonal, and not hunting enough, and that's the thing about Elementals. They ARE impersonal. A hunterheart would be the blizzard no one could run from, sneaker waves from the ocean, lightning that doesn't need to strike twice because the first strike left it's target dead. The scary thing about it is that when it's on your scent, you can't shake it and you can't beat it, you just have to hope it get what it wants and you manage to survive.

                                                Leave the "personal" to the beasts -- Elementals just get the job done.
