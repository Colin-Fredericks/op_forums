                                        04-06-2016, 02:38 PM
                                        Alright, I promised I'd try and get a review of what's actually been written in the docs at some point, so I finally found the time and connection to do so.

                                        Very first thought... what order am I supposed to read these in? Seriously, I have no idea. The doc titles are clear enough about the content, but it'd be a lot easier to put things into proper perspective if everything after the Introduction was given some sort of Chapter Number.

                                        Introduction
                                        Theme: Biblical Tragedy
                                        I've noted before that I'm cautious about this theme. The thing is, we've already got Vampire & Promethean for wangstfesting. Still, the description here makes me cautiously optimistic; the emphasis on the "biblical" scale of the tragedy, on the idea of personal failings being more a metaphor for the eternal battle between Order and Chaos, makes the "wallow in your misery" aspect feel downplayed. The tragedy is in your effect on the world around you, not on you personally.

                                        Mood: Stagnation
                                        First of all, I'm not sure if I'd call "Stagnation" a mood. The text conveys the feel of the Mood quite well, but to me, I just don't know if "stagnation" is an accurate summary of it. I think Predestination is probably more accurate.

                                        Anyway, the important part here is that this description is a lot more... I guess optimistic than the impression I had of it from the descriptions of the game's mood in the forum. Yes, it talks about how the Wicked Tribe are trapped in their cycle of events, but it places equal emphasis on how there is always the hope that, this time, things will be different, and that with their divine power, Leviathans have both hope and a real chance.

                                        This is important to me. I don't care how much an author goes on about "True Art is Angsty", if I utter those Eight Deadly Words about a game, I'm not going to touch it. Fortunately, this intro alone gives me hope that there is more to this game than doom and gloom. In fact, so far, I feel I could portray this akin to Lovecraft's "The Stars Are Not Right - But Will Be". The predestination mood actually mixes well with the idea touched upon earlier that the world goes through cycles of ascent and descent... how was it put?

                                        Originally posted by ajf115 View Post
                                        Plus it works well with the whole 'cyclic Chaoskampf' thing. Each time either side - leviathan or Marduk - thinks its won, it's only a matter of time before the cracks start to appear. Marduk 'killed' the Progenitors in prehistory and built this world upon their corpses. They build their towers of reason ever higher upon the bodies until they crack under their own weight and come tumbling down into the Primordial sea. The Progenitors rise again and eventually are slain again.

                                        When it comes to Leviathan itself, the themes rather remind me of a yin-yang symbol. The leviathans are by nature monstrous beings - evil as we understand it -, but they have the potential to do good, even if it's far more difficult for them than it is for humans. Marduk represents reason, progress, humanity. But also the problems with those things. Reason is cold, lifeless. Progress tears down that which came before, often indiscriminately. Humanity can create art, music and wondrous technology, but is capable of horrible cruelty. Perhaps this is too grey for your vision but for me the grey-and-grey morality is part of what makes both oWoD and CoD so interesting.
                                        Ah, that's it. Yes, this is what I'd like out of Leviathan, and from the Introduction, I may get what I want.

                                        Mad Arab
                                        Not really a lot to say here. Just a fun little bit of fluff notes, though kind of incomplete. It makes me wonder if Corvus Rex is a native English speaker, because there's quite a few subtle errors and almost-but-not-quite word choices scattered here. "Blaspheme", for example, is the verb-form of the term he's intending to use in the Dagon notes; blaspheme means "to commit blasphemy". In other docs, I've made suggestions where I could to fix these, but I can't do that here.

                                        Systems and Advantages
                                        I figured I should look at the crunch block before I went to the more fluff-related stuff, so this was my next stop along my reviewing list.

                                        Surprisingly, no spelling errors popping out here that I saw, maybe because Corvus Rex has been spending so much time and focus on this particular chapter?

                                        Anyway, I'm not much of a crunch guy. Mechanics don't usually have a lot of meaning to me, so I can't give the most elaborate and painstaking of reviews here. Some things that did catch my attention:
                                        * The Ocean Minds sidebar really encapsulates the Leviathan's mind and its nightmarish blending of eldritch abomination and primordial beast well; the difficulty in controlling its own mind nicely offset by the way that a leviathan's mind is "reader: beware" for any other race.
                                        * I remembered that "Tranquility" is supposed to be a shorthand for "the lower of either Phronesis or Eunoia", or at least I think that's what it's supposed to be, but the doc could stand to make that distinction a little more obvious.

                                        Strains
                                        Here we reach the first big "draw" of the gameline, the "species" sub-lines.

                                        Bahamutans: My first impression of these guys was, honestly, really positive. If these are setting the standard for the Strains, I'm going to be quite impressed with this section. Villain protagonists are very hard to do right, but these guys manage it pretty well. Attention is paid to their negative traits, but also to their positive ones; their need to protect, their attempts to focus their natural urges and influences towards the benevolence of their Beloved. The Bahamutan strain, at a reading, seems to embody not merely a Vice, but an Anti-Virtue, the ideal of Piety or Zeal embraced and taken to that warped point where a person will shamelessly do the most atrocious things because someone else provides the assurance that to do so is right. The Tribe may be The Most Wicked, but at a glance, even with their Slothful natures and their synonymy with conformity and tradition, the Bahamutans feels very, very sympathetic. Very well done.

                                        Dagonites: Although certainly not as inherently sympathetic as the Bahamutans, there's still an all-too-human and recognizable aspect to the children of Dagon. Being torn between their divine power and their awareness of the even worse things out there, they're really not so different to the humans who worship them, and even as they can come across as bullies and frauds, they are ultimately based in urges man felt since he capable of recognizing shapes in the darkness beyond the nest and which still remains a prominent aspect in people's mind today. They are the Oldest Law and its struggle against the Young Laws, a reflection in miniature of the great struggle symbolized by Leviathans as a whole. The section on their private ecology is filled with fridge brilliance, calling to mind the traditionally fecund yet irresponsibly cantankerous and fractious dynasties of pagan gods, homaging their Lovecraftian counterpart's roots in the fear of miscegenation and birth defects, all wrapped in the horrible tragedy that a Dagonite can either be alone, or see its progeny fall into madness and mutation. So far, two for two.

                                        Lahamin: This is the strain I have the least to say about, thus far. Nothing really sticks out about them, good or bad. The creepy crawly with eerie prophetic powers is an interesting idea, but their portrayal just... doesn't make them really catch my attention. They feel kind of gridholed as "the stealthy/scouty ones"... just, really, really bland. The most I can say about these Leviathans is that they sound the most lonely and sorrowful of their kind, alienated even from their own kind, and as much as it's at least partially self-induced, it kind of makes me want to comfort one.

                                        Nu: This strain, more than any other, seems to emphasize the idea that the Leviathans are as much victims of their natures as they are victimizers. Reading the description of these guys just brings to mind the idea of being the only sane man surrounded by insane idiots, and that makes me shudder in fellow feeling. I feel sympathetic towards these guys just because I can contemplate and understand the kind of hell they have to live in because of their very nature. Alongside the Bahamutans, the Nu strain are the first to jump to mind when I consider the words "good/sympathetic Leviathan". Another well-written strain.

                                        Oceaneads: This strain feels like a counterpoint to the Dagonites, the opposite side of their coin. I can easily see the connections linking the Oceaneads to pagan deities, to the gods as beings whose glory is matched by their pettiness. They are the Beautiful Elite of the Tribe, the embodiment of man's willingness to subliminate itself to that which it finds beautiful, even as the source of their worship laughs at how pathetic man is. Sympathetic? No, not really, but then, the Pretty Ones of the various splats rarely are. Understandable, and relatable? Oh, most definitely.

                                        Tanninim: Alpha predators. Beautifully contrasting the Oceaneads, who are the strain that represents The Evil of God, the Tanninim are The Evil of Beasts, the most savage aspects of nature given vehement life. Worship and fear are so very much intertwined, after all. And yet, they're not entirely unsympathetic. When one's mind and body are driven by urges from beyond the mortal era, it's only natural to seek some element of control over yourself, and your surroundings. The need for control, the need to submit, these have a huge impact on humanity's collective psyche. Our entire culture has arguably evolved out of these feelings. So, as alien and as monstrous as the Tanninim are at first glance, at the core, there's still something awfully recognizable, and understandable, behind them.

                                        Thalassans: Greedy and avaricious, and yet with a certain sense of self-awareness shown by their quote. Like the Tanninim, the Thalassans are amongst the basest and more "biological" of the Tribe, rooted in the deepest and most primordial instincts of man and beast. They are horrors, but more than anything because we can see ourselves in them so readily, and admit that there are times when we were worse. They may not be very sympathetic, but they are understandable, and that's the important thing.

                                        Overall, a fairly positive chapter in my opinion. Two strains that I can personally sympathise with effortlessly, and four that are, while darker, still not painted so utterly black that I think it's impossible to empathise with them. The only flaw that really stands out to me is the Lahamin, who just don't leave any sort of impression on me. They're just... there. That's all I can say about them.

                                        Schools
                                        I was going to do this School by School like I did for the Strains above, but eventually I just gave it up; it'd be too repetitive. Spelling/grammatical errors aside (I'm sure that [Name's] Devoted reads far more smoothly than [Name's] Devotion for the members of the School, it looks so much like Corvus Rex getting his English fouled up again), these are all excellently written. They're believable, they're sensible, they're rational - and none of them are portrayed as inherently evil, or "the bad guys". They're all different answers to the same question, but none of them feels forced or inherently flawed. Each takes pains to explain how it works out as part of a Cohort and what it can offer to Leviathans, individually and as a greater whole.

                                        And, best of all, they all counter the perception of "forced grimdark". They all have a very strong, hopeful feeling, an essential contrast to the dark tones presented in the Introduction. There's a vibrancy in the Schools that makes the hardest part deciding which one I like best of all - there's never even the slightest feeling that I would be better served going with a School-less Leviathan PC.

                                        Cults
                                        Again, mostly crunch focused, so I can't really offer a lot of brilliant insights here. I can recognize some resemblance to Mummy's 1e Cult rules, but the Leviathan version feels unique and seems to quite effectively cover the unique hassles Leviathans have to put up with. Mummies "only" use their own mystical might to impress or intimidate mortals into service, and in fact may often find themselves being treated as just toys of their own cults (generally to the cult's eternal chagrin once the Mummy is properly awake, though). Leviathans have the added blessing - and curse - of mindraping people into fanatical zealots who will give their lives for their master without a second's thought. The Fervor and Piety mechanics cover this quite nicely.

                                        Atolls
                                        A good, solid chapter, a nice blending of mechanics and fluff that adequately conveys the delicate balance between the Leviathan's raw need for meaningful human contact and the fact that toying with the emotions of a primordial monster-god are a good way to get hurt.

                                        Only thing I really have to comment on here is that, while I'm not a fan of the default assumption that "Atolls are only as such whilst human", it least the sidebar admits it is possible. I'll be ignoring that assumption in my own games, as it feels... well, kind of pointless to me.

                                        I'm also not sure why Wake Muting went from an inherent part of the Atoll's powers to something that requires merit points. But I guess it makes more sense for balancing mechanically this way, so I have no real problem with it.

                                        Lahmasu and Hybrids
                                        I just... don't have a lot to say about this chapter. Seems solid enough, but it's more crunch than anything, and it fails to really explains why Hybrids are somehow human despite their monstrous shells.

                                        School of Thunder
                                        The chapter itself... I'll be honest, it needs a lot of polish. Feels so sparse and barebones at the moment, it's hard to take a lot of interest. That said, what's there elicited a surprising reaction from me - a negative reaction. My first thought when I read the description of the school was... well, it was too profane to directly post here. Despite the fact that there's practically no detail here explaining why a Leviathan would join the School of Thunder, they still elicited a strong, hostile response. These are guys that just immediately stand out as bastards - the other Leviathans are struggling with finding their own path, and these lazy slime-suckers just betray their kin and beg like sniveling worms. A lot of potential here for antagonists, it just needs to get expanded to make more sense as to the how and the why.

                                        Ahabs
                                        I went into this chapter not really expecting a lot. I can see the fundamental point behind the creation of Ahabs, but, rather like Ghouls in Vampire: The Requiem, they just feel sort of an obligational inclusion. "They're here because you got to have them", essentially.

                                        Ahabs honestly kind of remind me of Beast's Heroes. Only with more mechanical weight and a lot better fluff. There's a genuine connection, almost intimacy, between Leviathan and Ahab that I honestly feel makes them superior to their "official" analogues. They're antagonists, but there's a real feeling of tragedy and pity to them, making me feel sorry for them even as I plot their deaths in my own games.

                                        So, congratulations on taking what I thought would be a dull, "by the numbers" enemy and turning it into something that I can readily see as the primary source for non-mortal antagonists in my own Leviathan games.

                                        Empyrean Theurgists
                                        Mortals who have become the proxies of the Young Gods, standing in opposition to those Old Gods who linger on Earth (aka The Tribe). It's a simple, yet awesome idea, and yet... the actual interpretation of them feels, honestly, "blah".

                                        They're not Mary Sues or soul-crushingly overpowering. Far from it. They just feel... flat. Boring.

                                        Maybe if the inherent hypocrisy of the Theurgists was focused on more. Yes, the Young Gods gave mankind civilization, they created law and order, but they were often terrible, terrible people themselves. Blood sacrifices and petty cruelty, hedonism and arrogance; Virtue became an excuse and a shield for the Young Gods, leading to much the same tyranny as the Old Gods.

                                        The Chaoskampf is the story of Order vs. Chaos. It is not the story of Good vs. Evil, and treating it that way is doing it a serious disservice.

                                        At the least the document admits that Theurgists aren't always progressive reformers, and that their Virtues can lead them to behaviors that are, in many ways, no better than those of the Leviathans. It's the best part in an otherwise bland chapter.

                                        Primordial Theurgists
                                        Now, these guys, they impressed me. There's not even any mechanical stuff here, it's just pure fluff, and that fluff was awesome. Scholar-mystics who, from desperation or arrogance or other understandable, human failings, seek to mystically leech power from the Leviathans to work their own occult rituals.

                                        It's great stuff, and especially because there's no clumsy attempt to make them "in the right" for it. They're insane and they're dangerous, to themselves and those around them. They're not guaranteed to succeed, and they walk the knife-edge between success and oblivion each time they power their rituals - at least, that's what I hope will be the case, because I can't think of many things more risky or more likely to blow up in your face than rummaging through a Leviathan's mind to try and siphon off some of her power.

                                        And that's good. Meddling with any sort of higher being, even a fallen primordial god, should not be something undertaken lightly. At least, not by anyone who values their life or is recognizably sane.

                                        If dramatic failures for Primordial Theurgists don't carry at least some chance of the Leviathan they were scanning immediately becoming aware of who they are and how to find them, I will be disappointed indeed.

                                        Marduk Society
                                        I left this one for last on purpose, just because I was worried about what I'd find after all of the bitter arguing and worrying commentary spouted at various points in the thread.

                                        I had a glimmering of hope when the Cults document mentions that, by comparison, Marduk as a whole could be treated as Cult with Piety 5 and Fervor 7. Still, I was cautious going in.

                                        The opening parts of the fluff further fuelled that hope. Marduk is described as corrupt almost from the beginning; Marduk's followers murdering their founder and turning against mankind in order to feed on its despair, all to preserve their own power.

                                        This is great stuff, as it actually meshes well with the cyclical nature of Order vs. Chaos and the "Tribe and Marduk aren't so different" thing I quoted so far above.

                                        The description of the Chaoskampf further fuels that hope. Yes, Leviathans aren't really suited for fighting quick, high-altitude fliers. But the Marduk Society will not just indiscriminately carpetbomb if they can avoid it. So there's more of a balance between the two than Marduk is honestly comfortable with.

                                        ...In all honesty, the more of this I read, the more I wonder where the fears about Marduk Society being overpowered and Mary Sue came from in the first place. Because I really can't see where the impression of Marduk being All Powerful came from, not if this is what was on the table to begin with.

                                        Maybe Corvus Rex was just that bad at portraying his vision for his pet group. Maybe he actually took peoples' complaints into account and retailored them. Whatever the reason, this isn't what I feared at all.

                                        Actually, reading this doc kind of makes me feel a little sorry for the Society. Between all the damage they took to their infrastructure during their civil war, the fact they were stretched to the limit to keep everything both running AND secret even before that, their issues in providing funding in a steady flow when they need so much of it just to keep all of their stuff working right (especially when it's a hairline's thinness between even a basic plane working and it being a worthless pile of junk)... Not to mention the entire subchapter of "the naked truth" which opens outright with "As an organization, Marduk is highly ineffiecient"...

                                        Well, I've stopped wondering "how the hell are the Leviathans supposed to survive with these guys running around?" and started wondering "how are these guys still clinging to existence as a coherent group?" I went in dreading a Technocracy-homaging Mary Sue group. I found a half-dead, confused, bleeding technocratic cult that, from the fluff text, wasn't actually that powerful and deadly even before it fought a civil war. Still enough teeth to have some bite, Space Station Babylon is still operating and it's still got its arsenal trained on the world below, but it's not "you get a single failed Ripple roll, your entire Cohort will be obliterated", which was the impression early comments on the Society gave.

                                        Really, about the only complaint I have left is that the doc talks about the Marduk Society having a civil war, but doesn't address any details about it. We don't know who fought, or why, and even just how much damage was done to it in the process is left a little vague.

                                        As an aside, I think it is warranted to cover Marduk's opinions of/interactions with the world around them. Yes, Marduk itself embodies Idealised Progress, but this is the World of Darkness; the contrast between what Marduk does with Super-Science and what the rest of the world does with or in the name of Progress should be given some attention. At the very least, I think it warrants mentioning as to why Marduk doesn't sell off "old, not-quite-so-super-science" gear instead of just the fact that it doesn't. Does Marduk fear the possibility that mankind may use their scientific advances to the world's detriment? Do they fear being made just a tool of the corrupt politics of the modern world, forced to wage war against the Government's definition of "the enemy" rather than the real dangers?
