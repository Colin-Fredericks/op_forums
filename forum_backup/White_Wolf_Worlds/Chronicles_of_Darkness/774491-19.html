    Mana in 1e was either incredibly rare (if you only had a Hallow, which usually meant all of 1 Mana per person per day, which often meant you couldn't reasonably expect to recharge at all during a story), or incredibly trivial (if you had an artifact, a familiar, or Prime).

                                    Originally posted by Kumiko View Post
                                    That would only be an issue for low wisdom mages or the Mad. Though I'm not liking how hard raising wisdom seems to be It's ticket for players to just say "fuck that noise" and do whatever you wanted to in the first place.
                                    The thing is, it isn't. Mages with high Wisdom still suffer the "leak" of their Nimbus. Shadow Names ground that leak in their mystical life, though, and the strength of it determines how closely connected one needs to be with the Mage for things to occur. What this means is, your average Mage is more likely to see odd things occurring. A high Wisdom Thyrsus might see their prayer site start to become a locus, attracting spirits; a low Wisdom Obrimos starts to attract street preachers to their usual subway route, who start proclaiming the glory of She Who Lies Beneath.

                                    Whatever the case, it sounds like it's largely narrative in origin, and that's a thing I like.

                                    As far as raising Wisdom goes, I think you're missing some of the point. Morality stats have tried previously to be a way to keep players moral, rather than enforce any style of gameplay; steal a wallet, develop schizophrenia, as the 1e maxim goes. In 2e, the stats are largely being shifted as a way towards showing consequences. Wisdom isn't an easy thing to hold and maintain; it's a representation of your restraint and self-control. If players decide "Fuck that noise" because they and their character believe that a given tenet of Wisdom just isn't worth maintaining, that's good; it means the mechanics have done their job to emphasize a theme; a theme that tells us that those who are willing to discard the concepts of knock-on effects and the rights of others are generally going to do so elsewhere as well.

                                    Mage should be a game where you are willing to look at Wisdom and say "Fuck that noise" if you sincerely disagree with it. You just have to consider and accept what saying that means.

                                                        It's not forgotten, I just didn't feel the need to bring it up; the chief benefits for high Wisdom are a better relation with spirits, better control over Paradox, and less Nimbus leakage. Someone who is high enough Wisdom to have that penalty is the sort that'd be respectful and rare in their dealings (as in, deal making) with spirits and rare in their Paradoxes to begin with. Sympathy, meanwhile, starts at Intimate first and works more distant, meaning your friends and loved ones get targeted first no matter how Wise you are... Unless you produce a Shadow Name and ground that in your mystic life, in which case, you've distinctly separated the two.

                                                        I don't think it'll also create sympathetic links for you, either, unless I'm mistaken.

                                                        That all brings me back to my point: The consequences for having low Wisdom are really self-inflicted: You only really start suffering from bargaining with spirits if you're a right evil bastard to begin with, at which point you're probably not bargaining with them anyway. The sort of person who needs Wisdom the most to absorb Paradox is the one who's casting the most vulgar spells (i.e. the unWise). The people whose Nimbus leaks beyond their immediate circle are the ones who are already likely casually casting spells on strangers because screw that guy for littering, now he has chlamydia.

                                                        In other words, every possible benefit Wisdom can bring you fits this wonderful dynamic: Those who use their power sparingly and deliberately are better at it than those who throw it about mindlessly.
