    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    The WoD really demonstrated how much overdoing the "everything is relative and shares off grey," can end up muddying the water more than making the games more fun to play. It's good to have antagonists that are darker hues and all, but a large interconnected supernatural horror game needs some solid black in there.

    It's also creates a lot of uncomfortable thing when you try to always make the bad guys sympathetic. The trying to make factions like the Black Spiral Dancers into "having a point, but they're just a bit extreme about it," sounds really bad when their tactics include torture, sexual violence, mass murder, and so on. I don't think a lot of people actually want to play in a setting where there's some "hard to refute point" being made by those sorts of actions. If your side thinks those are valid tactics, your side doesn't have a hard to refuse point, the means refute it easily.

    So either you cut out the worst of the worst... and the setting is less horrific and less dark... or you end up making the setting really squicky because it validates horrific things.

    Edit:

    Since I'm a bit slow on the typing:

    Rather than "primary" antagonist, I think the important distinction is whether or not the antagonists are meant to be philosophic ones or not. If the game needs a strong philosophic foil (Pentacle vs. the Seers), sure, the Seers need to have a point. If the game doesn't so much, you can safely use other types of conflicts (The Pure and The Bale Hounds really don't need to have a point; even if The Pure might sorta have a point, their actions clearly fall into the auto-refutation category).
    Yeah I kinda mean that it's good to have a philosophical antagonist in some form for a horror game line. It isn't needed for some but I think they do add something special. Refuting their point doesn't necessarily need to be hard but if you're plainly playing a monster, having your antagonist group doing monstrous things with a similar justification to your own helps ram that point home that you're in character jumping through hoops to justify yourself.

                                                        It's somewhat of a question of how much fantasy aspects you want to leave in. If you're sticking with the existence of the supernatural are part of the premise, modern horror doesn't change that much from older horror. Things get updated for the times of course, but there isn't some radically different take on supernatural creatures to deal with.

                                                        If you go more "weird" horror with a lot of "could be supernatural, could be mundane without any known explanation," stuff, you're in a different territory. Though it's a hard territory to build a larger setting around. It works fine in a "hunter" sort of set up where you're humans (maybe enhanced a bit) deal with all this weirdness, but you lose the "play as the monster" side of things if you don't define what the monsters are.

                                                            I'd like to keep Frankenstein's monsters/golems...but I think there might be a neat way we could mix that with the ghost/ dead among the living. I guess I would have the golems explore what it means to live, maybe asking themselves if they are even living in the first place, and I'd have ghosts explore what it means to die...but the concepts are not unrelated. Actually, I guess I would break it down into New Life (Promethean), a Fragment of a Life as it's own entity(Fetch), a Warped and Twisted Life(Remade), and the fading remains of a Life(Ghost). Might be a bit much for one gameline now that I type this down, but I think the themes are connectable....If they end up being their own game lines, I'd like Fetches or something like them to be playable. I don't know, I think it would be fun for a gameline of things that think they are human until they realize that they are only pieces of one.

                                                            I'd probably remove demons as its own line. I'd rather wrap their archetype into fairies, or mythical monsters. Honestly, I'd also involve Mage with Hunter, as people who embrace the supernatural, and people who reject it. Both are humans who regularly interact with the supernatural, and it makes some sense to me that they would be related.

                                                            Mythic monsters in my mind should be where most of the modern horror comes in. Base a lot of it on ancient myths, modern urban legends, and some of the weirder bits of semi-modern psychology in one big mess. For example I really like the idea that Beasts are embodiments of Id, whereas Heroes are embodiments of Super Ego, (and neither make a functional mind alone). I think a further exploration of themes like that would be cool.

                                                            I'd like mummies or an equivalent to continue that non-linear time-jumping thing that specifically isn't in their control. That's a really cool way to explore immortality for a tabletop game. I don't know, I think there is a lot of potential there.
