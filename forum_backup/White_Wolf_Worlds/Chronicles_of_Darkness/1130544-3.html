                                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                        This, exactly. David's Seemings fit perfectly into that framework, representing how you said "this is my story." With the new, more general version of Seeming, it doesn't represent that any more. It doesn't, from what I have read, represent anything. Most X and Y splats in second edition answer questions. "What have I become?" and "What do I do now?" in Vampire; "What do you hunt?" and "How do you hunt it?" in Werewolf. In Underhill Changeling, it was going to be "How did your Keeper change you?" and "How did you change yourself?" What question do Kith and Seeming (or Seeming and Court, if you like) answer now?
                                        I think the question answered by Kith is "What did your Keeper make you into?", and Seeming is "What have you become?" Seeming to me seems like a changeling's perception of themselves. It is what they see when they look in the mirror, beyond simply their Kith.

                                        Those who identify strongly with the horrible experience they went through have the Seeming that matches what their Keeper desired. They are defining themselves according to their trauma. Basically, they are saying, "This is who I am now. Look at me! This is what They did to me!" An Ogre dwells on the constant abuse they suffered. A Beast laments their loss of humanity as they were forced to act as an animal. Changelings with Seemings gained from their Durance are dwelling in the past, unable and perhaps unwilling to let go of those defining traumas. No matter how far they run or what they do, they will always see themselves as victims of the Others.

                                        Others focus on the moment at which they changed things, taking their freedom into their own hands. They define themselves according to that moment. They are saying, "This is who I am now. One decision made all the difference, and now my life is my own." An Ogre recounts the day they stopped working and decided to fight their way out. A Darkling can't get over the moment that they left their friend behind. For good or ill, these changelings dwell on a past moment as well, but in their case, it could be as simple as one choice that has marked them forever. It was still their choice, however, and that is an important distinction.

                                        Then there's the ones that focus on what they're doing now. They are saying, "This is who I am now. I will do things my way, so that I never end up like that again." An Ogre lashes out at anyone around them, either out of a love for the fight or fear of anyone growing too close. A Darkling clings to the shadows, finding comfort in the disturbing reputation they've built. These changelings are basing their identity on choice as well, but in this case, it is an ongoing mode of action rather than a potential one time decision. It is easy to say that they are looking to the future rather than dwelling on the past, but that's not entirely fair. Their actions might very well be based on past trauma, but they still focus on how they're going to function from now on.

                                        The Underhill version of Seemings was interesting, don't get me wrong. It was still limited, however. My wife hated it, because she felt like it forced her into a certain type of backstory. It kind of did, too. If you wanted to play an Ogre character, you had to fight your way out in some way. You couldn't play one that had an ogre-like Keeper that made all its changelings into flesh-eating ogres, and one night snuck out while everyone was sleeping, leaving its brother behind. That character would more likely end up a Darkling in the Underhill version. At the very least, you'd need to have the character kill a guard on the way out to keep them an Ogre. Instead of having an Ogre that escapes the day it says, "Okay, I'm done with violence," it needs to say, "Okay, this is the last one." It also closes off the door to a Seeming for a changeling who was neglected or abandoned by their Keeper. About the only way to have multiple options of Seeming for a given backstory would be if the changeling's escape fulfilled multiple criteria. A warrior who called the others together and led a bloody rampage out of the doors to Arcadia could be a Fairest or an Ogre, but probably not an Elemental or Darkling.

                                        TLDR: The Underhill version of Seeming asked "How did one moment forever define what you would be?" while the new version seems to ask "How are you defined?" While it is still possible to make it about a single moment, it also opens up options for defining oneself based upon the trauma one has suffered or the method one is using to cope with that on an ongoing basis.
