                One thing I played with a while back was having the beast represent attachment to a dark and twisted entity. This was sometimes believed to be a specific entity, but some question that and suggested that multiple forms represented the various aspects of the Curse. Others go so far as to say that each clan has their own patron. Some suggest all are part of something greater. Anyhow you slice it, though, these hungry blood gods live somewhere and that could be your otherworld.

                Vampires corrupting things (people, places) would be aligning them more closely to their otherworld. Especially dramatic corruption could lead to magical effects beyond the expected or even passage between realms.

                Even if this is true, most vampires wouldn't necessarily know it and factions would spring up that interpret the evidence very differently. One form might believe that consuming the souls of opposing clans would gain them favor. Others might seek to separate from these masters, in an effort to become something greater.



                ed. To clarify: Vampires wouldn't work any differently just because of this; it's mostly a behind-the-scenes detail that can slip in, here and there.

                If there are competing blood gods out there, they might be vying for domination over the blood in the material realm. The striges might represent a jealous bloodline patron in conflict with the others or a thing that resents the existence of the realm..

                                            Originally posted by Baaldam View Post
                                            Think of the Hedge for Changelings or the Shadow for Uratha,
                                            Bad comparison, because both of these are relationships where the player splat can theoretically eke out a living indefinitely without ever dealing with people and the main reason vampires deal with people is specifically that combination of "they need the context to maintain their self-control and social graces to whatever diminishing degree they still can" and "you are what you eat." Werewolves can sustain themselves until death on hunting spirits in the Shadow and changelings can live in the Hedge for the rest of their days as long as they make peace with being Delusional and needing a roommate or the right kind of entitlement, but if you leave a vampire alone in another dimension for long enough they will cease to be a playable character unless they take a low-set bane and sleep off the torpor of giving up Touchstones (and even then, they will be extremely susceptible to frenzy).

                                            Vampires in Requiem are people who are struggling to stay people while the unholy power in their stolen blood warps them toward being a means of stealing more blood, and an entire wing of the cosmology that doesn't have people in it to hold up that delicate contextualizing process is a distraction most ardently pursued by the inhuman and the extremely well-off. The street-level games neonates play and even the city-level games ancillae play have bills to pay and mouths to feed, and so do most of the things they're bringing into the world.

                                            Having a Kindred-specific equivalent of drilling for red-oil in the Underworld or harvesting blood-fruit in the Hedge or hunting the essence of parasite-kings in the Shadow is the kind of thing you bring into a setting as a local complication, not a ground-level concern that any given vampire might worry about when they can just hide out in "mundane" inaccessible places like the vents of an apartment building or under the water at a port or in somebody's basement or bedroom closet. The vampire ecology is rooted in the mortal world, and their needing to occupy space there is one more way it drives the pursuit of Havens and Feeding Grounds as means of safety and sustenance.

                                            ChroD has enough of a problem with people overlooking the supernatural's interface with the mundane world without sectioning off one of the most mundanely-connected varieties of supernatural creature away from the need to maintain that interface to play Annihilation, especially for a splat that is a bunch of different creatures just similar enough to share game mechanics through convergent evolution.

                                                Originally posted by Live Bait View Post
                                                Personally I think giving vampires an otherworld where they’re safe from humans would undermine the game unless there were dangers there that made it perhaps more dangerous than the real world. The Masquerade is a major theme for Vampire.
                                                There's no need for this otherworld to be a place safe from humans. Actually it rarely is.

                                                While I understand Satchel's point that in many games those otherworlds work for the benefit of overlooking the interface with the mundane if the game group so desires, which I don't see as necessarily a problem, but I get where it may harm the themes, it doesn't mean that they have to even in those other games.

                                                My idea of the "vampiric otherworld" being a twisting of whatever environment they stay in for long accounts for that, but even a full blown distinct plane of existence doesn't have to be a place that safeguards vampires. Does this place has natural sources of Vitae? What happens in it during the day? Does it even exists by day? How can humans cross into it? How can vampires be brought to and from it, by their will or against it? What happens to a vampire's physiology in this place? What if torpor creeps in more easily, staying there bringing the risk of a long slumber in the wrong time? What horrors lurk there that can threaten Kindred that stay too long?

                                                You not only can answer such questions in a way that makes it interesting to explore, but unfeasible to stay too long and completely drop the human world, you can actually chose answers that make a stable grounding in the mundane world all the more necessary for any explorer to get back in one piece and in time for dinner.

                                                One example of such ruling is in cWoD's Changeling the Dreaming, if you set aside the dials defaulting to a safer exploration. Exploring the Dreaming is wonderful and nurturing to the Changelings, but staying there too long causes Bedlam. You slowly gets crazier and crazier until you become a thing of madness, a Lost One, and Lost Ones are effectively unplayable, partly because of how crippling their madness is, part because they become tied to a single location. And the way to avoid or recover from Bedlam is precisely by staying sufficiently grounded to your mundane life, working, paying the bills, creating something without magic that humans can appreciate. Just an example.

                                                If Kindred leave behind a sign of their passing both in and out, so when they travel to this place mortals can follow through, and when they leave the things that lurk there can come out? Maybe those things can't interact with mortals, so hunters have an edge in this place where the vampires are the prey, and back in the mundane world the Kindred need to mingle into society to lose eventual pursuers. Maybe staying there simply requires so much mundane resources that vampires cannot prepare an expedition without a solid foothold and steady supplies. Maybe it simply doesn't exist as a single continuity in space and time, but as bubbles that form and expire, risking to leave undead explorers back in the mortal world unprotected in a bad situation if they cross the limit.

                                                As you make rules for this otherworld you can make it an alternative for playing in the mundane world, or something that reinforces even more some themes as it makes even the characters that achieved to isolate themselves anyway to have an interest in going back to contact with society.
