    Session 4

    Aegis triggered Like I Built It to try and determine where they were. Their mind expanded, revealing the true scope of this place they had found themselves in. It was an enormous tunnel, a seemingly natural cavern running the entire width of the continent. The floor of the cavern was paved over, converting the entire expanse into a highway running from east to west beneath the USA. But there was no sign of any entrance or exit, not even any means by which they could have come here.

    The revelation opened Aegis's mind to the next Key in their Cipher, Fire Drill. They turned the key to gain access to their Interlock, Home Is Where The Heart Is, an ability that allowed them to attach occult alarms to the area around them (see the following post for the full write-up).

    There were smaller caves and alcoves branching off from the main cavern every few hundred metres. Aegis and Veritas decided to investigate the closest one. They got back into Aegis's truck and drove. They found two cars parked just outside the alcove, and shut their truck's engine off the moment the other vehicles came into view. The two Demons approached the cars carefully, conscious that they were in unknown territory. There was no one about, but they did find one of the cars unlocked. Rifling through the contents of the car, Veritas found identification belonging to a Colby Hoffman, a young man he vaguely recognised from Cole's Ford.

    The Demons looked towards the entrance of the alcove, a downwards-sloping tunnel that disappeared into the darkness. It seemed unlikely to them that a group of teenagers from the town could have ended up here of their own will. Whatever had happened to them, it was undoubtedly going to be dangerous. Aegis retrieved their rifle from the truck, and they decided to investigate.

    The tunnel descended, making several sharp turns, before ending in a hole covered by an ironwork arrangement resembling a sewer grate. Aegis used their new Interlock to leave a trigger behind them that would sound an alert if someone tried to cut them off and trap them inside. They hauled the sewer grate aside. A dim red light was coming from down below, revealing a ladder. Veritas descended first, noting that the air became more humid as he reached the bottom and lacked the smokey car fumes of the larger tunnel. The Demons reached the bottom and took stock of their surroundings.

    There was a doorway in front of them, covered only with a thin curtain. The red light was coming from behind the curtain. Above the doorway were a number of neon letters of disparate shape, size, and colour, spelling out the words, "The Motel Connection".

    Veritas took the lead. He seized the curtain and hauled it open. Beyond was a small chamber, filled with blankets and pillows. A group of four young men were tangled together in the midst of it, bare flesh glimpsed in places as they moved together. They did not seem to notice the Demons staring at them. After a moment Veritas grabbed the curtain and shut it again. He and Aegis shared a baffled look.

    They took a few minutes to reevaluate the situation. Veritas opened the curtain again, this time trying to take note of everything besides the sex. The chamber looked like an artificial structure, metal walls in a circular shape at odds with the stone walls of the main tunnel. There was a recess at the back of the chamber, and he realised he could see something moving there. It noticed him too. A voice came from the shadows.

    "Who disturbs the ceremony?"

    The men broke apart with a yelp. Veritas watched as a creature drew itself upright. She was a thing of slick, sweaty flesh and rounded curves. She drew herself upright with impossibly long arms and legs, each tipped with the same three finger-like appendages. She was too tall for the chamber, standing stooped with her naked torso barely concealed by thin wrappings of almost transparent gauze. Her head was bald, lacking nose and nostrils, with a too-wide mouth that opened in a smile as she beckoned Veritas and Aegis to enter the chamber. They could see her many tongues flicking over empty gums, licking her lips as she looked them up and down.

    "Uh, we're lost," Veritas stammered out. "Do you know the way out?"

    The creature's voice was liquid and suggestive at once. She said that they had found their way to the Understate Route One. Only the Father's servants could come and go as they pleased. The Father's servants, and her own worshippers. The latter was spoken like an invitation.

    The two Demons were feeling more than a little out of their depth. Veritas was emphatically not interested in any worship, and suggested they take their chances on the highway. This seemed to annoy the creature. Her eyes, half-closed throughout this exchange, snapped open and Veritas found himself falling into them. His thoughts were overwhelmed by the sensations that overwhelmed him, leaving him stunned, distracted, as the creature turned her attentions to the more receptive Aegis. Her name was Sister Serenity, Aegis discovered. She drew him in, back to her bed.

    Veritas recovered from his sensory overload and was able to focus enough to study the chamber around him as Aegis kept Serenity and her followers occupied. If the Understate was God-Machine Infrastructure, probably a Facility of some kind, then this chamber did not appear to be a part of it. It was more like Serenity was some kind of parasite, latching on to the Infrastructure to sustain herself.

    The two Demons left the chamber some time later, Aegis feeling rather drained. They explained to Veritas the terms of the agreement they had apparently just entered into. Serenity had some kind of control over the Understate's access codes. Her followers, including Aegis apparently, could come as go as they pleased. She would allow them to leave immediately, and if they ever wished to return they had only to bring her a gift. She prized items representing love and devotion, wedding rings, anniversary presents, and did not particularly care where they came from. Failing that, a small, live animal would do. Or a small, live child.

    Back on the surface, Stitch and Smith had returned to Stitch's home to wait for the others. Around five in the morning, with no sign of the others, they decided to go and wake Axis. They arrived at his apartment block to find Veritas already there, waiting for Axis to answer the intercom and let him inside. He'd dropped Aegis off back at their home and driven out here to speak with Axis about the Understate. He promised to tell the others everything once they were inside.

    They were halfway up the stairs when Stitch thought to check on Jack Berwick. The consensus among the group was that he must still be dead despite the day resetting, why else would the God-Machine go through the effort of killing him? Stitch hurried downstairs and pressed the intercom for Jack's room. There was a long pause, and then a disgruntled voice half-shouted "What?" down the line.

    Stitch, assuming that Jack would remember the day before, made a passing reference to him being alive again. This seemed to confuse Jack, and he quickly stopped replying to Stitch's messages. Axis decided to try and remedy the situation. He knocked on the order, asking Jack to let him in. The door opened a crack, and Axis quickly spun some story about a stranger in the lobby spinning stories about Jack's impending death. Jack let Axis inside, and a quick conversation made Axis reasonably sure that Jack didn't remember the previous day. In fact, Jack seemed not to know his life was in danger at all. Axis saw that Jack had his service revolver on him however, and it was quite clear that the man intended to head downstairs and sort out the threat on his own. Axis was able to talk him down, pretended to call the police, and continued to try and get Jack to reveal what he knew was going on. Jack largely stonewalled him though, so eventually Axis went upstairs and placed an actual call to the police to maintain his cover.

    After that, the group spoke about the Understate at some length. Smith advocated against attempts to destroy it, arguing that if it could be made to work for them then it could be a useful asset indeed. The others were less inclined to leave such a significant piece of Infrastructure standing, although Axis did like Smith's idea that it could be an escape route out of town if things went south. Eventually the group disbanded without a conclusive stance being reached and agreed to reconvene at the community centre after church with Aegis.

    Aegis took Elise and Ajax on a brief hunting trip to scrounge up a rabbit, in case they needed an offering for Sister Serenity. Elise promised to check the snares throughout the day, and Aegis joined the others at the church. Together they sat through the same sermon they had been listening to for the last few days. After church they watched Goodenough Jones excuse himself early, Hope gossip at Mr Timbre, and the rest of the routine they were becoming quickly familiar with. Axis left early and called Fletcher.

    "Carlie Sinclair."

    "Excuse me?" The abrupt start to the conversation clearly threw Fletcher, kept him off-balance. Axis explained that he knew that Fletcher might not remember him, but that Fletcher himself had given him the name to persuade Fletcher to trust him. He asked Fletcher if he had a letter.

    "Wait, are you Chuck Davis?" Axis frowned. The name was only barely familiar. He found out that Fletcher had written a letter to a man named Chuck Davis this day, instead of Jack Berwick. The letter read "11:59:59, stay out of the woods". Axis thanks Fletcher profusely and hung up, hurrying inside to relay the information to the ring.

    Aegis recognised the name. Chuck Davis was a truck driver responsible for hauling logs from the logging camp to the town saw mill. They went around the community centre asking if there was anyone who might know where Chuck might be found. Joe Moherty ended up giving Aegis their best lead, telling them that if Chuck wasn't at home drinking then he might be out near the woods. Last night (the Saturday before the Sundays started repeating, presumably) Chuck had apparently been in Joe's bar swearing that if he caught whoever was putting spikes on the road, he would kill them.

    The ring promptly drove out to the woods. They found a ute with empty beer bottles on the passenger seat parked close to the start of the road that led up to the camp. Veritas and Axis stood watch over the vehicle, the other three proceeded on foot to see if they could track Chuck, with Aegis taking the lead. It was already past 10.

    They never found him alive. It was well past midday by the time Stitch, Smith, and Aegis came upon a bloodied, beaten young woman curled into a ball next to Chuck's body. Chuck had a gunshot wound starting beneath his jaw that had handily removed the top of his head. Smith gently drew the woman aside to talk to her, while Stitch used Synthesis on the scene. They soon pieced together what had happened. The woman was named Desley Grant. She was eighteen, an ardent environmentalist. She'd taken to placing caltrops on the road to disrupt the logging industry. Chuck had found her and her bag full of twisted metal, Aegis found the bag in question not far away, and started to beat her with the butt of his rifle. She'd been terrified that he was going to kill her. Then everything slowed down. She grabbed at Chuck's gun and wrestled for it, feeling strength well up from hitherto unknown reserves. During the struggle the weapon went off. The next thing she knew she was standing over Chuck, his blood on her hands.

    They made their way back to the others with Desley in tow. Smith found messages on his phone from the Sheriff, one about the death of Jack Berwick (he'd been found dead in his apartment in circumstances similar to what had happened to him the previous day) and one regarding a kidnapping. With an internal groan Smith placed a phone call to the Sheriff and explained all the information he had on Norman. He also reported the death in the forest, and told him that he would bring Desley to the station in person. The ring made another plan to track the delivery van for the Pie House the next morning and, since they had mostly been running without sleep for more than a day, decided to head back to their respective homes to sleep.

    Smith and Axis were the exceptions. Smith made a phone call to an Integrator of his acquaintance named Exigence. She would be able to broker a deal with the God-Machine for him in return for information he had to hand. Smith revealed that he knew of a mortal, Fletcher Lynch, who had known of Jack Berwick's original death in advance. What he wanted in exchange for this information was the removal of security on the Pie House. Exigence indicated that she could make this deal.

    Across town, Axis was hard at work studying. He found passing reference in some of his older texts regarding a story of a medieval village where a host of people died at the stroke of midday. Supposedly, this came after, or possibly before, the town disappeared for many years. When it was found again those who lived there appeared not to have aged or noticed the passing of time, and that the day of the great tragedy had been only days before.

    Axis almost fell from his chair when he read this. It seemed that he had stumbled upon the God-Machine's plan, the reason for time repeating itself. He was convinced that another person would die with every passing day. Having heard about Jack's death from Smith, it seemed apparent to him that the deaths from preceding days would reoccur on top of each new death. When the fatalities approached critical mass, the town would be frozen in time for years, possibly even longer. Why the God-Machine was attempting this was still unclear, but the stakes were becoming obvious to him.

    That night, Aegis, Elise, Ajax, and a rabbit caught in their snares all bunked down in Aegis's hidden bolthole in the woods. When midnight struck, Aegis emerged from the bolthole with the rabbit and called the other members of the ring to wake them. Smith was the first to arrive at the Pie House, slipping through the unlocked front door and accessing the freezer to drain it dry of Aether and fill his personal stores. He was waiting outside when the others arrived, and they settled down in Stitch's van to wait for the arrival of the Deliverer.

    Sure enough, at 4pm, the plain white delivery van made its delivery. Veritas shadowed it around the corner and activated Tag and Release on the van, before coming back to join the others. They then tailed it out of town, and as before they followed it into the Understate without any need for the rabbit. Now that they were expecting the transition they were able to stay on the van's tail as it drove along Route 1. They were driving in the dark, following the lights ahead, for about half an hour before they saw a new pair of lights approaching. Blue and red, flashing on and off. The delivery van started to slow, forcing them to slow with it. Finally it came to a stop, just as a sheriff's department patrol car pulled up next to it. The doors opened, and the wings emerged.



    Again, courtesy of Aegis's player.


    The two creatures were like biblical Seraphim, clusters of wings around a central point, but instead of a human head the wings were arranged around a clockwork mass of gears and piping forming a great cavernous maw. The two beings seemed to unfold as they left the car, their wingspan too large to fit inside the vehicle they had just exited. They converged on the delivery van, and Axis attempted to use Eavesdrop to determine what was being said. "Present clearance", appeared to be the gist of it. The conversation was brief, and the delivery van continued on its way.

    At that point the angels turned towards the Demon's own van. They approached slowly, flanking the vehicle. Smith swapped seats with Veritas so that he was in the passenger seat, and wound down the window. A voice spoke with the hiss of steam and rattle of clanking pipes, although it was hard to tell which of the two beings were the source of the voice.

    "Present clearance authorising your unit's use of Understate Route 1."

    Smith smiled easily, activating the Authorised Embed as he stared down the angel. "We're security detail." He indicated the receding delivery van.

    This seemed to throw the angels. They paused. "There has been no communication regarding the assigning of security resources to this project." A pause. "You may proceed."

    The angels moved away. The demons took a moment to process the sudden retreat. Then Stitch started up the engine and they hurried to catch up to the delivery van. The patrol car receded behind them.

    Some time later, they watched the delivery van pull over into a car park lit by gas lamps. Rows of identical white vans sat in the car park. The Deliverer did not emerge from its van. They pulled over to watch. It didn't look like this was where the meat was coming from, but the van would need to go there some time. The only question now was how long they would need to wait.

        In addition, Aegis, Veritas, and Ms Stitch all unlocked the first keys to their Ciphers. See below:

        Aegis. Embeds: Like I Built It, Fire Drill

        Home Is Where The Heart Is
        Cost: None
        Dice Pool: Wits + Crafts
        Action: Instant

        Aegis can ward locations against their enemies. All they need to do is touch a nearby surface, and they will be alerted whenever someone passes with hostile intent.

        Dramatic Failure: A false alarm rings out, audible to everyone within 100 yards. The Demon’s enemies automatically discern that this is alerting them to the Demon’s presence.
        Failure: Nothing happens.
        Success: A small arcane symbol is burned into the surface the Demon is touching. This symbol strongly resembles a clock, but the numbers are labelled in an unknown language. Despite the apparent inertness of the symbol, its hands do move to a different position each time an individual looks at it. So long as the symbol exists, the Demon will be alerted anytime a being passes within 10 yards of the symbol. This alert rings out like an alarm only they can hear, and they can automatically discern which of their symbols it came from. This additional alertness grants them an initiative bonus equal to their Primum against any enemy who triggers an alarm for the remainder of the scene. These alarms last until the end of the scene, except for those placed in locations the Demon or one of their Covers considers “home”, which last forever.
        Exceptional Success: The Demon gains a brief, momentary vision of the individual who has triggered the alarm, and anyone who may be travelling with them.


        Veritas. Embeds: Victory At Any Price, Tag and Release

        To the Victor, the Spoils
        Cost: N/A
        Dice Pool: Wits + Larceny
        Action: Instant

        Veritas gains understanding through victory, the knowledge of what his enemy most desires. If Veritas defeats an opponent in combat, he may trigger this Interlock to leach secrets from his fallen foe.

        Dramatic Failure: Veritas renders himself open to the world. He automatically fails the next subterfuge roll he makes, and cannot benefit from his demonic nature in respect to lying until he does so.
        Failure: Nothing happens. Veritas may not attempt this Interlock again until he wins another combat.
        Success: Veritas learns the nature and location of something precious to his defeated enemy. The defeated foe must know the location of the precious thing themselves in order for him to extract this information.
        Exceptional Success: Veritas removes knowledge of this thing from the target’s mind. For the next 24 hours, they will not seek to locate or protect it.


        Ms Stitch. Embeds: Synthesis, Cause and Effect

        The Scene’s Master
        Cost: N/A
        Dice Pool: Intelligence + Science
        Action: Instant

        Ms Stitch can manipulate the world around her to an astonishing degree, her mind slipping through arcane channels and twisting events in her favour.

        Dramatic Failure: Ms Stitch’s efforts go horribly awry. The Storyteller may inflict an Environmental Tilt on the area designed specifically to impede or injure her.
        Failure: Nothing happens.
        Success: Ms Stitch exerts her will on the scene, altering the environment and area to suit her needs. She chooses a target and specifies a single existing environmental bonus or penalty that the target is likely to experience on a later action. The environmental penalty will then be increased or decreased by an amount equal to her Primum (although it can never exceed +5 or go below -5) on the target when they make an action that would be affected by the environment. Any subsequent use of this Embed before its effect has triggered undoes the original effect in favour of the new one.
        Exceptional Success: At the GM’s discretion, an Environmental Tilt may be inflicted on the scene with an impact on the Skill specified. Note that this Environmental Tilt will affect everyone, even Ms Stitch, equally. This is in addition to the effects of a Success.

                    Session 5

                    Rather than spend a full day waiting for the van to make a move, the Ring decided to move on. Smith and Stitch wanted to investigate this Sister Serenity creature the others had discovered, so Aegis made an offer to introduce them. They headed back the way they had come down the Understate. Mr Smith, having spent some time analysing the means by which angels tried to penetrate his Cover, had learned the Deep Cover Embed. His investigation into his Cipher suggested that, as a Manipulation-based Mundane Embed, it could be the next key in his Cipher. He plugged it in. There was a soul-wrenching grind as the gears of his Cipher caught and jammed, wounding him deeply even as his physical body sat in the car unmoved and unblinking. In a moment of understanding, he knew two things. His next Key was actually Unperson. His final Key was Deep Cover.

                    Meanwhile, the Ring made the trip without encountering the Angels in the patrol car again. There was no sign of the teenagers' cars Veritas and Aegis had seen, either. Stitch pulled up at the alcove. Axis remained outside as the others entered, manifesting his radio suite form power to listen in on transmissions in the area. He picked up a large amount of background noise, and a clear message in some kind of machine code. His mastery of electronic language allowed him to decipher it.

                    "Priority One. Prevent the biological entity designated Fletcher Lynch from departing zone CF. The escape of other entities in pursuit of this mission is deemed acceptable."

                    Inside, the group found Serenity lying along among the pillows and blankets of her chamber. She looked as if she had been crying. Aegis went to her, and she explained that she was frustrated. She did perk up when she saw the others, new worshippers to add to her flock.

                    Mr Smith had manifested Aura Sight and was busy scanning Serenity for any sign of what she could be. She registered as an Angel to his senses, but there was a void where a connection to the God-Machine should be. He figured she must be an Exile. Based on his other readings, he determined that one of her aspirations was to control the church and that her greatest fear was the congregation.

                    Armed with that knowledge, he began to ingratiate himself to Serenity, praising her beauty and intelligence as he did so. With a combination of his own charms, promises to help her with the church, and subtle put-downs aimed at Aegis and her other followers, it didn't take long for Smith to get her talking. He wanted to understand how Serenity controlled the Understate. She explained that the Father had built her with access codes to help in her mission, but then cast her out into the world without telling her what that mission was. She wanted to catch His attention again. Her plan was to seize the church of Cole's Ford, where so much of the Father's attention was fixated. She asked that her new followers bring her a young man named Owen Michaels, to help fuel her plot.

                    Understanding that Serenity wasn't able to give him access codes to the Understate, Smith was ready to leave. He threw one veiled comment too many at Aegis. The Saboteur, already feeling strangely jealous at his comrade capturing so much of the monster's attention, lashed out. There was a short, quick fight that led to Smith quickly yielding, his eye blackened by a few quick blows from Aegis. Serenity appeared startled by the sudden violence. There was a hurried farewell, and the demons left the Exile alone in her chamber once again.

                    It was just past dawn when Stitch's van emerged on the streets of Cole's Ford, lifted from the Understate by Serenity's favour. Prolonged exposure to the Understate was proving insightful to more members of the Ring than just Smith. Axis, Aegis, and Stitch were all progressing quickly through their Ciphers.

                    The group returned to their homes and reconvened only a short time later at the Church. They took their seats in the pews by rote and listened to the beginnings of Goodenough Jones's speech. This time, however, the speech was interrupted by a groan near the front rows. A man collapsed, clutching his head, and Goodenough immediately broke off his speech. A crowd started to gather, and the town GP pushed his way through. Ms Stitch was able to get to the front of the crowd, and helped to lift the man to his feet. The doctor asked for help getting the man to his car, and Stitch and a few other members of the crowd helped him through. At the back of the crowd, Aegis was pestering those further forward to find out what was going on. By doing so she was able to get a name, Carlisle Foxworth.

                    The sermon was cancelled, and the traditional after-church gathering as well. Smith went to activate Homogenous Memory to manipulate those present into thinking Serenity had done something to gain their faith, but he felt something powerful actively preventing him from doing so. He ceased the attempt before the Angel, as he suspected it was, could determine his location. Stitch, Smith, and Aegis decided to see if they could find Owen Michaels, reasoning that staying on Serenity's good books was a good idea. Axis and Veritas split off from the group, refusing to say why. Aegis was able to find out that Owen was assigned to the skeleton crew at the saw mill, so the three of them set off there.

                    Axis figured that, as the bluntest and most transparent of their group, Veritas was the best to trust with the knowledge that Fletcher Lynch was in danger. Axis called ahead, name dropping Carlie Sinclair again to get Fletcher to talk to him. Fletcher sounded worried and refused to say whose name he had written today over the phone. When they arrived at the motel, the reason became clear. He let them into his room and showed them the note.

                    "Fletcher Lynch. 11:59:59. Don't be then."

                    Axis quizzed Fletcher for as much information as he could. He asked Fletcher if his notes had ever prevented anyone from dying. Fletcher said they had, but it didn't very often. Looking at the instruction, Axis decided he had a solution. A bolthole's stasis effect would be as good a way as any from removing Fletcher from time long enough for the Broken Clock to pass him by. The only problem was that he didn't trust the Ring at the moment.

                    Leaving Veritas to comfort Fletcher, Axis stepped outside and made a call to Will. He explained that he needed a Bolthole and asked if Will had one available, banking on the other Demon's reckless nature to offer one up. He was in luck, Will told him to go to the Butcher's store. She would tell her work she was sick and meet him there.

                    Axis went back inside. Veritas had not done a good job of keeping Fletcher calm, but Axis was able to get the man to stop panicking and come with him. He briefly explained the plan to Veritas, and said that he would need to go alone to meet his contact. He left Will's name out of it, giving Veritas no real clue as to where he was going. He left with Fletcher.

                    The idea of using a Bolthole did make Veritas think. What would happen if he applied the same trick to one of the Broken Clock's previous victims? Jack Berwick was sure to have inside knowledge about the God-Machine's projects in Cole's Ford, and seemed to be a pretty capable guy. He decided to head over there immediately, and called Stitch to ask if he could make use of her Bolthole. Stitch agreed easily, telling him where it was located and how to get in.

                    Axis reached the Butcher's Store on Main Street, where he was greeted by a young kid who'd been paid money to direct him round the back. He went through the back entrance and saw Julian Moat, the town butcher, inside. He asked Julian where Will was, and Julian answered that she wasn't able to make it. He then turned and hauled open the door of one refrigerator, revealing a room that seemed to consist solely of a path between an infinite procession of cow carcasses hung on meat hooks. His Ring's bolthole.

                    Axis ushered Fletcher inside. When the door was shut behind him, Axis asked Julian some more questions. With a show of reluctance, Julian revealed that he was Will's "other face", the other part of the Demon called Janus.

                    Across town, Veritas was knocking on Jack Berwick's door. When Jack answered, Veritas promptly blurted out the whole story so far, revealing his knowledge of the Broken Clock, the repeating days, Jack's death, and Veritas's own status as a Demon. Jack responded to most of this by levelling a gun at Veritas's chest, but the more Veritas spoke the more information it was clear he had. He talked about the files in Jack's desk about his service for the military and the God-Machine, and details about how the Broken Clock had killed him. For once, Veritas was able to make it through a conversation without his Cover's damaged nature interfering, and he convinced Jack to come with him so smoothly that the former God-Machine agent asked for no further guarantees that what Veritas said was true. They left together, heading for Stitch's abode.

                    The rest of the group reached the saw mill, a complex of interlocking red wooden buildings and steel slides. Even from outside the raw of machinery was clearly audible. Stacks of freshly cut lumber sat in the yard on pallets awaiting shipment. A few men could be seen operating forklifts beyond. The entire mill was walled in by a tall concrete barrier topped with razor wire, the only entrance blocked by a traffic barrier manned by a security guard. The guard was known to all the Demons as Egg, a thug and a brute infamous in town for his unpredictable temper. Still, it was no major challenge for Mr Smith. The Authorised Embed got them past on the guise of a surprise Occupational Health and Safety inspection, and they headed straight for the administration offices on the second floor.

                    Walking through the saw mill was unnerving. It was possibly the least subtle piece of infrastructure in town, an enormous example of Elimination Infrastructure capable of devouring anything the God-Machine's servants fed it. The scream of machines as they carved entire trees apart was a constant, painful reminder of what the infrastructure would do to them given half a chance. There was more activity than seemed possible given the small handful of workers they could see walking around.

                    Once they reached the second floor and stepped through the door to the small, cool set of rooms housing the saw mill's administration, everything went quiet. The rooms must have been soundproof. They could clearly hear the sounds of shouting from the next room, two women almost screaming insults at one another. Aegis triggered Like I Built It to try and work out what was in the next room. Their consciousness filled the saw mill.                                                         A thick, clear liquid ran up and down the two central spires. Small pieces of machinery floating in a loose cloud around the spires formed together into a clockwork heart. The fluid absorbed this heart, and with every beat it grew larger and larger. The creature grew larger and larger until it was the size of a small house, its malleable form sprouting and reforming limbs as it rushed through empty air towards them. The folds of its translucent flesh peeled back to reveal the piston-driven fangs of a spider-like maw. It bellowed and charged at Ms Stitch.

                                                        ​


                                                        Every Demon save for Stitch found themselves frozen with fear at that first roar. The Clockwork Shoggoth collided with the Demon, biting down on her left arm. Crying out with pain, Stitch kicked back off it and into space, firing blasts of light from the weapon concealed in her right arm. They seemed to bother the angel, but they failed to slow it down. As Stitch fled to use the Platform as Cover, it launched a stream of boiling tar from its mouth. The blast struck Veritas, and Stitch switched targets. Their next attack was aimed at the delicate clockwork almost a hundred metres away. The shot hit and it was as if time skipped forward leaving frozen afterimages behind to show what had happened. At the same instant, a second blast of tar came from where their attacker had first manifested, striking Stitch in the chest and almost killing her outright.

                                                        Aegis regained their senses. They activated their Interlocks, tearing an arcane storm from Elise that slammed into the monster. As it staggered back, Aegis found their relationship to time changing. They could be two things at once, every second a moment that allowed them to attack and retreat, fight and defend. They took flight, launching waves of iron spikes into the attacker.

                                                        Veritas and Axis rushed the creature together, trying to seize it before it could finish off Stitch. Axis reached it first thanks to his wings, and landed on its back, hoping to avoid the brunt of the creature's assault. He was still gravely injured from his earlier fights. Smith, following in their footsteps, launched himself from the platform as he prepared his Corrupt the Angelic Interlock for use.

                                                        Aegis struck again before the others could make their moves. They drove down from the sky, launching another wave of spikes into the beast. This strike seemed to be too much. Its body seemed to harden, then crack, and finally shatter. Aegis folded their wings.

                                                        The Ring, exhausted but all alive, approached the clock together. It had been damaged, slightly, by Stitch's attack, but it was still working. As far as they could determine, the damage would manifest as minor fluctuations in time around the town. A man might be stuck at a traffic light for an hour. Another might find their food go cold in seconds. Strange little events, but not entirely catastrophic.

                                                        Aegis watched the door while the others examined the Infrastructure more closely. It was certainly the device triggered by the Broken Clock to reset the day in Cole's Ford. But, with four Inquisitors to examine the device, they were pretty sure they could turn it to a separate purpose. If one of their number were to connect to the device, they could tear Cole's Ford itself away into a splinter of time, this single day ever-repeating, away from the God-Machine's control and influence. They could be free to rule this one day in time. They could even leave Cole's Ford through the Midnight Spiral, save for the one who bonded to the clock. The others could come and go, using Cole's Ford as a refuge from the God-Machine's grasp, but the one who stayed behind would be there forever.

                                                        Ms Stitch volunteered for that task. She opened her soul, her Primum, and fused it to the device. She saw the day in its entirety, every moment of time in frozen perfection. And then it was gone, stored away, kept forever in her heart. Infrastructure whirred to a halt across town, or else spun in endless loops shedding aether without purpose. Angels vanished or quietly fell as the entire town split away into a splinter of time beyond the Machine's ken. The rest of the ring descended to Cole's Ford, ready to begin a brand new day without fear.

                                                        And in her citadel Stitch watched it all, making plans. There were so many experiments she could run, so many variables she could test in this private world. Every day would be a new experiment, each more radical than the one before. Perhaps, one day, she would begin to change things permanently until Cole's Ford became something entirely new.

                                                        This was her idea of Hell.
