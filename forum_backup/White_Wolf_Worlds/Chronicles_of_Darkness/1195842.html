      So I am new to 2nd edition and I have looked over the Auspex powers. We are starting more powerful so I do have 5 dots in Auspex. Can I have some suggestions or advice on the most effective ways to use Auspex?
      It works pretty much the same as it did in 1st Edition. Dot by dot, some noteworthy changes to keep in mind are:

      •: This is the only power that’s totally different from 1e. Think of this like a Aura Reading, but only surface level, and for the scene as a whole rather than an individual. So you can check if the scene is tense of relaxed, what’s the most likely source of danger, that kind of thing. What’s nice is that the first use in the scene is free, so you can start off every scene with a quick scan to see if anyone wants to hurt you or whatever. Very nice.

      ••: The Q&A format makes it look more different than it is, but practically speaking, it’s the same power. Instead of getting Aura colors, you get sensory descriptions, but you still use it the same way otherwise. Scan people and see what their emotional state is, if they’re a vampire, if they’re stained by diablerie, etc. Again, the first use against each person in a scene is free, so there’s no reason not to start of every interaction with a quick scan for their emotional state, if they’re hiding something from you, etc.

      •••: Again, basically the same power as Spirit’s Touch, only now instead of getting a vision mostly determined by the ST, you have a little more say in what you learn about the object in the form of the Q&A.

      ••••: This is still basically telepathy. The nice thing is that the ability to assault someone’s mind with it is a bit better codified now. Use it to have private conversations that can’t be listened in on, to read people’s minds, or to give people horrifying visions just like before, now with the added benefit of knowing exactly what Condition they’ll get if you do the latter. Free to initiate telepathic contact, 1 vitae per memory viewed or projected.

      •••••: This really has no practical change from 1st Edition. The biggest thing to keep in mind is that other vampires can sense your projection with only Auspex 1, if they think to ask about it. So it’s a little less effective at infiltration if there are vampires in the place you’re infiltrating.

      Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.

          As mentioned, with 1-2, using them their free time is something you may as well get into a habit of.

          With 3, another thing to mention that couldn't be done in 1e is that you can use it on *locations* too. For Ordo (and to a lesser extent, Circle/Lance), being able to ask if it's a "Supernatural Location" is great for discovering Nests. Not sure what your covenant is though.

          If you can reliably get exceptional successes on level 4, it can become a fairly large buff/debuff ability. Grant a bunch of Inspired, Steadfast, etc, or by contrast inflict with spooked, guilty, etc; each of those free memories is another condition you can inflict if you don't want to be secretive about what you're doing.

          (As a note, AUspex 1 could sense Auspex 5 in 1e too, and since you blatantly need to ASK now instead of just popping heightened senses, you're actually much safer since, in my experience at least, few mekhet will randomly check for psychic projections in every scene)

                                  "Other vampires have no way to know when someone is using Auspex to discover their secrets" doesn't really contradict this general facet of Using Disciplines:

                                  Each Discipline calls on the Beast, whether using its talents overtly or expressing it through the blood inside. While the mechanical effect of a player failing her Discipline roll is that nothing happens, that’s not strictly true. Something happens. The Beast won’t just accept the idea that tapping its power produces no results. Maybe the lights flicker, or the vampire’s flesh writhes on her bones for just a second. Maybe her victim sees a second, screaming face trying to escape the vampire’s head for just a second. Whatever it is, it’s not enough to properly freak her victim out — that’s the domain of the Nosferatu, through their Nightmare Discipline. It’s just something that reminds the vampire that she’s not human, she’s tapping something within herself that she doesn’t fully understand and cannot truly control. The Storyteller and player should work together to come up with a couple of potential minor signs that the vampire’s tried to use a Discipline, even though nothing particularly supernatural has happened.
