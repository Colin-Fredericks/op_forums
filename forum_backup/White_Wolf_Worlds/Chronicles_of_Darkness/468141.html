    Converting Moongose's Infernum to NWoD

    04-04-2015, 02:28 AM
    Anyway, I just remembered, and redownloaded, this little gem of a d20 setting, wherein the players are demons. In hell, and mostly unconcerned with the world outside. That was probably a mistake, along with making the demons as well, demonic as possible. I'm not saying the grimdark was unwarranted, it's just that it tends to constrict character options-there's only so many pure villains it's possible to make. More importantly, though, was the complete focus on the Pit; if I'm a demon, I want to play the soul merchant with meat on the hoof, as it were.

    What it did right, however, was the society of the Pit and its technology. The feudal society of general backstabbers, psychically enforced promises, and sorcery is a unique and interesting one.

    So, I'mma going to start my own conversion of it (Mr. Paraduo has another) with some WoDifying alterations, to make them the truth behind Hunter and Inferno demons. To an extent, at least-part of the change is that the world the Hellkin live in isn't the afterlife, but an alternate Underworld, and like the other Underworld, souls move on. Eventually. They certainly stay long enough for the iliaster to flow.

    So, themes:

    Theme: The hellkin, for all their posturing about being above humans, ultimately possess Virtues and Vices, albeit multiple Vices. They aren't that inhuman, and one of the more human aspects to them is their need to better themselves. Thus, the ultimate theme of Hellspawn: the Infernum is one of relentless ambition; the mercenary that seeks to forge a new House, the Zethu wonderworker who blazes new fields of study for its Duke and its own elevation, the faustian sorcerer who will make the Houses acknowledge her as an equal or die trying...perhaps even that rare demon who, faced with the growth of a conscience, seeks to make something of its life not forged on the backs of the damned and the weak. The desire to be more, and what a person will burn in order to be more, is very key to understanding the society of the Pit and its incursions into the mortal world.

    Mood: Hellkin don't think they're evil, but that's mostly because they don't have much to compare it to. As a race that literally needs to conquer and corrupt to survive, demons have become a ruthless and brutal species by necessity, seeking every possible way in which they might be able to somehow gain a few more souls, a few more damned to extract the precious iliaster from. Thus, Hellspawn's mood is one of brutal survival and no small degree of cynicism, where everyone has an angle and a plan for betrayal not out of malice, but because anyone who lives in the Infernum already does. And yet, there is hope-even the most amoral hellkin can offer a Covenant out of existing loyalty or offer kindness to its Retinue because they are its friends, nothing more. The relentlessly dark nature of Hellspawn society means nothing if there are no bright spots to compare it to.

          Okay, I've very little experience with homebrewing, especially for NWoD, but I own the original and GMC corebooks, all Infernum books, and I LOVE Infernum, so I would love to get in on this project, if you'll take me.

          Couple of suggestions, off of the bat:

          * Morality-analogue needs to be handled *very* carefully. Hellspawn have more in common with humans than they care to admit, but at the same time they're also very different. They're sparks of angelic essence wedded to prototype anatomies. They are born to feed on the essence of the soul itself, living weapons engineered by fallen angels. It wouldn't make sense to me if their morality meter was mostly the same as a human's. Even beyond my distaste for that in twilitfolk in general, there's also the fact that otherwise, all Hellspawn would be completely unplayable due to insanity - they need to feed off of torture and murder or die a horrible death, after all. They simply cannot be as bothered by torture, murder and soul-rending as humans are.

          ** Perhaps Corruption might serve as the basis for morality meter? In Infernum, Corruption is both a strength - many mutations are more potent based on your corruption, and for mortals/angels, corruption could be used to get powerups - and a weakness (makes you easier to bind).

          ** Alternatively, maybe the Covenant could be useful for that role? At least in terms of inspiration?

          * For power stat and food, I would go with the approach of "demons feed upon and use Essence, but they personally call it iliaster". This meshes neatly with the existing NWoD lore - ghosts and spirits and "empowered mortals" all use Essence, keeps things simple, and allows for a certain homage to Infernum: it allows high Primal Urge urathra to quite neatly take the role of Infernum's benandanti.

          * Obvious route is to make Breed the "biological axis" (clan, tribe, seeming, etc) and House the "social axis".

                  Oh, I definitely agree that redemption should be possible. NWoD recognizes that it's the light within the darkness that makes the game worth playing, as Infernum does; if there's no hope to be something better, then there's little incentive to play, after all.

                  Yes, Harmony or Satiety would be my suggested models for the Hellspawn morality equivalent. Especially if we use Corruption to take its place.

                  Hmm... what if Iliaster is, as you suggest, "refined" Essence, but Hellspawn can pick up powers or devices that allow them to directly convert Essence into iliaster? I am recalling the palace augment which allows hellspawn to gain iliaster from having people worship them, as just an example. This would help with your goal of having hellspawn be redeemable; it's not something every hellspawn can do, but those who are willing to try can find other sources of nourishment that will work.

                  A "House Vice" that works as a Virtue sounds like a great idea to me; really helps emphasize just how different hellspawn are to any of the other twilitfolk in the NWoD.

                  The book's Mutations are the obvious source of powers/gifts/etc, but here's a thought dimly related: NWoD is many things, but it isn't as possible to "power-boost" as it is in D&D. Moreover, I disliked the whole "mutation tax to gain decent elemental protection" thing in the D20 game. So, here's what I'd suggest: to reflect the inherent Fire Resistance of hellspawn, part of the basic template powers is that all hellspawn downgrade fire damage to Bashing. Similar elemental resistances work the same way - the first dot in the Chain of Frost downgrades cold damage to Bashing, chain of Storms downgrades electrical damage to Bashing, etc. And higher value gifts render that kind of damage completely ineffective.

                  I'll need to pull out my reference books first, but, I could try and take a shot at drafting the Hellspawn generic template and the Breed subtemplates, just to give us somewhere to start.

                      Actually, I can think of the Questionnaire:

                      Lies and Elements of Truth

                      Demons are fallen angels who war on god:

                      Some may be , but not us. If you believe the Morningstar (a rather stupid move) we were created by them, but hellkin are natives of hell, made from true spawn and turned intelligent. Other beings under hellspawn include the aforementioned true spawn, living humans who moved or were born in the Pit, and those double-damned Abhori. As for the God thing, honestly we don't care if there's a monotheist deity in the first place, let alone war on Him-if He gets in our way, though...

                      Demons are the evil inhabitants of an afterlife reserved for the wicked, who torture the souls of the damned.

                      Not true! The Pit's only a stop on to wherever souls go when they leave! (laughs) In all seriousness, flesh, I don't think "evil" exists except as a cultural way to avoid how brutal this universe really is, but given our biology? I think only the truly delusional among us would seriously be offended by the e-word, if you get my meaning. And while not all the souls we get are bastards, nearly all of the ones who fall to us are, and their pain makes lots of the iliaster we need to survive, so the torture part's pretty much spot-on. If you like, I can show you some of my favored field techniques.

                      Demons are immaterial beings who possess humans to work their designs on Earth.

                      Do I look immaterial? This canard isn't entirely false, though-hellkin can and will project their minds into humans, other hellkin, once-humans...it's kind of a headache for politics. When we're out here on Mundus, though? Most of us come as we are or weave human skins around ourselves. That isn't to say, flesh, that there aren't any immaterial demons-it's just that they happen to be nobles, and a side effect of getting a fortress in the Pit is that the noble can no longer be fleshy normally-they need a domain to "rule" invisibly if they don't want to get crushed under their own metaphysical weight.

                      Demons are merchants of souls who will give you anything in return for it.

                      We tend to start low and work up to souls, and demons are bound by the resources we can actually pay with, but that's true. Being in the ownership of a demon is about the only gurantee that a soul will be damned after death and feeding the maws of thirsty demons and industry in the Pit. It's not so bad, though, flesh-I'm not the most vicious iliaster harvester in the world...

                      Demons are untrustworty liars who will backstab you as soon as it becomes advantageous.

                      Given some of the idiots I've worked for, I wish this was more true than it actually is. See, we'll do and say anything to get ahead of the cutthroats we call fellow demons, but there's also a system of mystical pacts called Covenants-ie, debts of service and loyalty that are mystically enforced by the power of the Infernum's reality itself. It's hard to slip or even work off the Covenant without getting my brain fried, so I can't help but honor any deal I agree to. For the most part.

                      Sorcerers and demons are natural allies.

                      If you're talking about faustians, true. It's easy enough for a knowledgable demon to offer sorcery in exchange for whatever, all showing some would-be demon-caller proper finger twiddling is time and tested patience. A few faustians even move to the Infernum, taking advantage of the time dilation we have to "trick" their bodies into not aging. Because they're often better than us at sorcery and are more resistant to Covenants, they're valued members of our society. Kind of. Lot of us resent a living human who isn't being ground underneath their heel. Not me though-I am perfectly fine with you knowing magic. I just expect a little, you know, working relationship afterwards.

                      Hell exists.

                      You got that right. It's not anything particularly accurate to modern mythology, but it resembles it enough in passing. It's not something you're eternally bound to should you die and get damned, and I hear it's possible for some particularly ambitious souls to become hellkin (don't quote me on that), but it's certainly a place where souls go if they've fucked up in life and do not have a fun time of it. They can't-we need their suffering.

                                                          Humans are dangerous as masses, in the NWoD, by virtue of the fact that humans outnumber twilitfolk at a ratio of about 1000 to 1. Hellspawn number in the billions, equivalent to humanity easily. They also breed much more effectively than humans - it takes 1 demon to make up to 18 new ones, if you look at the Spawning Pit description in Book 3 - to the point that the biggest role of the Circle of Slaughter in the modern era is keeping the population of hellspawn *down* to manageable levels.

                                                          Furthermore, it takes the better part of 2 decades to take a human from "conception" to "trained soldier ready to kill". For hellspawn, it takes eighteen *months*. So not only is a hellspawn army easily as big as a human one? They can reinforce themselves much more effectively. When a human soldier's supply lines are cut and he runs out of ammo, he's pretty much screwed. A hellspawn is a living weapon. From the moment it claws its way out of the pupation chrysalis, a hellspawn is ready to kill. No training, nor education, nor armament needed.

                                                          So it's a good thing for both sides that war isn't likely to happen. A hellspawn invasion would be a nightmare for humanity, and as useful as fatebending can be, let's not forget that the modern army depends a lot on fancy electronics to do its job - gear that's utterly useless in the Pit.

                                                          Anyway, I'm not saying that war between earth and hell should be looked into. Just that hellspawn have a place where *they* belong, and that should be taken into account.

                                                          A human in Infernum has to explore the horror of having gone from the top of the foodchain to the bottom. Even a hunter in the NWoD can take a grim hope in the knowledge that others will take up the Vigil. In the Pit? You are the one who must hide and fear the wrath of the slumbering giant if it wakes.
