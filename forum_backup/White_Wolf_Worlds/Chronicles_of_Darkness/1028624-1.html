        Originally posted by Lord Dynel View Post
        Since there were no major changes to the Healing rules from nWoD to CofD, (that I could locate - the GMC doesn't have any changes), I think referring to those rules for a clearer picture is a valid option.

        Both books say a Dexteritty + Medicine check can be attempted in the field, or the ER. Both say that achieving "sufficient successes" that equal to the total Health lost "restores one Health point to bashing damage." My earlier post, and Charlquin's "second interpretation," suggested that this statement meant that one point downgraded from lethal to bashing. I have since changed my mind, because of that wording. So, Snapdragyn, I think your interpretation of my post is correct.

        However, I think the nWod book does a much better job expounding upon skills. I feel that it's a major part of the CofD book that's lacking - the nWod core book does an excellent job breaking down specific tasks relating to each skill. Maybe the developers left that out of CofD because they figured most players were coming from nWoD and had that as a resource and didn't need to reprint it, or perhaps they were pressed for space with the God Machine Campaign they wanted to add. Who knows.

        But, there's a major piece that is missing from the CofD book, dealing with this very subject. Under the Medicine skill, page 61 of the nWoD core book, it addresses this:

        "Achieving sufficient successes (equal to the total, overall Health points lost by the patient) restores one Health point lost to bashing damage, in addition to any healing that the character already does under his own power. A patient may regain no more that one 'extra' Health point by this means per day. Alternatively, a patient who's bleeding to death or in a coma is stablized if one success is achieved on the roll (The loss of Health points to aggrivated damage each minute is stopped; see 'Incapatation,' p. 173)" (nWod core book, p. 61)

        Since this check can be made in the field, I feel it covers the issue.

        I feel this handles it quite nicely. My previous example of Jack and the nurse is still pretty much valid; the only difference is that Jack would remain at 5 aggrivated and 3 lethal and would not lose any more lethal to aggrivated. The nurse can then call 911 and get an ambulance to Jack where he'll receive proper medical attention in the hospital.
        Very much agree that the CofD rules are lacking on skills descriptions.

        This gets back to my original post, however - the nWoD stated '1 success' rule on p.61 is immediately contradicted in the text. I've since found that this not only happens in the example (where, yeah, rules override examples in cases of conflict), BUT also in the detailed rules breakdown of roll results on p.62.

        An example: "Until you acquire the total number of successes required to stop a patient from bleeding to death, one more of his wounds turns from lethal to aggravated per minute." (And additional similar quotes within the same section.)

        This says 'total number of successES', plural. So now we have 2 places (once in an example, then multiple times within the roll results section) that reference multiple successes on an extended action, vs. a single sentence declaring 1 success needed.

        I think the nWoD error here was the 1 success statement, & it didn't get caught in editing. The CofD error is that no rule even exists.

            I think this is the key phrase:

            Originally posted by Chronicles of Darkness

            When a character’s rightmost Health box has lethal damage marked in it, he takes another point of damage each minute (upgrading existing lethal damage to aggravated) until he receives medical attention, mundane or supernatural.
            This is very much up for interpretation, but: I think the idea is, any medical attention will stabilize you; or, to put it another way, narratively speaking, if you get some medical attention, the game system assumes you're not going to bleed out, unless the doctor/medic screws up. Be that a successful first aid roll, or getting you into surgery

            Or, to put it yet another way, while I agree that it's probably an oversight that it's not more specific, that makes sense to me. The game doesn't want you to die for weird mechanical loopholes.

            However, that "restored to bashing damage" is definitely weird. I think Charlaquin is right, or else (under a very literal interpretation of the rules) first aid is literally impossible if you're full up on lethal. From Lord Dynel's post, it looks like it was a phrase replicated from first edition that wasn't clarified.

            Edit: To put it yet another another way, a weird subsystem error got cut in favour of a narrative thing (assuming it was intentional).

                I agree with you, that there is some error with the "one success" statement. Honestly, I think the example is correct - it's awfully detailed to be erroneous, in my opinion. Interestingly, the "one success" wording is gone from the CofD book.

                So, I think the rules are there in the CofD book, I just think you have to infer more than you do in the nWoD (because of the extended explanation and the example). If you read the healing entry on page 61 of the nWoD book, then read the healing section on page 95 of the

                Page 95 of the CofD book tells us:
                • If you're Health pool's rightmost box has bashing, you need to roll Stamina every round of fall unconscious, which will heal in 15 minutes.
                • If all the Health boxes are full of lethal, that doesn't heal by itself, and you're bleeding out. You need prompt medical attention. Otherwise you upgrade one point of lethal to aggravated every minute until attended to or dead.

                Page 96 of the CofD books tells us:
                • Medical care is an extended action, and you need a number of successes equal to the damage suffered by the patient.
                • Medical care can be done in the field or ER (Dexterity + Medicine) with an interval of 1 minute, or in hospital care (Intelligence + Medicine), with an interval of 1 hour.
                • Achieving "sufficient success" (which infer successes = to current damage total) on one of these checks can recover a point of bashing damage, plus damage that normally heals over time.
                • Hospital care can downgrade wounds by one degree, focusing on the worst damage first. Only one wound per day can be downgraded this way.

                Page 61 of the nWoD book tells us:
                • Dexterity + Medicine is rolled when a patient is dying, when trying to stabilize the patient.
                • According to the example - which demonstrates a character who has taken full lethal damage - after an amount of Dexterity + Medicine successes equal to the damage total are made, the patient/victim is stabilized. He does not gain any Health back, but stops upgrading lethal to aggravated. We can infer from this the rule as it is intended.

                I think the "one success" statement can be dropped. I think these points cover everything, does it not?

                    The thing I don't particularly like about the extended roll having a target equal to health boxes is that hardier characters are tougher to save. Theoretically they have more rolls before they bleed out, but since extended actions only get rolls equal to the attribute + skill pool your average first responder is only going to roll three or four times before they're out of chances, regardless of whether the victim has six health boxes or ten. To that end I've been running it as a flat target of 5 successes on an extended roll.

                            This isn't based off of a reading of the rules, confusing or not, but if you want the character to have a chance at survival without somehow downgrading damage in the field you could apply something like the advanced duration rules to your heal check. In this case every success on the first aid check would extend the amount of time it takes for each lethal point to change to aggravated, thus buying time for the character to undergo surgery in a hospital. One minute becomes ten with one success, perhaps twenty with two or an hour with three.
