    My biggest issue, I think, is the whole Werewolves versus Vampires trope again. Can't that just die out and stop painting entire groups of people with the same brush? Ugggghhhhh....

    Putting aside the social issue aspects of the Darkness games, my big priority when looking at these games are "something new." I'm bored with the same old stories repeated ad nausium. Vampires are corporate sharks and werewolves are grand soldiers at war. Something new please!

    Generalities being what they are, I'm going to wait to see the actual books before making a final judgement. But I'm hoping its really not going back that way.

    Yeah, there's the whole OneWoD v. CofD things going on, but that's no different than pretty much every culture in existance. Conservative versus new, globilization versus nationalism... we need a balance of these things, but its not very clear if we'll ever find them, let alone in a fan base for a game. So, I'm going to stick with just worrying abotu me and my group on game issues. I'll check out the books once they come out, I'll hope for a well done video game, but that's it.


    Also, as per D&D 3.5 v. 4e v. 5e - one thing I do love on 5e is the attempt to put more effort into interaction and exploration over just combat. I love that they're trying. There needs to be a whole lot more done in that direction, but hopefully its a start.

            Originally posted by TelperionST View Post
            I watched the hour-long Key Notes and then I watched the hour-long Q&A session. And then I read the interview that was basically a response to the other two. In my mind there's nothing spectacular or special here. The guys, and gal, over at White Wolf Entertainment are trying really hard to separate themselves from Onyx Path Publishing. They need to aggressively establish that White Wolf Entertainment and Onyx Path Publishing are two completely different things, so that they can create their own company image without getting into a messy situation where WWE (wait that's already taken...WWEN?) is mixed with OPP. The whole thing was just straight up posturing for fans. Fan service in the simplest and most direct way that I can think of. They didn't have any products to show. They had some images to show. They didn't have a new direction that WWEN is taking. They had, what sounded to me like, recycled ideas from the past dragged to the present.

            From this perspective it makes perfect sense to have OPP change the name of their core products. There is no more New World of Darkness. There is just the Chronicles of Darkness. And then there's the One World of Darkness, which is different, because it's hearkening back to the Good Old Days, but update to the current era. Well, at least they will attempt to do that.

            OPP is clearly listed as a partner of WWEN. So, it's not like these people actively hate each other. Aside from the cringe-worthy SJW bs I saw nothing noteworthy during the entire Q&A-session. And of course they have to pander to that demographic, because it seems to me that everyone over at WWEN needs to pander to every demographic. At least until they have established themselves as a comany. So, in my opinion the Q&A-session was a horrid mess, but largely inconsequential and meaningless. They don't want to drive sales to OPP, even if they like OPP. Therefore, it makes no sense to start talking about OPP and its products in a WWEN marketing conference. I know that it's called the Grand Masquerade, and there's more to it than what I saw, but honestly what I saw was a half-baked marketing conference followed by a really messy Q&A-session. Nothing noteworthy until they actually do something. WWEN is a new company that needs to create its own image, and it is using WW nostalgia to get it going. Whether or not that's going to work in the long-run I have no idea. It sounds like a good idea. However, the truth is in the products and right now they have nothing.

            What I really didn't like about the WWEN Key Notes presentation was the implication that these people are done writing RPG books. They have done their share, and now it is time to get some freelancers to write the new books. It seems to me that if you want to bring back the Good Old Days, then the most direct way to do it is to have the people who started this whole glorious World of Darkness return to writing. Except, it was repeatedly pointed out that they are not the right people to do the writing. And the writers are exactly the people who are going to make or break WWEN. I don't know how this is all going to work out, but it sounded a lot like &quot;we here at White Wolf Entertainment are going to concentrate on the bigger picture, and allow others to create the content that you want according to our rules and guidelines.&quot; Now that's something that I don't like.

            So, right now it seems to me that WWEN has an existing fanbase, which will have to wait for at least 18 months (probably more, would be my guess) for V V. And along the way WWEN will probably try to sell all kinds of other stuff to their fans. To me it sounded a lot like covering every possible market and filling it with WWEN products. Works in theory. The way I see it the more partners and different kinds of people you bring onboard the more different kinds of interpretations you are going to get. And I know that they talked about this &quot;bible&quot; for OWOD, which is going to be sent out to every affiliate and partner, so they can all play together nicely. It will be very interesting to see how all of that works out in real-life.

            Also, OPP is still publishing cWoD books. I wonder how that's going to work out in the future?
            Well said, all of it, well said. WWEN is way too cocky for a company that has directly produced nothing, but a trailer and an extra layer of bureacracy for Onyx Path, the people actually making stuff to deal with.

            Abd yeah that Q&A was an absolute mess, bit to be fair the lions share of that belongs to the podium guy and an audience that behaved poorly for the most part.
