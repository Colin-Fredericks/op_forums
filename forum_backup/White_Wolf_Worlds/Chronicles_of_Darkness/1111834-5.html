    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    That's not a counter argument.
    Actually, I do have to agree with Wyrd here. Roleplaying games, as a whole, are cooperative games. There should be coordinated efforts from all members of the motley to finish up a plot, especially one as big as killing a Title. That's like saying that combat should be equal to espionage in a Demon game. That doesn't make sense.

                                            I want to be surprised that there's a gross misunderstanding about the way Summer works or even how Changeling, a game about faeries, works....but this has unfortunately become par for the course.

                                            War for Summer was never a one dimensional subject. Summer had sorcerers and thieves and accountants and riddle makers(who were probably also accountants) and everything of the sort. The difference is that the spectrum of activity was geared towards uncompromising aggression towards the Strangers, where as Autumn** was all about that critical hinge point that exists in compromise, the power that comes from being afraid, the power that comes from being at the center of pledges. Summer plays chess with the fae on six or seven dimensions, maybe more, but it's all about taking down that king-and as any mediocre chess player knows, you can't rely one one type of "movement" to win the game.

                                            Does that mean physical combat is never the answer? The whole lore of faerie tales says that's not the case, and Changeling facilitates it. But just as often, it's a matter of setting up that combat. You can't beat the Moon King with any sword, you need the Sword Unbreakable*. The battle can't happen anywhere, the Dark Lord must die on the hollow of bones he was born in. Physical combat can't just be physical combat, it has to have gravitas, meaning, it must be an actualization. This is how combat has always worked in faerie tales-the ones worth reading, anyways.

                                            Final thing: When a 10% chance is mentioned that a Character who has the Aspiration of defeating a Stranger by taking their head off will be let down, that's....not the fault of the game. The game gives you the tools to make that happen. If it does happen, it's because the Storyteller is a rank dick(and probably the rest of the players, if they aren't confronting said Storyteller).

                                            Broaden your reading, sharpen your understanding. I know some people are tired of me saying that, but Changeling is not a game you can just charge your thoughts at it straight on. The world of the fae is sideways to expectation and will gladly trip and devour any who try.

                                            *Poor reference, I know, but follow me here.
                                            **As the other major war-like faction.

                                                            I don't really follow the line of thought. In any edition, it doesn't make much sense to have a changeling focus on becoming skilled with the sword unless there is some meaning to the effort. If the meaning is focused on the Keeper then sword fighting must be relevant to the Keeper in some way. The Keeper is created to match the changeling. I wouldn't expect the swordsman's Keeper to be a scientist working at the center of a maze full of traps. How does it work out in 2e?

                                                            Seems to me a Keeper is destroyed by forcing it to break the Contract that binds its existence. That might be telling a unique story, winning a fair duel, navigating a maze, or evading capture for a certain amount of time within a designated area. Some work for combat heavy types while others do not. Works out equally for every type of character. Then there are loopholes to exploit that are easy for anyone but getting the chance is the trick. Pulling a sword from a stone might require defeating the guardian, a master swordsman.

                                                            I don't think they are invalidating character concepts with this new approach. I do think they are trying to avoid the option of using more dakka. If one changeling can kill a True Fae by dealing damage by learning a few (perhaps more than a few) skills then 5 or 10 changelings could do the same without the need for dedication. I think they want the concept created to defeat a Keeper to be made specifically to defeat that Keeper because if someone wants to focus their character that much they should be rewarded with "Great shot, kid.
