            As a general thing, the Pure believe that what spirits choose to do isn't the Pure's problem. If a bunch of spirits want to get together and form a cult the Pure as a whole probably generally don't care. But then it comes down to the individuals involved. Is one of the Pure a former cultist who was rescued from a cult in their childhood? They may not take kindly to the spirits forming one. Or maybe one of the Pure has a cousin who has been claimed by a spirit involved with the cult, that could be a problem. Or if the cult is competing with the Pure pack for any given resource / hunting grounds, that could be a source of conflict.

            As to the Purified, there's less guidance going across games, but I think it's largely going to be in the same theme as the above, not our problem.

            It is worth noting though, the Pure are more likely to have spirit allies than the Forsaken and as such are more likely to be dragged into drama in the spirit world involving stuff like this than it might seem at the onset. It's entirely possible that the Flame Touched are friendly with a group of spirits who do have a problem with spirit cultists, or heck, even have a problem with a human who thinks to transform himself into a spirit. Spirits are individuals to, after all.

            So ya, a Pure / Purified / Shadow Occultist alliance could work, or they could all be at each other's throats. It wouldn't be entirely unreasonable for it to be both situations at the same time. Maybe one pack of Pure is backing a Purified lead group of Shadow Occultists in another area as a weapon to use against the Forsaken while another pack of Pure has various reasons to oppose that group. Bonus points if the packs are legitimately friendly with one another.

                    Mechanically, Abjuration is just an opposed roll that uses a character's Resolve + Composure, which every major splat has. Virtue and high Integrity can give bonuses to the roll, and vice versa Vice and low Integrity can give negatives. So, certain splat traits like Cover or Harmony or even Satiety that don't really map to Integrity that well are a bit awkward, thematically, but you could keep Abjurations available but just not hand out those bonuses. If you're including Abjuration in your game because you want more options to interact with ephemeral entities for your players I'd just handwave it and say that whatever they've got is close enough to a soul to work.

                    As for mortals, I don't remember if anyone's brought it up explicitly, but summoning alone can power an entire magic system for a game. Most ephemeral entities have some kind of power, so summon something and then convince or compel it to use its powers on your behalf. Spend time researching previous occultists efforts to learn names, summoning conditions, pre-existing agreements, etc. Mortal sorcerers from Mummy can summon and control Amhkhata and fiends from Duat, which further expand your options.

                                You don't necessarily have to compel ephemeral entities with other magic; if they're sentient and able to communicate you can bribe them, leverage their Ban or Bane, or maybe use the Chronicles of Darkness conversation system to strike a deal with them, or just roleplay it. If you take the core gameplay mechanic for getting your summons to do what you want to be talking to them, you can build some open rites or ceremonies or whatever to support that rather than building a ton of rituals to try to cover everything.
                                This would be dangerous, a lot of the time, at least without a lot of prep and research, but that's part of the fun. I'd lean towards having mortal occultists build relationships with a handful of ephemeral entities of whatever kind, getting to know them, summoning them to trade favors with, building trust, or trying to assert dominance. You can help keep a ghost around and strong by helping preserve their memory, shepherd some kind of spirit in Twilight by keeping the Resonant condition active somewhere, maintain some Infrastructure for an angel, etc. With a decent Occult skill you should be able to research these kinds of activities from previous generations of practical occultists as well; giving your characters some idea how to do it, survive it, mistakes to avoid, and maybe some names, bans, banes, and anchors or whatever to get them started.
                                In addition to the usual suspects, the CoD corebook has a little sidebar about building your own kind of ephemeral entities; you basically just have to decide what rules they follow and which they put their own spin on. So if you wanted to create, like, elemental spirits, or djinn, or gremlins or something you can do that too if you want to give mortal occultists more options for summoning that don't quite follow the existing rules for spirits.
