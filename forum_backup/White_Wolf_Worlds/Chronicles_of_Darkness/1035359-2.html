    Benighted Candle (Effigy •)
    Size 1; Durability 1; Structure 2

    This candle was found in the home of a housewife named Rebekah Morgan, in the center of a ritual circle drawn in her own blood, as well as that of several cats. Rebekah herself went missing and neither she nor her body have been located since. For those with knowledge of the supernatural another artifact found in the house may provide a clue as to her fate: A copy of Das Buch von Sorgen, the Book of Sorrows, by the 18th century German occultist and rumored necromancer Oswald Schwartz.

    The candle itself is hard to light and burns with only a weak, almost hesitant flame. However, neither wax nor wick is consumed by this flame, a quality which has seen it pass from the evidence locker to a number of private collectors. The most recent is a member of the idle rich with an interest in the occult - though his behavior has become more erratic in the past few months, and some of his fellow dabblers wonder whether some of his rites might have gone awry.

    Power: Lighting the candle requires an Instant action, and keeping the flame alight if it moves requires a Reflexive Wits + Dexterity action for each turn of movement, with penalties as appropriate to the conditions. If the candle is deliberately snuffed (another Instant action), all non-magical light sources in the scene are snuffed out and cannot be lit again until repaired somehow - wicks and kindling become strangely damp and bulbs blow in electric lamps. The candle's user may attempt a Clash of Wills to snuff out any supernatural light sources.

    Curse: The guttering light of the benighted candle serves only to make the darkness of the unknown seem all the more daunting. In any scene in which the candle is present, all characters attempting Abjuration treat their Integrity as one lower. Additionally, seeing the candle's light inflicts the Spooked condition on mortals. Mummies who light the candle may choose not to inflict the Spooked condition, while the Tef-Aabhi can contain its effect on mortal souls.

        Ankh of the Executioner (Amulet level 2)

        In Ancient Egypt the Ankh was a symbol of life and the eternal cycle of creation. Amulets bearing this symbol were meant to ward off ill fortune and disease. As was so often the case, however, their Iremite forebears had a darker interpretation. For them, the Ankh represented the lifeforce of the individual, the inherent mote of Sekhem beating within the living. Those that bore the symbol of the Ankh were in tasked with collecting this vital essence. It was thus the last thing many prisoners saw before being flayed upon the pillars of the Nameless City. Over time the collective feelings of terror, dread, and suffering of those executed imbued itself into the Amulet. No one knows how many of these grim symbols survived the fall of Irem, but the Maa Kep have encountered over a dozen documented cases thus far and suspect that thrice that amount at least are still at large. At one time a cache of these relics was evidently discovered by a crusading regiment of knights, who mistook the shape of the Ankh for the symbol of the cross and brought their find back to Rome. Stories of vampires and other malignant entities fleeing before the sight of the Christian symbol may be conflated with catholic monster hunters wielding these ancient artifact in service to the church. Attempts to access the reliquaries of the Papacy to obtain these dangerous artifacts have thus far failed.

        Power: Like many Iremite Amulets, the Ankh of the Executioner is imbued with the knowledge and thoughts of the past. Unlike other amulets, the psychic anguish contained with this relic is not meant to contact the mind of the user. When presented forcefully at one the wielder considers an enemy, the Amulet unleashes an overwhelming cacophony of terror, dread and anguish from countless victims of the Nameless Empire. The target immediately withdraw from the sight of the wielder or lose one willpower point each turn. In addition, even those targets that suffer that loss are unable to approach the wielder of the Amulet closer than ten yards and suffer a -3 penalty to attempts to attack from a distance, including using firearms or thrown objects. Creatures such as vampires that suffer from animalistic curses such as the Beast may additional have to make a check for frenzy at the Storyteller's discretion.

        Curse: The contempt and arrogance of the executioners towards their prisoners lingers in the Amulet at well. Enemies slowly become less than human in the eyes of the Ankh bearer, worth nothing except as bloody sacrifices to ancient gods. This attitude expands inch by inch to include everyone the bearer knows, beginning with casual relationships and pressing on to the most intimate of friendships, lovers and family members. This attitude creates a -2 penalty to social interactions involving certain relationships of the character (when and upon whom the penalty should apply is left in the hands of storytellers with character input). Long term possession of the Amulet will also incur a Derangement of the Storyteller's choice, again applied at his discretion.

            Infernis Aeterna (Level 4 Regia)

            Alternatively called the Eternal Flame of Kallinikos, this relic has long been a favorite among Mesen-Nebu scholars as a prime example of how the ever shifting currents of Dedwen can defy and expand the very definition of what makes a vessel a vessel. In this case, the Regia is not in the proper sense a finished product of alchemy at all so much as an unending act of chemical combustion. Put more simply, the Infernis Aeterna is an living flame an eldritch green in color, forever burning without need of fuel or oxygen. Attempts to douse the fire are futile- even complete emergence in freezing water or the vacuum of space will not prevent the fire from consuming whatever it has attached itself to. Fortunately there are methods of transferring the flame once it has begun to burn that can prevent the immolation of persons and property, but such techniques become progressively harder to invoke.

            The tale of the Eternal Flame begins in the 7th century AD in the chemical workshop of the Kallinikos, the alleged creator of Greek Fire. Although reports of his time in the Egyptian city of Heliopolis (on the site of present day Cairo and according to some accounts tantalizingly close to foundations of Irem when that city still stood in the land of the living) are disputed, the records of the Mesen-Nebu insist it was here that the chemist strove to perfect the formula of the legendary weapon of the Byzantines. An early experiment that dangerously combined the comparatively mundane alchemical formulas of the current era with eldritch rituals invoking the blessing of Re resulted in an elemental regia that destroyed the young chemist's laboratory and nearly ended his life. Not long after Islamic conquerors forced him to flee to Constantinople, where he would subsequently perfect the remarkable but utterly mundane substance known as Greek Fire. What became of the Infernis Aeterna from there is obscure (many occult scholars make the not unreasonable assumption that records that might provide a deeper history of the regia were consumed by flame). Evidence suggests it had a hand in starting or spreading both the Great Fire of London and Chicago. More recently, it appears to have played some part in the coal mine disaster at Centralia, Pennsylvania. At present the Mesen-Nebu have directed their cultists to work closely with the efforts of the esteemed Lampros family, the descendants of Kallinikos who dedicated themselves long ago to destroying his wayward creation.

            Power: The Eternal Flame requires no fuel to burn, but will happily consume any combustible substance intentionally sacrificed to it. Whenever such a gift is made or in the Arisen a pillar point of Ab is spent to 'feed' the regia the flame becomes subservient to the wishes of the giver. The flame can be directed to enter a lantern, brazier, even light the tip of a candle- in this state it will become relatively docile and can be properly "stored" for various uses. It provides illumination appropriate to the device that holds it. If used as a weapon the regia will cause one point of aggravated damage per turn to any single target by default. Additional sacrifices of fuel or pillar points will allow it to grow in size if directed up to the size of an inferno, inflicting appropriate levels of damage along the way. The flame can not be put out, but a burning target may at any time touch another flammable object and "will" the regia to move to that substance. Such a transfer removes all trace of flames from the target. Oddly, the eternal flame never causes additional mundane fires in its wake, even at its most volatile. An additional function of the regia is to serve as the power source for various types of machines and equipment that run on gasoline, coal or other forms of combustion. When directed to do so the eternal flame can enter a gas tank, furnace or similar and provide a limitless source of power to the device. Aided by some magical instinct this act is always performed without harming the device. Indeed, the device will perform at far greater efficiency than ever before, offering a +4 bonus to appropriate actions as determined by the Storyteller (for example, pushing a car to a high rate of speed). If used in the crafting of other substances, such as at a forge, the item in question will be of exceptional quality and provide an additional +2 equipment bonus beyond any normally applicable to the item. The only apparent limitations known to this aspect of the regia is that it is unable to power anything larger than a single three story building. Obviously devices that run purely on electricity or similar power sources cannot be aided in this way by the flame.

            Curse: The Eternal Flame has been described on occasion as a living entity, and in a sense this is so. It is hungry, volatile and tempermental. Whenever directed to perform a task for more than two days that does not involve the infliction of lethal damage upon a sentient being the flame will grow restless. Machines powered by the flame will begin to act unpredictably. Buildings will experience unfortunate accidents in their power grids, vehicles will become prone to sudden stops or jerks that run drivers off the road, etc. Whether through self sacrifice or malice, the flame requires suffering to remain calm. After two weeks these requirements move to wholesale murder, the flame typically demanding a living person be sacrificed for it to consume (although it will settle for indirect methods, such as using a car powered by the flame to run over a pedestrian). If this is not done daily, the flame will seek to destroy whatever machine it has powered and/or whatever person it has served up until that time. After this disaster the flame will grow comparatively docile again. It will then seek an appropriately hidden object or location designed to contain a fire- a lantern, oil lamp, brazier, fire place, etc. and enter a kind of dormant state. It is at this time it can be moved and safely handled, perhaps even carried to a tomb and sent back to Duat, without resistance.

                            Made myself an uter, after reading the story Katalepsis on Royal Road (hard recommend) and getting inspired by the character Zheng. Ankarama is the Mummy character I'm playing around with, and the teeth are rather her trademark.

                            The Teeth of Ankarama (Uter •••)
                            Durability 2, Size 1, Structure 3

                            This uter consists of a collection of crocodile’s teeth whose roots have been carved into sharp points.

                            Power: A character who has the Teeth of Ankarama gains a 1L bite attack with no initiative penalty and Armor Piercing 2. Her bite attacks ignore 2 Durability, she can eat and gain nourishment from anything she can consume, and has no limit to the amount she can eat.

                            Additionally, when the user inflicts damage with her bite attack against a mortal in excess of the target’s Resolve, she may spend point of Willpower to enter a Clash of Wills against the target. On a success, she inflicts the Soulless Condition upon the target, devouring their soul and keeping it within herself. She may release it at a later point, either to return to its original bearer, to pass on, or for some other use.

                            Finally, if the user knows Blessed is the God-King, instead of manifesting a divine weapon she may increase her bite’s rating to 3L, and treat her bite attack as her divine weapon for all purposes. If she does this, her head becomes that of a crocodile.

                            Curse: To use the relic, the teeth must be inserted into the mouth in place of a previous set - an agonizing process for a mortal which inflicts 2 Lethal damage and a -1 wound penalty, neither of which can heal while the teeth remain. If the teeth are removed the damage can hea, with the wound penalty vanishing once all damage is healed, but teeth do not grow back. In addition, they suffer a -2 penalty to any social rolls which would be penalized by having a mouth full of crocodile’s teeth, though this also applies as a bonus when relevant (e.g. some intimidation rolls).

                            Mummies suffer only the social penalty, while those of the Su-Menent can so harmonize the teeth with their sahu that they are not normally visible.

                                    I'm conflicted about Ankh Relics. Much like Effigies, I find them too conceptually similar to Amulets. However, I did give some thought to different kinds of these Relics, and came up with these odd ideas.

                                    Ankhs harness breath and water, so why not use them to make soap bubbles? Ancient Egyptians had soap, and Ankh even has a loop at the end. A plastic toy for making bubbles could be a basis for a modern Ankh. If water and breath don't need to be used at the same time, an Ankh can take the form of a pinwheel.

                                    A more out there idea I had was to make Ankhs a distraction. I thought what conceptual space wasn't used by Relics yet, and came up with body modifications. We can assume Maar-Kherit were ingesting strange substances, undergoing ascetic regimens and accepting scars and tattoos. All that to make their bodies into living Relics, activated through non-magical Ankhs. That's why bootstrapping them into Rite of Return went so wrong. Their inner Sekhem rebelled.

                                        Today in Fun Magic Items:

                                        The Finchale Nail (Effigy ••••)

                                        Carved from a strange, dark wood as hard as any metal, this seven-inch nail feels strangely chill in the hand.

                                        Power: If the nail is driven into the forehead or heart of a living or Deathless being* (inflicting three levels of Aggravated damage), it drives the victim's spirit from their body, but pins it to it. The body becomes incapacitated, while the spirit lingers in its place in ghostly Twilight. The spirit is pinned by the same nail and cannot move or take physical action save within its reach, but can converse with those who can perceive the ghostly frequency of Twilight.

                                        In addition, the nail's power renders body and spirit alike sacrosanct, locked in time - and, some say, guarded even from the progression of Fate itself. Even the mightiest magics of eldritch gods cannot touch them. Poisons, diseases and other negative effects are not cured, but do not progress while the nail remains in place, and aging and the waning of Sekhem are likewise paused. Healing and other beneficial effects are put on hold as well, as are the durations of curses and blessings. Mummies are considered to be in a death cycle for the purposes of their magic, though they have not actually died and do not suffer the normal effects of a death cycle.

                                        If the nail is withdrawn, all effects end, and any effects put on hold resume as they were. It is feared that using the Nail to avoid one's duty as an Arisen for two long might lead to another mummy arising with a Judge-given purpose to punish the offender.

                                        Curse: The nail drives an axis through the fabric of the physical world, fraying that all-too-thin barrier and piercing through a chink into a strange place beyond, from which deathly Essence flows. While the nail is driven into a creature, the area within forty-nine feet (about fifteen meters) is treated as having the Open and Anchor Conditions for all ghosts. Mummies know enough of Iremite magic as to wield the nail with greater precision; they only inflict the Open Condition, while if the nail is driven by one of the Tef-Aabhi no Condition is inflicted at all.

                                        *Attempting this feat in combat requires a Dexterity + Weaponry roll at a -3 penalty, in addition to the target's Defense.

                                                            Chalice of Goodwill (Regia •)

                                                            This double-handled goblet is plated with gold, and its upper rim is studded with a ring of carnelians. It is large enough to hold about a pint of liquid. Historical records trace its first appearance back to the Norman invasion of Britain, though its creator is lost to the mists of time. It's been traded around as a favor among both Arisen and occultists, most of whom finding it difficult to hang onto for long.

                                                            Power: When the Chalice is filled with a liquid, the user holds it aloft and utters a blessing in any language. The liquid inside is then transformed into a full-bodied red wine (a oenophile might place it as a Syrah, but with a tantalizingly mysterious note of spice to its undertaste). The Relic purifies and neutralizes any toxins in the transmogrified liquid, leaving it as safe to drink as any alcohol, and even the undead may enjoy the wine's dark flavors. Anyone who partakes of the wine adds one die to all Socialize rolls for the rest of the scene.

                                                            Curse: Although it may not feel like a Curse in the moment, the merriness of good wine is infectious. For the rest of the scene, the user is treated as having one less Door for Social Maneuvering, and moves their Impression level up one step in relation to everyone present. Multiple uses of the Relic in the same scene stack (to a minimum of one Door and a maximum of Perfect Impression). The Arisen keep their normal number of Doors, and the Mesen-Nebu manage to calm their hearts entirely while wielding the Chalice.
