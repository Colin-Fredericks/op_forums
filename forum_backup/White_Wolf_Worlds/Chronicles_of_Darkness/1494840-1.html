        To try better explain it, I understand, Fate is the manipulation of consequence. When Fate is present, it strongly affects things toward a particular outcome. It really doesn't matter what Arthur does beforehand, he will eventually draw the sword from the stone. He could not have done anything else, not because he is unable to make the decision to not pick up sword but circumstances will mean that trying to drawing the sword will always happen. Camelot will always be great and glorious until it isn't because the outcome is effectively already been decided. When all of your Fate is directed to being a problem to someone else, your choices about the matter have stopped mattering. You can walk to work, take a bus, or stay home, and somehow it will be their problem when it would prevent their aspirations. You might get run over on the curb, get in a car wreck, or get in trouble for not showing up to work, and your target will hear about it. You didn't do it on purpose, but now they have to deal with the consequences. You had a choice, it just all lead to the same outcome. Aka, you are in trouble, they need to decide whether to help you or not. Sure it did in different ways, but the essentials are the same. You could be on the other side of the world, and circumstances will still force the outcome that they will hear about it, and need to choose to bail you out, at the cost of their dreams.

        It's a lot like in video games with dialogue options. You can say a lot of crazy things, but it's the rare game that actually has your choice truly matter. You can be as rude as you want, and if the plot requires that person to be in your party, they will eventually join up. Sure some games will have them betray you later or something, but in most games, it never comes up again. Your choice never mattered outside of how it made you feel in the moment. You effectively never made a choice at all. Extra Credits did a good video on it in video games.

        Being a Millstone is horrible, you keep getting into freak accidents, and your best plans are for naught, and while nice people keep helping you, you can see it draining them. I don't know what you think about people, but pretty much everyone I know would take major issue with someone doing that to them. Most might wearily accept it eventually, maybe even feel entitled to getting help, but how long that takes depends on the person. People are also good at pattern recognition, and they might eventually figure it out. So, a smart person rigs it so the blame won't be directed at them, and basically is a ghost in the background. If a Millstone does figure it out, well, no real loss. They will still serve the purpose you made them for, they literally can't do otherwise. You could hop in a pocket dimension, and they will still be doing their thing. Even if they want to get even, the kinds of actions they would need to take to do so would be exactly the kinds of actions that would easily result in needing to be bailed out, basically tempting Fate. But they still could get lucky, and still manage to hurt you. It's an unnecessary risk. Commanding them directly just gives them more opportunities to figure it out and try to get you. Commanding them through others may cause you to lose both assets. So just walk away. Observe, sure. But you really only should get involved if you have to.

        I hope that helps. I actually think they are a good idea, so I hope we can make them workable for everyone.

                    The duality of humanity, indeed. Personally, I like their concept. If there is any game where their themes can be explored well, it’s mage.
