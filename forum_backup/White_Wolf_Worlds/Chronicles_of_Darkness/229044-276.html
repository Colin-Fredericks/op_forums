                                                        I'm a tad concerned that we may be getting into the writers' heads, with all of our criticism. Personally, I love the tone of the first draft. But I'm making my way through the re-draft . . . and some of the rewrites seem like they're trying to soft-pedal some of the more dramatic "cut scenes", and spend a lot of time trying to justify themselves. Too many cooks, and all that.

                                                        For example, I think its entirely appropriate for Brent (in the feeding/trick-or-treat cut scene) to be a high school student . . .and not a frat boy. There are tons of horror and ghost stories focusing on kids . . . and stealing trick-or-treat candy is such a high school thing to do. Maybe its changed, but when I was a kid, it was always the high schoolers who got into that kind of trouble. Too old to trick-or-treat themselves, but not old enough to hit the bars with the college kids . . .

                                                        As a frat boy, he'd have been out clubbing on Halloween, trying to hit on the girls in their skimpy costumes. And if he did steal some kid's candy . . . well, he'd be old enough to justify calling the cops. And in the suburbs (where this scene seems to be set), they'd definitely would call the cops.

                                                        But as a high school/mid-older teen, he'd be likely to get away with it. Oh, sure, his mother might make him apologize . . . but he'd have already eaten the candy by then. And he might well track down the "little brats who squealed" on him, and beat them up. A little monstrous fear might well be the only justice possible. And he'd be young to still feel some of the fear of a childhood boogeyman like Mrs. Winter . . . but old enough to be really angry about it later.

                                                        As for it being child abuse . . . someone has to punish kids. And enforce some degree of order (kids tend to be a little . . . Darwinian, otherwise). Is it child abuse to ground a kid, or spank him (remember, this is the World of Darkness we're talking about)? Plus, there's a huge difference between a six year old, and a sixteen year old.

                                                        If the morality of the situation is bothering people, I'd rather see a follow-up scene, where Brent goes Hero, and breaks into her house to slay the monster. Decked out in football pads, carrying a tire iron . . .and his grandfather's old service revolver, loaded with the three bullets he brought from the war - just in case he ever "ran across a monster like them Nazis" again . . .

                                                        And if that's still not enough, maybe a third excerpt - this time in the form of a news reporter. About how a local teen has just been arrested for beating old Mrs. Winters to death. And - after hearing disturbing stories from the other neighbors - the police are investigating whether Mrs. Winters may have been sexually abusing Brent and the other kids in the neighborhood. Which would make the whole thing a pyrrhic victory for both Hero and Beast - the Beast's lesson is lost, but so is the Hero's glory and reputation (not much panache in being a suspected abuse victim).

                                                        But the main point is that I like the tone of the original draft. This is a game that needs to go dark. So, Beast writers, if you're reading this? Take all of our criticisms with a grain of salt, and try to build the world you originally wanted to. Being a little controversial is not a bad thing.




                                                        P.S. Let me join the chorus, though, and say that I do like the outsider view sidebars. Great way to add some color. However, I'm also one of the guys who liked the old Soul/Homecoming idea, over this new Devouring/Horror stuff.
