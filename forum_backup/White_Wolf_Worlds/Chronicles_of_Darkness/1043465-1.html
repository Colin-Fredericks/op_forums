                                        Part of the problem, as I see it, is the investment and later games, which tend to build off of previously-made games. Requiem set a standard, many of the games since seemed to have worked off of that. For instance the idea of Majesty, though not the specific effects, can be emulated by Beast's cheaper alternative, Alien Allure. Obfuscate 1 and 3 could be replicated by From the Shadows, Auspex by Mimir's Wisdom. Plus we're working off a limited experience pool and Disciplines have to be bought sequentially while many other games offer alternatives.

                                                I think Disciplines are fine, they're just structured in such a way that if you want, for example, the ability to turn invisible, you can't get that ability as a Discipline without also paying for the ability to become preternaturally unassuming and the ability to make objects similarly unassuming. Which works in Vampire because the different levels of a Discipline represent gradually increasing mastery of a single, powerful supernatural ability. But when the thought experiment is to pick your favorite a-la-carte powers from any splat, people will often want just the invisibility, and often another splat will have that by itself for cheaper. Like with me taking Second Skin and Skinthief instead of Protean 3. Technically, Protean gets me a lot more for the investment - 5xp total to basically be able to turn into a fox whenever I want (or to look like someone I've skinned for a month but I wouldn't ever do that), compared to 9xp to be able to meld into the ground, gain my choice of up to three animal features that I can swap out from day to day, and the ability to turn into up to three different animals whenever I want. But since turning into a fox was the only part I specifically wanted, it those extra abilities weren't worth the extra 4xp, even though they would be for most vampire characters.
