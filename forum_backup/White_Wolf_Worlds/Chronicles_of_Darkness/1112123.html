    Query: Difference between V:tR and V:tM - A Player's Perspective

    07-06-2017, 11:39 AM
    Ok, so I have a few prefaces to my question.

    First is that I couldn't find a topic already on this, the closest being this thread which was more about CofD vs WoD in general and focused on mechanics and setting. My question's a little different so I apologize if this topic has already been brought up.

    Second, I am posting this in the V:tR area rather than the V:tM area because I figured there is a higher chance of players having played both lines and players who prefer V:tR (and WofD in general) as I do.

    Third is the backstory to why I ask:
    My friend played V:tM way back in the day before there was anything else other than just V:tM so she has a lot of history with that game line and it's setting. When I started getting into CofD about 8 years ago she told me that she used to play V:tM back in the day and so this was the first she had heard of Requiem. After hearing me talk about it and my group and after a little research she decided that she didn't like V:tR and preferred V:tM due to some simplifications and some of the clans being taken out and other things. I suspect she prefers V:tM for the same reason I prefer V:tR: It was what we started with. (I have basically the same opinion of V:tM as she has to V:tR. I like the x/y system, the more focus on intrigue and webs of plans, and the mystery and and such)

    Well, her daughter is showing interest in V:tM so my friend might be running a V:tM game and has asked if I'm interested in joining. I'm not sure I like the idea of playing V:tM as I think I will see all the things I like about Requiem and not see them work like that in Masquerade. But I've never played Masquerade so I recognise that this is just a knee-jerk reaction. So I'd like to better know if I'd enjoy playing Masquerade.



    (tl:dr)


    So my question is this: From a players perspective: what is the difference between playing V:tR and V:tM?

    I know the setting of Requiem is more gothic horror and, under the skin, as it were where Masquerade's setting is almost Cyberpunk but without the cyber (I envision dirty streets and rough gangs of supernatural predators and gaudy Coven palaces on the edge of the city a la Underworld). I know the factions are pretty different and the clan differences are more pronounced in Masquerade.

    But what I'm looking for is what it's like to play in a V:tM game vs a V:tR game before I agree to play. To know if I'm going to enjoy myself or not.

      Originally posted by xiongrey View Post
      So my question is this: From a players perspective: what is the difference between playing V:tR and V:tM?
      Now, I don't know about the new Masquerade books, but there's a few notable differences I remember from pre-Gehenna era games.

      Clan is both your organization and your family. Yes, we technically have Camarilla / Anarch / Sabat / Tremere lines that sort of line up with Invictus / Carthian / Lance + CotC / Ordo Dracul lines, but those divisions are almost entirely clan based. As a whole, your allegence tends to be to the clan, not to any sect. Some clans are pretty bad, possibly offensive stereotypes as well, depending.

      Neonates have far less pull and sway. The Rulers That Be are powerful figures, and the way Generation works means that you won't really grow into powerful figures - there's no potential for it unless you start diablarizing. You can never Dominate your betters. You can never be stronger than them. They are eternal unless you somehow pull a coup and kill them through superior tactics and numbers.

      Its been over 15 years since my last game, and that's all I remember off the top of my head. A lot of it comes down to how well a GM downplays the bad and can run with the fun.

          I've played a lot of both, and from a player perspective I find the biggest difference is the setting. I've rarely felt that differently playing either version of Vampire, as long as the ST is good. I like the Requiem setting and systems better, but I've usually had more or less the same experience...I haven't played Masquerade in a while, though, so maybe I'd notice the difference now? It really does depend on the Storyteller. You can run Masquerade very intrigue-y and setting neutral, and you can run Requiem really punk rock and inclusive of all the lore.

          I do find that the Masquerade Disciplines are showing their age compared to VtR2E; choosing Disciplines in Requiem is much more exciting. The basic Disciplines in Masquerade feel like a step backward to me now. And VtM Celerity is a nightmare for any player who didn't take it. Multiple actions are terrible always and forever. I don't throw around the word broken, but it's broken. Apart from that, though, Masquerade Disciplines are a bit bland in comparison.

          I suppose the biggest difference, beyond the setting, is that (Celerity notwithstanding) VtM vampires feel a little weak now.

                                      Now, I've only played a few sessions of Masquerade, so this is largely an outsider perspective. But from where I sit, one of the key differences is in where the central conflict is directed. In Masquerade, the struggle is external. You get brought into a world where, even with your newfound supernatural abilities, you are completely powerless. The people above you are there not by their own merit but by birthright, and yet they have access to power you can literally never achieve on your own - the only way to get it is to steal it from them. It's a game about fighting back against a rigid system that seems built specifically to screw you over. Even the impending apocalypse is just another expression of that same metaphor - you're trapped in a doomed world, and the best you can hope to do when faced with the inevitable end is to fuck shit up for the assholes who let it get this bad in the first place.

                                      Requiem, conversely, is an internally-focused struggle. You are handed thr power to indulge your darkest desires, and then have to figure out what to do with it. The elders aren't the old blood that you can never hope to achieve, they're living examples of what you can and will become as the centuries pass and you gradually lose sight of who you were before.

                                          As a player there is a few differences between Requiem and Masquerade.

                                          The main one is that normally you know what you are getting into with Masquerade. There is sort of a "default way" to masquerade that you normally know that some common character concepts are gonna be viable in any masquerade game and DM will take the effort to let you know when they arent. For example: if i got a common character concept like "snotty Ventrue" or "Creepy lurker nosferatu" or "punk anarch" you know is gonna fly 99% of the time unless the DM is gonna change that, and he will let you know when he does if he would run "in this game all ventrue got killed off in X date, or the anarchs here joined the sabbat, etc etc"

                                          While in Requiem such "default way" doesnt exist. NWoD like to hammer in and on how you can make each city unique and not conform to the "Standard" but doesnt really give you an standard to rebel against. So a firebrand "muh democracy" Carthian might not viable if the DM decided that in his city all anarchs are fascist regime.

                                          With masquerade i have a common floor to think characters in for any game with any DM. A person tells me "i am gonna run Masquerade" and i already got in my mind a few character concept i can play, and when those character arent compatible is the exception rather than the rule. The same isnt equal in Requiem, in Requiem i have to see the city first to see what character concept i can begin to think for the game.

                                          In short the Chargen process (mechanics aside) goes:
                                          Masquerade: PC think broad strokes of a concept, DM comunicates any difference in the setting, player accomodates.

                                          Requiem: DM comunicates everything about it setting, player comes up with a concept that fit that.

                                          Mechanics aside both system arent very good. While OWoD is more simpler sometimes it also has some Chargen traps that have been mentioned. Discipline is a mix bag, Requiem have more mechanically tight disciplines but Masquerade has the fun ones like turning yourself into a Werewolf or control technology with blood magic.

                                          Theme wise, Requiem deal with occupying your time. The big question in Requiem is "You are inmortal, now what?" and all the covenants are answer to vampire who have way too much time on their hands now and need to do something.

                                          Masquerade on the other hand deals similarly but without acknowledging the issue. You can play a vampire who just does his own thing but you will be dragged into the Vampire society one way or another as everyone want you dead on their side. Its a very 80s feel, in which people feer the "apocalypse" that is mostly spout by crazy people and they just live their lives to the fullest.

                                          Aside from that another difference is the organizations. People tend to make the false equivalence of comparing both games Clans vs Clans and Covenants Vs Sect. Which is false, the correct comparison is Masquerade Clans vs Requiems Covenants as the groups you can "choose" to belong in the game and do politics with the others. Requiem Clans are politically meaningless unless the DM is making them a "Thing" and Sect is also meaningless because you dont choose your sect, your DM chooses it when he says "i am gonna run a camarilla/sabbat game" and unless he is comfortable with all the players switching side or is one of those weird games in which 2 of the 5 members of the coterie are Sabbat and the rest are camarilla the other faction is the enemy and thats that.

                                          Originally posted by Malus View Post
                                          YMMV, but to me there's little reason to play WoD unless you explore that there's an apocalypse (or 3 concurrent ones) about to happen.
                                          I disagree, while i think that the splat´s apocalypse should inform the conflict and decisions of some of the characters in the setting/game. The apocalypse itself doesnt need to feature in the game. A good example would be the Walking dead telltale games (havent watched the series), it informs the character and conflict (being in the zombie apocalypse) but beyond that, the cause, development and conclusion of the event itself (the zombie apocalypse) is brushed aside in favor of the personal journey of the main character who are only trying to survive.
