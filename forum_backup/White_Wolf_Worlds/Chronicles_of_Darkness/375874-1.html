    I love the idea of other Mummies around the world, spawned as Iremite imitations or original creations. In Mummy: the Curse, though, I like thinking of them all as Lifeless, and only Mummies whose origins are bound up in the Rite of Return are Deathless.
    I could also see the Arisen counting various other types of immortal or undead as Lifeless without being part of Mummy: the Curse's official entourage - eternals and harvesters particularly come to mind. Which means that you don't necessarily have to make a mummy from another cultural foundation "lesser" as such; but doing them (and the Deathless) justice probably needs keeping the comparison as far out of your head as you can while putting them together.

    While we're on the subject, while it's not exactly about Irem itself, I do have the relics of Reliquary using Sekhem (Reliquary describes a process happening naturally, which the Shan'iatu in effect learned to industrialise), and various of the Immortals immortals do ultimately derive from Iremite lore (the eternals, wardens, and harvesters). To put it another way, my version of Irem hasn't vanished entirely without trace, even if the traces aren't always recognised for what they are. And there are still ruins to find.

    Still, I think my perspective lines up with the game; after all, when people think of a horror-movie-monster Mummy they think Egyptian, bar none.

            I find it pretty interesting that the Sadikh are considered to be Lifeless; especially considering the heavy "these guys are us" theme these guys have going on.

            (So when they choose (intentionally) to accept an Arisen as their master, where they freely give away their true names, they chain their entire existence to the Arisen - they're only active and alive when their Arisen master is, experiencing something like a virtual reality false life while the Arisen is in Duat. A Sadikh cannot, under any circumstance, disobey his master while in his presence. They get to have powers, yes, but those are only borrowed from the Arisen, who can at any time choose not to let the Sadikh access them. The Sadikh can resurrect, but only if the Arisen allows it.)

            (.. It's a subversion of the whole "Workers, not Kings" theme. The Arisen are to the Sadikh as the Judges are to the Arisen. In a setting in which the Arisen have, essentially, been reduced to eternal service as errand boys for some pretender gods of the underworld, creating a Sadikh is basically their only way of passing on the chains of slavery to others. And yet - as the Arisen get to completely control a human being, heart and soul, by turning him into a Sadikh, they themselves are similarly enslaved by the Judges.).

                            For Sadikh, it clearly depends on the structure of the game. If you want to run a game with one Mummy, a ghost/revenant, and a Sadikh, you're obviously going to be letting players look at particular segments of the Storytelling section. They're only going to be an option for player character if that is the format the storyteller chooses. It is one supported by the game rules, but not the default.

                            Personally, I had a player in my game who wanted to take a Retainer anyway. I asked him if he wanted to pay the extra dot to make it a Witness Retainer, and when he said yes, offered a Sadikh as an alternative (plus the extra 8 experience for the Willpower dot). So in order to purchase one, it cost the Retainer merit, plus an extra dot for the Witness upgrade, plus eight experience to represent the Willpower dot. He reckons it was a fantastic deal. Sadikh are explicitly listed as Witnesses, so I just assume you build them like a Witness Retainer and take away the owning character's Willpower. Seems like a reasonable cost for me.

                                I like a relatively diverse Irem, at least in its early and middle periods. I decided Ubar was the sumerian werewolf and scelesti hotbed, and when it fell to Irem, it added a dash of protosumerian/babylonian flavor to Irem in general but especially Eastern Irem. There was an ongoing tug of war in the South, with Nubia/Kush and its invasion being central to a lot of flashbacks. Like Atamajakki, my Irem also had conflicts with the horse nomads in kazakhstan. Irem, like most other empires, failed when it came to fully dominating Afghanistan. They did engage in peaceful trade with proto India and proto China. Iremite colonies existed in the mediterranean islands, though the "sea people" kept them from conquering Greece or Italty. Irem had lots of flavor variation between city-states, though aspects of egypt and babylon are found throughout. Iremite explorers had reached northern europe and asia, but never got beyond outposts. Im still unsure just how much undeath was rampant, but i suspect nenitu slaves, ancestor cults, and the occassional duat-thing should come into the picture. So far, though, the flashbacks have been fairly simplistic in terms of culture setting, as its more focused on the romance between a mummy and his now-reincarnate slave lover. Im looking forward to Sothis Ascends, cuz iirc there will be more solid details on irem and its successors in Uruk and Egypt.

                                                        Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                                                        I'm glad I'm not the only one that has Irem fighting with Ubar, potentially drawing from the Sumerian werewolf god-kings in the Forsaken Chronicler's Guide. And I definitely have an appropriately mythic proto-China (perhaps the Xia Dynasty?) that they had distant trade with.
                                                        While modern "this is the truth behind the myths" theories tend to conflate Irem and Ubar, that's not really consistent with the source material (for one thing, Ubar was a legendary region, not a legendary city). So making them different polities works better, I agree. As I said earlier on, I personally put Ubar in Central Asia rather than Mesopotamia or Arabia - the reason the names show up particularly in Arabian legendry is that that's where they met, and thus where the natives had most reason to refer to them distinctively by name.

                                                        Speaking of which, one thing that is different about my own Irem from the canon: Irem was the city's, and by extension the empires, actual name; it's not a placeholder. The sacrifice of its name is more along the lines of how the Arisen's names are held in Duat - they're not gone and unknowable, they just belong to someone other than those to whom they refer - so it is with Irem.

                                                        How far do you guys Iremite explorers making it to? I'm torn on whether or not they made it to the Americas or not.
                                                        Mm, if they were stalled to the east by Ubar, which is canon, I think it's a bit odd to have them stretch limitlessly in every other direction. Keeping it roughly on a par with the greatest extent of how far Egypt reached makes for a better tone IMO. Into a lot of North and East Africa, probably, even if they didn't colonise there; same with the coast of Europe - the Middle East, some of Turkey - but not India, Asia, the Americas, or the Pacific.

                                                        I mentioned earlier that I have the Shan'iatu being a lot less powerful than they seem in the Book of the Deceived. A reason I didn't mention, but which is part of my thinking, is that I like that part of Irem's success was down to its technological advantage, so I don't want the magic to be so powerful as to render that meaningless. It's an interesting comparison: Irem was a place where new technologies abounded, where a new medium for communication was revolutionising society, and where concepts that hadn't existed a century before were a part of daily life; no wonder mummies can adjust to the present.
