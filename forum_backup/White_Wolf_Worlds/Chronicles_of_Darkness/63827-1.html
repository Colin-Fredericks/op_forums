                Time Limit

                Oh yeah, I get that most times that most combats don't go on for that long - but for me it's not about the mechanical aspect as much as the thematic one - and when I say that, it's not necessarily the thematic one according to just Forsaken. Werewolves that are portrayed as "Garou" in nature in film/tv/literature only tend to come out of their state by choice, by sun up, by calming down or by death. And not because they're a bit knackered. - Which is how it's always come across to me.

                I honestly would run my games without it, but that being said as there seems to be quite a case both for and against removing the time limit.

                What I think the Chronicle book would really suit as a half way point -and what would be thematic to this book and what has gone before it- is your suggestion of rolling for death rage to keep in it and in control of it. I think losing yourself to it would keep the themes in tact. It also allows is both a time limit and an unlimited duration, depending on your point of view.

                Now how long it would take before you start rolling for it, and how much of a penalty you would take is another and different debate I think, especially if the Chronicle is anything like B&S and GMC where a lot of combat and rolls in general can come down to a chance die without the expenditure of hard to return Willpower points. - If I have any of that wrong I'm sorry, been awhile since I've had the core in hands.

                So basically I think that folk who like a limited time, and those who don't, might be able to find some middle ground here. There is of course then always the point where folk who liked the older rules of a time limit can stick with that, and those who don't can just take out a time limit and have elder garou sitting around smoking their peace pipes never quite sure when the other might try to rip them a new one.

                Lunacy

                With Lunacy I think it's harder. There seem to be folk who really don't like it and want it gone, and others who really do like it and want it to stay. There's no real middle ground, except if you change it thematically and have it as an active power, which again some people really like (and think it's reminiscent of the new Predatory Aura rules for B&S in a way) and some who really don't.

                My preference for getting rid of it, or at least really toning it down is that

                a) werewolves have a lot of natural weaponry and gifts to deal with breaches of their world from humans already.

                b) I think that many of the games in general separate the supernatural from the mortal world too much as it is (Lunacy in Werewolf, Mage's Paradox, Disbelief and Unravelling, Vampires Mas...no I like that one) and that true horror for both mortals and supernatural survival is when you have to go that extra mile to hide yourself knowing there are mortals in the know (or you're a mortal and you have SEEN a werewolf

                c) although the supernatural books are About the supernaturals (obviously), they are surrounded by mortals and have to interact with them on some level nearly all of the time, yet the games almost seem to forget the mortal presence at times. - Of course, this is ultimately down to how the ST of any given game presents this, but I think there should be more of a push to drive home that running around in a werewolf form is going to attract attention, steps have to be taken. It's too easy for a lazy ST to "Lunacy it up" so to speak. - this might also be down to the fact that although you should adjust the numbers up and down for your personal game it always comes across to me that there's a pack in every city block else the spirit world is horribly unmanageable (and by extension if you're playing cross-over, vampires, mages, changelings etc all over the place.

                Thanks to everyone

                I am really very interested in what folk have to say about this topic. I know Devs are very interested in what fans have to say and that there's a good chance that they'll steer towards what the majority of the fans want (as long as it fits within their vision), and I would rather it be this way even if they go with a change that I really don't like. I know that these topics can get very heated because folk are very passionate about their games, so thanks for keeping it civil so far on on topic. After Mage, I think werewolf is the game that gets the most contention after all.
