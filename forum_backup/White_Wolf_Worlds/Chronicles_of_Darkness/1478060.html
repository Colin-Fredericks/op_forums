    Dark Heroes in CoFD

    04-05-2022, 12:13 PM
    I've been trying to give Dark Heroes from Mirrors a homebrew update that would bring them into CoFD and also make them like other supernatural templates, and I need some help with it. So far, I've been working on the set of origins, which would be like clans from Vampire or Auspcies from Werewolf. Here's what I have so far:
    Descendants:
    These are the descendants of gods whether children or someone further down the line. Whether it be Zeus, Odin, or someone else, this is the source of their power. It can also be the sources of their strife if they’re not careful. After all, enemies of their parents could be after them too.
    Examples: Heracles and the Volsungs.
    Starting Attribute: One of Resistance.
    Boon: Great Passions: Descendants feel emotions more strongly than normal humans do. They get to pick an emotion in addition to their virtue and vice. They can replenish a point of willpower per day if they get in a scene where they feel that emotion.
    Flaw: Weakness to Hubris: Descendants are particularly weak to Hubris. They take a -3 (maybe increase the penalty?) penalty to all rolls to resist losing Hubris.
    Stereotypes:
    Prodigies: It would seem that humanity has potential too. Good. We need sidekicks, after all.
    Incarnations: It’s like looking in a mirror.
    Champions: Why doesn’t dad love me like he loves you? And why do you have to be every bit as good as us? I’d be more pissed if you weren’t so awesome!

    Prodigies:
    These are people who have unlocked the inner potential of humanity. They have gained power most humans can’t even dream of and look for the next step.
    Examples: Odysseus and Fionn mac Cumhaill.
    Starting Attribute: One of Power.
    Boon: Potential Unleashed: Prodigies spend 1 less experience point on skills (maybe attributes or something different?).
    Flaw: Origins in Humanity: The fact that Prodigies don’t have a higher power as a source of their strength leaves them fundamentally human in some key ways. They get a point of health for every two levels of Arete instead of one.
    Stereotypes:
    Descendants: They’re a bit arrogant, but if they have your back, you can count on them.
    Incarnations: You were a god in a past life? Damn.
    Champions: I feel sorry for them. They have the same power we do but must work for someone to get it.

    Incarnations:
    These are either gods that have incarnated in human form or ancient heroes who’ve reincarnated into new bodies. They acquired the power of their predecessors and may even be able to unlock their memories.
    Examples: Most Hindu heroes.
    Starting Attribute: One of Finesse.
    Boon: This Seems Familiar. Choose someone you’re the incarnation or reincarnation of. When you encounter a situation that is similar to something from a myth or legend involving your past self, a specific story, roll Intelligence+Wits. Each success adds to a dice pool that can be spent on any rolls in the scene. This ability can only be used once per scene and you can only use each die in the pool once.
    Flaw: Incarnations risk being overwhelmed by their memories. If they use the above boon more than twice a day, they must roll Resolve. A failure means the memories are too much for their brain to handle and they lose half the bonus they rolled and a point of Willpower. A dramatic failure also applies the stunned tilt.
    Stereotypes:
    Descendants: These guys get us.
    Prodigies: Normal mortals who’ve reached supernatural levels of talent. Impressive.
    Champions: They are in service to someone. Let’s hope it’s someone friendly.

    Champions:
    A god, powerful spirit, or perhaps even an order has bestowed incredible power on these people. Their great power comes at its own cost, as they are beholden to whoever gave them this power in one way or another.
    Examples: King Arthur and David.
    Starting Attribute: One of Power.
    Boon: Blessed by Power: Once per day, Champions may call upon the power of whatever patron they have. This gives them bonus dice equal to their Arete for a single roll.
    Flaw: Required Obedience: Each session, the Storyteller gets a pool of dice equal to the Champion’s Arete. If a champion does something their patron would not approve of or go against their orders, they may use some of that pool to reduce the Champion’s own dice pool.
    Stereotypes:
    Descendants: They have a lot of bravado, but it’s to cover up their daddy issues. Still, they have legitimate reasons for those. Give them your sympathy.
    Prodigies: They have more suspicion of non-humans than the rest of us. They might be the smartest of all.
    Incarnations: They have similar origins to the Descendants but none of the bravado. It’s really weird.

    Any thoughts? Also, I need ideas for the fifth of these origins.

          I'm putting in an X-Splat to make the Dark Heroes fit the normal Chronicles of Darkness format. This is to make them more fleshed-out than what they were in Mirrors.                                       I'm putting in an X-Splat to make the Dark Heroes fit the normal Chronicles of Darkness format. This is to make them more fleshed-out than what they were in Mirrors. In terms of lore, the X-Splat describes the origins of the characters' powers. In terms of mechanics, the X-Splat is there to give the Dark Heroes both an extra advantage and an extra weakness depending on which the player chooses.                                       *rubs the bridge of her nose*
                                      *puts on her choice of hubris-chasing protagonist song*

                                      So, " to make a thing fit the format" is not just a bad argument in almost any creative venture and is how you end up with cliche riddled properties (and very bad Beast Y/Z-Splat conversations, but that's an unrelated gripe), It's also just not true for either major templates or major gamelines. Changeling has 6 X-Splats, Demon has 4, Beast and Deviant functionally have two different kinds of X-Splats, Hunter famously has none (a useful note on that later, though), and Dark Heroes aren't even the only major published template without splats at all, with the Purified from Immortals also not relying on the splat set up. Moving away from major templates, you can also construct Dark Heroes using a variety of options, including merit templates as seen in Hurt Locker (on record, this is probably what I would go to as a Second Edition incarnation for Dark Heroes), B-Splats, Merits, or just otherwise comprehensive lesser templates, all of which also do not rely on the X-Splat argument.

                                      I get wanting to to flesh them out, but it's got to be a purposeful direction, otherwise you start deviating away from their themes and ideas. As a quick and ready example, by putting an emphasis on their Origin, this presents a game where the protagonists have a particular relationship to that origin (look no further than Deviant literally demonstrating that fact)-which can be interesting, but you have to be sure that's what you want to do either in adjoinment to the splat's theme of Heroic Hubris.

                                      THe big thing is that Dark Heroes work for the context their presented in their context. They're a potent template, but they're not a complicated template. Asides from updating them to 2nd Edition, they work pretty fine. They don't worry about things like where they come from because they are people whose talents and skills make them an honest to god challenge for the powers of the world handle. Because so much of who they are depends on what they specifically do, boxing with conventional X-splats has the risk of limiting character creation-and because Heroes are modeled after lone world-changers, the conventional y-splat feels like it often gets in the way of how Dark Heroes are supposed to feel like their personal ambitions and investments are things which people gather around, and which other like people are often only to going to connect to on the band level and otherwise are likely to be such big personalities with impactful actions and decisions that it keeps Dark Heroes from developing conventional organizations or philosophies.

                                      Updating it means having an understanding of why things changed between editions and finding the right blend of keeping to that to bending the intent for Dark Heroes themes*. Adding something means making a decision of how much to try and keep to the original themes, moods, and atmospheres of the original versus how much to deviate from, and how much an addition does that.

                                      So I can give an example so I can sort of more readily demonstrate what I'm talking about it-so a fair while back I got the random urge to give Hunter, of all gamelines, an X-Splat-and I started that exercise with an acknowledgement of all the reasons why Hunter didn't need an X-Splat so I could be square in knowing why, what, and how I was doing that exact thing. In this case, I made the x-splat Wounds, broad categorizations of the sort of traumas that ignite the desperation that defines almost every hunter's Vigil, with the goal of it being an add-on that players or storyteller could apply to signal more personally driven, emotionally grounded stories that centers on the fact that most, if not all, hunters are damaged people with an unhealthy coping mechanism, and with that incentivize desperate action trying to prevent or avenge similar traumas while also creating conditions where coming across reminders of that trauma is spiralling, and point out how the desperation that comes with dealing with trauma parallels other supernatural instances**. And I did all of that because I felt like that was a viable and interesting thing that related to the themes and ideas of Hunter that I felt weren't as well expressed in 2nd Edition.

                                      And the thing of that is that while it's what I went with for that experiment, it's not the only way I could have gone. I noted, in my justification, that in first edtion for Hunter, the closest thing that that it had to an x-splat was the Professional Training merit, due to what was, at the time, an exclusive presence in that game line that gave direction on what your character, where they came from, and what they did. From that little notice, it's not hard to imagine Hunter having an X-Splat of Service, which would not only guarentee you dots in the Professional Training merit, but also offer ways in which you excel in situations where it lines up, offers benefits for creatively reapplying those skills and expectations, and pointing out ways that training trips up hunters. Not only is that just as rooted in what Hunter has done before, but it also anchors it in the working class mentality that first edition was largely grounded in, really bringing in how the everyman can step up to the shadows and make their difference, but also comment on how the modern world and that very same mentality contributes to the deliberate non-focus on the shadows in the first place.

                                      And both of those are motivated by not just wanting to create a X-splat for Hunter just because, but by taking portions of Hunters text and creating an alternative play style that emphasizes certain themes and ideas in Hunter to create a more distinctive style of play-and mind you, those are alternatives, because I basically have no good argument that Hunter should have an X-Splat, because no, they don't.

                                      ANYWAYS.

                                      If you're really dedicated to the idea of an X-Splat, and assumedly an adjacent y-splat, then I would probably start around the idea of heroic archetype and common cause to commit hubris over, to help players focus on what sort of character they want to be in play, and with that stated, I might recommend looking over at how Mage does things for ideas (in fact, while I wouldn't suggest making Dark Heroes Supernally-powered, a lazy part of me actually kind of groks the idea that Dark Heroes are also in the Orders), and I would also point at Beast's Hungers for ways to think on more directed hubris-but again, I would really start on interrogating on what you think a fleshed out Dark Hero splat should be like, and what any planned additions would actually contribute to that ideal.

                                              Okay, first off, thank you to everyone who's posted on this thread so far. You've all helped me a lot, and I'm hoping that I understand what you're saying. So, to answer a few questions:
                                              ArcaneArts , I am not dedicated to the idea of making an X-Splat. I thought that would be a good way to start, and you have a point that it might not need an update, so I'll rethink the x-splat thing in general. It also seems that I should have been thinking of the themes before the mechanics and not the other way around. I don't necessarily want to limit the player options either.
                                              monteparnas , I don't think I can answer all your questions, which is part of the problem that I had in the first place, but they gave me a general idea of what I want to do that I'll tell everyone now.
                                              It might be a bit too ambitious, so let me know what you think. I'm presenting my logic pure and simple, and I am more than willing to remove things if you think I'm trying to handle too much or the themes won't work.

                                              Basically, to put it in terms of other games, it's sort of a mixture of Mage and Deviant in terms of its themes.
                                              On the mage end, the idea is that the dark heroes are all people ascending to a high level of power and possible divinity; however, they must deal with their own hubris in the process. Here I'm drawing a bit on Werewolf's version of Integrity. Basically, the idea is that if hubris gets too high, they'll think of themselves too highly and it'll make them suffer in some way. But if hubris gets too low, they'll risk suffering by giving up on everything because they'll lose hope. The idea is still in its infancy and I'm not sure if that's the best way to go about it or what the exact mechanical effects would be.

                                              On the sort of Deviant end, the reason why I thought an X-Splat would be a good idea (which I'm now reconsidering) is because I thought dark heroes having to deal with their origins was part of the whole mythos of mythical heroes in general. For example, let's say you have a dark hero who is the result of human potential coming forth. From their perspective, they might have earned their powers while other people just got lucky. Or they might feel inadequate because their origins are in humanity while dealing with other beings that may have been born supernatural. Both those could affect hubris, and an X-Splat may or may not be the best way to go about it. I'm not certain.

                                              Once more, thank you for commenting here, and I'm sorry if I'm a bit dense here. This is the first time I'm doing this sort of thing.
