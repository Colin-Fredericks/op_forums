    Come to think of it, Mummy is a natural fit for this: Iberia wasn't part of the Nameless Empire, but it was damn close to their borders was likely known if not visited. That's before you factor in later migrations and conquests up through the Carthaginians, Romans, Visigoths, and Berbers, all of whom contributed to the region's history across the centuries. An Arisen on the peninsula is just as likely to be an old pagan god as they are a secret Christian saint or a secret among the Moors, and they likely last awoke for the Sothic Turn ages ago in Roman times or during one of the many religious (re)conquests. To be Arisen in such a time means navigating layers of courtly intrigue, varied ethnic groups all with deep tensions between each other, and a great many faiths, none of which look anything like the proper worship of the Judges.

    And all that, of course, is before the native vampires mistake you for a truly ancient elder or some worse threat to their lovely parasitic status quo.

            Originally posted by reseru View Post
            I feel like there's a lot of ideas for everything but Vampire. Why do ya'll think that is?

            Maybe this question is kinda outside the scope of this thread, but does Vampire's themes resonate with the CofD audience the same way that Masquerade did to roleplayers two decades ago? Or even the same way Changeling, or Beast, seem to nowadays?
            I think Vampire lost a lot more fans in the switch between Masquerade and Requiem than the other lines for a lot of reasons beyond the scope of this thread, and I think that even people who would like it dismiss it as somehow the most basic or generic of them all and move onto the others, to say nothing of the line going dormant for two years after its 2e release. There's a lot more vampire fiction/media of every genre imaginable than there are for werewolves, wizards, people kidnapped and traumatized by fairies and now struggling to rebuild their lives, amnesiac Predynastic Egyptian immortals, etc, and I think folks are afraid of reinventing the wheel there when other options exist.

            For what it's worth, I think it's a damn shame. Requiem is a damn good game and one I consider an outright improvement over Masquerade, and I think that because they have to stick so close to their mortal herds and because they're functionally immortal there's more potential for them in historical setting than any other line. A product like Dark Eras is a godsend for Vampire because you can realistically have the same player characters originate in one or possibly even several.

            Here's hoping Dark Eras 2 gives Requiem a lot more love.

            Remi. she/her. game designer.
