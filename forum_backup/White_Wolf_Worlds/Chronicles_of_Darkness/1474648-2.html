            Originally posted by orathaic View Post
            If mage Tim hears that spiders can respond faster thanphysics/biology would allow...
            I think the problem with this premise, is that mages should basically all know that this is a bad starting point. There's no such thing as "a biological organism that does something the rules of biology don't allow." That's not how the rules of the Fallen World work. Without access to the Supernal, creatures are bound by the rules of the world they exist in, so the idea that a naturally occurring animal can do things impossible for biological organisms to do just don't work. So either:

            1) It's sensationalized/mythologized lay misconceptions about what science cannot explain, or has not explained yet.

            2) It's something Quiescence or Quiescence-like where a supernatural effect was mistaken for a natural one and got twisted around into an odd science publication (likely starting #1 in the first place).

            Mages are not the kind of people that take Sleeper science at face value (they understand the true rules of the world far better than scientists can), and are going to be even more skeptical of all of the wild pop-culture science "facts" that happen because science reporting is rarely both good and interesting (and interesting usually beats good in terms of what makes non-scientific publication).

            Mages are Obsessed with Mysteries. That sort of inquisitive mentality leads to a great deal of skepticism for "too good to be true" claims. It might not count as a Mystery, but most mages would at least Google something like that to see that it's just one of those silly science pop-culture myths and not something real.

                                        So, this conversation is still going on?

                                        I think I can parse the matter in a simpler manner. I'm not sure, but I'll try.

                                        Mage the Ascension was about reality being ultimately defined by belief, and weird results could arise when conflicting beliefs clashed. This is not Ascension.

                                        Quite the contrary, Awakening has the same open-ended magic system built upon the opposite premise in-lore: reality has well defined natural rules and magic work upon them. It happens that science is ill suited to find its actual rules, while Mages have unfair advantages to do so.

                                        From there, it is the setting's natural laws that aren't necessarily as similar to RL as it seems at first glance, but that doesn't mean they are not as strict. They are undefined from the player's perspective, but that's it. At the end of the day, a Mage is as incapable as anyone of doing something actually impossible.

                                        Knowledge and Ability with technical details aren't required. Why? The same reason Chemistry, Electrodynamics and Cooking are distinct abilities through and through despite dealing with the exact same body of phenomena. Knowing all of them may give you new insights with what to do with them, but you don't need to know one to understand the other, neither becomes good at one by learning the other. A Chemist with deep knowledge of electrodynamics and no cooking experience will still be a terrible cook.

                                        At the end, a Mage with enough Life has enough knowledge of the "Magical Science" to: see and understand the Symbol for a spider's senses and reactions, to understand how this Symbol can be applied to a person, and what effects this will have from the standpoint of the Supernal Symbols. It works because it is possible, not just because the Mage wills it or imagines it (even if imagining is part of the process, just as putting your hands in the dough is part of the baking process). If the Mage tries the actually impossible, it fails because it is impossible, but the Mage's understanding of the result under other sciences, while valid, doesn't change if it will work or not.

                                        No penalty is necessary. To keep with the example, the magic works because its magic, but specifically in terms of the setting it works because the end result (a person with functional spider senses and no drawbacks) is what happens when the Symbol for the spider senses is correctly attached to the person without the addition of any drawback Symbols, and the natural laws of Supernal phenomena determine that the organism adapt in such and such ways to comply. The Mage needs not to know how it works under the laws of "mundane" Biology, but it does also work under the laws of mundane CofD Biology all the same. Indeed, a Mage with enough Biology knowledge could then seek to study the end result as a Mystery in itself, because the Mage knows enough of Biology to understand the peculiarities of the result from that perspective, that is still a valid one.

                                                    Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                                    The only thing to add is 'a Mage with enough Life...' - without enough life some things will fail.
                                                    Such failure is more akin to the failure due to lack of knowledge.

                                                    The key here is that the Symbol must be understood and correctly manipulated and attached. A Mage without enough Life either can't grasp enough of the Symbol to be sure about the right one or to be sure how to attach them. The Arcana represent knowledge about a set of Symbols, including the knowledge needed to identify them. Perception is also learned, after all.

                                                    Anyone can look at a rock that has crystals in it. Only a properly trained person can identify the minerals in that rock, perceive nuances and find the pieces that will shine if polished. To others it is just a dull rock and nothing can even be attempted except study it further. A trained person looks at a piece of meat and sees the telltale signs of where one cut becomes another, others just see a piece of meat and may at most have the impression that it seems odd. A blind person that undergoes some procedure to acquire vision can't tell apart a tree from a slab of concrete, they don't just can't name them, they literally can't tell them apart until they have enough experience. All of this is actual IRL facts about perception.

                                                    Imagine now a blind person undergoing a procedure to acquire sight, then studying the minutiae of gem cutting and jewelry. That's a Mage in a nutshell.

                                                    From this, the mechanical differences between Magic and other Abilities is merely a design choice. Each dot represent a greater gap in knowledge and understanding, but not an actual distinct process, at least not in a demonstrable way. It is just that making similar tables and rules for mundane Abilities won't have the same pay off for the game.

                                                            Magic also overrules whatever phenomena the symbols apply to, but nothing beyond that. Create Life is a classic example:

                                                            The mage casts a Making

                                                            Supernal: Ok, he is creating a cat. Orange fur, stripes, fat, yep, got all those symbols. Now where is that last...aha, the eats lasagna property. Symbols assembled, ship the Imago.

                                                            Garfield appears.

                                                            The mage casts another Making

                                                            Supernal: This time its a dragon, based on Ancalagon. Scale factor checks out. Fire breath via a flamethrower like mechanism, I think I can make it using a better version of the bombardier beetle's symbols. Armored hide, dense skeleton, easy. Razor fangs, sharp claws, leathery wings. Ok, all set, ship the Imago.

                                                            Fallen World: Hey, just a quick check. Did that Imago include any Forces symbols to overwrite the laws of physics ?

                                                            Supernal: No....

                                                            Fallen World: Well then...carry on

                                                            Dragon materializes

                                                            Dragon: cough...my bones collapsed on themselves, why, what is the Square-Cube Law ? My wings cannot fly, what are aerodynamics? EVERYTHING IS PAIN !!!

                                                            Understanding Fallen principles allows the mage to nudge things to their desired outcome more easily. A person who is already exhausted can be put to sleep with a Compelling rather than Weaving of Mind.

                                                            It may also provide inspiration for spells, such as comprehending neural networks might inspire a mage to use a Making to create an electronic intelligence using Forces, allowing it to "Possess" electronic devices. But ultimately you need to work within the set of possible symbols that define your Arcanum. Going beyond that might require a mundane solution, some kind of supernatural material, the aid of a supernatural being, adding conjunctional Arcana or just creative use of more spells to patch the flaws.

                                                            There is no Consensus in Awakening. There is the Fallen with things that are, the Supernal with things that could be, the Abyss with things that cannot be and so on. But these are in no way informed by the thoughts of the masses, they are independent fundamental pillars of reality. Mages specialize in being able to search the nigh infinite possibility space of the Supernal and use it to overwrite reality.
