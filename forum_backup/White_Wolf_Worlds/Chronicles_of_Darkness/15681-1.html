    I have two ideas. One is that the God-Machine is the God humanity has built for itself in the image of how we want Gods to operate, a celestial slave or a vending machine that answered our prayers on demand. There's no set origin point as Causality is one of the tools used in the God-Machine, but there have been major events in it's creation. However, this God has other ideas obviously, that or it's roundabout methodology of answering our prayers has mutated into something far less benign.

    The other idea I've had is less philosophical and more connected to Mage lore, in that the God-Machine is what evolved primary from the leftovers of Atlantis's War on Heaven, and probably from the Irem's mass production of mummies, as the complex means to the Rite of Return probably left an impression on the fabric of reality. In any case, the ruling intelligences of the fundamental forces of reality have been more or less murdered, replaced, or rewritten by humanity, but whatever remains has collelesed into what we now call the God-Machine, which piggybacks on the creativity of Humanity as both it's creator, enemy, and tool.

    I think if I was to run a game of Demon, my God-Machine won't have a single origin point, and if I had to use an origin, it would be a combination of the above two ideas. It would be an entity that's tied to humanity, maybe even created by it...but by now, it owes us nothing and is an independent and active entity that quietly looms behind the scenes, sometimes blind, sometimes all-seeing, but always dangerous no matter who or what you are. It's not everywhere...but it is anywhere.

        Michael.

        I am not a fan of the Godmachine concept at all and don't want to implement this post modern deus ex machina in my historic setting (it would ruin its flavour in my opinion). But the rules system offered for Demon characters in Demon: The Descent is very intriguing. In particular the concept of Cover is just pure genius. Written bargains for souls that have a major impact in-play ? Sign me in !

        So the question for me was, how to deal with all the references to the God Machine, Infrastructure and technology in the rules?

        Obviously, the God Machine is not simply "God": "The God-Machine has spies throughout the world. Although not omniscient or omnipotent, it carefully monitors events in the world for evidence of rogue agents and takes steps to remove potential obstacles to its plans." (quoted from Demon: The Descent)

        So I have made the decision to replace the Godmachine with the Archangel Michael in my chronicle: "The Archangel Michael has spies throughout the world. Although not omniscient or omnipotent, he carefully monitors events in the world for evidence of rogue (fallen) angels and takes steps to remove potential obstacles to his plans." This makes perfect sense in a medieval/early modern age campaign. "Infrastructure" does not exist as described, but is the schemes and occult matrices of the Archangels.

        Of course, many technology references also have to be removed from the descriptions and some abilities, but otherwise, this general replacement allows me to use Demon: The Descent in my chronicle without too much difficulties.

            I've only recently been putting much thought into what the God-Machine is as a whole. I assume some of it will be revealed in a chronicle that I'm starting up soon.

            As far as where it came from, I've started running with the idea that it started as universe "flotsam", possibly from some ancient extinct civilization, or maybe just the right set of debris came together to create an accidental Occult Matrix. From that, it began to propagate itself, infecting worlds it came across, including Earth. It has burrowed deep into these worlds and after so long, has become a part of them. So much so that it would be impossible to be rid of it, and dangerous as well. The God-Machine crosses dimensions as well. Not just to places like the Shadow or the Underworld, but places most have never, and likely will never see. It has found ways to keep them separate, and expends countless resources against, either to fix or control, tears between the worlds or those that have found ways to move about them.

            It's keeps us separate for its experiments, so that it can have uncontaminated results. The inhabitants of the World of Darkness like to think they're special, but to the God-Machine, they're just a petri dish. The purpose of these experiments are unknown. It would be impossible to fully decipher the motivations of an entity as encompassing as the God-Machine.

            The God-Machine falls short of omniscience. It's not even capable of actively monitoring every spot where it has infected a world. Just as a human looking at the palm of it's hand cannot see the back of it. It must decide where to divide its attention. It's former agents are of special interest and often take a high priority. It has some ability to look through time. It does not know the future, though. It sees hundreds of thousands of possible futures, which is often why glitches arise in its methods. If a person is a threat in one possible future, or a key component in another, the GM ma act to both motivate and destroy them at the same time.

            I've got more, but I haven't quite hammered out all the details.

            One thing I'm going for is that there are holes between the worlds where the God-Machine crosses between. Places where it needs to use to send things between dimensions, to places that are either uninhabited, or the inhabitants aren't as cooperative as humans. I'm also envisioning a world that some may come across that is like a giant city of pipes, gears, and machinery that some might suspect as possibly the heart of the God-Machine. The only problem is, it's impossible to tell if this is one world or many, or even just a space between the worlds.

                It is a God. As such, it is a vast conscious which circles the whole world. It has messengers which does its will and powers which shape the cosmos. It can penetrate both space and time, and influence everything which is found in its sphere of influence. It cares about the world, even for purely selfish reasons, and work toward a goal which either make people worship it in vain or lights up the fire of rebellion in their heart.

                It is a Machine. As such, it follows a certain pattern and certain rules. It require resources in order to function and collect certain materials in order to make a certain products. It works in cycles, like the wheel of seasons or ebb and flow. It isn't an independent being, but serves the purpose of its builder. Its products are great and terrible, but they are still products- which means that someone out there gains something from the its constant work.

                So what is the God Machine? Well, I think i is something like the Kicksey-winsey from the Death gate Cycle. It was made ages ago by God, race of gods or other great being to help the World Of Darkness in a time of great need- the world was on the verge of Chaos or destruction, or simply didn't was about to change in a way that the creator didn't wished the world to be. Acting out of hubris, the creator decided to make a great machine which will fix the world- but it couldn't do so right away. The machine wasn't strong enough, and for that reason the maker gave it two missions to fulfill until it will gain enough power- first, to grow, so one day it will become strong enough. The second, to maintain the Statue Que, so while things won't become better, they won't turn worse either. In order to maintain a fluid and clean function of the machine- and to give itself some free time to do other stuff except of maintaining the machine- the creator gave the machine an intelligence of its own. A limited one, sure, by still a vast and powerful one- one which was so vast that a lesser being may even worship the machine and name it as a god.

                Yet, before the creator could move to "phase 2", something happened. Maybe the creator died. maybe it left after losing hope. Maybe it went to sleep and still didn't woke up. Maybe he simply forgot. Anyway, the machine was left alone, and without new orders it did the only things it knew- it grew and maintained the Status Que. It is still waiting for the new orders, for someone who will come and tell it that it is the time to fix the world. But there is no one who knows what should be done and how in order to control it, to allow it to fulfill its ancient purpose..

                The God Machine is Fallen, but it May Rise Again.

                Edit: yes, I'm optimistic and I think that the GM is nice deep down, Sue me.

                        I've been meaning to contribute to this thread and keep putting it off... but now that it's fresh in my mind, here we go:

                        The construct that grew into what is known as the God Machine was built in response to the Fallen World and Supernal Realms being cut off by the Abyss. Some ancient culture or race tried to produce a machine that could reconnect the worlds because while the Supernal Realms aren't entirely the end-all be-all of reality, they could still serve as something of a control room. And even establishing the magical equivalent of tin cans and string across that divide would have been something more than what the universe had at the time. (even with paths to those realms, like the Hedge, still in existence in some form or another) They didn't get it exactly right, but they built a scaffold of sorts from which they could build and improve.

                        Those creators (whatever they were, whether human or Gnome of Zurich or something else altogether) are gone. Not simply dead, not simply forgotten, but written out of existence by some greater force (if not Imperial Magic, certainly something on par with it) while their machine was somehow spared. Bits of the world that existed when it was first constructed have been washed away like sand castles when the tide rolls in. But between the fact that its creators who properly understood it are gone and it was designed to improve upon and 'correct' a world that doesn't exist in that same form, it's grown out of control. There is a Design, there is a Greater Plan. It's not entirely stupid and random. It was designed to be able to improvise and make decisions when need be; the creators knew it would be able to come up with solutions they couldn't faster than they could so they left it some capacity to function on its own.

                        However, it was not meant to function without guidance and it was not meant to function in the world as it exists today. It's made itself smarter and more powerful and it's grown in the ways it thinks it needs to grow but the end result has been a living technological cancer spreading behind the skin of reality. Its grand design can't strictly be known, its full agenda functions outside humanity's capacity for logic, but one of its priorities is keeping the world functioning and mostly intact (hence those operations where it diverts comets or keeps super-volcanoes from erupting or prevents humanity from developing the Mazarin-Rashoud Node). But it doesn't have the context to understand that there are subtleties to 'functioning' and 'intact.' It's like the teleporter from "The Fly" (Cronenberg remake), when it would turn things inside out because it didn't understand that the 'parts' needed to be in a certain order. Not full-blown Blind Idiot God, but close.

                                    My personal take on the God Machine question is the same as Anaximander's one, wrote in a thread about the God Machine and Principle natures some time ago:
                                    Originally posted by Anaximander, Old White Wolf Forums
                                    My personal interpretation is that the God-Machine and The Principle are the "right hand" and the "left hand" of the nWoD cosmos itself. Nearly every gameline has a cataclysm in the distant past (the fall of Atlantis; Father Wolf dying; the destruction of Irem). No matter the details or the "truth," an axiom of the nWoD is that the world is BROKEN in some fundamental way. It's why it's a world of darkness. The God-Machine and The Principle are the products of the world recognizing its brokenness. The God-Machine is the "band-aid." It's all that's keeping the world together rather than flying off into a million pieces. It's what keeps the Abyss from devouring over the Fallen World, why the Gentry can't do mass invasions through the Hedge. It's far from fully successful, but it does its job well enough. The Principle, on the other hand, is the "healing process." It's the world trying to make itself better. It is the part of the world undergoing it's own "Pilgrimage" for a "New Dawn" on the cosmic scale. Neither of these are "nice." They do "good" and "bad" for their own reasons, which even the Ascended can't fully grasp. They use similar tools because they're twins born of the world's needs, though they are different because they are addressing different parts of the problem and were "designed" to do different things.

                                                My personal take is that the God-Machine is a separate parasitic reality. When the world was whole, in the mythic age before the sundering of spirit and matter and the fall of Atlantis, the GM could not penetrate our reality. It had it's own reality, which it had all but used up. It would be something on the order of a Kardashev 10, having harnessed and USED all the power in it's entire REALITY to grow itself. And it was, slowly, dying. It had nowhere to grow, nowhere to gain more power to feed it's endless need.

                                                And then we broke our reality. Spirit and Matter were sundered, the Supernal and Material planes severed and the Abyss came into being. And those cracks gave the God-Machine access. A way to squeeze the tiniest fraction of it's being into our realm. Now it works towards Three ends. One, to create occult matricies that funnel power into it's home reality to fuel it. Two, to bend and rewrite our reality to be more like it's own, so that it can, one day, move in. Three, to widen the cracks and prepare the way so that, once it has enough power and once this reality is enough like it's home reality, it can shift the greater portion of it's being into our nice, warm, cozy, energy rich reality.

                                                The reason it has trouble with supernatural beings is that it's home reality was purely material. Things like mortals existed, but not things like Vampires or Werewolves.
                                                The reason Demons are able to escape is, when they fall, they become a part of THIS reality, separated from the GM's native reality.
