        Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
        Is there something akin to personal Archetypes, or just Krewe ones?
        Mechanically, your Root and Bloom (and, to a lesser degree, your krewe's Virtue and Creed) fill the role that Archetype did in 1e.

        Also, is there anything similar to Vampire Bloodlines, Mage Legacy or Changeling Entitlement for Geists? Any edition would do.
        Structurally, your krewe Archetype is your characters' shared Y-splat and developing the krewe further is used for that type of character refinement; individually, your character's Remembrance provides the kind of accumulative semi-personalized bonuses a bloodline, Legacy, or entitlement would provide, with Krewe Regalia filling in the gaps somewhat; simply raising your Synergy won't make your crisis point trigger narrower or give you access to Plasm-powered Skills and Merits, nor will it allow you to temporarily boost your Rank, enhance Skill rolls, or gain new Keys.

        Similar to how in Promethean 2e Athanors were changed from a Z-splat to material keys to the Azothic Memory that facilitate Promethean characters going through the Pilgrimage with less of a direct line of tutelage required (and the effects of the New Dawn are more mechanically defined), and similar to how in Demon the Cipher is an optional method of diving deeper into mystical secrets for a splat with an incentive to stay under the radar and an inclination to think in terms of purpose, and similar to how the core Deviant template is incredibly bare-bones in no small part because it's sharing bookspace with the most mechanically involved version of the organization mechanics, Geist 2e takes the tack that when you are playing a character whose specific driving goals are to resolve at least one of two personal quests so there's nothing making you especially regret dying before your time, a specific separate character development axis with a fully separate purpose from your Burden, Remembrance, and krewe Doctrine is kind of beyond the scope of need for a typical PC.

        In 1e, as Teatime notes, expanding your krewe's channel allows it to double as a Z-splat as well as a player group, built out like one would a Hunter conspiracy with a side of weird syncretic mythology, and expand it as one would expect for a death cult powered in part by initiated headcount.

                                                        Originally posted by Hyuse View Post
                                                        In my Mage campaign, we are dealing with ghouls and revenants. Revenants not being the Vampire kind but the kind that are people who don't die and basically possess their own bodies. Do Geists/Bound deal with these things and how functionally different are they from each other?
                                                        Geist assumes that every ghost who has not descended to the Underworld has their body or their gravesite as one of their Anchors and provides a Merit for Absent characters (ghosts who trade for Memories to supplement their more conventional means of gaining Essence and serve as the gameline's secondary playable character type much as ghouls in Vampire or wolf-blooded in Werewolf) that models being bound to one's own dead body in a more enduring fashion than the scene at a time that the Possession Manifestation allows, which gives the character Health instead of Corpus and requires the consumption of a substantial amount of raw meat (or a smaller amount of raw human meat) per week for upkeep and healing, as well as requiring the character spend Willpower to interact with other people socially.

                                                        Outside of those contexts, there's the well-known capacity for ghosts to simply Possess a dead body (whether their own or, if they're a geist, a body whose means of death matches their innate Key) on a limited basis and any stranger phenomena that don't have consistent mechanics to model them.

                                                            Bold by me, this, a lot of attention to this.

                                                            There's a number of distinct ways to have "Revenants" in the setting already covered, and a heavy implication that this isn't a particularly uncommon result for weird phenomena.

                                                            Which means, for the second question, that it isn't straightforward to state how they functionally differ. Each Revenant may follow rules with significant distinctions in any aspect. A commonality that you'll find is a lack of natural healing and some form of decay over time, but even those can't be guaranteed. Revenants usually don't have many powers beyond their physical bodies and whatever they do to heal and/or come back, but then again, no consistent, general rules.

                                                            For the first question, yes, though what "deal" means depends. But yes, since those are usually ghosts possessing bodies, they're within the purview of the Bound, and if if one isn't the Bound won't know without a proper investigation.
