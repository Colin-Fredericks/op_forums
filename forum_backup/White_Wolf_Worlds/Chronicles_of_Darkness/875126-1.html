                                                And don't forget the Seers, who are all about being privileged jerks who are there for power and getting away with enjoying the best out of life.

                                                Stereotypes are narrow visions that don't even apply to the games as designed. The stereotypical vampire is basically the spider in the web of contacts and social influence, but the Lance, Circle, Ordo and VII don't fit that stereotype in the first place. While Rahu and Irraka do tend to be "melee monsters," the Cahalith, Itheur and Elodoth tend to have more social and mystical leanings, and often lack combat skills. The stereotypical mage is basically a wizard in their library (ie the Mysterium) - but that's only one of many Orders. Frankenstein is the sterotypical Promethean, but we can have golems and robots as well, who act nothing like Shelly's creation. And so on.

                                                Now, perhaps a better question would be "how do we play outside of each game's specialty?" Werewolves are hunters. Vampires use cooersion. Mages investigate. Prometheans evolve, changelings use illusions, hunters fight monsters, Bound serve as mediums and psychopomps, demons spy. Etc. If a vampire avoids use of any cooercive abilities, how well do they play? Well, pretty well to an extent - this is what a High Humanity vampire looks like, and playing one is highly rewarding if you like that kind of game. Mages that don't investigate? Less so - investigating strange things is the core of the game, so if you don't investigate, there's no game. Same with a lot of others. Playing against your specialty generally means you don't have any involvement in the plot of the games.

                                                            Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                                            Now, this I could see if done like Flashman (or Ciaphas Cain)
                                                            Actually, that's an Arrow heresy. You basically get kicked out, but people still come to you for advice if you're well known enough. Its already a well known phenomeon in the AA.

                                                            What about a non-Banisher that just wants to hide their head in the sand (but the circumstances and the Fallen World don't agree with them)? They don't investigate Mysteries, they get thrown into them and just try to survive.
                                                            That's a paradox, no pun intended. All mage powers revolve around investigation. If something happened to this non-Banisher, they would use their abilities to look at this mystery and figure it out to survive. The only way your suggestion is remotely viable is if our non-Banisher in question deliberately ignores anything that registers to his otherworldly senses, shuts his eyes and ears, and runs away all the time. At which point? That's going out of your way to avoid the game's plot.

                                                            And that's not touching on how Obsessions and Consilium work. Mages literally have an innate drive to study mysteries and become addicted to them. Consilium are literally built around mysteries that mages gather 'round to study. If you don't want the mystery, you simply ask for help (other mages would love to study the mystery) and leave the city for somewhere banal. At which point, there's no plot anymore, and thus no game. You can't even argue that its one's destiny to be bothered this way, because a simple Fate 2 shield will protect you against that. A mage that wants to bury their head in the sand can pull it off easily.

                                                            Furthermore, this is a thread about how easy it is to play against type. Your suggestion has nothing to do with playing against type, and everything to do with an ST forcefully creating an abnormal plot to enable this one thing. That's pretty much the definition of "duper difficult" if you have to design an entire campaign around one PC and try to shoehorn everything in.
