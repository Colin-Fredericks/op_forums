                    To just point out what Dave has already said and others have already posted in this forum (once more):
                    DaveB !!kn3vuELOelB 12/03/15(Thu)05:50:08

                    In the scene directly following exposure to the Supernal or Abyssal, a Sleeper forgets what she saw, or else changes how she remembers it toward the mundane. Any serious attempt to prod at those false memories, bring them into question, or correct them with magic trigger a breaking point as if she were witnessing the traumatic event again for the first time, followed by the memory being removed again. It is as harrowing in memory is it is in reality. Quiescence, like Dissonance, is not limited to the spells of mages; Sleepers’ memories are clouded by the interior of ruins of the Time Before, the presence of the Mad, Emanation Realms, and sighting Supernal or Abyssal entities.

                    Pretty much any Hunter tactic to take notes, record what's happening, or prod at Quiescence because you think a mage is nearby will put you on a one-way train to Crazytown, Paranoia. population: you.

                    Unless I can interest you in this shiny one-dot Merit called "Sleepwalker"...
                    So:
                    • Memory's can be wiped clean (i.e. a gap in your memory or they can become re-written to explain away the magic a of a scene to be mundane.
                    • it is the trauma of seeing magic that causes the integrity loss, and the memory warping or repression is a means to protect the sleepers mind from further harm.
                    • Mentioning offhand won't trigger any issues, but forcing a confrontation between the warped memory/lack of it and reality so as to make someone aware of what happened causes the trauma to re-occur and the defenses to kick back in (i.e. integrity loss and memory alteration). This includes reviewing notes, having someone tell you, watching a video, etc.
                    • it is not just obvious magic, but essentially anything of the abyss or supernal, that causes this damage and forces the memory changes in the sleeper to allow them to stay sane(-ish).
                    • Dave state a Hunter who wants to investigate and see supernal magic needs the sleepwalker merit, or will face this memory loss, or insanity if they try to overcome it.

                    There is also specific mention in another Dave post that the trauma is due to the Abyss response to the act of experiencing the Supernal (or being forced to re-experience it) and if someone didn't forget but still had the abyssal shard, they would quickly go insane from seeing an act and then reliving it (and sufferingthe corresponding trauma) again and again.

                    Here is what I get from this:
                    • It sucks to be a mage. Sure, you have this nice added hammer to the fireball you throw at those annoying sleepers (if you are willing to take a Wisdom hit, at least), but it means you can't use obvious magic around anyone with out mind raping them and warping their memory. So all your friends and family? Unless you lucked out to be born into a bunch of sleepwalker/Proximi, expect to never be able to reveal anything unless you want to traumatize dear old mom and dad (and then have them forget anyway). Save that school bus if kids by blasting the rampaging demon back into the Shadow? Great, you just ripped up their souls a bit and no one will remember your heroics (beyond maybe being the weirdo who ran into a bus and shot a wierd bear with a rocket launcher, or something). Heck, it means Mages trying to investigate such an attack cannot ask witnesses of it info and get anything accurate without traumatizing the sleepers again. Mages are walking Cthullus who reveal themselves anytime they reveal their power, and even the memory of what

                      Essentially, the weirdness censor around mages hurts everyone, not just hunters, including sometimes the mage themselves (for the Wisdom risk). If Dresden followed Mage rules, half of Chicago likely would have gone insane by now with warped recollections of days worth of memories.
                    • That said, we know sleepers can help and participate in the Orders, and even know someone is a mage/can do magic. They don't forget that or go into shock from it. This points to the fact that while obvious magic, along with everything abyssal and supernal, is shielded by the curse, knowledge about such things can be learned. A sleeper could know their master needs a virgin sacrifice for a spell and deliver her/him to the mage, and later clean up the mess. Their master could explain that said sacrifice will let them cast a spell assuring their enemies lie in ruin, and then the sleeper hears about the mysterious death across town of a powerful gangster. But if they saw the spell itself being cast, or witnessed the effects, it likely would be scrubbed from their mind.
                    • Dave has given us the answer to what you can do if you want Hunters to have means to remember: take the merit. And considering its cost versus all the damage you could take from not buying it, it seems like a no brainer, especially since the first encounter with obvious magic would likely earn a good number of beats in my game, especially with the trauma component).

                    So, can sleeper Hunters hunt an Awakened? Sure. It just means that Mages are not going to be tracked by sleepers for the big, showy acts, but by patterns that form around them: the guy who is way to lucky, the person who seems to always know what people want, the guy who walked away from a car crash for the 4th time in as many years. Their going to note the circles they associate with, the tools and oddities. They will track manifests and disappearances. They can investigate the outcomes of scenes and put together potential magical explanations for the outcomes. While they may be pretty damn certain, they will never be able to have concrete certainty as any obvious magic they witness will be scrubbed from memory, any evidence drying their brain again and again.

                    What this means is that Hunters who are not sleepwalker will have to hunt mages on circumstantial evidence, rather than concrete proof (as such proof causes insanity and memory issues). So that guy you shot from across the street could be a Mage, or just a mortal occultist who happened to get lucky.

                    TL;DNR: Awakened Mages won't be obviously magical, as subtle magic is... subtle, and obvious magic causes memory re-writes and mind rape. It doesn't mean they don't leave mundane evidence and can't be tracked, it just means a Hunter will never be certain except in a scene where they are directly exposed to any obvious magic, and hunting mages without the sleepwalker merit is not good for one's sanity.
