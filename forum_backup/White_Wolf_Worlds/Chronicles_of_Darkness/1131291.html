    The Etymology Thread [Just For Fun]

    08-19-2017, 11:47 AM
    Honestly, I can't say how many times I've looked at a new, cool thing which showed up in the CofD, be it a splat, an otherworld or an antagonist, and I thought for myself whether its name is made in actual language, a made up language, or a made up language inspired by real life language. While some of those words are easy to be found online, there's apparently a limit to Google's ability to find dictionaries in long dead languages, leaving me to forever wonder whether the word actually exist, or if it was made up. Most of the time, it doesn't matter whether the word is real or not, but sometimes it does- especially when you want to focus on a certain time or place in your setting, or make up new antagonists using the same language as canon used. This thread is for anyone who, like me, has words they have always wondered about where they came from and wants to get an answer.

    So, the words I want to ask about are-

    - Neglatu (it sounds like Sumerian, but I'm really not sure)
    -Noctuku (again, same thing as the Neglatu)
    - Acamoth and Gulmoth (is it made up? It sounds made up)

    so, those are mine- can anyone tell me from where those words come from? Anyone else has words they are wondering about?

      A part of me thinks that the Noctuku is a Classic World of Darkness callback to the Nictuku, who were Nosferatu Vampires empowered by the Nosferatu Antediluvian (think Clan progenitor) to wipe out the rest of his clan as an attempt to apologize to Caine and remove his curse (which was supernatural ugliness). The renaming also feels similar to how the Malkovian Bloodline were a callback to the Malkavian Clan in the cWoD (replacing one of the vowels with an o that is).

                                      Originally posted by Michael View Post

                                      White Wolf/Onyx Path have a long history of using words with modified spellings, so I tired messing with alternate spellings. 'Achamoth' is a term from gnosticism, though I'm not terribly clear on what it means.
                                      Achamoth is challenging to explain. I think I have this correct though.

                                      Gnostics generally believe that their true God is Eternity, from which emanate five groups of beings known as Aeons, all of which are in Male/Female pairs. One of the female Aeons is Sophia, "Wisdom." Sophia is sometimes portrayed dualistically, with Achamoth representing "fallen wisdom." Note though that Achamoth wasn't evil, Fallen in this context is closer to the meaning of the Fallen World. Achamoth is fleshly and imperfect, yet still shields Souls from harm and begs the unfallen Sophia to send Christ to Earth to free humanity from Ialdabaōth.

                                      If Acamoth is derived from Achamoth, the relation of the word to the Abyssal Beings is tenouous, but Gnosticism is probably your best bet for new Mage words.
