    Homebrew Legacy: The Kalachakra (feedback wanted)

    08-11-2018, 06:40 PM
    I'm looking for feedback and constructive criticism on this legacy. I'm fleshing out some stories that leverage it for a few NPCs and I'd like to make it available for my players later on. I'm particularly interested in hearing about ways this is going to conflict with game rules and the normal assumptions of the setting that might cause difficult issues during gameplay. I really don't want to get surprised by some thorny conflict with the rest of Mage in the middle of a session; I want this legacy to operate smoothly. Thanks for any help.

    Legacy: The Kalachakra
    This legacy is inspired by The First Fifteen Lives of Harry August. In short, Kalachakra are mages who are reborn as themselves at their original time and circumstances of birth when they die – a Kalachakra lives the same life over and over, retaining his memories across rebirths. If you like the ideas explored here then I highly recommend you read the book!

    This legacy should not be available to players in a typical campaign. Rather, it belongs in a campaign structured around it, where the Storyteller plans to incorporate the rebirth cycle into the story and/or where the Storyteller wants to justify players starting with one or more lifetimes worth of memories and experience as mages. It makes little sense for a character to join the Kalachakra legacy during the course of a chronicle unless the death and rebirth of the Kalachakra will somehow feature in the chronicle – a hairy proposition unless all of the player characters are members of the legacy. Kalachakra can also make interesting adversaries and allies for the players – imagine facing an implacable enemy you’ve never met who holds a grudge for offenses you never committed, or a mysterious friend devoted to your success because you earned his affection in another timeline.

    Roleplay Themes

    If you knew then what you know now, would you have made different choices? If you had your life to live over again, what would you change? Could you resist the temptation to exploit your foreknowledge of future events for personal gain? Could you conceal your nature from others, or would you slip and reveal yourself to those would seek to exploit your gift? Can your mind and integrity endure the endless repetition inherent to your existence, or will it drive you to madness and self-destruction?

    Beyond the Legacy Rules

    The Kalachakra depart from some of the rulebook norms of legacies per the “Strange Attainments” note on pg. 199 of the core rulebook.

    The Kalachakra legacy springs from a mysterious work of Imperial magic (a.k.a. “a wizard did it”). It’s not possible to become a Kalachakra through individual effort: daimonomkon, soul study, or tutelage. Rather, a Kalachakra manifests her legacy spontaneously the moment she obtains Gnosis 3. To non-members, every Kalachakra reads as though he belongs to a unique self-founded legacy.

    In addition to the typical array of legacy bonuses, Kalachakra participate in a special cycle of rebirth. When a Kalachakra dies, from her perspective her consciousness immediately transports back in time into the body of her roughly four-year-old self. She retains all of her mental and magical faculties across rebirths: memories, knowledge, Mental and Social attributes, skills, Arcana ratings, Gnosis, Wisdom, willpower points, aspiration, obsessions, mental conditions, many merits (use your common sense), etc… Most everything not solely related to her physical body (e.g. physical conditions) or life circumstances (e.g. Order status) stay with her. Kalachakra who die Mad are reborn Mad, with all the terrible consequences that implies.

    A Kalachakra can only be destroyed in three ways. The first is if he dies when not in possession of his soul. Additionally, he also faces destruction if the timeline changes so that he’s never born or if he receives a different sympathetic name after birth. In most cases this occurs as a side-effect of other mages meddling in the timeline with Time spells or due to actions taken by Kalachakra born before her that affect the circumstances of her birth.

    Origins

    Parentage: Moros; not associated with any Order

    Nickname: None. Kalachakra are very small in number, very secretive, and refer to one another by the name of the legacy.

    Background: No consistent pattern. Anyone can become a Kalachakra; it’s as random and unpredictable as the Awakening itself.

    Kalachakra have a unique society influenced by their cycle of rebirth and its rules. Kalachakra who coexist in the same lifespan can achieve unparalleled intimacy, evolving from acquaintances to friends to bitter enemies to inseparable lovers and back again across their innumerable rebirths.

    Partially-overlapping lifespans also enable Kalachakra to communicate with one another up and down the time stream. One Kalachakra in childhood can relay a message to another about to die of old age. When that one dies, he’ll himself be reborn as a child earlier in time and can relay the message even further back. One Kalachakra can bury a treasure and send forward the location via the reverse mechanism to an ally far in the future. In this way, Kalachakra separated in time by centuries and millennia can trade favors and information with a latency measured in lifetimes. There may be Kalachakra from the time of Atlantis and the far-future advent of the Hieromage participating in the network.

    The social relationships among Kalachakra bolster the natural instinct for self-preservation to create a strong cultural aversion to radical changes in the timeline. An arrogant Kalachakra who uses her foreknowledge of future events to alter the timeline can destroy innumerable future Kalachakra by preventing the circumstances of their births. Such reckless behavior typically motivates other Kalachakra to seek out her parents and prevent their meeting or even murder her in the womb to restore the consensus normal timeline. Far from puppet-mastering the flow of history, Kalachakra who survive step lightly through their iterations and avoid disturbing the natural progression of important events.

    For obvious reasons, Kalachakra are uniformly paranoid about concealing their ancestry and place and time of birth. One of the only ways a Kalachakra can be destroyed is for an enemy to interfere with her before she receives her sympathetic name, thus causing her to become a different person or not to be born at all.

    Kalachakra are in fact uniformly secretive about their very nature and existence. No Kalachakra wants himself or the others of his legacy to end up enslaved to those would would forcibly extract their knowledge of the future or experiment on them to try and unlock the secrets of immortality. Any but the greenest Kalachakra who reveal themselves or their legacy to non-members get targeted for destruction by their peers to cull such timelines from possibility. As a result the Kalachakra truly are completely unknown to outsiders.

    Suggested Obsessions

    Discovering the nature of Kalachakra: Why did I become a Kalachakra? Why do Kalachakra exist? Who creates Kalachakra, and how? How does the rebirth function?

    Living a “perfect” life: If I have an infinite number of attempts, can I learn from my mistakes and achieve the impossible?

    Investigating a one-in-a-lifetime phenomenon: You know from your previous rebirths who will Awaken and when. Can you observe – or manipulate -- the process to gain special insight into the Awakening?


    Preserving the timeline: Your soulmate for lifetime after lifetime is born fifteen years after you. Can you defend the timeline against reckless mages and rogue Kalachakra who might accidentally or purposefully destroy her? For how many lifetimes can you maintain your defense? Can you even defend yourself against such chaotic forces?

    Doctrine

    Prerequisites: Time 1, Death 1, Eidetic Memory

    Initiation: None. Kalachakra are taken by the legacy rather than the other way around. However, via their first attainment Kalachakra have an uncanny ability to find and identify one another. An experienced Kalachakra can teach a newcomer to the legacy about its yantras, oblations, and culture.

    Magic

    Ruling Arcanum: Time

    Yantras: Ouroboros icons (+1), mandalas depicting imagery relevant to the spell (+1), the seashore exactly at the tide line (+1), the dawn (+1), bird ashes (+1, or +2 if the bird had been fed a portion of its own ashes prior to its death, e.g. from a burned feather)

    Oblations: Meditation immediately before sleep and again immediately after waking, meditation while suffering from at least one lethal or aggravated wound

    Attainments

    First: Mounting the Wheel

    Prerequisites: Membership in the legacy

    Only a Kalachakra can recognize another Kalachakra, and only through the use of this attainment. It provides two effects.

    The first effect uses Time to aid in finding other Kalachakra. All newly-minted members of the legacy intuitively grasp its existence and purpose. The mage may ask regarding any action she plans to take whether it will help or hurt her chances of finding another of her legacy. The answer may be “yes,” “no,” or “irrelevant.” This attainment also functions for goals stated as “Will this help me figure out what the hell I am?” and similar; any lone Kalachakra reborn for the first time will seek the answer to that question as a top priority.

    The second effect requires Death 1 and operates identically to the Death spell Soul Marks, with the additional free benefit of revealing whether the target is a Kalachakra.

    Second: Wheel of Many Spokes

    Prerequisites: Having died at least once

    A Kalachakra has many equally true pasts and futures – a violation of a fundamental assumption of most Time spells. This can result in confusing and contradictory results for those mages who try to inspect her timeline.

    When another mage uses Time to interact with the Kalachakra’s past (e.g. via Postcognition or Temporal Summoning) the Kalachakra’s player may select which of her past iterations manifests for the other mage. For example, if the Kalachakra has lived through 4:15pm on September 8, 2004 seven times and another mage uses Postcognition to scry at her on that date and time, she may pick any of those seven histories to reveal. If the player needs to make up those events on the fly then the Storyteller’s discretion determines what constitutes a reasonable history.

    With the addition of Death 2, the player may also select any of her character’s histories in which she had died prior to the targeted date and time. In that case, the other mage encounters her physical remains from that iteration and will likely be quite confused at finding someone who seems to have returned from the dead.

    These effects do not occur due to intentional effort from the targeted Kalachakra; they’re natural consequences of her nature and the legacy’s influence on her soul. They do not themselves provoke a Clash of Wills because they don’t actually interfere with the other mage’s intended spell – it’s simply unnatural to formulate an imago that differentiates among a Kalachakra’s iterations. They all happened; they’re all equally true. A mage who has somehow managed to study Kalachakra (or is one himself) may be able to devise a way to overcome this natural defense and work that into the imago as a reach effect.

    Third: The Unbreakable Circle

    Prerequisites: Time 3, Resolve 3

    Reliving through the same span of time bolsters the Kalachakra’s defenses against magic that would interfere with her cycle. This attainment operates as per Constant Presence with Advanced Duration.

    With the addition of Death 3, she also gains the benefit of Soul Armor with Advanced Duration. This helps her prevent destruction through dying when not in possession of her soul.

    Progressing this far in the legacy also provides some automatic reflexive defense against other Kalachakra who would seek to learn her origins. Another Kalachakra who seeks to use Time magic to scry on her at age five or younger must succeed in a Clash of Wills. The defending mage gets the bonus for Advanced Duration on her roll. From a cultural standpoint, even attempting such an act of scrying is highly taboo and certain to make enemies if discovered, unless the targeted Kalachakra is herself a pariah.

    Fourth: Pausing the Wheel

    Prerequisites: Time 4, Composure 3

    The Kalachakra may briefly pause the flow of time to draw strength from reflection on his state of immortality. Once per day, he may halt time to meditate. While frozen in time, he may neither move nor take other actions that affect the world or himself aside from meditation. Any conditions or tilts with a time component pause. The mage may only meditate toward one of three ends:
    1. Regain mana equal to his Time rating, limited by his Gnosis-derived mana per turn limit
    2. Regain willpower as if he had satisfied his Virtue
    3. Gain a circumstance bonus for his next skill roll equal to his Time rating

    With Death 4, the mage may optionally harvest additional willpower or mana equal to his Death rating from the patterns of his other iterations. His Gnosis-derived mana limit per turn does not apply when harvesting mana using Death. This power comes with a drawback, however. Any time the Kalachakra first obtains a lethal or aggravated wound, roll dice equal to his Death rating. The player loses any available points of mana and/or willpower as chosen by the player equal to the number of successes rolled -- one of this other iterations has stolen them away using this attainment. This drawback never occurs if the player never opts to leverage this optional effect.

    Fifth: Familiar Revolutions

    Prerequisites: Time 5, Manipulation 3

    Lifetimes of experience absorbed with a perfect memory can leave the Kalachakra in an almost permanent state of deja vu. It’s entirely possible that the Kalachakra has played out any given social interaction previously in other iterations of her life, and will play them out again in future iterations. Through this attainment, a Kalachakra can harmonize her experiences across her various lifetimes to perfectly navigate social challenges. She's seen it all, done it all, and knows exactly what to say and do to get her way with impossible expediency.

    In any social interaction, the player automatically reduces the subject’s doors by her Time rating.

    With Death 5, she may immediately conclude the waiting interval between attempts to open the subject’s doors. Alternatively, if she herself is the target of the social challenge, she may instead immediately terminate any attempt to open her doors, allowing for no possibility of success and forcing the other party to wait the required interval before making another attempt.

          What about phoenix imagery as yantras ? Seems in line with the theme of rebirth.

          One thing I worry about is how archmastery would affects this Legacy. When you casts any Imperial spell (aka 6+ dot spells), it causes an effect called aponeia, which are changes to reality, they could be anything, including who raised them, the relationship to their loved ones, even their sympathetic name. For example, its common that the result of their first spell (becoming an archmage) causes The Scream as they realized the price paid to become a post human gnostic monster. The transformation also comes with eternal youth, so by default, they could possibly die and be stuck as a 4 year old for good. In addition, archmages are immune to having their souls stolen, so the Soul Armor attainment would instantly lose its value.

          There are also many ways to bypass their defenses. For example, Mind magic dodges the Time shielding. And Prime can detect the truth when they conceal their identity, or give 8 or 9 again to spells scrutinizing your past, they can also use a simple Knowing spell to reveal the 16 year old who is a third degree master along with every other relevant bit of arcane knowledge, including their Gnosis rating. Fate can be used to all but guarantee exceptional successes, bypassing the Withstand of the shields or even refunds for all the mana weaker mages would use to prevent Paradox by over reaching. There is also the challenge of their Nimbus growing stronger as their Gnosis increases, being easier to detect and leaving an imprint for longer. I think adding some of the more stealthy spells from Death could possibly help mitigate this somehow, such as Suppress Aura, Without a Trace and Empty Presence. Ultimately, if a mage cabal wants to know, there is very little that can stop them, being able to not draw their attention in the first place or redirect it somewhere else would probably help keep their society a secret for longer.

          I also wonder what their relationship is to other immortals, like the Reborn: http://whitewolf.wikia.com/wiki/Immortal_(CofD)

          Its a very cool Legacy, just reading the write up gives lots of ideas on interesting plot hooks, what if scenarios, intrigue and Mysteries to throw at the party.
