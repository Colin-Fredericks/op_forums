    Originally posted by Father Enoch View Post
    I'm not even sure why Vampires and Sin Eaters would fight.

    Vampires and Werewolves both want the territory to hunt, even if for different prey. Vampires and Changelings both feed off people, even if in different ways. Magi want people to Awaken, which they can't do if they get turned into Vampires (they also really dig uncovering mysteries and Vampires like having their mystery, thank you, Gandalf). I thought Gheist was mostly about helping Wraiths move on.

    I can imagine a Vampire being responsible for the creation of one or two of the ghosts that seek out Gheists, but not all of them.
    Vampires and Sin-Eaters are most likely to conflict over persons of interest and locales of power. Sin-Eaters, Geists, and ghosts all have people who they rely on for resource reasons, nevermind the deeper and more cathartic investments, and when and where that crosses over with vampiric Touchstones and nodes of power/access, it can get messy. While haunted locations aren't of immediate interest to all vampires for their necrocosmic principles, a haunted place is a hell of a Haven, and whether yer cutting it by Clan or by Covenant, about 3 out of 5 of either of those have more personal reasons to have stake in such.

    Oh, and the obvious-vampires contribute to the dead frequently enough that it warrants some haunting, and that return of grievance can, in sufficient enough numbers, can incentivize a vampiric community to start favoring their mutations that lean into their role as...well, the dead, ie lean into those branches of the Kindred curses that tap into the necrocosmic.

    EDIT: Oh yeah, and also, the mundane resources that keep the lights going, the cults fed, and the cops out of your business are the sort of materials every supernatural fights over. Only so many people the deli owner or the senator can be in the pocket of.

    EDIT: Also, it's not hard to see how some Krewes might react poorly to vampires-Furies who deal with systemic injustices tend to have opinions on monsters who kind of often thrive off of systemic corruption, as a minor example. Politics and theology are alive and well, after all.

        Originally posted by Father Enoch View Post
        I thought Gheist was mostly about helping Wraiths move on.

        I can imagine a Vampire being responsible for the creation of one or two of the ghosts that seek out Gheists, but not all of them.
        The default game Geist is written for, as of 2e, is that you have your own Burden that drove you to accept the Bargain and return to the land of the living, your geist's Remembrance offering them the opportunity for catharsis so they can move on, and your krewe's fledgeling inquiries into the nature of life and death (and magic) as they work against the Underworld's system of suffering.

        The game's core conflict of Self vs Service is such that you can probably engineer some beef between a krewe trying to shift the meaning of death and a domain with a particularly high body count and degrading body disposal methods, but there's krewes of Bound (antagonists, in the base game) who've twisted their Bargains in their own favor and broken the will of their geists such that they'd get along just fine with the aforementioned Bastard Vampires and might instead get in trouble with, say, a ghost-savvy bloodline of Nosferatu. "Ghosts are people, even if they're not actually the people they're made from" is one of the theses of Geist, and a great many ghosts are badly damaged people just by virtue of their creation involving death or at least life-altering trauma.

        None of which really compares, as you note, to "these people can be reasonably likened to dangerous magical animals who don't like the prospect of having to share hunting grounds even before any ideological conflict they have with each other."

                            Originally posted by Aurumae View Post

                            I said there was a lot of chicanery.

                            If you dig into the Werewolf merits you'll quickly find that the PU limits on attributes are at best guidelines. In this case to get 24 dice on the roll we have Stamina + Survival + Purity. Our stamina is 5 + 1 from favored form, +1 using totemic empowerment and +1 from either embodiment of the firstborn or the totem advantage. We'll activate the gift in either Urshul or Gauru for +2 stamina, bringing our stamina total to 10. We then add to that 5 Survival + 1 from totem advantages (so 6) plus 5 Purity plus willpower (3) gives us 24 dice total.

                            Through similar chicanery our wolf has 6 brawl (plus area of expertise) and 7 strength going up to 8 in their favoured form (probably Gauru) before form bonuses. This all stacks along with the relevant Gifts to give us 24 dice as a base attack pool with however many successes * 2 extra dice from Crimson Spasm (I went with 7 as I said giving us 38).



                            There are a number of assumptions here I feel are unwarranted. For Force of Nature you've clearly assumed the Vampire has 5 Stamina and 5 Dex, I didn't assume this because it costs too much exp and the Vampire was already much more expensive than the Werewolf. If you want to spend another 12 exp getting the Vampire those attribute dots it puts him at 40 exp more expensive than the Werewolf and you have to reasonably start asking what the Werewolf is spending all that exp on (Tongue of Flame is always fun). And if you're going to be using force of nature, that that's 8 vitae and an instant action. Your BP 5 vampire only has 15 vitae and can only spend 5 per turn. In other words, that's 2 full turns before you can even start using the devotion, and then you need to spend another 2 instant actions to shift the dots around, and after all that you will only have 1 turn of full physical intensity blood boosting (assuming you spend blood on nothing else). I think it's fair to say that as your Vampire spends 4 turns &quot;powering up&quot; you're going to be absolutely eviscerated by the Werewolf.

                            Secondly, you're assuming a frenzy here. This I think is unwarranted. Vampires can't just choose to Frenzy (unlike Werewolves and Death Rage), and when they do Frenzy they can't choose what the Beast's goal is (and &quot;get the fuck away from the big Werewolf&quot; is more likely than &quot;attack suicidally&quot. Even if we assume the Vampire is riding the wave, this can take several turns to activate and there's no guarantee that the check succeeds.



                            I ignored Swarm form since it doesn't directly boost the Vampire's dice pools and that was what I was most interested in. If we're going down that route though, Tongue of Flame is a hard counter to Swarm form. Also if you think about it Grasp of Howling winds should be devastating to a Vampire in Swarm form but obviously that's getting into the realm of ST fiat.



                            I think it's pretty clear that you can either have claws of the unholy OR swarm form but not both at the same time. Claws of the Unholy also isn't free, it requires frenzy, which leads to the issues described above. Furthermore, if you take Claws of the Unholy, that means you are not taking advantage of Protean 4's Barbed Hands meaning you lose the + Protean to grappling pools (meaning you lose grapples against the Werewolf and it can auto grapple you on a normal attack roll whenever it wishes). Lastly you're doing + 0 agg damage with claws of the unholy. Once you account for defense etc (be prepared for the Werewolf to have at least 12 defense) and things like Berserker's Might, you're going to be dealing very little if any damage
                            About attributes assumptions, I assumed that also werewolf attributes were all max.

                            About frenzy, frenzy is triggered by almost everything, also just simple verbal offenses, an angry werewolf is for sure enough of a threat to trigger frenzy. Tricky is to resist frenzy not let yourself fall to it.

                            Nothing is preventing you to deal aggravated damages in swarm form: all protean transformations are cumulative, bats with claws (unholy claws) are still legit. Then you can houserule they are not because you think it's too OP, but don't call it as an hard rule.

                            Defense 12 is not much of a deal when you roll 40 dice and have 5 automatic damages with Vigor.
                            Talking about defense, and using all the book possibilities, there's also coil of the wyrm which add Blood Potency to health and defense.

                            I think here we are getting back to a fandom debate between vampires vs werewolf to which I would rather abstain.
