            The Devil may quote Scripture and the gods play dice with the lives of men. A hunter who treats supernatural actors in general as a talkative set of iconography with widely varying degrees of not-having-her-best-interests-at-heart is not going to be especially shaken by one of them committing deeply to the supposed pretense of virtue unless she actually has doubts about whether driving the people around her to wrack and ruination like the monsters do is really all that bad.

            The problems a zealot runs into are the same as the fella who pals around with the monsters, but in reverse: The supernatural does not exist in a vacuum, and sooner or later you're going to run into human friends and supporters who're going to defend their werewolf protector in a way that you can't solve without some strain of breaking the Code. As with Mage, eventually you're going to hit a threshold where cascade failure is a real and present danger in your lifestyle, and by that point you either set up the necessary precautions or go down screaming.

                    Wow, this debate has almost singlehanded given me so much interest in Hunter. While this is a meta/okc discussion, I'd love to see it in game. Take Ashwood Abbey. If the Hunter Code really does just, theoretically, come down to Human vs Monster... I could see a lot of debates and gray areas. The Abbey follows the Code, clearly. But are they letting go of their humanity instead, relying on the Code like an addiction? Would your Hunter be okay with them? Would they stop an ashwood, even if it meant questioning whether you are better, or the same, or worse? Would you kill a Hunter who obeys the Code code but not their integrity? Would you kill a Hunter who obeys their humanity but not the code?

                    Reversed, the Lucifuge shirks the code in favor of integrity. Despite sympathizing with monsters and risking the Code and their understanding of the Most Dangerous Game, the biggest difference between a Lucifuge and the Children of the Devil is that the Lucifuge holds into their humanitu, code be Damned, while a true son of Satan cares nothing of either but could take it either way.

                    LIKE RISKING WILLPOWER. It looks a lot like deliberately risking Integrity is now a part of Hunter, and the Y splats are differentiated by what parts of innate human integrity vs the Code they follow. Would you murder a human who was Ashwood? Would you forgive them because they still hunt monsters? Are either of you the same anymore? Are both of you monsters, holding up the Vigil' s candle from outside?

                    I would agree that I think that there should be more than one "breaking point" that you can change, I'd also suggest you should be able to change innate ones too. Id lovd it if it was unlimited, but escalating, exponential. Change one and you're iffy. Change two and you're weird. Change three, good luck convincing hunters you are human. Change five and prepare to be hunted like Dracula.
                    And every kind of hunter blurs the line between you and the monsters, and every situation is still a risk and reward question. If my Hunter feels confident in killing an asswood, it's a risk not because the line is clearly defined between man and monster, but because huntehunters justify their lives on black and white when the world is gray. I would expect an asswood would roll for Integrity as often as a Lucifuge, just for wholly different reasons (remdmber, the human side of Hunter integrity is still a little human, so "My first time getting high on V" would still be a breaking point for humans, even if not for the Code.)

                    And sadly, the extreme sides of being a Hunter rather than a well adjusted mortal do make sense. MR. killer could maybe stay a Hunter, and sane, because he compartmentalizes things. That won't stop other hunters from risking their own beliefs and sanity to take him down (see Hermits and Waywards of owod hunter). On top of the Code, there are very human breaking points I truly believe we rent addressed in the pkaytest because that side has been playtested in every Cofd game. I still do see how Ms. Compassion could go insane with her risks to the code, because she a) bulwarks with human touchstones, b) if she isn't just playing around, she will invest in willpower attributes, c) have good modifiers via virtue and ST judgement for old childhood friendships or proof of the monster doing effort to being good. But it's possible a human-serial-killer sympathizer who burns Eleven and young Louis the Vampire stays sane and Ms Compassion could go slasher, it's possible the other way is true because the Code isn't as ingrained as actual human integrity. While all Hunters use the Code to justify most of what they do, I truly believe beyond the playtest, there will be MORE human breaking points. And thus extremists towards the Code or Humanity will betray the other side of themselves, and more mentally balanced hunters will look at both askance.

                    As much as the CofD has moved away from the OWoD, I think Compassion, Zeal, and Vision can still exist. They all risk their Integrity, though, just like they risk willpower. You put Faith in what you believe is right, and you roll the dice. Whether you roll it because you're obeying the code and denying what sounds morally right and human, or whether you're rolling the dice because you were obeyying your humanity and ignoring the code... It doesn't matter in the end. The sad fact is... Hunters are in fact a monster Splat. They don't see themselves that way, but unfortunately even compassionate Hunters can become slashers. Just like zealous Hunters can keep themselves from degrading into real monsters. Part of it is creating a carefully and demanding mindset, and part of it is literally risking your sanity every time you go out and do your job.

                                        So, 1st Edition Code versus 2nd Edition Code.

                                        Perfectly honest, I hated The Code in First Edition back when I first read it and ran my game (Which, as an ironic twist for this conversation, ended up with the group being more like a team of negotiators between monstrous factions). I felt like it was an attempt to paper up the conflict with Morality, which at the time was a major thing for me-that the game had consequences for not being a good person was hella awesome for me, and the Code kind of took away that struggle. I still kind of feel that way about it now, even though I have a better context on what Morality was trying to be and the various ways it was broken as well as less of an inclination towards ease as concerns morals and ethics. And honestly, I wouldn't use it now. I would rather just let people deal with their morality.

                                        Second Edition Code though? It cements a lot of what's going on with Hunter and it's conflicts, particularly as it now incarnates in Second Edition. Since Integrity represents how well you have held up to psychological and emotional stresses, it suddenly makes a lot more sense for there to be some kind of 'papering up' mechanic, a way of numbing yourself to the horrors you subject yourself. I like that it does create a hard line between monsters and humans and places hunters firmly in some shade of human supremacist, and I do like that that complicates both times where they are gainly employing the supernatural or having some kind of work relationship with monsters and where the groups are softer and less typical hunters, because I think that creates a thematically more consistent game alongside some really hard hitting questions and dynamics to play with, as it forces confrontation with some of our easiest dark instincts in othering people and justifying our actions, particularly given that, yeah, sometimes there are plenty of surrounding reasons that are justified. That alongside the Conditions you pick up with sliding Integrity make the stresses of the game that much more potent, much more character centered, and dynamic in question. I like that it implies that there is always some blood on your hands, even with your best intentions, and the ways you own or discard that creates intensely personal stories.

                                        So, yeah. I, uh. I'm strongly in opposition to the crowd who has a problem. My Hunters are monsters now, and that only makes them fuller and more deeply human to me, and it makes any effort I spend trying to be a good guy in this games way more interesting and fulfilling.

                                                        What definition of oppression are you using?

                                                        I'm using "the exercise of authority or power in a burdensome, cruel, or unjust manner." which covers any case where a monster uses their supernatural power to prey or feed upon humanity. So any Hunter who's Vigil is about protecting people from monstrous predation is fighting oppression.

                                                        Hunter isn't only a game about fighting oppression, but it certainly includes fighting oppression as a major element.

                                                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                                        But the game is about hunting monsters, and that means you characters are always, on some level, discriminatory. It might be more like "I'm not racist towards black people, but-" style discrimination instead of Ku Klux Klan style discrimination,
                                                        Baring edge cases like VASCU or a Cell of police officers who treat mortal and supernatural criminals roughly the same, I concede the point. Even when a Cell makes a point of only Hunting monsters when they have proof of wrongdoing, they're still treating monsters differently from the equally dangerous humans who they leave to the police.

                                                        However that doesn't mean there isn't massive room for improvement. Here's the key phrase from the Call a Spade a Spade sidebar: "He might want to view the cooperative vampire as a person". Once you start thinking of them as not even people you've gone way beyond "I'm not racist but", you've gone way beyond Jim Crow and Separate But Equal. You're somewhere on the level of Nazi death camps, which should be presented as the tail end of hunter discrimination rather than the base case.

                                                        Basically. Some amount of discrimination is inevitable but there's still room for improvement. The presentation could certainly be dialed back significantly. For example, rather than saying killing Monsters never causes a breaking point you could say that killing an innocent monster is a breaking point but a guilty one is not and contrast that to humans where killing is always a breaking point for a Hunter who isn't rapidly approaching Slasherdom. You could replace the Spade a Spade sidebar with a new sidebar that says the Code as presented is optimized for certain gameplay considerations and is not supposed to imply that a Hunter would automatically be ok with killing people who haven't hurt anyone just because they have supernatural power.
