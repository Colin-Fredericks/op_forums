    Am I wrong in reading the rules of Supernal summoning as meaning you actually have very little input on what kind of being you get at all? Like, I presume that if its second primary Arcanum can be up to the Storyteller barring spending Mana and having a 3+ Arcanum to specify, then everything else about it has the same discretion rather than beings of the same archetype having different Arcana stats.

    In which case, I'd think the simplest explanation for being unable to summon Paragons while not in their Emanation is because you don't have that level of control over the summoning for anything, and Paragons would be so down low on the list of things that might be fitting to answer the summons.

    That and how I generally interpret Supernal inhabitants as not corresponding to geography but conceptual resonance. You can call upon Metatron because Metatron exists adjacent to everywhere that certain kinds of punitive justice exist simultaneously, whereas a given Paragon has a more fixed point in space that is literally far away from you.

    Unless you're within the same Emanation, and even then I wouldn't see the summoning as something you can specify down to any type or individual. It's just putting out a signal into the realm that keys off of a Paragon's Supernal quality to obligate them to turn up, pose a Trial, and be at the service of the mage who passes it. It just does so with whatever Paragon is most fitting.

    (Likewise, you cannot summon Supernal Ochemata because you cannot specify for them and they are definitely bound to an authority that does not make them available to answer the beck and call of the Phenomenal.)

    I have approximate knowledge of many things.
    Write up as I play Xenoblade Chronicles.

                                                        Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                                                        Whats the point of artefacts?
                                                        Mysteries to study, spells you can cast without needing to know the Arcana, potentially higher Gnosis with which to cast the spells (and thus faster ritual casting times along with the extra dice), distinctive Path Tools which might be semiotically suitable for things the normal categories or your Dedicated Tool can't manage, extra Mana storage, and any narrative effects that rely on that particular Artifact.

                                                        what advantage do they give to a person that already can cast those spells?
                                                        Most of the above plus leverage if someone else wants to be able to use the spell but you don't want to teach it to them.
