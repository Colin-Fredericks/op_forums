                                    "Mortal magic" in CoD is mostly undefined. I think Hurt Locker has the most on the actual subject, and that is more incidental than anything else. Unless we count Endowments in Hunter the Vigil.

                                    That said, there is a lot of other kinds of magic that can and do hook into and build upon said magic. (Mummy) Ritual Sorcery and Necromancer Ceromonies are the most obvious, but they are very different in metaphysical terms. They also tend to fall into 'draw power from elsewhere to empower what was already there". (Duat for Sorcery and the Underworld for Ceromonies).
                                    If pressed, I would consider Ceromonies to be closer to mortal magic, as the Underworld could be argued to be part of the Phenomenonal/Fallen World the same way the Shadow/Hisil is. While neither require membership (unlike Werewolf's Rites), they are still not connected directly to the 'mortal' world of CoD. This applies to Shadow Occultists (see NH:Shunned by the Moon) and Alchemists (Promethean 2e; subset called Insatiable) fall into similar categories while also being prone to mental instability, if not psychopathy.

                                    Fun Fact: even Open Rites from Werewolf require you to have a totem spirit; the Pack even has a merit that allows fully human 'packs' to to take a totem and use said Rites as it's main benefit.

                                    Most magic in the setting that mortals can use is usually filtered through another entity. Heck, the Changeling splat itself is almost exclusively using such filtered magic (Contracts). Overusing such magic is called out as a potential Divergence in Deviant.
                                    Come to think of it, magic in CoD is very transformative. Drink vamp blood? No longer purely mortal. Touch a strange gear sticking out of the Church basement? Now you see mechanisms of False Heaven. See a wolf-monster? Congrats, the wolf is now in your blood. Stay too long in the world hidden in your closet? Congrats on being the Queen of the Goblins there, (ok this one is properly your fault).

                                    Ritual Sorcey and Necromantic Ceremonies are among the few systems of magic that are accessible to mortals and don't inherently change them into something else...well aside from Sleepwalkers. But pretty much everything turns a baseline into a Sleepwalker. Benedictions turn a human into a Sleepwalker.

                                    Which brings me back to Hunter. Hunter is Blue Book+ in CoD. Normal humans encountering the supernatural and stepping up in how to deal with it. H:tV just assumes you've done that first Story at character creation, whereas the Chronicles book is essentially built for preludes. As you go up in tiers in Hunter, you tie yourself more closely to certain aspects of the setting that aren't present/noticeable from a Blue Book perspective, (as community is a big theme in Hunter). At tier 3 you can get an Endowment from your Conspiracy. 99% of Endownments are magic. All Endowments are intended for use by human mortals, and I would look at any requests for exceptions very closely.
                                    If need be, Endowments are my first choice for canonical system of magic for mundanes.
                                    That said, 1st edition's Second Sight was exactly the kind of thing that is perfect for this. An unoffical update to this book exists on the Vault called Second Sight: Third Eye.

                                    On the subject of alchemy, there are at least 4 kinds listed in CoD and all of them can (technically) be used by baseline mortal humans.
                                    • Promethean style Alchemy involves Pyros to mimic the Transmutations of the gameline's protagonists. Mostly unclear on how it works, the sample antagonist does not follow the suggested rules and most depictions in the book are called out as crazy fanatics willing to do terrible things. Justified by selected bias; sane alchemists would not hunt Prometheans and generally be Antagonists to them. (At least, no more likely than anyone else and may in fact, be able to recognize Disquiet, maybe even have a plan to deal with it.) Not recommended for players.
                                    • The Ascending Ones Conspiracy in Hunter the Vigil practice a kind of alchemy. Works fine for PCs.
                                    • The Mesen-Nebu Guild of Irem's Arisen practiced a kind of Alchemy. While it has largely been unsupported mechanically, 1st edition implied it was in fact, a subset or style of Ritual Sorcery, much like creating a Vestiges is in 2e. This would produce Relics if done on the same scale. Heck, we have an outline for pyropus (M:tC pg 224) if we were to create a ritual for it. However this is largely not explored in the texts, so for now it is not recommended for players.
                                    • 'Supernal' Alchemy (Signs of Sorcery) does not technically require Awakened magic. All you need to do is take the (mostly) pure material over near-dimensional boundaries every few days over a relatively short period (around a month). I'd argue that Uratha and Sin-Eaters should have more access to this stuff than Mages, but since anyone can open a gate to the Underworld with its corresponding key, this is open mundanes as well. Perfectly fine for players.
                                    Have fun!
