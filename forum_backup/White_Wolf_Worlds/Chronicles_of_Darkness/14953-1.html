    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    Right; and back when all ephemera were of the same type, that was a somewhat decent justification for them — although the spirit-orientation still felt odd. It worked back then (to the extent that it did) because all ephemera were made of the same stuff, and because spirits were pretty much anything ephemeral that wasn’t a ghost — excepting the qashmallim. In many ways, Pyros does a better job of representing the Fifth Element of Æther as it was conceived by alchemists than ectoplasm does.
    As far back as Werewolf Core one needed different equipment to interact with ghosts than with spirits, a concept that carried over in Mage Core and Changeling Core both, with Promethean being the sole hiccup along the way that nevertheless didn't posit much in terms of interaction between ghosts and spirits as a whole.

    What I'm saying here is that "all ephemera were the same type" was never actually true. Ectoplasm is not ephemera of any type, either, being instead a byproduct of interaction between the mundane world and certain manifestations of ephemera.

    Spirits don’t really strike me as being in an “Above/Below” relationship with the material world without squinting and looking at it sideways — in other words, shoehorning them into the role for lack of anything better suited.

    They’re not a bad fit; and they’re a marginally better fit than ghosts; but only marginally so, and they don’t even have that much of an edge compared to, say, angels or astral entities. I don’t see why they should be singled out as the otherworldly entities that Quintessence-based Prometheans should be attuned to.
    They're the ephemeral being that has the most concrete ties to "big picture" cosmology while still being closely tied to the mundane world, up to and including the astrological planets.

    Ghosts are tied up with purely human concerns or locked up in the Underworld, Astral entities can't manifest, the amkhata aren't much more involved than ghosts are on account of their being essentially Pandorans with more inanimate targets, chthonians are among the most alien beings in the World of Darkness, and angels and the qashmallim run up and down the ladder of cosmological scale but nevertheless are more concerned with a divine or pseudodivine Plan than being understandable pre-hindsight.

    Spirits, at least, are concerned with the living world, exist in a context of holding up a cosmological mirror to conventional reality, and can offer a greater variety of agendas — even outside the Shadow — that can prompt an Ulgan to do Weird Things to deal with them that underline the challenges the Riven face in their Pilgrimages.

    Remember, I’m not proposing changing Ulgans; they’re fine as is. I’m proposing adding new Lineages that are similar to Ulgans, but with different foci.
    It would rather by necessity have to involve changing Ulgans, since otherwise the Riven encompass most other ephemeral Lineage roles in practical terms.

    And the only disagreement we have here is that you’re saying that this ought to be a singular Lineage, whereas I see no reason why it shouldn’t be a plurality.
    If you want to read "place this two-floor-plus-basement family home that's been furnished with conversation pieces and a number of interesting pets in the same category as a couple of small apartments, each with a box of items labeled with the name of a different type of ephemeral being" as "making a singular into part of a plural," that is certainly your prerogative, but it is not my argument.

    If you have an issue with the HazMat Lineages, please take it up in the topic devoted to discussing them; this topic isn’t about them*.
    My issue with them is the same as my issue with sectioning off the Ulgan into a box of Ephemeral Lineages; confusing my drawing a parallel in explaining why I don't like either with a desire to critique a thing which I have not bothered with in the years since its initial presentation is not helpful to discussion of either.

    Getting back to the main point, I don’t see what’s so arbitrary about “different kinds of ephemera”, or how introducing Lineages inspired by ghosts, angels, and other ephemeral beings sequesters the Ulgans. To me, that sounds like you’re saying that anything that intrudes on the Ulgans’ current monopoly on dealing with the Other damages the Ulgans.
    Hoo, boy.

    There is ostensibly a place for me to talk about the similarities between the Shadow and the Hedge or Tartarus and Arcadia, or how the storytelling advice for Underworld ghosts aligns with the symptoms of talecrafting addiction and virtually identical language is used in the Book of Spirits and Geist Core in describing their respective ephemeral beings' being highly desirous creatures, or how the idigam's species-wide singularity aligns in description with the wording used to describe the uniqueness of the Extempore, or the way that the interval for a body's viable freshness for both the generative act and the post-mortem Embrace is the same, or any number of other things in the giant pile of strings and clippings I've accumulated over the years.

    This place is not that place, though, so I'll just keep it brief and point out that when a Lineage is aspected toward "the visible light spectrum," it looks a bit goofy to push for Lineages aspected toward "warm colors" and "cool colors" with no especial concern for the deeper roots of those elements and what they bring to the table beyond "they are more specific bands of color." The cosmology is not so neatly divided as all that, and motions to the effect that it ought to be consequently tend to ring shallow to me.

        How do I put this?

        Before GMC, ghosts and spirits were the “same type of being” in that they were made of the same ephemeral stuff, and thus could see and touch each other while in Twilight. I never claimed that they were interchangeable with each other, and have myself pointed out how the ghost/spirit divide is as old as ghosts and spirits coexisting in the setting (though I usually point to Second Sight for supporting evidence).

        Yes, the Ulgans are far more developed than the other “Ephemeral Lineages”. This is to be expected, because Ulgans have been thoroughly developed by professional game designers whereas the other Ephemeral Lineages are being developed by amateur fans. Sometimes amateurs produce professional-quality work; but that’s the exception rather than the rule. And it’s certainly not to be expected when the development is in the brainstorming stage.

        As for the light spectrum analogy: to me, it isn’t about “visible light” Ulgans vs. “warm colors” and “cool colors”; it’s about visible light vs. infrared, ultraviolet, and x-rays: there’s a bit of overlap on the fringes, but that’s about it. Everything about how the Ulgans are written focuses primarily on spirits — the denizens of the Shadow who were first introduced in Werewolf. Could the Ulgans be rewritten to have a broader scope and to be the one and only Lineage that deals with every kind of ephemeral being equally (in the analogy, could they be broadened from visible light to encompass the entire electromagnetic spectrum)? Sure; but that would be a rewrite, and one that would largely water them down by trying to make them cover too many distinct things that only have an ephemeral state of being in common: ghosts, spirits, angels, and demons share very little in common with each other thematically, something that you pointed out at the start of your post.

        If you don’t like the direction I’m discussing here, so be it — reasonable people can disagree. But what I’m hearing from you isn’t that it isn’t your cup of tea; what I’m hearing is a declaration that no reasonable person could like the idea. If I read too much into your claims, I apologize; but frankly, I’m getting a distinct sense of being attacked for presuming to even suggest such a thing. In short, there’s no constructive criticism; just “abandon all hope, ye who enter here”.
