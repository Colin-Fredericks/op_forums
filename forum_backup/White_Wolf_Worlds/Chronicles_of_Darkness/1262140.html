    Pure in 2E?

    10-22-2018, 10:43 AM
    So I came into Werewolf: the Forsaken back when the first edition was fresh from the printers (just as a side anecdote: I got the book from the gaming store and started reading it on the train back home that evening, was very cool to read a horror story starting in a train car when I was in one of those), but I haven't fully consumed every supplement and sourcebook that came out.

    One of the things I didn't read deeply into was the Pure sourcebook – mostly because I found it choked up with long-winded, vague, kind of pointless walls of repetitive yet totally uninforming text and when I (somehow) got to the part where the writers casually went "so they rape…" I just went "no, thank you" and closed the book. Even though I did later see the next paragraph essentially saying "#NotAllPure" It's been a handy paper-presser ever since.

    Point is, all I know about the Pure nowadays essentially comes from their description in the "Prey" Chapter of the new (and wonderful, I should say) edition's rulebook.
    Since a lot of fluff (we should really say "fur") has changed between the two editions, I find that I don't actually get the Pure.
    I have a vague understanding of what they're about – rejection of Luna, opposite attitude towards the Shadow, scarification is cool, silver is the worst and anyone who uses it for any reason is a #$^&wit etc.
    But I don't actually get them.

    The things that I'm unclear about are:

    What They Actually Want – the book mentions that "some [of them]" want a return to the hunter's paradise of primordial times, others see the Shadow as their "true heritage". That's a little vague, though – like, isn't the "hunter's paradise" something that the Hunters in Darkness (and many other Forsaken) want as well, in one way or another? How's that different?
    And what does the Shadow being their "true heritage" mean, exactly? Do they want to rule it? Join it and become fully spirit? What?
    Ivory Claws are very unclear to me as well. So they're kind of like the Werewolf version of a Ventrue/Tzimisce mix with the old Silver Fangs concept kind of?

    What Their Relationship with the Shadow Is Like – so does the spirit realm as a whole distinguish between werewolves? If a spirit sees a Pure werewolf does it recognize the werewolf as an ally or something? Do Pure werewolves hang out in the Hisil, having some BBQ with eldritch monsters like an office outing? Do the Pure just consider the Shadow to be off-limits and stay out of its business? Do they not ever hunt spirits, claimed, urged, hosts and whatever else comes out of there? Does the Shadow and its denizens affect Pure and their Wolf-Blooded differently than they do the Forsaken and their Wolf-Blooded?

    What Do the Pure Actually Do in the World? – so what do they actually do? Do they have an active agenda they all work towards? Are they kind of like the Werewolf version of the old Sabbat? Its mentioned that their relationship with their Tribes is deeper than that of Forsaken – does that mean that all Fire-Touched are preacher priests of the Shadow, for instance? Seems very narrow for what is supposed to be the largest werewolf Tribes.
    Do they live differently in the world than Forsaken do? Like, I get that most Predator Kings would kind of like be the old Red Talons and live as wolves – what about the other Pure?
    Do they hunt humans? What do they do?

    And please excuse my ignorance.
    Will there be a 2E guide book for the Pure as well?

    Thanks in advance

                  My understanding of the Pure is that they are culturally stuck in the past. The Forsaken deal with the world as it is now, accepting the burden of atonement for slaying their progenitor and taking up the mantle of the Great Predator and its duties in keeping balance between the two worlds; the Pure, on the other hand, act out of nostalgia. They hate the Forsaken for killing Wolf, and think the world is worse without him. Because of this, they're, essentially, werewolf supremacists; they don't care about the balance between the two worlds, and whereas the Urdaga as a generalization try to remember their mortal ties, the Anshega (again as a generalization) see humanity as just another thing to hunt (and although the Iron Masters do hold humans as their sacred prey, their conception of hunting mortals is more along the line of "addressing harmful or problematic elements within my territory," whereas the Pure might call Siskur-Dah on humans because they have something the Pure want, or because the pack are hungry, etc).

                  The Pure can hunt pretty much anything, depending on the circumstances; the Fire-Touched might call a Sacred Hunt on the Azlu if the Spider Hosts are preventing favored spirits from crossing over. Each Tribe has its own Sacred Prey, which are more abstract and ethos-driven than the Forsaken's.

                  Although one-for-one analogies end up glossing over a lot of nuance, I think it can also be helpful to consider the three Pure tribes as dark reflections of three of the Forsaken tribes, namely:

                  Fire-Touched/Bone Shadows: The Bone Shadow Ban of paying each spirit in kind often means that they're willing to play Shadow politics with dangerous and harmful beings, while the Fire-Touched turn this up to eleven and actively worship spirit nobles in exchange for mystical powers.

                  Predator Kings/Hunters in Darkness: Both tribes are big on maintaining their territories and have a predilection for wilderness over urban areas, but the Hunters do this with an eye for keeping balance between worlds, while the Predators are attempting to recreate Pangaea on Earth.

                  Ivory Claws/Storm Lords: The Storm Lords tend to be the most politically minded Uratha, managing Protectorates and honing their mettle (ostensibly) for the good of all. The Ivory Claws, on the other hand, believe werewolves to be inherently superior to other lifeforms, and their own carefully curated bloodlines the cream of the crop.

                  Hopefully that's helpful. I know it doesn't cover all your questions, but those are my most coherent thoughts on the Anshega. Where the Forsaken see themselves bound by ancient duty, the Pure are rather driven by fanaticism and resentment.

                                  In 2E, the Pure tribes aren’t monolithic entities, either as individual tribes, or as a ‘nation’, any more than the Forsaken are. This is important because regional and cultural influences are going to shift what the Pure “want” in any given area. Different packs in different regions in different countries in different continents aren’t necessarily going to want the same things on a micro scale, though they share enough similarities that their Firstborn patrons recognise them all as belonging to its tribe.

                                  With that said, IMO what the Pure want is based on romanticized and inaccurate view of a time that no one really remembers. In this romanticizing, they’ve actually forsaken everything Father Wolf stood for and are more fallen than those they hate. While the Pure are deluded enough not to understand or acknowledge it, their ancestors and Firstborn forebearers are cowards and traitors to their birthright.

                                  The Fire-Touched want to elevate the Shadow above the world of flesh, but they ignore that Urfarah specifically worked tirelessly to prevent the two from coming together. He was the god of boundaries and wanted to keep these things apart.

                                  The Ivory Claws are obsessed with bloodline and lineage and breeding towards a future avatar of Father Wolf. They don’t know that Urfarah spread his seed far and wide, mating with all manner of creatures to spawn the different Firstborn, and that the Uratha are no more than an enduring cross-breed that Urfarah ignored as much as his other children.

                                  The Predator Kings want a return to Pangaea, to their mythical perfect hunting grounds where they were dominant predators. They blame humanity for introducing weakness to the world and to Urfarah. They ignore the fact that of all the creatures, Father Wolf chose humanity as the basis for the Uratha, and without humans the Predator Kings are nothing.

                                  But to answer your question in a more straight forward manner. The Pure mostly want to run their own territories however they see fit, hate the Forsaken, and grab whatever power they can while cravenly bowing to the whims of powerful spirit masters. They’re extremists who live with hate in their hearts because to do otherwise is to open themselves to admitting how pointless their goals are. For every Pure who lives the party line with their heart and soul, there’s one who pays lip service and mostly just wants to be a badass motherfucker werewolf.

                                  They’re cunning and powerful opponents for Forsaken because they are free thinking beings who can be whatever you want them to be. They can work with the Forsaken when it suits their purpose, they can work against Forsaken when it doesn’t. They can do as much evil shit as humanity can think up, because at their core they’re products of humanity as much as Forsaken are.

                                          I think this is a great topic that is worth exploring in depth. Different playstyles are, of course, going to treat these antagonists differently. Groups who prefer a relatively clear-cut black hat/white hat binary have it fairly easy: the Pure are the werewolf KKK- ignorant fanatics with a delusional nostalgia for a time that never really existed.
                                          I find this a pretty unsatisfying take, not only because it neuters any kind of moral quandaries the PCs might face about how "pure" their own motives are as Forsaken, but also because it stretches credulity a bit. How could a faction that is so patently insane hope to get any recruits at all, other than through straight-up brainwashing? How could they entice Forsaken to come over to their side? We're repeatedly told that the Fire-Touched are persuasive evangelists, but I haven't seen anything like a compelling example of what that would look like.

                                          For me, an important element of defining an antagonist is establishing what they are right about. It's pretty unusual that any ideology that has any traction in the real world is completely based on thin air. Much more often, they've started at some reasonable premise and taken it in an utterly unreasonable direction. Things the Pure have some moral standing on:

                                          Was it absolutely necessary for the Forsaken to have committed patricide? Did they really exhaust all other options before committing themselves to that course? (Of course, there is going to be disagreement about this. What if the Pure loremasters have "knowledge" of an ancient rite that could have healed Father Wolf?)
                                          Were the urdaga's motives really pure, consequences aside? Were at least some of them motivated by the desire to be top predator more than to "save the world"?
                                          Was the cure possibly worse than the affliction? What's so bad, really, about a world where the Shadow dominates the Flesh? And to the extent that it is bad, is it really that much better to live in a world where the Spirit of the world is severed from its Body? (There is room in here for a compelling vision of Pure spirituality that might actually make sense to many Forsaken.)
                                          Aren't humans kinda f'ed up? I mean, plenty of humans agree that there are too many humans, with not enough ecological "management". We players, as humans, are going to be prone to an anthropocentric worldview, but werewolves will naturally shed that prejudice over time. There is a valid argument to be made that humans are no different than any other animal, and subject to the same ecological forces. Werewolves have not only the right, but the responsibility to manage their numbers and activity, and sometimes that means feeding them to spirits.


                                          Finally, I think it's good to look beyond ideology. Factional conflicts might start out as ideological, but over time they will tend to become pragmatic; and vice versa. The spread of Islam into southern Africa and Southeast Asia, for example, might have had something to do with the appeal of the doctrine, but it was just as much a matter of the mercantile ambitions of natives who saw the advantages of belonging to this global network of believers. For a lot of younger werewolves, for whom a bunch of Just So Stories about some folkloric wolf-god are not going to be very compelling, the attraction to align with Pure or Forsaken is going to depend a great deal on what those factions have to offer in terms of power, security, opportunity for advancement in a hierarchy, etc.


                                          I'd be especially interested to hear how any of you have run "conversion" scenarios, in either direction. The Pure are a lot scarier, IMO, when they might be on to something...

                                              Adding to what robothedino said, I too like to think of the Pure ethos as a reasonable, yet profoundly misguided philosophy. Espousing a doctrine of hatred and bigotry may seem a little far-fetched for the Uratha, but there are certain elements of the werewolf existence that could help explain that kind of poisonous beliefs:

                                              - Uratha are apex predators, and humans are no less prey than any other creature, as far as their primal urges are concerned. That can alienate an Uratha from mundane human experiences and it can objectify former human acquaintances into mere cattle and resources (vampires have a similar problem, too).

                                              - Uratha deal with the constant threat of a supernatural rage always boiling beneath the surface, and that can be pretty difficult to manage. A doctrine that provides convenient scapegoats in the form of the Forsaken and which victimizes fellow Pure for their situation can help rationalize said rage and provide a context by which the Pure can vent off against their chosen enemy.

                                              - Hostile pressure from the spirit world might make siding with it a convenient stance, especially since so many powerful spirits can offer the Uratha considerable temporal and mystical power, often in exchance of terrible moral degradation. So a certain degree of spirit worship can help the Pure to feel entitled to their position as gatekeepers between both worlds, even as they clearly favor one over the other.

                                              - The Forsaken are no less prone to anger, hatred and bigotry than any Pure, and many tenets of the Oath of the Moon can conflict with their personal ethics and morals. The Pure provide justification and a sense of elation for many young uratha, relieving them from their human sense of guilt, showing them ways to repent and atone for what is an almost inevitably traumatic existence as hunters in a world of monsters.

                                              All in all, I think the Pure have their fair share of true believers and hard-liners, just as much as the Forsaken do, but they can also have their hanger-ons and casual members. The fundamental difference between the two, I think, is that the Pure truly embrace the more violent and monstruous side of their nature as a way to reach inner Harmony as children of Urfarah, while the Forsaken are more flexible as to what form their paths to Harmony can take, while the Oath of the Moon enforces a little more empathetic perspective that can help them relate to the their human side.

It's just as possible that someone would have a bad time with the Forsaken, or ghost wolves. Lunes aren't nice, they can come down and curse everyone who saw you change to madness. Forsaken can treat you cruelly, groups like the Temple of Apollo can lead you astray. Forsaken and ghost wolves are just as capable of evil misdeeds as the Pure. And to someone who's had that kind of experience, the Pure come with promises of a better way.
