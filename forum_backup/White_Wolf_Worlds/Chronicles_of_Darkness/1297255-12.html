            I had a larger post and then refreshed the page without posting it, so I'll probably have to deal with the majority of those thoughts when I come back to Lessons proper, but for now:

            Simply put, pure nihilism is a bad baseline for designing game character bases because basically it incentives unmotivated characters, and an unmotivated character is a character who doesn't do anything, which is antithetical to telling good stories at a table with other people(see where Genius First's presentation fucked up). You can also see this in the shift with the Acanthus in Awakening, from a character who tells you they don't aspire to anything because they just manage their way through whatever to a character who seems effortless at what they do because they are furiously pedaling behind the scenes to makes things work exactly the way they want them, which in short translates to "Character who never gives a shit about anything" to "Character who gives all the shits about this thing in particular, what could it be, YOU DECIDE AND DELIVER".

            By simply introducing Lessons as a cultural concept for the character type, you get to have your nihilism cake and eat it too-you can challenge it or accept it, have both stand points challenged, and have nihilism ultimately affirmed in a more meaningful way through the nuance of the conversation than just simple binge-eating to binge-eat. It creates motivated characters who will inherently have a character conflict baked in, because if nothing else, at all times a Beast has to ask themselves "What the fuck am I doing with my life" no matter whether they pursue it or leave it alone.

            Just tackling nihilism straight away for no other reason may still allow for that freedom, but as the initial draft of Beast proved, it tends to more often result in people asking "Well then why the fuck am I bothering?", again ala Genius First. Better to give players something clear to chase after and heavily question along the way than nothing to do at the beginning.

                                                            Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post
                                                            While all of the CofD gamelines have some sort of metaphor for playing as "the other", they all offer shortcuts so the negative side of it that can be side-stepped. It not being necessary to drop mortal hunters (Vigil hunters or otherwise) on any of the gameline's monsters, being a monster carrying its own duties that don't involved being ostracized as an "other" (Werewolf, Geist), being the "other" is portrayed as cool, sexy, and empowering (Vampire, Changeling), or even being cathartic in allowing PCs to change the world into a better place (Mage, Hunter).

                                                            Plus, despite CofD (and by extension, its progenitor WoD) lofty (or more cynically, "pretentious") goals of trying to offer more mature and in-depth storytelling versus the EVILS of murderhobo "rollplaying" from mainstream titles like D&D or Pathfinder...there is still an element of escapist power-fantasy involved in the previous CofD games.

                                                            Beast doesn't really allow for that. At least, not in the conventional way.

                                                            For instance, looking at Satiety and the other "feeding" methods of the other CofD gamelines...

                                                            Mages and their Mysteries don't require human victims at all, Promethean's Disquiet being something they can't help, Changelings aren't required to be the source of whatever generated emotion is needed to create Glamour, etc.

                                                            Beast, as of right now, doesn't have those convenient shortcuts.
