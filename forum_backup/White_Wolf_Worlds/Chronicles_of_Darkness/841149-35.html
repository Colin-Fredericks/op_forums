    Originally posted by White Oak Dragon View Post

    I'm going to need to give this a bit of thought. As it stands, it was an extremely potent blessing in the first place. They gained a dot in an Attribute, 8-Again on 3 different skills under most circumstances (as opposed to 8-Again on a single skill regardless of circumstances), and adding the current Wyrd benefit makes it so that even a starting character can ensure a decent dice pool to take full advantage of said 8-Again effect.

    The change to only Athletics gaining 8-Again was a good one, and is definitely a step in the right direction. I'd be reluctant to allow the Wyrd benefit to cover as many circumstances as it does, though. Especially for combat rolls. It leads to "My character is throwing a knife. My base dice pool is 6. He had a four Defense, bringing me down to 2. I'll switch to Wyrd, which brings me back to 6. I'm also going to target his eye to blind him and bypass his armor. Still at 6 dice with the 8-Again effect." Allowing the Wyrd benefit to function that way effectively allows the character to bypass Defense, most Armor, and penalties for called shots and environmental difficulties, with 8-Again if it's Athletics based. I'll need to compare it to some of the other physically focused kiths, but it might work as simply as having a Glamour or Willpower cost in order to use the suggested Wyrd benefit for a turn. I'll give it a bit of thought.
    Here is another revision to the Apollonian.

    -=-

    Apollonian

    Blessing:     She gains the 8-again quality on all Athletics rolls, and a dot of Strength or Dexterity which can bring her beyond the limit of her Wyrd. Furthermore, when making a Brawl or Weaponry attack, she may choose to use her Wyrd in place of her normal dice pool.

        Originally posted by Deionscribe View Post

        Here is another revision to the Apollonian.

        -=-

        Apollonian

        Blessing:         She gains the 8-again quality on all Athletics rolls, and a dot of Strength or Dexterity which can bring her beyond the limit of her Wyrd. Furthermore, when making a Brawl or Weaponry attack, she may choose to use her Wyrd in place of her normal dice pool.
        This is much better, as it keeps the character from coupling the 8-Again effect with the ability to ignore all penalties when attacking. That said, the ability to ignore all penalties when attacking is still pretty powerful. I'll use a couple examples, assuming 3 in each Attribute (4 for Apollonian, to reflect an extra dot of Strength being chosen) and Skill and Potent Kith 5 for each comparison.

        Let's start with improvised weapons. Bloodbrute gets to ignore the -1 penalty to attack with an improvised weapon, and gets to keep using it. The Apollonian gets to ignore the penalty as well, and also gets to ignore Defense, potentially ignore Armor (using a called shot is made to strike a vulnerable spot), and also make a called shot without any added difficulty. In effect, the Apollonian is more skilled at wielding improvised weapons, but the Bloodbrute gets to keep using them for longer.

        Looking at called shots, a Hunterheart can ignore penalties for a called shot equal to Wyrd. In this situation, both the Hunterheart and Apollonian will be able to ignore most penalties for called shots, and both will be able to bypass some worn Armor. An Apollonian will also ignore Defense, however. This is a much closer match, though, because while the Apollonian wins on called shots, the Hunterheart's got some goodies to help even the odds. It's debatable whether innate lethal damage and the ability to cause fear by locking eyes would be greater or less than 8-Again on Athletics, a bonus Attribute dot, and the ability to ignore Defense and environmental penalties during attacks, though I think I'd give the win to the Apollonian there.

        Now, on to inflicting Tilts. A Razorhand can spend a point of Glamour to make the next successful attack inflict one of four Tilts without needing to make a called shot. Assuming that they are facing an opponent with a Defense of 4, this will put the Razorhand at 2 dice on his attack roll. If he hits, he'll do a lot of damage with his attack and inflict the Tilt, but the odds aren't in his favor unless he spends Willpower. The Apollonian needs to make a called shot to be able to inflict one of those Tilts, but doing so doesn't penalize him and he's able to ignore Defense as well, giving him a base dice pool of six thanks to the Wyrd effect. This makes the Apollonian more skilled at applying the Tilts, but requires that he bring a weapon along to the fight if he wants to match the Razorhand in damage during successful attacks.

        On the whole, it seems to me that the Wyrd benefit in combat places the Apollonian on stronger ground than some of the other kiths that specialize in that arena, and that's before figuring in the 8-Again effect on Athletics and the bonus Attribute dot. That said, while potent, your current version isn't completely unreasonable. Back when it gave 8-Again to all of the above rolls, though... I'm glad that you changed that.

        Here's my thoughts on some potential Blessings:

        Blessing: The Apollonian was intended to be a model of physical perfection, and as such, gains an additional dot to either Strength or Dexterity, even if this takes him above the limit imposed by his Wyrd. Additionally, the character gains the 8-Again effect on Atheletics rolls involving his enhanced Attribute. When in situations intended to demonstrate his prowess to onlookers, such as during duels or competitions, the Apollonian ignores penalties to all Physical Skill dice-pools equal to half his Wyrd. He may increase this to his full Wyrd for a single action by spending a point of Glamour. If the Apollonian spends Glamour in this manner outside of such situations, the benefit is only equal to half his Wyrd for a single action.

        The above enables the character to excel physically wherever they go, but to truly shine in front of an audience. It also means that the character will be able to compete using Skills besides the combat ones, such as a Drive challenge for a chariot race or Larceny to reflect impressive feats of manual dexterity. It also means that a character with impressive Defense, such as a Skitterskulk (whose tripled Defense would just be outright ignored using the originally suggested Wyrd benefit) could continue to be more of a challenge to take down than an ordinary opponent, while still giving the Apollonian a greater chance of managing it than most others.

                I'm glad that you liked them, Deionscribe.

                So, on another thread, people have been saying that they'd like to see a hacker kith in the future. Here's my attempt at such a thing:

                Cybersage

                She's so weird. Yeah, she's smart, but she always seems distracted, and sometimes, she just starts laughing for no reason in the middle of class.

                The rapid rise of technology in mortal society has not gone unnoticed by the Others, and some have chosen to embrace the new toys that the mortals have created. Cybersages are the tools of choice for these Gentry, who use them as espionage agents or soldiers on the field of cyberwarfare. Tasked with undermining the efforts of other technologically savvy Fae, Cybersages frequently burn out from the constant need for their talents.

                Beast: This adorable kitten is always laughing about something.
                Fairest: She resembles a video game avatar, beautiful to behold, but firmly in the uncanny valley.
                Grimm: Glowing lines of binary code flow constantly beneath her glass skin.
                Ogre: This ill-tempered troll has a foul mouth, thick glasses, and surprisingly dexterous fingers.

                Blessing: Cybersages add their Wyrd to dice pools using the Computer Skill. A Cybersage may also interface with any electronic device she can physically touch, bypassing mundane security measures like passwords or firewalls, although supernatural protection provokes a Clash of Wills. Additionally, by spending a point of Glamour, a Cybersage may mentally access the Internet for the scene as though she had a smartphone or laptop.

                        Here's another kith which uses a Merit from the Hurt Locker drafts.

                        -=-

                        Spellbinder
                        You tore my life apart. Now, I'll do the same to yours.

                        There is power found in one's image, and those who subvert it can hold great power over him. Changelings transformed into Spellbinders were made to punish their Keepers' captives enemies with equal measure. They wove charms bearing pieces of their victims, and used them to inflict all manner of harm. Some were brutal and powerful in their curses that they would often leave a trail of death in their wake..

                        Blessing: Whenever she makes an Occult roll, the changeling receives an exceptional success at three instead of five. She also can spend Glamour or Willpower to invoke the Curse Effigy Merit, and has automatic successes equal to half her Wyrd in addition to the roll results.
