        The Peacemaker ●●●●●
        The “Peacemaker” is a Colt Single Action Army revolver, manufactured in 1874. The revolver is constructed of dark steel with a rich mahogany grip and features intricate engraving. The weapon once belonged to a notorious bounty hunter in the old west and has passed through the hands of numerous collectors in subsequent years. It remains in pristine working condition.
        Damage: 2L Initiative: -2 Size: 1 Durability: 3(8) Capacity: 6 Range: 35/70/140 Availability: 4
        Powers
        Slayer: When activated, the Peacemaker deals Aggravated Damage to ephemeral beings or any creature without an Integrity score. The pistol deals normal weapon damage to mortals.
        Cost:
        Slayer: 1 Willpower/Attack
        Special Ammunition: While capable of firing standard bullets (that deal normal damage), the powers of the weapon can only be used with specially prepared ammunition. The rounds must use a specific gunpowder mixture, the casing must be silver, and each round must be blessed. The weapon currently contains 4 rounds. Creating new ammunition requires the Relic Maker Merit (Second Sight, pg. 85) or equivalent supernatural powers. Only 6 rounds can be created at a time.

        The Phantom Gun●●
        This pistol is a snub nosed .38 revolver of unknown make that dates to the mid-seventies. The handle is taped and the weapon shows signs of heavy use.
        Damage: 1L Initiative: 0 Size: 1 Durability: 3(5) Capacity: 6 Range: 20/40/80 Availability: 2
        Powers
        Bad Penny: The Phantom Pistol always appears close to someone who would be tempted to use it to commit a crime.
        Untraceable: The weapon is difficult to track; it retains no fingerprints (ballistic or otherwise) and imposes a -5 penalty to rolls to tie it or its owner to a crime.
        Curses
        Danger Magnet: If not discarded within 24 hours of committing a crime, the user draws the attention of the authorities.
        Ill-Effect: The user suffers a -3 penalty to all rolls for 24 hours after using the pistol to commit a crime.
        Cost: The weapon has no cost to activate, but if it isn’t discarded within 24 hours after committing a crime, the user suffers the Danger Magnet Curse as well.

        The Pilgrims Flask ●●
        The Pilgrims flask is an antique flask made of tarnished silver, its sides engraved with prayers written in Arabic. The flask is roughly 100 years old and in fully working condition. The water poured from the flask is considered holy water for ritual purposes.
        Equipment Bonus: +1 (Survival), +2 Occult (Abjuration) Size: 1 Durability: 3(5) Availability: 3
        Powers
        Burning Purity: The water from the flask burns demons, ghosts and undead creatures. Each splash deals 2L damage and is considered damage from fire for the effected creatures. A full flask contains enough water for 3 splashes.
        Protection from Hunger & Thirst: Wits +Survival; Drinking from the flask (at least a third of the water) protects the user from Deprivation for success/days.
        Cost:
        Burning Purity: 1 willpower/per attack.
        Protection from Hunger and Thirst: 1 willpower/per drink

                    The cult that held this weapon previously named it Gods Own Gun. The boy that stole it from them thought of it as his Retcon Revolver. The PC's called it the Innocent Gun briefly before they destroyed it.

                    The Innocent Gun
                    This gun appears to be an antique bone handled revolver. Closer inspection reveals that the barrel is rifled and that any inner surface of the gun, such as where bone meets metal in the handle, is inscribed with gear like runes. The first property most marksmen will notice when testing the gun is it's extreme lethality, it's weapon damage of 4 Lethal.

                    It's real power is what happens after a victim is dead. Within seconds the body fades away and all record of the individual fades with them. They now never existed. No loved ones will remmeber them, no government record shows their birth, their home has never known their touch. Even the gun itself has the bullets once more in it's chambers, never having had a target to fire upon.

                    This erasure is not complete, however. It's a "minimal change" erasure of history. For example if a doctor is killed the patients they saved were still saved but nobody knows who did it. A cop that caught a dangerous serial killer before being killed by the gun might leave behind a world in which the serial killer was caught by sheer luck rather than careful detective work. In addition there is another important omission to the gun's erasure. Any sapient witnesses will retain their full memories and can even keep a small possessions safe from the deletion. For example, a woman that sees her spouse killed by the gun will still remember said spouse and even have their wedding ring and photos of them on their phone. Nobody else may remember that they were ever married and all backups in "the cloud" will be deleted but they will have the memories and photos for as long as they live.

                                            Thanks for the feedback! Tinkering with game mechanics is one of my hobbies, so I'm glad someone can get use out of these little baubles.

                                            a few of my latest.

                                            The Dead Cell ●●
                                            The device known as “The Dead Cell” is a “RAZR” style cellular phone that was popular in the mid-late 2000’s. This particular device is matte black in color and has the number sequence “#22558433323” scratched into its back casing.
                                            Size: 1 Durability: 2(4) Availability: 1
                                            Powers
                                            Call the Dead: Wits + Empathy; If the roll is successful, the user opens a line to a deceased person they knew in life. The deceased person is always someone that was a positive influence, and the conversation is uplifting, granting the user the Inspired Condition. On an exceptional success, the user regains point of willpower as well.
                                            On a dramatic failure, the user contacts something malevolent and gains the Shaken Condition.
                                            This power may only be used once per day.
                                            Cost: 1Willpower

                                            --------------------------

                                            Formula 16-Zeta ●●●●
                                            This small case contains several 10ml glass vials. While the case is capable of holding 8 of these vials, only 5 remain. Each vial contains a clear fluid with an oily texture and slight red tint. The formula is a Toxicity 6 lethal poison when injected into a living subject.
                                            Size: 0 Durability: 1(5) Availability: 4
                                            Powers
                                            Reanimation: When injected into dead tissue, the formula grants reanimation. Reanimating a creature requires 1ml for a size 5 creature, with proportionately less or more depending on the subject’s size. The Reanimated creature is a Zombie with the following traits

                                            The creature uses its original traits, with Power, Finesse, and Resistance equal to the Average of its Power, Finesse and Resistance traits (rounded down). The Creature then gains an additional point to Power and Resistance. These three Attributes don't encompass most mental or any social functions. Power represents the creature’s pure brute power; Finesse represents its motor control and awareness, while Resistance measures the creature’s durability.

                                            Defense: 0 Health: [Resistance + Size] Initiative: [Finesse] Integrity: [10 – days of Decomposition] Speed: [Power + Finesse]

                                            Physical Integrity: The creature has Physical Integrity of (10- days of decomposition). Physical Integrity limits the maximum number of dice in the creature’s dice pool. Every 4 hours, the creature loses a point of Physical Integrity. When taking damage, once a zombie's Health points are gone, further damage subtracts from Physical Integrity. Once it reaches 0, the creature is forever destroyed.

                                            Combat: The creature gains a +2 die bonus when making unarmed attacks or attacks with weapons it may have picked up. Its unarmed strikes deal bashing damage, and its bites deal lethal damage.

                                            Indestructible: The creature is not vulnerable to a specific form of attack or targeted weak points. The creature can only be destroyed by reducing their Physical Integrity to 0. The creature does not suffer wound penalties, are immune diseases, poisons and the need to breathe or sleep. While they can be targeted by most physical-based Tilts, such as Arm Wrack, they are immune to mental or social Tilts and Conditions. Their undead bodies are also immune to the Beaten Down and Stunned Tilt, but they can be Knocked Down.

                                            Limited Intelligence: The creature has a limited capacity for problem solving. They may turn doorknobs, climb ladders (if they have Finesse 2 or greater), and pick up basic handheld weapons.

                                            Residual Memories: When presented with a loved one or something else it cared about from life, the creature will stop attacking for (Resistance) turns. After that, it will attack even on its former loved ones.

                                            The Harvester ●●●●●
                                            “The Harvester” is a ritual sickle of unknown origin and unusual design. The sturdy bronze blade is smooth edged and razor sharp. The handle is carved from a ram’s horn and is wrapped in leather. The blade is stamped with symbols that do not correspond with any commonly known language or cipher. The remarkably well preserved blade was excavated from a construction site in rural Poland nearly ninety years ago, but research has concluded that it is at least 300 years old.
                                            Damage: 1L Initiative: -1 Strength: 1 Size: 2 Durability: 2(7) Availability: 3
                                            Powers
                                            Soul Cutter: Harvester is capable of cutting a person’s soul free from their bodies. After using Harvester to inflict at least 3 points of lethal damage against a victim, the user may make a reflexive Presence+ Manipulation contested by the victims Resolve +Composure. The successful, the victim gains the Soulless Condition as their soul becomes bound to Harvester. Once the victim is bound, the user may draw Willpower from him at any time, from any distance. The victim loses a point of willpower which is immediately transferred to the user. Harvester can only bind one soul at a time. This ability can be used until the victim gains the Thrall condition, at which point their soul has been completely consumed and is no longer bound to Harvester.
                                            The user may release a soul at any time by spending a point of willpower, ending the victims Soulless Condition. A victim that takes control of Harvester from its current user immediately ends the Soulless Condition. Harvester can only affect creature with an Integrity score, the spiritual nature of other beings are beyond its ability to effect.
                                            Cost: 3 Willpower

                                            The Moonlight Lullaby ●●●
                                            “The Moonlight Lullaby” is a handmade, exquisitely crafted music box that plays Beethoven’s “Moonlight Sonata” for 3 minutes. The box was crafted in 1901 by Fritz Rothstein, a watchmaker of exceptional talent who crafted the box as a way to calm the sleep of his daughter, who was frequently plagued by nightmares. It grants its equipment bonus to rolls involving meditation or relaxation.
                                            Die Bonus: +1 Size: 2 Durability: 2(5) Availability: 2
                                            Powers
                                            Restful Slumber: Wits + Composure; Those who keep the box by their bedside and listen to its music sleep soundly and restfully. When activated, any conditions that prevent a restful sleep are suppressed while under the protection of the Moonlight Lullaby. In addition, any supernatural ability that would affect the sleeper while they are using the Moonlight Lullaby suffers a -3 penalty. On an exceptional success, the user awakens with the Steadfast Condition. On a dramatic failure, the user gains no benefits and awakens with the Exhausted Condition.
                                            Cost: None to use; but the user must establish a bond with the relic. Over the course of 5 nights, the user must fall asleep listing to the music box, investing a point of will power each time. Once a total of 5 points is invested, they user may activate the device with no further costs. In addition, any of the user’s offspring or grandchildren may also benefit from this bond.

Cypron’s Journal grants its die bonus to research rolls involving Vlad Dracul.
                                                Die Bonus: +2 Size: 1 Durability: 1 Availability: 3

Corpse Dolls: Corpse Dolls (no one knows how many there are or what is involved in making one) resemble mannequins, but with faint veins visible under the skin. If someone spills a sufficient quantity of their blood onto one (equivalent to 1L damage), the mannequin shifts, turning into a corpse physically identical to the source of the blood. It takes a total of 20 accumulated successes on a relevant roll (Wits+Medicine, Wits+Occult, or a relevant supernatural power of perception) to tell that anything is off, and even if the successes are met, one feels only a vague feeling of unease. There are, however, ways to "cheat" this- if someone has a psychometric ability and uses it on the doll, they would see the person the doll is mimicking seemingly standing over his own corpse- though they might interpret this to mean that something stole the person's identity and killed them. The transformed doll is a flawless mimic- DNA tests, dental records, fingerprints, and so on will all match the person who contributed the blood. There is, however, one tiny exception. At the base of the skull, there are several tiny scratches that when viewed under a microscope spell out "Infinite Forms" in Latin. If someone recites this phrase while touching the doll, it will return to its mannequin state.

                                                    The one exception to the doll's normal complete resemblance to a human corpse happens if someone attempts to use it or parts of it for any sort of ritual or magical effect that requires a human corpse. If they try to do so, whatever magic they were attempting goes wrong in some way. Similarly, if a vampire attempts to drink blood from the doll, the blood tastes heavily diluted and unsatisfying.

                                                    ??+1: The Eviction Notice: The Notice resembles a revolver of indeterminate make capable of accepting any bullets that would conceivably fit a large revolver (it uses the heavy pistol's stats to determine damage). The barrel, however, is on the opposite side: the weapon is reversed so that it points at the person carrying it.

                                                    To use it, the wielder must spend a willpower point to force themself to pull the trigger. Then the player rolls their willpower cap (not the amount they currently have) versus the weapon's attack roll. If the attack roll is greater, the wielder takes damage as normal. If the willpower roll is greater, the wielder takes only 1L damage. If there happens to be an entity possessing or bound or anchored to the wielder, however, it takes lethal damage equal to the combined successes of both rolls. If the wielder is currently bearing some sort of curse or magical effect, the successes are counted towards the number of successes on whatever roll it would take to dispel it.

                                                    ??+2: The Needle of Truth: This object resembles a long stiletto needle with a dome-shaped cap engraved with the image of an eye on the end opposite the point instead of a handle. To use it, the bearer spends one willpower point to stab themself in the eye with it, taking a point of lethal damage as the needle is pushed into the brain. Removing the needle requires another point of willpower. The point of lethal damage incurred by it remains as long as the needle does. If it is removed, the point can be healed, but the eye remains permanently blinded, and the bearer must spend another willpower point and take damage to put it back in.

                                                    While the needle is in place, the bearer suffers a -3 penalty to all social rolls other than intimidation, but gets a +1 bonus to any rolls involving sight-based perception. While the needle is in, the bearer can see entities in Twilight. By pushing it in further, causing more damage, the bearer can see even more. Pushing the needle in costs another willpower point, and deals lethal damage equal to the effect desired. After an hour, the damage heals and the needle is pushed back to normal position. Healing the damage causes the needle to return to normal prematurely.

                                                    1L (in addition to the 1L that remains whenever the needle is in place): The bearer can see through the Mask as well as minor illusions such as lower levels of Obfuscate. The bearer also gains a +1 bonus to see through any visual deception.

                                                    2L: The bearer can see into the Shadow. By concentrating on any area, they focus and can see its correspondence in the shadow.

                                                    3L: The bearer can see through higher level illusionary effects and is unaffected by Lunacy. They can also see through anything less than one inch thick.

                                                    4L: The wearer gains a gaze attack that can be used on anyone they have eye contact with. Its dice pool is Resolve+Willpower and the user can choose to have it deal damage with a 2B modifier or inflict an appropriate Tilt.

                                                    ??+3 The Essence: Many have theorized that all supernatural processes work on the central principle of equivalent exchange. Something is sacrificed, something is gained. Scholars, sorcerors, and mystics well versed in the occult have long believed that this principle holds the key to understanding all magic. The Essence is the culmination of this research, the result of numerous wizards and sages and assorted willworkers attempting to distill the core principle down to its barest essence: something is given, something is gained.

                                                    The Essence appears to be an off brand knockoff earlier generation iPod, made of hard, shiny plastic. When activated by its single button, it speaks with two voices, one male and one female, speaking in unison. Both use 1920s vernacular and seem quite affable and helpful. The device works very simply: the user makes his wish, and the Essence tells him what it needs. All prices are final. By whatever method, the price is always exactly metaphysically equal in value to the wish, though they are usually something the wisher could do if the wish is reasonable. This means, among other things, that truly massive wishes are almost impossible to carry out. Wishing for the universe, or even just the earth or another celestial body, to be destroyed would require a massive amount of cosmic-level labor likely impossible even for the mightiest beings in the world of darkness. The cost is directly related to how difficult the task would be to carry out. For example, a single sacrifice, or multiple animal or material sacrifices would be enough to kill someone in poor health. But a very healthy person living in a fortified bunker surrounded by guards might take several sacrifices and a burning pile of money to kill.

                                                    The Essence can even bring back the dead, but it's quite costly. One life sacrificed can be enough to take another life, but it's never enough to bring someone back. Bringing someone back requires a high cost and usually results in the target coming back as a vampire, Sin-Eater, intelligent ghost, or something similar. The Essence has said that it can bring a person fully and completely back to life, but it needs a lot to do it. So far no one has ever completed the costs it has given for this objective.

                                                    The Essence has its own personality, and if activated can converse on a wide variety of topics. It seems to genuinely wish to help whoever is using it- it is impossible to make an accidental wish, and the device grants wishes according to the spirit as well as the word, even asking for additional details or clarification. "You want a million bucks but you don't want anyone getting knocked off over it? I can do it, but that'll be an extra charge."

                                                    The final detail of the Essence is that each subsequent wish by the same user is slightly more expensive. A more significant wish makes the cost higher next time, but even wishing for small things quickly builds up the cost. It is unknown of this trait is necessary to the device's magical operations, is a restriction placed on it by its creators, or is simply a decision by the intelligence inhabiting it.

                                                        Breathless Jar ●

                                                        When your character moved in, behind some detritus in the attic they found a missing couple square feet of wall, a tiny cubby hole nestled in the roof insulation. Inside they found some pebbles, some very old loose change, a tiny penknife, and a jar.

                                                        It's a small, mid-century mason jar, dull, rough, but unrusted, screwed tight and sealed tight at the lip with some kind of waxy stuff. Inside are perhaps a dozen dead moths, of an unremarkable local variety.

                                                        The jar does nothing but sit quietly wherever it's put... almost always. From time to time, however, there is the faintest sound of fluttering. The jar isn't haunted, itself, it's just tugged briefly into wakefulness when the whispers of the Underworld grow too loud. If its property is discovered, it can act as a detector as regarding Death resonance, but its ability to read impressions is rather weak, and can be somewhat unreliable. Any vanilla mortal trying to use it as a makeshift geiger counter will probably either get lost, or have it burst into furious buzzing activity as the watcher realizes they're being watched.

                                                        If the jar is unscrewed, the moths fall silently for a moment, then scatter wildly, as an overwhelming sensation of suffocation crashes over whomever opened it; failure or dramatic failure on a reflexive resistance roll result in either momentary unconsciousness from asphyxiation, or much longer unconsciousness and a brief instant of pulminary and cardiac silence.

                                                        If the jar is broken, the jar is just broken.
