    God-Machine Lineage

    11-06-2013, 12:23 AM
    For my new campaign I am taking a crack at writing up a Lineage that ties Promethean's into the machinations of the God-Machine. It was inspired by the quite cool NPCs presented in Saturnine Nights who were each made by a different kind of supernatural being. It's pretty barebones right now because, despite liking the concept, I'm not sure exactly how to execute it. I think much of this could change before I'm done. I'd love feedback as I'm hoping to make this Lineage available to my PCs if it is hammered out.
    Seraphim

    Progenitor: Created in modern nights, the Demiurge responsible for the Seraphim may still be alive in the world. During the brief time the first Seraphim knew his Demiurge he was told only that his Demiurge was himself a divine being who had disobeyed God and sought to make one of his own kind for succor.

    Other Nicknames: The Blessed

    Appearance: Though there are few Seraphim in the world, all are Unfleshed.

    Humour: The ruling humour of the Seraphim is unique to their Lineage, manna. Imbued with the divine nature of their demiurge, Seraphim are prone to seeing patterns where others see chaos, fate where others see coincidence, and suffer from frequent bursts of moral outrage against a world that does not meet their stringent standards. The unusual humour drives some Seraphim to be martyrs just as much as it drives others to be relentless judges.

    Element: Seraphim are not associated with a particular element. They regain a point of Pyros when they rest amongst functioning complex machines (sleeping in a running car, in front of a TV that’s turned on, or surrounded by personal electronic devices).

    Bestowment: No idea.

    Torment: When suffering through the throes of Torment, the Seraphim cast righteous judgment on themselves and others. If the Seraphim has judged himself to be wicked or morally failing, this Torment might involve self-flagellation, attempting to make amends (no matter the cost to him or others) to those he believes he has wronged, or long periods of retreat from the world. If he judges others it may involve inflicting whatever the Seraphim has decided is an appropriate judgment on the individual or individuals.

    Disquiet: Mortals suffering from the Disquiet of the Seraphim believe themselves to be superior to others, most especially the irritatingly holier-than-thou Seraphim. Early stages look like cold disregard or curt disapprovals of the Seraphim while later levels involve fanatical witch hunts to correct perceived wrongs committed by the Seraphim.

    Wasteland: Their mechanical nature colors the effect Seraphim have on the world around them. While plants whither, advanced thinking life begins acting in more and more predictable patterns. Technology seems to work even better than normal at earlier levels but as Wasteland advances the technology becomes glitch and performs functions it should not be able to (PCs will link with various secure and insecure networks it should have no access to, cell phones will begin making calls or financial transactions, microwaves will self-start and combust, etc.).

      If you want to tie them closer to the god-machine, give them the element of order, so in places where things happen as they should with efficiency, they regain pyros.

      Likewise, you could make the disquiet paranoia, people begin thinking that their choices were laid out before them, their free will is gone, and it's all the seraphim's fault.

      Self Righteousness isn't really right for their godmachine origins, I'd make it judgement based on perfection, not morality. Failure in general, not wickedness which the godmachine couldn't care less about.

      As for bestowment, I'd go with spare parts the alternate frankenstein bestowment for repairing or tinkering with themselves with machine parts, the scrutiny to make them prophets sort of, or ephemeral flesh since angels are a form of ephemeral being.

                                                  I like the idea you've got here, but I think I would do it very differently. Just off the top of my head:

                                                  The Lazares
                                                  Element: Aether; Pyros is recovered by sleeping before a TV showing static, or to the sound of white noise, or beneath the aurora.
                                                  Humour: Cerebrospinal fluid, granted an appropriately mystic name; Swedenborg's "spirituous lymph" might suffice.
                                                  Torment: Paranoid schizophrenia, more or less. The Lazarus sees sinister patterns in everything, and percieves himself as the victim (or, perhaps, the hand) of conspiracy. Ominous hallucinations are common.
                                                  Disquiet: Similar to the Torment, in that it causes paranoia and the perception of omens. It's subtler, though (no hallucinations), and tends to seed seemingly inexplicable social groups in the affected community; all the local adulterers might form a clique, even though none of them know about the others' adultery.
                                                  Wasteland: Yours is very good, so I'm keeping it. I could see there being some minor temporal anomalies during the last stage, though.
                                                  Disfigurements: The Lazares are quasi-Unfleshed, so their disfigurements are the mechanisms (functional or otherwise) integrated into their flesh. Some are almost entirely mechanical, with only the CSF and maybe some token organic matter (like an eye, or teeth, or a whole hand) to separate them from the truly mechanical.
                                                  Bestowment: I'm thinking that it should be some kind of bonuses to perception checks, as well as Computer and Investigation. It would manifest as a weaker, beneficial form of their Torment, making them very aware of patterns and capable of surprisingly accurate leaps of logic.
