        Originally posted by MugaSofer View Post
        ...the New Dawn already erases huge chunks of your personality.
        Umm... That's like saying medicine for psychosis erases huge chunks of your personality. I'm going to go with a huge "no" with this one. New Dawn doesn't erase anything. It balances things out.

        What? No, you're no longer physically inconvenienced by the downsides of the Promethean condition.
        Prometheans were never really physically inconvienced by Disquiet, Wastelands or Torment. PtC is the Resistance game; the Created are survivors first and foremost. They're built to survive all these problems. Any Promethean can head to the Wastes and jsut chill there.

        The real issue, however, is the fact that these things pretty much screw up any chance of having a life of some kind. Want a relationship? Want to grow a garden? Want to not have a hair trigger temper or throw yourself at the first available person when you're in need of comfort? These aren't physical dangers, they never were. They're things that affect other people, or are a psychological imbalance.

        Literally the entire point of this thread is to provide alternatives to the New Dawn to fit the same narrative and mechanical role.
        And I'm not saying to not have alternatives. I'm saying that you're missing the entire point of the game. Don't want to be human, but something else? Sure, go for it. But you need to address the real issues and flaws that Prometheans have that make their lives complete and utter shit. You are missing those issues and leaving the Promethean's life to be crappy.

                I'm working on a mechanics/themes hack for Promethean 2e, and my concept for the New Dawn is that it's something very personal and with potentially infinite possibilities, but even rarer and more difficult than it is in canon.

                I'm going to have a list of possibilities, and establish that these aren't even the only ones. Examples would be: becoming human, changing lineage, becoming a different kind of supernatural creature, becoming a minor template, becoming something like a Promethean but different in some way, becoming a tree, becoming a beehive, becoming a bodiless data intelligence spread across the Internet, and so on. You'd need some connection to what you're trying to become- you can't just say "I want to be a Mage" if you haven't spent a lot of time studying the supernal, interacting with mages, and so on.

                There's also a rumor that if a Pandoran were to reach the New Dawn, they'd become a Promethean. And another that humans can reach a New Dawn too...

                                            That's still not addressing the fundamental problem; the existing Refinements are focused on studiying aspects of humanity/... so, tweaking those to become other forms of New Dawn? It's not going to work.

                                            I think the better idea here is to start from the end and work backwards. Figure out "what are the alternate things a Promethean could strive to turn into?" and then deduce backwards to find the things that could work right.

                                            Become another kind of monster, that's clearly tied into Argentum, and it works as it's a sensible end-goal. But what about trying to become an animal? Or to attain one-ness with one's inherent element(s)? What about trying to transcend this plane and find a new world - a permanent migration to the Astral, the Dreamscape, the Spirit World? What about attaining "harmony", retaining some (weakened) Promethean powers & general attributes but lessening the penalties, ala Mummies and their Apothesis?

                                            The New Dawn, ultimately, boils down to "become something other than the incomplete creation you are". For this project to succeed, we need to stop focusing on the journey and more on the destination; once we know what they can become, then we can worry about the mechanics of becoming.

                                                I think wyrdhamster might be onto something — only it's not the Refinements that matter; it's the Roles. And every one of the thirty existing Roles is either humanocentric or neutral. So what would be needed for a successful non-human New Dawn would be new Roles oriented toward a non-human goal. Grandfather Crow probably achieved his dream by discovering and mastering crow-oriented Roles for whichever Refinements he discovered, so that when he achieved his New Dawn he was able to build his new existence out of crow-oriented Roles. Unless he passed on the knowledge of the Roles he pioneered, they're probably lost; so a hypothetical Promethean who wants to become a crow will have to rediscover them hirself.

                                                This breaks the implied rule that there are exactly three Roles per Refinement. But I have no problem with that; I'm inclined to view the lists of Roles as indicative rather than comprehensive.

                                                And I think VoidDrifter also has a point: before you start devising Roles that are oriented toward alternate New Dawns, it's probably a good idea to have a sense of what alternatives exist.
