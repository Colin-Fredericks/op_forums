    I don't really have anything against Promethean as an Anti-Transhumanist game myself, even if I normally really like transhumanism in stories, probably because what really attracts me to transhumanism is the transcendence aspect of it. And Promethean has plenty of that.

    Outcasts shunned by society, children from disfunctional families… yeah, there’s no metaphoric horror there… :P
    I don't really think that you want to imply that the only response to being shunned by society (which can happen entirely out of society's faults) or coming from a dysfunctional family (which in no way is you fault) is to stop being what you are to fit in. Really, I think it's a terrible message and I'm really glad Promethean isn't about that.

    So please think of a better example.

            These are humans who are so terrified of death, infirmity, and ignorance that they have lost sight of love, fellowship and the value of mundane accomplishment. Bit by bit, they sacrifice their humanity for power and knowledge, until they have turned themselves into something monstrous.

            • Their problem is that they consider death etc to be too bad. They should be less afraid of death.
            • A "monstrous condition" - like that of the Created - is characterized by "power, longevity, and superlative durability". These are things all good people would leave behind, trading them for "simple pleasures".
            In sort, it implies that the tradeoff is a Good Thing for a Bad Thing - like sacrificing a baby to summon a world-destroying demon.

                    Originally posted by Ravraxas View Post
                    I don't really think that you want to imply that the only response to being shunned by society (which can happen entirely out of society's faults) or coming from a dysfunctional family (which in no way is you fault) is to stop being what you are to fit in. Really, I think it's a terrible message and I'm really glad Promethean isn't about that.
                    Well… yes and no. Promethean starts with the notion that the protagonist is, well, not broken per se, as that would imply that there was a time when he was better than he is now; but certainly incomplete, a being who is missing something that everyone else has and the lack of which results in the world turning against him. As well, this fault that causes people to feel uneasy around him is not the result of anything he did; it’s inherent in what he is. And as such, the only way he’s going to get rid of that unease and have a shot at being accepted by society is to change what he is — or, put in somewhat less inflammatory terms, to “grow up”.

                    So yeah; if you want to, you can read Promethean as “society shuns you because of what you are, and you’re only going to change that by changing yourself”. And if you want to, you can generalize that from the specifics of the Promethean condition to a general declaration about conformity being good. I don’t particularly want to, and I especially see the latter as an overgeneralization.

                    But more to the point, I wasn’t talking about what the moral of the Promethean story is; I was talking about where the human horror is; and the human horror is that it sucks to be the outsider, to never know love or acceptance.

                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                            So yeah; if you want to, you can read Promethean as “society shuns you because of what you are, and you’re only going to change that by changing yourself”. And if you want to, you can generalize that from the specifics of the Promethean condition to a general declaration about conformity being good. I don’t particularly want to, and I especially see the latter as an overgeneralization.
                            Everybody forgets about Torment.

                            I bring up pursuit of self-worth as an aspect of the Pilgrimage in no small part because my last reread of the line highlighted connections between the Promethean condition and those truisms about how people can smell self-loathing from a mile off, how the quality does not bring out the best in those around you for any length of time, and how getting out of a depression is something that requires conscious action on your part.

                            Calling Promethean assimilationist, as some have apparently done in the past, ignores that there's an entire core Refinement dedicated to self-reflection and retreat from society, that this same philosophy works to minimize the impact of the one drawback of carrying the Fire that doesn't simply make people and nature reject you, that that very drawback is the one that is unambiguously a problem with your character's emotional locus of control, and that a large part of the Refinement's common practices involve seeing that everything changes, that nothing lasts forever, that everything acts on everything else and changes.

                            Knowing that there are Created who think of themselves as "not completely human yet" makes it pretty clear that the Pilgrimage, once stripped of the trappings of artifice and vulcanism and thin veneers of normality, is very much a quest to be able to feel like a human being despite coming from an otherwise awful lot in life. That's part of why alchemy and the optimistic reading of the ending of Pandora's Box are so apropos - lead into gold, the New Dawn after the Saturnine Night, the power of the Transformative Will, Hope at the bottom of the box?

                            Fairly high up on Promethean's list of messages is the notion that even if you're having a long terrible night and you feel more like a horrible good-for-nothing monster than a person with rights and feelings and worth, as long as you pick up the pieces, move forward, and keep on making steps, no matter how small the progress, however long it takes, the sun will come out tomorrow and you will be there when it happens.

                            That is Mortality: this too shall pass.

If they can feel that they are incomplete, that would spur them on the Pilgrimage for sure. And it is this very incompleteness that somehow makes them truly gifted in terms of alchemy, capable of unholy feats no man can accomplish. I hope this gets played up more in 2e.

                                        There's irony, too, in how a Promethean is absolutely, 100% the Magnum Opus of their Demiurge and yet still know they are in some way unfinished.

                                        I do appreciate the inherent tragedy of Promethean; none of them can help that they are monsters. But to paraphrase what I said before, currently Prometheans are the whipping boy of the World of Darkness. I want them to actually earn some of that ire by doing more monstrous things to get by, or by having monstrous urges to complete themselves that even they don't understand. While it's true that The Monster didn't mean to kill that little girl, he did still kill that little girl. The Golem of Prague is deactivated because he may desecrate the Sabbath, or becomes violent. In A.I.: Artificial Intelligence David (Haley Joel Osment) is obsessed with his mother to the point of being disturbing.

                                        These motivations are far more interesting to me as sources of Disquiet than proximity to Prometheans, or Disfigurements.
