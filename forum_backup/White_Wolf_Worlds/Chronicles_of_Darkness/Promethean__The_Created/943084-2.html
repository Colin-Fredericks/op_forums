    Originally posted by Leliel View Post
    I don't see the association with "evil" and "mental illness" though. The Divine Fire's always been associated with madness, so it makes sense that people who toy with it, particularly in such a reckless fashion, are a little...off. But that's me (I go "Well, they tinker with neurochemistry when trying to enhance their minds, and they have no fucking clue what they're doing. Of course their sanity takes a beating, the idiots").
    Honestly, the very idea of "madness" is a little ableist. Obviously it isn't always when it's handled responsibly, but doing so requires a great deal of care and intentionality. It's one thing to say that Alchemists regularly deal with powers that are dangerous for the human mind and as a result often end up traumatized and/or mentally scarred. It's another thing to call all the Alchemists who don't murder perople for their spare parts "sane". Doing so implies by contrast that all the ones that do murder people for their spare parts are "insane", and paints a picture of the world where mentally healthy people are not murderous and mentally ill people are. Which apart from being untrue on both counts contributes to an ongoing stigma about mental illness, where people with visible mental illness are to be feared and people without visible mental illness are to be trusted. The RPG industry has a long history of stigmatizing mental illness, and in general CofD 2e has made great strides to remedy that. Particularly in products that David Hill has been involved in. But there are still lingering issues with the RPG idea of "madness" being perpetuated, particularly in Mage and Promethean.

                Originally posted by Azahul View Post
                An in-universe justification only goes so far. A writer has total control over what they write, so anything they write is subject to that control. Which means if they write a reason for their bad guys to all be insane, that doesn't alleviate responsibility for writing a group of bad guys as all being insane.

                I'm certainly not arguing with the themes used in the Alchemists. Reckless transhumanism is perfectly fine as the basis for a villain. The insistence that they're all a bit crazy though feels wrong when a villain can easily have rational, "ends justify the means" arguments for during abominable things. Do you take apart a dozen malformed would-be child-things in the hope that it prevents any child from dying ever again? There's a character you can make there with reasons for why he thinks this is a good idea, who can have a rational argument for his actions, who isn't automatically mentally ill. Still a character who, I would argue, is wrong in their actions, is certainly a villain.
                Okay, I see where you're coming from. And yes, "madness" can be a bit ableist as a concept-it's just easier to use one word than "maladaptive behaviors that may pose a risk to themselves and those around them, but may and likely often belong to an otherwise good or okay person." Which a compulsion to collecting dead bodies to imbue with life is generally an example of, but I digress.

                Still, it makes sense that they would develop bad Persistent Conditions, or that they already had them-the latter case is what drove them to insatiate alchemy, the former the inevitable aftereffects of toying with what you don't understand by sticking your ungloved hand into the the bright hot flickering thing that has signs saying "DANGER: APPROACH WITH CAUTION" all over it. I also meant to imply that madness was not something I was associating with "evil" but what happens when the inevitable burns manifest as brain damage, which was meant to include things like losing an arm, no longer being able to tolerate sunlight...one of a number of Pyros-induced disabilities that underline the question "was this worth it?" And as motives to overcome humanity's limitations to begin with in such an extreme way.

                (I fundamentally agree with the criticism that it increasingly seems like Matt has a difficult time writing sympathetic villains, or acknowledging the potential for sympathy-the whole subsection felt like a step in the right direction, but yeah, this has flaws in it).

                                            "I will see this person as an object as I vivisect them for scientific advancement" is pretty textbook insane. "I will strive to achieve this goal, even to the point of destruction of myself or everything I hold dear" is also textbook insanity. "I care only about my well being to the exclusion of everything and everyone else" is also another example of insanity. These are the viewpoints and attitudes you see in the Alchemists that pursue Prometheans as a means to an end. This is why they are considered insane while alchemists that wisely avoid Prometheans, the ones that know the dangers of interacting with Prometheans, are considered sane. I don't see one group of alchemists as being inherently "eeevil" and the other "bastions of moral goodness". But I sure do see one group as being more conscious of the repercussions associated with their actions and behavior, and that is very much an indicator of one's sanity or lack thereof. I don't see sanity being conflated with morality here the way some others do. If anything there seems to be a large dose of amorality on the alchemists' part.

                                            Insanity is measured by degrees, it isn't an all or nothing proposition, but the alchemists in the antagonist section seem to be the people who are well on the extreme negative end of that spectrum. These aren't people that would just like to be a little better physically and mentally than other people, these are people who are absolutely driven to cast aside humanity along with the weaknesses they believe it entails, and don't care about how they achieve that end. These antagonist alchemists are like heroin junkies who don't care about how they get their next fix, just that they get it. I've specifically dealt with people like that, they most certainly were not sane.

                                            ​So yes, you can count me in the camp of people who are just fine with the antagonist splat not being suitable player options straight out of the core rules. Focus on the Proetheans in the core book about Prometheans, leave playable antagonists for later projects, if ever.
