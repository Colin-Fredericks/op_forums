            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

            No... the text is actually fairly explicit that it's because the Alchemists getting the focus are supposed to be antagonists. Even with the various details on specific Insatiate Alchemists in the write up there are characters you can sympathize with (even some of the more monstrous ones).

            "Good" Alchemists aren't given a reason to interact with the PCs (and are actually given reasons to largely avoid the PCs) because that doesn't serve any narrative niche for the stories Promethean is aimed at creating. Having these Alchemists successfully seek out Prometheans to for working relations with them runs counter to a lot of the thematic elements of the game, so that's not something the base-line game encourages. Having them as antagonists doesn't really work if they're not actually antagonizing the PCs.

            The Insatiate Alchemists have a niche to fill as antagonists. So that's the focus the text takes.

            Why, exactly, is that a problem? After all this I still don't see why that choice, beyond personal preference, is bad.



            His rather large body of work (including a number of writing credits on CofD books dedicated to antagonists) would suggest this isn't the case. So it seems more appropriate to give him a bit more charity in the reading about how the editorial choice to make the antagonists in PtC 2e the way they are was for a design reason rather than some deficiency in his skills.

            Perhaps, for example, the focus is on things that want to kill Prometheans (and it isn't just the Alchemists, who don't really want to kill Prometheans as much as killing them is a side effect of what they really want) is because part of the core themes of the game are how the Created are outsiders that are supernaturally rejected by the world around them. That isolation and alienation is a large part of their drive to seek out the Great Pilgrimage.
            What about a good Alchemist who just really needs some juice from a Promethean so she can do something the audience would find important, like save her daughter? Or a good alchemist who just didn't get the memo that Prometheans are bad news and wants to study one, before falling to Disquiet? Why doesn't the text support telling those stories? I mean, the text itself is kind of confused, in that it goes on rants about how all 'Black' alchemists are evil, then presents a rather sympathetic Alchemist as a sample character.

            And relatively friendly Alchemists won't damage the themes of the book. After all, they'd still fall victim to disquiet, and start wanting to cut up the Promethean or whatever.
            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
            As a note on the entire "Matt can't write antagonists beyond 'these guys want to kill you' front", you are talking about the man who is responsible for every bit of information we have on fetches in Changeling the Lost.

            You might want to reconsider your position.
            And his Heroes and Alchemists have been completly devoid of what makes a good antagonist beyond 'they want to kill you.' So either he's always had trouble making good antagonists, and has thus far been moderated by other writers, or he's forgotten how to make good antagonists.
            Originally posted by blackhatmatt View Post


            Regardless of whether or not I know how to write antagonists (YMMV), alchemists were written before Heroes, and I didn't get either of them "wrong." You just don't like them.

            (But, as others point out, shockingly the alchemists in the antagonists section are meant to be antagonists. I can't remember, when VtR first came out, were there big threads complaining about how the book didn't support playing VII?)
            I find the first kind of hard to believe seeing as how the Alchemists got a sidebar of 'there are good alchemists, you'll just never see them,' just like heroes did. And the Heroes sidebar was in reaction to massive fan backlash. I'll just file this alongside the 'Beasts aren't meant to be a metaphor for minorities.'

            And alchemists and heroes both share the problem of having the text attempt to shove their evils down the readers throat, evils that aren't really borne out in a deeper reading, leading to increased confusion.

            And you do realize that you can have sympathetic antagonists, right? Like, the Seers, the Pure, Belials Brood, VII, even Loyalists can be sympathetic figures. In fact, being on some level sympathetic is part of what makes an antagonist good. However, you seem to fear that making them at all sympathetic will ruin the game or something. (And anyways, keep tilting at that windmill of people wanting playable alchemists. There's been like two people that have been saying that, and neither of them's been paticularly loud). The real problems with alchemists are how hard you try to make them unsympathetic, the ableism, and the fact that the text is rather confused as to wether or not alchemists can be sympathetic.

                                                        Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                                                        THERE IS NO "OTHER GAME" THAT USES THE THEMES OF ALCHEMY AND ARTIFIAL LIFE. We don't have somewhere else to go and discus what that game should be. Most of the ideas we would like to use for that game are already a part of this one. I can only speak for myself, but I sincerely mean no harm to a game that clearly means a lot to you. I wish you well, and I apologize for having derailed this thread; it was just that PenDragon had so eloquently described the issues I have with the game, namely it's focus being a bit TOO narrow for my very small gaming group.

                                                        So, with that in mind, and knowing that Promethean isn't for everyone, it would be nice if, when you see someone is working on a hack, you just walk away. The game you love will still be there, safe and sound and exactly as intended; we're just using some...leftover clay. An ember or two of stolen fire. To put it another way, think of us as trying to build our own creature, but we don't even have a proper lab or castle, and all the local graveyards are very well guarded. You are correct, we are NOT enemies, and I don't want to be. But please don't tell me to take anything of mine and burn it. My attitude, at least the one I hold in my heart, but may not convey well in text form, is one of wanting to work on a project in peace, but having seen far too many labs set ablaze. I really do think Onyx Path trapped lightning in a bottle, and I want some of that lightning for myself.
                                                        I said I was out of this thread, but I do feel a need to come back and echo Mangle for a moment: Can people stop telling posters who want to change a game or disagree with some core concept to "find another game"? It's condescending and, frankly, only serves to make people double down on their opinions. I know the intentions are good, but to someone who is displeased with all or part of a book it comes off more like, "Your ideas are wrong, go away".

                                                        Shifting systems isn't always the easy sideways shuffle its made out to be. For starters there are costs, finding a new game can require a lot of time, money and effort. And, in all likelihood, the same amount of hacking it into desired shape as one would have to put into Promethean (or whatever game they take issue with). In the end there might not even be another game that comes closer to the desired intent, and if there is it might be strictly inferior in quality. Onyx Path sets a pretty high bar which can be hard for other games to match. Then there is the matter of teaching a group (and oneself) a brand new system and learning all the little quirks and traits not immediately apparent in the system. Anyone who has ever GM'd a less than system savvy group will not want to go through that process too often.

                                                        Individually, there may also be the matter of world continuity. I, for instance, run all my ChroD games in a shared universe, which makes switching games functionally impossible (or at least a heap-load of work). I suspect I'm not alone in that, and that many theme hackers want to maintain their own vision of an overarching world of darkness. And that's a good thing, I don't think ChroD would be half as successful if players only ever stuck to one line.

                                                        Theme hacking comes from a place of love. A person does not delve that deep into a game they couldn't give two shits about. What sort of love can vary, perhaps its a love of the Chronicles of Darkness as a whole or maybe just some small facet of a splat that snagged the heart strings. Telling someone to abandon that thing they love, that they are wrong, that it wasn't made for them...what can you honestly expect to happen? Are they going to say "oh, I get it" and walk away? No, you'll be met with bitterness and staunch resolution, which helps no one.

                                                        So please, you don't have to acquiesce to every complaint and assertion, just don't tell posters to find another game.

                                                        That's all.
