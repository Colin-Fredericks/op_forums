            Originally posted by eberg View Post
            Why do Prometheans have to be a metaphor for something? Why can't they just be a particular type of creature inspired by created beings in literature and legend, put into a game designed to explore themes of isolation and what it means to be human? I understand that some people may identify personally with certain themes or project their own meaning onto aspects of the game and that's great, but it is a mistake to assume your interpretation of the game was intended by the designers. I can't speak for the other writers, but the parts I wrote weren't intended to reflect any real-world groups or issues, they were entirely written to serve the structure and themes of the game as laid out by the developer. Conversations with other writers never discussed parallels or metaphors, they were concerned with things like internal consistency, rewarding game play, clarity of writing, and mechanical cohesion. The rules and mechanics of the game are to serve the purpose of the game. You may disagree with how effectively they do so, but treating them as commentary on some aspect of society is a mistake.
            Death of the author, man.

            And actually, that's fine. This kind of discussion - including what Promethean might or might not mean in metaphorical or interpretative terms - is totally fine. While I don't tend to interpret the Pilgrimage one way or another across the board in my own games, I've certainly seen (and deliberately drawn) parallels between the difficulties that the Created have with human interaction and the difficulties that folks on the autism spectrum have with social interaction (subject to comfort discussions with players, obviously).

            That doesn't mean I think that Promethean is a metaphor for autism and learning to assimilate. I do think that what a couple of people have said in this thread - that the Pilgrimage is about crafting a place for yourself in the world and being able to accept yourself, and for the world to accept you - is pretty apt.

            'Cause let's flip this just a sec. I'm sure we all know folks who are aggressive, arrogant, know-it-all assholes. I was one for a long time. Not only did I not cultivate empathy, I just didn't see the point. It would simplistic to say "I grew up," but if I'm being honest, that's pretty much what happened. But it's not like that happened all at once, and it's not like there was one formative moment that did it. There were a number of formative moments, many of which were intensely personal and make no sense out of context, that created the person I am today, and it's a person I'm pretty happy with.

            (The punch line, of course, is that as people our "Pilgrimage" never stops - you never stop learning, you never stop growing, or if you do, that's not good. Prometheans, in that way, have it a little easier; their story ends when they reach the New Dawn, or at least becomes a radically different story.)

            So, it's not true to say that Promethean is deliberately a metaphor for anything. It's a metaphor for what you need it to be. I would humbly submit that if what you want is a game where you start as a supernatural creature and become a robotic spider, this isn't the game for you. And that's totally fine! I don't like D&D, but I don't hang around the WotC forums telling people that I think their game about killing goblins for gold pieces would be awesomer if they introduced some kind of mechanic for semaphore.

Especially now, when the New Dawn changes world to accommodate the identity the Promethean has built/discovered.

To me, ChroD lines are primarily about exploring monster archetypes, and metaphors and parallels are used to enhance that- they come second, not first. Now, I obviously don't agree with some of the ways ChroD has handled those explorations, but I consider it to be a good guiding principle, and I agree with Eric on that.

                            For example, I consider Demon to be the best ChroD line for SJ/transition/disability readings, but that's because I know it wasn't deliberately constructed for that. If the game had basically been saying "demons are just like gay/trans/disabled people! It's a metaphor!" it wouldn't have worked, but it works because the theme of establishing and fighting for your own identity emerges organically from the exploration of the archetype.
