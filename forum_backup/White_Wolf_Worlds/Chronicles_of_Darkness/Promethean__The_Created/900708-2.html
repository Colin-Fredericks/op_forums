                        Originally posted by MCN View Post
                        For some people, its not. The standard CofD depictation of mortals comes off as mentally fragile - see something supernatural, you break. There are a million things out there that prey on you. By nature, you have to live in denial, be short sighted, and have no free will. Its a pretty cynical take on humanity, and a lot of people react to that depictation as being an undesirable state of being. And I have to agree - being one of the sheeple is an undesirable state of being. Humanism caries certain assumptions that becoming one of the rank and file of CofD mortals doesn't fulfill. Don't forget that we're supposed to invest in these characters - mentally, emotionally, chonologically. We're supposed to empathize with them. I find the idea of being willfully ignorant and somehow losing memories of my original state of being to be something I can't empathize with.
                        Note that this wasn't so much of a problem in the first edition, when Integrity breaking points for witnessing the supernatural weren't a thing and Lunacy and the Sleeping Curse weren't supercharged, armor-piercing extensions thereof. That is, humans in the nWoD occasionally had a few problems with the supernatural; but it was nothing compared to what they face in ChroD. It's one of two points of contention that I have with the ChroD developers (the other one being the segregation of the various ephemeral Twilights).

                        That said, the Promethean designer's position on this, to my knowledge, has always been that the goal isn't bog-normal humans, but rather cream-of-the-crop humans. With that in mind, my patch-job while waiting for 2e to come out is to restructure the Final Azoth Roll so that amnesia is a failure result (total amnesia for a botch, filtering memories through a “mundanity lens” for a regular failure*) and immunity to “witness the supernatural” breaking points and all anti-mortal agency-robbing effects (Lunacy, Sleeping Curse, Disquiet, Sybaris, etc.) is a result of a normal success (with an Exceptional Success granting a suitable Redeemed Boon). That is, a successful Redemption results in a human who's made of sterner stuff than your run-of-the-mill ChroD mortal, and doesn't instinctively shrink away when the darkness intrudes.

                        * “mundanity lens”: you remember encounters with the supernatural in general, but reinterpret them as much as possible in mundane terms. It's what Mage's Sleeping Curse imposes on all Sleepers whenever they witness obvious magic. For the Redeemed who fail their Final Azoth Roll, this results in a sort of swiss-cheese memory: the broad strokes of their former life is still there, and they'll recognize people and places they encountered back then; but they'll forget that they used to be monsters, and will misremember scenes where Transmutations were used, etc.

If someone wants to play Promethean with a different spin, more power to them. Who are you to tell them what is or is not the right way to have fun with a game?
                        You'll note that I didn't enter this conversation until after someone started tossing around accusations likening the “you're on fire” camp to mobs with torches and pitchforks. He has since apologized, so I will say no more on the subject — just, this isn't “the innocent visionaries who just want the freedom to do things their own way are being suppressed by those dastardly purist elitists”.

                            If I were to use transhumanism as a theme, I'd do it via a Centimanus with good intentions and strange ideas who is searching for the New Dusk. Make of that term what you will.

                            Besides, it's not like humanism is a restrictive theme. Anything that people have valued immensely over the course of history is fair game. You don't have to find your answers only in the past, either. Prometheans can learn from visions and divine intercessions just as much as the journals and footprints of their ancestors.

                            There is just so much you can explore. And no one said that a Promethean who becomes human has to be average or conform to the local definition of normalcy. They can be revolutionary and remembered for centuries, which is something they cannot achieve as disjointed alchemical forges.

                            And no, Prometheans are not a metaphor for this or that marginalized group in real life. To make them so would be an oversimplification that can easily result in unfortunate implications. Any sort of isolation can become the game's focus, be it systemic or individual, cultural or spiritual, you name it. That's the beauty of it: taking all these disparate elements and combining them into a journey that speaks to you.
