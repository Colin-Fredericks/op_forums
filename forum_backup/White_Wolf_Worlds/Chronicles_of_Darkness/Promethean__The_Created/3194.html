    HazMat Lineages

    11-05-2013, 01:45 AM
    (Salvaged from the old forums)

    One idea that I've wanted to explore for some time now is that of "HazMat Lineages": a trio of Promethean Lineages that are inherently anathema to life. The first of these, and the original inspiration for the idea, are the Nuclear Prometheans described in Saturnine Night. They would be joined by two other varieties: the plague-bearers (i.e., "Biological Prometheans") and the toxic or "Chemical Prometheans". As I envision it, each write-up would be modeled after the "Destroyer of Worlds" chapter from Saturnine Night:
    • Generic rules for plagues and toxins, respectively. The plague rules should expand on the basic disease rules to include the tendency of diseases to spread (thus “plagues”); the toxin rules should build on the rules for poisons and drugs, including a mechanic for developing tolerances and dependencies: Chemical Prometheans should be as much about the abuse of harmful chemicals, which includes drugs every bit as much as cyanide.
    • A Lineage write-up.
    • The Promethean Condition, as modified to reflect the variety of hazardous material with which the Lineage is associated. What replaces Electricity as a means of healing? (Note that the aforementioned generic rules for plagues and toxins can come in handy here.) What about Torment, Wasteland, and Disquiet? What kinds of Milestones might be appropriate for them? Like the Zeka, their Biological and Chemical counterparts should have a tendency toward the Refinement of Flux rather than Stannum.
    • Athanors. Not strictly necessary, as it's unlikely that there have been any HazMat Prometheans who have developed one.
    • Bestowments. Re-Animator and Radioactive Affinity ought to be easily adapted to conform to the alternate hazardous materials.
    • Transmutations. The Zeka get a set of Irradiation Transmutations. Their biological counterparts should get a set of Infection Transmutations, and their chemical counterparts should get a set of Intoxication Transmutations. Infection and Intoxication Transmutations can leverage the aforementioned plague and toxin rules the way Irradiation Transmutations leverage the Radiation rules.
    • Mockeries. HazMat Prometheans are bad enough; what are their Pandoran counterparts like?
    This idea was originally triggered by the “Prodigal Demiurge” section in Magnum Opus, where it was describing how the various Lineages might correlate to the seven deadly sins; in particular, the entry on Lust mentioned that there might be an as-yet-uncreated seventh Lineage that's based on communicable diseases. The Biological Prometheans were originally conceived to be this “seventh Lineage”, the creation of which would likely have Apocalyptic results. In a setting built around this concept, the Chemical Prometheans have no place — but then, neither do Dream-born, Extempore, or Unfleshed.

    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                  Originally posted by Verge
                  My main point is that Nukes are emblematic of the Divine Fire, whereas Toxins and Plagues, while notable for their influence on human history, are best known for being causes of death and sickness. That's not a bash on the concept, but I'd love to find some way to elevate Bio/Chem to that same level of awesome terrifying magnificence.
                  Ah. Well, they're not going to. At least, not in terms of raw power. Toxins and plagues are more insidious threats than nukes are, and that's why these Lineages aren't as visible as their Nuclear counterparts: to use a military analogy, nukes are berserkers; plagues and toxins are assassins. Both will kill you; the difference is that you'll see the first one coming.

                  Actually, plagues do have a lot of potential here: unlike nukes and toxins, they spread. The expanded Disease rules need something to reflect the fact that diseases can be contagious, and that you can get full-blown epidemics. You don't even get that with nukes; at worst, you get a place that kills anyone who visits it — but that's inherently limited by geography: don't visit the place, and you won't experience any radiation poisoning. Toxins also have a rather nasty aspect to them that's not covered in the existing rules — or rather, drugs do: namely, addiction.

                  In terms of rules, here are some thoughts:
                  • Drugs should have vectors just like toxins do, reflecting how they're administered.
                  • Diseases and toxins should potentially have additional effects similar to drugs ("symptoms" for short) instead of or in addition to damage. In fact, a drug should be a toxin that inflicts symptoms instead of damage (effective Toxicity 0).
                  • Diseases and many toxins should bear a dual rating: how deadly they are (Toxicity for toxins; Virulence for diseases?), and how quickly they act (Intensity). Much of this can be mapped to existing rules; but this should provide a unified terminology, and further draws parallels to radiation poisoning.
                  • Drugs and toxins should also have a duration: how many rolls you can make before it has run its course. Diseases generally shouldn't: part of what makes them scary is that they usually don't go away on their own.
                  • Diseases should have rules for contagion; these rules should include giving diseases toxin-like vectors.
                  • Drugs/Toxins should have rules for tolerance and addiction (flip sides of the same coin). Tolerance protects you from the toxicity and/or symptoms, but it generally comes with an addiction that inflicts symptoms if you don't take the drug or toxin. It should be difficult or impossible to build up a tolerance without first becoming addicted.

                  Originally posted by Verge
                  I'd also want to avoid absolutes in the Plaguebearer origins. Extempore is a toolkit origin, so let's not rely on it solely; keep it so a ST who wants Plagueboys in his chronicle doesn't have to allow for Extempore if he doesn't want to. It could easily be a more likely proposition, but a mythical demiurge still cements the Lineage to core understandings of the game. It's only necessary that a demiurge sees potential Generation in using disease, or diseased conditions, to create life. And we aren't talking about viruses or bacteria either: before the pathogenic theory of medicine, numerous explanations for disease existed. Miasma theory, for example, says dirty conditions lead to disease, and ties quite nicely to ideas from spontaneous generation, through primordial slime, to modern theories of abiogenesis. What if a pre-Aristotelian like Anaximenes or Empedocles put their theories to the test, supposing that sunlight and swampiness could give life to a corpse, not realizing they were coalescing Pestilence into a malevolent being?
                  OK. I'd just like to keep it such that it's entirely possible that a Plague Lineage doesn't exist at all yet, to allow for the inclusion of an adventure centered around its creation (or the avoidance thereof). More than most, their fluff needs to be heavily dosed with "these guys very likely don't exist; but if they did, this is what they'd be like." If done right, this can be done in a way that the writer is implying that they do exist, but are so scary that he can't bring himself to acknowledge that they do. The ultimate bogeyman.

                                  Originally posted by Verge
                                  The earliest draft of an expanded disease system. The Infection Trans will probably rely heavily on Symptoms.
                                  Expanded Disease and Toxin Rules Diseases have the following traits.

                                  Virulence describes the ease with which a disease spreads from person to person. A disease’s virulence is rolled whenever a potential victim is exposed to the disease, so long as the circumstances allow the disease to spread. For example, a blood-borne disease is not capable of spreading unless an infected host’s blood is introduced into the blood of a healthy candidate, despite having a virulence of 5, while the common cold might rampage through a kindergarten. A disease without a virulence trait is not contagious, owing to causes that are not pathogens.

                                  Morbidity describes both how quickly the disease spreads through its host and how deeply it incapacitates infected victims. At the end of the period of time described by the disease’s Progression, an infected character takes an amount of bashing damage equal to the disease’s Morbidity rating. Until the disease is cured or remits, the character does not naturally heal bashing damage if it would drop him below the Morbidity rating. For example, if a character has a disease with Morbidity 3, he won’t heal bashing damage in the first three boxes of his damage track. Morbidity is also used for determining how severe a given symptom’s effects might be.

                                  Progression is a rough interval of time that describes how long it takes for an infection to become symptomatic. A disease with a Progression of 3 hours would be a very quick example, while anything measured in more than a day would be considered slow. At the end of a Progression, the disease inflicts symptoms and Morbidity damage, and the host may make Stamina + Resolve rolls to overcome the disease.

                                  Recovery or Remission reflects the difficulty in beating a disease in terms of successes. Ailments with a Recovery trait are cured once the target number has been reached, and will have no further effect on the character. Diseases with a Remission trait are incurable, but they go into remission and have no further immediate effect, but chronically flare up again. If a Remission trait has a time period listed, the Storyteller may choose to afflict the character again once that time has passed.

                                  Symptoms are how the disease presents itself, as it alters cellular function and agitates the body’s immune system. These are highly varied, and many are often comorbid with one another. Most illnesses have more than the handful of symptoms described in the following entries, but for ease of use, they have been reduced to a number that does not exceed the disease’s Morbidity score. Symptoms with a number don’t present until the character has taken that much damage from the disease, or suffered through that many Progressions. Symptoms use the disease’s Morbidity as a penalty for affected dice pools.
                                  • Ataxia: the character suffers a penalty to Defense, Initiative, and Speed.
                                  • Confusion: the character suffers a penalty for Intelligence- and Wits-based rolls.
                                  • Cough: the character suffers a penalty to Stamina and Stealth rolls.
                                  • Cramps: the character suffers a penalty to Strength and Dexterity rolls.
                                  • Dyspnea: whenever the character exerts himself physically, he takes a -2 penalty to all physical rolls for a number of turns equal to the disease’s Morbidity.
                                  • Edema: the character takes a penalty to Dexterity and Stamina rolls.
                                  • Fever: the Morbidity of the disease is doubled for penalty purposes;

                                  • Soreness: when health boxes filled with damage exceed his Stamina score, the character takes a -1 penalty to all actions. This penalty is not cumulative with other damage penalties.

Let's see: "Injection", "Ingestion", "Inhalation", "Touch"; "Aerosol", "Subcutaneous", "Gastric", "Dermal". How else might the vectors be named? The basic rules for disease state that a given disease causes bashing or lethal damage (and possibly aggravated, if supernatural in nature); as such, Morbidity should probably be likewise qualified by type of damage inflicted. Either that, or use the Symptoms to note this (i.e., "Deadly" for lethal damage; "Insidious" for aggravated damage). My inclination would be toward the former approach.

                                      1. Exposure. Roll the disease's "Virulence" dots vs. the target's Stamina + Resolve. If the Virulence is equal or higher, the target is infected.
                                      2. Onset. The disease's incubation period ends, and the host takes bashing damage equal to the disease's Morbidity dots, and the symptoms take effect. The host may make a Stamina + Resolve roll to reduce the damage by one box or forestall a symptom of the Storyteller's choice per success. Alternately, successes may be accumulated towards Recovery or Remission.
                                      3. Progression: At the end of each stage of Progression, the host takes bashing damage equal to the disease's Morbidity dots. The host may make a Stamina + Resolve roll to reduce the damage by one box or forestall a symptom of the Storyteller's choice per success. Alternately, successes may be accumulated towards Recovery or Remission.

At the storyteller's discretion, there may be lasting effects from the character's battle with the illness, especially if the disease managed to inflict any aggravated damage, or lethal in excess of the character's Stamina. Dramatic failures, prolonged recoveries, or a high Morbidity may also justify long-term effects.

However, a dramatic failure on either the patient's or the caretaker's roll (or both) allows the disease to inflict additional, unmitigated damage as though an interval or Progression just occurred.
                                      A line from the beginning of the Asylum section: doctors don't heal you; they just remove obstacles to you healing yourself. A patient who's spending his successes fighting off the damage and symptoms of a disease isn't going to have much left over to devote toward Recovery. Treatment never gives you more successes on Recovery; but it frees you up to use more of your own successes in that manner. Make the Treatment roll before the Progression occurs (at a penalty based on how poorly understood the disease is), and allocate its successes to fight Symptoms and damage at the doctor's discretion; then resolve the Progression.
