    Demon/Promethean crossover theory

    02-25-2016, 02:35 PM
    So, I've been working on this for a while, and I should probably come back to it after 2e comes out, but here's a little God-Machine-inclusive theory about where the Divine Fire comes from.

    So, as it turns out, the "god" that the Divine Fire was stolen from was actually the God-Machine, and it wasn't so much stolen as "illegally downloaded and tampered with". See, a Demon or group of Demons waaaaaay back in the ancient reaches of antiquity managed to corrupt some of the God-Machine's Aether.

    They essentially turned it into a self-replicating angel virus that acts by increasing anthropomorphic tendencies, with the goal of pushing infected angels towards falling. The God-Machine was essentially forced to do a full product recall, modifying its angels to "vaccinate" them and wipe out the "stolen fire" virus.

    But this piece of corrupted divine machinery had begun to act like an actual virus- it mutated and jumped hosts in an effort to preserve itself. The mutated strain couldn't effect "vaccinated" angels, but it could potentially effect anything unliving that had the barest anthropic potential- at first, it could only infect objects with existing human architecture- meaning that it could effectively reanimate the dead. Another mutant strain, however, soon emerged that could effect entirely nonliving matter, provided that some sort of "spark" designated it as having the potential to be "awakened" (by spark, I mean a sort of designator- like a statue being built in a human shape and having humanity "imprinted" on it by a shaman, or an artificial intelligence designed to mimic humanity).

    The God Machine originally waged an aggressive campaign to wipe out this "divine fire", but found it impossible- or calculated that it was an unwise allocation of resources. The virus was too flighty to be pinned down, and it was devilishly hard to detect. Not only that, but the crusade gave Demon Rings plenty of chances to trick and trap the machine's agents, as well as assaulting Infrastructure left unguarded. The Machine also realized that some of the infected eventually became completely human, effectively neutralizing their threat potential. Eventually, the Machine ended its attempt to wipe out the Divine Fire- though some believe it did leave one protective mechanism: Disquiet. A passive, omnipresent defense against the spread of the virus, and one that would limit the potential of the infected as well as providing incentive for them to become human.

      1- It continues the infamous "GM metaplot", whereas everything is linked to the GM, created by it, or at war with it. In a Demon game, it's fine. Not so much in a Promethean one.

      4- It turns the Principle into... a mission to destroy the GM? Their shared code? A side GM? I didn't quite understand that part.

          Agreed with Magnum Opus. Mind you, Promethean has a history with the God-Machine, being the first gameline to publish a chapter featuring an encounter with the GM; so I don't have a problem with some sort of link being established between the God-Machine and the Principle. But not a causal relationship, with the GM being the source of the Divine Fire.

          The theory I'm more inclined to go with is a variant of the “Divine Demiurge” option that was presented in Magnum Opus*: there was a war in Heaven, and the loser was cast down to Earth where it became the god of this world. That's what the GM is: more “devil” or “Gnostic Demiurge” than “God”.

          The Principle, meanwhile, is the Creator and the Destroyer in one. It is not of this Earth the way the God-Machine is, but intrudes into this world for Its own inscrutable reasons. If you buy the notion of the Empyrean briefly mentioned in Inferno and Imperial Mysteries, that is the likely abode of the Principle. If you want to juxtapose Promethean against the God-Machine, I'd look more toward the New Dawn: every time a Promethean Redeems, a human enters the world who is not entangled in the GM's machinations, and who has the potential to serve as a monkey wrench in the Machine's gears just by being there.

          Of course, I also tend to think that the Redeemed ought to be presented as being among the best humanity has to offer, sort of like how a naturalized citizen tends to be far more informed and patriotic than a natural-born citizen because the former had to struggle to achieve what the latter inherited. In a similar way, a Redeemed human has the benefit of all the experience she gained during her Pilgrimage, and a much deeper appreciation of the human condition — which, getting back to the main topic at hand, makes her a natural fit as an advocate for human agency. I'm hoping that Promethean 2e does a better job in this regard than 1e did. But that's a topic for another thread.

          * The book, not the poster; that would just be wrong…

          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                  The Principle isn't benevolent in a traditional sense. It seems fond of humanity, and considers the development of human souls via the Pilgrimage to be worth its utter devotion, but that doesn't make it benevolent. We can't know what the Principle thinks, qashmallim are completely useless in trying to understand it in any way. The Principle transforms, breaks down, brings things together, all those things. I'm personally quite fond of the idea of the GM trying to uphold the status quo in the face of the Principle's constant change. The Principle doesn't even have any measurable goals like the GM does. As much as the GM is a vast eldritch thing, the Principle is totally ineffable. Both are basically untouchable to Mortal and supernatural interference barring extenuating circumstances.

                                  But, for the sake of discussion, let's say the Principle actually is benevolent- that it cares about humans. In a Biblical sense, it loves humanity. Not individuals, but humanity- perhaps it can barely even comprehend individuality. If each qashmal is a 'thought' of the Principle, perhaps it sees each human as a thought of humanity as a collective entity, the thing it is in love with. Pyros is sentient, it remembers, and the Pilgrimage is teaching it what humans are like, showing it how to enter a relationship with humanity.

                                  Only problem is, it can't touch the world without burning it, but that's alright. Humans are changing. Every Promethean teaches it something about how to interact with humanity as Pyros is used and Vitriol accrued. Every New Dawn starts to prepare humanity for the touch of the Divine Fire. Maybe, one day, the Principle will be able to show how much it loves us.
