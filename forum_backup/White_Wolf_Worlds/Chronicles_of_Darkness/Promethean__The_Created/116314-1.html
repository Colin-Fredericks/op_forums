        I've always been of the mind that to the general WOD population, and to the Mages especially, the Prometheans always read as "other". They don't come across completely as alive or dead, as real or not real, or as this or that. I always toyed with the idea that (and I know this would be totally abhorrent to most Mage players) the reason Supernal magics can't replicate or detect Prometheans and Divine Fire is because there is no Supernal version of them. They exist so completely outside the scope of such things that the Supernal simply cannot deal with them.

        As has been said above, the "weirdness", "otherness", and "WTF-ness" is part of the game and anything to ratchet that up is only a positive to me.

                    My preferred take is that the Principle and Pyros come from outside Mage’s Supernal/Phenomenal dynamic entirely. Others have suggested the Lower Depths as its origin (a world that Mage describes as being to the Fallen World as the Fallen is to the Supernal); were I to try to tie Prometheans into Mage’s cosmology, I’d go the other way:
                    The Empyrean
                    Confronted with a mutable Supernal, archmasters postulate that there must be an eternal, immutable causal plane beyond it: a Heaven, Nirvana, or Principle that fuels the Supernal with raw, undifferentiated meaning. Conservatives say that such a theory is unnecessary, given that the Supernal cannot be accurately perceived by any means short of Ascension, and may be truly pure under some veil of illusion. It is even said that some paths to Ascension bypass the Supernal and bring mages in accord with this true World Above — an idea Seekers invested in the Golden Road treat with disdain or curiosity.
                    — Imperial Mysteries, page 53
                    This is another “off the edge of the map” thing, where mages (or, in this case, archmasters) are giving a name to something and then pretending that this means that they understand it: “the Lower Depths are to the Fallen World as the Fallen World is to the Supernal World; and the Empyrean is to the Supernal World as the Supernal World is to the Fallen World” — whatever that means. Heck, archmasters aren’t even in agreement as to whether or not the Empyrean exists; in practice, the term is effectively shorthand for “something that’s not Supernal, Abyssal, Phenomenal/Fallen, or even of the mostly unknown Lower Depths”.

                        Whoever suggested The Lower Depths must not have read Promethean: The Created. Pyros and Azoth is described as pure adulterated force of inspiration, creation and brilliance. Something is fundamentally vibrant, that even to leave it just laying around will cause spontaneous creation of something, be it a storm of fire or a bunny. Azoth can spark life, animation and sentience back into dead flesh something that (to my knowledge anyway) the Life and Death Arcana, even combined, cannot truely do. If we look at the things thats been confirmed to be connected to The Lower Depths, we're dealing with things like The Decay from summoners, a being whose very pressence crumbles the world around it to dust, and who you feel on an instinctual level is just wrong.
                        We also have the sinful, cruel and selfish demons from Inferno, which i hardly think requires reasoning why they are bad candidates for Azoth-things.

                        I could understand why you'de have come to that conclusion about Flux though. And in my opinion, that would make a cool contrast, with Azoth being the sorta "Ultimate source of Good-Creation-Brightness energy" and Flux being the sorta "Ultimate source of Bad-Destruction-Darkness energy". Then Azoth would ebb from the sorta Supernal.^2 World, where as Flux would ebb from the sorta Lower.^2 Depths. That would put the "Fallen" World in a curious middle ground, where the forceses of Good and Bad are balanced inherently, and only our own choises define wich one takes hold pressently, which again would play into Prometheans main themes of philosophy and "Blah-Blah-We-All-Know-This-Song".

                        Generally, I am actually against pinning any definitive conclusions on the nature of Azoth and Flux. I think a good deal of their punch is that they're so mystical, and so powerful, and that the Prometheans dont really care about how powerful or mystical it is. To them, its just the force thats going to make them human once again, and liberate them from their tormentet existance. Thats all they need to know.

                            First off, I would recommend abandoning the notion that Supernal-Fallen-Depths form a moral hierarchy; they don’t. An ongoing argument that DaveB has been making is that the Supernal and Phenomenal are two interdependant halves of a whole, that neither could meaningfully exist without the other, and that neither is fundamentally “better” than the other.

                            Second, Flux is Pyros; though Pyros is only sometimes Flux. What Flux contrasts with is Elpis. And Elpis/Flux isn’t always a good/bad distinction, either: Pandora’s Book includes a Lilithim who frees a Promethean from torture at the hands of what amounts to Ashwood Abbey and then proceeds to punish the torturers; and Elpis arguably also includes “improvements” with nasty unintended consequences.

                                    Imperial Mysteries. It’s a less disparaging term for what is more commonly called the Fallen World. As for the demons of the Inferno: yes, they’re inherently sinful and selfish. But that’s arguably because the thing they’re missing is morals. And the Inferno is merely a tiny corner of the Lower Depths: the common feature of all of its denizens is that they’re lacking something that the Phenomenal World has, and that the only way to fill that void it to take what they’re lacking from the Phenomenal.

                                    And besides, there are plenty of beings native to the Phenomenal World who are every bit as inherently sinful and selfish as the Inferno’s demons. And the closest thing to an official answer about the “location” of Changeling’s Arcadia places it in a remote region of the Supernal World (Imperial Mysteries again; look up the Old Gods of Thistle); the Gentry are not what I’d call pillars of morality. Indeed, Old Gods in general don’t seem to have much in the way of moral superiority; nor do the Exarchs who deposed them. The only known regions of the Supernal that might safely be characterized as having or facilitating morally good character are the Watchtower Realms.
