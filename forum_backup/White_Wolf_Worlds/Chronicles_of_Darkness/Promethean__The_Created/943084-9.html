    Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post

    Yep! And this is a horror game, so I'm completely fine with the pursuit of power and immortality turning you into a horrible monster.

    It's just that this is a game about being monsters, and as horrible as the Ordo Dracul they're not just fully playable but incredibly sympathetic. In fact, you could make a case that they're the "most lesser evil" among the Covenants, in that their end goal would be the most beneficial to humanity. That doesn't make them not monsters, but it makes them twisted vampire mad scientists that someone could look at and think, "this is a shitty situation and these guys are making the best of it".

    Because that's the other side of "it's a horror setting". Things kind of suck. You could die or be hollowed out by magic rats at any moment through no fault of your own. You're a weak, squishy, ignorant nobody. "Evening the odds" has been brought up before, but it bears repeating. ChroD is a dark, scary setting, and the games hammer home that you often have to do things you'll regret to survive. It isn't just the monsters- Hunters can do horrific things, break law after law, and modify themselves into something inhuman, but the alternative is to just lie down and be complacent cattle for the monsters.

    That, to me, is the best way to look at alchemists sympathetically while also keeping them twisted and monstrous.

    They are the ones who didn't lie down.

    Hunters are the people who saw monsters preying on humanity, and didn't lie down. Alchemists saw diseases, aging, and death preying on humanity, and didn't lie down. Both of them work with powers they don't understand and do things that are hard to justify, but the alternative is, well, death. The Hunter says she refuses to let some Draugr drink her or her friends dry. The alchemist says she refuses to let cancer or aging or an accident bring her or her friends down. Both light a candle. They may burn out. Their fire may burn out of control. In fact, those things are probably likely. But without them, darkness is all there would be.
    It helps (from the Alchemist's perspective) that their preys are walking blights who can turn a normal city into Fallout Las Vegas given enough time.

            Excellent points, ArcaneArts. I think we're more or less in the same boat, my issues just lie in a few specific instances where it seems like an attempt to give breathing room for the "my daughter needs your Vitriol and I won't take no for an answer" Alchemists was made, and then self-sabotaged.

            The sidebar almost opens the door for a broader spectrum of Alchemists, but instead of saying "there's a lot of Alchemists out there, but the ones best suited to a Promethean game are the really fucked up ones, so that's what we're going to focus on here," it says "there are sane alchemists and there are fucked up alchemists. This section is about the latter because they're the ones best suited to a Promethean game." It's a subtle difference, but the former implies that there is a spectrum that's not being fully explored while the latter implies that there is only one option that isn't being explored. It sets out to expand options and ends up restricting them.

            The Alchemists and their Prey section almost explored the possibility of Alchemists who start out saying "DON'T TEMPT ME, FRODO" but over the course of the game get to the point where they're saying "IT COULD'VE BEEN MINE. IT SHOULD'VE BEEN MINE!" But what it presents as a good example of that is an Alchemest who has been saying "If I could but borrow the ring..." all along. It sets out to point out that the road to insatiety (thank you for pointing that out, by the way) is paved with good intentions, and instead says that even Alchemists who look like they might have good intentions are actually just lying.

            It's like... So close to presenting great, on-theme antagonists while leaving the door open for a different take down the road that the places where it falls short of that and undermines itself are all the more frustrating. Where you see "unsympathetic enough to make note of, but not enough to care about", I see "close enough to sympathetic to care that it's falling short of its potential."

                                The monster wanted to belong in a world that could not accept him-that simply was not ready for him...or was it?

                                I wonder, sometimes, how the monster might have faired in today's world. The book frames his problems as primarily being grotesque, and it makes me wonder what kind of a scientist would harbor revulsion long for their creation before the joy of the impossible came back in, before the exultation came flowing. Certainly, in today's world, with our X-Mens and Inuyashas and our civil rights movements and scientific progress against deformity and social progress for deformity and wiser humanitarian acts in desperate countries and many other features, I wonder if Frankenstein would have had the insight, if not of a scientist, then of dweller in today's culture to better control his response, to at least continue his work to try and make the monster presentable at least. Having fled, wouldn't there be punks and goths who would take one look at the monster and exclaim how fucking radical it was-in fact, would it be impossible for the monster to have become a king of goth culture, a decorated writer and oft-invited guest to talk shows? Would there be twitter feeds and tumblr posts labeled #giveadamabride, or perhaps #giveadamahusband, if he was shown in the right lens? Are these things impossible, or would they be inevitable?

                                And then I wonder: does he need a modern era? Maybe all he had was bad luck, missing the person with the right Unless. Do we really believe that there was ever no one in the world without a heart for a monster, in a myriad of cultures that have always gladly bent their knee to that altar? We envy our monsters, secretly aspire to be them, in ways. The monster was more human than most we have wanted to be over the generations-surely, it was not impossible.

                                The monster almost assuredly knew, and felt cursed from early in his life, that his was an uphill battle. His creator rejected him, so how could anyone else. And yet, despite that, in his heart-he wanted what all humans want. We're a social species, we want to-have to-be with someone, sometime-to be part of something. We do not want to be alone. Are we surprised, then, that the creature, as well as all other monsters that we create, so also desire to be part of something-to be in the great human family, somehow?

                                One thing strikes me as certain, in relation to the wider thread:

                                You cannot mourn that alchemists are treated unsympathetically without having sympathy for humanity. To say there is nothing in humanity worth becoming for the Prometheans is to say that there is nothing worthy of becoming as an alchemist.

                                They are, after all, still human. If the desire to escape the human condition is anything, it is, perhaps in some ways, the most human thing of all.

                                All things strive to become something else. Begrudge not those who seek their inclusion.

                                That is the terrible theme of Frankenstein.

                                                    Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
                                                    From what Charlaquin is saying, I suppose there's more of a problem with how the alternatives and the little explicit room given to those are addressed?
                                                    There are a few different problems that different people have. It seems Geckopirateship and others who don't care for the humanist themes in Promethean are seeing the generally unsympathetic presentation of Alchemists as one more item on the laundry list of issues they take with the game as a whole.

                                                    Others, such as myself, are fine with the generally unsympathetic portrayal of Alchemists in the core book, but would like to see a more nuanced take on them in the future. Unfortunately, the places where it seems like the book was trying to leave room for that ended up coming off to some of us as shutting down that potential rather than opening it up.

                                                    In particular, one of the primary reasons PenDragon started this thread in the first place was a sidebar that appears to be an attempt to explain that the reason this section is so down on Alchemists is that it's focused on the alchemists that Prometheans are most likely to encounter - the ones who are so obsessed with transcending humanity that they are willing to hunt down, maim, and kill Prometheans for their Pyros and Vitriol. After all, if they weren't so obsessed, they'd probably be avoiding Prometheans, because the divine fire in them is dangerous, and a wise Alchemist would want to stay away from it.

                                                    That's all well and good, the problem is it talks in very polarizing terms of "rational, sane Alchemists" and "Insatiate" Alchemists, which is what the "sane" ones call the ones that kill Prometheans. It makes it sound like there are exactly two kinds of alchemists: the perfectly reasonable, decent human beings who Prometheans will never run into, and the power-hungry, insane Vitriol addicts who will tear a Promethean open as soon as look at zir. No room is left for a spectrum of Alchemists, who might be driven by desperation, or curiosity, or otherwise justifying Promethean slaughter to themselves "just this once". It reads like Insatiety is an on/off switch rather than a road paved with good intentions. For such a humanist game, it's ironically reluctant to play up the deeply human qualities that might drive an Alchemist from rational to Insatiate.

                                                    I also have a problem with a particular passage that talks about what sort of antagonistic role Alchemists fill in the game. It explains that they are often a physical threat, posing the danger of violence and bodily harm against the protagonists, but if that were all they had to offer, that role could be filled by pandorans. What sets them apart is that they are intelligent, rational(ish), and most importantly, tragic. They mirror Prometheans as humans on a sort of Pilgrimage to leave humanity behind, which can call a lot of a Promethean's values into question. "This human was born with the thing I want so badly, and they're willing to butcher and mutilate me just to get rid of it. Maybe being human isn't so much better than being a monster after all, if this human so desprate not to be one." It even says that this theme is strengthened when a reoccurring Alchemist antagonist doesn't start out wanting to murder the Throng and suck the Vitriolic marrow from their bones, but gets to that point more gradually.

                                                    Again, all well and good, but then the examples it gives of possible motivations for such characters are:

                                                    Maybe she claims that she is trying to escape the world of alchemy but needs some Pyros to pull off one last preparation. Maybe she wants to learn something from the Prometheans before she guts them, so she pretends to have a different motivation. Black alchemists are obsessives, addicts, users, and abusers; chances are good that any who try to talk to Prometheans are deluding themselves, the throng, or even both.
                                                    That's not a character who doesn't start out wanting to kill the Throng. That's a character who starts out wanting to kill the throng but lies about it to give himself a better shot at it. Which is a perfectly valid way to run an Alchemist antagonist, but it's not the nuanced take the rest of the paragraph seemed like it was trying to discuss. It really reads like it's trying to make Alchemists into complex characters, but is afraid to do so in a way that might make you empathize with them at all.

                                                            I'm with ArcaneArts on their first point, it's pretty easy to turn these guys playable or make them sympathetic.

                                                            For sympathetic causes, the Emerald Crucible seems like the 'nicest' of the groups, providing you're not a Promethean.                                                             But you still have to get past a lot of the wording and portrayal. From the start, alchemists don't seem like particularly scary antagonists. They seem repulsive, but it's a repulsive to general reader sensibilities, not necessarily the Promethean, for whom they're just a different shade of asshole trying to steal their Vitriol. If alchemists are not too busy killing each other, they want you, but even then they're so pathetic the game actually has to warn you you can make them too pathetic.

                                                            'The Storyteller should be as sadistic as possible without crippling the alchemist to the point that she is no longer a threat to the Prometheans she hunts.
