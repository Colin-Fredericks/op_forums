                    In case you missed it, this is why the transhumanism arguments rankle the forumites immediately: it ALWAYS turns into a fight. Never mind the merits of the argument.                                     If we're going to talk about this comparison, I need to talk about some of the "whys" in how Promethean not only hooked me into ChroD, but helped me as a person. I won't bore you with the whole story, I'll try and keep it Reader's Digest, but keep in mind when I get to the end that this means some of my explanation will feel a little out of left field.

                                    A very significant portion of my young life is marked by being alone. I grew up in El Paso, Texas, as a white, upper middle class, geeky scrawny kid in an area and school district filled with poor Hispanic farm-workers and their kids who had to live with the problems-in my elementary school, I was one of three white people, the other being my brother and the nurse, to give a proper sense of scale. I had a target on my back for most of my life for reasons I didn't comprehend 'til I was an adult, and I had very few friends. And then I moved to Colorado, to a school with an noticeable uptick in black population(even with an upswing in financial positioning, though I was still upper to their middle-class), and every trick I had learned for just getting by didn't work here-you couldn't fight the biggest, baddest mofo and earn respect that way, no one wanted to actually fight, and got mad if you did, and verbal sparring was less creative. Even after I got a stable friend base, larger than I had before, most of my friends went to a different school. I spent a good portion of my youth hating people, because the majority just never gave me a chance, and I never once thought why that might have been the case. I just let it reinforce my bitterness towards people.

                                    I wanted in though, so badly. Even when I lied to myself about it, I wanted in. I wanted to belong. Promethean made me recognize how the pain and lumps of isolation made joining in all the more beautiful, but it's main effect was retroactive, really coming to life on my mission.

                                    See, on my mission, I became a rock star in our little community. And sure, a lot of that came from the fact that I was a brilliant-but-lazy missionary who knew scriptures, modern prophet speakings, Meso-American archaeology, some of the science of genetic tracing, Judaic symbolism, and more(basically, I was a genius). But honestly, while I didn't actually change who I was a missionary, that being a tabletop/anime nerd who knew way too much about the occult, the mission and how it let me understand people and open up as a response to this understanding of people is the key reason. With the effects of my mission, I connected to others a lot more. With myself, I was a unique, interesting, strange, and bold character. I didn't have to change, I just had to connect. And honestly, that's been the way it's been ever since. I've never had to stray from who I was unless I chose to, but as I learn to connect and understand those around me, I live a happier life, and I'm much more in than I was previously, while still being my own self.

                                    Here's the thing about Prometheans-they start off with less than I have. They don't have a personhood to start with-that's something that they have to build and develop. But Promethean, talking about people, never says "Give up who you are to fit in". It's about how taking what good there is and adding to it. It's about how it can be hard to feel at home in your own skin and how there are various forces that can make it very easy for people to ostracize you. It's about how no one really starts with the ability to know how to connect with our fellows in humanity, how to make friends and lovers and all this jazz. Promethean isn't about an abandonment of self, it's about a settling of self, of learning who you are and how to connect to other people, of learning who they are and how to let their lives matter to you, to stop being the outsider and to bring your unique self in, while still building your unique self.

                                    I don't see the angle of Promethean telling people to conform, outside of the normal success stripping them of their memory(Which, I admit, was always weird). Connection isn't conformity. To be in, to have actually meaningful relationships, doesn't mean to be like everyone else-often times, that just pushes more people away, in the deep senses.

                                    So, no, I don't agree with you.

                                                    The Pilgrimage is a quest for identity, not conformity. Prometheans aren't just different, they're fundamentally broken, and humanity is their reward for fixing themselves. It's not about shedding your uniqueness and dissolving into the grey mass of boring nobodies, it's about finding your true self and proclaiming it with such certainty that the universe can't help but agree.

                                                    Superpowers aren't the thing that make Prometheans unique, they're just a function of their unnatural bodies. This is not who - not what, who - Prometheans really are, it's just a chrysalis of a real person that both carries the Promethean through the metamorphosis and holds her back along the way. Prometheans begin their existence as nonpersons - not because of their powers or their bodies or the effect they have on others, but because they lack identity - they have no name by which others would know them, they have no memories, no experiences affecting their personality, none of the things that make a person. The goal is not to shed your superpowers so that others will accept you, but to experience new things, create new memories, build connections, find your true self so that others can accept you. The metamorphosis of the body is just the final step, and by the time you make it, you shouldn't feel any sense of loss, because you lose nothing of value.

                                                        I feel as though I fall somewhere in between both sides of the argument. I am a purist at heart, and typically the themes of the CofD resonate with me. I believe from the developer's standpoint, Promethean is a game about being abandoned, misunderstood, struggling to find a place to belong, to truly become human. Becoming human isn't synonymous with being normal. Sure, it isn't about cool powers, righting wrongs, or transforming into a legend. It is instead finding love, compassion, community, and kinship. These themes run deep in both the setting of the game and the mechanics, and many of the fans find them not only appealing, but tantamount to the experience of Promethean.

                                                        Now, that being said...I don't see why you can't twist and tweak to get a different feel from the game. As some have pointed out, your feel will be very different than what was intended by the developers, and you know what, that is perfectly fine. It is ultimately your gaming table, with your stories to tell and act out. Much like poetry, it can be open to interpretation. The way one runs their game does not invalidate the way another does and so on. I encourage creativity. As long as neither disparage the other, we are all better for it in the end.

                                                        If the rules for Disquiet and/or Wastelands aren't working out for your idea of the hero game, perhaps just slice them away? I can see that if you are using the idea of created beings who are becoming bastions of light in a world of darkness things like alienation, rejection, and violence toward the burgeoning champion might be clumsy to your theme. Maybe even allow them to creep in when the hero doesn't pursue loftier ideals? That could be interesting in a CofD kind of way. Heroes who are forced to do good by a cosmic force...or else.
