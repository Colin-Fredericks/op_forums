    The Orders of the Qashmallim

    12-15-2014, 04:10 PM
    So, I was inspired to do this partially by my interpretation of the united nWoD mythos, but also by Mr. Paraduo's Angelic Hierarchy in the Demon: The Descent forums. So, below is my take on the qashmallim and the lilithim. I prefer, just due to the vibe the qashmallim have going on, to use the Hebrew names for the orders. The qashmallim are readily enough understood, but I decided to translate the names for the lilithim just because they are significantly less well known. In this take, the Principle is the 'rightful' Creator and prime mover of the Universe, and the God-Machine is a Demiurge; an upstart. However, due to the nature of Infrastructure and the fact that its angels are much less limited in their ability to think or act than the qashmallim the Principle is currently fighting something of a losing battle.

    *****

    The qashmallim spring fully formed from the heart of the Principle with a Mission. These Missions are the reason for the qashmallim’s existence. The Mission is all, their only purpose and motivation in life. The Mission is the only vehicle for thought the qashmallim have. They cannot conceive of anything outside the focus of their Missions. It doesn’t matter what the Mission is. The qashmal could be here to destroy a cult or to stop a specific person from dying, to start a plague, impart knowledge, or spout prophecy. It doesn’t matter. The Mission is all there is for the qashmal. When the Mission is over, successful or failed, the qashmal ceases to be. It returns to its source with the Principle. The qashmallim- and their destructive counterparts the lilithim -are capable of highly complex thought, but are not truly sentient. They do not possess free will, but everything they do is meant to somehow advance the Mission; including anything ranging from small talk to political manipulation. They do not typically learn, or suspect, they simply know in a way that the God-Machine’s angels, bound to data mines and logistical Infrastructure, rarely are capable of understanding.

    However, in spite of all that, there is evidence- rare and virtually impossible to find -that not all qashmallim are bound to their Mission. That some have Missions so all encompassing, so virtually impossible to complete, that they require not just eternity and full sentience, but free will, in order to accomplish them. Finding such a qashmal would almost certainly be next to impossible, seeing as such intelligent, influential, and powerful servants of the God-Machine's enemy would be prime targets for powerful Destroyers. However, should such being exist, not only would they be almost unimaginably ancient, but possessing powers beyond those of even the greatest qashmallim, including- perhaps -the ability to alter a qashmal’s Mission, command it, or grant it sentience. Perhaps such a being would even have the power to perform the Magnum Opus for a Promethean.

              Functions of the Qashmallim

              Lesser
              A lesser qashmal is a being devoted to a single, typically simple, task. They are street-level pushers for the Principle, and while all are equipped with powers and talents that even the least prove a daunting foe to most beings that would seek to stop them, many are born and die without revealing their nature or utilizing their powers- those that do complete their missions successfully only do so once. They work on a local level- affecting things which have only a small immediate effect, but like the God-Machine’s occult matrixes, may expand into something greater.

              Ishim- These are the most basic servants of the Principle. Beyond even those influenced by the actions of the Principle, these qashmallim are the only ones who could truly, intrinsically, understand a Mission and be able to fulfill it with speed and precision. One of the Ishim could be a face in the crowd who only stepped into the flow- and existence -a block ago, and who only exists to slow you down so that you miss a bus and meet someone or see something. They could be the person in line at a coffee shop who says a single phrase that sticks with you, the stranger you flirted with on the bus you’ll never see again. They might even be a voice you think is your internal monologue, whispering an idea in your ear. They are beyond mission oriented. If disrupted, most will react with strong distress, like someone on the higher end of the Autism Spectrum. They do not possess much ability to think beyond a few scripted conversations that could help them along, and respond to probing about their supernatural nature in the manner described in Pandora’s Book (Pg. 69)

              Bene Elohim- These qashmallim, in their Mental, Physical, and Social faculties, appear human. They don’t have much of an interest or drive for anything that doesn’t involve their Mission, but if it is related- directly or even tangentially -they appear, for all intents and purposes, like a normal- if impassioned -individual. The Ishim measure their Missions by minutes and hours, the Bene Elohim measure their Missions in days ranging up to a week. Their missions are typically ones of spirituality, teaching, and motivation. They exist to reveal something, to encourage discovery and fire up motivation, to incite positive change and spiritual renewal. Their powers reflect this. Beyond one Numina dedicated to their self-defense, the other two are typically related to their mission, and are usually for imparting visions. They are often curious about humanity, and can be diverted for a time by discussing theology, psychology, philosophy, or sociology.

              Elohim- The Elohim are powerful in ways that many supernatural beings can only dream of. Unlike the Bene Elohim or the Ishim, who favor subtlety in their actions until such a time as they must reveal their nature (or not, depending on the Mission), the Elohim are beings of obvious divine origin. They exude power and majesty, even without the use of Numina. Where Ishim act in small ways, and Bene Elohim serve to give messages, knowledge, and vision, the Elohim use power. The land is healed, a drought ends, or someone is taken where they need to go. It could be even more overtly supernatural- diseases are healed, miraculous births occur, someone switches genders or becomes a rebis, a man is given supernatural power to hold in trust and use. The Elohim are beyond intelligent, knowing humans better than they know themselves- and while they may not abandon or fail their mission willingly, impassioned philosophical, sociological, or theological arguments may sway them to change their methods a little.

              Greater
              These qashmallim work on a grander scale than their fellows, and exist to cause grand changes in a fairly wide region. A greater qashmal could be tasked with destroying a city’s population of vampires, acting as a guardian angel to a person of interest, reversing deaths that should not have occurred, or other grand supernatural events. They could cause a spiritual revival, birth a cult, create a new prophet, or something equally massive. What the God-Machine can only do with networks of Infrastructure, a greater qashmal can accomplish with judicious use of Numina and a superior understanding of humans.

              Malakim- The name for this rank of qashmallim is Hebrew and means one of two things. It either means “messenger” or it means “lordly one”. Both apply to the Malakim, who are the kind of angel who can’t show up without saying, “do not be afraid”. Their Missions can last up to two months- longer, if they have the appropriate Numina. These are beings so far removed from the human condition that they are truly god-like. However, they possess minds much more similar to those of a human even than their lower-rank compatriots. The Malakim often have a great appreciation for history, culture, and politics along with the other subjects listed above. Where their fellows are capable of forethought, subtlety, and cunning, the Malakim can craft massively byzantine plans and follow them to fruition. Their Mission might even be complimentary or directly dependent upon the actions of lesser qashmallim. While they still fail to grasp certain oddities of the human condition, they can still grasp at abstract thought even beyond the scope of their Mission- for who knows what outside conditions could influence it.

              Cherubim- The most powerful of the Principle’s regular servants upon the earth, the cherubim possess Missions that can alter the fates of nations and sweep cultural, political, and spiritual changes across a continent. They are mighty in ways that make even the angels of the God-Machine tremble, a throwback to the days when the Principle ruled. Because even while Infrastructure provides more support to G.O.D’s angels, and even weak angels are mighty and capable of much more complex action than their qashmallim counterparts, the qashmallim are in many ways still more powerful. A cherub’s Mission could span years, even decades- grooming and protecting special child whose birth was orchestrated by well-placed and efficient Ishim, was given impetus and power by lesser angels, and whose way was prepared by the Malakim. The cherubim are capable of fully abstract thought, even unrelated to their Mission, though it is still everything to them. They can, and do, have an appreciation for the arts that goes beyond the ability to impart messages or appreciate craftsmanship, but that recognizes symbolism and beauty. While so far beyond human, the cherubim are the closest to it amongst the qashmallim.

              Arch-Qashmallim
              These qashmallim work on such a massive, secretive, scale that few- if any -people even believe they exist. Such beings are of immense power- so much so that manifestation could risk creating a Firestorm of epic proportions. And so, most Arch-Qashmallim rarely see the mortal world- instead they work from Twilight, the Underworld, and within the Shadow. Of course, that doesn’t mean that their effects are not felt. The mysterious, destructive Firestorms known as the Dirae (Pandora’s Book Pg. 91) are triggered by their actions. Arch-Qashmallim are some of the most powerful beings in the universe; and only a fool would think to treat them lightly, even if they are on a similar power scale.

              Seraphim- The Seraphim terrify their lesser brethren, and even those above them. While they are not the most powerful of the qashmallim, they are the most alien in thought and action- the closest thing to the direct will of the Principle that exists. A seraph is meant for one purpose and one purpose only- to serve the Principle through swift, decisive, dramatic action. A Malakim may have the mission to destroy all the vampires in a city, and it could do so through clever manipulation and planning, but a seraph does not need to plan or manipulate. A seraph could simply walk into the city’s Elysium, and destroy every vampire where they stand. A seraph’s Mission is usually violent and time-sensitive. Unlike its lower-rank counterparts, who will avoid detection or violence unless absolutely necessary, a seraph has no qualms about destroying anyone in its way, and it has the power to do so.

              Erelim- Powerful, noble, and inspiring, the Erelim’s name translates to “the valiant”, and it is a suitable description of these beings. Some are given the simple Mission of making sure a particular person has the strength to make an important decision. If they must speak, and are unsure of themselves, the qashmal guides their words. The difference between the Erelim and the Bene Elohim is one of scale. The Bene Elohim start on the street, and work someone up from there. The Erelim are assigned to people of importance, and require their power to hold off an onslaught of foes that also wish to use these individuals for their own purposes.

              Ophanim- These qashmallim of immense power serve to watch over the Principle’s plans and ensure they come to fruition. Only the Ophanim truly know all the plans of the Principle, and all its secrets. It is their job to make sure that things don’t go awry and correct them if they do.

              Chayot ha Kodesh- These qashmallim are the closest thing the Principle has to avatars. It is said that they number only four, but what power these four have is perhaps incomprehensible. It is rumored, however, that one of these four is dedicated entirely to the production of souls.

                  Functions of the Lilithim

                  Lesser
                  Gamallim- These lilithim are more or less always angry. They have a job to do, people to hurt, things to destroy, and don’t take kindly to people getting in their way. Not that you’d want to, because there’s something just a little off about them- like a person who walks down the street, eyes in shadow, muttering to themselves. While they won’t take time out of their day to kill you, due to the time sensitive nature of a Mission, they can still rough you up before going on their way. They react with the same sort of distress the Ishim do if interrupted, but are likely to respond with violence.

                  Theunim- These beings are meant to sow chaos and despair. They create self-destructive delusions, vicious social trends, or trigger localized epidemics. While they possess all the faculties of a human, like the Bene Elohim, these lilithim will typically profile as sociopaths, psychopaths, or individuals with borderline personality disorders. The Theunim are assassins, saboteurs, and counter-espionage agents. While they can seem utterly normal, even charming, the moment you turn them to something that relates to their Mission the destructive passion wells up and you can just tell that there’s something dangerous about them- something unsettling. They pride themselves on their ability to destroy, but due to the nature of lilithim in general, cannot help but indulge their ‘weakness’ which takes the form of a virtue. Some Theunim can be distracted by appealing to their vanity, discussing nihilism, existentialism, the nature of evil and morality, or politics.

                  Harabim Serapel- In some ways, the lesser lilithim below these are scalpels meant to excise things that have gone stagnant, that are counterproductive to the Mission of the Principle. The Harabim Serapel have no such view. They are blunt instruments whose very presence sows fear. They destroy crops, cause unnatural rains of blood, frogs, other organic material. They command small troops of Pandorans, unleash more virulent epidemics alongside random mutations, and in general do things that are blatantly, supernaturally, out of line with your average reality. Their job is to make sure that people know something unholy is going on, and that they’re afraid. And they do it well. Attempts to plead for mercy will often be met with powerful speeches of condemnation and derision. If there’s one thing these lilithim cannot stand, it’s hypocrisy, for they are often agents of punishment. Along with the subjects discussed above, they are often fascinated with the dark sides of psychology and sociology, and will occasionally treat their Mission as a great, macabre experiment. That being said, while they will not abandon or end a mission, they can be appealed to with groveling, impassioned philosophical discussion, or intercession on behalf of the one (or many) being targeted.

                  Greater
                  Zomilim- The Zomilim have a morbid, horrifying kind of charm. Charismatic and powerful, they are the unsettling rebels of society. Unlike the Malakim, the Zomilim often will place themselves in highly public positions and try to tear down respected individuals- especially if they truly do have something to hide. They often have a great appreciation for counterculture, fashion, and the darker sides of history. Possessing abstract thought which can comprehend things even beyond the scope of their Mission, they will often indulge their virtues liberally, congratulating themselves for their benevolence and mercy. They are experts at forcing introspection, and devilishly good at persuasion, even without Numina. They often force people to plumb the dark depths of their soul, and in many cases, the Zomilim will do so with their intended victim, so that they can see the justice of their executioner’s Mission. Zomilim usually work on a grander scale than their fellows, and might seek to simply kick-start a much bigger movement, giving powers to servants, or persuading immoral targets to join them to seek redemption.

                  Usilim- Nations tremble in the passing of these lilithim. They are effectively human in their thoughts to the point of being sentient (though they don’t possess free will), and can bear an unsettling resemblance to human fanatics. Cherubim can often be sent to watch over protégés, Usilim do the same thing- but where a cherub seeks to build up and improve things, the Usilim seek to tear it down. An unholy Rube Goldberg machine set in motion by its lesser brethren creates a perfect storm of chaos, which is occasionally supplemented by a Firestorm brought into being by the lilithim at the height of its Mission. The Usilim care about collateral damage, and will often seek to ensure those who do not deserve the horror it is about to give out can escape. They will often appear and offer a warning, but woe betide those who disobey the warning. They are met with a sudden, painless transmutation into an inorganic substance. Most Usilim see it as a mercy, compared to the fate of those they unleash their wrath upon.

                  Arch-Lilithim
                  Azarellim- Where Seraphim destroy things that have become stagnant, that are dragging the rest of the world down with it, the Azarellim tear down new things. Corporations and religious movements that skyrocketed in success, politicians, administrations, authors, or anyone who just has too much power and the wrong attitude. Like the Seraphim, they are beings of wholesale destruction, but unlike Seraphim the Azarellim will often choose to weed out the innocent from who deserve punishment.

                  Sheireil- Dark reflections of the Erelim, the Sheireil are beings of great power. But where the Erelim often work to defend someone against the horde of supernatural or mundane beings that wish to control them, the Sheireil are meant to blow through hordes of opposition and destroy an important target.

                  Ghogillim- Ophanim surround themselves with plots, plans, secrets, and lesser qashmallim. They are the strategists of the Principle when it comes to maintaining plans, and ensuring that the will of the Principle is done. What the Ophanim make sure occurs, the Ghogillim make sure never happens. Also privy to the secrets of the Principle, the Ghogillim stand between the Mission, and those things that would see it fail. They step into a gap, and make sure it remains a gap.

                  Catharillim- These lilithim are some of the most destructive beings known to exist in the universe. They are nuclear furnaces, and the quiet actions of the Firestorms they spawn, alongside the unholy pseudo-Prometheans they create, are what inspire the stories of the Zeka which haunt the legends- such as they are -of the Created. Where the Chayot ha Kodesh are engines of creation which likely aided to nurture the world, the Catharillim are nuclear furnaces geared to destruction; stars constantly exploding, meant to bring an end to all things.

                          It's not just that they become closer to the unity of Pyros and Flux, but also their continued appreciation for abstract thought, and the human condition, as well as the increased scope of their Mission. I imagine that the greater Lilithim are rather Miltonian figures; brooding, musing about the nature of evil and of their own existence. The more and more powerful they get, the more and more human they become, until once members of either Choir exceed Rank 5 they become utterly alien. I've always thought that something much, much more frightening than a complete monster is ambiguity. The higher Lilithim work, I think, as a threat and as a concept in a story or game, because there's the terrible, nagging idea that they may be right.

                          I can't take credit for all the ideas though, as I was inspired significantly by Jewish mysticism and Kabbalistic writings in particular.
