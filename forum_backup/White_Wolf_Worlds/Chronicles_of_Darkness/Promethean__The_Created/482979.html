    Prometheans and the God Machine

    04-16-2015, 08:53 PM
    This is likely a well-trod subject, and no doubt many would prefer to keep these cosmologies separate, but for those interested in the intersection of the two I had a thought to bounce off the board.

    What if Prometheans are basically "bugs" in the God Machine? An unintended side-effect of a core function (The Principle) that the GM can no longer control. Disquiet and Wasteland are the result of Azoth causing the local GM programming to glitch out and corrupt. In this concept I would also posit that the GM is actively opposed (in as much as it is "actively" anything) to the Promethean Pilgrimage. When a Promethean achieves the New Dawn those bugs become fully integrated into the system and could threaten to compromise the entire design.

    Again, not an underlying "truth" of the World of Darkness, just a crossover idea I personally find interesting. Discuss!

      My favorite explanation that I heard was that the GM and the Principle were formerly a singular Demiurge, but some horrible accident (or series of accident), possibly including the Fall of Atlantis and the Death of Father Wolf, cause them to split into the emotive, unpredictable Principle, born of untamed Pyros; and the God-Machine, a methodical, soul-less machine built of Arcane Physics. The two are unaware that anything's missing, resulting in neither bothering to seek each other out.

      In my personal cosmology, the Principle is the Empyrean, the Capital-T Truth, the Alpha and Omega, not only the ultimate God, but reality incarnate, and the God-Machine is a foreign invader. Maybe it's Autochthon, surviving the end of the Exalted universe, maybe he's the Top Dog Exarch, etc. Whatever it is, they're fighting it out. Prometheans are designed by the Principle as agents of Chaos, intended to disrupt society and as a result, break down the GM's Infrastructure.

              In my personal games, I've always house-ruled that the God-Machine doesn't like Prometheans because Disquiet disrupts the ability for Infrastructure to function properly, especially Infrastructure dependent on people acting as expected. Like many others, I've cast the God-Machine in the role of Demiurge and the Principle in the role of Creator. I've always been tantalized by a quick blurb in the Demon: The Descent corebook that implies it's a valid strategy for Demons to lure qashmallim and angels into crossing paths because they usually operate at cross purposes and will often find themselves at an impasse other than to fight it out. At which point the Unchained should be very far away. The idea of the Unfleshed being a God-Machine attempt to design Prometheans is always a fun one as well.

              In my personal cosmology, the God-Machine is a imperfect copy of the Principle from the future, designed by humans as a way to maintain homeostasis and the status quo because they managed to discover, measure, and understand the plans of the Principle and they were afraid of how it would change humanity in its relentless cycles of creation and evolution. It was sent back in time to make sure this wouldn't happen, and was preparing to oppose the Principle long before the Principle even 'thought' about using manifestations of its power to manipulate its interest. (IE. qashmallim and lilithim) It's been fighting a losing battle ever since then. Because of the time restrictions and "one shot" nature of qashmallim missions, I've painted the Principle as more respecting of free will. It's totally okay with its agents doing horrible things, using hallucinations to dellude you, etc. but it won't force someone to do something against their will. The God-Machine is... less forgiving, and it's agents don't run on a limited clock.

                  The OP's idea raises a few questions. First, why are the angels best known to the Created qashmallim instead of the machine angels? The Redeemed are immaculate; does their lack of an ancestry throw errors into the G-M's calculations, or does the dross of their extinguished Azoth corrode the gears?                       I like the take on it, it plays up nicely with the theme of God-Machine as the prime mover of the status quo. I think it could go a little further if the Supernal is added into the mix. Consider this:

                      ​The supernal holds the source of all existence in the material world. A supreme being is theorized to exist, known by some scholars as the Corpus Mysteriorum, the body of magic. However, whether they are ancient humans ascended or the symbol of human ambition and domination, the Exarchs have shifted the material world into a black iron prison of souls. The God-Machine is an emanation of the Exarchs control, it maintains the status quo of reality. This is why the Machine's angels use essence, one of the most abundant sources of supernatural fuel in the world. The Principle is an emanation of something different. Perhaps it's derived from the Oracles (where watchtowers are keyed to elements, so are promethean humors) as a function to restore souls trapped in the abyss. Perhaps it's an emanation of the supernal human quality for introspection. Maybe it too is part of the Exarchs cast down as their doubts and sorrows for what has become of humanity and the world. Regardless of the reason, this emanation burns the black iron prison with fires of change and entropy, creating wastelands and disquiet.

                      I think that might be fairly crossover friendly, as you can have mages, demons, and Prometheans all working towards the same philosophical ends.

                          I also really like Matt McFarland's musings on the two cosmic entities:

                          In The World of Darkness According to Matt: The Principle was first. The Principle is many things – the spark of humanity, the desire for human connection, the lightning that created life from the primordial soup. Throughout the first few billion years of Earth, it was evolving along with all of life, looking for…something. It was undergoing its own Pilgrimage. Once humanity arose, it found its purpose. It was able to shape servants – the qashmallim – from its endless reserve of Divine Fire, and send them into the world. Unfortunately, its own commitment to self-determination meant that it can’t really control qashmallim; they always have a bit of autonomy (which is why they fail sometimes).
                          Somewhere along the way, someone created the first Promethean. Might have been Hank, actually, or it might have been some collection of rocks arranged into a human form that some caveman, lonely after the destruction of his tribe, created. Who knows. But at that point, the Principle’s real purpose was cemented – guide the Pilgrimage of the Created. That is its chosen expression of its purpose.
                          And then there’s the God-Machine. The God-Machine is from the future, but the thing about time travel is that it’s irrelevant when it happens, as long as it does. The God-Machine has its own agenda, but its agenda is a lot more complicated than the Principle’s. In order to interact with the world, though, the God-Machine copied a lot of the Principle’s “software.” That’s why its angels always have enough free will to Fall.
