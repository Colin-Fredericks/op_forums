                Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                Thank you for being so civil about it. Even if we disagree, one less flaming mess of a thread is something to be happy about.
                I want to hug you for this comment. Too few of these.

                How are you liking the rest of the book?
                For me it's a mixed bag. Prommie has always been to me a flawed diamond, with lots of squandered potential. Plus I hack the shit out of everything I run, so take what I say with a grain of salt.
                • The Bestowments are cool, as are the different flavors of Torment. I'll be subbing out a DIY Lineage system for what I consider an excessively restrictive set of X-splats, but I don't like X-Splats in general so that's probably me.
                • Transmutations are largely fine, a couple I feel need tweaking, but that's fairly high praise from my camp. They are a definitive step up from 1e, that's for sure, and give Prommies a nice powerset at a level that doesn't jeopardize their themes (Mage supremacists will no doubt be pleased to hear).
                • There are a lot of subsystems built in to help players along in the game, from Roles to Milstones, to their Vice/Virtue analogs. Though the milage of that may vary I'm personally not fond of such clearly directed gameplay, but others may enjoy the sense of direction.
                • I could do without Centimanus being presented as antagonists, a more nuanced approach (like the Seers received in Awakening 2e) would have been appreciated. But I knew better than to expect one.
                • Firestorm rules are cool. They weren't something I had any idea what to do with, so the book provided great direction there.
                • Can't comment on the storytelling or regional chapters too much.
                • Weirdly I think the game perhaps puts too much emphasis on the New Dawn. It is so prevalent that I fear players (especially new ones) will prioritize hitting milestones and racing too humanity over digging into the story and giving real life to their character.
                All in all I'd give it maybe 3 of 5 stars. "Good for what it is intended for, but too restrictive for much else." Mind you, this is all based off power skimming, and the joint reading of myself and a friend. Bottom line though, anyone who loved Prommie 1e will probably enjoy 2e, anyone who didn't will probably continue to dislike the game.

                                                            An in-universe justification only goes so far. A writer has total control over what they write, so anything they write is subject to that control. Which means if they write a reason for their bad guys to all be insane, that doesn't alleviate responsibility for writing a group of bad guys as all being insane.

                                                            I'm certainly not arguing with the themes used in the Alchemists. Reckless transhumanism is perfectly fine as the basis for a villain. The insistence that they're all a bit crazy though feels wrong when a villain can easily have rational, "ends justify the means" arguments for during abominable things. Do you take apart a dozen malformed would-be child-things in the hope that it prevents any child from dying ever again? There's a character you can make there with reasons for why he thinks this is a good idea, who can have a rational argument for his actions, who isn't automatically mentally ill. Still a character who, I would argue, is wrong in their actions, is certainly a villain.
