    Phosphorus: The New Refinement in 2e

    09-23-2014, 04:01 AM
    We don't know too much about Promethean 2e yet. What we do know is that there will be 10 Refinements, and one of them will be the brand spankin' new Refinement of Phosphorus. But what does that mean??

    I did a bit of digging (read: cursory Wikipedia diving) and found some very interesting tidbits!

    Phosphorus was the first element to be "discovered" that ancient alchemists (the Greeks and Egyptians, most likely) hadn't known about. It comes in many colors and forms, but none of them occur "in nature" because phosphorus is highly reactive. In fact, white phosphorus catches fire just by being exposed to oxygen, and it burns bright and hot. Even when safely in a vacuum, phosphorus glows thanks to a phenomenon we now know as chemiluminescence.

    Which brings me to the name. Phosphorus was originally the name the ancient Greeks gave to the morning star, and it roughly translates to "light-bringer". Any idea what the name of Phosphorus' Roman equivalent is?

    Damn right: it's Lucifer.

    And apparently phosphorus was known as "the devil's element" in it's day. It's a necessary ingredient in explosives and some nasty toxins (including nerve agents) -- and yet it's also necessary for life. It's one of the major things that comprises modern fertilizers, our bones and teeth are full of the stuff, and it's an essential part of every living cell due to its role in energy transference.

    So what does this mean? Perhaps there's something to be said about the paradoxical nature of phosphorus. It's everywhere, but also nowhere. It's required for life, yet also takes it. It's deeply ingrained in the system, but named after one of the most famous rebels.

    Any other ideas on what the Refinement of Phosphorus may bring us?
