    So, something I quickly hashed together based on what was earlier said in this thread about Mad Science skills and what they let you do. It was clear I had some confusion on the exact way a Mad Science Skill worked, earlier, and I figure that perhaps doing some index and collating work would clarify things for me.

    What I’ve come up with so far, on the effects of Mad Science Skills. I’ve also added stuff that seemed to be considered (underlined) and ideas of my own (in italics) to flesh things out, especially for the later dots in the skill.

    Does this seem about right?

    What do Mad Science Skills Give You?

    The first dot in a Mad Science Skill:
    • Allows you to buy Innovations.
    • Allows you to use a particular type of Unobtanium to learn Feats, build Devices or Install Mods associated with the Mad Science Skill.
    • Allows you to use a particular type of Fuel to use Feats, Devices or Mods associated with the Mad Science Skill.
    • Grants immunity to Breaking Points based around the Breakthrough.
    • Unspecified effect around clues that make it harder to refuse them.
    • Grants a free Feat at its first dot
      • Reasoning: This means that, once breakthrough has been achieved, a Mad Science skill becomes immediately useful to the Mad Scientist without further Experience Expenditure.
    • Failures on Mind-Blowing clues or new supernatural phenomena always give the persistent Obsession Condition.
      • This would help the Mad Scientist actually, well, obsess over new phenomena to learn and explore.
    • Gives a penalty to breaking points from supernatural phenomena outside the breaking point.
      • -1 per unique Mad Science Skill?
      • Reasoning: The further the Mad Scientist goes down the rabbit hole, the more trouble they have not going down even further.


    Every dot in a Mad Science Skill:
    • Allows its use as a skill in identifying, answering questions, and uncovering Clues as an equivalent Science skill would.
    • Strengthens the Innovations the Mad Scientist possesses.
      • Reasoning: Incentivizes expending Experiences on the Mad Science.
    • Gives a bonus to dice rolls to use a Mad Science skill
      • Reasoning: Avoids XP tax to actually use the Innovations you have if you do not possess the associated skill.
    • Gives a bonus on breaking points that come from using your form of Mad Science.
      • +1 per level in the Mad Science Skill?
      • Reason: Gives an incentive to use Mad Science over normal science: Dr. Raymond Harris has a reason to use his Induced Combustion on pesky people who investigate his kidnapping, instead of just shooting them. More abstractly: The sort of Breaking Points that a Mad Scientist would normally go through from doing this have effectively been baked into the Breakthrough.

        I'll get back to you on the specifics here. But one thing you may have overlooked:

        What can you do with the Science skill? I mean the plain old, ordinary Mental Skill that's on everyone's character sheet. I ask because everything you can do with the Science skill, you can do with a Mad Science skill, but within the context of the Breakthrough. That's why it's a skill and not a Discipline or Gift or Arcanum etc.

        For instance, the Investigation rules normally use the Investigation skill to Uncover Clues; but it specifically says that you can use Science instead if it makes sense to do so (such as what Gabby does on NCIS). Well, Uncovering Clues within the field of your Breakthrough canshould be done using its Mad Science skill. Answering questions about the phenomena covered by the skill's expertise uses the skill. Recognizing when such phenomena might be in play uses the skill. And so on.

        In short, don't get so wrapped up with the unique things that a Mad Science skill can do but the Science skill can't that you neglect all of the options that are already built in there by virtue of it being a variant of the Science skill. For one thing, this may help you avoid reinventing the wheel: the brilliance of Vent0's use of the Investigation rules with Mad Science was that it made use of existing game systems instead of inventing new ones that do the same sort of thing.

                    As for Feats, my initial thought here was inspired by Fate Core's Stunts, which take three forms: Grant a bonus to a Skill, expand what the Skill covers, or let the skill break a rule that skills normally have to abide by. Specialties do the former; Feats were supposed to do the rest. That said, you're right: Feats are essentially Merits that enhance Skills, and should be treated as such. They should have Prerequisites, including a Mad Science skill and the skill being enhanced; they should have an Effect, as all Merits do; and they should always have a Drawback. They also count as Supernatural Merits due to depending on principles of Mad Science. And, as Merits, they can be Styles if that's appropriate.

                    Speaking of Merits, the Mad Science skills count as Science where Merits are concerned. So Professional Training that includes Science as an Asset Skill (academics, engineers, hackers, scientists, etc.) also includes all of the Mad Sciences the character knows as Asset Skills.

Without a dot in it, use of it counts as Untrained and suffers a -3 penalty. OTOH, it doesn't cost any Experiences to add a Mad Science skill to your list; you just need to achieve the Breakthrough.

                        Adding a dot to the Mad Science skill is a very good idea, though, as it negates that -3 penalty for using the skill untrained. Seeing as how you need to use said skill to research Innovations, and you need to research Innovations before you can use Unobtainium to Actualize them, the only benefit that works reasonably well without that first dot is the immunity to suitable Breaking Points. Adding that dot isn't required; but if you have the Experiences to do so, you probably should.

                        I wasn't considering a free Feat with the first dot; I was thinking about a free Innovation with the Breakthrough, and possibly even enough Unobtainium to actualize one instance of it. (I'm using “actualize an Innovation” as shorthand for “learn or teach a Feat”, “build a Device”, and “install a Mod”.) The motivation is to let the character do something useful with Mad Science as soon as possible; the rationale is that the process of achieving the Breakthrough involves successfully putting your new theories into practice. That is, you don't get a free Innovation when you achieve the Breakthrough; you achieve the Breakthrough when you successfully assemble the Clues and reveal your first Innovation.

                                                        Unobtainium and Fuel absolutely should be customized for each Mad Science. In theory, there's no upper limit to how many Mad Sciences a given character can achieve; he's always being driven to pursue more. The practical limit is a matter of playability: at what point do the difficulties and sheer logistics of keeping track of it all become too much?                                                         One set of examples that my (lack of) imagination leads me to is using the Mad Science rules in conjunction with existing gamelines:

                                                        • Instead of the official rules for Promethean's's Alchemists, treat Pyros Alchemy as a Mad Science. Unobtainium is Vitriol; Fuel is Pyros; the ability to create a particular Distillation is a Feat; and the Distillation itself is a Device. Note that in a Promethean game that uses this option, it's probably the only Mad Science that's likely to show up in the game.

                                                            Originally posted by Vent0 View Post

                                                            So Feat = Merit (just one based on a template and called by a different term for ease of reference with the rest of the system)?
                                                            This, right here, is the reason why I was leery about using Merits to represent Feats. No, it's not “Feat=Merit”; it's “Feat=subset of Merit”. The main purpose of the template for a Feat is to identify those characteristics that Merits, in general, may or may not have but that Feats, in particular, must have.

                                                            Crucially, a Feat must have a Skill as a Prerequisite because a Feat is something that extends what a Skill can do. Look over the existing Merits that have Prerequisite Skills: they all are things that are learned, and their Effects involve letting you do things that are related to the Skill but not normally covered by it. That aspect of it is why I'm calling it a Feat; the rest (the Breakthrough Prerequisite and the mandatory Drawback) are what make it part of Mad Science.

                                                            Indeed, I'd be OK with broadening the terminology so that “Feat” means “a Merit with a Prerequisite Skill”, and “Mad Feat” means “a Feat you can only get if you've achieved a Breakthrough”, with the mandatory Drawback and the Supernatural category just being consequences of the nature of Mad Science. There reason I finally settled on calling Feats a type of Merit was that it potentially allows for Style Feats; it was a different take on what a Feat can be than what I originally imagined. And if it can open up one such possibility, it might open up others as well.

                                                            Indeed, there may be other Merits associated with the Mad Science rules: installed Mods, for instance, might also be represented by Merits. They don't have to be, if it makes more sense not to; for instance, if you're using the Mad Science rules as an alternate means of acquiring Hunter's Endowments or Deviant's Variations, you'll represent them as Endowments or Variations rather than Merits. (See the Hunter 2e sneak peek about Endowments to understand why I'm drawing a distinction between Endowments and Merits.) Likewise, Merits can be used to represent signature possessions, and thus could be used to represent a Device you've built (see Hunter 1e's Advanced Armory as an example of Merits as gear) — but again, you don't have to represent Devices as Merits if you don't want to.

                                                            The point is that Merits can represent a lot of things; but Feats only represent a fairly narrow subset of that: the result of some sort of training.
