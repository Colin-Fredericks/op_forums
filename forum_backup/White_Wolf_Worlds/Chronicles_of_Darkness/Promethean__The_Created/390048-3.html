                    The question is how do you implement that in terms of game mechanics. Electricity may have issues but it can generally be depended on as a consistent source in most modern settings. Same with stuff like Vitae and Glamour that can generally just come from humans without a lot of outside factors other than the supernatural. Meanwhile stuff like Essence, Manna and Aether are rarer but are derived from sites intrinsically linked to this Supernatural creature in question.

                    Pyros is in a sort of odd halfway point. They generally state that Pyros doesn't generally exist in nature, but then try to make it easier by linking it to electricity. The mania idea has some similarities to human derived fuel sources like Vitae and Glamour, but has the issue of being rather specific. Inspiration is not something that Prometheans can easily inspire like Changelings can do with most emotions, it's rather specific.

                    Really I can see Pyros being handled either way, but we need to consider what those ways would mean for the game.

                    If Pyros is generated by humans, it needs to be able to occur without huge amounts of hassle. This is especially true given that Prometheans don't really do well with interacting with humans. A Promethean going out of its way to "inspire" a human is problematic, especially since such a method would be generally needed for more on the fly recharges (to supplement a Prometheans daily intake of Pyros)

                    If Pyros is generated by locations, first we need to consider how it would happen, but we also need to make sure it doesn't mess with the themes involved. Prometheans are supposed to be mobile, having sources of power be static works for Werewolves tied to their territory and Mages with their domains, but it doesn't work for Prommies. If a Promethean is encouraged to hang around an area that dispenses Pyros, it means they will be distracted from their pilgrimage.


                    Based off of this it may be better to make Pyros sites temporary as well. Let's say that general weirdness floods an area with Pyros (anything from a freak incident, a burst of inspiration from a mortal, a Quashmalin appearing, plenty of things.) This probably even has a compound effect given that the Pyros is likely to instill more inspiration in other people.

                    This essentially creates a Pyros field in the area that attracts even more weirdness, Prommies are drawn to the area based off of its radiance, Pandorans awaken and are more resistant to dormancy, and if there's enough of the stuff it will eventually create a firestorm. It means that Prommies will generally have reasons to congregate in small numbers while still being mobile, and it also ensures that Prommies will be fairly guaranteed to end up somewhere interesting given the attraction to places where weird stuff already happens.

                    Eventually however, the Prommies have to move on, typically due to a mixture of the end of the Pyros in the area as well as the locals collective disquiet and the growing wasteland in the area. But if the Pyros grows to high enough levels, you end up with a firestorm driving everyone out.

                    One thing this could also mean is that some Prometheans will see an obligation to settle Pyros fields down. Left unattended, Pyros will generally have a compound effect on itself as more and more weird things come to town and the Pyros of such impossible things increases in the area until it climaxes in a firestorm. If a Promethean can deal with the crazy and calm the area, they can have some time to focus on their pilgrimage while they bask in the remaining Pyros rather than being caught in the crazy.

                                The thing about fire is that is is very easy to weaponize for a normal, doo-da mortal. Every human being on earth, from Norway to Japan, from 16 to 61, has access to fire. It is the founding technology of humanity. This means it's very easy for any mortal to be able to discover the Promethean's weakness, and become a threat.

                                A small-town sheriff holds a bandaged Ulgan at bay using a homemade torch. "Don't move, or I'll burn you!" It's a simple scene, and easy to picture. It also relates back to the audience, because having fire in your face is legit scary. Now if that Ulgan's bane has to be gold, things start to get complicated. How did they discover that? And how is the sheriff suppose to weaponize that without some ludicrous explanation? If the weakness is ghosts, than there really isn't a way short of some goofy Ghostbusters bullshit.

                                Remember, Prometheans are highly uncommon. If their weaknesses are too varied or too weird, most of their potential enemies aren't going to know about it. The random chance someone in a mob is going to set something on fire is way higher than that they will be carrying gold bullets or ghost grenades.

                                Symbolically, fire is the friend of man. Pretty much all of our technology stems from it. Arguably use of fire is what makes us human, and fire being the bane of Prometheans is just one more thing that says to them that they are definitively not-human.

                                ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                                As far as underlying physical reasons are concerned, I'd say it's because Prometheans are largely unstable alchemical reactions, and their forms just react violently.

                                    Yeah but Prometheans are also creations of man. Hell they're even powered by the "Divine Fire", fire makes sense for vampires since they are actual predators of humanity (not to mention the whole fire is the sun controlled by humans idea.)

                                    But Prometheans are essentially described as vessels of divine fire, and it becomes nonsensical for electricity to empower them while fire burns them to a crisp.

                                    I agree that we need to keep in mind that the substance be possible to weaponize, but consider that Werewolves are weak to silver, a substance notoriously useless as a material for weapons. Changeling also has a similar bit with Cold Iron (A notoriously brittle and fragile version of regular iron.)

                                    The only reasons these substances were chosen over something more useful was because they were thematically appropriate enough to be commonly used. (Even a human who has no experience with werewolves can make a guess based on pop culture to bring silver if he believes a werewolf is hunting him, the cold Iron takes a tad more research (iron fearing fey creatures aren't as common in pop culture) but changelings are appropriately less dangerous than a raging werewolf.)

                                    In addition these creatures also have clearly logical reasons why they'd have such weaknesses. Werewolves have the connection to Luna, who's connected to silver. Changelings have numerous justifications for cold iron to choose from (generally either iron is symbolic of the mundane and thus the antithesis of faerie or because the True Fae broke a contract with the very concept of Iron.)

                                    Vampires' fire weakness fits all three categories of thematic, easily weaponized and logical.


                                    Prometheans' fire weakness is easily weaponized, but is only thematic when considering the Boris Karloff movie (again, Frankenstein's monster had very little problem with fire, it burned him once (that tends to happen to most people when they stick their hands in it.) he shows no subsequent dislike of fire and the book concludes with the implied ending that the monster commits suicide via self immolation.) Furthermore its anti-logical. Prometheans aren't undead like vampires, and are even stated to be powered by a fire-like substance that's transmitted by a source of energy that typically results in fire as well. (and again the lightning connection is only really thematic to Frankenstein as well, the other lineages have nothing to do with electricity.)
