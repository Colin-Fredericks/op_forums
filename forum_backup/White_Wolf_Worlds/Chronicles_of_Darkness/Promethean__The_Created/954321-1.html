    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    I gotta say, there are times that I wish that all of the mind-warping aura effects (e.g., Lunacy, Sleeping Curse, Disquiet, Sybaris) would collectively die in a fire, to be replaced with the good old-fashioned fear and prejudice that they resemble.
    Ok, but then you don't get a world where the supernatural is hidden. You get True Blood.

    For me, the metaphor works specifically because the hate is part of the supernatural metaphor. When you're just applying literal prejudice to monsters, the whole thing falls apart because now your stand-in for real minorities dealing with prejudice ARE THINGS PEOPLE HAVE A COMPLETELY LOGICAL REASON TO BE SCARED OF - which makes them a terrible stand in for othered human communities.

    So I'll take my supernatural monsters dealing with supernatural othering over dangerous monsters dealing with the prejudice that the actually unjustly othered have to deal with.

                                        If that's how you want to take it, that's fine. I think it's interesting grounds for a story to explore how someone might feel about undergoing Disquiet and doing something horrible to someone they otherwise liked. Do you treat whatever ugly thoughts the Disquiet brought to the fore in you as totally external to your person and way of thinking? Or does it just shine a light on something already inside of you, so that you can't ignore it any longer?

                                        When you killed someone who defended the thing you called your friend, or you chased your daughter's strange new girlfriend away with a kitchen knife and she saw you, do you blame the Promethean? Do you internalize your behavior while you were under Disquiet and incorporate that into your personality, because confronting the idea that it wasn't you requires acknowledging something about the world that you'd do anything to avoid?

                                        I think people treat these situations as clear-cut because of their player-side knowledge of mechanics, but in-play, in-character, the line between "you" and "Disquiet" is likely to be a lot more blurred, much in the same way that your unconscious bias/prejudice inhabits that liminal space between conscious "you" and "not-you."
