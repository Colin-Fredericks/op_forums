    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    Another breakthrough I just had:

    On the character sheet, Breakthroughs are represented by a section called “Mad Sciences”. Each entry on this list is a Skill — specifically, a variant on the Science Skill that works just like the Science Skill, except that it works for matters covered by the Breakthrough. The basic Science Skill can't be used for Breakthroughs, and the Mad Science Skills can only be used for their respective Breakthroughs. (Exception: Mind-blowing Clues that lead to a Breakthrough can be Uncovered using the standard Science Skill or by the appropriate Mad Science Skill; but in the latter case, they don't count as Mind-blowing.)
    So, each of the "Mad Sciences" is its own distinct Skill that the individual in question is pioneering? Or, I suppose, "plagiarizing" if they are learning it from another.

    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    As corollary to this, you cannot achieve a Breakthrough without spending enough Experiences to buy the first dot in the appropriate Mad Science Skill, as well as having Uncovered enough Mind-Blowing Clues to successfully Uncover the Truth about the Breakthrough. Alternately, achieving the Breakthrough lets you use the Mad Science Untrained until you spend enough Experiences for the first dot. Otherwise, Mad Sciences cannot be used Untrained.
    So, the MS Skill doesn't exist until you've completed the Breakthrough, and once you do, then you can use it and spend XP on it?

    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    Innovations show up on the character sheet as “Mad Specialties”: Crafts Specialties for Devices, and Medicine Specialties for Mods. There might be other kinds as well (e.g., Computer Specialties for Innovative software; possibly Brawling Specialties for Innovative fighting techniques). You cannot take a Mad Specialty without at least one dot in the appropriate Mad Science, as you need to Uncover Clues using that Mad Science in order to “unlock” a given Innovation. Mad Specialties differ from regular ones in that they don't improve your dice pool; they “merely” let you do something with that Skill that you otherwise wouldn't be able to do.
    Like Brawl (Percussive Waveform Transmission*), which lets you transmit kinetic energy with minimal loss along a vector. Or, "Punch Laser", in that you can hit things far away as if they were close. Can't do that normally with Brawl, but it is just an extension of Brawl.

                                                    Delurk

                                                    This seems interesting and I really like the concept. And, since I had to wait for mod approval before I could post anyway…I got to work on some ideas. Because Mad Science can never have too many independently-moving body parts and horribly-translated latin.

                                                    I’m not sure if I’ve got all the mechanics correct, yet. I drew heavily upon Vent0’s earlier example to make this. Hope you enjoy.

                                                    Neuroapstemics


                                                    Example Triggers

                                                    It is a late night for Dr. Printz when a patient arrives at her ER. The man is heavily injured, with one of his arms suffering from accelerated decay. She is eventually forced to amputate the appendage to prevent the infection from spreading any further. The victim does not make it but, to her surprise, the man’s arm continues trashing until his body dies.

                                                    The car accident crippled William Haney, leaving him without the use of his legs. While browsing a support forum, he reads a story by someone claiming to have regained the use of his legs through an unspecified regimen of electrical stimulation. Moderators quickly delete the post as spam, but William wonders: Could he walk again?

                                                    Not all people come to Mad Science with innocent intentions, and Martin Echidna is among them. A serial killer, he has a penchant for slow murder, slicing of various body parts and watching them bleed. During one particularly gruesome bloodletting, he suddenly feels as though he has cut off not his victim’s fingers but his own. How could this happen?

                                                    Example Investigations

                                                    Investigation: It is easy to experiment with the confluence of muscles and electricity even within the home, especially when you have a dead animal to do so.
                                                    Clue: Shocks of a particular intensity can cause certain muscles to lock or unlock in the absence of conscious nervous action.

                                                    Investigation: Phantom Limbs are a well-known neurosomatic issue in which a person without limbs feels as though they still had said limb.
                                                    Clue: By prodding a patient with proper shocks, it is possible to enhance or weaken the somatic effect.

                                                    Investigation: A woman accused of a public robbery claims in court that she did not do so, but was possessed by something that controlled her body.
                                                    Clue: Interviewing the woman, and performing a few tests on her, show lingering changes in her neural activity, almost as if something overrode her body from a distance.

                                                    Investigation: Certain dissociative drugs can cause out-of-body experiences, in which the user believes they are no longer inside their body.
                                                    Clue: Investigation shows the possibility of feeling ‘outside’ ones body but still being able to control it.

                                                    Investigation: You find a video in which a dimly-seen person gestures at a machine, and it starts moving on its own. You decide to investigate.
                                                    Clue: Tracking down the machine, you see that it has no ability to detect motion.

                                                    Investigation: A certain local spot is famous for blocking phone transmissions, despite reception being good in the surrounding area.
                                                    Clue: Placing a receiver and experimenting, it seems as though the electrical signal here is distorted somehow.

                                                    Investigation: Some animals have much faster reflexes than humans.
                                                    Clue: By performing careful measurements on the speed of various animals under moderate electrical stimulation, you find that it is possible to improve such speeds even further.

                                                    Investigation: Electric eels have the ability to conduct great amounts of electricity along their skin.
                                                    Clue: By letting various electrical equipment interact with the eel, you note the possibility of controlling equipment from a distance under careful training.

                                                    Investigation: Certain members of the biohacker community implant magnets beneath their skin, using them to gain various sensations not otherwise normal to human. How does this affect them?
                                                    Clue: By interviewing people who have undergone the procedure (or doing so yourself), you gain an understanding of what it is like to have senses beyond the human norm.

                                                    Breakthrough

                                                    Human neurons are capable of interfacing with far more than just the body. With the proper insight, action at a distance can be quite spooky indeed.

                                                    This Breakthrough Skill’s focus is mostly on the ability of the human nervous system to interact despite the severing or absence of neural links. Alternatively, the human can obtain sensory input in a like manner. This thematically allows for moving your hands even after they have been amputated, controlling a prosthetic as though it was a fully functional limb, and sensing the approach of people by merging your skin sensations with that of the floor.

                                                    Drawbacks are typically based around the fact that the human nervous system is not designed for combining with things other than human flesh. When interfacing, this tends to lead to various sensitivities and weaknesses.

                                                    Example Unobtania

                                                    Nervous tissue from any creature capable of moving objects without physical interaction.

                                                    An object that has stored a 3+ points of Aether for at least a year.

                                                    The Ampullae of Lorenzini, typically found in a shark.

                                                    Example Fuels

                                                    Electric Charge, used to aid the nervous system in expanding beyond its ordinary reach. The Breakthrough allows the gathering of electric charge in a manner similar to that of a Promethean (Promethean Second Edition, p. 170). However, while the Breakthrough allows for gathering Charge, they are not protected by doing so, taking half damage each turn from the power source (Chronicles of Darkness, p.97).

                                                    A particular drug, which is pricey (Resources 3 for five points of Fuel) and most likely illegal.

                                                    Aether, which must be stored in special containers (Resources 4, but reusable).

                                                    Innovations

                                                    Somatosensory Displacement (Feat)
                                                    By forcing Fuel into an object, the Mad Scientist can use it to greatly expand their own sense of touch, bring
                                                    Cost: 1 Unobtanium to learn. To use the technique, 1 Fuel if a significant part of the object is conducive to electricity, 2 if it is not.
                                                    Dice Pool: Wits + (Higher of Composure and Neuroapstemics)
                                                    Roll Result
                                                    Dramatic Failure: The Mad Scientist accidentally scrambles their own nervous system, taking a -2 penalty to all physical actions for the scene.
                                                    Failure: The Scientist fails to expand their senses.
                                                    Success: The scientist may expand their sense of touch into all objects in contact with them or another object within a number of yards equal to ten times their successes. This grants a bonus equal to the Mad Scientist’s Neuroapstemics to all rolls to detect things around them. This lasts for one scene.
                                                    Exceptional Success: The Mad Scientist’s control over his senses is good enough that they can draw back their Fuel after use.
                                                    Drawback: Expanding senses in this manner leaves the scientist vulnerable. Any impact on an object that causes at least one point of structure damage inflicts the Stunned tilt.

                                                    Electric control (Feat)
                                                    Through the use of Fuel, the Mad Scientist may now control any nearby electronic device of which they are aware.
                                                    Cost: 1 Unobtanium to learn, 1 Fuel to use
                                                    Dice Pool: Intelligence + (Higher of Computer and Neuroapstemics) - distance in yards
                                                    Roll Result
                                                    Dramatic Failure: The Mad Scientist’s power goes out of control, causing the device to short out or function in a manner diametrically opposed to their intent.
                                                    Failure: The Mad Scientist fails to control the device.
                                                    Success: The Mad Scientist may control a device, allowing them to use its ordinary functions.
                                                    Exceptional Success: The Mad Scientist manages to leave a lingering effect on any device they are aware of. Further uses within the next 24 hours do not require Fuel.
                                                    Drawback: This Feat channel goes both ways: damaging the Device inflicts a point of lethal damage on the Mad Scientist.

                                                    Neural Scrambler (Device)
                                                    Combining a burst of Fuel with the Techniques used to touch things at a distance, the Mad Scientist may temporarily scramble their victim’s neural system.
                                                    Cost: 1 Unobtanium to build, 1 Fuel to use
                                                    Dice Pool: Dexterity + (Higher of Firearms and Neuroapstemics)
                                                    Roll Result
                                                    Dramatic Failure: The Device shorts out: repairing it requires an hour of work and a point of Fuel.
                                                    Failure: The Mad Scientist fails to hit the target.
                                                    Success: The attack inflicts a point of lethal damage and lowers the target’s by the Mad Scientist’s rating in Neuroapstemics for the purpose of both dice Rolls and Defense. The penalty diminishes by 1 per turn, and repeated uses reset the penalty.
                                                    Exceptional Success: The attack inflicts the Stunned tilt in addition to its penalty.
                                                    Drawback: The electrical charge emitted does a point of bashing damage for each use after the first.
                                                    Special: This is a ranged attack roll: It is subject to the ordinary rules governing ranged attacks, and anyone capable of dodging firearms subtracts their defense.

                                                    Advanced Prosthetics (Device)
                                                    The Mad Scientist is capable of grafting artificial parts onto a human body, letting them fully interface with the prosthetics as though it was a natural part of their body.
                                                    Cost: 1 Unobtanium to build, 1 Fuel per week of use
                                                    Effect: Anyone wearing Advanced Prosthetics may store Fuel in the same manner as the Mad Scientist, up to a limit of the Mad Scientist’s Neuroapstemics. More importantly, the Prosthetics can cure any persistent natural Tilt or Condition, such as Arm Wrack, Leg Wrack, or Blindness.
                                                    Drawback: The Device costs significant Fuel to keep using. In addition to its weekly use, each failed dice roll costs a point of Fuel. Should the wearer ever run out, the Prosthetics cease functioning and inflict an appropriate Tilt or Condition. Further, any effect that would cause a persistent matching condition destroys the prosthetics, requiring a replacement.

                                                    Controlled Amputation (Mod)
                                                    A series of exotic transmitters and loosely attached nerve endings allow a being to control all parts of their body - even if that body is across the city.
                                                    Cost: 1 Unobtanium to install, 1 Fuel per use
                                                    Effect: The modified being may spend a point of Fuel to inflict remove a hand, arm, foot, leg, eye or ear from themselves. This inflicts appropriate tilts (in the case of eyes and ears, only if they are both removed), but the modified can continue using them as though they were still attached, allowing, for example, for spying from a distance by placing an eyeball. They may additionally reattach limbs using Fuel (even if said limbs were not removed by use of this Mod), and doing so heals both the Tilt and all unhealed damage their removal originally cost.
                                                    Drawback: The drawback to having such easily-removed limbs is that the modified is not the only one who can do so. Whenever the modified being is subjected to Arm Wrack, Leg Wrack, Blindness or Deafness, the appropriate body part is removed from their body. It can be reattached as normal.

                                                    Enhanced Neuron Performance (Mod)
                                                    By grafting Unobtanium into a being’s nervous system, the Mad Scientist may improve reflexes and bodily control.
                                                    Cost: 1 Unobtanium to install, 1 Fuel per use
                                                    Effect: The modified being can reflexively substitute the Mad Scientist’s Neuroapstemics for any Physical Skill for a single roll or defensive use.
                                                    Drawback: The Fuel increases the modified’s sensitivity to electricity: Electrical currents deal an extra point of bashing damage.

                                                        Welcome to the forums, and thank you for the worked example!

                                                        One critique: your rating in the Mad Science skill doesn't factor into the Innovation's pool; or if it does, it factors in as a cap (i.e., use the lower of, not the higher).

                                                        Where the Mad Science comes into play is in the discovery of the Feat: you don't automatically get knowledge of all (or even any) of a Mad Science's Innovations as soon as you achieve your Breakthrough — well, maybe the first one. The Mad Science skill lets you do that, adding the new Innovation to your character sheet as a special kind of Specialty (Crafts for Devices, Medicine for Mods, a suitable Skill for Feats); but after that, it has done its job.

                                                            One critique: your rating in the Mad Science skill doesn't factor into the Innovation's pool; or if it does, it factors in as a cap (i.e., use the lower of, not the higher).
                                                            I mostly used the higher, at the time, because of the options it gave: Using the lower of scores seemed like a significant Experience tax on actually using the Feat, while using just the skill would likewise mean a need to diversify in ways that don't always suit a particular concept. I don't actually have all that much experience at homebrewing though, so it's possible (probable, even!) that I'm wrong about that.

                                                            As a general rule, though, do you agree with the other implicit assumption I made? Namely, that effects should typically scale with the particular Mad Science Skill, to incentivize actually buying up points in the skill?

                                                            Where the Mad Science comes into play is in the discovery of the Feat: you don't automatically get knowledge of all (or even any) of a Mad Science's Innovations as soon as you achieve your Breakthrough — well, maybe the first one. But after that, you need to invent each one separately. The Mad Science skill lets you do that, adding the new Innovation to your character sheet as a special kind of Specialty (Crafts for Devices, Medicine for Mods, a suitable Skill for Feats); but after that, it has done its job.
                                                            An observation:

                                                            A specialty costs 1 Experience. It does not normally allow you to do anything new: It makes you better at a subset of something you could already do.

                                                            A one-dot Merit also costs 1 Experience. In many cases, it does not grant a dice bonus to doing something, but - particularly for a supernatural merit - does allow you to use existing skills in new ways.

                                                            So, within the existing terminology of the Chronicles of Darkness, wouldn't it be more accurate to perhaps see the Innovations as a new type of Merit rather than a specialty? Especially since that also provides a native, non-exception-based answer to questions like "If Innovations are specialties, how do they interact with Area of Expertise or Interdisciplinary Specialty?").

                                                            Again, I have very little experience in homebrewing CoD stuff, so it's quite possible that there's reasoning here that I'm missing.
