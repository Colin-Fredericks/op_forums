                        Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                        A computer doesn't bleed, scream, and beg you to stop killing it.
                        No, but an Unfleshed 'bleeds' oil and might not scream or beg you to stop killing it so much as start throwing fireballs. Remember, while a Promethean feels pain, it doesn't debilitate them the way it does living creatures.

                        We kill animals, too, that don't like it, to make sure people have food on the table. It's grisly, but it's not insane.

                        Prometheans, too, have the Savage, Vigilante, and Psychopomp roles. These all involve killing people or letting them die when you could have helped. Fulfilling these acts are a step forward on their Pilgrimage. As far as I can see, there's no special caveat that if the person they're killing bleeds, screams, or begs for you to stop killing it, it doesn't count.

                        A Promethean is expected to do certain things because it helps them on their journey. An alchemist on their own journey is an awful entity.

                        The buy-in for the game is made very clear, and was made clear months ago. It's about humanism, the goods and the bads. Except then we get a section portraying the bads in the form of alchemists and they're just wrong for it. The game goes a good distance in reaffirming the humanism angle by pointing out how disgusting Prometheans consider Centimani. The stereotypes about them make Prometheans out to be like the cast of Mean Girls, and they're called 'freaks,' for standing outside of the race for humanity. In all I think the book could have gone a bit lighter on the 'any who oppose this is wrong,' especially when the first chapters manage to get across so well the whys of humanism and the Pilgrimage for Prometheans. Painting the antagonists as awful is an understandable in-world angle for Prometheans, but when you're getting things across to players and storytellers it comes off a bit heavy-handed.

                            While I don't have anything meaningful to add to debate on whether or not the Alchemist section spends enough time on the "grey" areas or not. I see both sides. The practicality of what belongs in a core books vs. compelling things that would be fun to explore doesn't have a good solution.

                            I have to say though... as someone that's not neuro-typical... all the point complaining about "ableism," here have made me feel far, far, worse about my mental status than anything in Beast or Promethean 2e has printed. Sanity is not a mental health diagnosis, it's a legal ruling, and conflating them doesn't help in any way. It doesn't help writers create more nuanced games. It doesn't help larger societal issues. Sanity is an issue of being able to appreciate the consequences of your actions; which my diagnosis doesn't impact at all. It intersects of neuro-atypical issues, but they aren't the same thing. "Crazy" and "mad" are similarly not diagnostic criteria, or mental health conditions. Sane, crazy, and mad, are not in the DSM or ICD, because they have nothing to do with psychology, psychiatry, or actually helping people that have real mental health conditions.

                            I don't need defending against a game describing antagonists that have gone off the deep end in pursuit of an irrational path, with morally dubious methods, using terms that intersect with how poorly society discusses actual important issues. Nothing in the book has any relevance to things like the fact that every time I apply for a job, I need to decide if I want to opt to exercise my right to not disclose my mental health issues and possibly get the job but not have any assistance is (very simple) accommodations my employer could make to help me be a productive employee (and of course they're going to find out anyway, and living in a state where you can be fired very easily despite supposed protections "lying" isn't a great idea), or disclose and then risk not getting the job because I just gave them a subconscious reason to put me to the bottom of the pile regardless of my qualifications.

                                        Originally posted by reseru View Post
                                        How is it that Alchemists written as being crazed by lust for the Divine Fire is okay but it's not okay for Beast's Heroes to be driven to murder by a Beast's Astral rampages?
                                        The word that separates them is sympathy. Heroes, as Beast goes out of its way to tell us, are supposed to be unsympathetic. Beyond being ridiculously bad writing advice, it is simply wrong, there's no such thing as an unsympathetic human. Hence the presentation of heroes is something I cannot stand. Alchemists, despite several instances where the book cuts very close towards the Hero approach, are deeply sympathetic character, driven by a common fear we all share, and acting in groups to justify themselves. That's a much more compelling, realistic take.
                                        ​
                                        Originally posted by Okami View Post
                                        I guess what I'm saying is, after reading the section, I feel like your impression is really selective. I don't mean to be insulting, but am I making sense? Should I provide quotes from the text explaining why I feel this way? Do you think I'm off my rocker, how so?
                                        No offense taken. I am being selective, because my issue comes from a very select portion of the text: sane alchemists. Notably the Great Works sidebar.

                                        I find it extremely artificial that somehow all 'sane' alchemists somehow "know better" (how?). It's unrealistic, since when has the threat of danger been a hard stop to knowledge seekers? Some, for sure, but all of them? Where are the Promethean run ins with wide eyed youths who believe they can brave the dangers and befriend the monsters? Where are the 'sane' organizations committed to protecting Prometheans (as they are "wealths of alchemical wisdom"), but being gradually undermined by disquiet and wasteland? Where are the Alchemists who are mid fall, slipping towards Insensate but not yet there? Where are the Alchemists pursuing transhumanism for altruistic goals?

                                        I'd have been totally willing to accept slight different explanations, such as: "Prometheans are so rare that the only Alchemists who can routinely find them are also those who have been screwed hardest by Disquiet". Or: "Divine Lightning does not strike the stable mind, an individual must be on the path to abandoning their humanity before Alchemy even becomes possible." Or even: "Many of the worst Alchemical groups started off with good intentions, but were warped through by time, vitriol and disquiet into the grotesques they are today." Though I'd have best accepted: "Alchemists are manifold individuals, not all are calloused skinners of the Created. What we are showcasing here are merely the worst offenders, to give readers some idea of just how bad an Alchemist can get."

                                        But instead we get the Great Work sidebar. Charliquin already did a great job of capturing the issue. Indeed his explanation is clearer than any I an think up at this time. So I'll stop here, but I hope you can at least see where I'm coming from now?

                                                Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
                                                It's a my way or the highway sort of vibe, that I can't help but feel is a deliberate shutdown of everyone who wanted to
                                                There's a lot of fertile plot points in that middle ground, challenging stories about humanity, that go down the drain when you throw away the possibility of friendly (or at least non-murderous) Alchemists.

                                                Again though, let me make this clear, my beef is not with the many fucked up Alchemical societies presented in the book. Those are fine. It's exclusively with the book's often black-and-white presentation of the subsplat. Mind you, the book (thankfully) doesn't shoehorn in rules to prevent them, but the text irked me enough that I had to say something.
                                                For me it's a mixed bag. Prommie has always been to me a flawed diamond, with lots of squandered potential. Plus I hack the shit out of everything I run, so take what I say with a grain of salt.
                                                • The Bestowments are cool, as are the different flavors of Torment. I'll be subbing out a DIY Lineage system for what I consider an excessively restrictive set of X-splats, but I don't like X-Splats in general so that's probably me.
                                                • Transmutations are largely fine, a couple I feel need tweaking, but that's fairly high praise from my camp. They are a definitive step up from 1e, that's for sure, and give Prommies a nice powerset at a level that doesn't jeopardize their themes (Mage supremacists will no doubt be pleased to hear).
                                                • There are a lot of subsystems built in to help players along in the game, from Roles to Milstones, to their Vice/Virtue analogs. Though the milage of that may vary I'm personally not fond of such clearly directed gameplay, but others may enjoy the sense of direction.
                                                • I could do without Centimanus being presented as antagonists, a more nuanced approach (like the Seers received in Awakening 2e) would have been appreciated. But I knew better than to expect one.
                                                • Firestorm rules are cool. They weren't something I had any idea what to do with, so the book provided great direction there.
                                                • Can't comment on the storytelling or regional chapters too much.
                                                • Weirdly I think the game perhaps puts too much emphasis on the New Dawn. It is so prevalent that I fear players (especially new ones) will prioritize hitting milestones and racing too humanity over digging into the story and giving real life to their character.
                                                • All in all I'd give it maybe 3 of 5 stars. "Good for what it is intended for, but too restrictive for much else." Mind you, this is all based off power skimming, and the joint reading of myself and a friend. Bottom line though, anyone who loved Prommie 1e will probably enjoy 2e, anyone who didn't will probably continue to dislike the game.
                                                ​That is a great way to describe how I, and literally every player I know IRL, feel about P1E; and what seems to be the prevailing attitude on these forums is that*:
                                                If you like some/most of the rules for P:tC, but think that the downsides are too heavy handed to be much fun, then, YOU ARE WRONG! YOU ARE A HORRIBLE PERSON, WHO SHOULD NOT BE ALLOWED TO REPRODUCE! YOUR IDEAS ARE BAD, AND YOU SHOULD FEEL BAD! DIE IN A FIRE! DIE,DIE,DIE,DIE!!!!!!!!!!


                                                *Spoilered, because I'm actually NOT trying to turn this thread into a flaming mess, but the hostility towards the very NOTION of wanting to have Created beings that can actually form meaningful, lasting relationships outside their own splat, or that perhaps ALTERNATIVES to becoming a non-supernatural in the World/Chronicles of Darkness would have been nice, has made me a bit...cranky.

                                                        Originally posted by atamajakki View Post

                                                        Imagine you're a Promethean, a few years into you Pilgrimage; you understand some things about humanity, you know what sets you apart from it, and your days are spent striving towards becoming it. You come across a human being - a real human being, born into glorious normalcy with a genuine soul - who wants to kill you for the fire inside you. Somehow things reach a point where the two of you talk about things, and when you ask them why they want to steal your flame, they explain that they want to become more, become stronger and smarter and immortal and to surpass the circumstances of their birth.

                                                        I don't know about you, but I would find that infuriating. Here's some jerk who has everything you've ever wanted by no virtue or effort of their own other than a luckier origin than you're own, and they're not happy with it. They want more. They want to leave being human (magnificently human!) for another state of being, and worse, they want to kill you to fuel it.

                                                        You don't need "Pyros drives them hopeless maaaaaaaad!" for that to be an incredible point of ohilosophical contention in a game about the beauty of basic fundamental humanity.
                                                        Except, baring the objective statement that humanity really is special and superior, the Promethean is expressing a very flaw reasoning. They are ignoring the point of view of the Alchemist and presuming to know the value of being human better than the human does. They would seem to be so blinded by their want and greed for the green grass of the other side that they are rejecting any potential points the would-be transhumanist has.

                                                        It's only defensible in Promethean because the game itself makes that objective statement. But I'm not sure Prometheans know that. Making it so "Pyros and Vitriol unbalance the mortal mind" is far more easily justified.
