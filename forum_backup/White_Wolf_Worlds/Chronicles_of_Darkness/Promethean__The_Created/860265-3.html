                    Well... Prometheans are puppets of an outside force that wants them to understand what the meaning of their lives are. It's called The Principle. That said, it's not like they are just randomly guessing what they're supposed to do. They have Azothic Memory guiding their Pilgrimage, giving them an instinctive knowledge of how to move forward. The specifics they have to learn through trial and error, but they intuitively know what they're supposed to do in a general sense. From a meta-game perspective, the player knows what the Universal Milestones are if they've read the book, they know their Instinctive Milestones because they worked with the ST to create them, and they know the Perscribed Milestones because they're based on the character's Roles, which the player does choose.

                    Ok, before I respond to this, I want to make it absolutely clear that I appreciate this is a wonderful and important moment in your life, and that is awesome. Thank you for feeling comfortable enough with this community to share such a personal revelation here.

                    All that said, I think the way you frame this moment within the metaphor of Promethean is very telling as to your motivation for wanting player-chosen milestones. You talk about this as though God is your Storyteller and they had decided, against your wishes, that conforming with your culture's expectations of you and staying in the closet was going to be a milestone. This is not how it's supposed to work. The player and the storyteller are supposed to work together to create these milestones. If this metaphor was constructed in a way that matches with the way Milestones are intended to work, then coming to terms with your sexuality and deciding to seek out a partner that would make you happy would have been the Milestone that you decided on together, before the game even started, and society's expectations would have been the obstacle you had to overcome to achieve that Milestone.

                    The way you talk about this really makes it seem like your primary motivation here is safeguarding against Storytellers who will ignore players' desires and create Milestones without their input (I won't use the term "bad storytellers" because that's not an argument I want to have right now, and is ultimately off-topic anyway).

                                                    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                    I think it's weird that Created conscious decisions are not really Milestones. Some of them were in my life - it's seems illogical to me. And I think game miss very much without part of Milestones not being those.

                                                    Your decision was a Milestone in your life (or would have been, if you'd been one of the Created), and it led to something wonderful. But did you decide that that decision was going to be a Milestone in advance? I would say that a Promethean's decisions will lead them to their Milestones, rather than determine them. The Promethean chooses the road that they'll take, and that helps to determine what Milestones they'll find, but they don't decide at the beginning what they'll find along the way. A character's Aspirations should have some bearing on the Milestones, but they shouldn't determine the outcome. A Promethean with the Aspiration "Become a rock star" might have a Milestone of "Realize that you can achieve your dreams" (if the Aspiration is fulfilled) or "Realize that not every dream can come true" (if the Aspiration is abandoned). One that is looking for love might have Milestones related to the decision to do so, but the Milestone could be "Discover that someone else can love me for who I am", "Realize that some people will pretend to love you even when they don't", or "Love means different things to different people". The defining moments of life come about because of our decisions, but we don't decide what is going to be a defining moment of our life in advance. We discover them as we go. We don't decide that we're going to fall in love with someone that we meet by chance during the course of our day. We go about our day, and someone new enters our life. We might not realize that meeting that person was a defining moment of our lives until years later. Milestones are the defining moments. They have to discover them, because that's the way being a human works. That's why the characters don't decide any of them in the course of the game.
