        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
        A Ghost Wolf who ignores the Shadow and the Hunt is eventually going to become a berserker cannibal and a vampire who doesn't have some basic tie to humanity and/or a means of getting a lot of Vitae is going to get dragged down into torpor in short order.



        The wolf must hunt. He doesn't need to throw his hat in with the forsaken or the pure. He can ignore the shadow to a certain extent. A vampire needs blood. He doesn't really need to join a covenant or bother with vampiric politics. He can totally eschew vampiric society to focus on mortal interests and goals that appealed to him in life and continue to do so. He might get bored with it after a while, but that's that.



        You're going to have to name some specific points.


        Biggest one that comes to mind: Your refinement is the specific path you are taking to getting a soul. It also determines which transmutations you have an affinity for, and there's really no way to begin play without one. I mean, I suppose nothing's stopping you, but the book doesn't really give you any indication of what transmutations you start with, if you have any kind of affinity for any, or answers to a lot of other questions that pop up when you consider doing so. And what I've seen of 2E doesn't look poised to change that.



        There are methods of getting Vitriol outside the context of milestones and Second Edition has made non-nomadic play more sustainable thanks to the changes in how Disquiet works.




        You are correct on both counts, and these are changes I'm quite happy about.




        And here we run into the crux of the problem: with the exception of Beast, none of the games are built with crossover viability as a draw, and mislabeling characters who aren't assumed to be integrated into the political structures of their respective lines as "rejecting [their line's] themes" doesn't scan as an argument for "most Promethean characters in the setting being inclined to gradually move towards getting a soul and becoming human is preventing me from running a less goal-driven game."



        True, they aren't a "draw," but it's still supposed to be viable. That was a big advertisement when nWoD came about since crossovers were notoriously difficult in the OU. I'm not saying that not integrating into a political structure is rejecting the line's theme, persay. What I am saying is that it's a step towards creating a character who isn't tied to those specific themes, because those political structures are also pretty intrinsically tied into those themes. An unaligned vampire shows a distinct lack of interest in vampiric society. It's easier to understand why he may be interested in chumming it up with a changeling. And vice-versa, because the changeling probably won't want to hang out with a guy whose main interest is manipulating a power structure that he doesn't care about. And neither of those guys really understand or care about pangea and they likely wouldn't have much motivation to lock arms with a guy who's all "we have to patrol the gauntlet you guys!"

        And even still, I think you're missing my core point here, which is not that other splats can opt not to join their social group options. It's that it's exceedingly hard to come up with any reason why my created would, while trying to pursue the understanding of humanity, would be drawn to spending his time with a bunch of creatures which are decidedly not human. If he's given up on that goal, it makes more sense why he'd want to do that. But if the only option for the guy who's given up on that goal is a batshit centimani, then it becomes a question of why any of those guys would wanna hang out with the 8-armed lunatic cackling like a maniac.

                Originally posted by someguywithakatana View Post

                To a large extent, I'd agree with you. Where I'd argue with that is on 3 points. (More like 2 and half, though)

                Point the first, the examples you point out are fluff/world examples that would be enforced by the story-teller. They largely aren't built into the mechanics of the game. Nothing in the mechanics rulebook says that the society-eschewing vampire or the ghost wolf are going to get screwed over and have to fall into line eventually. That's a consequence of the world that is portrayed in the books, and it's up to the story-teller and the player whether to integrate that into their campaign and let that be a force that the character has to deal with eventually. Promethean, however, has that aspect built into the mechanics of the game. If you want to ignore the pilgrimage, the rules of the game fight you on it.
                Harmony and Humanity will fuck you over, Its right there in the mechanics. Wolves and Vampires have it worse than Prometheans at least centimari can get back on the road. You don't come back from Wassail or permanently becoming a spirit and too many breaks toward Flesh will you pretty much full on crazy and unplayable.




                And this leads me to my second (half) point. You can, for sake of example, play vampire as a hack and slash game. It's not the way the game was meant to be played and, I'd argue, it's a terrible idea to try to play it that way. The rules are designed for narrative-heavy games, where combat is an afterthought, there to help a narrative progress, but intentionally kept light so those combats don't bog down the narrative. But the game won't stop you from running it that way. The games all have certain ways you're *intended* to play them, but you're allowed to ignore the fluff at your leisure to play them in different ways. Promethean doesn't give you that option, though. The pilgrimage is built into the mechanics of how prometheans work.
                Yes i can cut out humanity and have an awkward but playable vampire. It excises a pretty major chunk of the themes of the book and a pretty major amount of the pages. But if I want to do that with Vampire.. I can do that with Promethean. Hack and slash Promethean made simple. Drop humanity. Drop Disquiet. Drop Wastelands. Refinements are now "What i think is really cool so I will go adventure about" Ok done. And no more of a break than dropping humanity from vampire.



                3rd point, the ghost wolf who goes out of his way not to be a proper uratha IS a shit character... for a game of werewolf. For a crossover game, he's actually a lot easier to integrate than anything else. nWoD/CoD is supposed to be super-modular, with it being possible to run different splats in the same game. The major difficulty in this is that the different games all have indivdual themes they're meant to explore, and these don't combine well. This is where it's insanely useful to have characters who reject those themes. Unaligned vampires, ghost wolves, and the like are much easier to integrate into a game that doesn't stick with any individual splat. A promethean trying to become human has no motivation to hang out with a changeling, a sin-eater, and a vampire. Hanging out with non-humans will, at best, do nothing for that goal, and may even hamper it, if he's following Aurum or something. A promethean with his own goals is easier to justify doing that, just like a ghost wolf requires way fewer mental gymnastics to explain why he'd want to hang out with that group than a blood talon or a hunter in darkness who's got a pack that he has to run with.

                Just.. no? The Pilgramige isn't the equivalent of being a Ghost WOlf or Unaligned. Its the Equivalent of "Does Vampire/werewolf things". A werewolf who want to hang out with vampires and mages and hunt shit? Perfectly playable. One who wants to just be a normal person and doesn't want to do anything wolfy? Mechanics will hammer him hard. An unaligned Vampire who wants to go reenact Being Human? Perfectly playable. One who wants to go around murdering people by the truckload? Not so much. Honestly a Tribed Wolf who's decided "this random bunch of misfits are my pack" makes more sense with current mechanics than "I want ot completely opt out"

                As for the Promethean, You are really over thinking stuff. The path to humanity is like having a Shadowrun character who's goal is "Retire to myown private island" but instead of being about getting money its about getting milestones. You get enough milestones you get to retire your character, you don't want to retire your character when you hit x? ask the ST to make it X+10. "But my character thinks he's ok like he is" Is he growing as a character and changing as a person? Pilgrimage. "My character is afraid of the new dawn" Are they growing as a person and developing as a character. Pilgrimage. "My character raises a fist in defiance against the world for daring to say I shouldn't exist" Sounds like a great Refinement of Tin concept to me. Yeah.. eventually they have to deal with their central character concept in.. order to retire as a character.

                Promethean 1e had 10 refinements, 2e will have 12 I believe. Here's how it will generally go "Hey st I want to play a character who is made(lineage) who's going to do these sorts of things(refinment). The Character then you know does Refinement sort of things. Gets quest rewards and gets new stuff. Or player decides they want to do other Refinementy things and goes off and does that. There's not really any particular demanded focus except what the player brings to it. Just like the Ghost wolf who doesn't want to play werewolf is a shit character a Promethean who's interpretation of the refinement they want to play that's at odd with the game is a shit character.

                So I as an ST say "hey friends I want to run a full crossover CoD game.. except mummies,demons and hunters because fuck you that's why" Someone who shows up with a "I want to play a promethean on the refinment of copper who lives in the woods and won't interact with anyone" is a shit character, he'd also be a shit character as a normal promethean game but an excellent npc and something that could easily exist in setting. When the players come back with "A promethean on the Refinement of Silver" A Beast and a Mage, its really really easy. Other groupings get progressiely harder but really Promethean things are 'have adventures and learn from them" they are perhaps the most explicitly "be a pc" splat they just have a place to go when the game ends.
