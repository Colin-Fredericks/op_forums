    It's much the same with Promethean. I broke out my book to poke through it and I notice one thing in particular: the corebook claims that Prometheans who refuse to go on the Pilgrimage still end up inching closer to Mortality. (Refusing the Dawn, p72) The difference is that "otherwise being human" is the very thing your Promethean supposedly isn't interested in.
    Contrariwise, being near the end of my readthrough of Strange Alchemies, a worthwhile excerpt from the chapter full of essays:

    When did the Promethean first learn about the Pilgrimage? Some Prometheans hear about the Great Work from the moment they are able to understand the concept, or even before. Others hear about it only as they are about to undertake their independence from their creators. Still others only hear their creator talk of it occasionally, or to other people, and they are never given any context for what it means. Some hear absolutely nothing about the Pilgrimage from their creators, and have to discover its existence from other Prometheans. The circumstances under which the Promethean hears of it, and from whom he hears it, can set the tone for his Pilgrimage. Those who hear of it from a cruel creator may walk it with hesitation, while others may consider the Pilgrimage the thing that took their beloved creators from them.

    Other Prometheans simply find themselves on the Pilgrimage. For some, they’ve somehow always known that their goal was to be come human; it simply took someone else putting it into words for them. Others seek to be near humans, to emulate them, but do not understand why they do these things. The Pilgrimage is an innate part of the Promethean existence — the Divine Fire is transformative, and rare is the Promethean who is simply content as he is. Prometheans are all driven by the desire for change, to become something else, and most of them find their role models in humanity.
    And another one from the chapter fiction:

    I had thought he’d leave after the fight in the motel room. The way he looked when he hurt me made me think maybe it was best if he did. But he’d stayed. Said he’d learned something there, not about fighting or Transmutations or the like. But when I forgave him, when I said I’d understood, he’d gotten all wide-eyed and you could see it come on inside like a light there in the darkness.

    How could I explain it to him, when I didn’t quite understand it myself? We didn’t keep going just because there were no other options. That wasn’t enough to keep trying, to keep striving. But it was those moments, like the one he’d had in the motel room, when things become clear, if only for a little while. Those times when we get a glimpse of what it is we could be, or when we see in the humans what they could be if they let themselves.

    That’s why we do it. That’s why we keep going. Something just keeps us following that little spark of Hope.

            Since there seems to be disagreement regarding Disquiet and Wasteland severity, might I suggest including both ends of the scale? A Promethean with Humanity 1 and Azoth 10 might be the stereotypical walking-chaos Zeka, while a Promethean with Humanity 10 and Azoth 1 might be essentially a clone - Created, but only distinguishable from Mortality under close examination.

            That way you can let the players choose how nomadic and antisocial they want to be as part of character creation.

            Originally posted by SoulGambit View Post
            I think this is one of those times when people aren't asking for what they really want. The issue with Promethean isn't that it has a lot of high brow philisophical undertones, or that it approaches the question of what it means to be human. Do people really want less depth to their game? Eh. Maybe, but I'd wager no.
            ​There's a difference between depth and complexity. People aren't saying they want the game to be even more shallow and superficial; they're saying they want it to be simpler and more direct. It's a question of refining elements to better put across the themes of the game, rather than making it into a puzzle.

            Incidentally, does anyone know what Transmutations are supposed to be? From a philosophical/alchemical perspective, they seem to undermine the game's themes ... surely they should be dangerous?

                        I want a big theme in this new edition to be discovery. A lot of discussion has centered around how Mummy does a great job of keeping players and characters in the dark about large parts of the world they live in, even on a mechanical level -- like how Mummy characters don't really "learn" Affinities as much as they come into them, despite most of their acquisitions being conscious choices by the player. (For the record I'm not advocating that as how Transmutations should go, it was just an example.)

                        Prometheans similarly have to stumble through the dark, just as man did before the advent of Prometheus' Gift. So I'd like for there to be no One True Way to make it to the New Dawn; or even One True New Dawn.

                        I'd like there to be no One True Way to even make a Promethean. Sure having Victor Frankenstein's notes would help, but he definitely got it wrong in certain regards and now you see that. You can do better, or more beautiful, or whatever subjective way you choose to judge your magnum opus.

                        Having the player learn things along with their character, whether that character's "fresh off the slab" or has been journeying through the Saturnine Night for centuries, is a really engaging style of play. By not nailing everything down to a formula (or even having formulae that just don't work as promised), the mystique deepens.

                        For the record I'm not advocating for a meta-plot either, but damn do I love meta-plot hooks. You may recall that the God-Machine came from just a few mentions of the concept in a couple of books scattered throughout the WoD lines.

                        Simulacre: An Alternative Morality Stat for Prometheans

                                                Originally posted by SoulGambit View Post
                                                While each Promethean is working towards being Human, does actually reaching that point add to the game?
                                                My opinion is "No." This is one of those videogame endings; a Promethean might hit Human at the end of a chronicle, with maybe an epilogue scene or two to wrap certain things up, but after that, the game is done. More than anything else, in Promethean, winning matters.

                                                It's completely different from, say, a Mage forging his Golden Road and becoming an Archmaster*; in the Mage's case, the structural themes of play continue to matter, just on a wider scale. In Promethean, innocence is achieved. It doesn't quite work with the WoD theme as a whole in that now you're a mortal in a somewhat crapsack world, but ignoring the wider milieu, it's like a fallen angel regaining their place in heaven. It's a good death, but it's a death. Not in the biological sense, but the metaphysical: you are no more. There is no Promethean tormented by the tragedy of his existence anymore.

                                                Honestly, I don't even like the term Redeemed. Redeemed implies that you were being punished for something. New Dawn works fine.

                                                        You know, I really like the idea that Humanity should start at 3. Making a point of buying Humanity, trying to be like a human when you don't need to, but want to.
