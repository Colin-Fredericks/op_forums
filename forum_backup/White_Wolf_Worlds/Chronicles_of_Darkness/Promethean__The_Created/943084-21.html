    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    you know, I'm looking at all of those 21 pages of the thread, and I really don't understand what all the fuss is about- is it because the only alchemists found in the antagonists section wants to kill you?
    No. It's that the reasons the alchemists want to kill you lack nuance.

    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    Matt has said a number of times it is mostly out of word count, and if he'll have a chance he'll expand them at a Player Guide.
    And the people he has said that two have responded multiple times that their issue with the presentation of alchemists is not with the fact that they're not playable out of the gate (in fact, they're pretty easy to play with some pretty minor hacking). It's that the book is presenting a dichotomy between playable alchemists and completely irredeemable antagonists. No word count needs to be spent to cover playable Alchemists, it's totally understandable that that isn't possible. But it would have been nice if the sections that attempt to acknowledge that they're only covering antagonist alchemists hadn't equated antagonist alchemists with psychopathic murderers and non-antagonist alchemists with reasonable people. Ally <-----> Antagonist and Swell Guy <-----> Cold-Blooded murderer should be two different spectrums. And it's ok to focus on one end of either or both spectrums, but it's frustrating to imply that the position on one is intrinsically linked with the position on the other.

    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    Is it because every alchemist who wants to be inhuman is said to be crazy?
    That is a separate, but related issue to the above, but yes, it is a problem to some.

    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    Most people who are considered to be sane don't use weird occult rites in order to transform themselves into inhuman being.
    I don't think it's helpful to associate "insane" with "morally reprehensible" and that's all I'm going to say on that subject.

    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    Is it because in Promethean being human is said to be something good and that you should aspire to it?
    Apparently, for some people, it is. I don't understand that complaint, as it is such a core conceit of Promethean.

    Originally posted by LostLight View Post
    It was the stated to be the case in the Open Development, so we all should have had enough time to prepare for it. If you love some aspects of the game, instead of using all of the word count in complaining about it and blaming the writers and devs, use that free time to hack the game (not "fix"- fix means that there is a fundamental problem in the game's premise, which is kinda insulting to the game' fans and writers since it is the game's point).

    seriously, if you want to play a transhumanist Promethean setting, make a transhumanist Promethean setting. No one is going to stop you, and no one is going to write it for you.
    Agreed.

        I don't think it's fair to ask for, "why not just try a different game," off the table. Sometimes that is the right answer. Hell, that's how I got started with the WoD back in the day (my other options at the time were WEG Star Wars and D&D... so you can imagine a lot of itches were left unscratched). I wanted something that would have been not worth hacking what I had, compared to the effort of starting with something new.

        Trying a different game doesn't have to mean leaving the CofD either (and Onyx Path has started putting a lot more free preview material out there which reduces the cost/risk issue). Between the other major game lines, and the rather large number of official, semi-official, and fan-made minor templates, I have a ton of material to work with if something isn't suiting me. CtL is practically a go-to game for lots of people I know for, "instead of a crossover mess, just make everything with Seeming/Kith combos and creative Durances," since the Seeming/Kith combinations cover almost anything you can imagine, and you can put any window dressing you want on it. Victor Frakenstein actually works surprisingly well as a Keeper who takes humans for "raw ingredients" and makes them into Creations in his Arcadian laboratory, only to never be satisfied with them and toss them aside for his next attempt; scattering his realm with various Creations stuck in a loop of hatred and longing for acceptance from him, while a rare few regain the agency to leave and set out on their own. With 2e's Court systems, you can have a setting were the local Courts actually encourage individuals to try out different Courts over time to explore their nature (mirroring changing Refinements). And I haven't had to house-rule anything.

                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                    I still feel it's a bit of an unfair reading of the text. It feels far more like, "I want Alchemists that aren't want the books did," is being allowed to bias the reading more than what the text actually says; esp. with the caveat the book makes in the beginning to not treat any absolute statements are anything more than, "usually."

                    As well, I think where the text is trying to make things binary, is different from the kind of spectrum people actually want to see.
                    I think we're talking past each other a bit here. I actually quite like the Alchemists as presented in the book. I just don't like the couple of places where it seems to me that the book actively attempts to quash the inherent potential for nuance that the Alchemists it presents do have.

                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                    When I read it (and I've reread it a few times because of this thread), I get the impression that Alchemists are more like drug-users, and the Insatiate are addicts (I'm aware I'm vastly oversimplifying the factors that go into addiction in this). You're either addicted to something or you aren't. There's plenty of room for a spectrum of how that addiction impacts you life, but being one or not is binary. Alchemists that avoid the addiction of the Insatiate do so by having to avoid using Pyros too much, and pretty much avoid Vitriol completely. A non-Insatiate Alchemist hanging out with Prometheans is like a sober alcoholic going to bars all the time. It's a rare few might find power in the challenge and successful resistance, but most are just going to find it painful to be constantly tempted and seek to avoid such situations instead.
                    Sure, and as I've said before, I'm fine with "Insatiate" and "not Insatiate" being a binary thing. What I'm not fine with is implications that Insatiate alchemists must hate their humanity enough to abandon it altogether, cannot be sane and rational, must be more interested in power than knowledge, must be driven to commit atrocities against the Created out of self-hating obsession and despising of humanity, and must not "know better" than to seek the Divine Fire and Vitriol. If Insatiate alchemists are addicts, where are the addicts who know they have a problem but can't stop themselves? Where are the addicts that want to get clean after this one last time? Where are the people who do take Vitriol but have managed to stave off becoming addicted? Where are the people who are not yet Vitriol addicts, but have gotten way into the gateway drug, Pyros, and are one little push away from trying Vitriol?

                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                    You can't really make that a spectrum, and that's where the division between the two seems to be intended to lie. Where you can put in a spectrum is really on all the different ways the Insatiate mentally cope with the fact that they're addicts to Vitriol. Do they try to justify it as serving some cause? Do they just not care? Etc. And I think the book does provide plenty of examples on that spectrum to illustrate that it should be highly varied.
                    Personally, I only see one example of the spectrum you mention being explored. And I love her! She's a great example of what I like about the Alchemist writeup and wish I saw more of. However, I see far more examples of the book implying that such a spectrum doesn't exist for the Insatiate. And that's very frustrating.

                                “insane” is a morally neutral term. As for Morality, 2e doesn't have such a stat.

                                All alchemists want Vitriol? Perhaps. But not all alchemists are willing to do what it takes to get Vitriol. Those alchemists who consider Prometheans to be people, out at least on par with animals, may well say “if I have to cut open a person in order to get Vitriol, then I'm going do without it.”

                                Of course, that's not a very stable position to take: between the fact that alchemists are very driven individuals (you don't get too tap into the Divine Fire without something obsessive driving you) and that they're subject to Disquiet, it's arguably only a matter of time before the alchemist starts rationalizing that his needs outweigh the Promethean's, and that maybe the Promethean isn't really a person after all. Still, even if it is inevitable that every alchemist will end up that way, they don't necessarily start that way; and potentially friendly interactions can take place in the meantime.
