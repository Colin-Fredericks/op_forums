    Originally posted by SunlessNick View Post
    I seem to recall seeing mention that many of the new mortal demiurges would be following the recipes of earlier ones, and so producing Prometheans who fit into an existing Lineage.

    I don't see how that follows - all the first edition Lineages descend from MD's, and they have Humours. Or when you say "Humour-based pseudo-Lineage system," do you mean the whole hog replacement of Lineage as an x-splat with Humour? (My own proponence - is that a word? - was only about a quarter of that hog).
    The Lineages have Humours, but they're the result of fortuitous and unique events happening over the course of millennia, possibly as part of a greater scheme. MD's working from a recipe shouldn't engender the same sort of exception. Humours are discredited as science, and yet mortals being able to replicate the originating creation from a recipe actually manages to be science. This strikes me as against the Principle (whatever it is), and also diminishes the specialness of Prometheans, who no longer are divinely inspired, but are ready-to-make monsters?

    Of course, what if this imbalance is a source of Disquiet too? By yielding up the Sanguine, a Muse's Demiurge loses his own, making him irritable and obsessive and prudish? He then begins to suck it out of others, spreading the Disquiet like a chain reaction?

            Nah, it's a GREAT argument! You just need the human desperation to see it, but we will get you there, Satch!

            I'd hate to call a MD creation part of a Lineage. It's made the same way, but it's not part of a Lineage. These are the Hackintoshes of the Created. It looks the same, it runs the same, and it's probably made with 10% less slave labor, but "Lineage" is inaccurate. Lineage is reduced from a personal inheritance back to the progenitor to a simple process carried out by a nutbag with a seriously sick sense of curiosity. Which is perfectly tits with me, but for me, I enjoy the baroque grandeur of the Created as children of the gods, midwifed by the insane, and cursed to commit original sin in order to redeem it. MD's haven't been a part of that experience for me, and I'd rather that if they're to be introduced, that they head their own unique Lineages, rather than crystallizing rituals around what was once a transgressive act.

            That is, it's not about mass-production, it's about semantics, dammit! The Pyros never sends angels out on the same mission twice, so why would it respond to the stale ceremonies of mortals trying to recreate what it already achieved? For Prometheans, the need to reproduce and the ability to transfer Azoth and Pyros is inherent, so new Wretched and Golems are a dime a dozen without the necessity of breaking the universe once more. If the Principle demands a new Promethean of a particular shade, it has a vine to pluck. Why would it waste the flameout of a would-be demiurge on more of the same?

            LFP: Foreign Bodies (Deviant: the Renegades)

                                        09-24-2014, 08:49 AM
                                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                        It makes a sort of balance sense in that it's useful but doesn't pin the Frankenstein's as a pure melee machine, but it's weird. It's weird every time I see it, and I think to myself that there has got be a better idea here.
                                        Oh! I doubt we're getting this, as apparently there's some innovative-but-secret plan in the works, but I have a half-finished writeup sitting on my harddrive that did this!

                                        It worked like this:

                                        All Transmutations are bought as a paired Elpis and Flux fversion, called the Creative and Destructive. (For example, you might buy Shoulders of Atlas, which doubles your lift capacity, as the Creative use - and Hammer Blow, which makes your unarmed attacks deal Lethal damage, as the Destructive use.)

                                        The Elpis and Flux versions of a Transmutation used different dicepools, so you could specialize. But if you failed a Creative roll, then the Destructive effect would happen to the target instead; and if you failed a Destructive roll, then the effect would happen to the wrong target. (Like an innocent bystander. Or your friends. Or the item you came here to get.)

                                        This meant you could always turn to the Destructive use of your harmless-seeming Creative powers in a pinch; but if you didn't specialize in them, they'd be less effective that way. On the other hand, even the most evil-seeming villain could pet the dog once in a while with the Elpis half of their power; it just wasn't as good. The same Frankenstein could hurl terrifying torment-laced Lightning and heal their friends with Electroshock Therapy. A Golem could rework metals with their burning red-hot (or acid-coated) hands ... without creating questions of why they couldn't melt a target into slag. The scary serial-killer could drive people insane using the same illusions the friendly Galateid PC uses to misdirect scary enemies.

                                        I must admit, I really liked that idea. I might finish writing it up sometime as a houserule if the new Transmutations aren't mindblowing.
