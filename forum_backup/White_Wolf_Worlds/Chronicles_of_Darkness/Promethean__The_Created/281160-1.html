    Alchemists don't add much that wasn't already there; they're just a logical outgrowth of the setting's assumptions. While they may be the best candidates for mortal demiurges, that sort of thing is still best left to ST fiat, not to codified rules. I'm hoping for some rules and guidance on making alchemists interesting and discrete antagonists, but in all honesty, I'd prefer getting the Zeky as antagonists instead.

    Still, maybe the OP folks will pick up on the demand for actual alchemist PCs before long, though I'd rather see it stuck in a Second Sight Second Edition.

                                                        Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                                        I feel differently. Promethian is about learning to be human, among other things, and if a theme is about 'students' there is always room for teachers.

                                                        Even the original Frankenstein had the blind man who tutored the monster. The Golem and the Djinn (rivalling Frankenstein for most appropraite Promethian inspiration) had Rabbi Meyer. Pinocchio has Geppetto.

                                                        In fact, it seems like every story about an artificial created being trying to learn humanity - whether they're rejected or accepted by mankind - has at least one mentor charachter. I'm fine with Disquiet, but I don't like the way Promethian locks out such a huge and thematically powerful part of the source material. I'd like to give alchemists an elixir to protect against disquiet.
                                                        My concern with an anti-Disquiet elixir is that it would tend to undermine the antagonistic relationship that the author is going for. The problem with an elixir that staves off Disquiet is that it can potentially mass-produced or shared. Now, if we were talking about Hunter’s Ascended Ones, I’d have less of a concern: given that they were conceived as protagonists, giving them a possible way around Disquiet falls under the “facilitating crossovers” ægis; and given that their elixirs are poison to anyone but themselves and other Ascended Ones, introducing such an Elixir into their repertoire is unlikely to have further unintended consequences.

                                                        More generally, I’m not against the notion of there being a way for mortals to become immune to Disquiet, in much the same way that mages have Sleepwalkers and werewolves have Wolf-Blooded; but I feel that in general, it ought to be the sort of thing that’s primarily in the Storyteller’s hands, and/or something that comes at great cost to the person who gets the immunity. For instance:
                                                        Fire-Touched: one-dot Supernatural Merit
                                                        Characters with this condition have encountered the Divine Fire and have come away with a touch of madness. Choose a Persistent Mental Condition that represents the insanity that you picked up from the Divine Fire. In any scene where that Condition might earn you a Beat, you may instead opt to lower one Disquiet Condition (if you have any) by one step, and to be exempt from rolls to resist Disquiet for the rest of that scene.

                                                        The idea is to intrinsically link immunity to Disquiet with there being something wrong with the mortal in question.

                                                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
