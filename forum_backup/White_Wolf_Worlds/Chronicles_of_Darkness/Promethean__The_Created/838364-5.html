    Which, to be fair, is probably just as much a result of the polarization that's going on here. We're pushing each other to the extremes, which creates exaggerated responces on both ends. I think we're all just taking this a little bit too seriously. There's no reason for anyone's goat to be gotten.

    Personally, I think that being human is awesome. There's soooo much potential in every human soul. And that's before we touch on magical immortality and psychic powers that a Refined soul will likely have access to. Sure, humans are flawed beings, and we fail a lot. As a cop, I see a lot of it every day. But I also see a lot of good that people do, and what we can acomplish together. The point of the transhumanist movement isn't to make us something inhuman. Its to make us Human+. Or H+. Better humans, but still very much human. I feel that its completely within theme for a Promethean to end up as an Integrity 10 immortal human with superior physical, mental, and mystical capabilities. But I also think its part of the game already.

    Of course, I think everyone here is familar with the idea that I don't run things according to most books. I detest D&Disms that slip into the games, I come up with homebrew personal theories, like the Mortal+ / Predator / Magical Being / Soul Forger / Creationism axis of supernaturals, and using it to create things like "The Supernal Realms are actually all part of Faerie that got conquered by mortals." Or blood working as iron. Or the Midnight Games, where the antagonism between Fang and Fur is the result of sportsmanship rivalry, and the Caspers are dedicated to win back the city championship from the Potters this season, come hell or high water. So, I have to be very careful with what I say and double check facts. My views of what the CofD should be are definitely non-standard. I don't think it would be well recieved if I started talking about how Geist is as much of a spiritual game as Promethean, and how the eight Archetypes are really the same 5+unaligned+2*antagonists formula that almost all other games use.

    Anyways, the point I'm trying to make is that inflamitory statements, which do come from both sides on here, aren't helping anyone. Its driving everyone to extremes and we need to have constructive conversations here. Or at least we should.

            I don't wish to give offense, but I legitimately cannot trace or understand the origin of this line of thinking. A lot of people liked it, clearly, but I think a lot of people are maybe just liking anything that says "no transhumanism" at this point because this thread for some reason is irresistible to them. One of the things I personally like about the Chronicles of Darkness is that it's so okay with heresy not only does it refuse the concept of canon and translate everything into vague and often repeatedly-retold Just-So stories, chatroom rumors, or scribbled diaries written by the mad, it actually once went so far as to publish an actual book called Blasphemies for the express purpose of ENCOURAGING this kind of thing.

            But you're saying that in a thread where the OP has repeatedly and in no uncertain terms outlined their desire and intention, you were unsure of either their desire and intention and defaulted to the assumption that THEY didn't understand what they were reading? I think there's something incredibly insulting about that. It bothers me that your sentiment is the one people are joining their thumbs-up voice with. It bothers me a lot, actually. But my impression is that there's a sanction here for telling OP this whole thing is wrong as long as you abstract it a little. I think Promethean is a great story and very cool, but I also think ripping its guts out to feed your dreams on is totally fine and those stories can also be great and very cool.
            This kind of proved my point about the "words on the internet" thing - my intention has been missed.
            What I was trying to say was a positive thing - you cannot, really, change something until you understand it.

            People misread things! It happens. I am trying to be positive, to allow that maybe there was a break in the logic of the reader, not because I am being an asshole (you will know when I am being an asshole, as the post will be deleted and replaced with "sorry, needed coffee") but because I want to understand why people are wanting this.
            These themes (transhumanity, which is not superhumanity, I wan to make that abundantly clear) which are very much opposed to Promethean - or at least, not within Promethean's intention, scope and mindset - are not going to be easy to put in, or reconcile with the game.
            I'm also aware that, from this side of the argument, there's likely some feelings that transhumnanity "shits all over the Promethean we love."
            Not to be derisive, but it kind of does, which is why I wonder to myself did they not get it? Moving towards being a better Promethean instead of being mortal kind of, vastly changes the game.

            You say "repeatedly and in no uncertain terms" but I really don't feel that way. Otherwise, I wouldn't have posted this, and then asked for what his interpretation of Frankenstein was so I could better understand from where he is coming in order to make suggestions that could help.

            People are going to run transhumanist Promethean, I'm pretty sure everyone here who has said some variation of "ew" is aware of that.
            This is about that "did they get it" part, though - you cannot fundamentally change a thing without first showing clear and correct understanding of the thing, and sometimes I worry about that.
            Didn't the Daemon in Frankenstein basically beg to join humanity and rage when he was rejected? Becoming human, man. Gotta understand it first.

            As for heresy - yes, I agree! Make things that are blasphemous. I loved LOVED the Hundred-Handred Refinement in 1e as a thing Prometheans who are sick or disillusioned with the Pilgrimage hop onto, and I will - unless the 2e version is amazing - likely run it as that.
            I wouldn't call that trans-human, though, I'd call that - well, you can be a monster or a monster.
            I really, really think that the trans-human aspect of Promethean is found after becoming human.

            Tangentially, if you want to run Prometheans who are "making their own Pilgrimage" - why do you even need a new Refinement, is a thought I keep coming back to.
            1. Provide easy access to the Disquiet/Wasteland mitigating Transmutations
            2. Play around with Pandoran Transmutations
            3. Allow custom Transmutations or power combinations (I cannot rightly recall their name, at the moment) which make those reductions easier to activate/maintain but at a price (harder to resist low Disquiet but can't progress to 3; Wastelands start sooner; caps to Pilgrimage Trait).
            Those are much easier to fit in and - this is important, I think - they provide room for PCs who are on the Pilgrimage to take them, and they also provide antagonist fodder.

            Originally posted by Kingdom of One View Post
            Anyway, the idea that it is in any sense difficult to "kludge" is the one I think it's going to actually be productive to deal with.

            It is incredibly easy to homebrew template elements in the Chronicles of Darkness, because they're only conceptually interrelated and those concepts are easily rewritten.

            Let's take it for granted that being mortal is pretty lame in a world where everyone knows someone who disappeared into the night. In fact even Promethean 1E understands this, offering vague suggestions of superhumanity (TRANShumanity, even?) and uncertain boons for those who complete their Pilgrimage as incentive. The writers were certainly conscious that "give up your awesome powers in exchange for not scaring the children so much" wasn't going to be an easy sell for everyone. So, okay. Screw that whole thing. So you have to kludge something different. I propose that this is not difficult.
            We already live in a world where everyone knows someone who disappeared into the night. Why would you want to have that be something that happens (because being a Vampire or Promethean or Mage is so safe) on top of having, you know, horrible never-ending curses?
            Promethean offers an out.
            Again, I understand wanting to play around with super-powers and be more than human.
            I guess I just don't understand the obsession with changing a horror game to do this. I try to understand it, but - and I mean this in a very positive way - I encourage people to instead burn down (haha, ha, ha) the Promethean they don't like and build back up the thing they want.
            If you end up with a bunch of super-corpses throwing around crazy super-powers and laughing about how much better they are than humanity, though, then that's a pretty far fall from both Promethean and Frankenstein.

            Originally posted by Kingdom of One View Post
            You've got some obvious drawbacks (Disquiet, Wastelands, Torment) and you've got the idea of the Divine Fire. Let's say that those bad things only happen because demiurges are crap at making Prometheans and they express Azoth imperfectly by nature, sort of like how a red strobelight is freaky even though there's nothing wrong with the lightbulb inside. So a Refinement, Lumium or whatever, that shapes you (spiritually and perhaps physically) to refine your channeling mechanisms to alleviate the bad stuff, the strobe effect as it were. Shine purely. Going with the OP's desire for human connection and the guardian angel idea, let's actually reverse the equation and say you can cause a kind of Disquiet that makes people look to you as a leader and trust you inherently - if you want tradeoffs, and OP mentioned they do, they also get really good at reading you and you still can't turn it off, so people are ALWAYS trusting you and looking to you as a leader. You can create Wastelands that frighten and eventually burn things in Twilight - but indiscriminately, so you end up with clean, modern, spiritually-dead towns that, lacking the usual "immune system" of a spiritual ecology, rely on you to defend and are somewhat more vulnerable to physical things moving in. Rather than Torment, you enter creative fugues where you are driven to build and refine Great Works, possibly that produce small measures of Vitriol, but these things draw Pandorans like a lighthouse and often demand rare and dangerous components SOMEONE has to go and get before they can get down to business and ride out the compulsion.

            How's that look?
            It looks pretty bad, honestly.
            First, you've changed the core conceit in such a way that now it fits a new refinement based on staying Promethean, but you'd have to re-jigger the rest of the Refinements to fit fully in this new core setting.
            Second, you have a "drawback" where you get free minions in a game that is predominately not designed Cults, so I'm not sure what powers you'll write-in to fit that new paradigm, since Prometheans already have a baker's dozen powers that re-write or mitigate their drawbacks. What would mitigate this one? Make it so people don't see you as a leader - bring you back to the baseline?
            Also, Disquiet itself is designed to build up to a an explosion that results in either Torment or the Promethean getting chased out, or fleeing, or some kind of forward-momentum action. This just builds up to you having a bunch of dudes who would always follow you? There's no release there.
            You mentioned that instead of Torment you'd have a fugue-state where you'd build and refine great works, but I've no idea what you mean by this - like crafting Athanors? Like being forced to do things that are beneficial? What would they try to make?

            You've also kludged approximately 0 mechanics, here. What will these look like. What will the new Conditions be. How will they interact with - or, as seems to be the case, replace - the existing rules, and how severe will they be? Are you going to just replace words and ideas in already-established systems or write up new ones? Most importantly, what kind of powers are you going to make that replace the ones these render obsolete - and, if intending to run these alongside each other in the same setting, what will you do to balance them together, and what changes are necessary to other parts of the Pilgrimage because of these new powers?

            Look, this post has already become very, very long. If anybody wants to discuss this thread with me, just PM me. I'll be around to critique mechanics and try to offer alternatives.

                    The best part for the transhumanist Promethean argument is that below either transhumanism or humanism, there is the idea of focusing destructive energies to produce a creative force.
