    More options for prometheans not on a pilgrimage?

    01-04-2016, 01:10 AM
    I'll start off by saying this for full disclosure: I was not a fan of 1st edition Promethean when it came out. I'm of the understanding that I overall wasn't in the minority in this, but I feel the need to begin by saying that.

    However, I'll also start off by saying that this is largely because of how much I really wanted to like it. I was amazingly excited when I heard about Promethean, and when I got the book, I did so already eager to make my first Promethean character. But the more I read of the book, the more I realized that it was... well... kind of broken... Thematically, mechanically, broken. I got the sense reading it that the 9 or so people on the writing staff had been working largely independently of each other and that no one was really communicating, because a lot of the ideas seemed fractured and half-realized, though still promising. Promethean was heart-breaking to me for the same reason the Star Wars prequels were heart-breaking to me, not because I thought it was horrendously bad (Not nearly as bad as most of the people around me seemed to think) but because I thought it was almost really, really good and then it wasn't.

    And that was heart-breaking to me, because more than any other line, I wanted Promethean to be good.

    The reason that I'm starting off by saying this is because as I read the teasers for the next Promethean books, I'm overall very pleased. Already, it seems like a lot of my major complaints and criticisms are being fixed and the game is, overall, getting much closer to the one I imagined in my head and became incredibly excited at the prospect of playing. I'm so incredibly happy about the bits and pieces that I've gotten to read.



    That having been said, the one element of the game that I took the greatest umbrage with seems to not only still be present, but to have become an even larger and more inescapable element within the game. And in case you clicked on this without reading the title, and I'm not sure why you did that, it's the pilgrimage.



    I don't think the pilgrimage is a bad idea. In fact, I think it's a great idea, to a point. What I didn't like about it was its implementation: that every single promethean was on one and trying to become human. And by and large, the ones that weren't were lunatics and freaks.

    I get that Promethean is a game about trying to become human. I just don't think it should be. I think giving it that incredibly narrow focus is missing out on a lot of the potential inherent in the concept. You're a creature which shouldn't be. Your creation and your existence are both unnatural. Reality itself hates you for existing. How do you react to that? What kind of existence do you try to carve out? Trying to "fix" it and become human is one potential idea, absolutely. And it should be represented in the rules. But there are plenty of other ways to explore that concept. Maybe your character sees the power at his disposal and decides to use it. Maybe, even recognizing how much it sucks being what she is, the thought of trying to become something else scares her or makes her uncomfortable. Maybe he even rejects the idea that he "shouldn't be" and, rather than apologizing for his own existence, decides to live out a kind of defiance and try to make a life for himself as he is.

    As it stands, all of those concepts have to be played out as a centimani, who are canonically more or less all crazy.

    And from the bits of 2e I've gotten to see, it only looks like the pilgrimage has become a more inescapable aspect of the game in its mechanics, and that the centimani have only become even more resistant to being player character concepts.

    This is all a prelude to asking if there is any talk at all of giving any kind of options to players who want to play a promethean, but don't want to then spend their game trying to become a squishy human?

                  Overall, I don't think you're going to end up satisfied on this front. Promethean is about the Pilgrimage. It's at the center of the game and pretty much always has been. As Matt's said, the Pilgrimage is the buy-in for Promethean, the hook that you start with and look for interest in. If you're not interested in the Pilgrimage? Hey, not a problem, everybody's got different tastes. But yours aren't gonna include Promethean. Same way that you don't dig into Vampire if you're not interested in moral ambiguity and compromises to sustain yourself, or Demon if you don't want a game of lies and counterintelligence.

                  But some individual responses:

                  Originally posted by someguywithakatana View Post
                  I don't think the pilgrimage is a bad idea. In fact, I think it's a great idea, to a point. What I didn't like about it was its implementation: that every single promethean was on one and trying to become human. And by and large, the ones that weren't were lunatics and freaks.

                  As it stands, all of those concepts have to be played out as a centimani, who are canonically more or less all crazy.
                  I don't know everything that's planned for second edition, but as I've always understood it, not every Promethean on the Pilgrimage knows he's on the Pilgrimage. There are many roads to the dawn and a lot of lessons to be learned, and many of those aren't about overtly studying humans and learning their ways. The limits of the Refinement of Gold are that you can't force your way into a lot of understandings about personhood. Many of them can't be found until you stop looking for them.

                  The constants, I think, are a hollow, gnawing feeling in the Promethean's gut: the Azoth has enough self-awareness to recognize that it doesn't belong, even before the world starts actively rejecting it. It has an instinctive drive to complete itself. If you know there's something awry, and you're trying to set it right, be better and grow? You're probably on the Pilgrimage in some sense. You might not be able to elucidate a Refinement or identify an end-point to this self-completing drive, but you're still able to progress toward it, even very slowly and haltingly. It still counts. The Hundred-Handed aren't just stalled on the Great Work. They're not just Prometheans who feel they have a right to live in this world. They're Prometheans who feel that gnawing hole deep within their selfhood and stretch it wider. They know they can be something more whole, something healthier, and they choose to run in the opposite direction. That's what's self-destructive about it.

                  I think there are some weaknesses in the game –

                  Originally posted by someguywithakatana View Post
                  As it stands, all of those concepts have to be played out as a centimani, who are canonically more or less all crazy.
                  – including that the Centimani we're presented with are flatter than what the concept as a whole describes, and also that there's probably more that orthodox Metamorphosis ought to be able to do with the Promethean body that doesn't reflect the chaotic breakdown of Pandoran Transmutations (gross shouldn't be the same as self-destructive). But I don't think all Prometheans are supposed to either be consciously, explicitly trying to transmute themselves into mortal humans or be Centimani. If you don't know what would put the capstone on your Great Work, or are following some alternate idea of it, you probably won't be taking the final step into the New Dawn as is. But that doesn't mean you aren't better preparing yourself for it.

                  Originally posted by someguywithakatana View Post
                  You're a creature which shouldn't be. Your creation and your existence are both unnatural. Reality itself hates you for existing. How do you react to that? What kind of existence do you try to carve out? Trying to "fix" it and become human is one potential idea, absolutely. And it should be represented in the rules. But there are plenty of other ways to explore that concept. Maybe your character sees the power at his disposal and decides to use it. Maybe, even recognizing how much it sucks being what she is, the thought of trying to become something else scares her or makes her uncomfortable. Maybe he even rejects the idea that he "shouldn't be" and, rather than apologizing for his own existence, decides to live out a kind of defiance and try to make a life for himself as he is.
                  Being something awful and wrong but trying your best to use that nature to good ends is one of the cores of Vampire, and to varying extents, all the WoD games. Being defiant and telling the world that no matter how terrible you may be, it's your nature and you don't need to apologize for it is Beast.

                  I'm not saying Promethean shouldn't have room for ideas like that. I think a lot of Prometheans who think they're stalled out on the Pilgrimage might still be progressing while trying to do some good with the Divine Fire they've been given. I'm more saying, a game gets its identity by staking out some position on what it is and means, beyond the aesthetics of whether you're a shaggy wolfman or a lumpy frankenstein. If you try to run in every direction at once you stretch yourself thin. Maybe Promethean could use a little more thought to exception cases, the way Vampire occasionally has mystic hermits trying to unlive in relative seclusion and peace, and Werewolf has the occasional lone wolf character without the camaraderie and structure of a dedicated pack. But it still won't be about those things as much as it's about the Pilgrimage, because that's the core pitch.

                  A second edition can make a lot of changes, but it's seldom expected to swerve the core pitch.

                                              To a large extent, I'd agree with you. Where I'd argue with that is on 3 points. (More like 2 and half, though)

                                              Point the first, the examples you point out are fluff/world examples that would be enforced by the story-teller. They largely aren't built into the mechanics of the game. Nothing in the mechanics rulebook says that the society-eschewing vampire or the ghost wolf are going to get screwed over and have to fall into line eventually. That's a consequence of the world that is portrayed in the books, and it's up to the story-teller and the player whether to integrate that into their campaign and let that be a force that the character has to deal with eventually. Promethean, however, has that aspect built into the mechanics of the game. If you want to ignore the pilgrimage, the rules of the game fight you on it.

                                              And this leads me to my second (half) point. You can, for sake of example, play vampire as a hack and slash game. It's not the way the game was meant to be played and, I'd argue, it's a terrible idea to try to play it that way. The rules are designed for narrative-heavy games, where combat is an afterthought, there to help a narrative progress, but intentionally kept light so those combats don't bog down the narrative. But the game won't stop you from running it that way. The games all have certain ways you're *intended* to play them, but you're allowed to ignore the fluff at your leisure to play them in different ways. Promethean doesn't give you that option, though. The pilgrimage is built into the mechanics of how prometheans work.

                                              3rd point, the ghost wolf who goes out of his way not to be a proper uratha IS a shit character... for a game of werewolf. For a crossover game, he's actually a lot easier to integrate than anything else. nWoD/CoD is supposed to be super-modular, with it being possible to run different splats in the same game. The major difficulty in this is that the different games all have indivdual themes they're meant to explore, and these don't combine well. This is where it's insanely useful to have characters who reject those themes. Unaligned vampires, ghost wolves, and the like are much easier to integrate into a game that doesn't stick with any individual splat. A promethean trying to become human has no motivation to hang out with a changeling, a sin-eater, and a vampire. Hanging out with non-humans will, at best, do nothing for that goal, and may even hamper it, if he's following Aurum or something. A promethean with his own goals is easier to justify doing that, just like a ghost wolf requires way fewer mental gymnastics to explain why he'd want to hang out with that group than a blood talon or a hunter in darkness who's got a pack that he has to run with.
