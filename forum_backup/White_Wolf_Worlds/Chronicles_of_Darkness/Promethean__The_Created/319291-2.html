        I just watched Terry Pratchett's Hogfather DVD the other night (a holiday tradition for us), and the depiction of Death was very Promethean in terms of being someone who quite loves humanity but just cannot QUITE be one regardless of how much he tries. It's very clear analog to the Refinement of Gold. The book adds depth to this, and the approach in other novels like Mort and Reaperman do as well, offering looks into possible expressions of Cuprum and maybe Stannum, respectively.

        Heck, now that I think about it, there's a LOT of appropriate themes throughout the Discworld novels. The character of Susan Sto Helit looks human but can't ever shake the touch of her grandfather's realm. Feat of Clay and subsequent stories about golems provide natural paralels. Even The Last Hero shows some hints at what a transformative pilgrimage might look like...

                    I think a lot of funky folk and fairy tales get overlooked for Promethean. The Steam Man of the Plains, for one. The Velveteen Rabbit for another (it even has a big cleansing fire metaphor at the end).

                    Even Frosty the Snowman has a weird riff on the Golem myth; instead of removing a letter on his forehead, it's the removing of his hat that deactivates him. He also has a fairly Disquieted fellow pursuing him, and even helps build his own wife with the help of his troupe of Demiurges.

                    Finally, let us all remember Elbis O'Shaughnessey in the final volume of Sandman.

                                                I'm going to throw down iZombie the comic book. It has three Created, a mummy, a coterie of vampires, and as you may have guessed, zombies. Plus were-terriers, talking chimps, and off-brand Cthulhu. It's not like, "Oh my god, that is sooo Promethean," but it hits a lot of the right notes. The protagonist is amnesiac, created with a purpose, and transcends her condition at the end of it all. I'd recommend the tv show as well, but not for the same reasons (if you're a Veronica Mars fan with a love of corny gags, your should definitely watch it).

                                                Also, Orphan Black. This one is a bit trippier, and probably further off the mark, but if you're the sort who does grapple with Promethean transhumanism, Orphan Black might be your girl. First, I'm going to recommend it just on Tatiana Maslany's incredible performances. You will forget that Sarah, Alison, et al., are being played by the same actress; you could take that feat of thespianism as an inspiration for how a given Promethean might approach each Role as an opportunity to try on a whole new personality. There is the overall conspiracy of Neolutionism, with its mad science, its underground counterculture, and its zealous opposition in the Proletheans, and the question they all raise is what is the place of the artificial person? It's probably also great inspiration for Demon and Deviant (presumably). There's also a distinct love for real, actual science and research, and while it's not always accurate, there's never a fast technobabble solution to anything.

                                                Maybe Humans? I kinda liked it, but never made it past the first couple of episodes. The new WestWorld looks like it's going to be told from the robots' point of view, which straight up Promethean, plus it's Jonathn Nolan, who's behind Person of Interest, which is anti-Promethean in that the demiurge here is trying to shepherd his incredible creation into a responsible adulthood. And always The Incredible Hulk, and to a lesser degree, Kung Fu Theater and The Fugitive.

                                                LFP: Foreign Bodies (Deviant: the Renegades)

                                                            Originally posted by Yue View Post
                                                            When I was writing Tammuz, the life of Tommy Wiseau (the guy that made The Room) came to mind. Pick up the Disaster Artist by Greg Sestero and tell me he doesn't sound like a Prommie.
                                                            Speaking of people who seem like Prometheans, I was going to mention Dr. Steve Brule as an Aurum follower.

                                                            Let's see, a lot of the good stuff about robots can be applied to Promethean. I'm someone who's against the focus on becoming human, so that prejudice might be evident here, but that doesn't mean I can't recognize a good narrative about a created being. Back to robots, Metropolis is an early one. Asimov's robot stories are also amazing. To me, they're almost like an ultimately more hopeful continuation of Frankenstein, since they posit that artificial life forms aren't necessarily destined to always turn against their creators.

                                                            Young Frankenstein, in addition to being hilarious, is one of the few adaptations of the original that doesn't twist it into some sort of anti-science parable.

                                                            I've always had a weird crossover headcanon that in the ChroD universe cyborg ninja Raiden would actually be a Promethean made from the corpse of the actual Raiden. He's interesting in that he comes very close to returning completely to being human, only to give it up when he decides that there are threats to humanity he needs to keep fighting.

                                                            Musically, Janelle Monae and The Protomen are both great as sources of inspiration for Promethean. Again, anything good about robots can be about Prometheans if you want it to be.
