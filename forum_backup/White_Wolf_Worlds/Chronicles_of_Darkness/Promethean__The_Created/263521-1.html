                            I mentioned this the last time Robocop was mentioned: the crucial difference between Robocop and a Promethean is that Robocop can’t become human; a Promethean can. If an enterprising scientist in the Robocop setting were to announce that he could now clone biological replacements for all of Robocop’s mechanical parts and restore him to being Alex Murphy, Robocop would likely take him up on the offer, sacrificing all of the bionic upgrades he received for a chance to be human again.

                            The other difference is that Robocop already knows what it’s like to be human, as he’s already been one; a Promethean has, at best, a few fleeting residual memories from the body’s prior host.

                            All that, and Robocop goes with the notion that Robocop is Alex Murphy clawing his way back from the dead; in that regard, it has more in common with Mummy than with Promethean.

                            The Centimani is already the transhumanist Refinement; Azoth is geared toward achieving the New Dawn and becoming human, and is covered by Mercurius. The reason why the Centimani come across in such a negative light is that even if you buy into the notion that “transhuman” is better than “human” (not an assumption Promethean makes), trying to skip straight from “proto-human” to “transhuman” is about as smart as trying to run before you’ve learned how to walk. The Centimani tend to be monsters because they don’t put a high priority on the one thing that would prevent them from being monsters; they try to build a tower without first laying the foundation.

                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                    Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                    Thing is, we have rumors sections for that - Strange Alchemies' Refinements chapter has three rumors for each of the five main Refinements, and at least three of those fifteen rumors concern a Promethean becoming something other than human.
                                                    In particular, one rumor is about Grandfather Crow wanting to become an ordinary crow (Aurum); another is about a Promethean becoming a tree (Cuprum); and a third is about a Promethean becoming a being of pure Pyros (Mercurius). The Ferrum Rumors also include one that could be interpreted as Prometheans Redeeming as Dark Heroes; only Stannum doesn’t mention a variant take on the New Dawn.

                                                    The issue isn’t whether 1e covers the topic somewhere in its supplements; it’s what 2e should cover in its core. I wouldn’t mind a bit of word-count being devoted to the notion of Prometheans who seek New Dawns other than becoming human, though I’d prefer it be presented in the form of “probably unattainable goals that some Prometheans set for themselves” rather than “here are mechanics for alternative New Dawns”.

                                                    In particular, what I don’t want to see is a New Dawn variant that’s basically “keep all the good stuff about being a Promethean and toss all the bad stuff”. “Become an ordinary plant or animal” avoids that particular issue, so I don’t have that much of a problem with the Storyteller actually allowing it to happen (though again, I prefer that sort of ending to be presented as “Prometheans can seek this if they want; but it’s an unattainable goal unless the Storyteller decides otherwise”).

                                                    “Becoming a being of pure Pyros” is something that I wouldn’t mind being a thing in 2e, but not as a conclusion to the Pilgrimage: rather, I like that as a ghost-analog for Prometheans: “sometimes, a Promethean who dies while pursuing some great task spawns a qashmal dedicated to completing it; in many cases, the resulting qashmal bears a striking resemblance to the Promethean that spawned it, and some even argue that the qashmal is what passed for the Promethean’s ‘soul’ — the Pyros that animated its body — pushing on toward one last goal before finally dispersing.”

                                                    The Ferrum-style “become a superhuman”, though, falls too close to “keep all the benefits and ditch the drawbacks” for my taste. The closest I’d be willing to go to that is the notion of the “final Azoth roll” granting ‘heric boosts’ to exceptionally successful New Dawns — with the caveat that the ‘boosts’ should always be arguably non-supernatural; it shouldn’t be “if you roll well enough at the end, you become transhuman”.
