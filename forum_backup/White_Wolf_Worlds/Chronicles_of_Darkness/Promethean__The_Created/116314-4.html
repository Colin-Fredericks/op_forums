        It was one of the theories I proposed earlier — with the caveat that it’s just a theory, in part because the Empyrean is just a theory: there is no confirmation, even among Archmasters, that it actually exists. You could point to Pyros as such evidence; but then you’re getting into circular reasoning.

        A few pieces of Mage terminology — especially Empyrean and Lower Depths — are mage-speak for “we don’t really know what this is.”

        If the Storyteller decides that Pyros emanates directly from the Empyrean, he’s free to so. I’m not sure how that differs in practice from “we have no idea where it comes from”, since the whole point of the Empyrean is that, if it exists, it’s something that mages and archmasters can’t really get a grip on, because it’s outside their context. They’re geared toward solving mysteries related to either the Supernal or the Fallen; anything from outside that context, such as the Abyss, the Lower Depths, or the Empyrean, gives them trouble.

        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                        Think of it this way: it’s not that it doesn’t fit; it’s that it doesn’t fit in the same way that everything else does: you can’t point to certain Arcana that deal with Promethean phenomena the way you can for other greater templates. (It’s worth noting that when Imperial Mysteries goes down the checklist of gameline templates and which Arcana are used to mess with them, it makes no mention of anything related to Promethean.) Setting aside the philosophical issues of whether or not mages can find a label that seems to fit, this is the important part: regardless of which explanation you use to fit Pyros into Mage’s metaphysics, all things Pyros remain largely beyond the scope of Supernal Magic.

                        • Pyros is covered by Supernal symbols that aren’t anywhere near the Watchtowers. Since the Arcana that mages study are near the Watchtowers, that still leaves Pyros beyond their reach.
                        • Pyros comes from the Abyss. Without delving into Abyssal Magic, Pyros is beyond a mage’s reach. Even with Abyssal Magic, nobody has been able to figure out how to mess with Pyros.
                        • Pyros comes from the Lower Depths. If this is true, then Pyros represents a void that the Arcana don’t cover.
                        • Pyros comes from the Empyrean. If this is true, then not even conjunctional use of all ten Arcana would be enough to cover Pyros.
                        • Pyros is purely of the Phenomenal World, with no source beyond the Phenomenal. Then Supernal Magic has nothing that can cover it.

                        Note that several of these options say things about Mage’s metaphysics, such as pointing to the expansiveness of the Supernal or the possibility of a “trans-Supernal world”, or even the possibility that the Supernal/Phenomenal interdependence isn’t as absolute as mages tend to think. These are possibilities that enrich Mage in a way that “Pyros is a weird kind of Prime” doesn’t.

                        Originally posted by Solar View Post
                        Though as I said, in a Mage game a Promethean is understandable and explainable by Mages, in a Promethean game it is not. I don't really like mixed splat parties so that conflict wouldn't come up for me, fortunately!
                        I’m saying that whether or not it’s understandable and explainable by mages is most likely to be important in a Mage game, not a Promethean game; and unless the Storyteller invokes his “I can do whatever I want, no matter what the books say” right, the answer still appears to be that Prometheans aren’t explainable by mages, or even by archmasters. They can hypothesize; but Promethean metaphysics lie beyond the boundaries of even Mage metaphysics.

                        Which simply means that Mage doesn’t have a Theory of Everything. That’s not a knock against Mage: the Awakening; unlike Mage: the Ascension, Awakening is more concerned with questions than with answers. The fact that there are questions that they don’t have answers to — and might not even be properly equipped to answer — merely means that there’s more work to be done.

                                                    Being that I've started the process of rereading Promethean in its entirety from the core in the last few days I feel a pointed reminder is necessary about how Supernal magic works and why, thematically, the Pyros is thus tricky for mortal manipulations of the fundament to grasp:

                                                    The Supernal World describes and acts upon the Phenomenal World. Take note of that first verb in particular — Awakening's made its share of references to magic's connection with language.

                                                    Meanwhile, I'm not even done with the first chapter of Promethean Core yet and already I've hit two or three references to the Pyros, the soul, and Azoth being indescribable mysteries.

                                                    The fact that the Divine Fire is pervasive is kind of a hint that the supernatural mechanisms of human understanding that divide the cosmos into a double-handful of phenomenal constellations aren't the right equipment for the sort of holistic approach needed to grasp the stuff in more than the abstract sense.
