                  I'm going to wait for the second edition before I write these up more fully, but I have the:

                  Dumuz: (Yes, this is a different transliteration of Tammuz; this is because the means of creating the Tammuz golems were derived from those of the Dumuz, who were the real embodiments of the Inanna backstory). As the results of an attempt to resurrect a loved one, they feel - and maybe have - a stronger connection to their bodies' former owners. Their Disquiet and Torment are elegiac, centred around mourning and feelings of loss; people have a tendency to see their loved ones reflected in the Dumuz and become angry when the reflection doesn't persist.

                  Delphine: Derived from the Oracle of Delphi; their origin myth claims that their progenitor called the Fire into her own flesh and became a Promethean, though that's not carried over to the creation of any today. Anyhow, they're oracles, creatures of ecstatic visions, and connected to both the Sun and underground places. Their Disquiet provokes alternating certainties and confusions.

                      I've created a lineage something like a year ago; its members are, amusingly enough, also called "Hollow".

                      Basic idea:

                      Some guy (his name isn't important, appropriately so for the Hollow, as you'll find out later) gets it into his head that his deceased wife can be brought back from the dead. He wants to reanimate her body.

                      What makes him different from other potential progenitors is that he empathically doesn't want to create *new* life - he wants his wife back.

                      So he creates an appropriate altar for the ritual of resurrection - ornating it with objects dear to his wife, that are supposed to bring her spirit closer to the world of the living. He cuts up the corpse. He puts it back together. He starts to pray.

                      His obsession is sufficient to draw the attention of the Principle and force the Pyros into this corpse. And the corpse of his wife opens her eyes.

                      This woman isn't his wife, though. She doesn't even always look like her. Sometimes her personality changes. Sometimes her physical features change. She's not a person. She's nobody.

                      It breaks his heart.



                      The quintessential idea of the Hollow is this: all Promethean, upon their creation, are new persons, without personality or memories. With the Hollow, this idea is drawn to its logical conclusion - in trying to bring someone back from the dead with her personality intact, the man achieved just the opposite - the resulting creature has even less personality and individuality than the average Promethean. The Hollow are social and physical chameleons - they have the means to be anyone they want. Which doesn't give them any personality of their own. Their Pilgrimage involves finding a way to be somebody - as opposed to nobody.

                      Humor: Void. The Hollow's Pyros doesn't have distinctive traits or humor of its own.

                      Bestowment: 9 again on rolls involving Subterfuge, disguise and pretending to be someone else. Failed rolls can be rerolled.

                      Many Hollow eventually learn Deception Transmutations.

                      Disquiet: People ignore the Hollow and do not acknowledge his existence. Sometimes people also refuse to trust the Hollow. Sometimes they try to manipulate him, attempting to force a change in his behaviour, outlook and personality.

                      Disfigurement: When using Pyros, the Hollow appears to have no physical features and face at all.

                      The Torment of the Hollow is that while those around them are rapidly losing identity, the Hollow is picking those traits up, searching for something, anything that will make them more real, more a person.
