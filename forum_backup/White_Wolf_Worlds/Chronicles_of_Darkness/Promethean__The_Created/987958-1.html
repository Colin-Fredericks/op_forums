                                                    Well, the Pilgrimage gets a check in that category, considering the whole game is built around it.

                                                    Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                                                    is it fun to play,
                                                    That's another check from me, I would assume not from you.

                                                    Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                                                    does it give the players choice and flexibility?
                                                    I'm going to have to challenge you on this metric. The only roleplayinf game with absolute flexibility and unconstrained choice is "let's play pretend". Which, granted, is a pretty awesome game, but you'd be hard pressed to find adults who agree it is the best RPG ever. Every game mechanic and every setting element constrains choice and flexibility, but that is not an inherently bad thing. Constraints foster creativity, which is why adults tend to prefer RPGs with more constraints than "let's play pretend", because they generally have a harder time exercising creativity than children do. However, I do agree that a certain amount of choice and flexibility are desireable in RPGs, and to a greater degree than in most other media, even other interactive media. So, I would say that a good RPG element strikes a balance between flexibility and constraint. Where exactly the optimal balance lies will vary from one person to the next. For me, Promethean falls within my tolerable range, and I take it it falls outside of yours. So, again, check from me, no check from you.

                                                    Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                                                    &quot;And yes, &quot;is this something I like&quot;, because, as I've said, there's no such thing as objectivity, and it's impossible to criticize anything other than spelling/grammar/factual consistency without involving opinion.
                                                    No arguments there, so again, check from me, no check from you. So all told, we've got a game that hits all the marks for some people, and misses most of them for you. Which is why we talk about these things in terms of personal preference. I do find Promethean fun. I do like it. I do think it strikes a good balance between flexibility and constraint. You don't. Of course, the people making the game know it is impossible to hit all the boxes for everyone. So instead, they make choices and they commit to them, in order to appeal to a certain target audience. The fact that you are not the target audience for Promethean doesn't make it a bad game for those of us who are. It just makes it a game you don't like. And that's ok.
