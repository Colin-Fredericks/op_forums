    I only have 1.5 bits of commentary, both on the Ultimate Life Form.

    1) Intent usually matters less to the Lineages than method for a reason: the alchemical processes that make the Promethean go are, well, processes. Both the Frankenstein* and Galatean Lineages have progenitors who were out to make perfect entities that lines up nicely with the goal of making ultimate life forms, but what matters to each is what they think that is specifically, and how one can attain it in material and supernatural materials and methods.

Instead of that arrogance of creation being a legacy, it is closer to a single generational element inflicted by the creator (whether that's a Demiurge or a Promethean), or even an element taken up by the Promethean himself on his journey.

    It wasn't, but now that you mention it Shadow fits Promethean better than most of the other inspirations for ULFs. Maybe not in terms of what he is, but he fits better in his history and how he relates to others.

    Originally posted by Verlan View Post

    In regards to the first route I think that that's definitely something that the table as a whole would have to talk about; apart from the one example Galateid in the 2e book, the books didn't seem to say much about how commonly the corpses of children were used in generative acts. In any case, the second idea that you had actually reminded me of taxidermy and I think that that is where the Lineage could find it's niche. I think that there is a bit of an overlap with the embalmed appearance of the Osiran Disfigurements, but the whole idea of preserved parts being used to construct some sort of plaything makes it seem distinct enough to me. In that case, Skin Horses would be defined by how their bodies are made up of processed, treated flesh, not in the way that a corpse is treated to preserve it for burial, but in the way that animal hides are treated to become useful goods, like you hinted at with the mention of leather. I think that the aesthetics of mass production and disposability could go a long way in helping to flesh them out. There is some overlap with the themes of Galateids, but one of the main thorough lines of their journey could be struggling to obtain a feeling of worth when they were constructed from cheap yet unsettlingly antiseptic spare parts already removed from their original state in nature. They do not even get a whole corpse that a family could have mourned, just random human body parts, and not even particularly notable ones, as is the case with the Frankenstein. While that does come close to encroaching on Galateid territory, I think that the Lineage that you propose would be distinct in that they were never intended to meet or surpass human perfection at all, which is part of why the Galateid fear that they are nothing but an imitation of humanity is relevant to them. They would be toys, constructed on a whim for trivial ends.
    Trivial to adults, anyways. I think the connection to children is an important element of the Skin Horses. There's great tension in how the worth of the Skin Horse can vary wildly in the eyes of everyone around him and how his most accessible role models don't have humanity figured out either.

    And I agree, the more I think about it, the more a processed element fits them well. Especially the leather. That's the most intuitive processing method, though incorporating real teeth or ground bones as a stuffing are other ghoulish methods I could see a Promethean coming up with. Can we apply this back to those created through more conventional toy parts?

                            The problem with Vat Born is what Satchel was explaining earlier: Prometheans are necessarily not born. They have no metabolism, no biological process, even if made out of biomass. They've never been alive except by fiat from the Divine Fire.

                            Calling them "born" gives the impression of a living process that didn't really exist (otherwise they wouldn't be Prometheans, they would be Deviants). They're made from a mass of cells grown in a vat, that's true, but this mass doesn't form an organism. They're either dead or, if living, unable to really live on as a whole organism. As far as creating a living organism is concerned, they are a failure.

                            Now, while a name is "just" a name, we've already seen in this very topic how much it can cause misunderstandings, and here it may cause a wrong impression about the actual themes of the Lineage.

                            Other than that, while Vat has an artificial connotation, it already has a strong theme of biomass. Vat Grown I think to be a very good option, referring to the way the biomass was obtained rather than the "organism" itself.

                            Another idea I just had is to name them Roboti, the original word used in the play R.U.R., as I think the roboti from the play are specially good examples of the themes of the Lineage and it would bypass the matter entirely (despite bringing certain confusion with the later meaning of the word robot).

                                        Some of my thoughts for the Pestovani:

                                        In stories of genetically engineered servants there is a frequent focus on the conflict between what they were created to do and their fundamental worth as a human or human-adjacent. That's certainly the case with some of the inspirations for the Pestovani. I think a good way of exploring this

                                        The Pestovani are a created servant lineage, like the Tammuz and Unfleshed, but their relationship to the role is quite different. They are servants by intent, not by nature. Indeed there's nothing in their Humour or Torment that makes them particularly servile or adapted to labor. Instead this element is expressed entirely through their Disquiet. People unnaturally compartmentalize the Pestovani into narrow, rigid, and devalued roles. Frequently these are negative stereotypes that justify dehumanizing attitudes of suspicion or exploitation, but even when they are positive or neutral stereotypes they are still devaluing roles like "one of the few good ones" or a "token friend," or "the help." Breaking free of these roles, even by something as minor as having the wrong opinion, will shock and frighten those in the grip of Disquiet and the Promethean will rapidly be recompartmentalized into a darker, less trusted role. And because the roles are so narrow and the rules of them frequently unstated it is difficult for a Pestovani to stay within them even if he wants to.

                                        Unsurprisingly the Pestovani have picked up a reputation for rebellion, although this too has nothing to do with their nature.

                                        While their Torment does not have anything to do with this theme directly, it is designed to play badly with their Disquiet. It makes it more difficult for them to play along with inflicted roles even if they want to, It's also an important reminder of their promethean nature, which is important because I think they need reminding of that.

                                        Tell me if this part makes sense:

                                        Because the Disquiet so often devalues them, quite a few Pestovani take the attitude that even if they are not human yet, they at least have (or at least should have) all the same worth and dignity. Now that's a good lesson if it is a hard earned step on the Pilgrimage, but it can be skipping quite a few steps for younger Prometheans who don't have a grasp on what dignity and worth even mean. This is not an attitude inherent to the lineage, just one that the circumstances they often find themselves in bring about.

                                        I also think that there is a small but notable minority (which with Promethean numbers, might mean two actual Pestovani) who are seeking to gain humanity but not to become humans. These few reason that humanity is just a quality humans have, not one restricted to them. They still seek to complete the Great Work they just don't intend to be quite human at the end of it. If any of them have achieved this, no living Promethean has heard about it.



                                        On another note, I have this idea that their demiurge died in the process of creating the first one. Basically he was working with only animal cells, but he fell in the vat during the final experiment and his body became the mass for the first of the Pestovani. As for when this happened, I could see these experiments happening any time from the mid 1800s on.

                                        This raises the possibility that Pestovani may be made at least partially from other species so long as at least some human mass is involved. It may be quite common for them to have animalistic features for disfigurements. Other possible disfigurements include unnatural growths and strange bone structure.
