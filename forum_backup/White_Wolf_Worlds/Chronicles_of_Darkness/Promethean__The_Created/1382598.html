    The Swoon Alembic

    04-19-2020, 05:38 PM
    Hi,

    I'm running a mixed game with one player as a Created.

    I do however keep running into an issue during action scenes or tense scenes with the Swoon Alembic (Core, pg.145). This is a roll of 3 sources minus 1 fixed stat, a single success knocks them unconscious.

    This basically plays out as, once per round one opponent is eliminated as a threat. There is no reasonable way that a character who is putting XP/stats behind their powers is going to be failing that roll.

    This has obvious issues in fights with a central figure/threat but also in all sorts of action scenes or tense scenes like a chase scene (just knock them out) or an infiltration scene (the guard blocking the way? Not anymore) etc.

    It's not unique in its ability to 'one shot' a threat. A Mage can cast a spell to incapacitate a target. The Vampire could Dominate them, etc. but this one feels more...egregious because it seem there's nothing the target can do to meaningfully resist beyond 'have had a backstory that caused you to have a very high Stamina stat'.

    How do you GMs deal with these kind of powers or abilities that shut down entire classes of threats/types of scene?

                      Yes, Prometheans can replenish Pyros from contact with dangerous levels of electricity. Thing is, the amount of time they can draw from a given power source before risking shorting it out entirely is measured in turns and depends on how strong a source of electricity it is. They can't just replenish to full off any wall socket, basically.
                      They do have other ways to gain Pyros, granted (interacting with mortals for a while, sleeping near meaningful quantities of their element, encountering a thunderstorm, the sun rising), so it's not exactly the hardest fuel stat to keep topped off among supernatural critters, but it's not that easy. And, yeah, there's the whole "spending Pyros can cause Disquiet in witnesses and (if enough is spent) create or worsen Wastelands" thing. So, uh... yeah, most Prometheans probably shouldn't be just tossing it around very lightly.

                          Originally posted by Scutarii View Post
                          Interesting, I may need the player to walk me through Azoth, Pyros and their power stat again (I am juggling 6 splats worth of stuff so am leaving the finer details to the players). From what I've understood so long as they can plug themselves into a power source they're able to keep charged up with Pyros and in a modern city that's not much of a challenge.
                          Unless you're dealing with big stuff like power stations and train lines, most casually-accessible sources of electricity will short out after a couple of turns; meanwhile, pretty much every other source of Pyros is a drip feed or requires time at the scale of hours to generate.

                          Azoth affects how likely you are to inflict Disquiet, how quickly your Wastelands form and how far they spread, how far away your presence awakens Pandorans from, and how likely you are to succumb to Torment. You can choose to reduce it to a lower effective rating, but (without making use of a location-bound Merit) doing so means you're locked into that rating for at least a day, and returning to a higher rating doesn't refill your Pyros pool on its own; if you're familiar with Demon, think of it like going loud but in reverse — you have to build up the rating to be able to pull it out of your back pocket and you need to fill the extra capacity on your own, but if you've got the right Transmutations for the job then you're going to be extremely effective.

                          Not all threats are mortals. I am not arguing that a mortal should be able to resist a top end supernatural power. I am rather more concerned about things like animal monstrosities some mad-creator has thrown at them (why would an amalgam of animals be immune to unconsciousness? It has an organic body and the like), Changelings and other supernatural creatures that run off a more human biology, etc.
                          There are many ways for characters to be immune to unconsciousness. Pretty much everything in the franchise that can be described as "undead" is, as are Prometheans, Pandorans, and clones (including animal hybrids); anything that heals bashing damage at an accelerated rate likewise reduces the interval for which the knockout lasts, including werewolves, mummies (albeit this is redundant with the preceding "pretty much everything that can be described as undead" point), some Beasts (whether fully-templated or the remains of one that's undergone the Merger), and some Deviants (or Manticores); and Hunter's got a core Tactic whose effects are "everyone who participates in this Tactic gets a bonus to their Resistance traits against mundane or supernatural attempts to instill fear or doubt and can't be knocked out from bashing damage."

                          "This thing stays awake in the face of physical/mental trauma" is a quality that very seldom needs extensive justification when dealing with supernatural weirdness. (Also, to turn your question around, why would an amalgam of animals be vulnerable to an ability that is part of a powerset premised on affecting human minds? What Integrity is going to be imperiled by the breaking point the power applies to it?)

                          Getting access to this power involves at least a bit of time in the philosophic frameworks of either blending in among humanity or purging spiritual weaknesses, and keeping the power outside of those frameworks necessitates spending a limited resource (that you could instead use to buy Pilgrimage or Azoth) on becoming good at terrifying people for the rest of your character's existence; using any of the powers without looking like an obviously inhuman being and risking Disquiet necessitates becoming worse at Empathy until you deliberately fail a Social roll with a human being. This isn't a power that exists without contextual grounding.
