                                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                        I would actually just chop out or severely diminish Disquiet and Wasteland, and perceive the refinements as modes of excellence. The material's there.
                                        You mean just not have Prometheans have those aspects? Or have the alternate New Dawn just get rid of them?

                                        I would have preferred that those mechanics be different or not exist, yes, but what I'm interested in isn't so much changing canon as working with it. I'm not saying, "what if disquiet didn't exist", because canon directly contradicts that. I am saying, what if a group of Prometheans wanted to get rid of it without becoming human, and would they be able to work something out? That's something that doesn't require as much alteration of canon to work in, and it's something that can coexist with existing canon.

                                        And I actually would want the alternate New Dawn to be more complicated than that. The thing is, and I should have made it clearer that this wasn't my intention, I don't want the game to be "Prometheans do the magic ritual and become super-robots and everything's perfect". This alternate path I'm working on should be hard. It requires sacrifice. The opening post was meant to be more of a description of the most idealistic aspirations of its adherents.

                                        As others have noted, WoD monsters aren't so much better than humans as different (personally, I think that this is why Mage and Beast have been so controversial: they haven't always handled this well). So becoming a...what are we calling them? What are we calling this whole New New Dawn? Whatever. Doing it should be a trade. The Promethean has to give something up. It's a hard road, harder than becoming human.

                                        That's something else I want to emphasize- something I dislike is how so many defenses of the Pilgrimage make being human sound so perfect and wonderful and euphoric and so on. Not just because it's not really true, because it makes the game seem like...just, like, everything sucks, you bust your ass becoming human, then, ding! Adventure over, everything's perfect!

                                        This alternate New Dawn isn't about that. Behind all the idealism and shiny robot dreams, it's an uncertain future. It's not having a definite end. It's not knowing when you'll be able to say "this is enough". That's hard. But, and here's what I would emphasize if I were writing it, it's worth it. It's a good fight. Seeking knowledge, breaking barriers, improving yourself, questioning everything up to the very laws of reality- there's nobility in it.

                                        Back to gameplay, I really do want the New New Dawn to be a trade. For example, one idea I like is that it becomes harder or even impossible to conceal your true appearance, even when not using Pyros. That's something you have to sacrifice. It's about accepting that you are a living statue, and that it's fine to be a living statue- so you kind of can't really do that well at not looking like a living statue.

                                        Edit: About the other refinements- the problem is that, if what I've heard about 2e is accurate, they're all ultimately about becoming human and mortal. And the Centimani are being de-emphasized as a player option, and what I've seen seems to be, sadly, making them more cartoon villain-y and less "a strange and disturbing path, but one that might be valid". And about the difference, the Centimani are about revelling in being a monster, and most importantly seem to have some level of hostility towards humanity. This refinement would have some space for contempt, but mostly it either supports ambivalence towards or compassion for humanity.

                                        And more gameplay ideas about the New New Dawn: I like the idea that it makes initial social interactions harder but pays off in the long run. So if someone meets you, they know right away something's off about you. They know you're a living statue or a robot or whatever. And that's a pretty big social penalty. But because there's no disquiet hanging over their head, they can move past that. Nothing stops them from realizing that even though you're a living statue, you're a kind, generous, and brave living statue that they can trust.

                                                        Bear in mind that as long as you're trying to develop something that's supposed to coexist in a game with Prometheans-as-written, you're going to run into problems. For instance: “monsters aren't better than humanity, so much as they're different.” This is largely managed by the fact that monsters have flaws and weaknesses as well as strengths. Now take these “super-Prometheans” that are the end goal of your alternate New Dawn (BTW, don't we already have a thread for that?): they retain all of the benefits of being a Promethean and none of the drawbacks (because having all of the benefits and a better capacity to manage the drawbacks doesn't require a New Dawn, just Saturninus). This makes them better than human — which is the whole point, as I understand it.

                                                        So if there's a “better than human” endgame option out there, why would any rational and informed Promethean pursue the “human” endgame? As I said in the other thread, I don't have a problem with non-human “New Dawns” (become qashmal, become animal, become plant, etc.); I only have a problem with the “have your cake and eat it too” Promethean-without-drawbacks and similar alternatives — and even then only if you try to put them alongside “become human” in the same setting.

                                                        And I'm pretty sure you'll run into similar problems in other areas of the rules, because the game as written is calibrated to the notion that becoming human is desirable; so alternatives that are based on the notion that transhumanism is desirable will at the very least introduce cognitive dissonance, and very likely mechanical clashes.

                                                        So I don't recommend trying to add transhuman options to the existing game; if you're going to try for transhumanism, you really should just change the game's calibration.
