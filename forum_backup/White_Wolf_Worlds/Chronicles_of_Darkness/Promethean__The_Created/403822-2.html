                            An Integrator has pulled off an elaborate tricks years in the making, and now has a piece of Infrastructure originally designed for producing new Angels. She knows that the Machine that created her is flawed, that it cannot be redeemed... but perhaps she can take its place? The obsession to create grows burns in her mechanical heart as she pores over the occult matrices used to create Covers out of thin air, but she wants to refine the process further, to create new life and cement her role as a replacement demiurge in the universe. The factory explodes in a shower of lightning and its master is immediately consumed by the blaze... but not before Aether was injected into the veins of the vat-grown body at its core. The new life stumbles out of the wreckage, their pale skin covered in tattoos of sacred mathematical formulae, and they begin a long Pilgrimage.

                            What does it mean to be a Cover with no true identity behind it, a spy without a cause or an enemy? The new life does not know, but they dream of gears and angels when they sleep.

                                        Originally posted by Leliel View Post
                                        Thevetat, The Mirror's Mendicant
                                        Finally got back to this:

                                        Storytelling Hints: Thevetat often comes off as wiser than he feels he has a right to be-he's never truly gotten over the fact that his intended purpose was a colossal failure, and he's convinced himself that until he has completed the Magnum Opus, his insights will be fundamentally tainted by the karma that gave birth to him. A long time ago, he blamed himself for existing, and after a particularly misguided decision on his part, came to believe he did not deserve humanity because of it-and in one of the most mixed blessings in the history of the Divine Fire, had also come to realize he had a right to exist. Seduced by Flux, Thevetat attempted to redefine himself as an Asura, one of the semi-divine demons born of people defined in their previous life by noble but self-destructive passions, especially righteous anger and the desire to change the world by force alone. This made him, if anything, even more miserable over time-his very humour wants him to be a teacher, and Asuras are monstrous warriors by nature and purpose. Nobody wants to be allies with such awful beings, let alone friends-but the actual nature of the Mirror's Mendicant indirectly saved him, when a Radiant Noble was drawn to the strange, surreal anomalies of Light surrounding his self-made monastery. She taught him that what he was doing wasn't the hope for a rebirth he claimed, but a habit he picked up to fill the hole his despair left behind-and then taught him to hope again, even through the growing Disquiet. Coming to his senses, Thevetat shed his Flux in a roar of Tin to understand what real righteous anger was, and from there found his way back to the path. He's realized the real curse of the Promethean condition is the simple fact that they do not have any personal karma-every curse on his existence is the repayment for the crimes of other creatures, and the tragedy is that he is to blame for none of it. By all rights, he should be able to happily continue the Saturnine Night indefinitely, but the very nature of the Created is an injustice forever inflicted on them, a barrier to Nirvana one that can never be rectified except by forcing himself into the wheel of reincarnation to ascend or fail on his own merits. This revelation hasn't made him bitter, though-he has also realized it isn't humanity's fault he brings out the worst in them either, it's only his demiurge's hubris and abuse of the natural order (this is one of the reasons he finds Theosophy a ridiculous philosophy, though).

                                        These days, the rage Thevetat once felt has been expended and calmed, and he's mostly a pacifist-in fact, the one thing he Calcified from Stannum was his ability to instantly nonlethally defeat someone suffering from Disquiet. Centimani and amoral walkers of the Pilgrimage quickly discover both his sordid past and the fact he is not an idiot, however-he Calcified the Disruption Alimbic from the Flux Transmutations as well, and in fact did so for the purpose of saving Centimani from themselves; it's his personal mission in life to turn at least one Freak away from the path that leads nowhere, and he is fully aware that most would rather eat him than stand still long enough for him to attack their self-justification, so he quite intentionally parlayed with Flux permanently in order to force the issue (thankfully for him, Flux is entropic, not evil-there is no physical law which states this is doomed to failure). He's also developed the quirk of giving funeral rites to any Pandorans he or the throngs he has allied himself with have killed; he's come to believe the reason they can't redefine themselves as Prometheans is that they actually do have personal karma, because they have souls-of pretas, the hungry ghosts driven by instinct. Yes, said pretas likely deserved such a miserable incarnation, but they are still people-and this fate is ultimately temporary. Once they are no longer a threat, he feels, it's better to at least recognize that was a person with hopes and dreams once.

                                        Refinement: Cuprum. Having worked out his self-hate through Stannum, understood his nature through Plumbum, and having realized the nature of negative karma through Cobalus (actually his personal favorite, as it allowed him to skip the messy process of therapy to impart inner peace directly), Thevetat has dedicated himself to learning what an actual teacher is, and from there immerse himself in the true natural order of things-being a true stranger to it, he needs to learn harmony academically as well as intrinsically before he can be one with it.

                                        Lineage: None, as of yet. While Thevetat is sure he's not an Extempore (he has Elpis visions), there's two reasons he has not made progeny, yet. The first is a religious conflict-if he fails, in his view, he's condemning at least one preta to an even more miserable existence for a period, likely more, and if he succeeds, then there's another living violation of karma in the world, one that echoes no human but humanity itself, increasing his progeny's misery tenfold. The other is a more substantial moral one-he's almost certain his generative preparation would involve mummifying another person alive, or something similar. Actually doing that himself is out of the question, and he's been so sure his act of multiplicato will be successful teaching of another Promethean he hasn't bothered scrounging the world for a suitable corpse. Still, if he is convinced there's a less horrifying way of making a perfect student and he's convinced there is no other way, he has no particular objections to the generative act-he knows the New Dawn is possible for his progeny, and again, pretas don't end up that way without deserving it.

                                        Humour: Dreamstuff. Somewhere between driven ectoplasm and intellectual phlegm lies Thevetat's personality core. He is naturally introspective and calmly self-aware about his motivations, and easily turns that towards empathic understanding of others-he's not always good at reading others, but he's naturally curious about the inner lives of other individuals and very aware of the web of subconscious drives, social influences, and fate that drives humanity and himself. Normally he is at peace with this, but when Torment strikes, he becomes painfully aware of how separate he is from the normal, and usually functional, mass of humanity. He feels alienated and ignored, and he blames his darker personality traits for everything that has gone wrong in his life, leading to spectacularly self-destructive acts in misguided attempts to expurgate the flaws in his spirit (and naturally, it is never enough, only ending his penance when his mind clears and he realizes how pointless his actions have been). His associated element is illumination-he must sleep in an area so well-lit there is no darkness but shadows to regain Pyros in his sleep. Ironically, this also means he leads an often nocturnal existence, as that is easiest to do during the day.

                                        Bestowment: Akashic Records. By meditating for five turns, Thevetat may spend one Pyros roll Intelligence + Resolve. For every success, his player may ask the Storyteller one question about the past, present, or immediate (within two days) future of a sentient person, a place, or an item he has encountered or had described accurately to him (though the later case inflicts a -2 dice penalty) that can be answered in seven words or less. The Storyteller must answer to the best of their ability-if they cannot phrase it in proper terms, the question is not wasted. While Thevetat can use this Bestowment on something multiple times, this comes at a cost-every reading he uses on a specific thing besides the first inflicts a cumulative -1 penalty to his next Torment roll, as being so close to true karma reminds him of how far he is from mortality.

                                        Disquiet: Thevetat's Disquiet is unique, as it actually inflicts admiration-at first. Anyone who pays attention quickly realizes it's not a healthy admiration either, the vicvtims always compare him favorably to themselves-he's more handsome, more wise, more honest, and so on-and he always seems to be judging them for their repeated failures to live up to his ideal. At first, people under the influence just ignore him to avoid their own growing shame and self-loathing, but as Disquiet progresses to its later stages, its victims gradually have a revelation that Thevetat is not as perfect as he seems-and it infuriates them, to realize they have been shamed and spat on by a hypocrite and a liar. By the time the lynch mobs come, they're far too morally outraged at his "deception" to hear his words of pleading as anything but more baldfaced lies pretending to be righteousness.

                                        Wasteland: The Divine Fire of dreamstuff changes the relationship between what is real and what is imaginary-mirages become common, objects lose solidity, and the inhabitants of Thevetat's Wasteland become prone to delusions and mood swings as what was previously subconscious becomes conscious and vice versa. As it worsens, improbable and unlikely events, especially those relating to superstitions (step on a crack, break your mother's back) become a fact of life, and bits of the Dreamlands flicker in and out of the waking world. Unlike most Wastelands, the inhabitants don't become grouchy or violent, just weird-the Light doesn't like hurting people. Unfortunately, the Dreamlands that manifest are those that correspond to the jealousy and desperation Thevetat feels-in other words, places where Amanojaku lair and plot. At extreme ends, Gates of Polished Horn may spontaneously form from random detritus in the area, ready to serve as portals for the eager invaders.
