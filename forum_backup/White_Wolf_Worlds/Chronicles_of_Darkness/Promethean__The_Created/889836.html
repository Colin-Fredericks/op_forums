    Hack - Principle as God-Machine?

    05-13-2016, 09:27 AM
    I like crossover games. If not as a mixed party, then at least having a game where vamps, wolves, mages, lings, and the others get together and can interact in a giant sandbox, much like Chicago did. In 1e, I used New York. Now, with 2e out, I've been using a custom designed city. Anyways, with the wealth of games out, I've decided to narrow everything down to just seven game lines - Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Promethean, Changeling, Geist, and Hunter. Vampire, Werewolf, Hunter and Changeling have been finalized and Mage is being worked in to my current all-'ling plot.

    So, I'm turning my attention to working Promethean into the Cage now. And... I find that, from my time playing Demon, I really like using the God-Machine. I like how the angels work, how everything is interconnected, I like the focus on strange yet modern alchemies and techgnosis. Meanwhile, I find that the Principle feels... kind of soft. Its just there. It interferes with the Pilgrimage, but nothing else really stood out. I remember there was a plague angel that took revenge on people in an orgy (rapists?), but I never got a sense of what or why beyond "because angelic-like beings." And I remember that, at the end of the Water of Life Chronicle, an arch-qashmal took a bunch of Prometheans to some Infrastructure, where they could use the gears to... do something, I forgot.

    Anyways! The point is that I think that I want to merge the God-Machine into my Promethean game and have the Principle and its qashmal become the same thing as the God-Machine and its angels instead of being separate. I've already worked on ways to fit Deva Corp into alchemists and the like.

    So, can anyone think of any reason why I shouldn't do this? That it would be bad for the game? I know that the qashmal have a separation of elipdos/flux energies that fuel evolution/mutations in the world, which doesn't seem to be a function of the GM, but I think that can be fixed with how I want to run the GM. Any other problems, ideas, thoughts, comments?

              when you talked about merging the GM and the Principle, I assumed about mixing all of their cosmologies which may include crossover with demon, hence the Falling- but apparently this is not the case.

              Anyway, I didn't said the GM doesn't mutate the world- but it is not its end goal. It's goal is maintaining order, which includes regulations and transformations- it is not the Weaver from Apocalypse, which tries to keep the world in eternal stasis. But it does work toward maintaining a specific state- it doesn't try to evolve the world as an whole, or change the laws of the universe. It doesn't care about the people who work for it or serve it- everything is a tool, a cog in the machine, part of a greater order. The Principle is quit different in attitude- it seeks to bring change as an end goal. It seeks to transform the world into something else, something greater, something better or worse. It has a personal interest in prometheans achieving their Pilgrim, while it may even inspire certain individuals into making them in the first place. It isn't a chaotic force of nature- but it is not as rigid as the God Machine either. It is about adapting, changing, transforming. It doesn't act through complicated conspiracies and great facilities, but touch each individual with divine inspiration and whispers them about a new world.

              That is, when the two beings are separated. As a single entity, you should decide how much of the Principle is the God Machine, and what is its true "end goal"- Order or Change. Are the prometheans simply regulations in the great computing system which require being "fixed" by turning human, or are they a great potential for transforming the world and key elements in its long term plans. The God Machine and the Principle can, undoubtedly, be mixed together to the same entity- but it would require some changes from how each is presented in its own gameline.

                              The key matter for me is theme. The GM is a vast alien entity whose interests in humanity are inscrutable and faint, while the Pyros is tied specifically to human aspiration. The GM has a usefor humanity, but it's not philanthropic, to be sure. The Pyros' Principle may indeed be as opaque and soulless as the Purpose, but it engages its subjects individually and relies on human behavior to achieve its purposes, whereas the GM rides over worlds roughshod and grinds cities and nations into pulp.
                              How this figures, replying to your points above:

                              -Descent from the God-Machine: Right, what is the connection? The Pyros is something alive and transformative, and now it's part of the GM, an energy caught up in its geartrain or somehow turning it. It shares a Purpose. My usual take on the GM-is-the-Pyros-is-the-GM are that the Pyros represents a power source for the GM, which have now become decoupled. Both are called out as Divine forces, so it stands to reason they are related. The Pyros seeks to drive change and confront stagnation, but the GM is less defined, and doesn't drive change so much as it effects change to reap the result. Does the Pyros' function continue as a GM appendage, and is the ensuing chaos (Firestorms, Disquiet, Wastelands) something the GM actually intends?

                              -Alchemy and Refinements: These are essentially soulworks. Promethean alchemy is classical in its conception, rather than overtly modern, and Refinements follow that conception. If you update that expectation, do the Refinements change, and how? For example, the Refinements are keyed to alchemically significant elements, but the expansion of the periodic table challenges the symbolism of, say, Copper, or Bronze. Plenty of common knowledge about various non-Refinement elements to challenge alchemical symbolism, such as the pharmacological and energy uses of Lithium, or the lifecycle of a star's elements from hydrogen to helium and beyond. Do Prometheans then view the New Dawn as some sort of superheavy metal in some hypothetical valence? And Refinements, in the original conception aren't devoid of Divine design, but represent a set of mysteries to be unlocked, and a set of powers that call on the Pyros' divine energies.

                              -Lineage: This'll probably change in 2e, but for me Lineages were about a literal connection to the original generation of that line. That generation might have disappeared into myth, but the distinctness of each Lineage and its surrounding mythology drove home the idea that Demiurges were extraordinary men and women who were in the right time and place to birth something new into the world. However, the 2E version of it being something of a recipe (arguably, let's not argue) fits more in line with the idea of it being a pattern. But if that's the case (and I'm not up on Demon or Flowers of Hell), are these configurations accidental, and when they are not, why would a free-willed Promethean be more useful in this role than a low-ranking angel built for that purpose?

                              -Centimani: In this function, I'd see them as Promethean Integrators. They refuse the Pilgrimage, because it makes them into mortals, where they are just grist for the GM and no longer part of a divine Purpose. So they debase their forms into mockeries, they churn up Flux, and they try and become occult matrices in themselves. Maybe the Created are conscious of this, maybe not, but if this is the case, the Prometheans are the true Fallen, rejecting their creator's designs, seeking a different end.

                              Your conception of the GM seems a bit more spiritually alive than mine. I think I see it a great big thresher, built to harvest and consume, lacking any actual sort of consciousness, and essentially an out-of-control machine without anybody actually at the wheel. It's not so much divine as it is capable of doing a god's work, but it is ultimately a machine, built because whatever hand that fashioned it had grown tired of doing the work itself. Cults, alchemies, and other investigations into its mysteries never find any sort of actual meaning or intent behind it, because there is no longer a meaning or intent to be found. The god is gone, there's a cinder block on the gas pedal, and it's going to keep going until it breaks down or crashes into something. A less cynical version of this would be to include things like Demons and Prometheans as failsafes and exhausts, sacrificed to prevent a fatal error (a multiversal blue screen of death, perhaps). The Pyros then is some sort of repair-function meant to purge faulty parts from sabotaging the system. Or maybe it's a flaw in the system, a point of friction where heat builds up and can't escape, destined to throw the entire machine into a halted state.

                                      Are you asserting that Lineages are uninspired human creations as written in PtC, or just in your chronicle? In 1e terms, the former is simply not the case, and 2e isn't revealed enough to remove the miraculous from standard progenerative acts. I'm not saying you have to keep them, just that I could argue all night and week that bog standard Promethean hinges on an engaged and boisterous god-essence, and the remove of humanity from the Created is a divide that Divinity seeks to bridge. And I traded all my vigor for rancor in the Great Home Depot Hat Debacle of the day.

                                      As to why those philosophies matter is that they are reliant on phenomena that this conception entangles explicitly with the GM, rather than skirting the issue. Refinements aren't philosophies, they're patterns and praxes that yield real results. Philosophy deals with the immeasurable and the unmeasured, action and will, and the serve to spackle over the gaps that befuddle a whole understanding of existence. Refinements and the Pilgrimage are scientific, and yield predictable results (Milestones, Transmutations, Mortality), even if the variables escape isolation. That means the Refinements and alchemy as the Created understand them are literally true, and that those truths have either premises or conclusions in the God-Machine.

                                      Also, Order/Fate/Destiny: this is where the Pyros is complementary, let's say. The Machine works through plans, requires complex infrastructure, and brutally enforces its needs by whatever means. its angels do engage human-level operatives, but they also act to complete their functions, and retire only after completion or failure. Qashmallim, on the other hand, often spawn at desperately inconvenient times, are often poorly equipped to complete their missions, and their efforts are never duplicated, and sometimes there's another working at cross-purposes. The Pyros that sends them only has a Principle, and that's to bring about change, whether that's through emergence or dissolution. This differs from a Purpose, an intent or goal.

                                      Big question though: are these changes intended for Promethean players? Azoth and its journey from fungible Pyros to distinct human soul gives shape to a Promethean for the player who inhabits the character, but if no one is playing a Promethean, so much is a moot point. As a non-Promethean character, I probably wouldn't care whether or not that Osiran had clockwork heart stolen from Infrastructure. As a player, I'd be leery of a GM connection, but that's just me.

                                      (This is all in friendliness and creative feedback. Any edges or elbows are meant to sharpen distinctions, not to assert Real True Prometheanism.)
