                                            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                            You're right. Both are terrible monsters who are super keen to throw their humanity out of the window so they can become an even more terrible monster.

                                            Man, that was easy!
                                            Yep! And this is a horror game, so I'm completely fine with the pursuit of power and immortality turning you into a horrible monster.

                                            It's just that this is a game about being monsters, and as horrible as the Ordo Dracul they're not just fully playable but incredibly sympathetic. In fact, you could make a case that they're the "most lesser evil" among the Covenants, in that their end goal would be the most beneficial to humanity. That doesn't make them not monsters, but it makes them twisted vampire mad scientists that someone could look at and think, "this is a shitty situation and these guys are making the best of it".

                                            Because that's the other side of "it's a horror setting". Things kind of suck. You could die or be hollowed out by magic rats at any moment through no fault of your own. You're a weak, squishy, ignorant nobody. "Evening the odds" has been brought up before, but it bears repeating. ChroD is a dark, scary setting, and the games hammer home that you often have to do things you'll regret to survive. It isn't just the monsters- Hunters can do horrific things, break law after law, and modify themselves into something inhuman, but the alternative is to just lie down and be complacent cattle for the monsters.

                                            That, to me, is the best way to look at alchemists sympathetically while also keeping them twisted and monstrous.

                                            They are the ones who didn't lie down.

                                            Hunters are the people who saw monsters preying on humanity, and didn't lie down. Alchemists saw diseases, aging, and death preying on humanity, and didn't lie down. Both of them work with powers they don't understand and do things that are hard to justify, but the alternative is, well, death. The Hunter says she refuses to let some Draugr drink her or her friends dry. The alchemist says she refuses to let cancer or aging or an accident bring her or her friends down. Both light a candle. They may burn out. Their fire may burn out of control. In fact, those things are probably likely. But without them, darkness is all there would be.

                                                            So, snark aside, I finished the section on Alchemists on Saturday, and this thread seems lively enough to warrant response.

                                                            So, I'm gonna break my opinion down on two points, being a) Are they portrayed as too unsympathetic?, and b) are they unplayable as PC's? I'm gonna start on that second one.

                                                            Are Alchemists Unplayable as PC's?

                                                            No.

                                                            Like seriously, if you want to play an Alchemist, the only mechanic you're missing is how exactly the make their potions and such, and that doesn't take much in the way of houseruling-extended action, hour per roll, Intelligence,Resolve, or Composure+Science, Occult, Expression Or Other+Magnitude depending on the Alchemical Tradition you're trying to evoke, 5 successes per level of the Alembic you're trying to take a distillation from(persistent level as 5, 1 Pyros level as 10, 2 pyros level as 15, 3 pyros level as 20). I thought that up just now. Everything else is available, except EXP costs. Go play your happy asses off. I wouldn't be opposed to fleshing that out in a future book.

                                                            Are Alchemists too unsympathetic?

                                                            Eh? Ish.

                                                            Let me get two biases out of the way before I expand on that The first is, having been here during the entire development cycle, I have a shit ton of spite for the Alchemist bias. Being frank, the road to this point has been filled with a lot of people who were obnoxious and rude about the entire thing, and that pisses me off more than anything else. I was really soured on the entire idea of alchemists before they got announced, and when people threw an even bigger bag of shit when the blog came out, I probably would have been well satisfied if they had been cut all together. That I am impressed with the Alchemist delivery in spite of the hateful vocal minority really inclining me to hate it anyways speaks to fact that Matt and his team know how to juxtapose elements in really evocative ways.

                                                            The second bias is that we've had a pretty direct address of the sympathetic alchemist in the sidebar. It's short and can't cover the dichotomies super well, but it's not like it's not addressed, and its' sense is fairly well grounded-as a general rule, players have enough common sense to figure out how to run more sympathetic alchemists based on everything in here. There's enough material for players to figure out how to make the spectrum work. My grand point is that it's not like it's absolutely ignored, so to complain at length like it is strikes me as useless.

                                                            Now that out of the way, let's me give my more level opinion. Because it is a little too unsympathetic, but not to any point I actually care about.

                                                            As Gecko blew out of proportion, this game is about taking a step outside of our assumptions to look at humanity and discover things about, to try and slip into a place where we can understand our condition from a detached position. For me, this is a positive, as that invites me to gain wisdom on flaws and strengths and understand more deeply things that can be done as well as appreciating my life more as it is. So for the purposes of the game, it pays to have an antithesis, people who are convinced that humanity has nothing worth examining and nothing to appreciate, people desperate to escape. And it just so happens, Insatiate alchemists neatly fit that slot. The problem, of course, is that by having the Insatiate(by the way, Insatiate, not Insensate, various people who are getting it wrong) as our main focus, it does miss out on the alchemists who create story hooks without being antagonist-the alchemist who thinks the only answer is a Promethean's Vitriol and begs them to help is not an indecent example.

                                                            There's some issue in terms of the matter of space and word count and intent, but for now I'm going to ignore that. I think a few story hooks before going into the Insatiate might have been good, because there is some room for the discussions that come up with people who know of Prometheans and advanced alchemy and have needs they might try to negotiate for-hell, the book outside of the sidebar mentions that. It is fairly heavy on the one side of the argument, and I can see some bother to be had over that.

                                                            Not a lot though.

                                                            Why I'm Okay with the Insatiate barring Metaphysics.

                                                            For starters, as the mention of outside the sidebar implies, I actually found that even with the heavy-handed presentation, there were still lots of concessions(and that's without applying any outside "usuallys"). There's enough mentioned to keep it, for me at least, from weighing too heavily to one side.

                                                            On top of that, I also find that a lot of those particular hooks don't necessarily need alchemists to make them happen, it just speeds the setup for Prometheans more if you have people outside of the characters push it. For example, the "My daughter needs help, and only your Vitriol can do the trick" scenario posits that an alchemist needs to present for that, but does it? Could a Promethean, whose been working on building a relationship with this family, not find out about the condition, try the transmutations they know, and then reach the hypothesis that maybe they're own Vitriol is what's needed? An Alchemist confirms those theories, or finds the flaws with them-they do great as sounding boards for the ideas, but you don't really need them to push the matter. And honestly, when an alchemist is there to push the matter, odds are it's because there's more on their own plate than simply being a good Humanitarian.

                                                            Like mages, alchemists are defined by what they do, and in relationship to the Prometheans, it's what they do with their alchemy in relationship to the Promethean's existence and goals that shape their place in the narrative. In many ways, the more antagonistic options are actually wider and offer better questions than the sympathetic ones, because these are realms that they are best allowed to guide Prometheans through, places they alone can show the way of. In a fit of irony, a lot of the people here who want more sympathetic alchemists are also the ones who harp on more about the "Humanity is the best" idea, often mangled into stupid configurations as it is. If humanity is not really a thing worth aspiring to, then it makes sense that the more powerful a human is, the more anti-aspirational they become, because their options for demonstrating those anti-aspirational attributes of humanity are that much more increased. In this way, the Insatiate form a definite anti-honeymoon collective of humanity, a very solid bunch that show that being human isn't sunshine and roses, but a collection of flaws, biologically, sociologogically, etc. If you want to prove that humanity is not "Hur-Dur, the best", Insatiate alchemists go a long way in revealing just that. If humanity not being great is a point you're going to make, you have to accept it of alchemists too.

                                                            I think, though, that even in the negativity of the alchemists there's something great for both Prometheans and players to pick up from. When I first joined the forums ages ago, I joined under the username of Being, with my signature being "All strive to be something else." It was sort of sardonic, but it had a great measure of truth. For Prometheans, it's easy to think that it'll all be worth it when they reach their destination of humanity, but having even someone, even or perhaps especially someone who's cruel, reinforces that there's always something bitter that needs fixing. It shows for the Promethean that regardless of form, life is a journey, that flaws so far are a part of life, and that we all share in the suffering, even when it's different. The destination is not about finally achieving happiness-happiness is not the whole of life. What the Insatiate lack is equanimity, serenity, the inner stillness that comes from being in.

                                                            And this leads to the other reason why, despite the heaviness, I don't mind so much-the Divine Fire.

                                                            Why I'm okay with the Insatiate including Metaphysics.

                                                            Now, honestly, I'm glad they didn't lean too much on this. Humans can be ugly without having to warp it and justify it with the Divine Fire. It'd be so easy to write off alchemists as antagonists under the premise of "Even the more sane and kindly alchemists will eventually do harm to a Promethean because of Disquiet." Instead, they got to the point and let it do the talking. And likewise, it's not ignored-they make it pretty clear that the Divine Fire, which is change incarnate, fucks these guys up with all the Conditions they get. But it cannot be overstated-too much exposure to the Divine Fire means you lack equanimity. It's possible to be a force of change while also maintaining a personal state of harmony, but the Divine Fire doesn't tolerate it-it pushes and shoves and demands change, fuck your comfort and place. As such, I have no problem believing that the more you do alchemy with the Divine Fire, the more everything is not enough. Going, again, to the example of "My daughter needs help, and only your Vitriol can do the trick", I don't personally believe for a second that a man who has a theory on the subject is going to accept No as an answer, because at that point, odds are that the alchemist has had enough exposure to Pyros to basically be in the kinetic state-this must move forward.

                                                            And it's a danger. While madness might be overselling it, the Divine Fire is change, and it relies on the strong conduits of obsession and desperation. The better you are at working with it, the more you are those things. I think that sets the stage for eventualities, that most of the people you see working with the Divine Fire are going to be Insatiate. Those who aren't probably make a sure point of saying "DON'T TEMPT ME, FRODO" to themselves when they get involved with it.

                                                            Conclusion

                                                            Still, I get the frustrations. It's not like that window doesn't exist, that there isn't room to ask "But what if?" Given the matter of space, it's not surprising, but it would have been nice to see some more exploration of the middle ground before doing the deep dive.

                                                            At the end of the day, though, I think the deep dive is more important for the game, that the Insatiate make points that Promethean need to hear more than what the moderates can tell them. So, are they too unsympathetic? Ish. Enough to note, not enough to care.
