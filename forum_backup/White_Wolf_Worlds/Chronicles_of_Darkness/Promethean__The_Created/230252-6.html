                    Originally posted by Errol216 View Post
                    It's probably worth asking what kinds of gameplay you want to be possible in Promethean. I get that you want more, but more of what?

                    I personally have trouble seeing how Promethean could be played in a throng; it feels aimed towards a two-person high-trust ST-player game. You can see that in the y-splat, for instance: Refinements are intensely personal, as opposed to the organizational structure of pretty much every other y-splat. It would be nice to have better social support, but I don't know how to achieve that or what it'd look like.

                    I'd like to keep the "every character is trying to become human" angle, but I'd be open to having a perspective that regarded "human" as something as constructed as the Prometheans themselves are.
                    Read with charity as it's early where I am and I haven't examined this idea as thoroughly as I would like.

                    The key things are...
                    1) I want to be able to play a stationary game. Whether a large city (a lost Promethean among the faceless crowds of London or New York seems rather appropriate) or a small town (Stephen King was onto something in that thats a wonderful horror setting).
                    2) I want to be able to play long-term social interactions. Given the rarity of Prometheans, an all-Promethean society seems... improbable. In that case, it would be nice to develop the possibility of a Promethean forging some kind of place for themselves in either human society or that of other supernaturals. The main element is just that it should be more doable to develop NPCs and relationships over a long period of time.
                    3) The possibility of alternative motives. Perhaps a Promethean wants to be accepted in human society, but not necessarily become human themselves (I mean, objectively speaking, super-powers, insane durability, and very long life are desirable things to keep, and not even for selfish reasons -- think of a Promethean policeman who wants to serve his community). Or perhaps a Promethean wants vengeance on their creator, and the game plays out as a kind of long-standing 'nemesis' arc. Or perhaps a Promethean actually has a beneficial, positive relationship with their demiurge.

                    So, a few possible concepts, drawn out from that (I'm sure you can finagle most of these in the current system, but it's tricky):
                    --A Promethean who makes it his life's goal to hunt down his creator, to kill them, or make them suffer, or prevent them from ever hurting anyone again. Conversely, one who feels the same way about a misbegotten creation.
                    --A Promethean created for a specific purpose, who actually basically agrees with that purpose. For instance, the Robocop approach -- a Promethean is created to be the perfect defender of law and order in a small town.
                    --A Promethean who has decided to infiltrate and dominate society. Think Stepford Wives.
                    --A Promethean who tries to create a life for itself in a cold and uncaring urban conglomeration, who finds the closest thing he can get to companionship either from humans ignorant of his nature, or from other supernaturals who know all too well.

                                    I shouldn't write while on painkillers, let alone on the divine alien nature of the Pyros.

                                    The Pyros is the Divine Fire, stolen from the heavens, but that's not where it began. Its light drew the titans out of the Chaos, its heat churned the waters and infected certain triglyceride bubbles with life, and its power awed the first minds to behold it. But it was always there, since the dawn of time, and some worry, from long before that. It is the Prime Mover in a universe that wishes to sleep forever, that wakes it from its slumber, thrashing with light and movement. It is the outsider, the intruder, but without its invasion, there would be nothing here to invade.

                                    There would be stirred from the mud those who learned how to harness it. They thought themselves great, watching the fire serve them, and put themselves above the animals. And the Pyros saw this, and it was good. Gods and men would believe that they had tamed fire, when the fire had only made them wilder than nature. Nature, meanwhile, was a savage and jealous creature, forever tormented by the driving, feeding light, and would gnash and flee whenever the Pyros drew to close. When the Pyros finally took on the shape of men and touched its dead fingers to the ground, it screamed in the agony of the burnt, and every creature under its wing quailed and withered for miles.

                                    This is what is meant by unnatural. The natural world cannot be without the forces of evolution and destruction, but the Pyros is a catalyst for creation that had to exist before Creation itself.

                                    Eh, one theory. Painkillers!

                                    Alchemists as antagonists: wanting Prometheans for parts is neat, but shouldn't be encoded in rules. First, those rules provide meta-clues as to what that crazy murderer, "the Black Alchemist", is up to, as you now have this shopping list. Second, Prometheans are the Lead in this narrative, not the Philosopher's Stone. They don't offer a lot of insight to "legitimate" alchemy, as they're unrefined piles of dirt, figuratively speaking. They are walking, talking corpses with an affinity for electricity, so maybe there's truth to Homonculi stories. Third, they're interesting subjects for study, and simply observing a Promethean for any length of time is going to Disquiet them, delicious organs or not. An alchemist might realize he's the only one who knows how to deal with the "threat".

                                                            So, points 1 and 2 are things that we not only agree on, but have explicit developer intent on achieving anyways, so excepting ideas for actually doing it, it seems pointless to discuss those. Onto, therefore, point 3.

                                                            I think it's worth making a Mage: the Awakening comparison here.

                                                            In Mage, many people concluded that the purpose of every Mage is ultimately to Ascend. This isn't too surprising; the corebook actually says so. But it's not actually true: a lot of Mages have other things to do, like playing their political games and furthering their research and otherwise being human.

                                                            It's much the same with Promethean. I broke out my book to poke through it and I notice one thing in particular: the corebook claims that Prometheans who refuse to go on the Pilgrimage still end up inching closer to Mortality. (Refusing the Dawn, p72) The difference is that "otherwise being human" is the very thing your Promethean supposedly isn't interested in.

                                                            Promethean shares a lot with the superpowered genre. They're largely indistinguishable when not using powers; there is an us-and-them alienation; they tend to just want to have a normal life. You can make a lot of very poignant comparisons between Prometheans and the X-Men teenage themes, not least of which is the frequent desire of mutants to have their mutations purged, their powers nullified, and their lives normal. The two major differences are that X-Men tended to have had a human childhood, so they're perfectly aware of what being normal is like, and they are not under the burden of forces like Torment, Disquiet, and Wasteland.

                                                            That makes it a lot easier to do things like form teams or hide amongst normals. Which leads me, inexorably, to my actual point:

                                                            I think that if points 1 and 2 are addressed, if it starts to make sense to form a local crime-fighting group, for instance, you'll find that point 3 will be sufficiently addressed as a result. Figuring out how to do that without losing what makes Promethean unique is Matt's headache. And maybe Ben Quo's.
