        Hmm.

        OK, I actually think these guys are actually pretty sympathetic - the writeup maybe went too far in making it super-clear that they're not intended as the good guys, like Marvel's Civil War did with the pro-registration side.

        The unscrupulous scientist desperate for funding is a stock character in science fiction. So is the project leader who overrules his underlings who warn him that It's Not Ready. So is the overlooked glory-hound who will finally Show Them All when they cure death or whatever.

        I think what these guys are afraid of is, say, the Lizard from the Amazing Spider-Man film: someone who, afraid that they're going to run out of money before they've completed their (Great) work, uses an unfinished and dangerous prototype ... and ends up creating monsters.

        And they're right! That sort of thing is how you end up summoning Lillithim, creating Pandorans and, I dunno, whatever it is that Alchemists do that "turns them into monsters" in exchange for superpowers.*

        It's just that it never occurs to them that the poverty is the problem, not the poor people taking risks to escape it. They're policing in the right place, but not addressing the underlying problem. (Hey, like actual police!)

                    Originally posted by Gabby View Post
                    Edit: On further thought, it especially bugs me because so much else in the game is geared toward showing what a shitshow humans are. Disquiet is basically a dramatic way of conveying the message "Humans are stupid, violent bullies that react in the worst way possible to things that are different". Given that, the moral of this "humanistic" tale seems to be "aspire to fit in, so we'll stop tormenting you", which is a metaphor that I find perhaps even more uncomfortable than some of Changeling's themes, which I didn't previously think possible. It looks at the tale of Frankenstein's monster, and validates the villagers with pitchforks.
                    I take the opposite view on the validating the villagers mostly because most prometheans aren't going to be with the villagers. They are going to be with the leapers. The other sections of humanity that fall under of the same alienation (though less disquiet fueled) that differ from social norms and suffer under the shadow of the majority. Minorities such as African Americans, Hispanics, Gays and Trans all suffer the same "Get with the program or we'll be beat the living crap out of you" but still those communities exist and are quite rich for milestone mining for Promethean games. Nearly all cultures have the disenfranchised, the distraught, and the outcasts and Promethean naturally gravitates to them and nothing binds people together like shared suffering.

                    It's through this shared suffering that Promethean really shines as it starts asking "Who am I and who do I wish to be?". Topics of gender identity, politics, and more start coming into formation by direct experience the Promethean has and plays into the milestones. It's starts asking harder and harder questions. The nature of the Divine, The nature of self sacrifice for a group, The purpose of life it's self and more.

                    If they wish to be something more than human, they're more than certainly willing to have a go but even then there are mile stones. The nature of power, Should the strong care for the weak? Should humanity even be worth attempting if they are such pitiful bullies all the time and if so, why? (not that we don't already have a game about Transhumanism, it's called Mage but it would be interesting to see how Promethean deals with it.), What happens if you fully lose the humanity you had to begin with (Vampire) or even if you start to consider yourself human to begin with (Werewolf).

                    The theme of Alienation is quite a disturbing one and one which the game hammers home quite well but overall, to use a metaphor, the game isn't validating the guards in the death camp but rather the humanity of the minorities inside it. The minorities that share in the same isolation and stigma that Prometheans deal with and while the game should make more effort in highlighting it, it is a natural starting point of where most Promethean games end up.

                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                            But these “oppressed minorities” suffer from Disquiet every bit as much as the “oppressive majority” does. This runs counter to the “bound together by shared suffering” idea.
                            Every metaphor breaks down, the closer it gets to reality. But even here, opposed minorities don't find solidarity across demographics, they find it within their own. Press those demos together, make them fight for resources, and you will see no unity between them. This is why gangs proliferate in prison, for example, or why pink triangles were bottom of the heap in the camps.

                            That doesn't mean it's not a useful lens. A throng is its own society, cast against mortals and others who would crush them, for no other reason than their differences. Disquiet works well to communicate the "feel" of oppression, but it flattens the various "isms" that are at work. Sexism is a different beast than racism, and both are separate from the classism, ableism, homophobia, sizeism, and ageism that cut across those lines. And a Promethean can be on the wrong side of none, any, or all of these.

                                1st edition paints a bleak picture but honestly, using Disquiet as a cudgel for every single human interaction is both painful for storytelling and gets in the way of the theme of discovering humanity if used too much. As a houserule (for my one time actually setting up the game), I've ruled that disquiet affects first impressions, on how well said promethean is blending in with the subculture, and shared suffering that said peers of that subculture experience with the promethean. Shared suffering and experience, for better or for worse, does lay the foundation of including binding people into groups. especially if those groups are being persecuted.

                                In such groups, I've typically greatly slowed down the clock for disquiet for prometheans PCs to capitalize on the social trust that they have earned long enough for them to get a few milestones.
