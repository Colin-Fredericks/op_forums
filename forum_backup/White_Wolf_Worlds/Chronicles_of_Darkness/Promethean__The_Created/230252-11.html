        Again, don’t forget the Milestones: if they’re being kept, progress on your Pilgrimage can be measured in terms of how many Milestones are left to complete. Admittedly, since the Storyteller is the only one who knows what Milestones you have yet to complete, it’s not a measurement that the player is aware of. Sure, he knows how many Milestones he has completed already; but he doesn’t know how many are left.

        I’m not comfortable with a hard-and-fast rule such as “once you reach Azoth 10, you can become a mortal”. All I was suggesting was to replace Humanity with Azoth in the Crucible roll — and I’d amend that with a penalty for each Milestone you’ve yet to reach — not any sort of absolute criteria that must be met. You may attempt the Crucible roll any time you like, subject to Storyteller veto if he thinks you’re nowhere close to ready. High Azoth and completed Milestones are indicators that you’re probably ready; but it should be theoretically possible to Redeem with as little as a single dot of Azoth.

        I’d be half-inclined to make the Crucible roll be the one roll in the game that can be reduced below a Chance Die. If the dice penalties would reduce the dice pool below zero, you cannot succeed: either the Storyteller tells you this flat out, or he lets you roll a single die anyway — but a 10 doesn’t count as a success, and you have to roll higher than the amount the penalties exceeded the dice pool in order to avoid a botch (e.g., if your dice pool was reduced to -3, the ”botch threshold” is 3, not 1). And a botch on the Crucible roll should be a really bad thing, ranging from losing a dot of Humanity to gaining a new Milestone to having your Milestones be reset, forcing you to restart your Pilgrimage from scratch. You do not attempt the Crucible until you’re ready.

        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                Originally posted by Metamurpho View Post
                                Origin, Catalyst, Impetus -- I think they all work, and glad you dig the idea. And while I understand wanting the flip side of this to be involved too, I would worry that Destination would &quot;lock in&quot; what you want to be. I think Prometheans change their minds a lot about what the want out of the New Dawn.
                                Besides, we already have two mechanisms for “what do you want to become?”: Milestones, and the Athanor.

                                Go back to the definitions of Virtue and Vice as given in GMC:
                                • A character's Virtue should be something that gives her a great sense of self-actualization, but which she is often tempted to ignore. It's her higher calling, the personality trait that would be at the fore all the time if she didn't live in the World of Darkness. It should be both difficult to act upon and make her feel good when she does make the effort.
                                • A character's Vice, on the other hand, is a source of short-term comfort and a distraction from the world. Vice should be the easy way out, the sand he buries his head in, like a bully lashing out to avoid his own fear or an alcoholic drinking to forget.
                                Now let’s take that “Origin” idea and apply it to both, thinking of a newly-created Promethean as an organic machine that has yet to achieve any agency. The equivalent of “Virtue” for such a creation would be its Purpose: the role that its creator made it to fill. Filling that role is a higher calling, a means of self-actualization; and fulfilling one’s Purpose serves as a powerful source of positive reinforcement.

                                Conversely, the equivalent of “Vice” is a reflection of the fact that any time you create something as elaborate and complex as a Promethean, it’s bound to develop unanticipated behaviors: the Quirk represents some aspect of the Promethean’s personality which was not chosen for it by its creator: if this Quirk interferes with the Promethean’s Purpose, it’s typically viewed as a bug or a glitch, and is the sort of thing the creator might spend time and effort trying to purge from his creation. (“Quirk” may not be the best name for it; but hopefully I’ve successfully communicated the idea I’m thinking of.)

                                Note also the advice that GMC gives for changing Virtue and Vice. IMHO, Prometheans (being “works in progress” and conduits for the agent of change known as “Pyros”) should have more opportunities to change their Virtue/Vice equivalents: say, every time a Promethean resolves a Milestone, it can change its Purpose and/or Quirk to reflect the revelation that the fulfilled Milestone represents. Which brings me to:

                                Originally posted by Metamurpho View Post
                                Don't forget about the size of said Milestones, too -- per the core, they're supposed to vary in Vitriol reward.

                                And you know what would be awesome? Milestones/Burdens expressed as Conditions. Sometimes they'd be secret, sometimes the player would know about them... fun stuff to figure out. Not all of them, of course, but I think Conditions will fit very nicely into Promethean 2e.
                                Generally, the reason for the varying sizes of Milestones was to allow more momentous milestones to grant better rewards. But with a Milestones-as-Conditions mechanic, that distinction can be phrased as “the harder you have to work for it, the more you get out of it” — and that, in turn, can be addressed by having the more “momentous” Milestones grant more Vitriol Beats leading up to the resolution of the Milestone: as a Persistent Condition, it grants Beats (of Vitriol) whenever the Promethean makes significant progress toward resolving the Milestone. There might not be a need to distinguish between different intensities of Milestones — or there might be.

                                The other thing that I like about the “Milestone as Condition” approach is that the notion of a Burden becomes a natural extension of it: a Burden is merely a Milestone that inflicts some sort of penalty or downside on the Promethean while he’s working to resolve it — which would give even more opportunities to earn Vitriol, as you’d generate a Beat whenever the Burden complicates matters, not just when you make progress toward resolving it. (For the record, I like the notion of Burdens because they would provide another way besides Disquiet and Wasteland to illustrate the faulty nature of a Promethean — in this case, through its incompleteness. It would also provide a possible Bane-like mechanic: if you lose a point of Humanity and want to avoid losing more in a similar situation, take on a Burden that reflects the Breaking Point that you want immunity from. Unlike Banes, Burdens would reward you for playing them up, and you’d eventually want to resolve them before attempting the New Dawn: even if the developers decide not to apply penalties to the Crucible roll for unresolved Milestones, Burdens should apply such a penalty. Which brings me to:

                                Originally posted by Metamurpho View Post
                                I personally hate the Crucible roll. Something so monumental to a character and based off mostly plot elements shouldn't be reduced to something that can be mathematically gamed. Sure it's easy enough to excise, but it always rubbed me wrong that there was a most efficient and mathematically sound way to spend your XP to meet the "win condition". Much more mystery in this department, please!
                                I get where you’re coming from; but I don’t agree: “mathematically gaming” the Crucible roll is about the same in my mind as “mathematically gaming” social influence. I do think more mystery is called for; but I tend to think that it is best introduced by including “hidden factors” that the player isn’t privvy to in the Crucible roll (which is why I suggest penalizing the roll for unresolved Milestones) rather than by doing away with the roll entirely, which would force the decision of whether or not the Crucible succeeds entirely into the realm of Storyteller fiat.

                                Mind you, I’m OK with both approaches getting coverage in Promethean 2e; but I don’t want to see the Crucible roll option dumped completely because some gaming groups would prefer to handle it sans game mechanics. Which brings me to:

                                Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                First: I’m against requiring Azoth 10 in order to be able to Redeem: both Azoth and Humanity should help with different aspects of the New Dawn; but neither should be required to be 10.
                                Originally posted by MugaSofer View Post
                                Huh. Why not?
                                Paradoxically, for a reason very similar to the one just voiced by Metamurpho for doing away with the Crucible roll altogether: the Crucible should become available when story concerns indicate that it makes sense for it to become available, not when a rigid checklist of prerequisites are satisfied. There should be more Storyteller and Player freedom in deciding when it’s appropriate to attempt the Crucible than would be the case if it only became an option upon maximizing one of your character traits. Really, if I had to select one absolute condition that must be met before the Crucible can be attempted, it would be having no Milestones left to resolve, not Azoth 10 or Humanity 10; and even “no Milestones left” strikes me as a needlessly harsh restriction.

                                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
