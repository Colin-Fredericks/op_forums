    How do you affect players with disquiet without letting them in on it?

    11-19-2015, 11:20 PM
    Is there a good way to affect player characters with disquiet without directly telling the players about it?

      depends how "meta" you want to get, but I'd suggest going with descriptions of the Promethean's actions that would make the players hate the Promethean.

      "He smiles at you and thanks you for your help." could easily become something along the lines of "He looks at you with contempt and forces a smile. Thanks... he mutters by convention more than respect."

      "She's sitting at the café, looking at the streets and she sigh" -----> "She's sitting there, scanning the streets. Her eyes spot a young kid and you catch her licking her lips."

      "He offers his help with enthusiasm" ----> "He seems almost too eager to join you"

      The way you describe this particular character can have a huge effect on the way your players will interact with it. And it's fine if you "lie" to your players - that's what Disquiet is about, twisting their perception until they hate the Promethean.

      Maybe, at some point their perception could be twisted to the point where they open the Promethean's fridge and see a nice meat slab tagged "Caucasian T-Bone"
      Maybe your character's girlfriend is suddenly looking at every guy around, every time, and that stupid guy from the credit company is writing her letters again!
      Maybe that homeless guy should really get a job instead of hypnotizing every walker - you saw a guy on TV once who could do that.
      Maybe that barista with a tattoo on his forehead just served you a smoothie that tastes like hot, fresh blood.

      And if they commit horrible, horrible things to the Promethean? Well, after a while and a police investigation, they'll be told that it was just a steak. That the letters were generic credit offers. That the homeless man was simply begging. That the Barista made a strawberry smoothie, just like you asked for.

      And that's the real horror of Disquiet - it twists your vision until you can't understand that you're doing the wrong thing. But to pull it off correctly, you need to go smoothly. Act like they discover his evil plots a little more every time. Like they're the only ones realizing it.

      Make them roll Empathy, Investigation, etc... when they meet him, and give them misleading clues when they succeed.

              That's very similar to what I did in a Hunter game where the PCs interacted with a Promethean. I roleplayed the Promethean in a gradually more negative way, leading the PCs to react to this by viewing him and treating him more negatively. After things came to a head and they killed him over what turned out to be a misunderstanding, I dropped hints that their feelings of antipathy towards him began to fade for some reason. In-character they had no understanding of Disquiet or its effects, but out-of-character the epiphany this caused at the table was a great ST moment.

                                  Well, the Disquiet doesn't discriminate. Unless the players are the only people that the Promethean is in contact with, there's bound to be NPCs that suffer the effects of the Disquiet and they can then express thoughts like I described above to the players. Of course, they should be framed in a reasonable, sympathetic way, or else you'll be tipping your hand.

                                  At the end of the day, you can't really control what your players feel or how they react to something. You can only paint things in a certain way and hope for the best.

                                  If I wanted to nudge my players in a given direction with a Galateid, I'd have to consider genders and sexual orientations because sexuality and romance are big parts of a Galateid's "thing". For the players that are not sexually compatible with the Galateid, perhaps highlight how they've got everyone fooled and how easy they charm others, and how unfair it is that "douchebags" or "skanks" get all the rewards while honest, hardworking nice guys/gals get left in the dust. If they're the same gender, highlight any body or self-image issues the player character might have. Perhaps the player is rail-thin or overweight and the Galateid is muscled/curvy/slender and doesn't have any defects like a crooked nose or an ugly scar.

                                  If the Galateid and the players are sexually compatible, then that's a whole can of worms right there. Hell, even if the Galateid is not (or cannot) be sexually attracted to a player, it doesn't really matter. Both types of relationships have their own type of Disquiet. The unrequited lust (or even love) can manifest as the Galateid distracting the player constantly, without meaning to, while being completely unaware of their effect on the player. This can escalate as player plays out the obsession. If the attraction is corresponded, then it gets even worse. The Galateid is always simultaneously appealing and repulsive. Highlight whatever the player likes about the Galateid. Is she beautiful? Is he caring? Remark on those aspects every time the two meet. Then, introduce an undercurrent of sourness. Maybe the Galateid is too clingy, too suffocating, too needy. Maybe it's the opposite. They're cold and distant or abusive and moody. And when Torment strikes, oho. As the book says, maybe they debase themselves, asking the player to use them like a cheap whore. Maybe they make the player feel threatened and unsafe because they become sexually aggressive or possessive.

                                  It's very hard to describe the Disquiet of a Galateid because it's so personal. It basically attacks whatever weak points the character has about human relationships, body image, sex and/or self-worth.
