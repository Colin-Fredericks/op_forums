                                                Throwing in my 2 cents here.

                                                Been a while since I played Promethean, since I generally lean towards Mage. That said, the theme in 1e, which seems to being pulled even more into the setting of 2e, is Alchemy. Everything makes reference to the different precepts, from the 4 elements (plus spiritual quintessence) as the basic character types, the classic metals as the refinements, the concept of Magnum Opus, and so on. Sure, you could ascribe this to the Prometheans fitting Alchemy on to their journey (I.e. They ascribed the concepts of Alchemy, rather than their basis be derived from it) but the themes of the game itself ascribe to the idea of Alchemy being the central basis (just as Mage draws on Plato's Theory of Forms and Kaballah, along with other mystical traditions).

                                                The thing is, Alchemy is essentially a mystical science of reactions and processes (there is a reason it helped form the basis of what became modern chemistry). In Promethean, considering the strong theme of Alechemy within the setting, would it be going to far in saying that certain elements react in different ways, not because of what they are scientifically, but instead by their alchemical definitions? I mean, if you want to get into science, I could ask how a man restores life to a corpse he submerged in water, cut into 13 pieces of which he then sewed 12 of those pieces back together, spit on and then made eat rose petals while breathing mystical life into them? I am not saying this to be facetious, but to point out that mystical and symbolic processes are the basis of the games setting.

                                                Thus, fire and Promethean flesh not mixing well is another of those processes, regardless of whether you justify it as the flaring of the Pyros, to it preventing regeneration(a la trolls and hydras) or dead flesh burns easier (a la vampires.

                                                I always liked to apply Mage logic* of "everything is a symbol": Electricity heals as it Symbolically a source of energy, recharging, and power that is controlled (even Lightning was ascribed to gods being angry), while fire harms because it is symbolically a force of cleansing,destructive renewal that is at most contained but never fully controlled. Or, as my player (an Obrimos Adamantine arrow) put it when describing how to fight Prometheans: "when you start to die, we give you an electric shock with paddles**. When we want you to die, we set you on fire. Same principle works on these guys, just with a bit more oomph."

                                                *and like all Mage logic, I am sure that while trying to explain everything, I am at least off point if not completely wrong
                                                ** yes, I know even that electricity can kill if used improperly(I am an MD), but it is fun way to think about it.

                                                        Fire bad.

                                                        Really, it ought to be that simple. Forget the non-explanation of 1e; Prometheans are corpses infused with volatile chemicals and/or stuffed with dead plant matter, or machinery coated in oil now apparently. Fire as an inhibitor of regeneration also dates back at least to Greek myth (Hail Hydra!), which sets it up as a viable Promethean curse. The Pyros isn't fire, but is merely symbolized by fire, being a divine force of inspiration, disruption, and evolution. Prometheans aren't themselves crucibles, they're the materials transmuted inside, and enjoy no immunity themselves when the flames lick over the edge.

                                                        There aren't many suitable alternative banes. Gold would be cool, were it not for the shenanigans it would inspire. Supernally perfected metals, or alchemical admixtures, but those are less common than gold. Lead would be great, except then guns would be more lethal to the Created than they are to humans. Eagle feathers? Acids and/or bases? Scrimshawed bones?

                                                        Non-vulnerability seems inappropriate, given the central symbolism of the Pyros, but as a boon it would either be overpowered (alongside Electroshock Therapy), or counter to the pitchforks-and-torches imagery we love.
