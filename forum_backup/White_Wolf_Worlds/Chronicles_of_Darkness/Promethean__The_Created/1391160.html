    [Actual Play] Magnum Opus

    05-24-2020, 07:34 AM
    First, there is only pain. It defines your existence and the borders of your body. It seems almost… familiar. You realise you are able to categorise it. The burning in your head? A fractured skull. The hot, itchy feeling across your arms are cuts, the sharp aches in your chest broken ribs. There’s another pain, though, a pain that eclipses all the others and makes them seem insignificant. It’s feels like a hole in your chest, a burning cold, a sorrow without limit.

    You try to move a hand experimentally only to find you cannot. You try to draw a breath and take in lungfuls of dust. Choking, you realise you’ve been buried alive.

    Strength. You need strength. The thought sends fire into your muscles and you strain, rocks cascading off you as rise from the rubble, pulling yourself free with a sound somewhere between a cough and a roar.

    It is dark. You wish for a lantern, a candle, a torch and to your amazement the thought brings warmth to your flesh. Suddenly you are aglow, the dark shadows of your bones visible beneath your radiant flesh. The light is sufficient to see. You’re in a cave… no, a ruin. You can see now that a portion of the ceiling collapsed and buried you.

    Your memories are awhirl. Words and concepts float in and out of your mind, words like ‘pilgrimage’, ‘daughter’… You look for something to anchor yourself, and you’re surprised to find it. There is writing on the walls, etched into dressed stone. Flakes of paint and lacquer suggest this room was once more grandiose but time has worn away all beauty and gilt and left only the words beneath. The words are written in Latin, in Greek, in Arabic and English, French and Manding… In places, one script is used with the grammar and words of another language. It’s a resource that would eclipse the Rosetta stone, an almost impossible linguistic challenge, but you can read it. The words in this ancient chamber are written in handwriting you recognise. Your own.

    You try to find the start of it all, all too aware that in places the walls are too damaged to read and the rubble from which you emerged is blocking off sections of text.

    Dim memories of Greece stir in you, but details escape and the text is long-faded. There was a creator… a father? A lover? You remember… pain… Sadness.

    Your eyes and fingers follow the text as it transitions from Greek into Latin and memories flood back. Rome, the self-proclaimed greatest city. A city of vice and decadence, a place where you came to understand the beauty and horror of humankind’s impurity.

    ***

    Out of character introduction: As we’ve all got a lot more free time for RPGs for some reason recently, a long-time player-collaborator and I started something new. This player lives in a different city and was pretty busy in the Before Times, making online play essentially impossible, so we normally play 2-3 marathon games (generally played over a long weekend with approximately 24-36 hours of actual gameplay) a year. This, and difficulties scheduling with my regular local group, led to this game.

    This game is me combining my two impossible grail games into a horribly ambitious ur-game. I’m a big Chronicles of Darkness fan (and occasional contributor) and, like many people, consider Promethean: The Created my favourite game I never play. The amount of buy-in needed from players, the difficulty capturing the mood without being too twee or po-faced… It’s a tricky line to walk. I doubt anyone clicked this without knowledgeof Promethean, but just in case, it's a game of created things (Frankenstein's Monsters, golems) learning to be human with the hope they can actually become human.

    The other grail idea, something I had back when I first read Mummy: The Curse, was to play a chronicle set over multiple time periods (each with a theme) with an amnesiac protagonist in the modern era piecing their life together through flashbacks. Now would be a good time to say Planescape Torment is one of my favourite computer games, wouldn’t it? There will be similarities and outright references from day one.

    So this game (which does ignore/change one or two things about Promethean lifespan from 2e) is set across centuries, following a Promethean through different eras, in which they walk different Refinements (philosophies/paths/ways of living, for those unfamiliar with the setting) to understand themselves and eventually become human, piecing together their memories before a climax in the modern day.

    Our protagonist is a Galateid who awoke in Greece around 100BC. Named for the muse Melpomene, she’ll wear other names. In Rome, she is Pomponia. In Tudor England she is Melody Pomeney. In the modern era she’s Melanie.

    The first session, and first era we’re playing in (though not the first one in the main character’s life) is Rome in 62AD and she's walking the Refinement of Cobalt.

    ***

    Pomponia is relatively new to Rome, having previously served as a medici with the legions. She has come to Rome to look for humankind’s impurity, having previously walked the refinement of Gold, living among people but ultimately being disappointed by them. Pomponia finds sorrow the most compelling, human emotion and has taken the role of confessor.

    To this, end her first actions to try to carve a niche among the lower echelons of Ronan society. She made inroads with a local brothel, offering her services as a private doctor, and struck up a conversation with a beggar with a missing foot. Said beggar claimed to be a former legionary, though he was evasive and unconvincing under questioning and ultimately proved to be an escape slave.

    Pomponia visited a haruspex and asked for her soothsaying wisdom. Dutifully, Corvina consulted the entrails and gave her a prophecy.

    “Your life will be long and unhappy, but I think you know this. Four men will you meet; three will be your companions on a long journey. One will ask everything and nothing of you, one will offer friendship and one will offer a partnership. One will be a friend, one a bitter enemy and one a fellow traveller.”

    “What about the fourth?”

    “The fourth will be a lesson.”

    Out of character, I want to note that this wasn’t scripted or set up pre-game but was largely improvised based around ideas I had. I’d told the player augurs and haruspices existed and they sought one out. Prophecies, of course, do very much belong in Promethean. Though I have rough ideas for who will be who, these aren't set in stone and the prophecy is vague is enough to give me plenty of wiggle room. One of the most exciting parts of a game set over centuries is the rare few recurring NPCs (Prometheans and other), who will grow and change (and forget) from epoch to epoch.


    That evening, the brothel’s proprietor, Cilo, contacted Pomponia and had her treat an injured drunk as an audition. As she left, Pomponia noticed she was being followed. She attempted to ambush her pursuer only to find herself face-to-face with a hideous creature. A pallid face with bloodshot eyes, no nose to speak of and a nest of rotten fangs in its mouth.

    “What is your name?”

    “Pomponia.”

    “I am called Worm.”

    “Good evening, worm. What can I do for you?”

    “Worm is not sure... You intrigue me. My kindred, they fear you. You smell wrong; you set our teeth on edge. They think you are a monster, an unnatural thing. Are you?”

    ““How would someone who isn’t breathing define unnatural?”

    “Worms looks at himself and thinks: Worm is a monster. Worm is an unnatural thing. And where others flee in horror there is Worm. Worm is curious.”

    “Then yes. Who are your kin”

    “Telling you is a crime, yet Worm fears no censure for they are afraid to speak with you. Still, I must swear you to secrecy.

    “Naturally.”

    “All things have a shadow, yes? A dark counterpart. So too do the people of Rome. Not all that die remain dead and pass to the underworld, no, some stay on earth. The walking dead, the hungry dead. All things have a shadow; there is a shadow senate that meets in the night. You may have heard rumours that dead men race in the Circus Maximus by night, settling old scores? Not rumours. Truth.”

    “And you are one of these men?”

    “Yes.”

    “The shadow senate? What does it concern itself with?”

    “The same thing as the other senate. Prestige, who sets the laws, the interpretation of the gods’ will…”

    He made a dismissive gesture.

    “Nothing interesting,” Pomponia translated.

    ”Precisely. But you are interesting. It is…. Difficult for me to near you,” he stated, matter of factly.

    “It is no difficulty for me to be near you, though I’m sorry I cause you discomfort.”

    “It is good that you say. Many find it difficult to be near Worm, even his own people. That is why they call him Worm. They think it insults him. Better to be called a lion or a bear, yes? But I see how the lions and bears face in the coliseum. Better to be a worm, I am thinking? Worms thrive.”

    “Was it your idea to seek me, then? Or a lion, or a bear?

    “Worm’s idea. The lions and bears are not concerned with you, or afraid of you. But worm does not run from things others are afraid of. It seems you are a doctor?”

    “I am. I’ve never treated the dead, though the idea is fascinating…”

    “Worm does not think the dead need doctors”

    He asked where Pomponia lived; it was a difficult question to answer and Worm’s inhuman visage made for quite the poker face. She gave a vague answer which he accepted; he had no difficulty finding her.

    “Is there anything Worm can do for you?

    “How much do the dead have to do with the living?”

    “We live beside one another.”

    “I’m interested in those who suffer. Those who are struggling. If you can put me in touch with anyone that would be welcome. Anyone who might need my skills. “

    “Worm thinks, for the first at least, you should pick up a rock, throw it and you will find someone who is suffering. There is no shortage of suffering in this great city. Worm is not often in the company of those who need a doctor, and he is often willing to provide bloodletting himself.”

    Pomponia knew enough to understand the joke

    Worm promised to what he could, and also noted he was good at finding people. He admitted he wasn’t sure what to make of Pomponia, but he viewed her as something others overlooked or feared and as such something he felt he could make use of. Pomponia asked if he could warn her if people took steps to… act on their fear. Worm promised to what he could and admitted he felt an urge to run away from her, or to fight, but promised he was good at controlling his urges.

    Worm went on to ask what Pomponia was, describing himself as an owl, an eater of the dead. Pomponia described her a creation, aspiring to what he had lost. Worm scoffed a little at this.

    “Worm thinks you aspire to nothing of worth. Some might think it is terrible to be a worm, but better than being kine.”

    “You don’t miss the life you had?”

    “Adapting was… difficult. But there was opportunity. Worm does not miss his old life. It is unimportant. The worm emerges from the corpse and so it was with me.”

    “What does worm do?”

    “Worm enjoys being overlooked. In his own way, overlooked little worm is a powerful man. Big secret.”

    “I have nobody to tell.”

    “That is why worm told you.”

    The two struck up their strange alliance of sorts, including a way for Pomponia to contact the apparition (scrawling his namesake in this alleyway).

    Following this Pomponia went to a cauponae, a bar, and witnessed a violent altercation. Two drunken patrons were beating another man. She sat next to one of the other patrons (who weren’t intervening), using Transmutations to loosen tongues to ask why the man was being attacked and why nobody was helping. She learned he was a foreigner, and thus distrusted, and the watchers were nervous about getting involved. After shaming the man she’s sat by with a piercing question (“Would you have interfered if it was a Roman, not a foreigner?”), she followed the blood trail to assist the injured man, discovering he was a merchant from another part of Italy who had come to the city.

    Pomponia was surprised to be interrupted by another Promethean, a cadaverous figure. Tall, gaunt and with a face that hinted at rot.

    The stranger’s Measure, the sense Prometheans gain when meeting another of their kind, was similarly rotten feeling. There was a sense of turmoil but also a strange feeling of familiarity, of kinship.

    “Good evening.”

    “Good evening,” replied Pomponia, uncertain.

    The newcomer gestured to the injured man, walking away.

    “What did you learn?”

    “People think they’re braver than they are. Or want to tell themselves they would be.”

    “A fine lesson, is it not?”

    “It’s similar to the one I learned moments before… but good to have confirmation.”

    And how does that make you feel?” pressed the newcomer.

    “Disappointed.”

    “Disappointed?”

    “People could do an awful more if they didn’t tell themselves a story about why they can’t.”

    “Very true. This disappoints you? You are not accustomed to this; it is new to you?”

    “Yes.”

    “Interesting.”

    “This is not a new lesson for you, then.”

    “No.”

    Silence remained for a moment before Pomponia asked the stranger his age, learning he was slightly older than her.

    “We’ve learned some different lessons so far, then.”

    “So you have learned humankind is worthy of respect and admiration?”

    “Yes.”

    “I fear you have learned a false lesson, but perhaps in time you will see. It seems you are looking for the right things, the impurity within… “

    “Perhaps it’s a difference between the individual and the whole?”

    “So you’re suggesting a person is good, people are bad? Am I correct in my assessment?”

    “That would fit my current experience, yes.”

    “I see your model will change with evidence. Interesting. Good even. This has been enlightening.”

    “What have you learned most recently?”

    “Recently? Nothing .”

    “Most recently, however far back that may be.”

    “That I am incapable of surprise.“

    “We’ll have to see how we can change that, then.”

    “Yes, perhaps we will,” he replied with genuine warmth and a smile before walking away. Pompnia asked for his name but he had already disappeared into the night.

    Pomponia decided to seek out a Promethean she had seen before but never spoken with; Vigil. Vigil dresses as one of the vigiles, Rome’s night watch/fire service. His Measure was like being plunged into icy water, though not necessarily unpleasant after the initial shock. His skin was shrivelled around his lips and eyes and he smelled of salt. His eyes glittered with curiosity. After the initial pleasantries, Vigil asked Pomponia a question

    “May I ask you a question? I’m afraid it’s a philosophical question.”

    “I can’t make promises about the quality of my answer, but…”

    “That’s alright. What can change the nature of a man?”

    “Sorrow,” Pomponia replied, after only a moment’s thought. “When you reflect on something you can grow.

    “A fine answer, thank you.”

    “What do you believe to be the answer?”

    “Why do you think I ask the question?”

    “I feel I am unlikely to be the first person you’ve asked.”

    “Correct; I’ve heard many answers. None of them felt quite right to me yet. Not to say that your answer was wrong in any way. I have heard your answer before.”

    “Are the people here helping you find the answer?”

    “Perhaps. It is a slow process, but… we must not allow ourselves to be disheartened. Just because questions do not have easy answers does not mean they should not be asked, nor that the answers will forever elude us”

    “From what I’ve seen on the street,” Pomponia replied, thinking of the philosophers she’d seen in the forums, “there are lots of people asking similar questions.”

    “Yes, it is… enjoyable. I have been avoiding them of late.”

    “Why?”

    “They know me too well.”

    “Ah.”

    “Do you have a question?”

    “Why do good people do bad things?”

    “A conundrum. I have an answer, though it comes in the form of a question. Define good and define bad.”

    “I should have anticipated that question. Working on both.”

    “It is important to acknowledge when one does not know the answer. Far too many act as though they do; I have been led astray in the past.”

    After this Vigil wasn’t especially talkative, and was very comfortable with a contemplative silence, but he confirmed knowing about three other Prometheans (a tally that matched Pomponia’s own); Calidus, a gladiator (with an increasing popularity problem) and the unnamed man Pomponia had met earlier.

    They briefly discussed Calidus, noting he had made no approaches to others and they shared confusion at his choice of path.

    “I don’t know what lesson he’s learning. I don’t think I could learn the lesson in the same way.”

    “We all have a question that drives us,” opined Vigil.

    The conversation died away. Vigil seemed comfortable to remain in a thoughtful silence and Pomponia took her leave. She returned to her current home, a tiny room in the top floor of an apartment block, the events of the evening (and the prophecy she had paid for) running through her mind.

    The first session was quite info-dumpy and navel-gazey; we're both finding our feet with Promethean, online one-to-one play and playing a game set in a radically different time period. Future games will hopefully be a bit more exciting, but they're still going to be very dialogue heavy as this is very much the preferred style of both me and the player. His approach is always social/diplomatic first and he loves a big cast of NPCs to bounce off. It'll be interesting to see how the same character plays when walking the path of Iron or Copper!
