    Idea: Alternative(s) to Disquiet?

    05-27-2016, 01:42 AM
    So I've been running a campaign for a while now with an idea that I got from reading through the Promethean books.
    I've read in the books that Promethean are sometime (often?) created almost like a relief valve when the world needs a particular type of hero. To Me that was a very interesting idea.
    Somewhere along the way that stuck with Me and I thought that Heroes shouldn't be creating nuclear deathlands around them for staying in one place for too long. But I soon came to an alternative to disquiet. I even had story reason for the change that allowed one to develop other versions of disquiet if that was fitting etc.
    So in My most prominent alternative to Disquiet or as we call it "the D", was based off the Flaw (the name escapes Me atm?) that drew the supernatural to the player. This made sense. Heroes often get into the thick of it when they least expect it, and lets face it, sometimes it is not the Humans and mortals that need help, its the things that go bump in the night. Being that the campaign I run deals with a group of Hunters which not only work for mortals, but the supernatural, this worked out very nicely and also explained why the city itself was not dead wonderland.
    So looking into heroes, they tend to fall into all sorts of plots, schemes and often they came under fire from the humans around them if things stayed too ...comfortable without an en emy to bother the local populace. This sounded very close to what the current disquiet did with people not trusting them, or being jealous etc. So I quickly modified things so that more intense version of the flaw affected My Promethean character(s) and NPC's which took the Merit/condition. Because lets face it, not all Promethean are good guys. The flaw is optional, which explained why not all Promethean (or Unborn as they are sometimes called in the campaign) have it. It can be developed so that it gives certain bonus in exchange for certain increase in frequency or even appealing to certain types of supernatural phenomenon as well.
    I figured I would ask around and see if anyone else has worked with this kind of idea. Have you? *grins* Because the idea of a Hero is so important we even changed the name of the heroes from Beast: the Primordial to 'Champions' (lower case for regular ones and uppercase for the Apex Predators/Epic versions that would mirror Beasts) to distinguish them. Dont get Me wrong, its not that the BtP versions on heroes werent a great idea, but in a world of evil and EVIL(s) there is still a need for Heroes. In fact more so.
    With that, If you have ideas you would like to share, redesigns for these types of ideas, etc involving alternatives to disquiet that would allow players to stay in places while still impacting the immediate world around them that can be, fun, interesting and add multiple levels of horror to a campaign, I would love to hear them!
    Q: Wait, how is your version 'fun, interesting, and add horror' to your campaign?
    A: Well in My campaign Promethean would rid the world of evil(s), accomplish tasks similar to the way Qualishim or even Demon/Angel would. They would stumble into all sorts of plans, plots, and trouble areas. To ignore it would in a way increase the effect on the surrounding area in various ways, from more monsters coming into the area, to worse ones coming into the area, magic going awry in various ways, etc. Deal with the problem(s) and it lessens the pressure in the area. Sometimes even finding interesting ways to leave various energies (Azoth, pyros, etc), sometimes leaving clues, artifacts, gadgets and items needed to survive, adapt and move on should that be what was needed. And characters could feel when they were no longer needed, if the world was tying to push them out to a new location, etc. Its been working very well for the campaign, though the idea/thought has been brought up that they are a kind of supernatural sin eater for evil or bad situations that the universe/world needed them for. We seem to be ok with that, as they get to learn what humanity is about while defending it. A very good way to do just that. And in many movies, heroes are hated just as much as they are loved oft times.

                                                      A.I. is ...odd to place as a tragedy or a comedy, about a robot who's a boy who's obsessive love, childlike beliefs, and robotic nature leads to people questioning whether or not he is a boy or not, while his robot nature both gets him into trouble and out of trouble, and ends of him living a fantasy of just being a happy little boy with his mom for a day.
                                                      Blade Runner is Blade Runner.

                                                      But hey, all of that says to me this game needs to be more optimistic and transhumanist, am I right?

                                                      ​So, can anyone point to ANYTHING from Mary Shelly indicating that peoples responses to the creature where based on something other than it's gruesome appearance?
                                                      ANYTHING to imply Galatea and Pygmalion didn't live happily ever after?(Aside from being a Greek story, we all know how much they loved tragedies)
                                                      ANYTHING to make me think Osiris went on a quest to become a mortal human?
                                                      ANYTHING from these stories about these characters befouling the world just by being in it?
                                                      Gee, it's like things can be appropriated and reinterpreted to produce new and interesting angles on subjects. But hey, fidelity is the only thing that matters, am I right?

                                                      No? Then maybe don't tell me those things are vital to a game based on those stories. Maybe the Devs were wrong. It's happened before..
                                                      Gee, it's almost as though they were working on a canon of inspirations beyond the icons they used and listed it at the beginning of the book. But hey, who actually gives a shit what the real inspirations were, am I right? The people making their own books had no fucking clue what they really wanted to do, am I right? The fans who spring up only when Promethean gets a new release and not during the massive doldrum between the end of it's run and the announcement of Second Edition are the only sane and salient points of view that matter, am I right?

                                                      Right?

                                                      Look, I'll level with you. You don't like Promethean as is? Fine. I get that. It's a niche product in an already niche market. You think it can be improved, made more playable? Sure. Matthew McFarland agreed with you. He's spent a lot of time opening it up, loosening up some of the more restrictive gameplay, lightening the tone on a lot of subjects, so on and so forth.

                                                      I'll continue to level with you. I don't know what it is about Promethean that attracts transhumanists. I don't know what draws you in, expecting some radically different experience. I don't know what keeps you all here when you find out it isn't, what makes so many of you fight to see Promethean become your vision.

                                                      Maybe the problem isn't with the game.
