    Stolen Lives

    12-13-2013, 12:42 AM
    So, I'm busy trying to piece together my own take on the build-up Humanity, and I want to emphasize the "stolen lives" of the sub-subtitle. My main thrust is the Remnant, a piece of the Promethean's body's original inhabitant(s). So far, the Remnant imposes a single, personal ban on the Promethean, which counts as a Humanity 3 violation. It can be shed, but doing so means interacting with the donor's legacy, which risks all sorts of disaster. The payoff is Vitriol, naturally, and getting out from under the shadow of the stolen life, so that the Promethean is free to shape an existence that can someday truly be his own. In game terms, the Promethean eventually trades his Remnant in for a Virtue or Vice (which he begins without).

    I'm also using the Beacons and Burden concepts, with the idea that these are initially shaped by the Remnant, though the Promethean's creator and/or early experiences can also affect how these are chosen. Burdens are simply voluntarily adopted breaking points, while Beacons are the only humans whose injury can prompt a degeneration roll in the Promethean (until such time as the Burden becomes "baked-in" to the Promethean's Morality).

    The idea is that as the character progresses towards the New Dawn, he sheds the particulars of his "birth", develops a stronger sense of self, and gains some practical knowledge of Mortality.

    Anyway, bringing this up, because I'd like to hear other ideas to drive home the "stolen lives" idea. I think P:tC as written doesn't incentivize the investigation of where one's body comes from, which seems to me a key part of why the Created are such abominations.

    LFP: Foreign Bodies (Deviant: the Renegades)

          I'm still working on the other half of the equation; my idea is to have a Refinement resolve and become a "grayed-out" option on the list, with the exception of Torment, which cannot resolve this way.

          I like the Burdens too, but as I get older and more grizzled, I find the whole ST system riddled with twiddly bits. I've recently had the joy of playing the Weisman Marvel rpg, and Polaris some time before that, and their simplicity soothes me, after a passionate fling with D&D 4E that soured fast. The more elegant a solution I can pare things down to, the happier I am.

          It comes down to what you want from your system. Remember how they originally were Bane analogs? I think they work great for Vampire, because they're about staving off monstrosity, and each one represents a moral failure, or inhuman extreme, and is the mark of an ancient, compromised, and damaged individual. Burdens should be different, as they're a work that helps the Promethean to gain wisdom and understanding of a condition they aspire to. They are not punishments, and the notion that they are "picked up" also implies that they are not intrinsic to the Promethean, even though my working draft makes them mandatory to advance.

                  I think that Burdens make a good optional system, my only issue is making sure they make sense wherever they go. In my "stolen lives" framework, they're the keys to the next level of Humanity. They are simply experiential experiments: the Promethean is trying to understand why this thing is important. Why is stealing frowned upon? Why all the to-do about death and injury? Prometheans have to reverse-engineer the human soul, and then construct a mystical something they then have to transmute to get the final intangible product. That's a huge burden in and of itself, so if we throw additional weights on the Promethean's back, they need to be worth it.

                  When I say "worth it", I mean that they need to enhance the story. My conception here is that Burdens are metaphysical, spiritual burdens, and that Humanity ultimately is a blessed Burden, the blessed Burden, that Prometheans ultimately have to earn the right to carry, and as it contains all the Burdens that have gone before it, the Prometheans have to also develop the strength. That's not to say Banes-as-Burdens can't do that, but that their import to the Pilgrimage might be overshadowed by their immediate inconveniences.

                  BUT.

                  The only criteria for a mechanic should be: does it better the game? If rulesy Burdens spark a lot of creative storytelling, character anecdotes, and provide sincere challenges for our poor benighted misbegotten urchins, then they're keepers. Likewise, if adopted Breaking Points add nothing to the Pilgrimage, they ought to be tossed. This latter argument is where the forumites' consensus seems to be regarding classic, secret Milestones, pushed aside for Burdens.

                      This is some proof of concept work of the Remnant, hopefully providing some insight into my thinking.
                      New Trait: Remnant
                      No Promethean is made from new materials. Inside every Created lurks an imprint of the body's original inhabitant, and while it may be subtle, it is still influential. A Tammuz made from the body of a motorcyclist may unconsciously give SUVs and minivans a wide berth, while an Ulgan made from a church fanatic's corpse might panic if separated from his Bible. The Remnant itself is just a thought, a mere reflex, but can be overwhelming when it triggers.

                      As part of the Promethean's concept, you should decide what of the donor remains in the body the Promethean comes to inhabit. You don't need to go into great detail about the donor's life, but you should identify some action that the donor would fight tooth and nail to avoid in life. The Tammuz from the example above might choose "getting too close to a family vehicle", while the Ulgan could pick "failing to say Grace before a meal". The Ulgan might not even believe in God, and the Tammuz might be on foot, but the aversion is one of the few things they can viscerally feel. The act of violating the Remnant inflames it, and reminds the Promethean that his own existence is but a shadow of another's.

                      Milestone: A Promethean can shed or alter the Remnant, given sufficient Identity or Azoth. If the Promethean can achieve some sort of closure with the donor's life, the Remnant can be refined into a Virtue. If the Promethean instead adopts the the Remnant as a true part of his Identity and the person he wishes to be, then the Remnant can be refined into a Vice. For example, the Tammuz seeks out the donor's family, and learns that the donor saw his best friend killed by a distracted SUV driver. The Tammuz decides to confront the SUV driver, and ends up destroying her car. Having punished the source of the Remnant's fear, he sacrifices the Remnant, and picks 'Just' as his new Virtue. If instead he had heeded the Remnant's warning, and took up motorcycles in order to understand and accommodate the Remnant, he might have given the Remnant up for a Vice of 'Cautious'.
                      I'm finding that the Remnant is much more complex than I realized. As conceived, it needs to do the following:
                      • Be a holdover from the donor's life.
                      • Be a hook and a foil for the nascent self of the Promethean.
                      • Tie into Humanity (referred to in these rules as Identity)
                      It also should function similarly to one or more of the following traits:
                      • Virtue or Vice
                      • A Breaking Point
                      • A Milestone/Burden/Beacon
                      I think if I were to cross one of these off the list, it'd be the Breaking Point notion. The cost would be the definition of the Remnant, but its absence would be one less weight on modified Humanity rules. The trick is whether it's a Virtue or a Vice, and what takes the other half of the equation. In this conception, that part is covered by Refinement.

                      LFP: Foreign Bodies (Deviant: the Renegades)
