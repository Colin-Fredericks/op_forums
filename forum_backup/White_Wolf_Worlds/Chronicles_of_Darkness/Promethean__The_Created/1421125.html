          They were written to be an ethical dilemma. They start as a mystery, as they leave little impression upon Azothic Memory. The players will have to investigate and learn the rules themselves. Once the players realize they can farm Vitriol, they have to ask themselves if it's worth it. They're going to lose pretty much all their Pilgrimage progress. They're going to become targets, as most Pandorans don't have the intelligence to make a truce, and most Alchemists are too greedy to keep a truce forever. Centimani, at least, might honor a truce. Maybe.
          If the Storyteller wants to up the horror ante they can have the first couple of Lacunae happen when the Petrificatus is outside of their Role; the Stuck will moan a bit and try to shamble away. Once the players are complacent and try again, the Storyteller shows the Stuck in their Role. They scream, they cry for help, they beg the players to stop, threaten, bluster, run away, attack them back. For extra pathos, the ST can give glimpses of the possibility of restoration. Now the players are hurting someone they could be helping. They're torturing a person.

          The rules for Tormented Petrificati were cut pre-first draft, as I didn't have enough of a wordcount. One of the ways they could be created was repeatedly performing the Lacuna upon a normal Petrificatus. They have a Role that only activates when they have a chance to hurt people or when they are in danger. This Role uses 6 dice for all actions and will go out of their way to inflict injury and harm. Your easy-to-farm Vitriol cow just became a crazed killer who would be perfectly willing to cut open their own stomach to spray you with acid.

                              Could you make a petrificati Vitriol farm? I suppose, but it requires the characters to have read Night Horrors: The Tormented first. Plus, everything needs to go perfectly right when it is constantly threatening to go the way of Jurassic Park.

                              The biggest problem such an endeavor would have is logistical. Petrificati are defined by their Role and their Vitriol. Their Role is the first problem that needs to be overcome, as they are people for most intents and purposes while acting in it. What is more, it is difficult to control the Roles of the petrificati in your farm without going out and murdering people who you think will provide a specific Role [and you need to know that is how it works]. And even then, they might rise as something different for reasons you could not account for, and now you have a volatile Role among your "cattle". A panicking Automaton whose Role had been accidentally activated can spell disaster for the entire operation, because while their dice pool might not be very good, they can inadvertently endanger themselves and others with their Vitriol. And that is not even getting into how you might get Roles cascading into other Roles.

                              Which brings me to the second problem to overcome: the Vitriol. Petrificatus cannot just make new Automatons, they have to succeed on a roll first. Their pool for the generative act is equal to their current Vitriol Experiences. Most new petrificati are not going to have any Vitriol Experience, and so have a chance die to make another. That's a 9 in 10 chance to create one or more Pandorans. Even if they succeed, then that petrificatus now has a single die to perform the generative act, and a -1 penalty for every week the body has been dead. This is also the only way that they gain additional Vitriol Experiences, which most people do not know in-universe.

                              So, to keep a "farm" viable, you must keep enough Vitriol in the petrificati for reasonable chances of success performing the generative act [which you might not know], which means enough to keep them dangerous should their Roles activate. And for the best chances of increasing their Vitriol, you want to supply them with fresh corpses [which you might not know], and the sheer number of walking dead you are going to generate as part of your farm's normal operations will attract notice.

                              As for why not? Let us say the characters somehow know all of this, and somehow convince a mage to alter Fate to make petrificatus generation more likely despite Disquiet, and somehow convince the local graveyards to look the other way despite Disquiet, and have somehow acquired the infrastructure to reliably contain their ballooning Automaton population, and somehow identify Alchemists/Sublimati/Centimani that won't immediately harvest and/or eat them, and somehow convince them that the long-term gains are worth more than harvesting/eating them right now, and somehow managing to keep all this from self destructing because of Disquiet. The returns are still not going to be worth the effort. It requires new bodies constantly, I highly doubt destroyed petrificatus can be recycled given their propensity for dissolving, and every new body is only 1 more Vitriol. The myriad uses Vitriol has for Alchemists especially makes a constant supply extremely attractive. This is tailor-made to turn "reasonable" Alchemists into insatiable ones. Sublimati are irrational at the best of times and still need to eat Prometheans [or people] for Pyros. Plus they get pretty limited benefits from Vitriol, most of which come down to "the better to eat you with".
                              As Gizmit said, Centimani are probably the only group that you can hope to forge any kind of lasting peace with such a farm, and their thoughts and personality are constantly eroding under Flux's influence. I would not put much trust in them honoring any agreements they have made in the past. But, any Promethean participating is likely either already a Centimanus, on the fast track to becoming one, or stuck in a particularly cruel Role [and ironically, potentially at risk of becoming the very thing they're helping to farm], so who knows?

                              Basically, it is a ridiculous amount of work, requires extraordinarily intimate knowledge of the petrificatus condition, enough political power to get authorities to the look the other way, and at a certain point, it becomes easier to hunt and harvest Prometheans. So, while, technically, yes it can happen, the actual process is so contrived as to be basically impossible, requires a pet mage, will not realistically yield enough returns to be worth it, will collapse under its own weight eventually, and is very clearly not intended by the rules.
                              But if it works for your game and you're excited about the possibility, well, we can't stop you.

                              All of this said, it could be very cool to confront players with a ghost town full of petrificati leftover from a failed farming attempt. It could be almost like a zombie movie, but the real danger comes from all the Pandorans surely lying dormant nearby.

                              Posting gives me anxiety, but I'm still happy to be here
