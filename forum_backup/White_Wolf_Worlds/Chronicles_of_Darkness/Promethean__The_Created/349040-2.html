    Originally posted by Firanai View Post
    I know that each line of the new world of darkness is made with the idea to be it's own universe separated from the other lines.
    Err... No, they're not. They're generally designed to be played separately, but they're written with the assumption that they all exist in a shared universe. The fact that Mages, Prometheans, and Unchained all understand the universe differently is just human nature... or... supenatural nature, as the case may be. The books just contain a lot of seemingly conflicting information because they're all written as tough the way the eponymous supernatural creatures' understanding of the universe is essentially correct, because if everyone is playing that type of creature, that will probably be what the characters all believe. But they do all exist in a shared universe (unless the ST decides they don't for the purposes of his or her chronicle).

                                                    The thing is, several lines acknowledged the existence of one another within their own fiction. Im not trying to claim a platonic world of darkness any more than the books themselves present the idea of such a world. Each game is written with the assumption that it will be played indepenently of the other games (with the possible exception of Beast?) and doesn't concern itself with avoiding contradictions with other lines. However, they are also written as though the other lines do exist, they just rarely interact. The contradictions shouldn't really matter outside of crossover games because of everyone's playing a werewolf it doesn't much matter what Mages think of the shadow, and if you are playing Crossover, the ST is going to have to make calls about who's worldview is right and wrong and in what ways no matter what, so the books make a conscious effort not to limit the ST's ability to make those calls by stating "the truth" outright.
