    Talos Lineage

    06-28-2014, 11:31 AM
    Here is a Lineage I created a long time ago. I don't believe I ever shared it again after the change over to the new forum, so I shall share it now. If the accompanying story is desired as well, I can share that too. Enjoy.


    Talos

    Guardians

    Danger is a fact of life. Humanity has always been aware of this. Accidents happen. Animals attack. Criminals lurk in the shadows. Nations go to war. There is no true safety to be had. Despite this, humanity seeks to find this safety. They build bigger walls, and better weapons. They seek protectors.

    The Talos would willingly take up this mantle, if only they would be accepted. The first Talos was created to protect, and they hold to that purpose to this day. Every Talos has a protective instinct, and to them, it is the greatest tragedy that their Disquiet prevents them from being able to act upon it openly. Unfortunately, the Guardians find that their protection is often unwanted, and they find themselves declared the enemy. The Talos take this in stride, though. They are slow to anger, and acknowledge that mortals don't understand. This does not mean that they are any happier about the situation. Their distance from their charges grates at them, and even the Talos have their limits.

    The Talos value those that protect others, and almost invariably, any body chosen for the Creation act will have belonged to someone with strong protective instincts. The physical traits of the corpse are of little importance, as the Guardian knows that the weakness of the flesh shall be strengthened with metal. When the body has been put back together, it is quickly sliced down to the bone with a metal blade. The skeleton is reinforced with metal, often with rods lining the bones of the limbs, or metal plates affixed to the ribcage and skull. Some of the metal used in this fashion will invariably be one of the seven alchemical metals, although the exact metal and amount used varies greatly. These metal reinforcements are then fused to the bone with molten bronze. The final act that brings the Azoth into the body requires that a symbol be carved into the chest of the corpse, usually one of life or protection. This symbol is then filled with molten bronze mixed with the humours of the Promethean's Creator, bringing the creature to life.

    The Talos easily form protective attachments, and because of this most Creators are dedicated to teaching the young Promethean what they can before leaving them to their own Pilgrimage. The young Talos will then be on their own, and will often seek an outlet for their protective urges. Many choose a person or type of person to protect, although others have been known to choose places, objects, or even ideals to champion.

    Inevitably, though, a Guardian will be rejected for their efforts. When a Guardian falls to their Torment, it is dangerous to everyone around them, as their protective instincts will grow out of control. The Talos will guard their charge with extreme prejudice. Anyone who comes near the charge is liable to be declared an enemy. Additionally, the Guardian will see danger everywhere, and may very well protect their charge from themself. While in the grip of Torment, it is not uncommon for a Guardian to cause serious harm their charge to prevent them from entering a potentially dangerous situation, even if the situation is as simple as crossing a street. Upon overcoming their Torment, the Talos will understandably be upset by the results of their actions, although usually there is nothing that can be done to repair the damage.

    A throng that has gained the trust and loyalty of a Talos is fortunate indeed. The Talos tend to be very loyal, and their dedication to their role as Guardians is often extended to the protection of their fellow Prometheans. Their willingness to sacrifice themselves for the betterment of the group can mean the difference between life and death.

    Progenitor: Mortal legends speak of Talos, the giant bronze guardian of Crete. According to these legends, the bronze man protected the island by hurling great rocks at approaching enemy ships. The Guardians do not dispute this account, and trace their Lineage back to the bronze giant called Talos. They have their own version of the tale, of course.

    The Guardians claim that the first Talos was created by the legendary inventor, Daedalus. According to these tales, the creation act was performed for King Minos as restitution for the loss of a great warrior. The name itself is said to have been an attempt at penance for a sin Daedalus had commit long before coming to Crete.

    Talos was built to be a protector, and that is what he did. He helped to repel several attacks on the island. Unfortunately, his Disquiet did not allow him to remain long in that position. Inevitably, paranoia began to build, and people saw enemies at every turn. Much of this suspicion naturally fell on the shoulders of Talos, and he was forced to flee the island.

    It is known that he created others of his kind along his travels. He would often set them to the task of protecting a certain person, place, or thing from any that would despoil it, and these tasks were always embraced by those he created. The ultimate fate of Talos remains unknown. Although mortal legends speak of his death at the hands of Jason and Medea, the Guardians themselves attribute this aspect of the legend to mythology, and not to any actual account of their kind.

    Other Nicknames: Iron Men, Sentinels, Cyborgs

    Appearance: Most Talos choose the bodies of people that died protecting something, or that had lived a life of guardianship of some kind or another. Because of this, there are no common physical traits that all Guardians possess, and they can come from any culture or background. Many bodies chosen to become Talos are physically fit, if not necessarily imposing. Many Talos are fairly nondescript, and they often choose clothing that doesn't stand out.

    Disfigurements: When their disfigurements become visible, the Guardians' metallic origins become apparent. Their skin often takes on a metallic sheen, sometimes giving the impression that the Talos is forged from solid metal. The lines of their body become sharp and angular, their usual soft curves becoming harsh and menacing. Their eyes often become dark or metallic, although sometimes they take the form of molten metal. Regardless of how they change, they give the impression that they are taking everything in at once.

    The reinforcement given to their skeleton during their creation also becomes apparent. Metal rods may jut through their skin, or metal plates may pierce through the flesh of their chest. The symbol that had been carved onto their chest often blazes with the light of molten metal. Their movements are accompanied with the screech of metal on metal, or sometimes give the impression of clockwork mechanisms operating beneath their flesh.

    Humour: Choleric Deficient (patient, stubborn)

    Element: Metal is the element that rules over the Guardians. The element of metal is associated with organization and stability, and the Talos often exhibit these traits. Metal is a forceful element, and those that are attuned to it will seldom bend on an issue once they have made up their mind.

    Bestowment: Iron Will

    Metal is strong and unyielding, and so are the Guardians that are birthed from it. Those who would stand in the way of a Talos and their charge will soon find that the Guardian will not be swayed, and that even mystical control slides off of them like light glinting off of polished steel.

    Cost: None or 1 Pyros
    Action: Reflexive
    Transmutation Cost: Resolve x 7

    The Promethean benefits from the 9-again rule on any Resolve-based rolls. In addition, if he fails a roll, he can spend one Pyros to re-roll. (These re-rolled dice do not gain the 9-again benefit, although 10's may be re-rolled as usual.) Additionally, any time that the character's Resolve Attribute is used as a penalty to a supernatural effect, the Attribute is doubled. For example, a vampire might try to mesmerize the character by looking into their eyes. If the character's Resolve is 2, the vampire will suffer a penalty of 4 dice when attempting to use its power on the Talos. Finally, the Promethean can raise his Resolve above the mortal limit of five dots, regardless of his Azoth rating.

    Although it is not part of their creation, other Prometheans may gain the Iron Will Bestowment as a Transmutation. Such a change does not duplicate the Talos disfigurement, but it does necessitate subtle alterations to the humours of the Created on a metaphysical level. Gaining the Iron Will Bestowment requires a decrease in the level of choleric humour within the Promethean. This causes the Promethean to become more patient, but also causes them to become more stuck in their ways.

    Refinements: While the Talos can belong to any Refinement, most are slow to change to a new one once they've dedicated themselves to one. The most common Refinement by far among the Guardians is the Refinement of Ferrum. The idea of forging the body and adapting warrior philosophies appeals strongly to the protective instincts of the Talos, and most spend at least some time on their Pilgrimage following this Refinement. Many others, seeking to better understand their Promethean state, choose to follow the Mercurius Refinement. It isn't often that a Talos will turn to the Refinement of Flux, but the few Centimani of this Lineage often embrace their Refinement with a fervor that is terrifying to behold. Few Guardians choose to follow the Refinement of Cuprum for long periods of time, as the focus on the Self conflicts with their inherent desire to guard others.

    Character Creation: Most Talos have Physical as their primary Attributes, while Social skills are often tertiary. The Guardians desire to protect often puts them in danger, but they usually interact as little as possible with their charges. Talos also tend to have Physical Skills as their primary category. Social Skills are not always very high, but Intimidation is a Skill that most develop to some degree. Fast Reflexes and Danger Sense are two Merits that many Guardians develop over time.

    Common Virtues are Fortitude and Temperance, while common Vices among the Guardians are Pride and Wrath.

    Concepts: anonymous benefactor, blacksmith, bodyguard, martyr, night watchman, private detective, stalker, vigilante

    Quote:This individual is under my protection. If you don't leave now, you're going to have a problem on your hands.

    Stereotypes

    Frankenstein:It is tragic how you are created. I respect that you persevere in spite of it.
    Galatea:So proud, and yet so fragile. I don't know whether you need protection or a lesson in humility.
    Osiris:I will guard you, but I will not serve you.
    Tammuz:You are like us. I respect you.
    Ulgan:Why do you look to another world, when this is the one you want?

    *

    Vampires:    A danger to that which we would protect. Be cautious.
    Werewolves:You protect those that can't protect themselves. I respect you.
    Mages:So much power for a mortal to possess. You do not need me.
    Mortals:So priceless. So fragile.
    The Talos Condition

    Talos can regain Pyros by sleeping in contact with large amounts of metal. Many in modern times may even pierce themselves, and heal themselves in the morning with electricity.

    The Talos Disquiet causes a creeping sense of paranoia and distrust. The Talos will be recognized as being an enemy, or at least in league with enemies. With time, this paranoia will expand to alienate the person from everyone, as even their closest friends begin to seem suspicious. Inevitably, the people will rise up against the enemy that's clearly behind all of their problems (ie, the Talos).

    Talos Wastelands cause metal to corrode and become brittle. People will find their willpower and resolve fading, leaving them lethargic and apathetic. In more potent Wastelands, they may begin to exhibit symptoms of anemia.
    Gremlins (Talos Mockery)

    As strongly as the Talos seek to protect, the Gremlins seek to destroy. Even the least intelligent of the Mockery possess a cunning instinct that drives them to endanger the loved ones of a Promethean to draw out their prey. More intelligent Gremlins may even create situations where the Promethean is forced to choose between saving a loved one and saving themselves. More than perhaps any other Lineage, the Gremlins revel in creating havoc in the world around them.

    These Pandorans tend to be far more patient than many other Pandorans, silently watching their prey from the shadows. Gremlins often stalk their prey for long periods of time, creating disasters that seem to follow them wherever they go. Gremlins enjoy destruction for its own sake, and often turn this impulse towards alienating their prey further from society, causing problems that will eventually fall on the head of the Promethean as Disquiet catches up to it. The Gremlins will seek to remove any comfort or advantage that a Promethean may have, harrying it towards an inevitable confrontation.

    The Gremlins are fast and cowardly, and when attacking a Promethean, they often strike quickly from the shadows before fleeing, waiting for another opening to appear so that they might take advantage of it. Individual Gremlins will seldom attack groups of Prometheans, although Gremlins often gather together in large numbers. Most Gremlins favor swarm tactics to take down a Promethean, and will be reluctant to attack if they are alone. Gremlins also favor the creation of dangerous situations, and intelligent ones will usually seek to arrange a confrontation with a Promethean at locations that have already been subject to the Gremlin's attention. Gremlins often develop Transmutations that allow them to protect themselves or influence inanimate matter or machines, and they use these abilities to make the very environment hazardous to their prey.

    Gremlins often capture their prey when they are able to do so. Many that are intelligent will set traps using loved ones as bait to lure a Promethean. The Gremlin will bind the Promethean to the best of their ability, and will devour it slowly over the course of a long period of time, sometimes even allowing them access to electricity to heal themselves. These ones often enjoy the emotional pain of a Promethean, and will keep their captive aware of any 'accidents' that might befall a loved one.

    Gremlins often appear in a more or less humanoid body, although it is not uncommon for their limbs or head to seem wrong in size for their body, and most have other features that are clearly inhuman as well. All Gremlins have a metallic look to their skin, usually with large scaley patches of rust in various places over their bodies. Most Gremlins seem slightly smaller than an average person, and it is not uncommon for them to be far smaller due to Pandoran Transmutations.

    Dormant Form: When Gremlins enter Dormancy, they often appear to be objects of rusted metal. Mangled scrap metal, ruined car parts or other damaged technological devices, and abstract metal artwork are common forms for them to take. In areas away from civilization, Gremlins might take the forms of large ore rocks instead, although even these often have hints of rust on them.

    Bestowment: All Gremlins possess the Pandoran Transmutations Armor ( O ), Scurry ( OO ), as well as a Pandoran variant of the Promethean Transmutation Fist of Talos ( O ) which allows them to damage objects with physical attacks utilizing any natural weaponry they may have.

    Sublimati: Gremlin Sublimati tend to be patient and thoughtful, favoring elaborate ambushes and plots to obtain their desires. These Gremlins enjoy learning what they can about their quarries, and often take pleasure in destroying that which their enemies hold dear. These Gremlins have been known to form temporary alliances with Centimani and occasionally other desparate Prometheans when it suits their purposes, although these alliances are rare and often short lived.

    Weakness: The Gremlins are especially cowardly for Pandorans. For Gremlins to engage a group with superior numbers in direct physical combat, it requires an expenditure of a Willpower point. Additionally, every time a Gremlin is injured in combat, it must make a reflexive Resolve + Composure roll or flee in terror. The Gremlin requires a number of successes equal to the amount of damage taken. The number of enemies beyond the first count as a penalty to this roll, while allies give a bonus equal to the number of allies present. Should the Gremlin achieve an exceptional success on this roll, it does not need to roll again for the remainder of the scene, regardless of how much damage it takes.
