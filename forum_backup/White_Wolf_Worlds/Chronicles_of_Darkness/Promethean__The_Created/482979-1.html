                    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
                    Sorry, I think we have our wires crossed when it comes to what I was referring to as "important". Your comment about a Forrest Gump-esque merit putting the Promethean at the centre of human events seemed to be pushing towards an understanding of humanity only from the perspective of exceptional individuals.
                    Ok, you definately misread that. I wasn't talking about the Promethian's experiences at all. I was talking about why the Principle focuses on Created.

                    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
                    I absolutely agree about everything you say about learning about humanity. I just disagree with the notion that the Principle can only find the Pilgrimage important if it involves "big" events.
                    Remember it's not just "important". It's "most important".

                    Can the Principle care about every last Promethian. Sure. Heck, it might be God and the Church is very clear that God loves every single person.

                    But why does the Principle think that this random Golem is more important than that mortal president? Or that Mage? Or that mortal alchemist?

                    That's what I'm saying needs mechanical support
                    • If studying the Principle can teach a Promethian about humanity
                    • Then learning the Principle's reasons for focusing on the Pilgramage will be a Milestone in learning humanity.
                    • Which means that those reasons will make sense from a human perspective.
                    • If they make sense from a human perspective they'll have a visible effect on the world.
                    • That visible effect should have mechanics to let players interact with it.


                    The Promethian Forest Gump doesn't interact with big events because he can only learn from big events; he interacts with big events to justify why his Pilgramage is important enough to grab the Principle's attention.

                    Basically from a human perspective if the Principle had a choice between helping a random Promethian become human or making sure Hitler got into art school instead of politics; then obviously it makes sense to focus on Hitler. Any Promethian who learned that the Principle choose to focus on the Promethian instead would be moved away from humanity (an anti-milestone?) because at best they wasted their time trying to work out the Principle's inhuman logic, at worst they concluded that if the Principle chose it they should start thinking that way too.

                    So the only way you can have the Principle focus on Promethians is if the Created were the Principle's best tools for doing good that humans can recognise. The Principle introduces a Promethian to Hitler. The Created learns about humanity from interacting with him (if only, what not to do) and in the process either save Hitler from becoming evil, kills him, or infects him with some very slow Transmutation that's the real reason why WoD history looks like the real world rather than Nazi's winning the war.

                    But if the Principle will keep introducing a Promethian to would-be evil dictators so it can kill them. That should have mechanics.

                    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
                    Of course. Like I said, the original comment was a joke. Defining the goals of an unknowable cosmic tier entity is always going to make the setting smaller.
                    Absolutely. But some goals are still more limiting than others.

                    For example, saying that the Principle's goal is to guide humanity on a species wide pigramage. Sort of like the god of Wigg history. That leads to a wider setting than Matt's one simply because it's not about Promethians. It introduces something else to the setting.

                            "Some of us have been around a long time, still walking that road toward the New Dawn, no matter how many decades or eons that takes. Don't know if it's true. It's about the Principle, the source of all thought and design, and its creation. The spark of the the first fires and the cornerstone of the great monuments wanted to make something that was conscious, that was aware. In essence, it wanted to create a Promethean. It didn't build something like you or I would though, out of flesh and steel. No, it built a thing out of ideas. Concepts linked together until they formed self sustaining strings. Memetically describing both itself and the world it inhabited, until it by the time it was finished the world could never have existed without it being a part. We create life in our image. You or I might construct a man, but the Principle manufactured a god. Does it have a purpose? It believe it does, so it describes itself having one and then describes a world to accommodate that goal. The process is both ongoing and already long finished, each incantation and line of code making things as they always were. Only we can really see it. We weren't born into this world, so when the world changes we don't change with it. They won't believe us if we tell them, that's part of being on the outside. If any of us gets in though... At least according to some hobo bodhisattvas, all bets are off..."
