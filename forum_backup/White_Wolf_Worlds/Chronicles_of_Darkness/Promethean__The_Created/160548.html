    Is there anyway to combine Promethean with Wraith?

    06-06-2014, 12:16 AM
    Got my hands on Wraith the Oblivion (few of my ventures into cWoD) and a thought struck me. Prometheans suffer on the same level as Wraiths do but are going in two directions in terms of themes (one trying to leave the world while the other is attempting to join it). Is there anyway to shove Wraith into nWoD or am I just going to have to wait for a Giest-Wraith translation guide and importing from that?

                          Mirroring the themes is the main idea and actually seems to be the easiest thing out of all the above. Prometheans come into the world knowing that the world hates them. It actively despises them and their entire existence is geared towards an introverted quest on getting a soul. Wraiths tend to suffer the same thing. They are dumped into the Shadowlands into a world that actively being ripped apart by Oblivion and their only way get out of it is again, an introverted quest on resolving issues that keeps one in that state.

                          In a way, I view Prometheans as a reverse Wraith. They come literally from nothing (Oblivion), and either steadily progress towards entry of the world of the living or simply become something.. else. Either way, they are forces of pure creation spawn from the mouth of Oblivion in search of meaning.

                          Wraiths on the other hand, try to hold on to meaning long enough to resolve a goal and most don't even know what the goal is. They face an enemy that is ever present and is actively trying to strip away their meaning and experience. The complete opposite of what the goal of a promethean's pilgrimage is about.

                          All this combined with the image of a Promethean trek down the Shadow lands with the wasteland not really having any effect because well... the Shadowlands are shit to begin with as Oblivion's maw continues to consume.

                              What you want are the Lucid Dead and Impassioned Soul Numina from Mummy: the former reformats the ghost to use the traditional nine attributes and skills setup instead of the Ephemeral Beings’ abbreviates “three attributes” thing; Impassioned Soul modifies the ghost so that it’s now driven by a set of Passions instead of being kept on Earth by Anchors, and puts it on a countdown where it needs to pursue its Passions or face dissolution. Personally, I’ve house-ruled the latter to give Impassioned Souls a Sekhem-like power stat* that starts high and degrades over time; the main difference being that when the Impassioned Soul’s power stat runs out, that’s the end for it: if it hasn’t achieved transcendence by then, it’s toast. In the post-GMC WoD, I have this Sekhem-like power stat replace the ghost’s Rank, and I treat the combination of the two Numina plus attendant rules tweaks as the acquisition of a “Wraith” greater template by the ghost — the triggering event occurring when something removes the ghost’s “caul”, to borrow some terminology from Wraith (i.e., whatever it is that keeps most of nWoD’s ghosts trapped in a half-conscious state, where they’re unable to think lucid thoughts and have their passions subdued).

                              * technically more of a hybrid of Mummy’s Sekhem and Geist’s Psyche: I call it Psyche, and impose benefits and drawbacks such as Geist-like Anchors and interaction with the Underworld akin to what Sin-Eaters get. Other stuff I use from Geist are Thresholds (which define favored Numina that are easier to acquire). I’ve considered giving them access to Keys and Manifestations in a manner similar to Geist’s Unfettered Geists (that is, each Key/Manifestation pair as a separate Numina); but that’s still under consideration.

                              I also give Wraiths a slightly modified Memory stat in place of Morality/Integrity. In short, I run Impassioned Souls very much like Mummies on a Descent, save for them being ephemeral and being on their first, last, and only Descent. Removing a ghost’s caul reignites its passions; but it doesn’t restore its former memories, and those passions are now feeding on the wraith’s very being, putting him in a race against time to remember what his unfinished business is, complete it, and move on to whatever comes next before he burns out.

                              It’s much more of a Mummy-like thing than a Promethean-like thing; the main thing I borrow from Promethean is its Milestones and New Dawn mechanics, with “milestones” being recast as “unfinished business” and “New Dawn” being recast more along the lines of Wraith’s Transcendence in terms of what happens when it’s successful.

                              The biggest differences between this conception of a wraith and oWoD’s wraiths are the lack of a Shadow and Shadowguiding, and the absence of an all-pervasive society of the dead. These wraiths simply don’t stick around long enough to form a cohesive society of their own† (instead piggybacking on the societies that ordinary ghosts may or may not have); and the primary conflict is the aforementioned race against the clock instead of an inner struggle against a personal nemesis. If you want to add in the Shadowguiding stuff, go for it; but frankly, these wraiths have enough on their plates without it. But bear in mind that a wraith’s Passions are as capable of driving it to destructive actions as to uplifting ones, especially as they become more desperate; so the “you are your own worst enemy” thing is still in there — just not as a separate voice in your head‡.

                              † Some wraiths died centuries or even millenia ago; but no wraith has been a wraith for more than a decade; and very few survive more than a year or two.

                              ‡ Heck, I’ve even considered the possibility of letting a wraith create a Shade, reminiscent of Changeling’s fetches, onto which the wraith can push its undesirable urges. Not quite a Shadow and Shadowguiding; but close — and entirely optional: not every wraith has a Shade, and the frequency of this phenomenon is left up to the Storyteller. A possible mechanic for this: tie it to Memory Breaking Points. When you lose a point of Memory due to a degeneration check, you can insulate yourself against further losses by empowering your Shade. If you don’t have one yet, you spawn one immediately; and every time you do this, the Shade picks up an Echo Thematically tied to the Breaking Point you blocked off. Meanwhile, the Shade should have means and motive to interfere in the Wraith’s quest, possibly even a means of destroying and consuming the wraith.
