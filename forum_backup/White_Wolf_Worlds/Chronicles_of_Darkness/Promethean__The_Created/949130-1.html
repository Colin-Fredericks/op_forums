                        I want to address some of this, but it's difficult for a couple of reasons, chief among them that this is a tangle of related questions rather than one straightforward dealie. Proceeding as best I can on low sleep reserves:

                        I also think if they're not specifically supposed to be associated with the ghostly frequency of Twilight, then ephemera would be a better name for their Humour than ectoplasm.
                        Where this in particular runs into a problem is that "ectoplasm" has a real-world-and-echoed-here-in-the-game context that means more than "ghost stuff." Ectoplasm is a substance related to the human body (or at least some specific kinds of human bodies) that facilitates the manifestation of otherworldly occult phenomena, most notably ghosts but also any other sort of being that you might see a spiritual medium call to dwell in them. The ectoplasmic humour is stuff which humans can theoretically produce that gives ephemeral beings a grip on our world, not the substance of other worlds itself.


                        As for the matter of "why spirits?" Well, that gets into one of those things that's tricky to grok except in aggregate. This is going to get a little rambly, but please try to bear with me:

                        Much like Mage 2e highlights about ghosts, spirits only really suit the definition of "[animistic/chthonic] reflections of [concepts/people]" in the case of fairly basic and/or hyperfocused specimens. Setting aside the greater Celestines and their Big Recognizable Core Symbols, powerful spirits' portfolios become increasingly useless as guidelines for their psychology as they approach a critical mass of symbolism; spirit-nobles' spheres of influence are important as historical and political considerations, and they're the ones who tend to make it out of the Shadow. Lookaway Quickly may be a spirit of shame and unacknowledged wrongdoing in a general sense, but it's far more salient that it's a monster themed towards those qualities and their impact on its surroundings, whose ability to remain in this world is feeding on the Disquiet your Galateid throngmate is engendering. That's how spirits generally tend to become relevant to a setting whose main characters don't have the luxury of going into the Shadow on a regular basis — they're less clouds of living resonance and more ways the world echoes themes back at itself.

                        Relatedly, the Ulgans' primary historical role as shamans has a little more push toward spirits than ghosts for the simple reason that they cause more trouble in ways that call for more esoteric methods of negotiating than the personalized monomanias of a Mane or Lemure. It's generally better to have the weird hermit you keep out of the village proper deal with the problems that arise from the wilderness being strange and terrifying and Outside than to call on them to talk dirty laundry with something you can keep away with proper burial rites and a heaping helping of salt. The Shadow is just about the leakiest otherworld in the greater Chronicles of Darkness and its principal means of ingress to our world are specifically oriented around places nobody much goes to, which are a lot harder for a community to keep an eye on all of than the sites of bloody battles and mass murder and so on. On that note, the Ulgans' Orphean interpretation is still more broadly focused on the mysterious nature of the land of the dead and the things that dwell there than on the sort of stuff that keeps a newborn ghost anchored, which means the type of ghost that an Ulgan is likely to deal with outside the Underworld is roughly similar to the kind of spirit that they're likely to deal with outside the Shadow: powerful, storied, often too solid to ignore, generally given over to the common supernatural tendencies that make them even remotely predictable.

                        The Shadow is a landscape made up of the living thematic resonances of the material world and their interaction, but this fact serves as a point of interface with concerned parties from the material world, not the whole of its definition, and it's farther away the nearer you are to a lot of other people. The Ulgans were made, broadly speaking, to deal with the strange invisible monsters of myth and folklore so that someone else didn't have to, and that role isolates them in a simultaneous cause-and-effect relation to their ability to interact with (and therefore become entangled with) the invisible monsters that everyone stays away from, which serves as the core of the Lineage's typical desire for humanity.

                        Ghosts could be broadly considered reflective of a similar role to the Ulgan through traditions like the graveyard watcher or their common association with things people shy away from, but a ghost is an inhuman thing that thinks it's human and stands to get less human over time, whereas a spirit is an inhuman thing that looks at humans as resources and thinks like a predator crossed with a supercomputer; one of these things is more obviously a recurring Threat to make a supernatural guardian against, but Bad Stuff comes out of the deadlands as well and there's no real thematic reason to draw a line between the two for the purposes of a gameline where the handling methods are ultimately more or less the same save for the tenor of some of the rumors that surround a given monster.

                        Ulgans deal with spirits-and-ghost-but-mostly-spirits because those are the two most common varieties of ephemeral being that come from the closest ephemeral-being-harboring realms to the material world but one is a lot less location-locked than the other by both metaphysics and inclination.
