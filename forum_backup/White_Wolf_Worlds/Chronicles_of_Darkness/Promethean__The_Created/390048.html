    [2e Thoughts] Do we need the vulnerability to fire?

    02-02-2015, 01:32 PM
    This isn't a powerful/not powerful argument, just a thematic one. The vulnerability to fire is mentioned once in the Promethean corebook, but never seems to really come up in the fiction and certainly doesn't seem to carry the same level of mythic resonance that "fire-as-bane" does for Vampires and Mummies. The explanation of the Promethean's alchemical chemistry reacting badly to fire isn't all that deep or well-explained. It seems almost entirely forgotten about in the supplements and feels a little bit unnecessary to me. That line in the corebook could have been omitted by accident and I doubt anyone would have noticed.

    I know it's a Frankenstein "Fire Bad!" reference, but I guess I question it's necessity and thematic value to the game. Prometheans don't get appreciably that much more powerful taking lethal damage from fire rather than aggravated; they're still basically vulnerable to anything a mortal is vulnerable to, they just have to take more punishment before it starts to really hurt. They can be shot in the head, strangled, suffocated, starved, run over with a truck, etc. They can take a lot of punishment, but part of the Promethean theme is endurance (it's the "Resistance" game, after all) which is somewhat undermined by a relatively common threat they cannot endure in the slightest.

    I don't think that Prometheans need any specific bane (though something with a real basis in folklore or alchemy might work); they're already supposed to be bloody tough, but they aren't (at present) insurmountably so even without the use of fire, fire at lethal is still pretty damn dangerous, and I don't necessarily see the thematic value of avoiding fire (more than you'd avoid any lethal damage, mind you) that exists for Vampires and Mummies. I think the theme of Promethean is supported by the idea you can't put one down with any kind of specific weakness, like a silver bullet or fire, but with overwhelming force - they don't give up their Pilgrimage easily, but the forces arrayed against them (Disquiet, torch-and-pitchfork bearing mobs, etc) are overwhelming.

    Just a thought.

          In all the games I've been in involving Prometheans we dropped the "Fire Bad!" rule for reasons similar to what you outlined. It makes sense for Mummies and Vampires as, yeah, they're corpses, and corpses dry out and turn into kindling. Prometheans aren't undead or undying, but rather "unalive". Their tissues aren't desiccated, and mimic many biological functions.

          Further, if you build a Promethean using the Construct rules and, say, make a Galatead out of marble, Fire Bad makes even less sense. Same goes for most Unfleshed. Naturally if your Promethean was made out of wood or some other flammable material it should apply.

          At the same time it's one of those rules that's pretty easy to excise. I believe it only effects two other rules: 1) it would remove a Torment trigger and 2) the Sublimate Phlogoston Transmutation in Vulcanus would be slightly different. I imagine it also makes Pandorans a little tougher too but I can't recall offhand if they vulnerable to fire or not (they likely are).

                          Vampires have a “most damage I take is bashing” thing going for them; making fire an exception to that is rather important. I think the same holds true for mummies and zombies.

                          But Prometheans aren’t like that, necessarily: they may not be “dead things”. They eat and breathe, and I wouldn’t be surprised to learn that they have a pulse. Arguably, they shouldn’t benefit from “all damage is bashing”: unlike the undead, it’s entirely possible that their internal organs actually do something — though probably not what they would do if the Promethean was human. For instance, the stomache digests food — but in the case of a Promethean, this “digestion” is more of a metaphysical furnace feeding the Azoth that drives the Promethean. The lungs probably work in a similar way, feeding the Fire instead of oxygenating the blood. In effect, a Promethean’s body is an intricate alchemical engine that drives its ongoing animation; and if any of its parts get damaged, that likely throws the alchemical reactions out of whack. So swords and bullets do lethal rather than bashing.

                          If this is so, then special vulnerability to fire isn’t appropriate.

                          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                              We don't know what Disquiet and Wasteland will be like going forwards. And, to be honest, I feel that neither of those is really a bane. Its a plot point to encourage the theme of Pilgrimage on the Promethean. Its more akin to the vampire's social penalties for having low Humanity, ramped up.

                              Banes are something that is anathema to the supernatural. It unravels its very nature, and is primarily used offensively (often combat, but not always) against them. Iron used to contain one of the fae, fire to destroy the vampire's blood magics, etc.

                              Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                              It's something of a supernatural rule in nWoD that "dead things" - Vampires, Prometheans, Mummies - are all vulnerable to fire.
                              Major undead templates, yes. It should be noted that zombies made from mage-magic aren't. Nor are ghosts. So, that's not an absolute rule.

                              In either case, considering that Prometheans' own books, as well as Book of the Dead, ranks the Prometheans as living beings instead of undead, its a moot point. To use D&D terms, Prometheans are constructs and golems. The game covers magical (and occult science-y) Creations.

                              Vampires, mummies, strix, and some zombies are all described as running off some kind of Curse. Its the tag line of Mummy, and a recurring theme running in Vampire alongside the word Damned. Fire is the bane of the cursed undead. Ghosts, geister, and Bound/Sin-Eaters aren't cursed, even as they all run off ectoplasm; their banes are fluid, depending on the individual ban (yes, Bound get bans and banes via their krewes). Prometheans are constructs (possibly flesh, but not necessarily) powered by the divine fire. Why should Created have a ban? They don't have spirit connections, they don't run off a Curse.

                              Thematically, Frankenstein's monster was scared of fire. Fair enough. Franky has a lot in common with zombies. The golem, however, wasn't - his weakness was someone marring the True Name inscribed on his forehead (mage?). Osiris has much in common with the death-gods of GtSE and the Underworld, who are more likely to be weak to salt than fire. The legend of Galatea didn't involve killing her or chasing her with flames; on a related legend of Creepy Dolls, fire isn't as often a solution when compared to a sledgehammer. Unfleshed might come from robots - their "classical weakness" is electricity or someone hacking and reprogramming.

                              And so on.

                                          Vampire 2e's justification for the vulnerability to fire is purely metaphorical, but for the most part fire seems to serve a similar role in existing vampire lore to what it does in the battle against the Lernean Hydra: as a follow-up process to ensure that the thing doesn't keep restoring itself in short order.

                                          A Promethean is a complex balance of internal processes run by a phenomenon that resonates with anything that can be used as a power source. Fire is a notoriously and overtly dangerous instance of such things. Hang a circuitboard or a wire sculpture over an open flame and see how good it looks or how well it works after a swing through the part you're not supposed to touch.

                                          So much of the Pilgrimage is about refinement, about building ever-finer connections to one's potential humanity. Fine things burn notoriously rapidly. It's why kindling is the size that it is. In particular contrast, the Zeky run on an instance of the Divine Fire that's resonant with slow nonfunctional mutation at its most common levels and regular Prometheans restore themselves with a force associated with some of the finest and most complex devices in the world.

                                          Both of those forces can also burn you, but that's not their primary manifestation of power.

                                                      Fire's not as directed a force as electricity, and from philosophical and alchemical perspective has more destructive (entropic) properties than electricity. Lightning is associated with the heavens more often than fire directly, tying into the New Dawn. Electricity is a higher order of power in terms of mastery and use than fire, and Prometheans being based on that means that lesser channels for the Divine Fire are like poison(Which would explain why the Zeka heal from nuclear energy rather than electric).

                                                      There's a number of reasons why fire, a baser channel for the Divine Fire, wouldn't do it for the Prometheans who evoke some pretty high end concepts. At the root may be Frankenstein, but the idea branches out to a lot of other ideas that strengthen the narrative in it's alchemical, modern psuedo-scientific(electricity as elan vital, for example), and philosophical. Fire also has it's roots in Frankenstein but does much the same thing.

                                                      I think, really, we need to ask what reasons beyond Frankenstein was fire made a weakness before saying we need to get rid of it.
