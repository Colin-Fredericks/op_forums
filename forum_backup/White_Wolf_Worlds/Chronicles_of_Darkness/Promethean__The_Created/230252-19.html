            Something I think this new edition needs to focus on is allowing Promethean characters to be proactive rather than just reactive. In 1e, you ramble into town like Bruce Banner and things start happening to you -- maybe you get attacked from Pandorans, maybe your Disfigurements flair and Disquiet kicks in early. But why did you go to the town in the first place? In the book, some ideas include "looking for a job", "trying to blend in with humanity", or "drawn by Azoth", but those are very passive compared to Vampire's "scouting for new blood", Werewolf's "hunting a spirit", or Mage's "we're following the leyline to a source of incredible power". When you try to make long term plans, like maybe running for local office, building a house, or even constructing another Promethean, the game punishes you with the Pandoran/Disquiet/Wasteland Triumvirate and you have to deal with that instead.

            Even the Milestones, which are supposed to guide a Promethean, are totally unknown to the player and -- more importantly -- the character. Which means characters are sometimes going to stumble through the game and miss all of them. There's even a section of the book accounting for this.

            I understand that "getting in the way of stated goals" is essentially the conflict in any story, but that's not what I'm talking about here. What it boils down to is that most of the content presented in 1e makes "languishing under their Pilgrimage" the inciting incident of most stories, and it paints Prometheans as perpetual victims who are only trying to make their way in this crazy world. "Following their heart" is just not enough for me.

                Originally posted by Strange Alchemies, p104, "But A Man Nonetheless" - "Playing the Difference"
                • When did the Promethean first learn about the Pilgrimage? Some Prometheans hear about the Great Work from the moment they are able to understand the concept, or even before. Others hear about it only as they are about to undertake their independence from their creators. Still others only hear their creator talk of it occasionally, or to other people, and they are never given any context for what it means. Some hear absolutely nothing about the Pilgrimage from their creators, and have to discover its existence from other Prometheans. The circumstances under which the Promethean hears of it, and from whom he hears it, can set the tone for his Pilgrimage. Those who hear of it from a cruel creator may walk it with hesitation, while others may consider the Pilgrimage the thing that took their beloved creators from them.

                Other Prometheans simply find themselves on the Pilgrimage. For some, they’ve somehow always known that their goal was to be come human; it simply took someone else putting it into words for them. Others seek to be near humans, to emulate them, but do not understand why they do these things. The Pilgrimage is an innate part of the Promethean existence — the Divine Fire is transformative, and rare is the Promethean who is simply content as he is. Prometheans are all driven by the desire for change, to become something else, and most of them find their role models in humanity.

                                                    "Universal milestones" is an interesting and important addition. That there are more constants means that the Pilgrimage is less a blind fumble, and gives more grounding to the Refinements, in that there are actual things that they can achieve with some reliability, rather than "just" being philosophical practices.

                                                    The actual existence of Azothic Memory I can take or leave. I think 1e laid out a strong enough arc that adding a new Azothic Memory isn't adding much of anything that wasn't there already. A "Running Monkey" period for the Created is a perfect segment for exploring humanity through a neutral lens. Assuming a Disquiet/Wasteland framework as crushing as currently, of course. And the presence or absence of a Promethean creator in this time is much more poignant if no such Memory drives the newly Created.

                                                    Azothic Memory does step on notions of free will, if it provides reliable guidance to a "correct" path on the Pilgrimage. The drives humans experience keep them alive, but listening to them alone tends to fuck up any higher calling. While Prometheans are given over to a supernatural god-flame, this divinity is bugnuts consumed with a fizzy, frothing reality where nothing remains solid for long, and a predictable Pilgrimage isn't what I'd imagine it has prepared for player characters.

                                                        What Arcane said. Also, the choice isn’t between “there is always a way to complete te Pilgrimage” and “there is never a way to complete it”. 1e’s sample Extempore faced a rather nasty dilemma that he needed to create a progeny before he could Redeem, but had no way of knowing how to do so. One could take that as “he’s in an impossible predicament, and doomed to failure”; or you could take it as “he hasn’t been sufficiently creative and/or desperate to find a way out of his catch-22 yet”. After all, Promethean specializes in doing the impossible.

                                                        I’m wondering if “you must create progeny in order to complete the Pilgrimage” is still going to be a thing in 2e. If the primary reason for having it in there was to encourage the continuation of the species, 2e’s greater emphasis on demiurges largely undermines said rationale.

                                                        Also, to what extent will 2e’s Extempore be “created by accident”, and to what extent will they be “the catch-all category for any Promethean who doesn’t fit any of the regular Lineages”? That is, could a demiurge create an Extempore?

                                                        On demiurges: will there be rules for mortals performing the generative act, or will that be left in the realm of “it works when the Storyteller says it works”? If the former, will Prometheans be able to partake of the demiurge rules, allowing (say) a Frankenstein to create a Galeteid (with the creation of another Frankenstein merely being the easier option)?
