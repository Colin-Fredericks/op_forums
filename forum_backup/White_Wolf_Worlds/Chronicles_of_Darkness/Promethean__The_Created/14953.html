    Ephemeral Lineages

    11-20-2013, 07:31 PM
    The new World of Darkness has always drawn a distinction between spirits and ghosts; but God-Machine Chronicle has taken it up a notch by declaring that spirits and ghosts are made of different kinds of ephemera, and then adding a third kind of ephemeral being (angels) that are made of their own kind of ephemera.

    This, I think, is the genius behind Benjamin Beard’s Samedi and Seraphim Lineages: instead of going for a core of five Lineages (with Ulgans feeling like something of a fifth wheel), he appears to be going with a set of four “elemental Lineages” (the Fire-based Frankensteins, the Air-based Galeteids, the Water-based Osirans, and the Earth-based Tammuz) and a separate set of “ephemeral Lineages” (including the spirit-based Ulgans, the ghost-based Samedi, and the angel-based Seraphim). My initial reaction to that last one was “why?” — but given this context, I can definitely get behind the idea.

                      Thinking up elemental associations for the ephemeral Lineages is fun; but really, I wouldn’t want to try to shoehorn them into the four-elements model. It’s more important that each Lineage be reflective of the type of ephemera that it’s tied to; and the list of ephemeral bengs s likely to grow. As is, we have:

                      • from Vampire: the Requiem, the Strix are kinda-sorta ephemeral beings, though not really. I wouldn’t bother with a Lineage for them.
                      • Werewolf: the Forsaken gives us spirits. The resulting Ulgan Lineage really needs to reflect the animism on which the spirit world is based.
                      • Mage: the Awakening features a number of options, though the one that’s unique to the game is the Astral entities. Note that Magnum Opus has a Dream-born Lineage.
                      • Promethean: the Created has the qashmallim. However, they’re too closely related to Prometheans, and too reminiscent of the GM’s angels, to get their own Lineage IMHO.
                      • Changeling: the Lost has nothing. I wouldn’t mind adding Will-o-Wisps to the Hedge; but that would be a fan-splat in its own right, and I try not to do fan-splats based off of other fan-splats.
                      • Hunter: the Vigil has everything and nothing.
                      • Geist: the Sin-Eaters has ghosts, geists, Kerberoi, and other denizens of the Underworld.
                      • Mummy: the Curse has a class of Lifeless who reside in Twilight. I’m debating if they’d qualify as ghost-like entities, or if the unique nature of the Mummy metaphysics would classify them as a separate type of ephemera. For now, I’m classifying them as ghost-like.
                      • Demon: the Descent has the God-Machine’s angels.
                      • And finally, there are the demons of World of Darkness: Inferno. Some have merged with ghosts and spirits (qashmallim are explicitly prohibited, and I suspect that the GM’s angels are likewise), while others are “true” demons, neither ghost nor spirt nor anything else but themselves.

                      So we’ve got spirits, astral entities, ghosts, angels, and demons.

Their inclusion with the original Lineages was arbitrary; this feels more natural to me, and gives them their own place.

                                              For what it's worth, they never felt like a fifth wheel to me. They felt like outsiders, which is excellent - they are outsiders. Nobody but nobody knows the things they know or sees the shit they see. They are from stage one exposed to things that drive normal men crazy and make other Prometheans say "Bro, you're loose a screw."
                                              So if they feel like outsiders then I think that's probably exquisite design. Their Humour is a bit strange - and the book even calls them out on it, saying it's black where ectoplasm, traditionally, is white - and they interact with Spirits, but that means they see the world as no one can (or was meant to).

                                              The difference between a fifth wheel and an outsider, in this context, is that the "Fifth Wheel" is just along for funsies. They exist only by way of perceived "necessity" to the group, or they're the group's butt-monkey. They are shoe-horned in. The Riven, though, don't feel shoe-horned in to me; they feel fucked-up and strange and like they have brushed the alien, because they have, and that makes them scary. Arbitrary structure around "Ephemeral Lineages" (or replacing them with another ghost-based Lineage, cough-cough) would certainly make them seem like a fifth wheel.
                                              Conversely, "the outsider" is that guy or gal who is very clearly not like everyone else, but is still present. Carrie (from "Carrie") is an outsider. So is "May" (from "May"). H.P. Lovecraft's "The Outsider" does an exquisite job of being at once existentially terrifying and embodying what I'm talking about here. The Ulgan aren't an afterthought; they're freaking coming in from without and that's scary. They have a vantage point others don't; the fifth wheel, though, doesn't have any vantage point.
                                              He's in the trunk of the car while the other four have fun.

                                              You'll note that I said they were the outsider, and I think their design and write-up(s) gets that across wonderfully - if you're going to have an outsider, a weird one, in a collection of freaks and monsters and devils, then making that so by having them interact with a spirit-realm that would make a schizophrenic stop and go "hold up," is excellent.
                                              They're not "The Other," though. That's Pandorans and Qashmallim. Riven are strictly the outsider - they get to funk around in two worlds!
                                              They still get the short end of the stick.
