    Toronto by Night

    05-01-2018, 08:54 PM
    I really enjoyed updating and messing around with the New Orleans city book, so I thought I'd try my hand at making my own. and since Storyteller Vault still doesn't allow Requiem and CoD material, this seemed like the best place for me to share what I've written. I've decided to do the write-up for the city that's closest to where I live, Toronto. I do have to say it's really a shame that not only has there never been a Toronto city book, but none of the current Requiem books have any real information on Toronto as it appears in the world of darkness. This is all still very much a work in progress, so it'll likely change every now and then.

    If there's anything I missed, or got wrong, or whatever please leave a comment, I would love to hear all suggestions people may have. Even just suggestions for things that I could add would be very welcome.

    First up is a history of Toronto:

    European Arrival
    The first Europeans to arrive in the Toronto area were French fur trappers. Throughout the first half of the 18th century the trappers set up a number of trading posts in the area and began doing business with the local Iroquois. In between visits from the French, the Iroquois came under the influence of a powerful and dangerous entity of unknown name and origin. A shaman of the nearby Mississaugas tribe, who happened to be a powerful Bone Shadow werewolf, sensed the arrival of this entity. Wasting no time, the shaman gathered his people and launched an attack against the Iroquois. The battle was fierce, but in the end the entity was destroyed and the surviving Iroquois were forced to flee south. Although the Toronto region was now under Mississaugas control, they refused to live on what they considered to be cursed land. When the French returned to the area, they were surprised to find the Iroquois gone and their villages abandoned.

    In the later part of the 18th century the British seized control of most of Canada. The French were pushed out of the Toronto area, which was absorbed into the newly forming British Upper Canada.

    Town of York
    In 1796 Lieutenant Governor John Graves Simcoe went to the Toronto area in search of a town to make into the capitol of Upper Canada. He established the village of York to use as a temporary camp while he conducted his search. He looked at several settlements in the area before finally deciding that the best place for the capitol was actually York itself. It was during this time that the first European vampires arrived in the area.

    In 1812 war broke out between Canada and America. Before the Canadians had a chance to prepare a proper defence, an American army sailed right into the harbour at York. In order to buy time for the Canadians to evacuate, General Roger Sheaffe ordered the grand magazine torched. This huge structure at the mouth of the harbour was packed full of gunpowder and ammunition. It was a vampire, a Ventrue known as Bertrund, who led a small group of Canadian troops to the grand magazine. While his troops began lighting fires, Bertrund held off the American troops all by himself. The resulting explosion was so massive it rattled windows in settlements 50 km away. An entire division of American troops were killed in the blast, along with Bertrund and the commanding officer of the American army. The Americans stayed long enough to sack the city and burn down the government buildings, but were then forced to retreat.

    The Canadians retook York soon after the Americans left. Jonathan, one of Bertrund’s childer, played a major part in rebuilding the town and strengthening its defences. When the Americans returned in 1814, Jonothan was front and centre during the fighting. This time the Americans were driven off, then the war ended soon after. For all that he had done for the city, Jonothan was made the Prince of York.

    Early Toronto
    In the years following the war, York grew at a rapid pace. Unfortunately the city’s infrastructure couldn’t keep up with this growth. The city became a horrible place to live, earning nicknames such as “Muddy York" and “Dirty Little York”.

    In 1834, in an attempt to reinvent the city, York was renamed to Toronto and William Lyon Mackenzie became it’s first mayor. Despite numerous improvements that were made to the city, tension continued to rise between the ruling class and the merchant and worker classes. Although the tension started due to legitimate concerns, this escalation was due to the meddling of a Daeva called Isobel.

    A French noblewoman, Isobel had lost her only two of childer when the British took control of Canada. As soon as she learned of this, Isobel came to Canada seeking revenge. After cutting a bloody swatch through the country, Isobel made her way to Toronto. Before Prince Jonathan even knew what was happening, Isobel had whipped a good portion of the city’s population into open rebellion. She even managed to sink her claws into Mayor Mackenzie, who ended up becoming one of the rebellion’s leaders. Jonathan’s retribution was swift and bloody. In 1837 Isobel was destroyed and her rebellion was crushed.

    Mackenzie survived the failed rebellion and fled south into America. He lived there for many years before returning to Toronto in 1849, where he lived until his death in 1861. After his death, Mackenzie’s ghost has become one of the most powerful in Toronto, if not one of the most powerful in all of Canada. Other ghosts have been drawn to Mackenzie over the years, including those of his wife and several of his children. A Krew of powerful Geists once tried to banish Mackenzie and his fellow ghosts to the underworld, but even their collective powers weren’t enough. Mackenzie and his fellow ghosts continue to haunt his house even to this day.

    Toronto grew and developed rapidly throughout the rest of the 19th century. The only major disasters being a Typhus epidemic in 1847 that killed 863 Irish immigrants, and a fire in 1849 that destroyed a good-sized section of the city including the “Market Block”, St. James Cathedral, and part of the first city hall. Fortunately, only a couple neonates were caught and destroyed.

    20th Century
    At the conclusion of World War 2, Toronto suffered an unsustainable influx of European immigrants. Toronto, and several nearby towns, grew until they began to merge together. Toronto, which became known as Old Toronto, formed the core while the other towns were made into suburbs. In 1954 the Metropolitan Toronto regional government was set up to manage and develop Toronto and its new suburbs. Along with all the humans came an equally massive influx of Kindred. Jonathan was overwhelmed and soon began to struggle to keep track of all the new Kindred flooding into his domain. To help deal with this situation Jonathan appointed five of his most trusted allies to become Barons of the five outlaying suburbs. This allowed Jonathan to focus his attention on Old Toronto, the only part of the city he really cared about anyway.

    Only a few years later Jonathan decided it was time to enter a brief period of torpor. He made his protege, Nigel, Regent before placing himself into torpor in a secure and secret location.

    In 1998 the Metropolitan Toronto regional government was dissolved. Old Toronto and the suburbs were amalgamated into one Toronto "megacity". Despite the amalgamation, Nigel decided to leave the Barons in charge of the suburbs. The arrangement seemed to be working well enough, and Nigel didn’t want to make such a major change without Jonathan.

    21st Century
    Including the suburbs, the megacity of Toronto currently has a population over 2.5 million, making it the third largest city in North America. Old Toronto alone has a population of nearly 800,000. Modern day Toronto is known to be one of the most ethnically diverse cities in the world. The city is also home to a variety of supernatural creatures. If one looks hard enough they're likely to find at least one of everything lurking in the shadows of Toronto.

      And here is some information on the supernatural inhabitants of Toronto. I'm focusing on vampires, werewolves, and geists for now because those are the ones I know most about and they're the splatbooks I'm most interested in. In time I may add blubs for a couple others, like Mages and Mummies.

      Kindred
      The Invictus are firmly in control of Toronto, at least for now. The current Senechal, Bishop Carver, believes that Jonathan has grown weak, and Nigel was an inappropriate choice to be Regent. He believes that only he and the Lancea have the strength and conviction needed to cleanse Toronto of its undesirable elements, such as the resident members of the Circle of the Chrone, the Carthians, and the Ordo Dracul.

      Several small groups of Carthians have existed in Toronto for many years, but only recently has a group grown to any significant power. This group is trying to create a more modern, democratic-based form of government for the Kindred inhabitants of Toronto. They’re not interested in violent rebellion, but more a gradual and steady transition. However, even vampires have a limited amount of patience.

      The Ordo Dracul don’t often involve themselves in Kindred politics. Any political power they do have is usually spent trying to make sure they can continue to conduct their experiments and research in peace. This isn’t always easy, especially when more forward thinking members of the Ordo try to deal with the more anachronistic views of the Invictus and Lancea leaders of the city.

      At first glance one might assume the Circle of the Crone doesn’t have much of a presence in Toronto. In fact they have an entire shadow kingdom built up in the sewers, subways, and tunnels that run under most of the city. Because Jonothan had very little interest in the subterranean parts of his city, he allowed the Circle to move in, but only if they agreed to stay below street level and not do anything that would endanger the Masquerade. The Circle has abided by these rules, for the most part, but as their position within the city strengthens, and especially since Jonothan went into torpor, they have become bolder in testing how far they can bend the rules and seeing how much they can get away with.

      Werewolves
      When the Mississaugas shaman defeated the powerful entity that corrupted the Iroquois of the Toronto region, he created a pack whose job was to look after the area. In addition to their usual duties, they were also meant to watch out for any signs that the entity may be returning. Several generations have passed and there hasn’t been the slightest hint that the entity is coming back. The pack still exists, however, and remains vigilant. If it does reappear one day, there’s no telling how much death and destruction it could cause by corrupting one of the biggest cities in North America.

      Over the years many other werewolves have taken up residence in and around Toronto. The majority are Iron Masters, but you can find members of all the tribes in the city. Even the purebloods have a pack that operates within Toronto. This pack is made up of violent, human-hating troublemakers. Even Jonathan took steps to remove this pack from his city. Eradicating the Pureblood has proven to be extremely difficult. Nobody is even sure how many members the pack has, or where their lair is.

      Geists
      Toronto has it’s fair share of ghosts for a city of its size, a few of which are even quite powerful, but its never had a serious problem with ghosts. The small population of Geists that live within Toronto tend to work independently of each other, handling jobs solo. Krews that do form in Toronto are small and only stay together long enough to deal with a situation that happens to be beyond the abilities of a single Geist.

                                  I look forward to the retail release of the changeling 2E books. I would love to add Scheherazade's Freehold to my Toronto write-up. In the meantime, I've been working on writing up the districts of Toronto. It's turning out to be way too much to fit into a single post, so I'm going to have to split it into two posts (maybe more). I also plan on making a map once I've written out the districts and landmarks. Anyway, here's the first part:

                                  Toronto Islands - Just offshore from the city centre is a series of islands that are home to an exclusive community. Houses are so sought after there’s a waiting list over 300 people long. Vehicles are not allowed on the islands, both residents and visitors are allowed walking and cycling only. Regular ferries provide transport to and from the mainland.
                                  Many people visit the island to take advantage of it’s various recreational facilities. There are several swimming beaches, a number of locations for recreational boating, and a frisbee golf course. The primary attraction of the islands is Centreville, a children’s amusement park built in 1967. The park has a 19th century style, with a miniature railway and an antique carousel.
                                  The far Western part of the islands is the Toronto Island Airport, which provides local air travel. Residents of the island are constantly complaining about the amount of noise generated by the airport and desperately want it shut down.
                                  The Islands used to be sacred ground to the native Mississaugas tribe. They believed that the islands possessed remarkable healing properties. The sick and injured were often brought to the islands to help them recover. Ownership of the islands was contested all the way until 2010 where a settlement was finally reached and the Mississaugaus relinquished their claim to the island. Some are still not too happy about this arrangement.
                                  Overlooking th South-Western part of the islands stands the Gibraltar Point Lighthouse, best known as the home of one of Toronto’s most well-known ghosts: John Paul Radelmuller. Radelmuller became the lighthouse’s first keeper just after it opened in 1808. Two days into 1815 Radelmuller was attacked by two soldiers and thrown to his death off the top of the lighthouse. To cover up their crime, the soldiers mutilated the body and buried the pieces in multiple graves. The pieces were never found, and although the two soldiers were arrested, they were found not guilty due to lack of evidence. Radelmuller’s ghost haunts the lighthouse to this day, seeking vengeance for his brutal murder.

                                  Harbourfront - Toronto’s harbourfront is primarily made up of docks and warehouses mixed in with tiny parks and some condos. The harbourfront’s main claim to fame is the Harbourfront Centre, a massive structure that houses galleries, performance spaces, and four craft studios (ceramics, glass, metal, and textiles).
                                  Ferry services from the Jack Layton Ferry Terminal provide access to the Toronto Islands.

                                  Liberty Village - This district was originally meant to be residential, but when it was cut off by a newly laid rail line it instead became an industrial area. In the later part of the 20th century all the manufacturing facilities began to close down or move away. In the early 21st century the district reinvented itself as a residential district for young artists. Many of the old factories have been converted into restaurants, gyms, shops, and galleries. Numerous modern lofts and condos have been built to house all the new people.

                                  Entertainment District - This district was once called the Garment District. It was entirely industrial, filled with factories focussed on producing basic textiles and various types of clothing. In the first half of the 20th century the factories began to close down. At the beginning of 1980 Toronto’s first nightclub, The Twilight Zone, opened in an abandoned factory in the then nearly deserted Garment District. Many other nightclubs opened over the years, as well as numerous theatres, performing arts centres, and cultural attractions. Even today nearly all of Toronto’s nightclubs can be found in the Entertainment District. With the arrival of the nightclubs, the population of the district exploded.
                                  The Entertainment District is considered to be one of the best Racks in the city. Nigel has done his best to control access to the district, but competition for hunting rights is fierce and has led to more than one bloody altercation.

                                  Old Town - This is the oldest part of the city, comprising what used to be the town of York. York was originally built as a ten-block townsite laid out in a gridiron pattern. This pattern remains to this day, and many of the buildings in this district date back to the early 19th century. The building here are mostly the kind that have businesses on the bottom floor and apartments on the upper floors.

                                  Distillery District - This district was originally set up in 1832 as the Gooderham and Worts Distillery. By the 1860s it was the largest distillery in the world, exporting whisky around the world. In the late 20th century the distillery began to shut down and large sections were left abandoned. By the end of the 20th century the distillery had been completely shut down and the buildings were starting to be converted into condos and commercial space.
                                  The district’s unique look and Victorian architecture has made it a popular location for film shoots. Hundreds of movies and television shows have been filmed here in the past couple decades.
                                  The district has been designated a National Historic site of Canada. It still contains the largest collection of Victorian-era industrial architecture in North America, the oldest of them dating back to the mid 19th century. Vehicles are not allowed onto this district’s brick-paved streets.

                                  Trinity-Bellwoods - The western portion of this district is mostly residential housing, while the eastern portion contains a number of recreational facilities including tennis courts, a hockey rink, a greenhouse, a swimming pool, and the sizable Trinity Bellwoods park, which is where the district got its name.
                                  In the mid 20th century this district became home to many Portugese immigrants. Even today, much of the district’s character is influenced by Portugese culture, earning it the nickname “Little Portugal”.

                                  Chinatown - Toronto’s first Chinatown was established in the early 20th century. In the mid 20th century the original Chinatown was demolished to make way for the new City Hall and Nathan Philips Square. In the 1960 many of the Chinese businesses and residents moved into the neighbourhood around Spadina Avenue and Dundas Street West, forming a new Chinatown.
                                  Unknown to the human residents, the reason for the demolition of the original Chinatown was not simply to make way for the new Town Hall. A few years before entering Torpor, Jonathan had discovered that a Jiang Shi named Zheng Qiu had taken up residence in the original Chinatown and was leading a particularly nasty branch of the Triad. After an attack that wiped out most of the Triad and the subsequent demolishing of the neighbourhood, Jonothan believed he had solved the problem. Unfortunately, although Zheng was badly injured, he had managed to survive. Once Jonathan entered Torpor, Zheng resurfaced to help rebuild Chinatown, and reestablish his criminal organisation. Zheng had learned his lessen however. This time he was lot more cautious, and so far his efforts have allowed him to fly under the radar of Nigel and most of the other powerful Kindred of the city.

                                              Part 2 of the districts write-ups:
                                              Financial District - This district is the heart of Toronto’s financial industry. It’s the most densely built up area of toronto, filled with banks, corporate HQs, legal and accounting firms, insurance companies, stockbrokers, and advertising agencies. Prince Jonathan owns a series of offices here that he used when conducting official meetings. Nigel now uses them in Jonathan’s name.
                                              Most of the buildings in this district are connected together by a series of subterranean pedestrian walkways, known as PATH. The walkways that make up PATH are large, brightly lit, and lined with a variety of retail outlets, forming what is basically a giant subterranean shopping mall. PATH has always been hotly contested ground. The Shadow Kingdom claims that since PATH is below ground, it should be part of their domain. Prince Jonathan, on the other hand, always claimed that since PATH is technically just an extension of the building it connects to, it’s actually part of the Prince’s domain. More likely, Jonathan just didn’t want the Shadow kingdom living right under his feet. When Nigel became Regent he also took over the negotiations for PATH. The talks haven’t been going well, especially since most of the advice Nigel gets regarding PATH comes from Bishop Carver.

                                              Moss Park - This district was originally the heart of Toronto’s industrial area. The entire district was made up of factories and worker housing. In the mid 20th century most of the district was demolished and replaced with cheap housing. Although it’s seen some improvement in recent years, this district and the surrounding it is one of the poorest areas in the city.
                                              Moss Park got it’s name for the moss that covered the Allen family estate that was one of the richer houses in the district. The estate was demolished along with all the factories in the mid 20th century, and was replaced by a large park and a community centre.

                                              Palmerston - This is primarily a middle-class residential district surrounding a small commercial strip named Little Italy. Originally, the majority of this district’s residents were of Italian descent, but in recent years many Italian families have moved out to the suburbs. Even though the Italian population is now one of the smallest in the district, the restaurants and businesses have kept a distinct Italian style.

                                              Church and Wellesley - Although this residential district was only openly and officially recognised as an LGBT-oriented enclave near the end of the 20th century, it has been popular with gay subculture for many decades. The annual Pride Week celebration is hosted in this district, and is well known for being the largest of its kind in Canada. Numerous gay bars line Church St, the street that runs right though the centre of the district. Most of the bars here have always been friendly towards the gay community, even before the district’s official designation. This has caused problems for the bars in the past, but nothing too serious.
                                              This district is also an officially recognised historic community containing Victorian architecture dating back to the late 19th century.

                                              Cabbagetown - The original inhabitants of this residential district were Irish immigrants, who settled here in the 1840s. The district got its name from the way the immigrants would plant cabbages in their front yards. Even though this is no longer common practice, the name stuck. This district was a slum until its gentrification in the 1970s. Now, it’s a mid to high class residential area, although it does still have a sizable population of homeless.
                                              This is also an historic district that contains the largest area of Victorian housing in all of North America.

                                              The Annex - Sitting just north of the University of Toronto, the inhabitants of this residential district are primarily teachers and students. This district also contains a large number of fraternity houses. Although the majority of houses were built in the Victorian and Edwardian styles, a few were built with a unique style known as the Annex style. Invented in 1887, the main features of this style are large rounded arches, decorative accents such as turrets, and extra emphasis on the attics. These large buildings are made from either brick or sandstone. Although originally built as mansions for the rich, they have since been divided up into apartments.
                                              The Ordo Dracul have a strong presence in this district. Although they have Wyrm’s nests set up all over Toronto, two of their primary Crucibles can be found here. They are both well hidden, their locations known only to members of the Ordo.

                                              Casa Loma - This small but wealthy residential district takes its name from the castle it’s built around. The castle, built in the early 20th century, was originally meant to be privately owned. The owner went bankrupt, however, and gave the castle to the city.
                                              Even though the castle is fairly new, there are already stories of dozens of ghosts that live on the grounds or in the surrounding area. It’s difficult to tell how many of these ghosts are just marketing, are actual ghosts, or are the illusions of the castle’s resident Kindred, a Nosferatu who calls himself the Phantom of Casa Loma.

                                              Rosedale - This residential district is built around three ravines. It was named after the large number of wild roses that grew in the area. This is one of the richest districts in Toronto, with a lot of its residents coming from very old money. Vehicular travel is made difficult here due to the ravines and the wandering layout of the streets. Because of this, most people either walk or cycle wherever they need to go.
                                              Prince Jonathan owns a mansion here, one of the first to be built in this district. Nigel has taken temporary ownership of the mansion and is looking after it until Jonathan wakes from Torpor.

                                              Midtown - The western half of this large district is made up of wealthy residential neighbourhoods. The eastern half, on the other hand, is almost entirely made up of massive apartment buildings and condos. The central section of this district makes up Toronto’s secondary business district, which is built around the Yonge and Eglinton intersection. The area around the intersection contains numerous office towers, corporate HQs, and recreational facilities. The area around the intersection, including nearby residential neighbourhoods, is often considered to be the heart of Toronto. It’s one of the highest rated areas in Toronto in terms of crime rate, housing, healthcare, entertainment, etc.

                                              Sewers - Running under most of Toronto is a sewer system made up of massive tunnels and arched chambers. If it weren’t for the rivers of sewage you would think you were standing in a Victorian cathedral and not a sewer system. The sewers are old, but well maintained. Several sections of the sewer used to be creeks and small rivers, until the city was built on top of them. All the tunnels that make up the Toronto sewers eventually converge on a water treatment plant called "The Palace of Purification".
                                              The sewers are the primary domain of the Shadow Kingdom. The leaders of the Kingdom, Heirophant Amelia and her court, rule from a set of secret rooms hidden deep within the Palace of Purification. Jonathan was never truly aware of just how far the influence of the Kingdom reaches. Although technically The Kingdom extends throughout the sewer system and most of the subway system, Amelia is only really able to maintain her control over a good sized section around the Palace. The further one travels from the Palace, the more lawless, and dangerous, the sewers become.
                                              The Kingdom does what it can to maintain good relations with the Kindred on the surface. Amelia is smart enough to know that she and her Circle friends have it pretty good, and there’s no point in stirring up trouble. However, visitors to the Kingdom may not be met with immediate violence, but every Kindred in Toronto knows if you enter the sewers you take your life in your own hands.

This is where things really start to get interesting.

                                                  Casa Loma - This mansion was built in the early 20th century in a gothic-revival style. Fraught with delays and ever increasing debts, the owner, Sir Henry Pellatt, was forced to abandon the mansion. It was taken over by William Sparling, who turned it into a luxury hotel. Despite it’s popularity with Americans coming up to Canada during prohibition, the hotel never did well. By the end of the 1920s the hotel went bankrupt. The building was left abandoned for several years before the city took ownership and then leased it to the Kiwanis Club in the mid 1930s. The mansion was opened to the public as a museum. In the early 2000's, a botched attempt to renovate the exterior caused the Kiwanis Club to lose their lease. In 2014 a new company, Liberty Entertainment Group, took the lease and agreed to finish the renovations. The site has been used as a set for numerous movies and TV shows. It’s also used for private functions such as weddings. The mansion is rumoured to be haunted by numerous ghosts. Some are fake, some are real, but many are the projections of the mansion’s resident Nosferatu, who calls himself the Phantom of Casa Loma.
                                                  Casa Loma | Toronto's Majestic Castle
                                                  http://casaloma.ca/


                                                  City Hall - The current city hall was built in 1965. The previous city hall had been in use since 1899. Both old and new city halls sit on the edge of Nathan Philips Square, named for Toronto’s mayor of 1955-1962. Numerous festivals are held in the square, including New Years, and the annual Cavalcade of Lights Festival. The new city hall was built where the original Chinatown used to be located. There are rumours that Zheng Qiu still has numerous secret tunnels in the area, some of which could lead into the new city hall itself. The new city hall was built with a very unique style: a circular bowl-shaped council chamber flanked by two curved office towers, one seven stories taller than the other.
                                                  https://www.toronto.ca/services-paym...res/city-hall/
                                                  https://www.toronto.ca/services-paym...illips-square/

                                                  CN Tower - The most iconic landmark in Toronto, if not all of Canada. Most people think the tower is nothing more than an elevator with a restaurant at the top, but it’s also used as a communications tower with numerous television broadcasters, FM radio antennas, and cellular providers. The CN Tower was the tallest freestanding structure in the world from 1976-2007. Now is ninth tallest in the world, but still the tallest in the Western Hemisphere.
                                                  Canada's Celebration Destination
                                                  http://www.cntower.ca/en-ca/home.html
                                                  Welcome to the CN Tower. Whether you need to plan your visit, make a reservation for 360 Restaurant or book EdgeWalk, you can do it all here.


                                                  Fort York - The original Fort York was built in 1797, but it was destroyed in the battle of 1812 when the grand magazine was blown up. When Canadian forces retook York they rebuilt the fort on the same spot as the previous one. Fort York is now just a museum, but the nearby Fort York Armoury, built in 1933, houses several units of Primary Reserve and the Canadian Cadet Organization.
                                                  Home
                                                  https://www.fortyork.ca/
                                                  The Friends of Fort York and Garrison Common, is a not-for-profit organization dedicated to enhance and promote Fort York and the Garrison Common.


                                                  Gibraltar Point Lighthouse - Overlooking th South-Western part of the Toronto Islands stands the Gibraltar Point Lighthouse, home of one of Toronto’s most well-known ghosts: John Paul Radelmuller. Radelmuller became the lighthouse’s first keeper just after it opened in 1808. To pass the time Radelmuller used to brew beer. Two days into 1815 a pair of soldiers came by the lighthouse to enjoy some of Radelmuller’s beer, but when he turned them away they attacked the lighthouse keeper and threw him to his death off the top of the lighthouse. To cover up their crime, the soldiers mutilated the body and buried the pieces in multiple graves. The pieces were never found, and although the two soldiers were arrested, they were found not guilty due to lack of evidence. Radelmuller’s very angry ghost haunts the lighthouse to this day, seeking vengeance for his brutal murder.
                                                  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibral...int_Lighthouse

                                                  Graffiti Alley - At the south end of Chinatown is a well known stretch of alley where graffiti artists are allowed to paint the walls. This alley contains some amazing examples of street art, the kind of stuff one would normally see hanging on the walls of an art gallery. The artwork here is constantly being added to and replaced. You could visit the alley ever day and see new art that wasn’t there the day before.
                                                  https://localwiki.org/toronto/Graffiti_Alley

                                                  Hockey Hall of Fame - This building is a museum and hall of fame dedicated to the history of hockey. Originally established in Kingston, 1943, it didn’t get it’s own dedicated building until it moved to Toronto in 1993. Home to numerous hockey trophies, including the Stanley Cup when it’s not on tour. Displays exist for numerous individuals, including players, coaches, and team owners.
                                                  Hockey Hall of Fame - Home of the Stanley Cup & Hockey at its Best.
                                                  https://www.hhof.com/
                                                  Hockey Hall of Fame - Home of the Stanley Cup & Hockey at its Best.


                                                  Mount Pleasant Cemetery - This is one of the largest and oldest cemeteries in Toronto. Opened in 1876, this was the first cemetery in Toronto available to everyone. Previously, cemeteries were limited to members of the Roman Catholic Church and the Church of England. The monuments and crypts here show off some remarkable architecture.
                                                  http://www.mountpleasantgroup.com/Lo...-Pleasant.aspx

                                                  Night Market - Beneath the Bay street subway station is Lower Bay, a station that was only in use for six months in 1966. This station was part of an experiment by the TTC (Toronto Transit Commission) and was closed when the experiment failed. Although it’s still used for storage, and occasionally as a set for film shoots, Lower Bay now serves a greater purpose as the secret entrance to the Night Market. The entrance from Lower Bay is well hidden and protected by magical wards. Finding it without instructions, or a guide, is nearly impossible. The Night Market itself is a series of abandoned tunnels build around a large central chamber. It’s neutral ground where supernatural creatures of all types can buy, sell, and trade anything they want: mundane and magical, legal and illegal. You can find pretty much anything in the Night Market, as long as you’re willing to pay for it. No one is really certain who, if anyone, is actually in charge of the Market. Visitors and vendors alike take care of themselves, there’s no management, and no policing force beyond personal bodyguards. The night market works because everyone agrees to make it work.
                                                  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_station_(Toronto)

                                                  Queen’s Park - This urban park, opened in 1860, is actually owned by the University of Toronto, but is leased to the city. At the centre of the park sits the Ontario Legislative Building, constructed in 1892, which houses the Legislative Assembly of Ontario. This building is built on land that used to be occupied by the Auxiliary Female Asylum. The Asylum was founded in 1849, but closed down in the late 1860s when it moved to a new location. The asylum was left abandoned until it was demolished to make room for the Legislative Building. There are more than half a dozen ghosts haunting the building, including at least three of people who died in the Asylum.
                                                  https://www.ola.org/en

                                                  Rogers Centre - Originally named Skydome when it opened in 1989, this is a multi-purpose stadium. In addition to hosting sports events, the stadium hosts conventions, trade fairs, concerts, carnivals, and even monster truck shows. It’s the first ever stadium to have a fully-retractable roof. An attached hotel has numerous rooms that overlook the field. The stadium went bankrupt in 1998, then was bought by Rogers in 2004 who then made the wildly unpopular decision to rename the stadium.
                                                  Rogers Centre | Toronto Blue Jays
                                                  https://www.mlb.com/bluejays/ballpark
                                                  Rogers Centre has been the home of the Blue Jays since 1989.


                                                  Royal Alexander Theatre - Built in 1907, this is the oldest theatre in North America that has remained in continuously operation. It’s also commonly referred to as “Royal Alex”, “the Alex”, and “the R.A.T.”. It’s named for Queen Alexandria, wife of King Edwards VII, great-grandmother to the current Queen of Canada, Elizabeth II. The building is an architectural marvel, with an Italian marble lobby, Venetian mosaic floors, elaborately carved walnut and cherrywood stairs and railings, and silk wallpapers. The theatre struggled for a long time before being bought out and renovated in 1962. It was then reopened a year later and quickly became one of Toronto’s most well-known historic theatres. It was recently renovated once again, in 2016. The renovations helped restore the theatre, but the only major change made was to replace the seating with more modern versions. These new seats took up more space, reducing the overall capacity of the theatre, but were much more comfortable.
                                                  https://www.mirvish.com/theatres/roy...xandra-theatre

                                                  Royal Ontario Museum (ROM) - This museum of art, history, and world culture contains everything from fossils, to gems, to clothing and art. It’s one of the largest museums in North America, and is the largest in Canada. When it opened in 1914 it was closely linked to the University of Toronto. In 1968 it became fully independent, but still works closely with the University on numerous projects. The ROM is Canada’s largest field-research institution, with research and conservation projects all over the world.
                                                  Royal Ontario Museum (ROM)
                                                  https://www.rom.on.ca/en
                                                  Canada's leading museum, home to 18 million artworks, artifacts, and specimens from around the world and across the ages.


                                                  University of Toronto - Built in 1827, This university was originally named King’s College and was controlled by the Church of England. It was the first institution for higher learning in Upper Canada. It was renamed to University of Toronto in 1850 when the facility broke away from the church and became secular. The University of Toronto has earned a reputation for being one of the best educational institutions in the world, and the best in Canada. It’s most well known for it’s curricula in literary criticism and communication theory. It’s also one of the top research universities, mostly medical research, placing second in the world. It’s the birthplace of insulin and stem cell research. Fire gutted the university in 1890, destroying many books in the library. Restoration was done quickly, and the library was soon rebuilt better than before. Since being rebuilt, the Toronto University academic library became the third largest in North America, beaten only by Harvard and Yale. On the university grounds is Hart House, a massive student centre built in the Gothic-revival style, including several impressive stained glass windows. Hart House includes numerous recreational facilities and houses numerous clubs.
                                                  https://www.utoronto.ca/
