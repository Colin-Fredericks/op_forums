                    Alright, I just wanted to thank everyone who has posted here for being so civil. Truth is, on reflection, I think I've been rather unnecessarily hostile towards you all, and I want to apologize for that.

                    Really, I'm not having a problem with such disruptive players myself. And even if I do find the "HFYeahists" of Hunter somewhat annoying, that wasn't really what drove me to start this thread.

                    I've always been of the opinion that part of what makes Hunter a horror game is the emphasis of how, no matter what you achieve, you are still a small fish in a big pond. You hold a candle against the darkness, but the darkness is bigger than you are, and patient. You will go out, eventually. But, what I struggle with is understanding how to convey that in a meaningful manner.

                    How do you "get across" the theme of "you're fighting back, but that doesn't guarantee you'll win"? How do you make Hunter scary beyond the gore, the grotesqueries and the jump scares? Without doing something so blatantly unfair as "that witch you whacked was actually the harmless goth-girl daughter of a REAL Moros, who promptly uses his magic to explode your hearts in revenge", how do you showcase just how powerful the creatures of the night are, and make the point that even with the candles of the Vigil, this is still, and may always be, a World of Darkness?

                    That's ultimately what motivated me to come here and make this thread. I'm sorry for a misleading first post, and I'm sorry for all the attacks I've made on you posters during the course of this.

The whole defining difference between the two is that Humanity focuses on holding *back* the influence of the Beast, whilst Harmony is about trying to keep Flesh and Spirit in check but accepting them as both equally important aspects of the whole.

                    I'm not a huge fan of Humanity in 2e anyway - in contrast to Integrity or Harmony, it really does feel like a "punishment stat". But that's off topic.

                                Corvus Rex:
                                I see it more as a matter of scaling the threats up appropriately. Cell-tier? A coterie of vampires and the hints of covenants or bloodlines beyond are the reminder that there's more out there than you might be able to handle it. At Conspiracy-tier, you can easily handle vampires, even in small numbers... what keeps you up at night is the idea that there are *nations* of vampires out there, world-spanning conspiracies of blood-sucking monsters who manipulate the rise and fall of human civilization on a world level. A Conspiracy-tier hunter might not be afraid of a mage... but she *is* afraid of the Archmages, those inhuman gods who can erase her from history by sheer accident and not even notice.

                                Big league players face big league opponents. You can ignore those chumps in the Union worrying about having Walker-Breaker the car-pain-death Magath running around when you're focused on keeping Irinam the Colossus, mad spirit of America, from wiping the slate clean with a barrage of natural disasters or provoking World War III.

                                Hunter is a horror game first and foremost, or at least so I'm told. If being a Conspiracy hunter is when all the scary goes away, what's the point?

                                                            Originally posted by VoidDrifter View Post
                                                            That's ultimately what motivated me to come here and make this thread. I'm sorry for a misleading first post, and I'm sorry for all the attacks I've made on you posters during the course of this.
                                                            Were we attacked?

                                                            Now on a slightly more serious tone, you might want to edit your original post or at least include an edit with your real intention then. There are kind-of-lurkers like me that don't care for reading the whole of replies before posting something and basically reply to the OP.

                                                            Now, about your question... Well, i guess that boils down more to general horror GMing than HtV-specific advice. I think you might want to look for GMing advice on how to run horror games but mostly how to invoke despair.

                                                            "1.noun loss of hope; hopelessness.
                                                            2.verb (used without object) someone or something that causes hopelessness: He is the despair of his mother.
                                                            3.verb (used with object) to lose, give up, or be without hope (often followed by of): to despair of humanity.
                                                            4. Obsolete. to give up hope of."

                                                            There are many shades of hopelessness you might want to induce. You don't want to overdo it and end up with Darkness-Induced Audience Apathy, but if you are working on cell level things might get really close to that. I personally recommend TV Tropes, there are lots of examples and useful notes that might give you some inspiration.


                                                            Maybe take some notes from The Witcher games (I sure do). You usualy have choices to agonize over, but the game often deconstructs the idea of good choice or bad choice (and, perhaps sadistically, loves to subvert the "third, hidden, better, middle choice"), but more importantly, shows that while these choices influence the lives of people directly involved they don't change the bigger picture.

                                                            Case in point:
                                                            At the beggining of the first game there is a hellhound (a powerful evil spirit), well... hounding... the village, picking people off and leaving their grisly remains. You have to deal with it in your stay there (but just because that's involved with your own search anyway). People in town are medieval moroms and racist, mud-covered assholes, and they blame a witch for cursing the town. You might think "oh, it's probably not a witch witch, just a sage or something", but nope, it's a witch with dark practices a grudge on the asshole townspeople (that did bad things to her and that still come to her for help), with some poppets of the villagers and all, but that not only says she didn't summon a freaking hell-hound because that's impossible but also points that such a creature is drawn to evil, lots of evil (and later you found that's actually true). She still does black magic tho. You end up finding that lots of evil things that happened in the town and were blamed on the witch or other things were the works of pure evil human nature (a guy killed his own brother and buried his desecrated corpse on the yard to get his brother's fortune, for example). At a certain point the hellhound is still coming for the town, and the villagers -in a torch wielding mob form- cornered the witch on a cave. She begs for you to help her, and even offers herself to you to sweeten the deal (yeah, you might be that kind of asshole...). The villagers want to hang and / or burn her, but not before making her pay and torming / beating her. Then you have the choice to either
                                                            A) Give the dark-magic practioner to the asshole mob, with predictable results.
                                                            B) Protect her, with the protagonist saying this "You have two options. Wait until I leave and try to murder Abigail ... but then I'll come back. I'll slay every lice-ridden peasant, anything that moves and can't climb a tree. Or you can try to live honorable lives, clear your conscience, and start again. The choice is yours.". The mob disperses but some of the men (including the asshole mayor) do come after you and you have to kill them all for real.


                                                            "Nice story, DiBastet, but why is that example of lesser of two evils choice relevant?"
                                                            Well Jimmy, glad you asked. Because of the next part: The hellhound comes that night, as it did all nights before, and since everyone was outside, heading for their homes (either after burning the witch or because the mob dispersed), it dismembers and kills every single villager. Because of their bigotry they were stupid enough to be outside at night, thinking that killing a witch would fix their problems. They all die for that. It doesn't matter that you helped the witch or not, well, at least it only matters for the witch.

                                                            I think that's a great example. The world of witcher is a terrible, evil world with evil monsters and evil people. You might kill a monster, solve a curse, or even find out that no supernatural monster did something but a deranged guy was killing people on the streets. But in the end you can only change the small picture. The lives of people that you interacte with. While there is a point to be made that by ridding the world of the threat you saved other people in the future, the story never allows itself to focus on that; the story gives the impression that everything you do is just a small, local stone. That colors your perception of the world and induces on the player the aura of despair that is all around setting. On the other hand the setting avoids complete apathy by focusing instead on the small picture, and the good things you did. You then weights that with all that you couldn't and can't accomplish, and sees why the main character is so sour and detached.

                                                            Imagine this happened on a group's game. Sure, the villagers are all dead, ripped to pieces and scatered around for you to see, and while you did banish the supernatural hellhound, another similar evil spirit (or maybe this one comes back somehow) will surely come after the next town of evil assholes. BUT your group of hunters did something. Abigail is A) alive and saved from that cruel fate or B) dead like she deserves for being a evil witch. AND you guys might have lost some cell members ans maybe friends and contacts, but this hellhound's reign of terror is over.

                                                            ...yay?
