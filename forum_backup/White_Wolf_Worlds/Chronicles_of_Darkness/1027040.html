    Storytelling System as a Generic RPG?

    01-05-2017, 11:20 AM
    I would love to see the Storytelling system as a generic system. For several years I have been running and playing Savage Worlds and enjoyed the fact that I could use the same system for multiple genres. But there are issues with Savage Worlds as there are with any system and it doesn't work as well for every playing style or every genre. I recently decided to use Storytelling system for a straightforward Space Opera game using elements from Second Sight for Psionics and Mirrors: Infinite Macabre for Alien races. Turned out this was not so hard to do but it did take some work. It reminded me how great the Storytellig system really is and how easy it would be to adapt to lots of other styles of play and genres. I would love to encourage Onyx Path to produce a pdf geared at bringing together all the potential options for using the system in multiple rpg genres. Maybe they could even take a leaf out of the Savage world or Fate book and have a licence to use this generic version of the system for people who want to use it for their settings. It doesn't seem like it would take a great amount of work to do this by Onx path and i don't see a downside. The upside is that we who love this system could use it for more than just the NWod/CofD games.

      There are two downsides that I can see:

      1. It takes work to create and maintain a system, adding a generic* system to the mix would be a lot of work for what might be little reward.

      2. The Storytelling system is owned by White Wolf Publishing not Onyx Path. OnyxPath has the license to create CofD using it, but have to get apporvals from White Wolf. It would have to be White Wolf that decides to use the system in that fashion. Onyx Path does own the StoryPath system (see Scion and Trinity) and they could add additional games using it if they wanted.

      *Generic isn't really the correct way to look at it. The CofD core book really is about as generic as you can get (plus some horror stuff). Other 'generic' systems just have lots of different setting materials available for the different settings (usually less fleshed out and rich than you get from a more focused game line).

                                              While I will chime in with the folks who say getting an "Official" Genre-Neutral kit for CofD is unlikely, I can provide some advice on what are your best resources.

                                              As my key resources, I use Armory 2, and Mirrors. While both are 1rst edition books, Armory gives a entire section on how to alter the combat mechanics to suit a particular play style (from heroic high action, to gritty and deadly). Mirrors also offers many examples of how to adjust the baseline setting to accommodate different genres.

                                              I also use Hunter:the Vigil extensively. The "Endowment" framework of Merits (and the tools for creating your own) are some of my favorite toolkit mechanics in the system. More recently, Hurt Locker also provides several very interesting mechanical options, and provides a good look at creating "Minor" templates.

                                              Dark Eras give good examples of historical genres and has good advice for adjusting the skill selection to fit different time periods (which can be used for different genres as well).

                                              Stepping briefly outside the WoD/CofD , GURPS has probably some of the best researched sourcebooks for playing in different eras and genres. While I am not a big fan of the system itself, its resources are quite excellent.

                                              The Artificer's Workshop - A collection of Exalted Artifacts
                                              Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)
                                              The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
                                              Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
                                              Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities
