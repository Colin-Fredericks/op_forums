            Vampires out in the sunlight. Vampires with less risks to their Humanity.
            Werewolves that can wander around in War Form without a second thought.
            Werewolves and Mages that don't lose control of themselves.
            Mages who get to practice magic without any consequences.
            Prometheans who either don't want to be human or don't have to deal with the downside of being Created.
            Changelings who were never taken/kept under unpleasant circumstances. Changelings who get to just "let it all hang out" and don't have to hide.
            Hunters who aren't fighting a losing battle. Hunters who can always come out on top against the supernatural. Hunters who just wanna be friends with the creatures.
            Sin-Eaters who want to be in total sync with their Geists so there's no conflict. Sin-Eaters who don't feel compelled by their Geists, but get to do what they want.

            Of course, some of the God-Machine/X Chronicles/2nd Edition changes seem to be drifting away from that, so maybe the Chronicles are aiming for dark/urban fantasy rather than horror. To me, the line is drawn where the monster begins/ends. Dark/urban fantasy has monsters in it and the characters have to deal with that. Horror has the characters as the monsters and they have to deal with that. Dark/urban fantasy has the characters fight against the monsters and win. Horror has the characters fight the monsters and lose.

                                    Errol216 But by this definition...
                                    Originally posted by Greg Cox
                                    It sometimes seems to me that the main difference between urban fantasy and horror is simply a matter of competency. Urban fantasy characters generally take vampires and zombies in stride and react as competently as the reader would like to think they would do in similar straits.
                                    Horror characters, on the hand, tend to freak out, panic, doubt their sanity, make unwise decisions,, or even descend into gibbering madness—which is probably the more realistic approach!
                                    I know people who simply don’t get or can’t tolerate horror because they expect the protagonists to be as matter-of-fact as, say, Buffy when dealing with weird paranormal threats.
                                    THEY: “Well, why don’t they just go out and buy some silver bullets instead of freaking out over their werewolf issue?”
                                    ME: “Because horror is not about problem-solving. It’s about fear and guilt and madness and darkness, not competence. And, honestly, most normal people would not behave rationally when confronted with an actual ghost or succubi.”
                                    Yes, this is a debate I’ve had many times.
                                    None of the WorldChronicles of Darkness games are about horror by that definition. When was the last time your splat character freaked out about the monsters they face? Which is why I once again bring up the slew of Urban Fantasy shows. Yeah, they're not eliciting negative emotions primarily, but I feel that's not really an accurate summation of what Horror is. After all, an anime about high school girls has elicited fear and revulsion from me, but it was a romance drama, not "Horror". Worth noting is that most of those shows are listed as Horror on Wikipedia, except for Shadowhunters (which is what the Mortal Instruments show is called) and The Magicians, though since this is the only thing I've seen of The Magicians, I'm going to say that at least that scene is pretty horror. (Twilight didn't get thrown a bone). I disagree that Horror is about competency, though, just as I disagree that Horror is solely about negative emotions, because in that case, Drama is horror. My heart hurts more and I feel worse when watching a romance anime than when I watch Halloween 18: Michael Myers is Pretty Mean, even if one conjures images of unstoppable serial murderers lurking in the unlit hallways and the other doesn't. So clearly the trappings matter.
                                    Every urban fantasy setting I've seen has had the same kinds of horror that the CofD games do. You're rarely afraid of the Thing, you're afraid of what the Thing can do. More than that, you're afraid of what you might do in pursuit of your goals. One of the constant plots of the UK Being Human (and maybe the US one) is how the bad vampires are trying to get Mitchell to fall off the wagon. George is worried about flipping out and murdering everyone. In Buffy, yeah, it's got comedy, but she's also worried about how Slaying will effect her future, how it'll effect her family, and yeah, how it will effect her love life. Hell, every commercial I've seen for The Vampire Diaries is "oh no, they're going to find out about The Thing!" and worrying about what the fallout might be. Supernatural is all about Sam and Dean's relationship, and later Castiel, and later apparently Crowley or something but I lost track at that point. Sure, the fate of the world is at stake, but who cares about losing the world if you lose your family?
                                    All of those things? Those are the kind of horror that shows up in my games. You're a teenage Hunter. What do the teachers think when they see their bruises? What will your parents say when they see your report card (or will they even care)? What are you going to do when you get caught trespassing at 2am? Or maybe you're a Mage who's given up on having a mortal life. The Acamoth doesn't scare you because it's a horrible abomination from outside reality. It scares you because it wants to break you. Because it gives you an offer that, maybe, is just too good to refuse. As a Vampire, what scares you more: The thought of your rivals breaking your limbs and seeing if you can survive the morning on the roof, or your rivals finding out about the prostitute that you take to Denny's every Sunday, and doing the same to him? What makes that scenario less Horror when you change from a vampire to a crime boss who has a platonic affection for a rentboy? Why is it Horror if Supernatural shows a grisly death at the beginning of every episode, but not when CSI or Law & Order does it? And at this point, the only Law & Order on the air is the one about sex crimes, which most people would consider pretty horrible.
                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                    Have you been reading the same second edition I have? Because from what I've been reading, every second edition like has re-emphasized and doubled down on the horror themes of the line.
                                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                    Seconding that notion.
                                    Vampires are no longer the ones in From Dusk Til Dawn or John Carpenters: Vampires, where they explode into gouts of flame when they walk into the sun. Werewolves are actually worth a damn in combat. Paradox is more easily managed without the existence of Vulgar magic. Disquiet is no longer because you're a horrible abomination and everything about you is wrong and disgusting. There's also the fact that overall the second edition gamelines are much stronger, both mechanically and narratively. Demons can do things right out of the gate that are sequence breakingly powerful. Werewolves can cripple or outright flatten anything they come across. Beasts powers are one long list of ways to ruin someone's day. Even vampires no longer have to fear a gang of lucky thugs, and Mages, well, Mages can create earthquakes and invert gravity as starting characters.
                                    Like I said above, I don't consider strong or competent characters to be antithetical to horror, but I can see why some people feel that way. But, also like I said above, I don't see the horror of these games to be about bodily harm or really even sanity. It's the horror of what you'll have to do, and the horror of what might be done to you in an existential or social way. Honestly, in that regard, Werewolf, the game where you're the biggest badass motherfucker (to the point that its even codified as a power!) is the one with the strongest Horror elements. The horror isn't the spirits or the Hosts, the Horror is you, and the potential loss of control you have to manage to keep those spirits and hosts and other werewolves and humans and claimed and stranger things besides from hurting you and yours. Every time you go into battle, there's the chance that you might murder everyone you know and love, but that's the only way to keep them safe.

                                    Although, really, if we're going to talk about how some people want to play the games as not being Horror, we'd really have to agree on what is Horror, and I don't really think that's likely to happen. Charlaquin called Supernatural Horror, but Buffy not Horror. I'm sure for at least someone out there, the opposite is true. Neither of them seem entirely fitting with the simple definition of "designed to elicit negative emotions such as fear, shock, or revulsion".
