          People are people. But stereotypes do exist.

          You can either:
          1. Ignore the stereotype. It informs nothing about the character. Where they match or oppose it is only incidental.
          2. Play against stereotype. Pick some or all elements and do the exact opposite. Show how ignorant and/or artificial the stereotype is.
          3. Justify the stereotype. Pick some elements and figure out why they might have led to a stereotype to begin with. This can be mixed with 2 in that some things were hasty generalizations, or due to misunderstandings.
          Secondly, how prevalent is the stereotype, and how does the character react to it?
          1. Ignores it, and treats attempts to fit them into the box as stupid.
          2. Deliberately acts counter to it in defiance or to prove how wrong it is.
          3. Embraces it as a cultural touchstone, or factual statement. (Widespread reactions of this nature may be why the stereotype persists)
          4. Uses it. Pretends to elements to fool people.
          I'm probably missing some things. Big thing is: how big a role do you want the stereotype to play? If you don't care, then just eliminate or ignore it. But if it exists, figure out why and how accurate it might be.

          And always remember people will be people and deviate even from supported stereotypes (which are just commonalities, after all).

              A few things. First, remember that "native" and "tribe" are both considered racist terms when used to refer to communities in Africa. They are acceptable terms when used to refer to communities in North and South America. It's also more appropriate, regardless, to be more specific. "The Creek," etc. In Europe, it's pretty much always more appropriate to say "the Sami" than to talk about tribes.

              I'm going extremely general, but I hope this is helpful. It also all depends on what time period you're running the game in, but I've never heard an Africanist claim it's acceptable to use "native" or "tribe" in reference to communities in Africa. Keep that in mind.

              Also, never ever ever ever use ~you're cursed because ancient indigenous burial site~.

                                                          I've been thinking about this recently. I'm running Mage in the Los Angeles setting and I'd like to incorporate the Dreamspeakers legacy as part of the chronicle since they're so tied to the Astral and they represent a very different idea of magic than Diamond orthodoxy, but I'm carefully considering exactly how I want to do it. From an OOC/game design perspective, the Dreamspeakers are kinda derived from a melange of Native American and Aboriginal ideas about magic and shamanism, but they're written as having spread to other people as well, especially fringe psychotherapists and Timothy Leary-esque psychonauts who interpret the Legacy in a slightly different way. I think that origin and split is a cool idea to run with, and I want to have some Dreamspeaker NPCs who are Native and happen to live in LA, and others who aren't linked to traditional shamanism at all. What I don't want to do is depict Native characters as somehow inherently magical and/or linked to this one specific style of practicing Awakened magic. One way of addressing that might be depicting other Sleeper/Sleepwalker characters who are indigenous, and/or making it clear that the Dreamspeakers are a living, vibrant legacy with their own shit going on apart from just channeling the idyllic past somehow.

                                                          I think that's one of the big representation issues in CofD specifically because it's a setting about the supernatural hidden in the ordinary world, you don't want a situation where the only indigenous people who exist in your campaign are mysterious yet wise and helpful magical practitioners who only have agency relative to the PCs.
