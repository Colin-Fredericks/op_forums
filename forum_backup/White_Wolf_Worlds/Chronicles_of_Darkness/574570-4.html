            Originally posted by MachineIV View Post

            I'm not suggesting they become more arbitrary. What I want to see is to honour the internal logic of the setting.

            Your Seeming is chosen by the method in which you escape. That's going to depend upon a lot of factors: Form of your durance, escape opportunities, personality of keeper, and yes - your personality. I don't think it's arbitary to say that, e.g. having the Maker or Artist Kith is going to significantly increase the odds of you becoming Wizened because you quickly acquire useful skills and encounter Wizened-appropriate escape opportunities.

                                        The curse feels a bit simplistic to me. The definition of failure is a bit narrow and I feel like I'm kind of forced into playing someone who's not an absolute expert in their craft if I want it to still be relevant (and I like playing people who are good at one thing and one thing only). Imagine a kind of composer, who was a gentry's twisted vision of what a court composer should be. I'm not talking music student who took composition as a side project. I'm talking Bach levels of "this is what I do". Now it's not completely unreasonable such person would roll close to 10 dice when composing a symphony. Her chances for failure are abysmal, so the curse will almost never be a problem. But let's say she gets 1 success. The piece is competent. It doesn't have any blatant mistake, and some might say it's good. But according to her standards, it's really not. The theme might be a bit stale, this part might be a bit too long, that one poorly orchestrated, some tasteless harmonies here and there. So even if it was a mechanical success, for her it was a failure because the problem with perfection, is that the better you are at something, the bigger your expectations get.

                                        Now I've seen many artists who are perfectly fine with their mediocrity, in fact I'd say that's sadly most artists, but to me those people are not really Wizened. The "Wizened type" artists I know (and I would include myself in there if it wasn't utterly pretentious), as in people who are the thing that they do, all suffer from the "everything I do is shit" syndrome. Not that they can't be ever satisfied with what they do, but to them "success" and "failure" have different meanings. Also I talk about artists because I'm a musician and that's the field I know, but I'm pretty sure it can apply to anything a Wizened could be.

                                        What I'm saying is that I'd like it to either not be that mechanical like : "anytime your character considers the result of a roll related to her art a failure, clarity breaking point" with a loose guideline of how much successes would be considered a true success based on your dicepool. Or have some mechanic hardcode that hole "perceived failure" thing, like "anytime you roll less successes than half the amount of points you have in the skill needed to perform your art, you suffer a clarity breaking point". Something like that could also make some experts but not so clever changelings realize that they are reaching the end of their capacity (high skill low attributes means that you have high expectations for yourself but perhaps don't have the capacity to carry it out) or alternatively they could spend points of willpower anytime they practice their art which would make very very dedicated artists, or they could altogether stop because it hurts too much, and recovering the simple capacity of trying might be an arc in itself.

                                            Heh. “I'm a perfectionist! Anything less than an exceptional success is a failure in my eyes!" Mechanically, that might be a bit much: on the one hand, you only need to achieve three successes to get an exceptional success because of your Blessing; but on the other hand, how often do even three successes come up on a dice pool? Maybe the rule could be that if you don't get multiple successes on a roll pertaining to your craft, your efforts weren't good enough for you and you suffer a Breaking Point.

                                            Or how about this as a radically different approach (possibly too radical): if you invest Glamour into a roll pertaining to your craft, you set a bar of one success per point of Glamour invested. If you achieve that many successes, you get one more success per point of Glamour invested; but if you don't reach that threshold, you suffer an "inadequate performance” breaking point at a penalty equal to the amount of Glamour you invested — you still get the successes that you rolled, but they weren't good enough for your own perfectionist standards. As soon as you invest Glamour in your craft, you're committed: either you'll achieve a spectacular result or you'll crash and burn — and the more Glamour you invest, the more spectacular the result, one way or the other.

                                            If that's too radical for an innate Seeming power, maybe it could be a Seeming Contract somewhere…

                                                    I think that the Curse is fine. Two reasons:

                                                    1. Frequency - Wizened will likely be making this roll a lot. Even with a low chance of failure, you roll often enough and you're going to fail occasionally.

                                                    2. Negative modifiers - Have a crazy high dice pool, but want to make your curse relevant? Challenge yourself and accumulate negative modifiers. Work faster, with poorer tools, in adverse conditions. Parkour expert? Do it in the rain. Blindfolded. World class sculptor? Make your next sculpture out of butterfly wings. In a wind storm. Great painter? Paint a hyper-realistic portrait with no reference. In an hour. With crayons. People who are as passionate and driven as the Wizened are always pushing themselves. Sometimes to an insane degree.
