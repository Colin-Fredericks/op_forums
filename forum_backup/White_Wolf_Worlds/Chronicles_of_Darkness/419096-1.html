    So you're a werewolf, and you think you've got a handle on these Ukusgualu horrors. They're lamprey-monsters that work a lot like the other Hosts - they hollow people out and wear their skin, they fuck with the Gauntlet, and you need to make damn sure you clean up after them carefully if you want to properly kill them. At least you, as a werewolf, only have to concern yourself with eradicating them, not of being turned into a Host yourself, right? After all, it's not like you hear many stories of Azlu or Beshilu Shards chewing their way into Uratha and turning them into meat puppets, right? Right?

    It's true that the Lamprey Hosts can't infest and hybridise Uratha victims, but the Ukusgualu are parasites, and they're very good at being such.

    The Lamprey Hosts want blood. Werewolf blood is particularly rich with the power and Essence they desire. Of course, a werewolf will notice if a lamprey latches onto them and will just tear the fish off, even with the anaesthetising toxins of the Host's bite, so that's not going to work. Where the Ukusgualu have managed to warp the Gauntlet, however, an unwary werewolf may find themselves plagued by an unseen plague of lampreys all eagerly guzzling down their blood. It is possible to catch hold of such Gauntlet-leeches by digging a claw into one's own flesh where a lamprey is latched on, hooking up into the horror's many-toothed mouth and wrenching the thing into the real world - and besides, the werewolf can simply leave the area with the afflicted Gauntlet. The Lamprey Hosts are forced to detach where the Gauntlet loses its liquidity and becomes more stable.

    Smart Shards realise this, and use a particularly grisly solution. They worm their way out of the Gauntlet into the werewolf's guts, writhing and burrowing from there into the Uratha's meat. An unwary werewolf who doesn't realise what is happening will suffer the equivalent of agonising intestinal problems, passing blood shortly thereafter; but the incredible regeneration of the Uratha body makes this a survivable experience.

    A werewolf infested with Lamprey Hosts in this way will usually sicken and weaken somewhat, suffering occasional pangs of pain and bouts of nausea, Sometimes there will be undulations under their skin, the hint of shifting, slithering shapes. There are werewolves who, unaware of what has happened to them, simply live like this, the Hosts in their flesh suckling at their Essence-rich blood; because the Uratha's own Essence is drained in the process, they can end up hungrily seeking any opportunity to regain it, stealing from other werewolves' Loci or consuming human flesh. Most such victims are lone wolves, or occasionally Ghost Wolves in a small group of their fellows who don't understand what has happened, or why, and are not sure what to do about it.

    Unless the Hosts squirm back into the Gauntlet where it is liquid enough for them to do so, the afflicted werewolf is stuck with the lampreys until they either grow so fat and strong that they kill him outright, or they decide tear free and squirm off to find a proper vessel to infest and become the next stage of the life cycle. Some werewolves that realise what is writhing with them fall into such desperation or madness that they tear open their own flesh and rip the parasites out, counting on their regeneration to repair the damage. If a member of a pack becomes infested, the packmates will likely bind the victim down and perform this grisly surgery themselves. It's the hapless loners, the Ghost Wolves on the edge of society, who are thus most vulnerable to the predations of the Lamprey Hosts, and it is for this reason that those of the Ukusgualu who decide to take the risk for the promise of a constant supply of rich werewolf blood will specifically go after such victims if possible.

    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                Well, that's almost everything I can think of to post on them for now, without going into mechanics of any kind. The only other thing is to delve into their relationship with tides, sun and moon, and a bit more about what their goal is. Also possibly some tie-in with the Drowned - the Lamprey Hosts might be a branch off the same 'family' as the Drowned.

                In my Paris game, these guys are partially responsible for a catastrophe in 1969 where the Shadow went kinda crazy and all sorts of resonances and Loci ended up swirling around as if there were some sort of spiritual whirlpool in the city, leaving the packs all off-balance and, shortly thereafter, at each others' throats over territory due to the sudden change in where all the Essence was. Most Uratha have no clue the Ukusguala were involved at all. It being 1969, I think you can guess the nature of one of the other major parties involved in causing it :P

                Originally posted by nofather View Post

                Honestly though I think the most interesting part of it is what they do to the Gauntlet.
                I really prefer Hosts that do something to the Gauntlet; for me, it's the Gauntlet-mucking-about that is one of the key defining points of them, so when I use Hosts from material that hasn't really given them any Gauntlet-related stuff, I add it in.

                So for the Lampreys, having them interact with the Gauntlet in some way was key, and it had to thematically work for lampreys too. Spiders weaving webs and rats gnawing both work perfectly in terms of the themes of what they do to the Gauntlet, so I wanted something similar here. As I was already looking at having an event in my Paris setting that caused a big shift in where the Loci were in the city, then, an effect that caused stuff to move around seemed a good idea to build on.

                Plus I wanted gribbly parasite fish latching onto victims from within the Gauntlet and drinking their blood, so...
