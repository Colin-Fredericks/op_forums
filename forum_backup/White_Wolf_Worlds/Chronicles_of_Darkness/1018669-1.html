                    Originally posted by WHW View Post
                    I like the idea, but then I remember that Onyx Path is mostly made up from Freelancers that do this as a side job, and even Wizards of the Coast has problems keeping up with monthly content for their DND 5e. It sounds easy, but is actually hard to do unless you can dedicate a whole team to do this as their fulltime job.
                    That, or you will end up with a medicore material. Probably easiest thing to produce would be short adventures, and they have obvious problems of Chronicles being modular and hard to pinpoint the &quot;unified universal experience&quot; you would have to write for. Fictions is another good bet...but that's dangerously close to dreaded metaplot.

                    And remember, CofD is multiple lines published at once. You would have to either do something universal each month, do a content for EACH GAMELINE each month (imagine how many teams it would require), or make people like me not subscribe because I don't care about non-Mage stuff and I would not subscribe for Promethean or Werewolf materials.

                    EDIT
                    And as open dev showed many times, writing up inspired and balanced mechanics for CofD can be *hard*. Imagine forcing a group of people to spawn these once per month with minimal time to playtest these things. It would be...very...potentially...you know.
                    Note: All hypothetical here.

                    So long as the expectation wasn't massive, I don't think this is as concerning as you might think. When I'm writing full-time effectively, I crank out between 50-80,000 words a month. I could easily do 5,000-10,000 on a regular basis if I knew it was lined up in advance, partly because it'd be easy to backlog a little bit, and because I could just do it between other work as an exercise. For example, when I do bloodlines for the blog, those end up being about 1,000-2,000 words. If I were doing bloodlines, for example, I could easily do 2-3 of that caliber a month without much concern.

                    And if I were doing that with the expectation of, say, collecting and printing the lot of them after a year, I would use the opportunity to Open Dev and revise them with time.

                    I already release a bit here and there for the hell of it. If I had pay incentive, I'd have absolutely no trouble revising that content, and giving it a second pass to make it more workable.

                    So I don't think it's really as unrealistic as it sounds. I'm only one writer.

                            Honestly, I feel that there is a big opportunity for RPG games in the subscription based business plan. One of things that can keep RPG franchise alive is constant supply of new material; and CofD avoided a pitfall of "too much bloat will make people unwilling to start exploring the CofD, as TOO MANY BOOKS are unappealing" by making sure that each publication is relatively stand-alone and finished product that doesn't require completing your library with all the, say, "Complete XYZ" books. Open devs also showed that people are interested in periodic publications.

                            Tricky part is balancing it, writing it in a good quality, adapting to new/different pace and method of work, and somehow convincing your market that it's worth to keep paying the subscription fee; because if you lose their trust once, it's really hard to reclaim it, even if your material is superb.

                            I also feel that a lot of people think that writing periodic releases like this boils down basically to writing a forum homebrew once per month, and if they can do it in 30 mintues, then why writers shouldn't; it's not that easy. It takes logistic, backup plans, general direction and would probably need at least 2-3 months of prepwork to launch if you want to make sure the quality is top notch...and it's still a big risk. Maybe it should be a Kickstarter Project :P.
