    I've prepared nine illustrations thus far, which I thought I'd share to get the creative process rolling.
    You can view and download them from a folder in my Google Drive:

    https://drive.google.com/open?id=0B31Gud9WnwZXSzA0SV9DTTR5ZGs


    You might notice that most of these are colorized versions of the images present in the third-phase "Christmas in July" document. I felt it would be a good idea to make some artwork available to the project quickly, and this was the best means of doing so. I've restricted coloration to dragon images and I've cut out anything remotely related to humans in the artwork; my style is not conducive to the more realistic human portrayals employed by official Onyx Path documents, and rather than trying to change myself to suit that purpose I've decided to stick to my strengths. While this might seem like it would cause a disjoint in the visual aesthetic of the book (in regards to whosoever draws human images), I feel the sharp break between partial dragon and all dragon is appropriate for the themes of the template.

    I'm going to go into some of the motivation for individual illustrations. Consider skimming past it the cost of using them.

    Cruiser Dragon
    This illustration was the first crafted of a set intended to emphasize the difference in scale that various Degrees of Divinity represented. The idea of a dragon being roughly the size of a family vehicle lends itself to said dragon LIFTING a family vehicle. I started this illustration as a margin filler but did not finish before the July 25th deadline, so it never made it into the book.

    Dragon Versus Phoenix
    This image should be familiar; it is one of my micro-doodles (a style of which I am particularly fond) and was intended as an attempt to introduce that style to the book. Had it been well-received more images like this would have followed; the project didn't run long enough to find out, though. Since Raccoon is now deciding what is usable and what is not, I will be supplying more images like this in future to serve as text breaks.
    The dragon depicted is the same one present in "Wing Shroud Dragon", and the Phoenix is intended to be the same as that in "Enraged Phoenix".

    Enraged Phoenix
    This is a brand new illustration, intended to refresh my familiarity with the style of my other Embers work. I also felt it would be a good idea to depict what I was picturing as a major antagonist. In the event the Phoenixi do not make it to the final cut of the game, the illustration might still be used as an Oroboros (since they define their appearance by their own perceptions, it would be easy for one to take the form of a phoenix).
    Interestingly enough, I briefly considered making all female dragons Phoenixes, as that is the mythology attached to the Chinese Phoenix. While that would have certainly helped with depicting a clear gender for the images, I decided it wasn't a good fit for this project.

    Fido Chimera
    Another new illustration, this drawing was more off-the-cuff than Enraged Phoenix and intended to test my capacity to improvise (a good trait to have when producing gobs of artwork). As I progressed I tried to think about how something as sweet as a pet dog could be twisted into a horrible monstrosity by the weight of an Oroboros Heart. I settled on some lobster, cattle, and scorpion influences combined with a serpent. I also threw in the snake's head to emphasize that the Chimera need not preserve a standard vertebrate morphology; future images of similar beasts drawn by me will also likely feature multiple heads.
    If Chimera do not make the cut, this image can easily still be utilized either as a Nightmare or as an Oroboros who was too susceptible to outside opinion.

    Hanging Dragon
    This illustration was part of a bigger image intended to be the cover page splash for the document. I've cropped out the human slayer formerly on the left side of the page and colorized the dragon, resulting in a sort of inquisitive forest meditation scene.

    Prone Dragon
    Like Cruiser Dragon, this image was first crafted to emphasize scale difference in various Degrees of Divinity. It was not finished before the last phase of the project ended and the head needed improvement. I've since colorized it and I think it'd make a nice intimidating filler.

    Serpent Dragon
    This image was present in the Third Phase document, though it has been colorized. It was originally intended to be the dragon form of Cameron (from the inter-chapter stories).

    Slayer Orobori
    Most of this image was in the Third Phase document. It was painfully bad, though; I was trying too hard to relate draconic features to human features so that the creature would resemble its human form, and the result was... hideous. I've since corrected this error, making adjustments to the head and removing the other lingering human features. After finishing the update to this illustration I was certain: I'm going to stick to drawing monsters.

    Wing Shroud Dragon
    This illustration began life as concept art for a character I had run in an Embers campaign. He was a coal miner who found an Oroboros Heart after a shaft collapse and was forced to consume it to survive. He was a Blood Finder (in 3rd Phase context he'd be a Vizier Delver), and his most memorable moment was when he decided to cure his Black Lung (from working in the mine) by igniting the lingering particles within his bronchial tubes. He also carried his Heart around in his lunch pail, which was amusing.
    The drawing itself employed a new experimental technique I was using to illustrate; while it was okay for black-and-white it was incredibly taxing and the pay-off wasn't much better than my old style. I was unhappy with the feet, which is why he ended up being cropped in the Third Phase document.
    Raccoon re-drew this image and colorized it. This inspired me to give my drawings another look (and gave me a color scheme to apply, something I hadn't considered for the image). For that contribution I have retained Raccoon's artist mark on the image.


    I'll be preparing more images in this style at my leisure (it's a good way to decompress between other projects) and I'll notify your team when I have uploaded another collection of 8-10 images.

        Sample of Work

        What creature looms larger in humanity's imagination than the dragon? They have featured in myths from Asia to the Americas, from Indonesia to England. From the serpent in Eden to George's dragon to Nidhoggr to the world-devouring Cipactli of the Aztec, dragons are a part of every mythology. How, then, could such a creature, a being which winds its way through the human consciousness so thoroughly and completely, be nothing more than fantasy? Surely there must be some truth to the tales.

I would like to be involved in this because I would like to see it brought to the conclusion it deserves. I would like to be involved in bringing it to that conclusion and I intend, if I am accepted, to do the best I can to help it become the best and most enjoyable game it can be. I must admit that I would very much like to play this as a finished game.

                            Alrighties. I'm using these as a basis for the next paragraph.
                            For how to regain Breath: http://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.ph...ragonTheEmbers
                            For a general outline, specifically the Pneuma part (fun fact, Pneuma was named "Breath" before we changed it XD): https://docs.google.com/document/d/1...it?usp=sharing

                            New Advantage: Breath
                            Breath is more than just a vital sign of human life and a daily excersize to remain alive. To the Ourobori, Breath is what fuels the fire within the Dragon's Heart, that keeps them running faster and better than any human. It's motes of raw energy that Ourobori receive from either luxuriation in signs of their material wealth or consuming food. How much Breath an Ourobori can expend in one turn, as well as how much they can store is determined by their Heart/Furnace (whichever is the appropriate Supernatural Advantage).

                            Regaining Breath
                            -Ourobori can sleep with material objects of their wealth to regain Breath. The object must be of enough value to have a Resources cost applied to it, and the Ourobori must receive a full night's rest beside or on the object. Roll the object's Resources + the Ourobori's Furnace/Heart. Each success gives the Ourobori one point of Breath.
                            -Eating food allows an Ourobori to regain Breath, adding "fuel to the fire" so to speak. For every 500 Calories an Ourobori eats, they regain 1 point of Breath. If the food was highly flammable, such as high proof liquor, then the Ourobori instead gains 1 Breath for every 100 Calories he eats or drinks.
                            -Eating or drinking flammable but normally non-consumable liquids or solids also grants Breath at a greater rate than eating regular food, but at a price. An Ourobori regains 1 Point of Breath per quart of liquid or every Size 1 object he eats. However, the human body wasn't made to process things labelled "non-consumable". As such, for each point of Breath regained in this manner, the Ourobori suffers an equivalent penalty to all rolls made in human form.
                            -Eating a human allows an Ourobori to regain all of their Breath. However, this is considered as a grave sin against Guilt/Hubris.

                            Spending and Using Breath
                            Purview Activation: An Ourobori may need to spend Breath to activate Purviews.
                            Dream Sorcery: An Ourobori may need to spend Breath to make use of Dream Sorcery.
                            Flame and Frost Immunity: While an Ourobori has Breath, they are still more than human and not subject to the same limitations as Humans have. The Ourobori is considered immune to natural sources of heat and cold, making them unaffected by the Extreme Heat and Extreme Cold Tilts. If they have no Breath however, then they're subject to the full penalties and damages those tilts bring. Supernatural effects or attacks that deal with hot and cold and that target the Ourobori suffer a penalty equal to half the Ourobori's Furnace/Heart, rounded up.
                            Reflexive Transformation: With the expenditure of one Breath, an Ourobori may transform into his True Form as a reflexive action.

                            How does this look? I feel I should've done abit of a smaller thing.

                            And if I can say, I can't help but feel like Siren and Dragon have some similar features and yet are mostly opposites. Sirens have Flensers, who are hunters who want to eat Siren's flesh to extend their immortal lives (Eating Siren Flesh allows a person to stop aging for awhile), whereas Dragons have Slayers who hunt Dragons for their hearts. Sirens have Pneuma as a fuel stat (which was once named Breath, heck, Pneuma means "Divine Breath/Breath of Life") and Dragons have Breath. Dragons have been around since the start of existence to lead humanity like gods, whereas Sirens began existing when the Deluge, the end of existence, happened, and through the power of their progenitor have managed to connect to present day reality to try and stop the Deluge from happening. Sirens being more like Mediators between Civilization and Nature to the Dragon's Ruler. And of course, Sirens are associated with Water whereas Dragons are associated with Fire.

                            I'm not saying this because either project is doing wrong or is crossing over the other's line, I'm just noting the similarities and liking how they play similar and yet different. :3

                                                You're using the species term incorrectly.

                                                Oroboros is the singular form. Orobori is the plural. Even though Oroboros is a misspelling of the term Ouroboros it is intentional as the word is a palindrome.
                                                Oroboro <=> oroborO.
                                                "An Oroboros called all her friends together. All those Orobori decided to form a den."

                                                Some of those mechanics are dangerous waters; being able to jump to True Form (especially if it retains the potency it did in the second edition) would be extreme. Breath for transformation is more about triggering Ablutions quickly, heating up the parts of the body so that the 'human' facade can be washed away more quickly (that's why the powers are called Ablutions, its a divine cleansing of the body).
                                                How are you at writing conditions and tilts? I feel that eating or drinking something that isn't meant to be a consumable (like a box of tinder or a tank of gasoline) should inflict some lingering negative effect on the character, but damage is too extreme and a roll is too tedious. "Conditions" is a system which was not present when the Second Phase PDF launched and could not be used as a penalty, and is a perfect compromise between damage and a roll to give the gorging system some bite.

                                                If you're looking to write X/Y splats, I'd recommend starting with the X splats. It appears Raccoon is intending to keep Oroboros induction similar (eat a heart, its old owner begins to fossilize and you become a dragon), so you can look at what's already in the existing material and move from there. As for Y splats, you'd need to wait to see if the direction of the wind is shifting; Philosophies were formerly reactions to the impending extinction (Ash, Bone, Blood, Sweat, Tears) and then were replaced with unrelated calls to ruler-ship (Imperator, Paragon, Savant, Vizier, Caitiff). Until Raccoon decides on the themes he wishes to portray with Philosophies, though, it's best not to waste energy on full write-ups. You could perhaps outline pitches, though, since you're interested in writing those segments.
