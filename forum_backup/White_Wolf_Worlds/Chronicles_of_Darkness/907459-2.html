            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
            I'll grant that, technically, every house rule is about “changing something I don't like”; but there's a difference between “not liking” the fact that vampires only converted bullet damage to Bashing and not liking the fact that Werewolf makes heavy use of the Hisil and its denizens. (I choose this example because I'm a fan of hacks that allow you to downplay or even remove spirits from a Werewolf game; it's not about such a hack being inherently bad. But I do understand why most WtF fans think it's missing the point.)
            Except... not really. Its only a question on how much work you'll be putting into the game to make the hack work, and if the hack will be fun for the people playing with said hacks. There may come a point where its not worth the trade off, and may be beyond the skill of the people in question, but it is entirely a subjective matter that depends on the individuals involved.

            Even in the case of removing the Hisil, the "point" of the game is entirely subjective. The only two points that are consistant across the CofD games, as far as I can tell, are 1) provoke thought on social issues, and 2) have fun.

            True. And ironically, most “missing the point” arguments I've seen lately start out with “of course, you're free to do whstever you want; but there's why X is a bad idea/something I'd never do”.
            I don't think you should be telling someone that their ideas or bad or why you wouldn't do it, but rather just say "not for me" and avoid the thread. Its one thing to give a critical analysis and break things down on why they might go wrong; that's constructive criticism, and generally a good thing. Its another to just say you would never do something or its bad. The latter is just threadcrapping and potentially be kind of intimidating to new people.

            Yeah. Good thing I wasn't trying to do that. But thanks for clarifying that point.
            But you kinda did. The first post you mention sides. Take a look at Post number 5 as well. You are talking about "sides." Any time you reduce people to binary groups, or try to polarize things into groups, it just creates misunderstandings and mischaracterizations. It may be a trick the brain uses to process information easier, but its not entirely accurate either.

            You might not even realize you are doing it, but that's exactly what you are doing as soon as you try to put people into "sides." Life is a spectrum.

            I know of a few who would disagree with that characterization.
            In case it needs to be said, this is all my opinion. I fully expect people to disagree. Not quite sure why its noteworthy, but whatever.

            “Mages don't have enough downsides” and “the Seers aren't nuanced enough”. Gotcha.
            Not just downsides that are lacking. Its that their magic is sickenly powerful, one shot kills to the point only another mage with the proper shielding or an ephemeral with sickenly large Resistance stat can match. Its that the mages don't come off as monsters, even with the promise that they are. One of the most annoying things I feel about mages is that they're not even valid targets for Hunters to go after. Its like they're in their own little world. For those who like crossover games, mage is all but outright incompatable.

            I could go on, but I've recently come to the conclusion that 2e Mage simply has too much in it that doesn't work for what I want, and will not be investing in the game at anymore, nor posting on that forum.

            Actually, I was thinking of some posts lately along the lines of “[insert fantasy setting here] features magic users, so Mage should support that.” Dresden Files and Harry Potter are the most well known, but not the only ones.
            *shrugs* Dresden and Potter are actually highly doable. Those two are more of a question of the settings and the tiny details not matching up, rather than the magic. Its like throwing Dresden into Potterverse and suddenly expecting the detective to be able to still do things exactly the same. You can see where that would be an issue. "What do you mean children are given time magics and they're not ravening psychopaths out to detroy the world?!?!?"

            At the risk of derailing the thread, how did 1e actively discourage you from playing two members of the same Lineage in a throng?
            In general, having two or more of the same Lineage spread Disquiet faster, leading to pressure to avoid that at all cost. It felt restrictive in concept for a number of people. Mind you, it wasn't a problem most of the time, but it was one I noticed come up a few times at the start. That was years ago, though, so I imagine people simply adapted to it.

            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
            True; but “drinking tea in Gauru form” doesn't strike me as the sort of thing the Gift was designed for.
            Actually, I think that tea drinking is exactly what this is meant to do. Tea drinking, and pretty much any other kind of everyday stuff, including watching movies, give your kids back rides while holding onto their legs so they don't fall off, etc.

            What else would it be for? Certainly not combat.

                                    I will admit I have different issues with different rules. The new social maneuvering rules frustrate me the most because First impressions don't make sense. Time constraints usually have nothing at all to do with how much someone likes you unless it's some sort of court situation and you are going for long term political issues. Otherwise time constraints have usually no bearing on anything but tertiary factors. Also using all or nothing is bad because that isn't how it works at all and forces the players to actually lose rp nuance. Every other game out there recognizes that the time between rolls should take into account what you are doing. Courting? Long, trying to convince someone you have a mutually beneficial plan? hours to minutes. But by the new social rules, if the girl likes me enough I can charm her in seconds and it may take me weeks to convince my rivals to team up against an immediate threat. it just doesn't make any sense and requires hacking from the get go. Or losing all the appeal of a social system by making anything important an all or nothing rll even if it makes no sense with the characters.

                                    Mage; like others, I found the paths too generic, unlike the other core "types" it only implied to have anything to do with your character. Being an Irraka, Ventrue, Galatean, Torn, ect said more about you as a character than a Moros. Add in that in general if you rejected Atlantis there isn't much story or creative vibrancy built into the setting. Mages are just powerful and have less of an inbuilt struggle or drive. WereWolves hunt, Vamps feed, Prom create ruin and seek humanity, even Sin Eaters have at least a mechanical pull to interfere with ghosts. Add in that NPC mages are the most likely to be described as leading normalish lives and they seem at a weird angle for the rest of the setting. Mage is about Awesome people doing Awesome things. Nothing wrong with that but the setting seems to have a less dramatic pull. Add in that, like above, Hermetic magic is just so limiting and seems a needless hindrance when it would be simple to use the paths as different magical types instead.

                                    Werewolf- The new Harmony is weird for me, I love it and hate it. Storywise and how the mechanics effect things like changing/Karuth I love but the actual Breaking points don't really seem to work. Killing a human is a breaking point despite it saying in other places that Werewolves don't need to concern themselves with Human safety unless it is their pack, but killing a Werewolf isn't? Or are they saying killing a werewolf is a deliberate break from the oath of the Moon with a -2 penalty and ignoring the new change to murder? So Iron Masters and BloodTalons are doomed spiritually speaking? Killing a pack of Pure and now Huge mechanical penalties are in place and just seems to not be a natural progress of things. Also how they mechanically balance against each other. Personally, if the ST is trusted I feel it is a better game to have the ST judge what is a breaking point for each player as that seems more fair and makes sense in world and out with the story.

                                    Just really want a dramatic world but the rules need to work with that world and lend it credibility.
