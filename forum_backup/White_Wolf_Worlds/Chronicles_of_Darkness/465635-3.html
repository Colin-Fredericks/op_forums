            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
            For me, it's the "too much like The Matrix" complaint that's exasperating to hear all the time. It's an extremely common complaint, and really not at all accurate, though it can look that way on a superficial level. I imagine this is exactly how 1e Forsaken fans felt about the "spirit cops" complaints. I think Descent suffers from the same problem with presentation that Forsaken and Awakening did in their 1e core books. Too much focus on the wrong aspects of the setting.
            Honestly for me it's the overlap with the Godmachine Chronicles coming out as pretty much the new core. All of it feels so much less disposable by sheer dint of there being a new huge chunk of cosmology that is the Godmachine front and center of the new 2e. It's not as simple as "don't use the themes from demon" but also comes loaded with "don't use the themes from the new core." It's like if you told me that the setting for Exalted was optional when I complained that I didn't like it... at a certain point, why even bother playing that game when to dismantle that setting is to in some ways dismantle the game itself. Sure the mechanics for Exalted can stand on their own irrespective of setting, but that would leave a scant few pages of usable material in a book I'm paying for every page of.

            I love the godmachine as a concept, but I feel like the push for established overarching antagonists in each line, especially in the core mortals setting is just begging for this constant exodus of people turned off from those specific themes. Whereas the old blue book was incredibly open, the new lines feel much more like they are pushing a thematic agenda on those who pick them up... which if you aren't a fan of those particular themes is disappointing and off-putting.

            And I'm certainly aware that the old 1e books had their themes and antagonists, but it felt far more like being presented with a sandbox and tools, rather than an established and sprawling prebuilt sandcastle that we have to take the effort to knock down and smooth over if we want to build something notably different.

            Them's my two cents anyway.

                        Actually, yes. I never once thought that until someone else said it. Even now, I don't really think it.


                        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                        I can understand how the concept would be appealing to some folks. I don't know how to put it into words, but I can understand how it makes sense for a modern society religious setting. However, I personally don't enjoy it all that much. I wasn't denouncing it from the mountain peaks, if that's what you think. I was just stating I prefer the older version.
                        Here's the thing. Many cultures actually have mystical and spiritual connections that interact with technology. In Western culture, there's the idea that spirituality/religion/mysticism and science (and thus technology) are mutually opposed.

                        I think that mind set is what is really setting off some people. The idea that Science and Magic must be mutually opposed. Divine magic must be different from Arcane, which is different from Science! Its an old idea, and one highly rooted in the minds of many. Rejection is one of the four instinctive psychological reactions to something so radically different than we're used to. This dissonance between old ideas and new is not easy to deal with.


                        I do like DtD as it is right now. However, I am going to say that equating God and divine miracles to advanced technology is a rather radical idea for many people. Many people are unfamiliar with gnostic beliefs, which is everywhere in the nWoD, but is blindingly obvious in Demon. There's a lot of strikingly different things than we're used to, and that can be hard to enjoy for many. There's a reason we say that reality is stranger than fiction - because fiction, if it violates what people expect too much, is not enjoyable for many people.

                                    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                    I'm well aware of the popular saying "Magic is just science we don't understand yet." and I always found that a bit arrogant of a statement. Personally, I say that magic is accomplishing scientific results in non-scientific methods. Which coincides with the idea of arcane / divine magic being different from technology and science.
                                    Both words are only as meaningful as a setting makes them. The quote describes that conceptually, the two different approaches to these things aren't actually that different, and that what appears to be magic to one individual (say, someone from the Middle Ages watching you talk to someone in China on your cellphone) is actually technology (dude, it's just your smartphone.)

                                    Then, we extrapolate that to fancier stuff, and that's where you get the popular idea of that quote.

                                    But the most important thing to understand is that we're not talking about the real world, we're talking about fiction. Science and Magic mean to an individual setting only what they're stated to mean. This is why a mind control ray or a weather machine is considered science in in one setting, while those mystical communication tablets that can let you see someone's face on another continent might be considered magic in another setting. (hint: it's a phone.)

                                    We can try to put our own weird and entirely random definitions of both onto a setting, but that's not really making any attempt to understand how it operates, and it's gonna leave you with some really strange generalizations.

                                            We're used to living in an age where most cutting-edge scientific research is done in areas that seem totally inconsequential to us, because we've explained a large part of physics as it directly concerns us (and what is magic but trying to explain new laws of physics). Current physics research is hard for us to understand because we already KNOW what electricity is and how it can make someone's face appear.

                                            But now imagine that you could make someone's face appear in mid-air by waving your hands. How is that REALLY different from making it appear on a screen by pressing a button? Assuming that magic is but a set of laws that we haven't encountered yet, it isn't any different. It's not even necessarily a more ADVANCED set of physical laws. I can easily imagine a world in which the laws of electricity have not yet been discovered, and yet the laws of 'magic' (counter-factual or fictional laws of physics) are well-understood by even high-schoolers. "Duh, of course he's just using his mind to manipulate the water vapour in the air to create a prism that will refract the light such that an image appears". "What do you mean lightning can be told to create an image on a solid screen! That's just ridiculous! You must be either a magician [in the sense of someone doing physics they don't understand] or you're a fraud and you're using your mind to do it and making it LOOK like it's on the screen!"
