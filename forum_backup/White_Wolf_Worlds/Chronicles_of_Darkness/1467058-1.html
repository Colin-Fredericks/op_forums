        Alright, time for our first excerpt and commentary!

        The Star-Crossed are one of two new bloodlines in the book, with the power to put humans in bizarre biological time loops and then bleed them for a golden sap that can produce a whole cavalcade of strange, time-defying effects, As noted above, they're probably my favourite of the pair, though only just. I'm a big fan of bloodlines that are, to put it crudely, evil bastards. When you look past the self-pity and aesthetics, the Star-Crossed are monstrous, but in a way that I think can be compelling for players and Storytellers alike. Not everyone will want to go there, but I like my bloodlines morally thorny, and the Custodians are covered in vines.

        Conceptually, the Star-Crossed didn't change much through drafts, though we tinkered a lot with their mechanics. They always had their Purities, but creating them originally involved a Devotion rather than an inherent power. In development, it occurred to me that we didn't really need the roadblock of going out and buying a power, so their Purification power became something they can just do. That's a frequent theme in my theory of game design: let players do the thing.

        Now, those of you who've read Night Horrors: Spilled Blood are probably wondering what the connection to the Everlasting is. Originally? None. The author hadn't read them! At first, I worried the concept was too close to the Everlasting, but the more I thought about it, the more I liked the coincidence, and we decided it was more interesting to lean into it than to abandon it. That's how we developed the idea of them having a weird substance to harvest. The first draft of the Star-Crossed was a fun idea, but it lacked a bit of player utility, so I wanted to give them something they could justify their existence to the rest of the All Night Society. Electrum and Ichor work very differently, but there's enough overlap to make you wonder. We also included a rumor to suggest some possible conflicts and/or connections, and I tweaked their Discipline spread to match the four the Everlasting have access to (they originally had Vigor, but, apart from symmetry with the Everlasting, Resilience felt more appropriate anyway).

        One last detail that's just kind of funny: the "Once upon a midnight darkly..." entry for the bloodline was originally the Erzsébet's, and the Erzsébet's was originally the Star-Crossed's. However, the Custodian's mirror bane and general vibe were just too perfect not to reference Dorian Grey, and the Cavaliers already have Elizabeth Bathory as a possible founder, so I felt it was better not to overload their origins with references. So, the Star-Crossed author and I traded, and I think both bloodlines are the better for it.

        Anyhow, here's the Star-Crossed. Tomorrow, I'll be back with the other new bloodline, the Moda Mortale...

        Star-Crossed: The ones who covet you

        "Time is a river; I am the dam."

        Photos were insufficient to capture his beauty, or the grace with which he moved upon the dance floor. Yet the years peeled him back in layers, reducing him to a rotten husk. Eventually, the neighbors complained about the smell, and then the law came knocking. I fought like a demon to prevent them from taking away what was left of my love, but they buried him where I could never find him.

        In retrospect, I understand why my sire chose me, even if she came to regret it; I was halfway Star-Crossed already. Crossed in a few ways, really.

        Even in the Requiem, all I really wanted was him. I took to the streets looking for someone I could mold into his image, but the nights slid by in a rabid need that no amount of blood could slake. If I couldn’t have him, I would sooner tear out my own eyes than look upon another.

        So I did. Now I hunt by touch and sound alone, but I’ve managed to find three facsimiles. It takes some getting used to, all their weeping, but I just turn up the gramophone and wait for them — some night — to take up my offer to dance.

        Ah, to love again.

        Eternity is empty without devotion. And yet, most Daeva wile away their nights avoiding it, all for fear of getting caught on mortal lives the same way they slip hooks through their victims. Not all Serpents are willing to give up on happily ever after, but it’s the ever part that’s elusive. Their blood aches for humanity, yet humanity is fragile. The flesh wanes; the mind fades. Time is the enemy of perfection, and for every vampire who thinks she’s found true, eternal love, another is laying his soulmate to rest for the third time this century. The Embrace is no solution. Death sours relationships and adds another predator to the pack. Ghouls? Slavery isn’t love, and addiction is just another layer of removal.

        But the Star-Crossed aren’t content to give up on human affection; it’s the one thing in the Requiem they find worthy of possession or preservation. To keep their own despair at bay, the Custodians entrap kine who embody qualities they most admire or lust for, imprisoning them in bizarre time loops so they can bask in a captive’s unchanging state. Night after night they maintain this stasis, spending precious Vitae to ensure their “Purities” remain uncorrupted. Kept pristine long enough, these time-trapped bodies transmute their blood into a strange substance the Star-Crossed calls Electrum, after the amber tears of the Heliades, who eternally mourned the death of their brother Phaëthon, child of the Sun. Some (most) Custodians tap their Purities — oh so carefully — for this golden sap, bestowing its gifts on any who would consume it. Many Kindred would pay a pretty price for a vial.

        However, as much as they would wish it otherwise, nothing lasts forever. Sooner or later, a Custodian can’t keep up the maintenance, or her Purity escapes, or she befouls him with her Vitae. Then, all that lost time catches up with a vengeance, reducing the poor mortal to a wasted corpse.

        This loss pushes the Star-Crossed to self-destruction. The loathing that consumes them is as low as their euphoric obsession was high. Therefore, they find outlets for these emotional storms. Like Pygmalion, they craft their ideal in marble, or paint canvas after canvas to capture each coveted feature. Others hunt for a replacement among humanity’s ranks, and while they might never find a true doppelgänger, if the eyes are right on one, and the mouth on another… the parts might all add up to a perfect whole. For the Star-Crossed, solace can only be found in the spaces between life and death, where corruption halts and the night stands still.

        Why you want to be us

        You need not taint those you would pluck from Death’s greedy hands: We can show you how to make them into undying angels. Forsake the fruitless lust of other Serpents; it leads to nothing but despair. Instead, we’ll cup your hands around the most precious embers, fan them to blazing brightness, and then you — and you alone — will have been witness to a moment of true perfection.

        Why you should fear us

        Why do you keep trying to leave? There’s nowhere on earth where our love won’t lead us back to you. We would never stoop to polluting you as thralls, or pass on our Damnation. In our grasp, you’ll always be pristine, incorruptible against the ravages of time. You cannot wish to rot! What, you want to return to your “family?” No, we shall not let them tarnish your perfection. Don’t weep in your gilded prison, precious one. Freedom is a price worth paying for eternity.

        Why we should fear ourselves

        Whatever moron said, “If you love someone, let them go,” didn’t have to face fucking forever alone. We cling to our Purities or else our hearts become little more than deadweight. Without someone to safeguard and hold dear, we’re forced to confront the wretched things we’ve become in the name of love, losing ourselves in the purposeless depths.

        Parent Clan: Daeva

        Nicknames: Custodians, Sophists (disrespectful)

        Bloodline Bane (The Curse of Self-Reflection): A Star-Crossed obsesses outwardly to avoid the awful thing time warps her soul into. The closer she is to the Beast, the more visual representations of a Custodian resemble a monstrous, distorted parody of her form. If a mortal sees a facsimile of the vampire (in a mirror, a photo, a video, a drawing, etc.), [REDACTED].

        Disciplines: Celerity, Dominate, Majesty, Resilience

        Bloodline Origins (Excerpt)

        Once upon a midnight darkly, a beautiful young lord wished to age no more. Instead, his prized portrait would accept the burdens of flesh from his charmed shoulders, reflecting all his sins as he took his fill of life’s pleasures. The man ravaged the world, and in turn the world ravaged his painting. After many years and many depravities, smiling faces wronged in the lord’s past returned to haunt him, though whether they were truth or delusion is unknown. Nonetheless, he gave commands, and all smiles stopped together. He would hold against his past and future to protect his obsessions, even if it meant defying Time itself. So, the lord resolved to destroy the only true evidence of his crimes. He wallows in its remains still, a husk among the fragments of a ruined masterpiece — deliriously ever after.

                            Alright, let's do our second new bloodline!

                            Like the Star-Crossed, the Moda Mortale ("the deadly fashion") were conceptually pretty solid upon first draft. They're Daeva who play at being fashion icons (models, designers, etc.), but who secretly spread violent messages via the Cacophony, using their "official" jobs as cover. That mostly was there in the first draft, though originally they had an element of erasing their victims from public consciousness, which was really cool, but didn't quite fit narratively with them making statements through their killing. They also didn't have Protean, but I thought that would be an interesting twist on their being assassins and fashion icons. They can use their shapeshifting in non-obvious ways, mutating their bodies into weird, transhumanist forms for their runway walks, but also grow massive claws when they gut you over drinks later. They have what I think is one of the most interesting Discipline spreads in the book; I don't think there are any bloodlines with Obfuscate, Protean, and Majesty.

                            This is another bloodline I really like not only for really embracing its high concept, but for being pretty damn antagonistic. They're definitely playable, but I think a lot of fun comes from using them as rather frightening antagonists: bizarre, mutative assassins who can make you love them in one moment, and disappear from sight the next. I also really like the little mystery of their origins that the author seeded through the piece, especially what's really going on with their purported founder. I suspect Ginevra will return in future books...

                            Also, on a personal note, I'm just really pleased with how the fake newspaper scrap in their intro ended up looking. Layout can be kind of a thankless task, and that was fun to put together. On a side note, the except below will seem to cut off; it's intentional, but makes more sense with the proper formatting in the book.

                            Anyway, here's the Moda Mortale, in all their glory. Tomorrow we'll be looking at one of the updated bloodlines: The Children of Judas. And oh boy do I have a lot to say about them.

                            Moda Mortale: The ones who cut you out

                            "Watch yourself, honey: I'm dressed to kill."

                            Mob lawyer hung from office window

                            On Monday morning, a grisly scene greeted Seventh Avenue at Main Street, where defense attorney Blake Chilton’s remains were found on display at his former law firm. The cryptic phrase “BANG BANG” was emblazoned in seven-foot-high letters on the building’s exterior windows on the 27th floor, in what the authorities have confirmed was Mr. Chilton’s own blood. Police would not comment further on the message or its significance, and no firearm use was in evidence. Witnesses placed Mr. Chilton the night before at La Verità Italian Cuisine in the fashionable East End District around 7 p.m. He was last seen dining with an unidentified woman in her early- to mid-20s; she is considered a person of interest.

                            Over the past year, Mr. Chilton made a name for himself after a legal career of relative obscurity, representing high-profile clients with ties to organized crime, notably at the controversial trial and subsequent acquittal of Sigismund “Ziggy” Carmichael, an alleged capo of Arturo Sangiovanni.

                            This marks the third assassination of a high-profile criminal attorney in as many months, and Metro PD is calling these homicides “professional.” The perpetrator or perpetrators’ methods share little in common, aside from their theatricality, with a daring, grotesque display at each crime scene. Police are

                            In the vicious world of Kindred politics, sometimes you want a rival to disappear quietly — and sometimes, you want to send a message. For the latter, you need a killer with a keen sense of style. Of panache. In other words, you need the Moda Mortale.

                            The Fashionistas are where style meets substance in the All Night Society. From poisoning to strangulation, sniper’s scope to pistol whip, they’ve mastered the assassin’s handbook, and they’re as chic as they are deadly. You’ll see them coming because they want you to, in stiletto heels crafted from actual stilettos and Protean-perfect feather coifs. The couture angle isn’t just aesthetics, though. They really are models and designers, and they’re on the bleeding edge of fashion in a way few among the dead could ever hope to achieve. Some are even renowned beyond the Danse Macabre, voguing across the edge of the Masquerade.

                            Ironically, most Kindred don’t realize they’re anything but a bunch of fashionable Daeva, or that they’re a bloodline at all. For the Moda Mortale, covert means overt, using their “day jobs” as cover to travel between high-end events that just happen to coincide with a nasty murder or two. Kindred or kine, sire or childe, it’s all the same to these Widows, just so long as the check clears. But you’re not just paying to solve a problem: You’re paying for publicity. More than mere murderers, the Moda Mortale operate the Cacophony like a sewing machine, stitching in bloody messages the All Night Society can’t rip out.

                            An alderman dies in bed with his mistress, rose petals scattered around the bed; the Carthians cancel their plans for the dockyard. A research scientist takes a bullet through the head, but a knife defiles his corpse with the words Taking Note; the Kogaion flees town. A prominent occultist is crucified on the eve of the summer solstice, on the highest skyscraper in the city; the Mother’s Army declares war.

                            Are there easier ways to send a message? Sure, if you’ve got no taste. But if you had a simple message, you wouldn’t come to the Moda Mortale. Each death is a two-pronged solution, one that destroys whoever happens to be standing in the way of a client’s scheme and fosters an environment to further that plan. All without a paper trail — not the bad kind, at any rate. The Widows ensure you always make the headlines. Sometimes that means a little character assassination before the knives come out, destroying the victim’s social life in preparation for the “moral” they’ll be sending the Kindred. For the Widows, this is the ultimate edit. And fashion is all about editing.

                            The bloodline’s services are available only to the true elites of the All Night Society, yet to these upper echelons, the Moda Mortale are a complicated problem. Killing is just as much a crime among the dead as the living, though one might ask when moral and legal quandaries have ever really troubled the Damned. More troubling are rumors of Widows devouring their own when they fail their high-stakes missions, but surely a bloodline of assassins built around ritual diablerie couldn’t have been so… successful? Surely.

                            Why you want to be us

                            When you send someone across the final threshold, the least you can do is dress for the occasion, and we’ll reshape you into a couture killing machine. Joining us is getting on the Kindred A List, and isn’t it better to be part of the club than at the mercy of the hunt?

                            Why you should fear us

                            There’s death and then there’s death. The former is casting off your daylight life like last season’s furs; the latter is getting on the wrong side of our blades. Don’t worry, doll, we’ll make sure your end is unforgettable. They just won’t be talking about you.

                            Why we should fear ourselves

                            Being perfect takes constant rigor and endless competition. Look over your shoulder because the next bitch is coming up the ranks; how long before she makes you obsolete? Letting yourself be outshone, outmatched, outdated — that’s career suicide. And if you don’t work for us, you get the bad edit. So take a long, hard look in the mirror. Are you as good as you were yesterday? That’s not good enough. You’ve got to be fucking better. No sympathy for even the smallest glimpse of weakness in our ranks. One slip, and you’ll plummet into last season. Forever.

                            Parent Clan: Daeva

                            Nicknames: Fashionistas, Widows (among those in the know)

                            Bloodline Bane (The Impeccable Curse): The Widows are obsessed with perfection. While this makes them fastidious killers and fashion icons, it also means that if something disrupts their work, their manicured Requiems may crack. When a Fashionista suffers a setback or finds a flaw in her current plans (such as fulfilling an Aspiration), her player must roll Humanity. On a failure, [REDACTED].

                            Disciplines: Majesty, Obfuscate, Protean, Vigor

                            New Merit: Medium Is Message (• or ••)

                            Prerequisites: Moda Mortale Status •

                            Effect: When your character kills, the precise meaning and intent of the death will be clear to all Kindred, even those without any Cacophony Savvy. At one dot, roll Wits + Crafts/Expression to create a perfect crime scene, using whichever Skill would apply best to the message. At two dots, she can choose to make her message sensible only to intended recipients. Any attempt to decipher the meaning only succeeds with an exceptional success, and only after suffering a penalty equal to your character’s Crafts or Expression dots, whichever applied to the crime scene.

                                Welcome back to another episode of Yossarian rambles about Draculas!

                                Yesterday I promised I'd talk about the Children of Judas, who I think might be my favourite bloodline in the book. That was an unexpected outcome, as they were easily the bloodline I've liked the least of anything we've updated.

                                So, a little preamble: The original Suicide Kings entry took that name very literally. They made people commit suicide, and the book included a detailed list of the kinds of suicide one could inflict on people.

                                Suffice it to say, it was rough. There's a lot I could say about the old version, but I prefer to be positive in these posts, and I think whatever criticisms I have of it are pretty self-evident. You can have a look for yourselves, but be warned, it's not pretty. It's trying to do something it's wholly not equipped to do, and the results are very frustrating.

                                All that said, it wasn't unsalvageable. There were some interesting concepts, and the lore had a lot of cool plot seeds I thought we could take in fun directions. I hemmed and hawed a lot about including this bloodline at all, but I ultimately convinced myself when I decided the original write-up was a hit job. It's what the rest of the All Night Society says about the bloodline: that they can make you kill yourself! That they're obsessed with suicide!

                                Starting with that basic concept, that the CoJ suffer from bad press, the author decided the Hanged are actually addicted to grief and regret, not suicide. And who goes around with tons of that sort of baggage? The Kindred. Instead of "forcing people to commit suicide", it's a (rare) side effect of their desire to punish the wicked and feed on their lust for grief. Does this make them good guys? Big nope. They don't care if what they do is righteous; just that they get their awful fix. This kept the thorny morality I like so much (as noted above), but turned it into something that didn't feel icky. We also wanted to keep the idea that the bloodline sometimes arises spontaneously from suicide victims, which does hold to some older myths, and helps explain why they've survived so long. It also gave the Dolorae sisters (their purported founders) more to do, and you can see that thread move through each of the rumors.

                                Aside from reframing the bloodline's goals and methods, we also made some pretty big changes to their Disciplines and bane. Despond definitely wasn't going to make it into our update as a Discipline, but Nightmare slotted in very nicely. We also felt that Auspex still fit, due to their desire to seek out sins...but that left us with a quandary. The original CoJ had Auspex, Despond, Majesty, and Resilience -- three "special" Disciplines and only one of the Daeva clan Disciplines. I've mentioned before that my preference is to not have bloodlines with more than two special powers, and to almost never lose their signature power...but, here, I was persuaded otherwise. The author made a good argument that this bloodline is the "through a mirror darkly" of the Daeva. They still make you spill your guts, but not because you love them.

                                As for the bane, it actually isn't too different from the original, at least thematically, but it manifests (as Daeva banes often do) in feeding. In first edition, the CoJ lost Willpower for not "exploring" a victim's pain; now they need to feed on it. This isn't helped by the fact that the bloodline's "gift" of terrible visions only manifests when they drink from people they've fed from only once. They are still Serpents, after all. They're just addicted to a different aspect of you.

                                While these new Children of Judas are a lot less focused on suicide than the originals, that theme is still there. Because of that, it's not a bloodline you should bring into your game lightly. We talk about safety measures in Chapter Three of the book, but I think it bears repeating: if this bloodline's presence would upset players, please don't use them. I think there are interesting stories to be told with them, but not everyone will be in for them.

                                Anyway, here's the Children of Judas. Tomorrow, I'll be back with the Nelapsi.

                                Children of Judas: The ones who eat your grief.

                                "Think about what you've done."

                                First thing’s first: We didn’t name ourselves. Who the fuck would want to be called the Children of Judas? That name’s nothing but baggage, and we’ve got more than enough of that already. Unfortunately, we also have all the internal organization of an orgy for the blind, so we’re stuck with it.

                                Second, we didn’t ask to be this way. Ours isn’t the sort of illustrious lineage you join in search of wealth and prestige. Every single one of us is a victim, of one kind or another, and that’s before the rest of the dead-man-walking crowd starts taking swings. Probably why “Hanged” is our preferred sobriquet. We’re cheery like that.

                                For a bunch of killjoy grief-addicts that nobody wants at their party, we’ve been around awhile. You can find records of us in all sorts of domains going back centuries. They’re usually records of execution, but hey. It’s good to have a history. Of course, that isn’t the same as knowing where we come from, but we’ll get to that. Where we’ve been isn’t as important as what we do.

                                We taste things in the blood. Tragedy, sin, remorse, whatever you want to call it. If you’ve been naughty and we take a nip, we get a peek at your shame. Yes, it happens every time; no, we can’t stop it. Wish it weren’t so. Worse, we need those terrible, no good memories — need them almost as much as we need the Blood.

                                Picture a junkie. The most strung-out, hollow-eyed meth addict who ever pissed on a bus stop at 4AM on December 31st. That’s us, except instead of black tar and bricks, we’re craving your worst betrayals and most painful, secret failures, dredging them up from the bleakest annals of your mind in living color. Sure, any Serpent can make you spill your guts with a pout and a wiggle, but we make you feel bad about it. We can’t make you feel squat about us, but we can make you feel really, really terrible about yourself. Easy to see why we’re so popular.

                                Last thing: That oh-so-jolly nickname the rest of the no-pulse crowd slapped on us? Really overblown. It’s not as if we go around handing out cyanide and razor blades like Halloween candy. But… we’re addicts. Desperate ones. And you know who’s got just oodles of tasty atrocities squirreled away? Vampires, especially old ones. Apparently, when faced with several hundred years’ worth of remorse all at once, of suddenly caring about all the lives they’ve casually razed, some of our “donors” off themselves rather than experience one more moment of their rotten Requiems. That sort of thing tends to cause a stir, especially when it’s a prince or some other highly-visible asshole who decides to give themself a tan. Doesn’t matter it’s only happened, like, ten times in all of recorded history. A couple dozen at most. Accidentally cause a few horrid old leeches to dust themselves, and all of a sudden you’re the Suicide Kings.

                                Fucking great.

                                Why you want to be us

                                It’s a nasty burden to yearn for people’s worst crimes and regrets, and no one’s going to thank you for bringing that shit up. Still, there’s a poetry in what we do. In what we are. We’re consequences for those who face none, a conscience for monsters who’ve discarded their own. It’s almost worth being treated like a cross between a leper and the worst kind of snitch. Almost.

                                Why you should fear us

                                We know what you did. We know where all the bodies are buried, and we can count the exact number of ribs on each skeleton stuffed in your closet. Your worst choices are our best meals, but we need you to feel the guilt again to fill our bellies. It’s okay if you don’t remember how. We shall remind you.

                                Why we should fear ourselves

                                We show people the worst of themselves and lap up their tears like bloody gumdrops. It earns us exactly zero friends, a truly staggering amount of hate, and we can’t. Fucking. Stop. The only way to end our addiction to misery is to end ourselves, and if that isn’t some razor-sharp irony, nothing is.

                                Parent Clan: Daeva

                                Nicknames: The Hanged (among themselves), Suicide Kings (among others)

                                Bloodline Bane (The Grievous Curse): The Children of Judas are addicted to the blood of people exper*iencing grief, remorse, or regret. This goes beyond the drive to feed that all Kindred experience, as even Hanged full of Vitae still feel the need to taste misery on their fangs. If a Suicide King goes more than (Humanity) nights without consuming Vitae from one or more characters experiencing sorrowful emotions [REDACTED].

                                Disciplines: Auspex, Celerity, Nightmare, Vigor

                                Bloodline Origins (Excerpt)

                                Once upon a midnight darkly, there was a maiden locked in a tower surrounded by a deep, dark wood. Many a young man rode forth to rescue her, yet none survived, for one by one, the forest claimed them all. The maiden lamented their terrible fates, but eventually she could bear no more. She hung herself with her golden hair — grown long and thick in captivity — so that no more fools would throw away their precious lives. Soon after, however, a gallant knight finally conquered the perils of the wood, arriving at the tower only to find the maiden pale with death. Overcome, he wept for her, and when his tears fell upon her cold flesh, she awoke with a terrible thirst. She drank from the knight, first his tears, then his blood, then his life. Now she hides from the world in dark caves and dungeon keeps, or even atop the tallest mountain peaks, but no matter how treacherous the path, a brave champion will always reach her haven… and then she’ll drink him too — miserably ever after.

                                Bloodline Gift: A Taste of Grief

                                The Hanged would argue over their innate ability being called a “gift,” but they can’t deny it feeds their sorrowful addiction.

                                When a Child of Judas feeds from a vessel for the first time, she receives a brief vision of a tragic event from that person’s past. The vampire has no control over what she sees, but it’s almost always something the subject experienced a breaking point over; particularly young or innocent victims might, therefore, provide no vision at all.

                                The information provided by this gift may or may not be useful on its own, as these visions are rarely clear, but the insight can often guide the use of a Suicide King’s Despond Devotions.

                                This ability cannot be suppressed.

                                    Welcome back to our...several...part series. Today, let's turn to the Nelapsi.

                                    Unlike the Children of Judas, I've always been fond of the Nelapsi, Daeva who need to devour vast quantities of blood and use their Nightmare powers to haunt their domains, collecting their tithes while the mortals are too busy worrying about a ghost that doesn't exist.

                                    This was a cool concept, but the original bloodline write-up doesn't do a lot to explore it. Much of the emphasis is placed on the bloodline's weakness, which is certainly important, but the haunting thing was a much more interesting hook. So, we refined that a bit. Our Nelapsi don't just spook their domains: they craft legends for themselves to serve as a mask over their feedings, both from mortals and from other Kindred. No one really believes the old mansion just out of town is still inhabited, but the locals just can't help go check it out every now and then. Everyone's entitled to one good scare. And no one really thinks the devil will show up at the crossroads if you speak his name, but...you still go and try it out, don't you? No harm in that.

                                    To this end, the bloodline's "gift" is actually a collection of Merits and a Devotion. Rather than an inherent trait or a special Discipline, the Nelapsi cultivate their legends through mentorships with their elders. It keeps the bloodline's population down, assuring only the worthy gain the bloodline's blessing. This tied in to some of the first edition material, but makes it a bit more immediate to players.

                                    The other big mechanical changes we made were their bane and Disciplines. We tend not to change around Discipline spreads for bloodlines that didn't have a unique one in 1E (with a couple of exceptions), but the author made a pretty compelling argument that Resilience fit a lot better than Celerity. The "two hearts" myth from the original bloodline was something we wanted to do more with, especially considering its built right into their logo, so we felt that was a good representation of this legend. Plus it gave us an opportunity to come up with a nifty little Merit to convert their old Devotion. I did consider giving the Obfuscate when I was doing development, but I didn't want them too Nosferatu. They're Daeva: creating a cool legend is the point, not the gaslighting.

                                    As for the bane, we loved the idea of them needing vast herds to feed an endless bloodlust, but we also really didn't like the original's implementation. Depriving players of resources always feels like a sub-optimal design choice, so, instead, the Nelapsi now crave more blood. The Beast gets a little taste, and then it comes roaring back if it doesn't get enough.

                                    Without further ado, enjoy the Nelapsi! Tomorrow, we'll check in with the Duchagne.

                                    Nelapsi: The ones who feast on you

                                    "Don't test me. I haven't eaten."

                                    You’ve heard about him since the night he moved into that ominous townhouse at the end of the street. People say strange sounds and lights come through the windows in the middle of the night. The old folks cross themselves as they pass like it's haunted, and the jackoff teenage boys try to spook their girlfriends with tales of children going missing.

                                    He takes your hand, bending down to give it a little kiss. It’s a bit dorky, but the charm radiates like a sunny day, and those ivory teeth shine with every inviting smile. They’re having a party later tonight, he says. Come see what really goes down.

                                    How scary could it be?

                                    The Daeva know humans better than any Kindred, and the Nelapsi know the most primal human want of all: hunger. A Glutton can wake starving even if she entered daysleep with a bellyful of blood. Feeding grounds and herds are a painful point of necessity for these Serpents, and the source of many a neonate’s downfall when she devours them all in one gory gulp. Even when Vitae is free and plentiful, feeding is a careful balance between the safety of moderation and the false promises of satisfaction.

                                    The Nelapsi didn’t always believe such control was necessary. Once, they fed in whatever quantity they required, lording over private domains with only their childer for company. The Gluttons of old ruled not as royalty, but ideas, medieval legends that infested every mortal unfortunate enough to hear their tales. These were not the Daeva of dreams and fantasy, but greedy ghosts lairing in haunted castle keeps demanding tribute, or cunning demons awaiting supplicants in the low places of the earth. Each folkloric mask was a way to hide in plain sight at the top of the food chain. Even their name is just another guise, taken from a Slovakian beast that devoured livestock and killed with a glare — though, given their appetites, perhaps they gifted their name upon the monster.

                                    Cults to these nightmares once riddled Eastern Europe, from Bohemia to the Black Sea, and the bloodline held dozens of hamlets as their personal buffets. Few approached; none dared leave. And every now and then, an offering would gain favor as a Glutton’s clan curse gripped its soul, and its new childe would become yet another part of its mythos.

                                    Eventually, other Kindred took up arms against these Locusts, fearful for their own herds. It was a costly war. The bloodline’s terrible fortitude wasn’t easily broken, and the conflict provided even more grist to weave into their tales. Some Kindred say each Nelapsi bears two souls, their appetites only sated by feeding both. Others believe they devour mortals whole and wield the strength of two men, with two hearts pumping in their chests. Still others whisper that their curse is so all-consuming that it requires a pair of mortals to die for one Embrace.

                                    Yet these stories weren’t enough to save them. In the end, the Nelapsi were scattered, manses burned to ash as their human playthings were dragged away and made to forget. With the fall of the old guard came the diaspora, and a new ethos took hold to prevent future purges. The new blood’s philosophy is restrained compared to that of their forebears, though its adherents prefer to call it elegant.

                                    Modern Nelapsi distance themselves from their herds but still forge legends. They’re the friend-of-a-friend-of-an-acquaintance who can talk their way out of any situation, and they’ll do the same for you if you’ll meet them under the new moon. He’s the party animal who knows every regular but never shares his real name; if you learn it, you can summon him for one hell of a night. She’s Mephisto at the crossroads, offering wishes in exchange for a liter of your finest vintage.

                                    Rather than estates and stables of mortal morsels, tonight a Nelapsi lets his myth do the heavy lifting. His herds are vast because the story he’s crafted for himself is more than the sum of its parts, and it keeps the All Night Society from ever realizing the truth of the Locust flitting through its fields. A Glutton’s reputation will always precede him — for good and for ill.

                                    Why you want to be us

                                    Even when we try to be quiet, we wind up being bigger than life. Helps that we’re already dead. We can devour the world entire if it suits us; the Danse Macabre is just a frenzied summer ball before the winter chill sets in. Join us, little grasshoppers, for our larders are full and our herds are fat. We’ll make sure the dancing never stops — greedily ever after.

                                    Why you should fear us

                                    We are the virus of language, and to know our tales is to risk infection. You say our names in the mirror thrice upon a blood moon because you want the monsters to be real. You want them to spin hay to gold and chew on your firstborn’s bones in recompense, ridding you of the burden. One good story is worth a thousand cattle, and we’ll dine out on ours as long as you keep telling them.

                                    Why we should fear ourselves

                                    There is a hole. Something filled it once, but nothing remains now. We can never know what we’ve lost, because in each of us it’s a different hollow. The Beast, in its cunning, calls us to feed and fill the void. Fill it with stories. Fill it with power. Fill it with blood. But the hole was born in life, and it can never be filled in death.

                                    Parent Clan: Daeva

                                    Nicknames: Gluttons, Locusts

                                    Bloodline Bane (The Edacious Curse): While every vampire needs to feed, the Locusts have a hard time stopping once they start. After a feeding scene, roll a Nelapsi’s Humanity. On a failure, the vampire is [REDACTED].

                                    Disciplines: Majesty, Nightmare, Resilience, Vigor


                                    Bloodline Gift: The Lessons

                                    The Nelapsi pass down their ancient traditions through a rigid mentor–apprentice hierarchy. Each neonate suffers through a long period of discipleship under a predek (“ancestor”), usually one’s own sire or a Glutton of great status. The fledgling must learn three canonical lessons from the predek before she can be considered her own master.

                                    Each lesson is embodied mechanically by the Merits and Devotion below.
                                    • The First Lesson: Survival, represented by the Quickened Hunger and Shadow Heart Merits. This is the most basic and practical lesson, with elders teaching their childer to use their prey efficiently in the face of their curse and to escape the wrath of fearful Kindred.
                                    • The Second Lesson: Renown, represented by the Fame, Advanced Merit. This lesson isn’t so much taught as discovered, as the predek allows his student to forge her personal legend — though how far she needs to spread this myth is up to the mentor.
                                    • The Third Lesson: Domain, represented by the Cursed Fiefdom Devotion. This lesson varies the most between adherents of the new ways and the old. Modernist Gluttons believe land is only as necessary as it is useful, and they teach their protégés to incorporate territory into their mythoi on a case-by-case basis without growing attached to it. Conversely, predkove from the old country teach that domain is the pinnacle of a Nelapsi’s legend and Requiem, and that to have land is to attain true power.

                                    Nelapsi characters aren’t required to learn these traits in order (or at all, in theory), and some predkove teach the first two lessons more organically. However, they always pass on the Lesson of Domain last.

                                                        Alright, time to dive into the Duchagne.

                                                        As noted in my Erzsébet preview, this was my other bloodline, and similar to the Cavaliers, they went through a lot of rewrites. Partly, this stemmed from ambivalence about the original version. Daeva who can evoke extreme sensations in their victims is a strong concept, but I didn't love the execution, especially their Licencieux Discipline. I also thought that, while their French Revolution backstory was interesting, it felt a little arbitrary: the bloodline's concept feels fundamentally Daeva, but it's couched in a very specific time and place.

                                                        The biggest difficulty was figuring out what their power should look like. I briefly considered giving them a unique Discipline, one that works similar to Celerity, Praestantia, Resilience, Vigor, or Vitiate, but I couldn't come up with a good passive power that wasn't just Vitiate with a different flavor (though, if you did want to translate Licencieux using that power's effects but different narrative descriptions, you could do a lot worse). Ultimately, I felt giving them all three Physical powers and then building Devotions around those worked best. The powers they ended up with are reminiscent of the Khaibit and Liderc, with a baseline that's enhanced by other effects.

                                                        The other big mechanical change was their bane. I basically just threw the original out altogether, though I tried to keep with the idea that they get bored easily. Just in a very different way...

                                                        As for the backstory, I ended up keeping the French connection, but suggested that Duchagne-like vampires have appeared throughout history, and the current brood is only the latest iteration. What that means for your chronicle is up to you, but we hint at a few suggestions on how to use that plot hook.

                                                        Anyway, here's the Duchagne. That brings us to the end of our bloodlines; I have some thoughts I'd like to share on the Erzsebet and Xiao that I'll post tomorrow, but otherwise, I think I'll talk about the Blowouts chapter next, probably on Monday. I'm wrong, we've still got the Gulikan! We'll do them tomorrow.

                                                        Duchagne: The ones who inflame you

                                                        "I know what you need, but you haven't earned it."

                                                        Physical pleasure is too often dismissed as indolence. Mammals evolved to feel good — it keeps the young occupied and the mature focused on fucking. By the same token, pain also has its uses: Don’t touch the hot stove; don’t step on the broken glass; don’t let the dead thing bite you. Pain is a medium, and handled with care it offers its own delights. Yet the Kindred think they’ve grown beyond these drives. What earthly delight response matches a Kiss on the throat or Vitae on the tongue? Human sensation is a shadow of what a vampire can do just by bleeding.

                                                        To this, the Duchagne say, give us five minutes.

                                                        Call a Duchagne a hedonist and she’ll ask why you aren’t one too. The Embrace brings a silver platter of corruptions, but the Kindred act as if they’re on a diet, distracting themselves from the raw joys of death with intrigues and schemes. Not that there’s anything wrong with a good scheme, but vampires who make them the focus of the Requiem are missing the point of being dead. The Decadents know tending the body is the only worthwhile occupation, and with les arts licencieux, they take reign over sense memory, introducing beautiful pain and terrible pleasure to the human body in pursuit of a far more refined meal than blood alone can offer.

                                                        But this isn’t just for sadism the sake of sadism. A Decadent can squeeze more Vitae from her victims by flooding them with the very essence of life. Of course, most Kindred hear about these practices and go straight to the trite — fuzzy handcuffs and spanky sex and bodice rippers — but the Duchagne didn’t get where they are tonight by settling for clichés.

                                                        Take a trip with the tour guide. He brings his customers to all the places they won’t tell you about, the secret underbellies where real history festers like a wound. You can almost feel the bullet hit your chest where the mafia don got whacked, and your breath grows short where the lovely heiress dropped herself in the river.

                                                        Meet the insult comic. He defines your flaws in needle-sharp patter, and the resultant laughter tears through you like a chemical burn. Your flesh is rawer than a sunburn, but the balm is so soothing as he rips everyone else to pieces too.

                                                        Chat with the phone sex gal. She doesn’t look a day past 60 at 120, but that voice still flows like caramel. Tastes like it too. She made a fortune helping lonely hearts remember the good old days, but these nights she just does it for fun.

                                                        The modern Decadents first emerged in the French Revolution, but they had predecessors, cults to hedonism the All Night Society still recalls with shame and envy. All bloodlines claim contradictory origins, but the Duchagne can confirm a few of them. Elders with the same blood as the French clutch say they were Yangists in the Warring States, spreading the ethics of egoism among the All Night Society, and a handful remember lustful nights as Pan worshippers, ministering erotic floggings on behalf of pre-Acolyte cults. Records and common legends of the bloodline’s powers confirm these claims, and at least a dozen origin myths follow them from continent to continent. It’s as if the bloodline were a natural gravity the Daeva fall toward every few centuries — one that burns bright, then out. Only a handful of these elders still exist, and all claim their branch collapsed in a spectacular manner, whether from infighting or Kindred intervention. Perhaps this is the ultimate expression of their egoism: If you’ve got to go, better take everyone with you.

                                                        Why you want to be us

                                                        With us, you can to turn their nerves into a Swarovski crystal of sensation, salting the meat in ways no other Lick would dare to dream. Fair warning: You probably won’t make it. You’re not good enough yet, and maybe you’ll never be. But you don’t get anywhere without trying, do you?

                                                        Why you should fear us

                                                        Ever had an itch you can’t scratch? Let’s be more specific: Did you ever have an itch you had to wrench your shoulder to reach? One that wouldn’t go away with a metric ton of ointment or powder or heroin. One you couldn’t dent no matter how deep you dug through the skin. Yeah. That sure would suck.

                                                        Why we should fear ourselves

                                                        There’s a point where the human body hardens to pleasure. You only have so many ways to get people off, and joy is so individual. But pain? Pain is facile, and if you let it be the answer to every question, doling it out will be the only way you ever feel again.

                                                        Parent Clan: Daeva

                                                        Nicknames: Decadents, Bacchantes

                                                        Bloodline Bane (The Sybaritic Curse): It’s all so tiresome. The Kiss, the Blood, the Beast — all of it. To feel alive, Duchagne need something bracing to wake their dead nerves, or otherwise the Beast will find ways to gratify itself. Unless a Decadent experiences intense physical sensation as she feeds (pain, pleasure, extremes of temperature, etc.), she [REDACTED].

                                                        Disciplines: Celerity, Majesty, Resilience, Vigor

                                                        Bloodline Origins
                                                        • In the time of the French Revolution, a noble of the Invictus burned for her crimes. If any Lick deserved the flame, it was the Viscountess du Chagne. Embraced in a time when feudalism was radical, she spent her Requiem spreading misery for her own indulgence, torturing or exalting victims depending on her whim. Her boudoir was a place of beautiful agonies, and even her allies in the First Estate thought she got what was coming to her. The Carthians called her not just a counter*revolutionary, but an abomination, sentencing her to Hell by setting the flames on the Devil’s behalf. Yet even the inferno couldn’t match her lust. Some say she rose from the pyre and demanded the coals be made hotter, while others swear claim an unwitting double took her place. Whatever the truth, her brush with oblivion crystalized her philosophy, and she began a spree of Embraces across France. Most Decadents tonight trace their blood and name to the Viscountess, venerating her in the hope she’ll never turn her debauched eye on them.
                                                        • Before Viscountess du Chagne ever pissed off the Revolution, other Decadents ravaged their way through history. Their earliest antecedents were Cyrenaics and Epicureans in Ancient Greece, forming mystery cults to Dionysus, Pan, Zeus, and other oversexed gods. They were called Maenads in those nights, and their few survivors claim they fell in one bloody frenzy. So the story goes, they were the ones who devoured Orpheus, and drunk on the blood of the perfect muse, they ripped themselves asunder for the sheer pleasure of the act. A few of these Kindred still exist, along with Serpents from other parts of the world with similar tales of decadence and destruction. Wherever the Bacchantes rise, a fall is sure to follow.
                                                        • The Duchagne aren’t a bloodline at all, just a perversion that draws Clan Daeva ever closer to oblivion. Are they a disease? A curse? Evolution? Whatever else they are, they’re right about being the ultimate expression of their clan, but only in the sense that they’re a logical extreme. Every time they reappear, they return more virulent, and this French brood is especially resistant to destruction. For now. Some night, they might just gobble up the entire Serpent clan, and if they do, their inevitable destruction will spread through the All Night Society like a forest fire, entire domains succumbing to paroxysms of orgy and death. All Kindred will feel their destruction, and they won’t be able to get enough of it.
