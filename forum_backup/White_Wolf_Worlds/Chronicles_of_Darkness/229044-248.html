    Originally posted by Joker View Post
    Many things in the NWoD aren't like anything else, that's one of the biggest selling points. It's a huge world filled to the brim with weird and impossible horrors.
    Chosing to define the GM as the "outer border" of weirdness in the NWoD makes the whole setting suffer. Fun as it is, the GM should not be the apex of unique weirdness the setting has to offer.

    Similarly, many things in the setting are huge, very confusing and not understood. This is not a special, unique quality of the GM and it shouldn't be, for the sake of the larger setting. I am fully okay with making this specialness apply within a Demon only game, but once you include the larger NWoD the setting's larger concerns should take precedence.


    Nearly every single splat out there can do this, and Spoofing has always been more than enough to deal with this. Beasts really don't require special treatment in this regard. Just have the rules state how often Demons have to 're-spoof' to be protected from a Beast's super-sense.
    Didn't say nothing else was unique or special. Look later in the Beast book part of the explanation with Demons was mentioned as the GM being alien. This is not to say nothing else is alien more that the GM is something that is alien from the perspective of Beasts. Everything else has survival, life, and the details that come from actually being a living thing. (For the value of saying living when dealing with vampires and the like.) The GM lacks those desires to survive or any recognizable similarities to a living thing.

    I'm also saying that GM is rather central to Demon and defining it in more broad strokes is probably something best avoided lest you cause problems by conflicting with the primary antagonist needed for the entire Demon splat. It isn't a case of this being the only thing allowed to be too big and complex to understand. It is case of the GM and its creations being a blindspot in the sense of familiarity they have with everything else.

    To my knowledge the ability of other splats to recognize other splats tend to be abilities one would have to have and usually have cost associations.( Auspex, or some spell from a mage.) Unless you're just seeing the supernatural stuff that crops up like magic, disquiet, chrinos, etc. It's kind of just a passive power for Beasts.

    To put it in a simpler analogy just assume Demons lack a presence in the Primordial Dream. It makes sense, they weren't designed by humans, when they ran properly they were just programs that might have had to interact with people and so used a really complex people disguise, they are literally a quantum matrix/computer. Wouldn't be too hard to assume that the Unchained lack a presence in the Dream. After that it comes off rather similar to an encounter between a psyker and a blank in a Caiphus Caine story I once read.*

    *The psyker started screaming bloody murder about how the blank was nothing and shouldn't exist.Psykers are literally insane though, so no real complications arose from it after the psyker's freak out led to her passing out.

                    I'm looking forward to playing a sadistic monster myself. My bone with Devouring is that it's a bizarre and senseless choice, given what we know. It's not that dreams lack wisdom or fear isn't wise, but nightmares are irrational and fear is learned. Anyone who thinks that inflicting anxieties and worse on people who offend some dream-monster's sense of propriety is worth giving over their own soul as victuals is already a monster, choice or not. And this makes it harder to relate, for me, to a Devoured. Homecoming is bittersweet, because you have an understanding of who you are, and you have people who understand you now, but through no fault of your own you are hunted and despised. A Devoured basically buys a white elephant of Hunger and isolation, and then is tenuously bound not to be too much of a monster.

                    And while I guess that reads as agency, I still don't see how the Horror closes the deal. They either find people with dubious moral compasses and give them their sick hearts' desires, or they somehow convince Joe Dreamer that being a sadistic monster is actually a calling on par with pedagogy and mentoring. And while that's interesting, those motives are in line with these sorts of characters, while the choice (as described so far) filters out the people who should find conflict with their own nature and the depredations of their kindred. I'm not crying paradox, but there seems a circle needs squaring here.

                                                        Originally posted by Diggs View Post
                                                        I've been wondering why Demons seem to be forced into an antagonist role since the beginning.
                                                        They're not. Its just thematically inappropriate for the Unchained to be associated with the Dark Mother. Demon is predicated on the assumption that the God Machine is their Creator; you can't make the D.M. their mother without making the DM give birth to the GM, and that's a very large statement to make. They're also called "unchained" because they don't have any chains - any natural bonds - to reality or people, making it a stretch to assume they'd be connected to Beasts by default.

                                                        Meanwhile, it also provides a very nice "WTF, there are supernaturals who AREN'T tied to the Dark Mother?" moment for the Beasts, and eventually a plot hook, not antagonists. Demons provide the exception that leads to greater exploration instead of merely accepting the myths and stories. Its something different that breaks their happy little boxes that they put everyone in.

                                                        In short, its really just unthematic for Demons to be considered Kin, and they make good plot hooks as the exception. There's no reason to assume that they're the only exception out there, but Beast, as a game, is focusing on the PC splats to cross over with, and most of those other exceptions aren't part of that focus. Its also thought provoking - all this argument about how they should not be the only exception?

                                                        That's what the Beasts should be arguing about in character! "Holy shit, guys! The Dark Mother didn't mother everything! What else is out there? And what does that make us?" Ah, drama and plot!
