    Combat Math

    02-29-2024, 06:22 PM
    I am currently running my first game using the Chronicles of Darkness engine, although I have a lot of history with other versions of the Storyteller system, and I have to say, the combat math seems... off.

    Defense just seems too good.

    A character's athletics score seems to have a bigger impact on a fight than direct combat skills, and most people are rolling to attack with only a chance dice.

    At one point, we had an ordinary gym rat with 3 dexterity and 3 athletics fighting a werewolf. The mortal just kept dodging, and the werewolf simply could not hit him, as it only had a 9 dice pool to attack compared to his 12 dice pool to dodge. It just seemed silly.

    Is there some rule I am missing? Am I doing something wrong?





    Also, as a tangent, do Changelings have some innate resistance to damage in The Lost? One of my players is a longtime Dreaming player, and is used to being able to soak lethal damage when calling upon the weird. When I explained that Chronicles of Darkness doesn't have soak rolls, he was really pissed off as he felt that it was unfair that Changelings were no better than mortal when it comes to survival.

      The game doesn't want you to just sit around trading blows. It's very much meant for combat dice-pools to be low and easily put to a chance die, if you just sit there swing at each other.

      Some of this is a reaction to the first editions of the game, where Defense was nearly pointless and combat was really just "whoever goes first goes all offense and 99% of the time one-shots their opponent." With the relatively low amounts of Health compared to damage output in the CofD 2e rules, one or two hits can easily end a lot of fights, so low dice pools are still very dangerous. It's just much less certain.

      As to your example, someone with a 3 in both an Attribute and a Skill is generally not just an "ordinary" person. That's a decently experienced professional. In general the assumption is that 4 dice is more of what is an average non-professional or entry level professional would have. So that's already skewing things a bit (and 4 Defense isn't going to be winning against a werewolf with 9 attack dice). If someone is just Dodging all the time, you're also free to use All-out-Attack for+2 dice. So the werewolf vs. ordinary gym rat (before we get into why this fight even justifies the full combat rules and isn't just Down & Dirty combat where you can't use Dodge), should really be 11 dice vs. 8 dice (or a bit higher if both start tossing around Willpower as well). And we haven't even brought in any of the werewolf specific mechanics that can push things farther in the werewolf's favor. So the werewolf is going to win this the vast majority of the time.

      As for the Changeling? It seems a weird complaint. "Calling on the wyrd" means using supernatural powers. Lost have Kith Blessings (ex: the Stoneflesh Kith from the Kith and Kin supplement) and Contracts (ex: Red Revenge from Contracts of the Sword) that can penalize attacks or grant Armor in order to be hardier than normal people. Changelings were never the tanks of either the WoD or the CofD. If you want to make a Lost that can endure a lot of punishments, you just pick the right things; just like Dreaming.

          Dodging means not having an instant action that turn. That character can't deal damage back to the werewolf. When put against Defense directly, the werewolf should hit 66% of the time. WP brings it up to 88%.
          Your problem is likely the action economy. You can't Dodge your way to a victory unless you also have allies that can deal damage on behalf of the gym rat, and outnumbering a tough opponent can actually turn a battle into a cakewalk (barring some unlucky rolls).

          Changelings don't have anything innate, but there's a whole category of protective Contracts to use. (Though the two Contracts that grant actual Armor ratings are both Sword instead of Shield).

          As for the player being pissed off; the problem is his expectations. The best way to handle that might be to straight up tell him that Dreaming and Lost are simply two different games that happen to share title due to their shared influence in folklore. This is unlike Requiem that basically was a remake/reimagining of Masquerade.

                      The Chronicles of Darkness core book has a pretty helpful baseline for what skills and attribute dots represent:

                      1- Below average / hobbyists or someone who took an introductory class.
                      2- Average human / a trained professional.
                      3- Above average / a reference in their field.
                      4- Genius level / mover and shaker in their field.
                      5- Peak human ability / legendary level of skill.

                      So this gym rat has the conditioning to be a professional athlete or a stunt double. Lets assume circumstances didn't allow the werewolf to get the extra Strength die from Dalu form. Using an all out attack to grapple means you are able to almost match them, or exceed their pool with willpower expenditure. All the werewolf needs is for a grapple to succeed and they seize the win.

                      They cannot dodge forever and the lowered defense tempts them to retaliate, which can create the opening for the grapple. Will they try to escape or call for help instead or does the werewolf have a plan for that ?

                                                          It depends, if the person has things like 5 dots of Professional Training with Brawl, they get 9-again on related rolls and rote quality by spending Willpower. Now combine that with the Clinch Strike merit, which lets you inflict damage on a successful grapple, if you are lucky to get an exceptional success and choose the damage maneuver, it means you could inflict twice your successes in damage. Body as Weapon adds another point of damage. Five dots of Martial Arts makes their hand do 2 more damage and can now selectively inflict lethal. All with a weapon that can't be disarmed, is great at disabling single targets and can be quite silent. Despite its short range, it also does not reduce initiative, making it quite fast.

                                                          With firearms, use of the Covering Fire maneuver guarantees hits if the target doesn't go prone or running to cover, which is great for setting up traps and flanking.

                                                          Characters can be built to have more options via merits and Fighting Styles. They will be more precise, dangerous and use less Willpower. But just like investigating or being a diplomat, it requires sinking XP to specialize.

                                                          I would recommend the Chronicles of Darkness core book and Hurt Locker, both are great resources for mortal chronicles and flesh out the fighting system.
