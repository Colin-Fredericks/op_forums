    Originally posted by Flower,Innocent View Post
    I think I'd prefer a middle ground, really. A toolbox, but with a history, and maybe a step back from cataclysms and other big, sweeping changes that aren't somewhat comparable to realworld changes.
    This is what the CofD games are - when we say "toolbox" we mean "you design what your city looks like in this setting unless you decide to use one of our examples" and not "the history of this faction is entirely undefined".

    Like, Mage has six Orders who all have detailed histories and are filled with members of the five Paths. Legacies have backstories; when they were invented, how they spread.

    The composition of [i]your[i] Consilium is up to you. That's the "toolbox".

            Originally posted by Flower,Innocent View Post
            I'm aware that each faction has histories (well, I've only read requiem. I've heard a lot of humourous things about forksaken out of context) but I still think I need more.
            There's more. Trust me, there's more. It's not just background explaining where the different factions come from, there is an extensive and detailed history of all the supernatural creatures, their origins, the rises and falls of entire supernatural cultures and lineages, significant historical events, all intertwined with real life history. It's not a metaplot because there are no canon events or significant setting changes that occur after year 0, but that doesn't mean there isn't a story. I like to think of it like the Souls games - there is a huge amount of background and lore, but it's unobtrusive enough that you can control how much or how little that lore actually affects your play experience simply by chosing to engage or not engage with it.

            Frankly, the perception of Chronicles as this anemic setting with sparse history and no real depth - the same perception that leads to comments like Shane's little gem from this interview - comes from thinking that the Requiem 1e core book is indicative of the entirety of Chronicles of Darkness, and fails to recognize that the game has been around for well over a decade, building its universe that whole time.

                        Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                        Hm. I kind of personally prefer the SCP-style &quot;there is no canon&quot; approach, and there's a significant amount in the books that supports that. Ie, every one of them except Beast is written to be crossover-optional. I loved how Demon had a sidebar explaining how to run the game under the assumption that Demons only recently became free. I get that there's a &quot;default&quot;, but my favorite tack is always the multiversal &quot;this could be true&quot; approach. It's one of my favorite things about ChroD, and I like that it went to the opposite of metaplot.
                        Oh, absolutely! One of the strong points of Chronicles is its modularity. If you want to say, "Changelings don't exist in this chronicle" or, "in my game the Camarilla never fell" or, "in this story the Fall of Atlantis and the Sundering actually were the same event" or what ever, you easily can. But that's also just part of the medium. You can ignore the metaplot in WoD too, or play D&D in the Forgotten Realms but say Elminser and Drizt don't exist in your forgotten realms. Calling the canon of an RPG setting modular is about like calling a rule optional - it's somewhat redundant, as all RPG rules are optional, calling one out as such specifically mostly just serves to telegraph the fact that you won't break anything if you chose to ignore this particular rule. You won't break anything if you choose to ignore ChroD backstory or lore, and ChroD often helps facilitate modularity of canon by presenting "what if" scenarios and things like the Chronicler's Guides. Which is a great thing, playing to the medium's strengths. It's also part of the reason it doesn't work as well for transmedia as WoD does; ChroD really takes advantage of being an RPG and is really well-tailored to tabletop. WoD is a little more media-neutral, as the story could just as easily be told as a TV series, or a novel series, or a video game series, or all of the above.

                                            Sure, but your examples all have consistent settings shared between the individual shows/movies/games/whatever. Which you could do with Chronicles of Darkness, but not without sacrificing the setting modularity we were discussing. You can establish that Luke Cage is part of the same universe as the big green guy and the guy with the silver hammer through common setting elements, but if you say that in this show, Thor was born a human and gets his power from the hammer, but in that movie, he was born in Asgard and gets his power from being a literal God, saying they're the same universe doesn't really track.
