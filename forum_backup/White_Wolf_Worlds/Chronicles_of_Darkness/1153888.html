    Obession: Am I viewing it wrong?

    10-29-2017, 05:06 AM
    I will state this outright, I hate the Obsession condition. I hate how it can make an exceptional success even harder to get on a limited dice pool by denying 10 again, and outright deny the chance at one if you have a dice pool of four with no willpower you can spare. I don't care if it grants 9-again on actions towards fulfilling that obsession, because if you're stuck in a situation where you can't do it, you're screwed.

    A bit of context: I was in a Vampire: The Requiem game on when my ST decided fairly early on, based on a random die roll at the start of the session, that my character got a story so terrible he got an Obsession condition over destroying this author (my character was an editor trying to become an author). This was problematic at the start because the ST gave us a rather unforgiving time scale to do things during a winter night, and because by default I'm not a person who roleplays unpleasant people or do unpleasant things (not conductive for Chronicles of Darkness games, I know, I simply find no catharsis in it). It became far worse when we were getting inducted into the Invictus and had training out the wazoo, and thus had even less of our precious time to spend on trivialities like destroying an amatuer author...except for when we had challenges we needed to complete for fear of DEATH, meaning every penalty stung, and the ST was not giving many if any favors.

    Eventually this (among some other points from the aforementioned time-intensive training) led to me becoming extremely frustrated, with the ST noticing and deciding that it was best that I left the group, which was something I was already leaning towards doing given how my aforementioned aversion to doing horrible things was kind of dragging the group down. It was my first time playing Vampire, and so I don't hold any harsh feelings over the game.

    All this leads to a question though, which the title encapsulates: am I being overly harsh on Obsession? On the one hand, the ST introduced it randomly and didn't provide many if any real opportunities to fulfill it, and given he had played in another game with me he should have had an inkling I'd struggle with a cruelty-based task. It gives some...unpleasant ideas on his intentions, particularly since I was guarenteed no chance of an exceptional success on Dominate. On the other hand, another player pointed out that losing 10-again is a very minor thing all things considered, and as I had Professional Training I at least had a few dice pools with 10-again still.

    Is this a knee-jerk reaction from a bad experience, or is Obsession a condition that should be resolved as soon as possible to avoid circumstances like what I had above?

          Originally posted by Taidragon View Post
          Is this a knee-jerk reaction from a bad experience, or is Obsession a condition that should be resolved as soon as possible to avoid circumstances like what I had above?
          I'm going to vote knee-jerk reaction from a bad experience brought on by a ST using the system in an unfun way (regardless of whether or not you'd be happy with a CofD based game in general).

          As milo v3 noted, slapping you with an Obsession on a random roll like that is pretty much against the spirit of the rules here. While there's certainly lots of room in the game for rolls to lead to Conditions, Conditions should always stem from something your character is actively involved in. There should be a narrative reason (randomness involved or not) for a Condition to come into play. Persistent Conditions especially shouldn't come in without a significant factor happening, precisely because they'll be around for awhile. The ST should pretty much never apply a Persistent Condition on a PC without some input from the player on what the player feels would be an appropriate emotional reaction.

          That said, I think you're soured on the Obsession Condition out of proportion to its mechanics due to the circumstances the ST put you in. Getting 5+ successes on 4 dice is pretty rare (0.78% chance of happening with 10-again). You don't really miss out on much there. If you're looking for exceptional successes at low dice pools, you want something like the "exceptional success at 3+ successes" (ex: Inspired Condition), or automatically bumping any success to an exceptional success (ex: Informed Condition).

          Putting Inspired on a 4 dice roll with 10-again has a 13.81% chance of exceptional, and 8.37% without 10-again.

          Putting Informed on a 4 dice roll doesn't really care what kind of X-again you have, it's 75.99% odds of exceptional.

          As well, there's always situational modifiers beyond Willpower for bonus dice.

          It also makes me wonder if there's a bit of a character build problem to this as well. 4 dice on a Discipline roll is really low considering Disciplines generally use Attribute + Skill + Discipline as the base dice pool instead of Attribute + Skill like normal rolls. Especially for the concept as described, I'm kinda surprised you don't have at least Intelligence 3, Expression 2, and Dominate 1 for 6 dice as a starting level character not trying to trick out for Dominate. Intelligence + Expression is generally what you'd roll for long form writing for an author... so having those total at 3 to start doesn't really sound like a professional editor that's trying to break into writing, it sounds like a fan-fic author that's thinking of submitting their first story (which is going to get shot down) to a publication.

                      Thank you for replying so swiftly and in-depth, everyone. I've not been in many Chronicles of Darkness games, but that method of applying a condition seemed unusual. It's a bit soothing to learn that I wasn't just imagining that.

                      The condition itself was gained when my character received a story to edit, as he was a freelance editor, and was "Make Billy Wood suffer". The other players were all too happy to give plenty of ghoulish suggestions on...except as noted, time was a pretty limited factor (in spite of it being a winter night), and we kept getting stuff we pretty much absolutely had to attend. This wasn't helped by only one of us having a car, so public transit that late would have made things even trickier if I wanted to split off. I was unaware that therapy could actually remove the condition as well, though as noted the time limit we had probably wouldn't have allowed for it. We didn't really have elders to fall back on in the game, since the predatory aspect was pumped up considerably and we weren't even part of the Invictus when we started, so I think we supposed to be making those trusted links.

                      As for character build, I was mi-remembering since I hadn't looked at the character sheet and Dominate in a good while. My character had 3 Intelligence and 2 Expression, so overall his Dominate had 6 dice for Mesmerize. In hindsight, I think we were subtracting the opponents' Resolve from my roll, usually 2, which isn't how that is supposed to work, hence why I usually had 4 dice to work with.

                      The reason my Expression was so relatively low was because I wanted my character to be one who wanted to be a writer, but was only good at the technical aspects, not the creative ones. He even had a long term aspiration "Create a best seller story without plagiarizing", and had a specialty in Fiction, and it was covered by his Professional Training. However, that aspect never really grew as it was more of a mobster sort of story.

                      Since it seems like I'm bashing the ST a lot, I will say that they did make an effort to give my character things to do, like looking up his keystone (a male college crush) and having neighbors who wanted to become writers but needed an editor. The game started gradually, so we would get a foundation on where we were and what was happening, and they even allowed another player to remake their character when they wanted to do something different with her. It's just, well, the Invictus came first unless we wanted to go through a lot of hoops to not die, and one person breaking off caused more problems. Everyone else was having a fun time with it, though, so it was probably either a bad match with me and that ST, or Vampire: The Requiem is not really my game. Probably a mix of the two.
