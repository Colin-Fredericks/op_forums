    ​How Seasons communicate with the Lost?

    11-01-2014, 12:15 PM
    Question is in the topic. How Season is selecting it’s Monarch to represent in freehold? How changelings can “talk” with it’s Court Season... being? Are Seasons Hedge bound True Fae or very powerful Hobgoblins?

    I’m asking as I will run episodic game located in fictions small town in New England and I don’t much see there many changelings beside players. To be frank, I can’t imagine other Lost than player characters in the town of almost 2500 people. So I need a way to make PCs members of Courts without need of other changelings - because I love Seasonal Courts! Simplest solution it would be that Seasons themselves choose them. It could be Seasons agents to come to characters, if themselves don't see changelings from time to time - but who would work them? Specific Hobgoblins?

    I’m using here a bit the similar fan idea as with Firstborns of Forsaken that come from time to time and guide new werewolves in to they Tribes, even without other Uratha to teach them in the region.

      There are a couple of options to choose from.

      1: The seasons as entities have no direct hand in the process. Members of the court elect their King, or the previous season's monarch chooses who to hand the crown off to, or whoever can take the crown first keeps it.

      2: The season automatically chooses the monarch, but the people involved must consent. Once the crown is granted, it must be given up by an act of will, whereupon it disappears and immediately appears upon the head of the next monarch, who may deny it if he wishes.

      3: The season chooses, no matter what. The crown passes from person to person without any Changeling input, chosen by some arcane method the Changelings only vaguely understand. On the appropriate day, the Crown simply disappears from the previous monarch's head and appears on the head of the new monarch. A monarch must make an act of will or magic in order to try to claim an eternal monarchy like in Miami.

      4: The Seasons possess avatars, either voices or physical forms which appear in order to enact their will. Summer's avatar, for example, is The Burning Man, a figure all of white-hot flame, while Winter's is a wraith of freezing-cold wind.

      I personally am not a huge fan of option 4, as it makes the seasonal entities too accessible. In my games, it's generally some combination of 1-3.

                              The very first supplement in the line addressed this very question.

                              Originally posted by Rogue Changelings and the Courts
                              In most freeholds, the Courts as mystical pledges between an individual changeling and a season and the Courts as social and political entities made up of changelings all sworn to the same season are synonymous. When a changeling joins the Summer Court, for example, she swears her fealty to Summer by proxy when she makes her pledge to the [Summer] King. Thereafter, she is a member of the Summer Court in both the mystic and the political sense.

                              This isn’t always the case, however. While certainly a more difficult prospect, it isn’t entirely unreasonable that a changeling might enter his or her own pledge directly with the season, in much the same way that the Court founders did when they first created the Great Courts. Such an ordeal likely involves an arduous quest through the Hedge or some similarly significant trial, but it can be done — and changelings who are, for whatever reason, not welcome in polite freehold society have all the more reason to try.

                              Bear this in mind when looking at the statistics for the sample antagonists throughout this section. Just because a ravening privateer is listed as belonging to a Court doesn’t mean he’ll be showing up at the society ball any time soon.

                                      One of the side effects of forging a Court is that the founder has a permanent and deliberately unbreakable lesser alliance pledge with the phenomenon and feels compelled to promote their Court; considering even the least-fae changeling has about a decade longer to work with than a mortal would, there's certainly time to build up a marketing base.

                                      On the less esoteric end of things, Tom Hood brought the Seasonal Courts to Miami from Europe in the form of his own initiation into the Autumn Court and ancient books of mystical-philosophical theory that he used to initiate Rose Thorne and Grandfather Thunder into the Spring and Summer Courts (and which La Llorona stole to begin Miami's Winter Court).

                                      And on the most abstract level, we're talking about crazy people with an innate connection to Fate's daughtermom - if a Court already exists, it's entirely possible that some delusion or other might lead a Courtless wayward to sign on for the hard sell with the Black Tortoise or the Iron Spear.

                                                  Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                  If we have proto-freehold area with no Court or changling association, only the Lost PCs themselves - is there a way for them to get idea of the Courts? Could Seasons send some emissaries to them? Could PCs find books describing the Courts in mortals stuff? Could they Keeper knew about the Courts and would slip away in the time in Arcadia?
                                                  Just off the top of my head:
                                                  • The PCs have dreams with a strong seasonal and cyclic symbolism. Research into the subject (goblin-based, probably) reveals the existence of a vanished freehold.
                                                  • They find books penned by changelings in one of those weird little bookstores, offhandedly mentioning the Courts.
                                                  • A [seasonally appropriate animal] leads them to a seasonal almost-place at the edge of reality – a frozen pond in the middle of summer, a great autumn-red maple in winter, et cetera – where names are written. Signing on grants them a subtle dot in Mantle.
                                                  • An old stele tells them – straight-up tells them, with a voice and everything – about a community of Lost who were, but are no longer, and the crowns and cloaks they wore.
                                                  • Over time, they find indications of changelings that came before. They notice signs and marks only they can see, find hedgespun clothes in old wardrobes, see impossible flowers growing out of graves. Research, as above, is the key.

                                                  Obviously, you could combine any of the above into a coherent narrative. You could do the "signs of before" thing, and have their research lead them to the standing stone, which in turns gives them enough information to locate the signing-places.
