    That's the crux of my opinion - a less skilled person wielding a .38 can kill someone with a single shot. Not very often, granted, but can. The rules do not reflect this, in my opinion. We can dress it up or apply conditions all day long but I don't think the rules allow for it.
    If you apply a Tilt / Condition that reflects bleeding out, how is this NOT deadly?

    There's a Bleeding Tilt in Hurt Locker. On an average person (2 Dex, 1 firearms, wp as suggested), using a gun, an average roll is enough to inflict the Bleeding Tilt on an average victim. She is taking one point of damage per turn on top of what she already suffered from being. That's four rounds (ten seconds) to unconciousness, twelve rounds to death, without the most basic medical treatment. Couple that with the shock of being, well, shot. Stunned, insensate. And likely still being shot at. And suffering wound penalties, making it harder to think of what to do? Its possible that your average person (2 strength) will be unable to apply pressure to themselves, let alone be able to fight back, so they can't really treat themselves effectively in this case.

    Werewolves can heal themselves, so its unlikely they will bleed out (and vampires/mummies can't), but a single, simple changeling? Definitely. A demon without Just Bruised? Yes. Even a Beast without an appropriate Atavism will die from a gunshot wound. Fairly sure Prometheans could bleed out too without electricity to heal. A Sin-Eater that doesn't Bulwark the damage? Most likely, depending on 2e rules. We touched on mortals / hunters. Mages could Pattern Restore, but that takes mana and your action away from, well, surviving gunshots being aimed their way; for a mage, if you don't have mage armor up, guns are still very scary.

    For the overwhelming majority of people, let alone monsters, being shot is still a big deal with just that one tilt.

                                Originally posted by Lord Dynel View Post
                                Would you mind elaborating on this thought?
                                Sure. So, say you have a dice pool of 9 with your light pistol, going with the earlier example. The math of expected successes in CofD's dice pool system is a little complicated, but for simplicity's sake we'll use the general rule that you can expect 1/3 of your dice pool in successes on average. So with no additional penalties, you can expect to do around 4 damage per shot with that dice pool. However, if you can make a called shot to increase your damage - let's say, for example, your opponent is wearing Sports Gear - 2/0 armor that covers the torso and arms. Targeting the head for a -3 penalty is always worth it. You give up 1 expected success for 2 damage on a hit, for a net gain of +1 to your expected damage value. If you choose to not make the called shot, you are falling into a trap choice because numerically speaking, it is strictly worse than taking the called shot.

                                On the other hand, if your dice pool is 5, taking the called shot is the trap option, because the -3 penalty will take your expected number of successes below 1, and the extra 2 damage from getting around the armor only matters if you get at least one success on the dice roll.

                                EDIT: of course, all of this assumes extra damage is the only difference between a called shot and a not-called shot. Having Tilts apply on called shots solves this problem because rather than trading chance to hit (and therefore damage) for more damage, which has an objectively best option, you're trading chance to hit for a special effect, the value of which is situational. That makes it a choice instead of a math test.

                                                This is a rather frustrating statement. The average damage is not a cap on damage. That means they'll be dealing lethal attacks against average Health characters more frequently than you'd roll a natural 20 in D&D.                                                 So please, be careful with how to look at these numbers.
