                                The first Strain is done, probably the only one I'll do tonight. There's quite a lot of reused material, but also plenty of new stuff. I'd definitely like feedback on this.

                                Leliel, best of luck with The Mad Arab, my only request is that he's a stone cold badass, he survives by being patient, cautious, and balls to the walls crazy enough to look Chuthlu in the eye and go "yes, clearly stealing that guy's power is the best way to understand Allah's creation".

                                Bahamutans

                                [Picture: The Symbol of Bahamut: An oval, fish-like scale with iridescent rays. It is inlaid with intricate filigree-work in some precious metal. Over this has been crudely painted an open eye, with no iris but a wide black pupil. Fingerprints are still visible in the paint. ]

                                [Picture: Central. A fisherman is standing under a streetlight. He is a young man, with a general Latino appearance. His legs are in oilskin pants but he is barefoot.                                 Theology

                                In a world of chaos they cry out to the depth for guidance. What is just, what is moral, what is right? Mankind has always turned to the gods for answers to life's unanswerable questions. At their best philosophers and sages debate the wisdom of their ancestors or craft parables out of mighty gods and heroic mortals to teach ordinary men extraordinary ideas. At their lowest the gods become a shield to escape responsibility or a chain that binds nations to traditions that, if they ever made sense, only did so in a world that no longer exists. At the very lowest, in the darkest, murkiest depths of the oceans, you can find the Bahamatains.

                                The blood of Bahamut carries a strong protective instinct. It creates a strain driven to guard what it considers it's own, and even when it doesn't a Bahamatain can't help but be aware that they inhabit a world which is, to them, crushingly small and unbearably fragile. Bahamatains often seek to retreat from the world and dwell in solitude among their beloved, but in this the Strain's nature is at war with itself. The Leviathan's need for self restraint is a constant irritation, and when they decide to let go the results can be quite literally apocalyptic.

                                The cults that surround Bahamatains reflect this. A Bahamatuns's Beloved bask in that reassuring power, they do not ask the Leviathan for help with their problems so much as they ask the Leviathan for permission, placing the burden of responsibility on titanic shoulders large enough to bear it. The Strain often sees it's Beloved as tiny helpless creatures, who's neediness is both irritating and self-affirming. The greatest danger arises when the Cult calls upon their deity to abandon his self-restraint, when that happens something is going to snap.

                                Morphology

                                Even in a family of titans, Bahamutans are known for their size. Even when not expressing their Leviathan natures members of this Lineage tend towards broad shoulders and considerable height. When they Transform Bahamutans display characteristics of life famed for prodigious size or toughness. Among the strain one can find echoes the whale's titanic form, or the catfish dwarfing the other inhabitants of it's lake. Shells resembling a turtle or a crab are common, as are iron hard scales. The Strain's immense forms can seem to drift, untouchable and listless, through the Depths.

                                Bahamutans who favour Awareness typically manifest it their proclivity for size, with enormous eyes or forests of antenna. Their titanic bodies can provide a place of safety and sustenance to whole ecologies of strange and sometimes voracious life created by the Tribe's legendary Fecundity. Of course, it need not be said how a being of immense size might manifest divine Might.

                                Progenitor

                                The Bahamutans trace their descent back to a Progenitor labelled Bahamat. Reputedly a fish or whale of immense size, it was said that Bahamat was the spine of the world and supported whole cities on its back, his enormous size gave entire nations shelter from waves and storms unimaginable in today's post-antediluvian world, yet when roused his hanger would topple mountains and shake the very heart of the world.

                                Modern Bahamutans see obvious parallels between their Progenitor, their restraint and their relationship to their cult. For spiritual and more human Leviathans Bahamat's role as a source of life and safety is a point of pride and comfort. Others say the power and fury their Progenitor would display when angered proves the Strain possesses a lost tradition as warrior judges who punish traitors and troublemakers.

                                Puberty

                                It begins with distance. As master of the natural armours and Vitality of the tribe a Bahamatun's inheritance first manifests by a feelings of inner security. What once seemed like enormous problems seem small, easily solvable but also unimportant. In these early days the hardest and most important thing is for the Leviathan to maintain a sense of proportion. Some describe it as an almost dreamlike state where the world seems formed of mist, where force and reaction seem unrelated. What feels like a simple push topples trees, an attempt to rescue a drowning man churns the beach into muck. The first step taken by many Bahamatuans is to relearn their own strength.

                                Bahamatun families, even those unaware of their own heritage, have a moderate tendency to be surprisingly calm when a new Bahamatuan emerges. Sometimes this is stoicism, sometimes shock, but it always gives a little breathing room that gives the family time to get to grips, or succumb to the wake, before making any rash decisions.

                                A curious fact is that Bahamatuans usually emerge in a group and when they do, even if they had never met before, they lack the territorial instincts that most Leviathans feel against each other. They may also include any other Bahamatuan who was a family figure in their upbringing in their instinctual trust though this is unreliable. A Cohort formed of related Bahamatuans can be among the most stable and thus the most successful in the Tribe, however instincts to cooperate can repress disagreement leading to stagnation. Even worse, repressed feelings can escape in other self destructive ways or build up until they explode.

                                Genetics

                                Family

                                Bearing both the Vestiges of Fecundity and Vitality the Strain has the reputation for voracious breading but also having the ability to weather the rigours of reproduction. Bahamatain families are often impressively large, bearing more children than most strains and loosing far fewer to infighting or birth defects. This size and stability can make Bahamatain families a culture all onto themselves with outsiders marrying into the clan only after it's been determined that they will fit in without making waves. If no one can be found, the Strain is remarkably resistant to the dangers of incest.

                                Don't be fooled, Bahamat's blood has problems all of it's own. The tight-kit and self-sustaining structure of a Bahamatan clan conceals a stifling tendency to self-replicate and an abhorrence of change and independence. Many Bahamatains have found their family unchanged since the Leviathan that founded the clan, and awash with heirlooms, advice and talents uniquely sorted to whatever problems faced their ancestor. In such cases it is a great challenge to reform the clan in his own image, many Leviathans simply flee and start their own family elsewhere. It has been noted that once a generation of Leviathans departs, two generations at the most, Behamut's mystical inheritance departs and the clan is assimilated into the modern world or destroyed by their own obsolescence.

                                Bahamatain families can exist independent of an extended clan. Such families are marked by extreme internal loyalty and, while they are not shunned by outsiders it's obvious to all that their family comes first and they are treated accordingly. Leviathans from such families find that the hardest part is the irrevocable change in their relationship after claiming their Birthright, or the distance they must create to protect their family from the Wake.

                                Reproduction

                                There seems to be some sort of mechanism at work in bloodlines touched by Bahamut. Some subconscious system that prevents a Leviathan being born until there is space for a new arrival. Of course this isn't to say it's polite enough to wait for parents who genuinely want a Leviathan, yet as a general rule Bahamatuns are rarely born to parents struggling to get by, and most commonly emerge in times of prosperity and expansion. This trait is amplified when there are living Bahamatuns present: A Bahamatun finding herself pressed for resources will rarely discover that a nephew is the latest addition to their worries.

                                Bahamutans normally arrive in a group of between two and three at a time. These individuals often show some remarkable similarities even before coming into their Heritage: Appearance, mannerisms, an odd trait like shared allergies. This isn't that surprising in long running Bahamutan families but for Bahamatains with a culturally normal background it can be quite the shock meeting your nearly identical third cousin for the first time at a family reunion. Bahamatun families with a more Tribe oriented culture and no access to prophecies often look for odd similarities as portents of an future Leviathan. Identical twins (either actual identical twins or fraternal twins who happen to look identical, that happens a lot) are considered an especially auspicious sign. When born to a bloodline touched by Bahamut if one identical twin is a Leviathan the other certainly is too.

                                Lahmasu

                                The line of Lahmasu that Bahamut's lineage produces are the Gugal or "great bulls." The strain is distinguished by characteristically large builds and often a distinctly aquatic appearance, especially later in life. Uniquely among Lahmasu the Gugal tend towards genetically stable populations - often taken to extremes, a Gugal community eventually ends up populated with genetically identical inhabitants. Many prefer the company of their own, where their distinctive appearances are accepted.

                                There are entire underwater cities and nations populated exclusively by Gugal. Some have been a source of Leviathans for centuries, being remade each generation and producing Bahamutans who's ignorance of the surface and tales of underwater life both fascinate other Leviathans and leave them afraid of the damage that ignorance might cause. Other cities have long lost their share of Bahamat's mystical inheritance and retain only his genetic legacy, some of these cities worship ancestral Opheons or zealously worship long dead gods in hope that one day Bahamut's favoured children will return to them. Others, unshackled from the legacy of stifling self-replication, pursue strange sciences and magics unknown upon the surface and vow to defend their independence from any would be conqueror god, even their own children.

                                Vestiges

                                Vitality, Awareness, Fecundity, Might

                                Symbols and Associations

                                The Sea of Salahit, the Caspian Sea, the vice of Sloth.

                                Concepts

                                Awkward young linebacker, high-stakes fisherman, neighbourhood protector, surprisingly burly kindergarten teacher, professional wrestler, church camp counsellor

                                Quote

                                "This is a good place. I like it here, and I spent years making sure it remains the sort of place I like. Usually this involves making sure punks like you don’t bother the good people of this town. Then it becomes the sort of place they don’t like. You still sure you want to set up shop here?"

                                                    The beginnings of my attempt at the Mad Arab.

                                                    Beneficent One.

                                                    I have spent long and hard at the translations of the texts you bequeathed unto me. While I have spent all that was needed to fully clear the text of any errors, I plead your forgiveness; there is almost certainly problems in this English version. Not because of lack of competence on the efforts of my allies in academia, but because I am sure a significant portion of these books are not Medieval Arabic. The author seems to have either invented or miswrote several words, and both seem...problematic. The invented words don't generally follow Arabic conventions, but they seem awfully consistent for misprints.

                                                    For example, his name. The name "Abdul Alhazred" is, quite simply, phonetically impossible. "Al-" is a morpheme that cannot be used twice in a row. Since his name only appears once throughout the texts, I have transposed it into a close Modern Arabic equivalent, one whose meaning seems fitting enough. And yet, "Alhazred" makes sense from an                                                     English perspective, on the term "All has read" or even "Hazard", both of which make sense as pseudonyms.

                                                    Also, there's something else; he mentions your name.

                                                    The Travels of Abd-al-Hazred

                                                    ...It was at that point that I finally arrived at the final truth that had so long troubled me. The vision marked my form of humanity as small because it was small, a tiny part of the whole of Creation, A beauty eclipsed by grander beauty, a meaning lost in meaning. It was always a foolish thing to assume "the image of god" was two arms, two legs, and a mind to somehow confront the difficulties posed by such a form that has neither the strength of a beast or the magic of smokeless fire. No, Creation was far greater than me, the Earth, and even the stars in the Heavens themselves. And I knew from my vision, the beholding of the sea-jinn's soul, that even the fragment of the image that human's embodies was omniferrous and flexible, with even the capacity of becoming as a jinn itself.

                                                    A rather foolish proposition. The jinn are mighty, but humans in all their forms are the perfected creations of God. As the myths and stories state, a human, a true human, is able to bend them to their will. While that art is beyond me, and I have little doubt the thrill of such power corrupts lesser men, I certainly know enough of it to thieve the smokeless fire, however briefly, and my goal is purer. Through examining humanity's predecessors, I will harden the clay that composes me, and so attain a form capable of beholding Allah and the angels as they truly are, without my insignificant perceptions blinding me to their true glory...
