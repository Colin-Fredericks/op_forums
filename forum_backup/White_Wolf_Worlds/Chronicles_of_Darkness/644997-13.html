    Personally, as far as Quiescence is concerned I'm still not seeing what the big deal going against it as written is. You can't remember magic as magic, but instead rationalize it as something else, so what? How does that really impede Hunters from hunting mages? A Hunter should generally be able to figure out whether or not someone is a mage using indirect observation. Is it absolutely foolproof? No, but honestly I don't really feel like detection should be 100% flawless, and it should accurate enough for most purposes.

    And again, it isn't like it does anything during any sort of confrontation. If you bust into a mages sanctum and he starts creating golems out of the ground and sending them at you you see them and recognize him a doing such. So there's no real effect during a fight to contend with, it's the same as any other kind of hunt.

    As for afterwards, you remember the mage as attacking you mundanely. You don't forget he was a mage assuming you go into the fight knowing that, you just don't recall him actually using magic. This still doesn't impact whether you were able to hunt the mage successfully or not. Either you did and the guys dead or you didn't and you now have much worse things to worry about than the Sleeping Curse.

    So I'm not really seeing what else you could really want. I mean if you're looking for ways to figure out what sort of magic the mage is using there's already mechanics for that. It's called an investigations roll to piece together whatever evidence you can find on scene, including but not limited to:

    - Witness Testimonies
    - Analysis of Structural Damage
    - Identification of Strange Materials and Substances

    You don't really need any sort of special Tactic, or Merit, or whatever to do this. It's just a normal use of skills. And while there are some things that you probably can't understand purely from evidence (a lot of these things you might not understand even if you could remember what you saw), this only makes hunting mages a little bit more difficult, but still in no way impossible.

The Quiescence certainly makes this much more difficult when it comes to Awakened mages, including preparation for the hunt, but hardly impossible. I agree with Ashenrogue that the Quiescence, while an advantage for the Awakened, is not their greatest asset or insurmountable. I believe that the versatility and power of the Awakened, individually but much more so as cabals and consilia, are what makes them such potent prey. Unless your Hunters are Tier 3, this generally applies to all the major template supernaturals.

                        Originally posted by Maina View Post
                        I'd be fine with the idea that only 3rd tier Conspiracies can hunt mages. They have extra support. They exist to take down the bigger threads. But this idea of "Nothing can touch Dave's Mages no matter what anybody else says because Dave says so" is harmful to the nWoD 2e as a whole. As much as I love his work (he's easily one of my favorite devs), he's too protective of any perceived weakness applied to his baby. 1e Hunters had mana-draining devices and guns that made Vampires lose Vitae. It's not that out-there that the Conspiracies can develop counters to the Lie, even if it's rare, expensive, and filled with drawbacks.
                        I think you're significantly exaggerating Dave's comments about the purported absolute secrecy and invincibility of the Awakened. He simply stated that the Quiescence affects all Sleepers, and no mundane Tactic or similar measures provides immunity (if you have an inherent supernatural merit or ability, you are a de facto Sleepwalker and immune to the Sleeping Curse). Dave also emphasized that the Curse that affects Sleepers, Sleepwalkers and Awakened alike, just in different ways. He also made the relatively obvious and banal observation that the Awakened are indeed powerful compared to normal humans, as are most of the nWOD major template. Absent the advantages and likely supernatural assistance and power available to Tier 3 Hunters, mages should be exceptionally difficult to take down. In fact, at least in Hunter 1e, the Tier system is supposed to represent the demonstrable differences in organization, power and resources among Hunters.

                        I would additionally note that Dave correctly observed that a number of Hunter Tactics violated fundamental and immutable rules concerning templates besides the Awakened, and thus significant conflicts with the other nWOD game lines. As the Mage developer, Dave unsurprisingly suggested that when actually playing Mage, conflicts should be resolved in favor of Mage rules, mechanics and setting.
