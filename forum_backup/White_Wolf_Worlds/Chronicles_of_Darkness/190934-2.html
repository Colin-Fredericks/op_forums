What and why were the entities which so terrified the masters of Irem that they fled thousands of miles through the near Umbra to Egypt? Could they first have fled from Melanesia to Chile?

        One possibility lies in the heritage of headhunting practiced by the Jivaroan tribes. I remember being taught how the Inca regularly waged war to annihilate these tribes for practicing headshrinking. The Inca believed these practices destroyed the soul. Certainly, this would be a potent motivator for Pre-proto-Egyptian rulers to consider relocating. As I recall, headhunting was also practiced throughout the Torres Straits and regions of Polynesia and Melanesia.

        Quoting from Wikipedia:
        The practice of preparing shrunken heads originally had religious significance; shrinking the head of an enemy was believed to harness the spirit of that enemy and compel him to serve the shrinker. It was said to prevent the soul from avenging his death.

        Shuar believed in the existence of three fundamental spirits:
        • Wakani - innate to humans thus surviving their death.
        • Arutam - literally "vision" or "power", protects humans from a violent death.
        • Muisak - vengeful spirit, which surfaces when a person carrying an arutam spirit is murdered.
        To block a Muisak from using its powers, they severed their enemies' heads and shrank them. The process also served as a way of warning their enemies. Despite these precautions, the owner of the trophy did not keep it for long. Many heads were later used in religious ceremonies and feasts that celebrated the victories of the tribe. Accounts vary as to whether the heads would be discarded or stored.
        The Chango people, on whom I can find very little currently, appear to be the remains of the once great Chinchorro civilization.

            I gotta say, I feel like Ghost Numen (including the new ones from Mummy) provide all the powers you could possibly want or need to build other-culture Mummies. They can animate a mummified corpse as either a Revenant or an Intruder, reinforce its attributes, walk it through walls, fly, move things with their mind, create horrific phantasms, control animals, light fires, and so on. There is no limit preventing them from predating the Arisen, and with the Age Supernatural trait you can also make them highly resistant to other powers (anything more than 1,000 years old has Age 10, if I remember right). They can even be playable characters using the Lucid Dead Numen.

            The beauty of the nWoD is its toolbox nature, I find. You can take the elements and aspects that you want. Ghosts have none of the backstory that the Arisen have that requires them to be tied to Ancient Egypt, so it's entirely possible that as the Arisen spread across the world they could encounter even more ancient, mummified creatures that could certainly terrify a low-Sekhem Mummy. The Arisen are fundamentally tied to proto-Egypt, but if you want to build a mummy-like creature from another civilisation you have all tools you need to make one without any need for further official material.

                    I'm not sure how to really address the basic of the OP's original question. As that's been answered basically (with a resounding NO Mummy the Curse is not doing the "Mummy's from every culture that ever mummified a body thing") and moved on from.

                    So with the idea of an "official" expansion to the line including the type of "non-Iremite" Mummy out-the-window we are firmly in the realms of "in your game". The simple answer to that is - Yes, there can be Mummy's from other cultures and times in your game. Mainly because if you are choosing to do your own thing with your Mummy game, that's up to you and it really does not matter what other people think or say or do. If you want to change Mummy and run it more "multi-cultural" fine, go for it, have fun with that.

                    All I'll say is that Sothis Ascends might feature some of the "other Mummy's" in it. Though they likely will be derivatives and bastardizations of the Rite of Return and almost certainly Lifeless. I guess its the closest we get to a Wicked Dead style book that Reseru mentioned. I'm sure with a bit of work they could be made into less antagonistic versions that might be PC material. Add in the Purified from Immortals and bigo, already got a bevy of Mummy's from other cultures that are not from Irem.

                    Obviously, take the above with a pinch of salt, Sothis is still months from completion and we don't really have any idea about what these "other mummy's" its likely to feature are like at current.

                    One thing I have noticed though is that the OP seems to be making an argument that the CWoD was more "mulit-cultural" then the NWoD, which I think is a whole other argument and, in my opinion, not really the right term. I'd say that the CWoD was more "culturally-fetishistic" then the NWoD, one only has to look at Kindred of the East to see that (its take on Asia is...yeah, and lets not get into the racial implication of the way the 1,000 Hell's spreads). NWoD is more about supernatural beings as archetypes that while having roots in a particular culture are abstracted out to be "generic" enough to fit into all cultures.

                    For example, Mages in NWoD started in Atlantis which is a Greeko-Roman myth, but Mage's Atlantis only really shares a name with Plato's story, its not that Mage Atlantis was Greeko-Roman, it belonged to no culture, it was its own culture distinct from any present in the world today.

                    Another example is the Clans of Vampire the Requiem, largely based off the European idea of a Vampire (and the 19th-20the Century idea of a Vampire as well). However, between the large archetypes of each clan, the numerous bloodlines and the "other vampires" detailed in splat books for the line, you can pretty much play any time of vampiric creature with a bit of rules tweaking.

                    The other aspect we need to address is that some splats in the NWoD simply don't really draw on any real world culture. Werewolves and Demons for example have their own cosmology and metaphysical structure that might resemble some human cultures (shamanic traditions in the case of Werewolf) but are distinct from them. After all know one has a culture involved with the God-Machine in the real world. We hope...

                    Still other splats are very, very multi-cultural by their nature - Hunters come from all walks of life and all cultures, Sin-Eaters attach to death mythology the world over, Prometheans tie into various cultural myths about created creatures. Once again, it could be said they tie into the larger multi-cultural narratives of folk lore and supernatural belief schema's that exist in a consistent cross-cultural narrative.

                    The final point to make in regards to the NWoD approach to "supernatural culture" is that it is influenced by more then just cultural backdrop. Fictional narrative plays a roll as well (more so in some games then in others see Vampire vs Werewolf), as does distribution dynamics. So yes, Vampire the Requiem plays vampires as having elements of Bram Stoker and Ann Rice. Mummy has mummy's that hail from a proto-ancient Egypt. One type of Promethean is the Frankenstein.

                    This is because the developers are influenced by media and know that their audience is also familiar with this media. So they use the common tropes and ideas in these popular and culturally prevalent (in the west and increasingly globally) takes on classic folklore as a sort of "short-hand". Furthermore, these ideas are kinda ingrained in our social consciousness about these creatures these days. If I walked up to a person on the street and asked "What are the things that make a Vampire a Vampire?" or "Where do Mummy's come from?" I imagine I'd get a list of traits that describe Dracula in the former and Egypt for the latter.

                    Some people might think that last point about being influenced by popular (Western) culture is a bad thing. I don't, but that's just my opinion.

                    Ah, yes and as a final thing for the OP to think about. I notice a lot of CWoD getting thrown around in his posts (Wyrm, Near Umbra etc) so I was wondering if you were thinking of adapting Mummy the Curse into CWoD. If that's the case, awesome, go for it.

                    ​Though could you please spare the people on a NWoD board the veiled hints about some CWoD being superior to their NWoD iterations. Your comments about cultural myopia and "real" mages (Ascension I take it?) not buying into the Atlantis thing seem to point towards this view point (though I could be misreading you, if so I apologize). I'm simply saying this because the whole "Classic vs New" WoD argument is old, worn out and utterly pointless, we all have bits we like more from each, its not something that needs to be part of forum posts that are nothing to do with that debate.
