        Originally posted by Hogsy View Post
        Is there a way to counter Obfuscate? I thought Animalism could due to the wording of Obfuscate not including animals when it clearly makes the distinction between people, objects & animals but my ST does not agree with me.

        In first edition animals could be used to counter Obfuscate as animals were expressly stated to be immune to its applications. (As were children and the insane because they are used to seeing things that nobody else does.) But since animals also normally avoided vampires by default, ghouling them or using Animalism were the only ways to reliably control them.

        As per the current iteration, by the book itself, a high perception skill can currently detect Obfuscated characters or objects (There are several merits that can do this.) but not cancel it. Obfuscate penalizes perception rolls, it doesn't cancel them. Also, if you are targeted by Obfuscate, you are immune to that Obfuscate user's other applications of Obfuscate. Depending on how you phrase Auspex questions at certain levels, it can also undermine Obfuscate. Successfully Lashing Out on the Obfuscate user breaks all applications of Obfuscate they have running.

        Whether or not animals see through Obfuscate currently is up to the ST, if he says no, then it doesn't.

            In 2ed Obfuscate is generically stated as removing vampire from perception. I'd say Animals are included.
            Anyone can try to detect an obfuscated vampire by INDIRECT clues. That's meaning, if you succed the prolonged roll, you will be aware of the very current vampire's position but you still do not see him. I would allow such roll just if the character is aware that an obfuscated is in the area or if he has reasons to guess it. If you manage to perceive the vampire presence either by indirect clues or Auspex, you can then lash out to try to shatter his obfuscate power being then able to see him.

            Note that if someone using obfuscate 3 is attacking someone else in a crowd, Obfuscate will continue to work on anyone but the person being attacked. So others witnesses would just see someone fighting/being pulled away with/by void.

            Auspex 1 is definitely enough to ask if someone using obfuscate is here.

                To add to this for Hogsy :

                With Obfuscate 2 you can extend your Obfuscate effects to other items and people, so if you plan on attacking someone in a crowd while Obfuscated with Obfuscate 3, it's probably smart to spend a Vitae to obfuscate them with Touch of Shadow, which allows the others to roll a Wits + Composure minus your Obfuscate dots, but if they fail that roll, they do not notice what is happening, and you can torment your victim without anyone around him realizing what is going on.

                If you want to go even further, you could even spend two Vitae instead of just one to have your victim vanish completely (just like yourself), rather than just being overlooked, but I'm not 100% certain if that's allowed if you follow the rules as written (do you perhaps know, Marcus ?).

                As for animals and Obfuscate: I'd say animals see through Obfuscate 1, Face in the Crowd, but not Obfuscate 3, Cloak of Night. Why I say this? Because Obfuscate 1 merely makes you look like nobody important, it doesn't completely hide you from sight, it just makes you look uninteresting, and the effects of Face in the Crowd will cease to mask your presence the moment you do anything to draw attention to yourself or you're unexpected. Of course, what draws a human's attention is not the same thing as what draws a dog's attention. While a human wouldn't be bothered by the mailman entering his front garden to deliver the mail, his dog might be.


                As for how to counter Obfuscate:

                If you have Auspex, use that to find the obfuscated vampire.

                If you have Resilience, you might try to outlast your obfuscated enemy. If he attacks you, he will no longer be obfuscated. This is when you can use your Lashing Out ability to shatter his Obfuscate. If you are successful, he can no longer affect you with Obfuscate for that scene. If Lashing Out fails or isn't an option, he can go invisible again by spending another Vitae, but you can keep tanking his hits with Resilience, meaning he'll eventually give up or run out of Vitae, while you try to track him down.

                If you're with a group, I highly recommend you work together as a team with assist rolls to track the obfuscated vampire down. You will lose a couple of turns this way, but once you figure out the location of the vampire, you can all try to Lash Out against him. Only one person needs to win that contest to shatter the Obfuscate effect and then everyone can see the vampire. Keep in mind that if multiple people Lash Out against the obfuscated vampire, you will start to get penalties on your Lashing Out rolls, but it's still worth trying, if nothing else, because it will cost the obfuscated vampire a lot of Willpower to keep counter-lashing (unless he has higher Blood Potency).

                If you have Celerity and you're being attacked by someone obfuscated, it might be worth it to interrupt his attack with a grapple, but you can only do this if you are aware that you're being attacked. If you fail to notice the surprise attack, you aren't allowed to use Celerity to interrupt. Once you have the vampire in a grapple, he can't go invisible and run as long as you keep control over the grapple.

                If you have Majesty, you can try to use Awe and some Social rolls to persuade the obfuscated vampire to either stand down, run away, or face you like a man.

                        You can use Resilience in a preventive way. You can, on your own turn, activate the active effect of Resilience, and when you're being attacked on the enemy's turn, you can subtract your Resilience + 1 from the total damage done to you.

                        That being said, personally I always allow my players to use Resilience immediately after the damage has been dealt, even if that damage is dealt through a surprise attack. I'm not sure if that's RAW, but that's how I do it.

                        So how it goes when dealing with a surprise attack::

                        Beginning of the surprise attack action -> player gets to roll a Wits + Composure - Obfuscate to notice the surprise attack -> enemy rolls attack with or without calculating for the player's defense (depending on the result of his Wits + Composure roll) -> attack's damage is declared to the player -> player chooses to remove/downgrade damage with the active effect of Resilience -> player updates his Health track with the remaining damage -> end of action.

                        When dealing with a stake, the player character falls into torpor if he's still left with 5 damage from the attack after using Resilience.


                        I would like the opinion of other people on this though. How does everyone else handle Resilience? Do you allow players to use it after the damage from the attack has been declared or only before?
