            Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
            In regards to your critique in the post above though, I would postulate that it's problem isn't so much refusing to be anything (After everything I've read I believe it's definitely trying to be something), but rather it's refusing to be a Chronicles of Darkness game. And by that, and I mean this truly, it's refusing to be a horror game. (At the end of the day, Genius is a game about power fantasy rather than horror. One of the many vestiges of it's One World of Darkness heritage.)
            The Horror of Genius is a more personal Horror - the dread of knowing your mind is not entirely under your control and that the grand visions of possibility you see are crippled (perhaps terminally) by your own madness. It's knowing you can cure cancer, and knowing that if you try to publish or mass produce your work, being laughed at is the best outcome.

            It's also the Horror of knowing that your peers are capable of equally great wonders... and statistically possessing fewer scruples.

            Read (if you haven't already) Soon I Will Be Invincible and examine Doctor Impossible's plight.

            Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
            In Genius, a Wonder is an invention that's powered by a supernatural power source called Mania. A Wonder works because the Genius believes it does, and will either go haywire or self destruct if a regular mortal, supernatural creature, or just another Genius with a different set of beliefs examines it or sees it function. And the Lemurians, Geniuses who believe their delusions are real, use Wonders that are already scientifically impossible and only work because of the Mania powering it and because they truly believe it works. And Wonders are able to alter the very fabric of space and time to a degree that could spell the end of the entire world should one powerful enough get out of hand.

            That isn't science. That isn't even mad science. That's magic. Specifically, that is Mage: the Ascension Magik.
            A Wonder is an Invention that exploits scientific laws to achieve an outcome, like any other technology. Mania is what lets the fact that physics do not work that way here be ignored. A Wonder works because of the principles behind it, regardless of whether the Genius truly believes them (as proper Peers don't). It goes Haywire when exposed to others due to Havoc (Watsonian: incompatable observers collapse the waveform that makes Wonders stable | Doylist: Havoc is necessary for any kind of Masquerade, and for certain Mad Scientist tropes).

            It isn't science. It's Mad Science (which isn't actually related, except on surface aesthetic, to real science).

                            Lack of control in Genius is everywhere. Lack of control over your own mind. Lack of control over your country. Lack of control over your society.

                            It's basically Lovecraft's cosmic horror, but from a different angle. Lovecraft says the universe is too big for anything you do to matter. Genius says the world is too chaotic for anything you do to matter. I much prefer Genius' take because ultimately the universe beyond Earth doesn't have much effect on my life, and here on Earth chaos is more potent than vastness.

And of realising that everything you built is destined to crumble away: The American political system, an elegantly crafted wonder made by some of the finest minds slowly decays into Trump wielding unprecedented executive power.


                            * Technically in the real world there are things you can do about it; Trump's victory is far from garmented and a Trump presidency is not garmented to destroy America.                                                         Vent0 and The Kings Raven, your both entirely right about my mistake in comparing Wonders to Ascension Magik. Sorry about that, I knew I'd make some missteps with some of my example and just hoped they wouldn't be to large. Two thing I still believe I haven't misstepped though is my assertion that Wonders aren't Mad Science, and that Genius isn't a horror game. The latter I'll get into in a second because I've been wanting to talk about this but didn't know how well my first critique would be received (which was beyond my wildest hopes after all I've heard about the Genius fanbase, thank you all so much for helping me better understand this beautifully flawed game), but for the former I'm going to have to agree with ArcaneArts point that Genius' refusal to properly define how Wonders work and function might be one of the games biggest aesthetic shortcomings. It's a shortcoming I can accept though, so long as they more properly define Mania and it's source, but it's still a shortcoming.

                                                        And now nothing more to do and to get to it shall we? My second thesis if you will: Genius isn't a Horror Game.

                                                        Genius: the Transgression, like it's progenitors in the One World of Darkness, is a Urban (Science)Fantasy game with Horror Elements. It's core gameplay is power fantasy because it's Theme is Transgression but it has no real Accusers to punish the Geniuses should they transgress beyond having their own inventions go haywire, or loosing their grip on conventional reality for a time. It's set in the Chronicles of Darkness world because it's Mood is Bitter Disappointment, but there are no stakes or motivations to fuel said despair beyond having one's inventions blow up in one's face or loosing a grip on conventional reality.

                                                        My point: A good horror game merely need to paint a world where the player character become unavoidably tied to the unknown, that way horror can sink into the player characters as they slowly discover what they've become, who they really are, how the world truly is, how much remains unknown, an how horrifying those mysteries would be should they ever be answered.

                                                        And to Genius' credit it does try to do this. By the powers does it try so hard to be horror. But ultimately it falls flat because nothing cathartic is gained from the horror elements in the game. There's no lesson learned or question asked by the horror elements, so it winds up more set dressing than anything. And that's a real shame, because loss of control can be an excellent source of horror. Instead, it just becomes a side effect of failure. There's no horror in that.

                                                            Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                                            Well, it is called Inspiration. You can model prophets as well as physicists.
                                                            Dilution is a problem. A game about magic users has enough space for prophets and physicists. To say a game about mad scientists can model prophets as well as physicists sounds off. There's some technical truth there, but not as much as the former. It's weaker, it looses strength as it becomes spread out too thin. Our suspension of disbelief wavers, our investment in the icon that is trying to be sold here decreases.

                                                            Genius was always going to be a limited game in one of two ways-it could either broaden up and be a game about visionaries, artists, sociologists, etc, or it could really dig into it's area of science, at the cost of everything else.

                                                            Genius tells me it's about Mad Scientists, so I assume it thinks it chose the latter. It performed it poorly, and the fact that people keep telling me this is a game about visionaries tells me it didn't commit to that choice.

                                                            If you want a game about visionaries, make that game. And yeah, good fucking luck not making it Another Mage. If you want a game about Mad Scientists, cut your loses and make that game. You actually have a chance to make that Not Another Mage. It will not be as extensive or elaborate as some people want, but this is the price you pay for subject matter. You have to make to the scale of your concept.
