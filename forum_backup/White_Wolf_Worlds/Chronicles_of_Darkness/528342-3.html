                    Originally posted by Hexmeister Vic View Post
                    FWIW, I felt like the Fae-touched, from this reading, had more in common with Revenants than Ghouls, as far as the limits of their powers were concerned.
                    From a story perspective, yes, but mechanically speaking, they seem much closer to Ghouls to me. Revenants have no Clan and treat all Disciplines as out-of-clan, but can learn Disciplines on their own. Ghouls are considered the same Clan as their Regnant and pay in-Clan costs for their Clan Disciplines, but must be taught by a vampire. Fae-Touched are considered the same Seeming as the Changeling who helped them escape, start with two dots of their Seeming's Contracts, and can learn their Seeming's Contract Bonuses but must be taught by a changeling. Revenants have BP1, can only hold 5 + Stamina Vitae, and lose all Vitae when they awaken. Ghouls have BP 0, can hold Stamina Vitae, don't lose it except through normal use, and can't feed from mortals, but can be given Vitae by their Regnant. Fae-Touched in this draft have Wyrd 0, can hold 10 Glamour, don't lose it except through normal use, and can't harvest it from dreams, but can harvest it from emotions or Hedge Bounty.

                                On the Fae Touched themselves, I have mixed feelings. On the one hand I think Changeling has needed something akin to this for awhile. On the other, the origin of the Fae Touched seems a little...specific. Don't get me wrong, I like the idea that the Lost can have such an intimate tie to their version of the Ghoul (for lack of a better term). At the same time, when I consider all the ways the Wyrd can touch the lives of humanity it feels wrong that Fae-Touched only come about in these rather particular circumstances. I wonder if there aren't other, Fae-Touched like humans out there, exposed to the Wyrd in a somewhat different way with a similar outcome (similar to how sometimes a mortal can apparently be Ensorceled without ever making a Pledge).

                                Then too, there's the question of what happens to a Fae-Touched who's Changeling can't muster the memory to leave Arcadia? Do they still develop a Seeming? Since the majority of Changelings never make it back, that potentially means that the Fae-Touched outnumber the changelings, right? What happens to a Fae-Touched if their Changeling exceeds their Wyrd and becomes a True Fae? On that note, can the True Fae manifest their own Fae-Touched?

                                                    05-27-2015, 07:35 PM
                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    Not that many people are taken to Arcadia in the first place
                                                    I agree with the point that Fae-Touched are rare (although, defining "rare" in terms of a supernatural population that only exists as a fraction of a percent of the human population to begin with is a whole other issue), but do we actually know this? At least, in terms of those Changelings who escape? Going by 1E, Fae are bound to give their victims an opportunity to get the heck out of dodge, but they don't by any means need to make it easy. I've heard people speculate that for every Changeling who gets out, there could be ten or more left behind in Arcadia. It doesn't sound entirely unreasonable, given how much the Fae need their victims.

                                                    And from that point, the Fae-Touched start to diverge dramatically from the Changelings in a Freehold. If you were to construct a Venn Diagram of Fae-Touched and Changelings who escaped together, it would necessarily be small for all the reasons you described. But while becoming a Fae-Touched is unlikely, becoming a Changeling (at least, a post-Arcadia escapee) requires a Changeling to go through almost everything a Fae-Touched has to go through.

                                                    Finally, yeah, not all people who are taken are going to have others who have made deep, meaningful promises to them. But I wouldn't say that those who have are going to be "outliers". We humans are social beings, I would say the overwhelming majority of us rely on others in a whole range of different ways. Close ties to other human beings, to family and close friends and significant others, are kind of the norm aren't they? Narratively, yeah, we tend to depict them as something special, and they are certainly something special for those involved, but they're not unique by any stretch of the imagination.

                                                    I guess what I'm kind of grappling with here is that for every question you ask, I could ask a similar one about a Changeling. We kind of accept the existence of Changelings as the norm, the default that exists so that the Chronicle may happen, but see:

                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    Of those who do, not all of them will go looking for the missing promise
                                                    Of those who are taken to Arcadia, how many will actually try to escape?
                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    Of those who do, not all of them will ever make it into the Hedge
                                                    Of those Changelings who do try, how many will ever make it out of Arcadia and into the Hedge?
                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    Of those who do, not all of them will ever make it back out again
                                                    The Hedge is dangerous. The same goes for Changelings.

                                                    So yes, absolutely, Fae-Touched are rare as all heck. But so are Changelings. Freeholds may not be aware of the existence of all the Fae-Touched in their area, but Fae-Touched could easily rival the Changeling population in raw numbers. Probably a little smaller than a 1-to-1 ratio on account of those that would avoid Changelings or never encounter them after becoming Fae-Touched, but still a comparable number.

                                                    Of course, the added caveat that "there are as many as you want for a Chronicle" goes double in a world where the Wyrd exists, but rationally I see each as likely as the other.
