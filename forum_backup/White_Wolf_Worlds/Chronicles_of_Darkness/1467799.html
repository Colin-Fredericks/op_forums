    Hedge Help please

    11-14-2021, 12:53 PM
    So, due to a improbable series of events, it seems I will be running a Changeling game. Of course, my players seem to particularly interested in Hedge exploration, which means I'm going to need to know all the Hedge stuff backwards and forwards. Naturally I have some questions. Unfortunately I'm not sure they qualify as simple, but if they are I will move them to that thread.

    For reference, I only have Changeling 2e and Oak, Ash, and Thorn as my books but I'm trying to scavenge anything I can off of the internet. Thanks Satchel, the hedge navigation examples thread was very helpful.

    So, how unstable is the Hedge? If I shape the Hedge in a particular area, let say a forest to be a glacier, and none comes near that spot , will the glacier remain there? or will the hedge eventually go back to normal? Does the Hedge shape itself to match the seasons, at least in freehold territory?

    How out there can the Hedge get, environment wise? Like, can I have a section of the Hedge that's a fantasy space like Super Mario Galaxy, with a airy void with tiny worlds you must fly through or like the surface of the moon, cheese and all? If a trod goes through it, to a murky forest, is there in obvious boundaries or does it seem like the small forest planet your going toward, the more the world seems to expand around you until it seems to overtake the sky?

    So hedge navigation is dangerous, especially at low Wyrd and Clarity. If a Motley wanted to go exploring, what advice would a moderately helpful and cooperative freehold give, and what supplies would be recommended? There was a Goblin Fruit in 1e called Brumblebulb, apparently that granted instant exit from the Hedge. Should I have that available to them, or something like it?
    If it turns out the players really enjoy the Hedge, should I try to bring the Magi of the Gilded Thorn as a 2e style group entitlement?

    How many failed navigations should I say before they wind up back in Arcadia? I know you can make a Hedgeway going to The Hedge, but can you make one going back out into Ironside, or do you have to locate a exit?

    How "safe" are Hollows in and of themselves? Does the Hedge shape itself inside the Hollow? The Hegde Navigation thread had an example where someone was rolling to get back and forth inside his own Hollow, is that something I should do?

    Is it feasible to have the Freehold have a town Hollow inside of the hedge or is that too risky?

    Is a valid place in the Hedge a church with that offers Sanctuary for anyone and anything, as long they obey respect the Sanctuary of others. I was thinking of Hobs being the stained glass window people, able to move and show messages, but unable to speak.

      Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
      For reference, I only have Changeling 2e and Oak, Ash, and Thorn as my books but I'm trying to scavenge anything I can off of the internet. Thanks, the hedge navigation examples thread was very helpful.
      Glad it could help!

      (I will be getting back to that thing again sometime in the next couple of weeks, but I've been prepping for life stuff for the past several months. Will get to those mortal examples soon.)

      So, how unstable is the Hedge? If I shape the Hedge in a particular area, let say a forest to be a glacier, and none comes near that spot , will the glacier remain there? or will the hedge eventually go back to normal? Does the Hedge shape itself to match the seasons, at least in freehold territory?
      If nobody shapes it away, the glacier will stay where it is. The Hedge may shape itself such that the glacier goes away, but the Hedge only shapes itself in response to stimuli and most places are more stable than the Thorns, i.e. a change made in a place where nobody goes very often will probably still be there when you come back to visit. (Unless you were followed, of course.)

      The Hedge is liable to shape itself to match the seasons largely in response to Seasonal courtiers being active in those areas; minor aesthetic changes are easy to come by as the Hedge reacts, and the Hedge shaping itself is (among other things) 2e's equivalent of the mechanics in 1e that established how far out and for how long other characters could track you in the Hedge by the psychoreactive shifts in the environment that followed you. The environment twists to reflect its inhabitants, especially the magically potent ones.

      How out there can the Hedge get, environment wise? Like, can I have a section of the Hedge that's a fantasy space like Super Mario Galaxy, with a airy void with tiny worlds you must fly through or like the surface of the moon, cheese and all? If a trod goes through it, to a murky forest, is there in obvious boundaries or does it seem like the small forest planet your going toward, the more the world seems to expand around you until it seems to overtake the sky?
      Generally speaking, the spaces of the Hedge are labyrinthine and obscured enough to hide the draw distance. Environments that don't resemble the surface of a planet are more the demesne of Arcadian follies, though there's little stopping you from coming across patches of Hedge where gravity doesn't work normally, especially in high-altitude regions or places out to sea and in the dark; spaces where you can routinely do the impossible are a bit of a stretch, but if you can reasonably see something fitting into the same space as Environmental Tilts it can probably be persistent part of the locale.

      So hedge navigation is dangerous, especially at low Wyrd and Clarity. If a Motley wanted to go exploring, what advice would a moderately helpful and cooperative freehold give, and what supplies would be recommended?
      Short. Trips. The place is built to isolate, trap, and enslave you, underneath the breadth of things you can track down in there, and the more you can take advantage of that second part by better matching the Old Gods in power, the more you can beat it away from the first part.

      Stick together. Have a guide, preferably one who maintains high Clarity and has a kith or entitlement bonus that lets them Hedgespin to help you out.

      Stay on the path. Take advantage of trods, move through freehold territory whenever you can, and know the route you're taking if it's not your first trip.

      Be prepared to compromise and/or spend resources — taking on Goblin Debt to speed your way or giving up on your initial destination if you get Lost are both real possibilities verging on inevitabilities if given enough time.

      Stay positive and don't rush. The Hedge is a predator and it can smell fear. Self-explanatory.

      Have a ride if you can get one. Magic horses and whatnot can generally outpace the Hedge, and less of you on foot means less footpaths to get tangled up in the Briars.

      Be specific in your goals and have a backup plan. It's less disastrous if you fail to navigate to the Blue Alley Trod and have to circle around to the Drawbridge Clearing Hedgeway than if you get lost and have to reorient yourself before you can find Any Exit At All.

      There was a Goblin Fruit in 1e called Brumblebulb, apparently that granted instant exit from the Hedge. Should I have that available to them, or something like it?
      Frankly, I'd just encourage them to buy Wyrd dots if they intend to mess around in the Hedge a lot. Being inconvenienced by personal oaths, token drawbacks, Bedlam, Hedge-borne paradigm shifts, and Clarity damage should all provide a ready supply of Beats to bootstrap their Magic Stat upwards.

      Alternately, take a cue from Dark Eras 2's Mysterious Frontiers writeup and have them build a freestanding doorway, if they're not too fussed about where they come out.
      If it turns out the players really enjoy the Hedge, should I try to bring the Magi of the Gilded Thorn as a 2e style group entitlement?
      It certainly couldn't hurt.

      How many failed navigations should I say before they wind up back in Arcadia? I know you can make a Hedgeway going to The Hedge, but can you make one going back out into Ironside, or do you have to locate a exit?
      You don't have to locate an active Hedgeway, but making a new one back to Earth will require you to find (or, again, build) a closeable portal you can fit through, and generally those portals look the same in the Hedge as they do outside of it (i.e. a wooden door is a wooden door, a manhole is a manhole, a tent flap is a tent flap, etc).

      How "safe" are Hollows in and of themselves? Does the Hedge shape itself inside the Hollow? The Hegde Navigation thread had an example where someone was rolling to get back and forth inside his own Hollow, is that something I should do?
      Per the description of the Merit, a Hollow is by definition at least not in the Thorns and therefore, at worst, as plastic as the rest of the Hedge. Being inside one makes it harder to find out about you or track you down, which makes them safer for someone trying to avoid discovery in a place where finding you is as simple as succeeding at the process of finding you, but nothing about the inside of a Hollow intrinsically prevents you from getting hurt inside it.

      (For the record, the example of Hospitaller Bill involved a rush to get a patient from the front gate of his Hollow to a secure operating room; if he was just trying to get there to show off the place to a visitor it wouldn't have been a roll at all, but the same urgency that made that rush harder was what made the chance of failure interesting.

      Is it feasible to have the Freehold have a town Hollow inside of the hedge or is that too risky?
      Size Matters 2 is right there as far as scale goes, but the five-dot limit on Hollow means it may be infeasible as anything more than a general meeting space, especially given that benefiting from it requires a character to buy into it as a Motley Merit even if the "extra" dot does nothing.

      Practically speaking a large bit of Hedge infrastructure is more likely to be a series of Hollows connected through Route Zero and/or linked along one or more Stable Trods.

      Is a valid place in the Hedge a church with that offers Sanctuary for anyone and anything, as long they obey respect the Sanctuary of others. I was thinking of Hobs being the stained glass window people, able to move and show messages, but unable to speak.
      Aesthetically, yes, although I'd ask on what strength that offer is enforced and to what degree — hospitality oaths are generally an individual affair, and the most prominent place in which a general "make no trouble" agreement is in place is the average Goblin Market, so knowing what the catch is is paramount.

              Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post

              By all means.

              Oh, so if the Hedge decides you need a little excitement while in your Hollow, it can still harass you? I take it actually sleeping in the Hedge is a really bad idea.
              Interacting with the Hedge prompts a roll for Hedgespinning in a Hollow the same as anywhere else, but sleeping in the Hedge isn't likely to cause you problems from the act of sleeping so much as the fact that you're unconscious in a place that can change unpredictably — your own Hollow will generally be safe, but sleeping up a tree on a commonly-used path that isn't a trod is inviting trouble. (To say nothing of the prospect of a Huntsman just walking up and taking the freebie.)

              Hmm, I was thinking to be allowed in you must swear or the doors won't open. Breaking it might just throw you out with whatever you tried to claim Sanctuary from free to do what they want. I'd say while on the Church grounds there no physical violence, anything else goes. That's basically in theme right?
              Sounds about right. Keep mechanisms in mind for this unless there's some seriously powerful mojo backing it — a case could be made that this thing is basically a powerful Hollow with some extra stuff layered on top, and Hollows are already tricky to get into as-is.

              On that note, how does Goblin Market "make no trouble" oaths work? Do most have guards on the Trods or Hedgeways that ask people to swear an oath? Or does someone agree to a oath just by entering?
              For Goblin Markets it's not an oath so much as a rule enforced by bouncers. People are there to bargain, and they're to bargain with goods and services rather than naked threats — getting into an altercation that isn't part of a deal will typically have you made to stop (or at least take it outside). Even the True Fae will generally respect the sanctity of the Market, if for no other reason than it's more trouble than it's worth to find a new buyer or seller.
