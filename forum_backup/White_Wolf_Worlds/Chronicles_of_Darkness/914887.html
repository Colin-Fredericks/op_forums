    Going through the mirror- alternate universe hooks

    06-15-2016, 12:59 PM
    So, we have an entire supplement based on the idea of alternate universes, we have two editions of ChroD, we have oWoD, we have alternate timelines in Demon, and we have countless alternate universe and alternate mechanics proposals on this very forum.

    What I want to know is, has anyone ever run a campaign where the characters ended up traveling between universes? ChroD is big on the idea that there are unknowns even for supernatural creatures- Wicked Dead, Glimpses of the Unknown, and so on were all about taking supernaturals out of their comfort zones. And the breakdown of reality is a common blue book theme, especially for GMC. So has anyone thrown their players through the mirror?

    Imagine a group of Arisen winding up in an alternate universe where the Rite of Return was performed in South America (shamelessly ripped off from the non-Iremite mummies thread). Or a group of Awakened mages ending up in the world of Ascension somehow. Rumors has plenty of nods to alternate universes, has anyone done anything with those? Imagine a vampire waking from Torpor to find that there's a Clan he doesn't remember, but everyone acts like it's always been there. Or there's one missing. And so on.

                              The Best Of All Possible Worlds

                              The characters find themselves in a world where everything they ever hoped seems to have happened. (Which is different for every group, of course.) Mortal investigators have the answers they seek. Humane vampires can be cured, or control their conditions. Less humane vampires can lord it over humanity with impunity. Prometheans have the knowledge to perfect the Great Work. Hunters are lauded as heroes battling the supernatural threat, even defeating it once and for all. This can be a minor change that only effects them, or a huge rewrite of the whole world. (See Marvel's House Of M for an example of the latter.)

                              How do they respond? If they're suspicious, they're on the right track...

                                  Building a Better Tomorrow

                                  A shard has opened, and the shard is a fascinating one, to say the least. The supernatural is revealed, but instead of being a danger or sparking conflict it's accepted and a normal part of life. Vampires aid in medical testing by expertly analyzing blood samples and as changeling dream-divers act as licensed therapists. Shapeshifters serve as a special operation division of many police departments sniffing out illegal drugs and magical objects as mage orders act as fraternal organizations. Paranormal beings even hold public office, with several mayors either being werewolves, vampires, as prometheans make a living as semi-trailer drivers. Hell, the latest craze is the use of magical enchantments to put a coral reef in your own front yard to cultivate. Unfortunately, some wiseass kid mage decided to make magical jellyfish that infest them. Hunters, however, are the worst placed. While many conspiracies have carried over as official organizations under government control, many compacts and independent cells are seen as little more than hate groups.

                                  What happens if a character gets trapped in a world where their problems might be solved and replaced by dozens of new ones? And is it possible these hunter-terrorists might be this world's broken clock?

                                          Lost and Found

                                          Strange equipment is recovered from the bottom of a lake. Research is taken to an abrupt halt when everyone in the laboratory disappears. Their families grieve, and are forced to continue living without answers. When the hardware is dismantled to gather dust in a warehouse it seems to be the end of the project. A small few never give up hope for closure and their persistence is rewarded when two decades later local analogue TVs begin receiving transmissions.

                                          Strange equipment is recovered from the bottom of a lake. Research leads to a catastrophe, as everyone in the building is replicated only for the two versions to haphazardly fuse with each other. Most don't survive the trauma. A few lucky ones are one surgery away from resuming their life - this time with a twin they never had. The Institute is torn by in-fighting concerning the future fate of the project. Many need to reshape their lives and provide some explanation to their families. Many require constant medical assistance to survive in their fused state. When evidence comes to light that the experiment created an unstable timeline and there is no way of telling which one is which, all work is directed towards making contact and restarting the project on the other side.

                                          It won't be easy. The technology is alien and prone to unforeseen effects. Both scientists and investors are divided about the right way to proceed. Survivors are faced with the fact that they have other families. The less scrupulous use the resources for their own purposes. Also, once the unstable timeline is determined, the world might face an evacuation on an unprecedented scale.

                                              Hidden in the untamed wilds of the world and in the shadows of mankind's concrete prisons are the vines and flowers of the God-Tree, a gestalt consciousness of alien flora that dreams to reclaim the earth. It works through mortal servants and through its Angels, who hide the God-Trees countless Roots behind deceptive covers and far away from prying eyes. Its works push through the cracks of cities and sing beneath the soil of forests, conspiring to spread its spores and carpet everything in a sea of green.

Infrastructure doesn't hum with the sounds of machinery, they're grown over with algae, moss, and kudzu. The Unchained of this Shard are still on the run, but face further unwanted attention as their human Covers degrade over time, unnatural fungi growing within them before bursting forth like horrific cordyceps.
