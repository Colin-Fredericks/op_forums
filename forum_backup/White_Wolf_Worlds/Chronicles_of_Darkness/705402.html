    Vampires in the Neolithic

    10-17-2015, 01:43 PM
    I've been pondering about vampires back in that age, with far smaller population numbers to draw on. A single village would likely only house one or two vampires, and if another tried moving in, it'd likely be very brutal.

    So vampires would face themselves as threats, and have to be extremely careful in their feeding to not draw suspicion. Without the heavy politics and scheming of modern day nights though, what sort of challenges would a vampire face, long term, beyond those already listed?

      Yeah, I'd definitely expect that larger populations of kindred would be rare if at all present. I'd expect to see more open and symbiotic relationships between kindred and humans. I'd picture cults of worship. I'd picture warring tribes where the vampire lends his people unprecedented destruction on the battlefield and the path of conquest lends him plenty of vitae. I'd picture more nomadic kindred forced to conserve their energy and strike at varying settlements from afar to avoid getting hunted down.

      If I were doing a neolithic vampire game, I'd probably lean towards a barbarian fantasy angle. Mages and werewolves would probably feature prominently as threats. It would almost certainly necessitate a game where the character/s are forced to travel, because, well, the stone age village in the middle of nowhere likely wouldn't be an enthralling setting for very long.

                                              I think the idea that vampires as we understand them don't exist in the neolithic era fits well with the lore. Kindred arose in tandem with the rise of proper civilization and are a parasitic reflection of that growth. It's probably the route I'd take, but I could see early versions of the clans existing if someone wanted to take a different route. Here's how I'd see them fitting in.

                                              Nosferatu:For generations, they'd walk out into the wilderness. It was a dream-plague. They'd speak of nightmares, visions growing in intensity until finally, in the dead of night, they'd venture out barefoot. The villagers would later find their corpses strewn out, drained of blood. In desperation, they turned to sacrifice. Once a season the weakest among them is led out to the caves no one dares venture into. For now, this seems to placate the Beast.

                                              Mekhet: It's a region more advanced than most, blessed with fertile land, supporting a strip of settlements with maintained but minimal contact. When they do communicate, it's with whispers of a shared specter, a creature that walks among the shadows. It can appear in any home, and while bodies are sometimes found, most just bless the fact that these incidences are rare, that those who suffer the night visitations are lucky to wake up in a weakened state rather than dead.

                                              Ventrue: For generations, their slaughter goddess has brought them prosperity. While other tribes are forced to toil over their livestock, they merely take what they desire. They pour into villages in the dead of night, confident in the knowledge that the crude tools of their enemies have little power over their queen. That she limits their use of fire in the dead of night is a small price to pay. They've learned to adapt, to see better in the dark than they should be capable of, and the best of them are blessed with strength no mere mortal should possess.

                                              Daeva: They share in her blood, and she in theirs, and this is the way it's been for as long as they can remember. Those who have tried to make contact with them whisper of their lunacy, of fanatic blood orgies, and those few who have dared come with arms were sent back in pieces.

                                              Gangrel: Their livestock goes missing. This is hardly unusually, merely the price of life in early civilization. Yet these animals don't represent attacks by any predators they know. Yet, sometimes farmers go missing. They've sent out parties before, armed with makeshift spears and torches. Most nights they've seen nothing, but occasionally they'll catch sight of a creature that walks like a men but has the face of a beast. For a while, the killings abate, but whatever it is, it always returns.
