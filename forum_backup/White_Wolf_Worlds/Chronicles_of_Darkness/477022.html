          If your pack already went through a serious ordeal to get the Rite, I don't see any reason not to translate it fairly directly. Instead of Harmony, pick an Attribute + Skill dice pool. Rolls still take 15 minutes apiece, but you only get as many tries to roll as you have dice in your pool. If you don't hit your attempted target in the allotted time, the Rite fails, the Essence spent dissipates, and the target's regeneration is not supercharged and thus no special healing happens.
          As to symbols, I'd say cleansing, cycles, direction, and close ties.

          The Rite is still useful as aggravated damage otherwise takes 4 days per damage to heal and there's no other way around it. Even at the low end of this Rite, that's four times faster. The reason it was left out is to make aggravated damage seem like that much more of a big deal. I might still leave it out of the game, but since you've already had a reasonably big story to get it, I see no reason for your group to lose it.

                                              On the Pack Rite or Wolf Rite question: Personally, I don't understand the distinction at all. They were split into two groups, but I don't know why, and am not sure it matters. There's that one little side bar about how some Wolf-Blooded (and maybe some other spirit attuned people) can perform Pack Rites. If that's the main distinction and the distinction matters to you, I'd say call it a Pack Rite. That way, one of your Wolf-Blooded could run it on wolves that had Essence available. Also, I do think someone with Wolf's Meat could benefit from this Rite as their body works in almost the same way. They just couldn't spend the Essence.
                                              For the rest of Wolf-Blooded, I'd leave it questionable. Maybe one of your people is up to all-but-one Agg and a bashing and you're in a semi-dangerous situation (but have an hour or so reprieve). The Rite is all you've got, see what happens... (So with this one, I'm running with the ideas from the Regeneration essay in the Storyteller section of the book on page 286. Stories get interesting when the rules are not so stable and set in stone)

                                              Four symbols per Rite is more a rule of thumb than anything. A quick perusal showed that a lot have four. Fetish has five. Four isn't a magical number, it's just plenty for guidance while still allowing you to come up with compelling, personalized Rites.

                                              On the Licking symbol Flailbot caught my drift. I think the original Rite suggested would licking or some other cleansing action, hence I suggested cleansing.

                                              I think Rite time intervals are more standardized now. I'm not sure where I saw this, though, so I don't have a page reference. That said, so long as the time interval is standardized for the Rite (and you're not just throwing a new interval at the players each time all willy-nilly) you should be fine.
                                              More than I remember seeing a chart for this sort of thing I remember the general Extended Action rules saying "think about how long this sort of action should take overall and set the time scale based on that." From that perspective, the difference between 10 and 15 minutes doesn't mean a whole lot, unless you're in my aforementioned "we've only got an hour before the shit hits the fan again" scenario.

                                              Something I forgot to mention at first - Failed Rolls (no successes on one specific rolls) should probably offer the Stumbled Condition. Exceptional Success (overall) should grant the Uratha performing the Rite the Symbolic Focus Condition.

                                              Last thing: leaving this Rite out isn't so much a matter of Balance as it is an emphasis on the theme that, despite how powerful Werewolf regeneration is, there are still things out there that can mess you up right and proper. Aggravated damage is dangerous and will leave you screwed if you're not still careful. It might not be bad to have the Bale Hounds do something that makes the Rite not function anymore; maybe have a story in which the characters arive just as the Hounds are finishing some weird ritual, then have the Rite auto-fail next time they try to use it. Having this Rite and then losing it would really drive home the point. I'd apply Sanctity of Merits before too long, though.
