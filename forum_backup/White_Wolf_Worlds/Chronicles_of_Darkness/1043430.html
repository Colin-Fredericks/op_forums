    Antagonists & Sniper Rifles

    02-04-2017, 05:16 AM
    Here is my dilemma:

    For my tabletop group's Beast the Primordial Chronicle I'm going to be introducing a brother & sister Band of Heroes to serve as complications to the mystery the Brood has already set out to investigate in our last session. The sister Hero is a social themed antagonist who's main threat to the Brood comes from her rallying supporters against the Brood and social maneuvering to turn former allies into enemies and current enemies into supporters of the Band's cause. The brother Hero on the other hand is a military themed antagonist who is a trained soldier with skill as a sniper who's goal is to spy upon the Brood from afar and work alongside his sister to set up the battleground for when the Band goes in for the kill.

    The problem with this set up comes from the lack of agency the players will receive when going up against an antagonist that 1) if done right they won't have any idea exists until it's too late, and 2) the anticlimactic nature of having a player character in a horror story get killed by a sniper. It's a similar conundrum to making an antagonist who uses bombs to murder people, if done correctly then there should be very little chance of the target being able to stop them but that also means robbing the player characters of the agency needed to make an engaging story for the whole group.

    And this is where I need advice: how can I keep this cool idea but implement it in a way that's believable (these are Heroes after all, they need to be willing to do anything to kill their quarry) but also keeps the player's agency that's essential to tell a good horror story (in that unique way where the player character happily dooms themselves by investigating the scream that came from the woods during the night.)

    Any suggestions or advice would be greatly appreciated. Thank you.

              Heroes don't exist in a vacuum either, the brother could have a close friend getting increasingly worried about him and his developing obsession with the players. After a last attempt at talking him out of killing an ostensibly random person, the friend still can't inform the authorities due to loyalty, but resultingly feels compelled to warn the players.

              The players are given information that builds tension - sniper out to headshot you soon - but the friend's loyalty means they won't want to put the Hero in danger, so no other info is forthcoming.

              So they know to be careful, but also have to decide on how to deal with this person now, because obviously more information could be extracted through various means, leading to Hero reacting to his friend's situation, and so forth

                      Additionally, use the sniper in a more dramatic setting. Give the characters a chance to see a dramatic red dot before the shot hits a vital ally of them, while inside a small building with few exits, ideally all on the same side.                           Don't be afraid to use ST fiat (also known as the Black Beast of Convenience) to set the stage, as long as you don't abuse it.
                          ​For example; the sniper has set up his nest to take out a player as their character goes about his predictable, daily routine. He fires the shot but at that exact moment a purse snatcher pursued by cries of 'stop thief' slams into the target, intercepting the shot and taking one for the team.
                          ​The character goes down, dead purse snatcher atop him as an ignorant crowd drawn by the cries encircles them forming an unwitting shield around the target.
                          ​The police arrive to investigate the murder and determine that it was done by a high powered rifle (they even find the nest), the Beast knows some of this because the police question him extensively on the possibility that he was the actual target.
                          ​Now the player knows they should be taking precautions against a sniper and the police are involved further complicating the Heroes work.

                          ​It just occurred to me that if the Heroes have reason to suspect their first target is part of a larger group they might botch the initial attempt on purpose so they can see where and to whom the Beast flees.

                              The PC need auxiliary several support characters in their lives, people whom the PCs really like and enjoy role play interactions with.

                              Also in my opinion there needs to be a beloved sacrificial lamb. Remember the sniper only gets a single shot before the PCs know there is danger. I'd choose the obvious leader of the group, the one that is best respected by the group and well liked. That person suffers the sniper shot arbitrated fairly by all applicable mechanics which I would have neatly written out before hand on a sheet of paper. That PC should be shot in a way without any plot-tonium on either side.

                              Likely the PC will not survive... to tragic survivor-guilt invoking vengeance worthy drama. That could be fairly epic if handled just right ,or fall right on its face if not. Your mileage may vary.

                              If the character does not survive having the cast of mortal auxiliary support characters in their lives basically gives you a menu to offer the player and say please pick one and they'll become a Beast to become your new character. The NPC should have mortal based XP roughly equal to the PCs and just the beginning of the template but could provide an interesting play style.

                              You even could if you're early enough in the Chronicle have the Players make these 2 or 3 of NPCs each and let them know they are to be involved in their lives and might serve as replacement PCs if a PC dies.

                              That could be half a dozen sessions before the proposed "incident".

                              Hyper-agency and plot-immunity can be as bad as hypo-agency, and death as disempowering factor in a story can be very powerful and done in a way which is meaningful. I mean otherwise you might end up with a situation like these Walls of Fallen Heroes.

                              Also this shakes up the social dynamic, introduces new themes, and acts as a moment.

                              Now I would have some subtle foreshadowing, cautionary tales snipers an IEDs on the news playing in the background about the war on terror... Fate 0 playing in the background at an anime kids place, little motif details like that. Possibly hearing a rumor that is what a famous school shooting was really about, a couple Heroes killing Beasts... you know dark and ironic stuff.
