    I'm probably recapping a lot of things people have said earlier here (Atamajakki is right about the five covenants all, in some way, embodying way of dealing with becoming a vampire.) but first and foremost, as ElvesOfZion said, it's not really about better or worse. It's about difference. And difference and variety a big thing for the covenants of Requiem, especially, but not uniquely, In 2E.

    The Carthians are often firebrands opposing the status quo, but fundamentally they represent a challenge to the idea of elders ruling over younger vampires and accept that democracy has a place. They're also much more attuned to the modern world in many books/portrayals - part of the reason many Carthians opposed elder supremacy is, well, those guys can't use Google! In these days of catfishing. Ubers and other services, it's never been easier to get a meal without hitting the streets and leaving the safety of your haven - humans have so much to teach Kindred.
    The Movement grew from political experimentation, and this is still part of their ethos. They ape different human power structures, most importantly when they do run the show, which (as has been mentioned) can as varied as, say, a socialist commune or a neoconservative, Randian society focused on individualism, freedom and selfishness. The Carthians are occasionally also shown to be adapting the world to themselves. Did you think it was a coincidence that vampires have become sexy antiheroes and romantic leads in fiction rather than leering monsters? The Movement's been chipping away at the stone for some time (admittedly not solely). Heck, in Danse Macabre they were recast as wanting to eventually tear down the Masquerade (this is in no way canon, though with VtR's toolkit approach it could be), influencing human culture as mentioned and also having experimental villages where vampires lived openly,

    The Invictus are the status quo, but their system has stood the test of time for a reason. Elder rule the roost, sure, but the 1E sourcebook on them really explained how the Invictus society functions. The courtly ettiquette and political jockeying for power helps to keep elders grounded and lets vampires express their competitive, predatory urges without literally tearing one another apart. Why would younger vampire join them? The first reason is not just their wealth and influence, but a system of patronage. Sure, the elders run the show, but their favoured ancillae and neonates get access to their resources, their knowledge, their support and protection. Another huge part of the Invctus is that they, more than any other Covenant, are the conspiracy of silence. They're the Masquerade's fiercest defenders and many vampires are prepared to support, or fully sign up, for this alone.

    The Lancea are an interesting one. They're a Christian offshoot, not quite Catholic I'd argue (though variety within it relating to human subdivisions of Christianity never gets enough focus IMO) and they proclaim themselves holy predators,God's scourge against man. They're religiously empowered to be monsters! But with limits - they embody the 'Beast I am lest a Beast I become' more than any other Covenant. A good example is that the Lancea might spare good Christians, or might tempt them and punish them, like Job, to test their faith. They offer a reason and a place for vampires, and they don't want vampires to give in to the Beast. The most likely vampires to try to prevent you from becoming draugr are probably Santified. Also their Theban miracles can be convincing.That said, they're pretty happy to destroy anything that contradicts their official histories, especially in 2E. Something in 1e and 2e that's worth mentioning is that members of other Covenants may attend Midnight Mass and consider themselves Sanctified, though such dual-members are unlikely to be given any Theban lessons.

    The Circle, in both 1e and 2e, are incredibly diverse. They're inheritors of ancient traditons dating back thousands of years... and recent fads, all at once. The Crones in one city are likely to be entirely different to those in another, They share blood magic and a core belief that the vampire state isn't a curse, it's entirely natural and should be embraced (pun intended) and even shared, but not much else. I think the veneration of creation was played down a bit for 2e, though the vageuly matriarchal/strict gender roles is often ascribed to them to play up their pagan/witch sides, the diversity of different Crone cults makes even this far from a unifying concept. Their 'we're natural' pitch is a strong one too, though of course this can lead to them being far more accepting of Humanity loss...

    And the Dragons are pretty simple to grok, they're wanting to transcend their current state, with a mix of occultism and mad science. They also have a huge focus on evolving through struggle that makes them far more willing to endure burdens other vampires would consider insane, They like to experiment a lot, too, and they're sometimes prone to poking things just to see what happens. And, of course, the Coils strongly imply that their methodology works.

    Unaligned vampires are obviously individuals. I can see a lot of potential power for vampires who remain neutral, perhaps as arbiters, mercenaries, independent power-brokers the factions have to deal with, as well any non-joiners and Kindred 'losers'. There's always the potential for unaligned vampires to form local organisations, too - as has been mentioned, 2e provides examples of very local covenants that can be just as important, or more important, than any of the big five. There also places where the waters have muddied, occasionally even between the groups which seem the most obviously antagonistic (Circle/Lancea, Invictus/Carthian). . .

            RE: Defection

            In VTM, crossing the line from Camarilla to Sabbat was tantamount to suicide - only the strongest or most well connected even considered it and even then they would spend the rest of their unlives looking over their shoulders.

            In VTR, things are far more fluid and situational. There is no hard rule that says a vampire cannot jump from one covenant to another, and the fiction suggests this is not an uncommon practice, especially as the Vampire ages and their desires/motives change. However, expect bridges to be burned and feathers ruffled - most of the Covenants take a dim view of traitors (especially if there is a risk of mystic secrets being shared with the unworthy). Depending on the Vampires relationship to his Covenant and the exact circumstances of defection, it could be anything from a amicable split to a violent blood-vendetta.

            Having run both VTM and VTR extensively, the lack of monolithic world-spanning power blocks in VtR makes almost everything a bit more personal, which I consider a good thing.

            The Artificer's Workshop - A collection of Exalted Artifacts
            Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)
            The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
            Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
            Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities

                        Those aren't questions that have single answers. As you noted, the intent of the Covenants by default is to be "smaller" and more "local" than the VtM Sects.

                        While the major Covenants of Requiem are large spread philosophies, they are are varied as human philosophies. Just as Christianity is not the same everywhere you go, the Lancea et Sanctum is not the same everywhere you go. Local politics, customs, and culture matter more than what some other member of the Covenant is doing half a world away.

                        One city's Carthians might seek the destruction of a vampire seeking ultimate power over all other Kindred, while those in another might not care in the slightest as long as said vampire plays by the rules. If your lust for power can be sated without violating the laws of the undead, then you earned it.

                        Likewise the Invictus are not a monolith where all of them have a single opinion about an upstart power hungry non-Invictus vampire.

                                        The thing you have to understand about Vampire the Requiem is that everything - everything - is trends and generalities. Just like in real life, you can't say that anything is unilaterally true about a group of people (or in this case, a group of vampires). All you can talk about is how this group holds these ideals, or that group encourages that behavior, or members of this group are often like this. There will always be individual exceptions to everything.

                                        No, Covenant Status represents actual membership in the Covenant, and usually higher Status comes with more responsibilities, as well as greater influence. The rule in 2nd edition is that you can have Status in multiple Covenants, as long as your total Covenant Status isn't greater than 5, and your "primary" Covenant has the most dots. This basically represents the fact that the more influential you are in any Covenant, the less likely other Covenants are going to be to trust you in important roles within their organization. So you can dedicate yourself fully to one Covenant, or you can become a mid-ranking member of two, or you can become a lower-ranking member of three or four. Normally, you can't be a member of all five, but there is also a Carthian-only Merit that lets you ignore your Carthian Status for these purposes, so theoretically a Carthian could have a little bit of influence in every Covenant and still be an important figure within the Movement.

                                            RE: The Ordo

                                            Think of the Dragons as a direct analog to the Path of Metamorphosis. However, the Dragons differ in that they (generally) do not get hung up on physically expressing their inhuman nature. A Dragon would likely consider the Path of Metamorphosis an interesting, but somewhat crude approach to Transcendence. The Dragons seek to overcome the limitations imposed by the Curse of Vampirism. Once this is done, they will become something as far beyond vampire as Vampire is above Man.

                                            RE: Dual Membership

                                            By the rules, this is possibly, but will be heavily dependent on the campaign. Certain covenants are philosophically opposed enough that having status in both is unlikely ( Invictus/Carthian, Lancea/Crone) but stranger things have happened. Typically speaking, One covenant would take precedence over the other. In a domain where the Invictus and the Ordo share resources, A Status 3 Dragon might also hold Status 1 in the Invictus with no conflict of interest.

                                            RE: Local Changes

                                            They type of reception a Vampire receives when meeting up with covenant members in a different Domain is going to be based entirely on the domains in question (and more importantly, the Storyteller in question). The rules assume that each domain will be familiar enough to recognize the Covenants for what they are, but different enough that you can't take anything for granted. a Crone from a domain where the Crone are in charge is going to be in for a shock if they travel to a domain where the Crone are on the defensive.
                                            VTM was built heavily on stereotypes and had organizations with ideology that was very consistent on a global level. In VTR there are basic expectations, but the details can shift from domain to domain.

                                                            One thing I wanna toss out, since it doesn't seem to have come up, is that the contention between Covenants has dimmed down between Editions. As an example, the Carthians still casually point at the Invictus as their main example of why vampire society needs to change, but they also tend to appreciate that the system does neatly direct the Kindred condition into a stable community, and while the Invictus still see the Carthians as trying to fix a system that isn't broken, they do applaud the ways they've found to reinforce the standards of society effectively and quickly. In some cites, the old rivalry stands and they glare at each other from across the dinner table, but in other cities they work tightly together to create an thoroughly efficient, if at times brutal, vampire society, thick as thieves and reaping the benefits.

                                                            In Masquerade, the factions were much more fixed in their issues with one another, be they Sects or Clans. In Requiem, every city plays out a little differently, and more often than not, the Covenants actually do work together just as much as they backstab each other, using their silk gloves and iron gauntlets in equal measure with one another.
