    I hadn't considered the possibility that 2e might drop first-tier hunters, and I hope that it doesn't. To me, Practical Experience is the backbone of Hunter; and anyone who benefits from it is a hunter — including independent cells.

    As I see it, the "hook", as branford put it, lies in defining what Practical Experience is, who can get it and why, and what you can do with it. My answers to the questions:

    · it's a metagaming currency that exists solely allow hunters to grow and adapt to the supernatural quickly enough that they stand a chance of holding their own.
    · Those who take up the Vigil can earn Practical Experience, because they're the ones who face off against the supernatural and this can learn and adapt to it.
    · Practical Experience can give you edges that help you fight the supernatural, both mundane (such as Tactics) and supernatural (such as Endowments). Not everyone who can earn Practical Experience can spend it on the full spread of options, and the restrictions on what you can spend it on define which "tier" you're in., power-wise.

    More later; work calls.

                                                        I find it kinda weird how with almost 200 posts, no one has really touched on one of, if not the greatest weakness of the Awakened that Hunters can exploit up the wazoo: Hubris.

                                                        Yeah, Awakened have all kinds of ways to fuck over those lowly Hunter pests they might as well not bother with them, and that's where Hunters can get 'em. Oh, you filled the tall building you live in with all kinds of magical barriers and booby traps? How about that, I just blew up the main supports and made the entire building crumble with you in it. Did you just put yourself on a plane so that you can read anyone's mind for killing intent before they even have a chance to act on their plan? Too bad you didn't account for my little rocket launcher that I fired from the sea. Getting past your magical defenses must've been a fluke, huh? Yeah, keep thinking that while I proceed to Step "Owning Your Haughty Bitch-ass".

                                                        (I may or may not have recently watched Fate/Zero)

                                                        While on the subject of hubris, I feel that the whole attitude of "there's just no way to work around or cope with Quiescence, deal with it" is a major design flaw with Mage in general, not just on the subject of Hunters because it creates a disconnect with the theme of hubris. It's not hubris if there really is no way for ordinary folks to touch you, and ditching ordinary folks to bring on the Price of Hubris is quite the lost opportunity. After all, shock-dropping is a very effective means of delivering horror.

                                                        It's the very reason I'm still unnerved when facing even the tacky, hoop earring ReDeads of Wind Waker. You feel like a badass because all you have to do is simply whack enemies with your sword until they explode, then you meet one of them that takes it all away as it slowly approaches you. You can do the same thing to Mage players as Sleepers of all people suddenly have them in a bind, or are killing off their comrades with alarming frequency.
