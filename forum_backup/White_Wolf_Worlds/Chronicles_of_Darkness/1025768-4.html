                                                        I love the Open Dev process, but I'm less certain that it is a universal good. First, as Exalted 3e has shown dramatically, you need developer buy-in. A developer that doesn't really want to do it that's doing it because it's a big thing for the company (not all of Onyx Path stuff sees much Open Dev, it's more than the main line developers are into it so it feels very prominent when they're doing core books)... can do far more damage than good.

                                                        Second, it actually takes decent structuring and the fanbase to buy into it. Back when V20 came out, there was Open Dev, but the team really hadn't gotten the hang of it yet. A lot of stuff got messy and unproductive because it turned into a shouting match between people trying to get the dev team to make their ideal version of VtM instead of giving feedback on what the dev team was working on. It's a balancing act that takes time to learn.

                                                        I also think that there are other ways to achieve community interactions that can equal Open Dev that WWP is actively pursuing by pushing for curated community generated content instead. It's another way to achieve a lot of the community engagement (and more eyes/ears) benefits you get with Open Dev.

                                                        Another issue is that Open Dev's success stories tend to come from games with established communities working on new editions to existing games. You don't see nearly as many awesome Open Dev stories for new games. And in many ways WoD 5e is going to be a "new game" in how it's built compared to CofD 2e.
