    Mummy, Reworked - society, creed, judges, decrees

    02-21-2014, 08:07 AM
    So I've spent the last couple days thinking a lot about the setting of Mummy: the Curse, particularly in the context of the Guildhalls book. There are a couple things I'd like to change in both the setting and the system. I'd like to post this all here. Maybe an interesting discussion happens. Maybe someone else will find my ideas interesting and find some way to make his/her game better.

    I'll gladly explain why I changed these things the way I did. I'm not really interested in criticism along the line of "don't change what isn't broken", because customizing a game isn't really about that; it's about adapting the game so that it better fits the needs of an individual gaming group.

    So there!

      SOCIETY

      The largest unit of social interaction is the meret. There are no guilds. No nomes, no guildmasters, no authorities. If they don't work alone (as many are prone to do - some mummies have known each other for 6000 years and have lots of reasons to hate each other), they organize in small groups of like-minded mummies.

      The Y-Splat is not the guild. Mummies don't have guilds - the Nameless Empire might have had political factions, but noone really remembers anything about them. The Y-splat is not based on any sort of society that might or not might have existed in the Nameless Empire - it's sorta like the Refinement in Promethean in that it represents a mummy's way of dealing with its Arisen condition.

      In this setting hack, mummies ultimately remember and know far less about the Nameless Empire than they do in the official game. They know about the Shan'iatu and the Judges. They know that they've all done something to gain the attention of these beings, and that they've been chosen to serve for all eternity.

      Nothing substantial about the society of the Nameless Empire is known to mummies who have not gained Apotheosis.

          JUDGES

          The Judges are not the distant figures of the official setting. Their existence is not a matter of faith but fact. On occasion, they deign to talk to their servants. They give commands. They punish when the Arisen fail. On rare occasions, they can be bargained with.

          Sometimes, it's not a cult, but a Judge who sends a mummy back to life.

          The Judges need the Arisen - mummies are the only thing that ties them to the living world, a world in which Sekhem and magical resources are plenty.
          The Arisen need the Judges - it is the Judges who own their true names, who control their Sekhem, who give them purpose and meaning.

              CREED

              Replace the concept of "guild" with "creed".

              Mummies don't "join" creeds, because creeds are not societies - they are ways of dealing with the Arisen condition, the Descent, the lack of memory, their metaphysical status as servants of the Judges, etc.

              This also means that one's creed affiliation is not as fixed as one's guild, in the official game. Mummies can change their basic outlook anytime they choose to do so by spending a willpower dot.

              There are scarcely few Arisen who haven't been part of all 5 creeds at least one throughout the millenia.

              In the context of a game whose protagonists are (un)living metaphors for old age, the creeds represent ways of dealing with old age.

              Interactions between the various should be sources of conflict - the Su Menent don't like less conservative mummies; the Sesha Hebsu's focus on the past clashes with the Tef Aabhi's attempts to control the future; Maa Kep might try and force the staunchly individualist Mesen Nebu to join their numbers, etc.

                  SU MENENT

                  To be Arisen is to flirt with madness. The endless cycles of living, dying and being reborn to a world that has changed and will continue to change for all eternity requires minds and souls stronger than those some Arisen possess.

                  The Su Menent deal with the Arisen condition by focusing on the only things that remain constant throughout their entire existence - their personal gods, the Judges of Du'at.

                  The Shepherds of the Chamber are the traditionalists and dogmatics of the Arisen. They decide to put their entire faith in the Judges. All for the Judges, nothing for the self. They reclaim relics for the gods of Duat. They complete their first purposes - mandates of the gods - as soon as possible upon Arising. They police their peers. They police humans - remember how all of the 42 Judges have sins that are part of their purview? A Su Menent punishes those who sin against her individual Judge.

                  Occasionally, this means that the Su Menent make the World of Darkness a little better. Other times, they punish sins that have lost any meaning in the 21st century. The Su Menent are beholden to a concept of morality that was ancient at the time of the Old Kingdom.

                  Benefit: The Su Menent are the favored servants of the Judges of Duat; as thus, they get to enjoy supernatural benefits closed to other creeds. Shepherds have an easier time learning the darkest, most powerful affinities and utterances compared to the other creeds. The Judges sometimes earn Descent resets by acting as the agents of the Judges in the living world.

                  Weakness: all mummies are slaves to the Judges. The Su Menent *embrace* their status as slaves, choosing to obey the strange, divine wishes the gods. Sometimes your boss will tell you to do things you don't want to do. They always expect the Su Menent's absolute obedience in all things.

                      MESEN NEBU

                      Mesen Nebu live and die for themselves and themselves primarily. Their principal ethos is that of gaining power. A few Born of Gold cooperate, seeing merit in the concept of group efforts.

                      The mission of those Born of Gold is to accumulate wealth and power (mystical and otherwise) as efficiently as possible. Achieving their first purpose is a secondary goal that should be achieved as long as it doesn't distract from the quest to power, and to achieve a better position than their rivals in the eyes of their Judge.

                      The Mesen Nebu don't worship the Judges as the Su Menent do - they attempt to bargain. It serves the Mesen Nebu to do the bidding of the Judges, as it's the Judges who own their Sekhem - until it doesn't.

                      Some Mesen Nebu believe that they will one day buy their freedom from the Judges. There are different theories as to how to do that, or what price to pay - slaves buried alive? Relics? Sekhem? To be Born of Gold is to be optimistic that there is a way to achieve something better than slavery.

                      Benefit: the Mesen Nebu's cults are the most wealthy, most well connected and powerful ones. Their options are without limit. Mesen Nebu gain certain merits - resources, retainers, allies, contacts - at a startling pace.

                      Weakness: Those Born of Gold, as the most public and influential of the Arisen, have more enemies than other mummies. It's a dog eat dog world - other Mesen Nebu hate you, because they want your relics. Mummies on other creeds envy you because of what you have and they lack. Your cultists might rebel. The more powerful you are, the harder it gets to hold onto your wealth and power, especially in the context of the cycles of life, death and rebirth of the Arisen.

                          SESHA HEBSU

                          Sesha Hebsu look to the past for guidance. The Closed Books act as keepers of records. They collect information. They investigate their own past and seek to regain their memories.

                          All Closed Books seek to buy up their memory ratings, gaining sebayt experience whenever possible. Their first purpose usually comes secondary, unless fulfilling it allows them to gain information they need.

                          The Sesha Hebsu write and collect history. When asked questions about the past, it isn't always in their best interest to tell the truth. You don't tell your ally that you killed him and destroyed his cult three hundred years ago. Sometimes they lie, sometime they change history.

                          The Sesha Hebsu creed is not well loved by the Judges. It does not serve the interests of the gods of Duat for their servants to investigate their past, the Nameless Empire and the City of Pillars. They're the creed most likely to rebel openly against the Judges, earning the enmity of the more loyal Arisen, particularly the Su Menent.

                          Benefit: Mummies who want to know who they are and how they've spent the long, cold millenia, go to the Sesha Hebsu. It is the Closed Books who know the most about the past - their own and that of others.

                          Weakness: Other than a generally uneasy relationship with the Judges, the Closed Book's focus on the past sometimes comes at the expense of the present and future.

                              TEF AABHI

                              The Tef Aabhi grow weary of their undying nature. They've seen the world pass them by. They know how, ultimately, mummies are static creatures with little ability to adapt to a world that keeps changing and leaving them behind. The future is always in motion, while the Arisen will always be creatures of ancient past.

                              So the Tef Aabhi attempt to control that future. They attempt to soften the blow of culture clash and dying and being reborn in a world that has irrevocably changed every so often by attempting to exercise influence on how the world changes.

                              The Tef Aabhi try to build things that last forever. They infiltrate and influence society. They change the shape of history, building a future that exists for them, and in their image, without crumbling and fading away into the past. City-wide planning, eugenics, population control - all valid instruments in their quest to create a world that welcomes the Arisen instead of rejecting them.

                              Some Tef Aabhi would like nothing more than to resurrect the Nameless Empire. Others want a futuristic utopia that transcends the flaws of Irem. All of them try to take an active hand in directing the course of Fate and creating the future of their dreams.

                              The first purpose and the demands of the cults and the 42 Judges are sometimes uncomfortable necessities, on occasion, compatible with the goals of the Tef Aabhi. Some Tef Aabhi try to create a world that pleases their Judge, too. Many Tef Aabhi prefer to keep their Judge happy so as to earn additional time spent in the living world. It's a simple matter of practicality.

                              Benefit: the Tef Aabhi are the creed most in tune with the modern world and living humans, by simple necessity. You can't create the future of your desire if you don't interact with the present.

                              Weakness: the Tef Aabhi often lack the organisation and cooperation necessary to achieve their grandiose goals. Directing the future often requires more time and power than available to them. The Tef Aabhi focus on the present on the future also keeps them from learning from their own mistakes and those of history.

                                  MAA KEP

                                  The Maa Kep are the only creed that can rightfully be called a "guild". While mummies on other creeds work towards their goals individually or in small groups, the Maa Kep explicitly believe in group efforts, organisation and hierarchy.

                                  The Maa Kep claim to be the ideological heirs of an actual guild that existed in the Nameless Empire - a faction of overseers, slave-drivers and secret police. The Bearers of the Engraved seek to bring unity and order to the Arisen. They have a hierarchy - guild masters, artisans, journeymen and apprentices. They cooperate. They assist each other when serving their first purposes and the Judges of Duat.

                                  Individual positions on the Judges, the Arisen state of being and their purpose varies a lot among the Bearers of the Engraved - what defines them is their willingness to cooperate on a scale larger than that of the meret or the individual.

                                  Sometimes they force members of other creeds to join their guild. All for the greater good.

                                  Benefit: All Maa Kep can rely on the cooperation of their Arisen allies.

                                  Weakness: Assisting other mummies in need is mandatory.
                                  Mummies from other creeds serve only the Judges or their own interests - the Maa Kep take commands from those higher in the hierarchy of the guild, and are expected to obey.

                                      PILLARS

                                      Mummies don't meditate to regain their pillars. Time is too precious a resource for the Arisen to spend any of it on meditation. Mummies are creatures of action.

                                      All mummies regain 1 pillar point at the end of every scene. The storyteller decides which pillar is regained; based on the dramatic focus on the scene - Ab for interaction with humans, Ren for scenes of careful planning and deliberation, Ba for scenes of action and peril, Sheut for interaction with ghosts and supernatural creatures, Essence for everything else.

                                      The player may instead choose not to regain the offered point - the player will then get to choose the nature of the regained pillar point at end of the next scene.

                                      (Example: after a scene of intense interaction with ghosts, the storyteller offers a mummy character 1 point of Sheut. The mummy character may decide to take the point. Alternately, she can decide not to take it.
                                      At the end of the next scene, the mummy character may decide to regain any 1 point of pillar on her sheet).

                                      The point is, 1) the Arisen condition is governed by strange rules. Their power ebbs and flows according to rules as determined by their fivefold souls; and 2) mummies may decide to arrange circumstances so as to maximise their ability to regain their source of power. But it isn't easy.

                                          DECREE
                                          As said above - mummies are creatures of action - their souls thrive on success and achievement.
                                          A mummy's decree and defining pillar governs his existence more than the other four parts of his soul - it's the part of him that keeps him going on.
                                          In game terms, the decrees each encourage a certain modus operandi and style of running a mummy character. Succeeding while playing a character a certain way will cause a mummy to regain his powers more quickly.
                                          Replace the various ways the decrees regain pillars as per the official system by the following:
                                          Bull-Headed: Regain all missing Ka pillar points when reaching meaningful goals by persistence and patience.

                                          Falcon-Headed: Regain all missing Ba pillar points when reaching meaningful goals by improvisation and instant action.

                                          Jackal-Headed: Regain all missing Ab pillar points when reaching meaningful goals by intense and dramatic interaction with ghosts or the Lifeless.

                                          Lion-Headed: Regain all missing Ab pillar points when reaching meaningful goals by intense and dramatic interaction with living mortals.

                                          Serpent-Headed: Regain all missing Ren pillar points when reaching meaningful goals as a consequence of careful planning and deliberation.
                                          Pillar points gain be regained that way only once per session.

                                              Thoughts, in order:

                                              Society: For mummies, the scale of their society is something I actually see changing over time. A lot of the time, they'll be consumed by the relatively solitary interests as they're woken by their cults or other random factors - only crossing paths when they're interested in the same thing - under these circumstances, even merets may have trouble enduring. But in a Sothic turn - especially with the suggestion that fate may conspire in getting them to encounter one another - more rules governing their interactions may be called for. I could actually see mummies having what amounts to two social orders - one as you describe, and one more in line with Guildhalls that asserts itself when mummies awake en masse.

                                              Judges: I like the sound of all this. Maybe a major incidence the appropriate Judge's jurisdiction of sins can be sensed by a sleeping mummy, similarly to how a waking one can sense a relic within a certain range.

                                              Creed: Are you still tying the Creeds to types of relic? My own view of the Guilds (from the core book) is that they were social societies (and might still function that way in a Sothic rising - cf what I said about a different social order at such times), but most of the time function more psychologically - like Creeds - than socially. But, in the old age parallel, what you've been is an inescapable part of what you are now - hence keeping it hard to change them as easily as a Promethean (who's exploring their new being) changes Refinement.

                                              Pillars: I like it. I'd favour coming up with a more definied category of scene for Essence (so a recovery will not be at the end of every scene, but many or even most). I don't know what category I'd choose, though - perhaps scenes of endurance and perseverence, to tie in with what you suggest for Decree.

                                              Decree: I also like this.
