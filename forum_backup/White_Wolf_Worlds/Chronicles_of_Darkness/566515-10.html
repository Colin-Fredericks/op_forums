        Originally posted by Darksider View Post
        Or the Tengu and Strix are roughly analogous to Forsaken and Pure. Different members of the same family.
        The key problem with this is that the Strix are like the Kindred in that the term exists as an umbrella for a bunch of similar creatures that have gotten more similar over time through mutual assembly and consumption, whereas the Tengu are a type of supernatural being that resemble shadowy birds, possess corpses and vampires, and are thought by werewolves to be a class of spirit.

        None of the latter qualities are particularly uncommon in the supernatural world and both the eye color and the agenda are off-model for the Strix, to say nothing of how the Three and Four Diamonds writeup makes no actual mention of any peculiarities of behavior among the Tokyo Strix and Beast's writeup for Tokyo corroborates the Uratha's assessment of the Tengu's nature.

        If I had to draw an analogy, it'd be more to do with the relationship between the Principle and the God-Machine — two vaguely similar aesthetics on two similarly mysterious factions with conspicuous differences in behavior and methodology, whereby there is sufficient grounds to either suggest an ontological link between the two or reject such a connection as suits any given game.

                                            Aggravated damage is rare from Gifts and other powers for all supernatural creatures and for good reasons. But there are no problem to bring aggravated damage potential to any prepared fight and I see no issue with that.

                                            I also hate any discussion with even trying to compare two combatants when removing the narrative or any environment that can effect the outcome of an encounter.

                                            I also strongly dislike any discussion where one dimensional total fictional characters with totally ridiculous stats are discussed. To get back to Werewolf versus Vampire fight for one thing.

                                            I would say that given the same amount of experience most Werewolves would prevail over a Vampire in a sudden and violent situational confrontation. But if you put it into a narrative story I would say its more about the intent and conviction by either party and which medium they use to try and destroy the other and for what reason they do so.

                                            When it comes to Vampires versus Werewolves in general there are very large gaps in experience between a young wolf and an old vampire. You can't really compare a 150 year old vampire with a 35 year old Werewolf when it comes to general power and influence... even if said Werewolf would kill that vampire in single combat in the blink of an eye. Would that Werewolf be able to bypass all the combined powers the vampire possess unless this is a random brawl on the street?

                                            A pack of young and passionate Werewolves might feel invincible on paper but they really are not, they still have limited experience, knowledge and influences in order to deal with a vast majority of really old nasties out there. Sure... they can beat pretty much anything in single combat at their own terms... but will it always be like that in reality though?

                                            Most Vampires for example will not be direct combat monsters and even those that are will eventually revert to less combative demeanor as they age, purely as a survival mechanism. That is what it takes for a vampire to survive in the long run.

                                            I believe this is mostly true for "real" Werewolves as well. Young Werewolves might be bad asses with high physical attributes and appropriate gifts and merits. But as they age they will tend to rely more on mental and social attributes and skills rather than brute force... they will also see their physical prowess to start waning as well... I do guess that Werewoves need to keep training physical strength in order to keep it in the same way a regular human need to, which is why you will not see Werewolves with 5 in all physical attributes that often (if ever)... and the ones that have will only be able to retain them for a short brief time anyway. This is speaking from a narrative point of view.

                                            How much experience must not these supreme combat monsters have acquired and how do they survive that long without much competence in other areas?
                                            There will always be someone stronger and/or just luckier or plain smarter than them to beat them in their rather violent life. In order to attain these ridiculous power levels in combat you will have to survive a ridiculous amount of life and death situations. How likely are such characters to exist, especially for a Werewolf with such limited time span before they get old and weak?
                                            Remember that it usually takes a failure to gain much experience (for the most part) and it should be harder and harder to fail as you grow in power. I can also see that the possible disastrous effect of failure should be higher the tougher you actually are since the forces opposing you need to more and more lethal.

                                            And then... who is so brainless as they plan to fight anyone at anything resembling equal terms?
                                            What creature would go on the offensive if ambushed not knowing what the nature of the ambush or the trap is?
                                            Anyone who lived long enough and managed to get the experience needed to get this far would flee and fight another day during such encounters. The same is true for a failed ambush if you encounter some tough or unexpected problems.

                                            Single combat between two entities, especially between different supernatural beings should be extremely rare. Perhaps if there is a personal vendetta between two creatures with a similar type of notion, such as the way of the warrior and honor perhaps?

                                            Anyway... just because one system have the "potential" of producing the more perfect warrior does not mean it is realistic to find such a character in a game outside of meta-gaming or pure number crunching.
