    Originally posted by Camilla View Post
    And as a disenfranchised former WoD LARP'er, I can attest to that. So, I'll tell you a story about my experiences here in Virginia that is relevant to my point.

    I'm a Requiem fan but the Roanoke VA LARP and White Wolf scene is dominated by hardcore oWoD fans who despise nWoD/CofD just because it's not the game they played back in 1996. For example, when Mind's Eye Society finally established a joint Roanoke/New River Valley-based chapter, the MES people from New River Valley (mostly college girls my age from the college towns of Blacksburg and Radford) initially wanted to do a nWoD Accord venue chronicle, even hosting a regional event for it in March of 2014 at a local Holiday Inn. I was in attendance and I played an Italian-American Daeva in the Circle of the Crone who was a devout Roman polytheist by the name of Tony Cardona, I even dressed up in-character for the occasion. But the Roanoke gamers, being the hardcore oWoD fans they were, threw a huge fit after the LARP, enraged that MES dared to run a nWoD game in the Roanoke Valley. So, while there is still a Mind's Eye Society chapter in Roanoke and the New River Valley, it is exclusively oWoD, only playing the Masquerade and Apocalypse venues. In our particular group, my fellow Millennials were outnumbered by bitter Gen X gamers blinded by nostalgia. So I decided not to officially register with MES, at least not in the Roanoke/New River Valley chapter. It's a shame because my character Tony Cardona was one of my favorite characters I ever made, and I only got to play as him once. Now, if I move to a new area that has a CofD/nWoD friendly Mind's Eye Society chapter, I may officially register with MES and join them. But between the hostility towards nWoD/CofD in my area's gaming scene combined with the fact that I personally dislike By Night Studios' new LARP rules for both mechanical and metaplot reasons, I am done with oWoD LARP, at least organized oWoD LARP.
    Have you checked out any of the MES troupes in northern VA and central MD? NWoD makes up more than half the total MES games there.

    I won't dispute however that there exist frustrating OWoD partisans who refuse to even acknowledge or entertain the NWoD for reasons I can't begin to fathom. That being said, a number of gamers I know who used to fit that description have since come around to being NWoD fans as well, so even then there exists the possibility for everyone to win.

            Funnily enough, I feel similarly, but that places me on 4E's side. 4E was not necessarily a successful experiment for DnD, but it tried to solve some of the longest standing problems with Dungeons and Dragons and the d20 system and actually succeeded in some interesting ways that would have been great to see be built upon.

                                            A spot on example of creative vision not working is the whole Star Wars special editions. George Lucas has an artistic "vision" of how the movies should be and the fans have another. George Lucas kept ignoring the fans to follow with the vision in an unreasonable way as he could have release unmodified and modified versions.

                                            Now disney has the right and pretty much sold the unmodified version as that what people want along with the modified to cater to all fans.
                                            My position is not that creator vision is inherently better. I've argued with creators enough to know that.

                                            Not, mind you, that fan vision is better. Fans have demonstrated to me, across several fandoms, that they have just as much propensity for not knowing what they are talking about as creators.

                                            Really, the nature of the subject is not about the spectrum between creators and fandoms, as far as this goes. I have some very strong opinions on the subject, of course, but that wasn't what I was talking about.

                                            DnD 3.5 hit a ceiling in ways-in many ways, it was as good as DnD was going to be without challenging some of it's core assumptions. This didn't make it a good game-it's very clearly a flawed game. Pathfinder and DnD 4th are flawed games that nevertheless did more with what DnD could be based on how you read the game and how you focused it. They demonstrably tried to make DnD a better game.

                                            DnD 5E's problem is that it didn't really to try to make DnD a better game, learning from the advancements that worked in 4th and Pathfinder and some of it's other contemporaries. It's main concern was simply backpedaling from the fact that 4E was a big commercial failure (a fact even I will cop to) and appealing to it's fanbase without really trying to continue advancing the game.

                                            My problem is not that there was not a gun-sticking to creator vision, but that there was a failure to significantly improve the game through experimentation. The fact that this is done as a fear response to the fanbase, while understandable from a business standpoint, just makes it more annoying.

                                            Appeasement is a factor for businesses, and really a certain amount of that is always going to be needed for any kind of communication, but it shouldn't come at the cost of improving your product. 5E has done very little to try and improve it's product, and actively made it worse in a number of ways.

                                            As for the Guide to the Technocracy. There were significant problems with the fanbase framing the Technocracy as equally good choices for players as the Traditions, and where GttT could have cleared that up, it instead just muddled the issue further and increased the popularity of them*. Even disregarding the social impact that came with this and the way this promoted a lot of toxic attitudes on the internet that don't need reinforcing, it also fucked up the White Wolf consumer base's expectations of how all antagonists were supposed to operate. No, dear reader, the Pure are not people you should want to be. No, the Seers are not supposed to be people you'd willingly join. You should dread all the ways you could become them. They should be relatable, understandable-you should look and know all that keeps you from them and how thin a line that actually is. But you should never want to be them.Like, every time I make the point of how so many people would actually be Seers, I don't say that with joy, with an expectation of people thinking they're doing the right thing, that it is a fair and equal thing to the Pentacle Alliance.

                                            I deeply enjoyed Guide to the Technocracy, and I enjoy the moral ambiguity at work in Ascension with the conflict between the Traditions and the Technocracy, with both sides being genuinely fucked up. I think in a lot of ways Ascension became better when it became clear how everyone's good intentions were going to fuck the world over on every level. But it was, in so many ways, a fuck-up despite every ounce of good that came out of it, and I think as far as fan vs creator conversations go(which is a false dichotomy), it is a clear example of how thorny these subjects can be.

                                            Because, yeah, in a lot of ways, it made Ascension better, but in a lot of ways, it made Ascension's fanbase worse, and it has proven to be a thorn in the side of the World/Chronicles of Darkness discussions ever since, nevermind wider culture.

                                            To point out these problems as Dave did is not to go "My Vision or The Highway". It's to be actually cognizant of the full picture. It's to avoid making really damning mistakes.

                                            *Keep in mind that I say this as a fan of the Technocracy, as someone who would probably take his chances with all the ways the Technocracy are fucked up and bound to destroy the world and it's agents just for the principle at it's heart worth fighting for that might, just might, find a way to overcome the rotten husk the Technocracy was always bound to become.

                                                            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                            Oh, totally. I play 5E now when I play D&D. 4E was a big improvement over 3.X in my opinion, but it was deeply flawed in all new ways. 5E just abandons 4E's design lessons almost entirely, which is a shame cause it threw the baby out with the bath water. I'd love to see a D&D that combines the best parts of both edition's designs, but between the two I ultimately prefer 5E.
                                                            I love 5E. I don't necessarily love every aspect of it, but as an overall product I adore it. It feels like a greatest hits collection, and I think if you scratch away the top coat you'll find some pieces of 4E rattling around in there.

                                                            I'm genuinely surprised, this is the first new edition of D&D I haven't reflexively irrationally hated. I started at the dawn of new millennium with a 2E starter kit and a bunch of hand-me-down 1E books. It took until winter '02 for 3E to actually find its way to West Virginia and for people I knew to start playing it, and I utterly detested it. It wasn't my D&D! No sir! I categorically refused to play it until about '06, but over time it did slowly grow on me. I didn't love every aspect of it, but as an overall product I came to like it. I had pretty much the same reaction a second time when 4E dropped, albeit with a certain wry amusement that people my age were complaining about it not being "real" D&D. I've given it several tries over the years, but 4E still never really clicked with me. It's hard to say exactly why, but I just don't engage with the system. 5E has the advantage of taking the parts of 4E I liked and throwing them on a mixtape with my old favorites from the previous three editions.

                                                            Like I said, its not perfect. I love the idea of making magic items more unique and story-driven, moving away from the shopping list mentality 3E exemplified, but at the same time its kind of broken their treasure system. I love the concept of the sub-classes, but it does front load a lot of your significant build choices. I love what they've done with Cantrips, but wish they hadn't retreated back into a pseudo-Vancian system again. All that being said, I've had better success recruiting new gamers with 5E than I have with any other edition.

                                                            The best success I and my group have had with 3E has been turning its intense granularity into a world-building feature, using it to probe underlying metaphysical assumptions implied by the mechanics and the socio-cultural implications if said mechanics are taken to logical end points.
