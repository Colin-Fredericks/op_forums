                            New Trait: Colony
                            All Monarchs are tended to by many lesser creatures, all various strains of the Endless made to serve her. They act as everything from a labor force, a private army, personal servants, and everything in between, all at her beck and call.
                            Traditionally, Colonies are located in Beneath, a place underground that skirts between realworld location and alternate reality, usually accessed through wormholes or through spatially folded physical locations. As a general rule a Monarch may access or pull forces from her Colony from any Wormholes from her own Court or via physically entering the Beneath through the realspace anchors of her Colony. Generally speaking, Wormholes are safer and more expensive, but the anchor point of a Colony is a vulnerability that anyone with the knowledge can find.

                            But as many servants and warriors a Monarch is able to own, there are limits to how much this force can do, its capabilities, its ability to replenish losses, its own lacks from incomplete Geneweb, and other factors.
                            Colonies are represented by five attributes, each representing an estimation of the Colony’s abilities at given tasks. These are:
                            Military representing the sheer physical and warmaking capabilities of the Colony
                            Knowledge representing the ability of the Colony to research and learn as well as create experiments.
                            Industry representing sheer economic and financial power
                            Espionage repressing the Colony’s discrete information network as well as ability to influence events from the shadows.
                            Connection represents the Colony’s political capital and influence and ability.
                            All are rated from 1 to 10, with 1 dot in each at the start, plus an extra dot in the Monarch’s primary area of focus. Raising Colony Attributes is the cost single merit dot.
                            Colony Attributes represent the various strengths the Colony has in various fields and are rolled in situations when the Colony needs to be rolled. However, the Attributes also respent a reserve of power and resources, available to the Monarch to spend. More on this later, but these are called Resources.
                            Group Play: Conclaves
                            While the imagery of a single reigning ruler lording over a single colony is appealing, the World of Darkness is designed for multiple players. This however doesn’t mean you need multiple Colonies each and real world nature and fictional material is rife with examples of large mega-colonies with multiple Queens birthing young. So as Art imitates Life, Colonies with more than one Monarch aren’t unheard of.
                            Said Colonies are lead by what is known as a Conclave, a group of elites designated as a ruler.
                            The Colony begins play as a normal Colony and each Monarch that joins into it adds their resources together to make the initial starting Colony more efficient than it would have been otherwise.
                            Each Monarch has a vote which it may use to dictate matters involving the Colony, with a simple majority dictating terms most of the time, with the catch that Monarchs of an appropriate Caste for a given issue effectively have their votes working as 2 votes. (i.e. Marshalls for Security and War issues) Additionally, a Voice of the Conclave is elected to settle Ties, should they occur. It is within the rights for a given member of a Conclave to initiate a vote on any topic when all members of the Conclave are in psychic range of each other, though for courtesy’s sake do not usually “recount” votes unless a situation has drastically changed.

                            Conclaves begin play with 1 dot in all Attributes. And each Monarch’s Caste adds 1 dot to a respective attribute.

                            Colony Actions
                            At any point of time, the ruling Monarch or Monarchs may order their Colony to do a task. Tasks can either be automatic and are simply done, or require something done.
                            Similar to a Cult, a Colony is able to do a number of tasks automatically, following the list of things a Cult’s Reach and Grasp. For the purposes of this, any of the Colony’s attributes count as Reach and Grasp and determining which Attribute is used for an action or a reaction is based on context and judgment. Using the Reach and Grasp actions of a Cult are not free however. Using any of them reduces the given Attribute’s “pool”, as above, by 1.
                            As stated before, Colony Attributes determine the amount of resources can be thrown at any given time. What this means is, a Colony can spend “points” of an attribute, not reducing the attribute dots, but still expending a small portion of temporary resources and using them to enact certain actions, up to the limit of the Attribute until the Colony is “refreshed”.
                            For instance, via Connections, a Monarch wishes to see 11 separate CEOs. She has the Connection attribute high enough to arrange meetings with powerful people, but she can’t do it for all of them in the same day. Each CEO will reduce her Connection’s pool until they are refreshed but as she is at the limit of 10, she can’t see the last one just yet. The next day, the Storyteller declares all Colonies refresh their resources, allowing her to see the last CEO.
                            How long it takes for a Colony to refresh is not standardized. This is to allow Storytellers to tailor how often Colonies are able to act as well as to represent the fact that situations may be more active or inactive depending on danger or desperation. In any time the Storyteller wishes to refresh Colonies, he can declare it any time he desires. However, the catch is, this happens to all Colonies, including enemy holdings. This is better explained in Strategic and Tactical Turns below.





                            Simple Colony Actions
                            Colonies have more actions available to them than the standard Reach and Grasp system cults employ. Colonies possess 5 special actions that they may use at any time, simply by spending the resources to activate them. Each action takes 1 Scene to complete.
                            For these actions, you may convert resources from one category to the next, however doing so requires spending 2 points, in any combination of any of the other categories, making this very inefficient. Use when desperate.
                            There is no limit to the amount of times a Colony may use these actions, aside from the lack of resources.
                            Conscripts: Order a small contingent of warrior Endless to come with you, one for each point of Military spent. These warriors are smart enough to understand almost any order and obedient to you alone. Their stats are:


                            All physical Attributes 3. All mental Attributes 1. Size 4, health 7.
                            0 Defense (never evades attacks). 2/2 Armor. 1 damage claws, AP 2.
                            3 brawl, weaponry.
                            Never takes untrained penalties. And is immune to all fear effects.




                            1 Damage Claws.

                            Spy Order your Colony to Spy on a target location or creature via dispatching smaller Endless. Roll Espionage twice to determine their effectiveness at stealth and camouflage. You receive only basic details such as “target is flying away” “target notices us”.
                            Alternatively, this action may be used to counter an existing Spy attempt, using Espionage twice as the dice pool. 1 Espionage is spent regardless of success or failure.

                            Create Your Colony creates for you simple items or devices that are mundane in function, though exotic in nature. This can range to simple weapons, body armor, and electronic devices, however where such things would be made out of metal and plastic where they mundane, they are alien in appearance and nature. Each item or group of similar small items costs 1 Industry.


                            Research Your Colony is tasked with learning all of the secrets of a given scene or item and rapidly produces worthwhile intel. This ability produces Clues. The cost of this ability varies based on the number of clues already gathered from any source including this ability. 1 Knowledge will reveal the first Clue, but additional Clues beyond the first cost 1 additional Knowledge for each Clue already obtained.
                            Research may be used multiple times on the same instance, but each use obviously increases the requirements.
                            Influence Your Colony is tasked with diplomatically influencing a subject, using all manner of persuasion and bribery it has. This ability opens Doors in social interaction provided there is a means to influence the person. The cost of this ability varies based on the number of Doors already gathered from any source including this ability. 1 Connection will open the first Door, but additional Door beyond the first cost 1 additional Connection for each Door already obtained.
                            Influence may be used multiple times on the same instance, but each use obviously increases the requirements.





                            Province Actions
                            One other thing Monarchs may employ their Colonies for is use their Provinces. Provinces add actions and rules to them, which can benefit the Colony… provided the appropriate resources are spent to activate and maintain them.
                            Where as other Supernaturals employ powerful artifacts and relics, for Monarchs, this is taken on by control of the land itself.
                            See Provinces for details.


                            Strategic and Tactical Turns
                            While in a normal game of World/Chronicles of Darkness, turns happen based on the person who has the best initiative roll. In the vast scale of commanding an Empire, such a system breaks down in this sort of scale.
                            Enter Strategic and Tactical turn orders, which are more abstract and follow their own rules to make managing a government easier. How long these turns last is varied based on the Storyteller’s whims, effectively being ticked over at when would be the most appropriate time. As a general rule, for every 1-3 Tactical turns, there should be one 1 Strategic turn.
                            In Strategic and Tactical turns, all players and Colonies move simultaneously and resolve their actions simultaneously. It is the main way to keep track and organize Empire and Warfare actions and is organized into phases, explained in depth below, in chronological sequence of events.
                            The start of Strategic turns are also when all Colonies and Empires regain their Attributes supply. For those of you who played Magic the Gathering, this system was heavily influenced by that.

                            Tactical Turns are the GM’s way of keeping track of War progress and are used to determine how things are going.

                            Start Strategic Turn
                            -Refresh Attributes All Colonies and Empires restore up their attribute total.
                            -Spend on Upkeep Colonies spend any Upkeep they are required to spend.
                            -Trigger Abilities If the Monarch wishes to use a Colony or Province ability, she may use it.
                            Begin Tactical Turn
                            -Activate Abilities If the Monarch wishes to use an ability tied to Minions, she may use it now.
                            -Attack Phase All players choose what Minions, they wish to send on an attack and which opponent they wish to attack. All attacks resolve immediately. Count the damage dealt this way by summing up the Power of each Minion appropriately and where it went.
                            -Defense Phase All players then may choose to negate the damage they received. To do this, they may choose a Minion (including one that has been used in an attack) and have it block the damage, reducing the value of damage received by a number equal to the Minion’s Toughness. If the Minion’s toughness is reduced to 0, the Minion is destroyed any remaining damage is still bled through and still damage the targeted player. A player may use more than one minion, each one at a time. And may use his minions to block the damage taken by another player, important for any Alliance. All units.
                            -War Score Phase After all of the damage is done. Each player rolls a number of dice equal to the damage they successfully dealt to their opponents. Each success grants them 1 War Score pertaining to a specific opponent they attacked. This is used in the Spoils Phase. If two characters have War Score over each other, the player with the lower War Score removes his War Score and reduces the larger player’s War Score by an appropriate amount.
                            End Tactical Turn

                            After All Tactical Turns
                            Spoils Phase Here, War Score may be spent to specific ends, usually the reason for war in the first place. As described in the War, Simplied table.
                            End Strategic Turn


                            War, Simplified
                            A full, and proper military campaign is far from the abilities of a tabletop game to express. However, War Score allows you to keep track of how much is needed to achieve certain goals or maybe make the war easier on yourself.
                            Just as a note, War Score costs vary based on the Authority of the person you have War Score over, representing the sheer control and willingness to keep fighting of a higher and stronger Monarch. As a rule. Multiply the costs below by 2 for characters with 4+ Authority, 3 times for +8 Authority. Excluding all Vassalize or Exterminate.
                            Free/Demand Captive (2) - Your opponent releases a captive or releases a person under her care/custody to yours.
                            Deny Province (2) - You deny your opponent the ability to use a specific Province as long as you are hostile to him. This lasts until the War is over.
                            Annex Province (5 per ability) - You force your opponent to cede a land title or possess enough political power to forcefully take a Province from your enemy and keep it. It is now yours
                            Acquire Artifact (1 per dot rating) - Your opponent releases an item into your custody.
                            Vassalize Defeated (10 per Authority) - You force your opponent to be your servant. This may only be done to enemies with less Titles than you.
                            Exterminate Defeated (20 per Authority) - You completely eradicate your opponent and take the rest of his belongings and thralls as your own. This may only be done if your opponent is reduced to a single holding.

(i.e. Marshalls for Security and War issues) Additionally, a Voice of the Conclave is elected to settle Ties, should they occur. It is within the rights for a given member of a Conclave to initiate a vote on any topic when all members of the Conclave are in psychic range of each other, though for courtesy’s sake do not usually “recount” votes unless a situation has drastically changed.

                                                Conclaves begin play with 1 dot in all Attributes. And each Monarch’s Caste adds 1 dot to a respective attribute.

                                                Colony Actions
                                                At any point of time, the ruling Monarch or Monarchs may order their Colony to do a task. Tasks can either be automatic and are simply done, or require something done.
                                                Similar to a Cult, a Colony is able to do a number of tasks automatically, following the list of things a Cult’s Reach and Grasp. For the purposes of this, any of the Colony’s attributes count as Reach and Grasp and determining which Attribute is used for an action or a reaction is based on context and judgment. Using the Reach and Grasp actions of a Cult are not free however. Using any of them reduces the given Attribute’s “pool”, as above, by 1.
                                                As stated before, Colony Attributes determine the amount of resources can be thrown at any given time. What this means is, a Colony can spend “points” of an attribute, not reducing the attribute dots, but still expending a small portion of temporary resources and using them to enact certain actions, up to the limit of the Attribute until the Colony is “refreshed”.
                                                For instance, via Connections, a Monarch wishes to see 11 separate CEOs. She has the Connection attribute high enough to arrange meetings with powerful people, but she can’t do it for all of them in the same day. Each CEO will reduce her Connection’s pool until they are refreshed but as she is at the limit of 10, she can’t see the last one just yet. The next day, the Storyteller declares all Colonies refresh their resources, allowing her to see the last CEO.
                                                How long it takes for a Colony to refresh is not standardized. This is to allow Storytellers to tailor how often Colonies are able to act as well as to represent the fact that situations may be more active or inactive depending on danger or desperation. In any time the Storyteller wishes to refresh Colonies, he can declare it any time he desires. However, the catch is, this happens to all Colonies, including enemy holdings. This is better explained in Strategic and Tactical Turns below.





                                                Simple Colony Actions
                                                Colonies have more actions available to them than the standard Reach and Grasp system cults employ. Colonies possess 5 special actions that they may use at any time, simply by spending the resources to activate them. Each action takes 1 Scene to complete.
                                                For these actions, you may convert resources from one category to the next, however doing so requires spending 2 points, in any combination of any of the other categories, making this very inefficient. Use when desperate.
                                                There is no limit to the amount of times a Colony may use these actions, aside from the lack of resources.
                                                Conscripts: Order a small contingent of warrior Endless to come with you, one for each point of Military spent. These warriors are smart enough to understand almost any order and obedient to you alone. Their stats are:


                                                All physical Attributes 3. All mental Attributes 1. Size 4, health 7.
                                                0 Defense (never evades attacks). 2/2 Armor. 1 damage claws, AP 2.
                                                3 brawl, weaponry.
                                                Never takes untrained penalties. And is immune to all fear effects.




                                                1 Damage Claws.

                                                Spy Order your Colony to Spy on a target location or creature via dispatching smaller Endless. Roll Espionage twice to determine their effectiveness at stealth and camouflage. You receive only basic details such as “target is flying away” “target notices us”.
                                                Alternatively, this action may be used to counter an existing Spy attempt, using Espionage twice as the dice pool. 1 Espionage is spent regardless of success or failure.

                                                Create Your Colony creates for you simple items or devices that are mundane in function, though exotic in nature. This can range to simple weapons, body armor, and electronic devices, however where such things would be made out of metal and plastic where they mundane, they are alien in appearance and nature. Each item or group of similar small items costs 1 Industry.


                                                Research Your Colony is tasked with learning all of the secrets of a given scene or item and rapidly produces worthwhile intel. This ability produces Clues. The cost of this ability varies based on the number of clues already gathered from any source including this ability. 1 Knowledge will reveal the first Clue, but additional Clues beyond the first cost 1 additional Knowledge for each Clue already obtained.
                                                Research may be used multiple times on the same instance, but each use obviously increases the requirements.
                                                Influence Your Colony is tasked with diplomatically influencing a subject, using all manner of persuasion and bribery it has. This ability opens Doors in social interaction provided there is a means to influence the person. The cost of this ability varies based on the number of Doors already gathered from any source including this ability. 1 Connection will open the first Door, but additional Door beyond the first cost 1 additional Connection for each Door already obtained.
                                                Influence may be used multiple times on the same instance, but each use obviously increases the requirements.





                                                Province Actions
                                                One other thing Monarchs may employ their Colonies for is use their Provinces. Provinces add actions and rules to them, which can benefit the Colony… provided the appropriate resources are spent to activate and maintain them.
                                                Where as other Supernaturals employ powerful artifacts and relics, for Monarchs, this is taken on by control of the land itself.
                                                See Provinces for details.


                                                Strategic and Tactical Turns
                                                While in a normal game of World/Chronicles of Darkness, turns happen based on the person who has the best initiative roll. In the vast scale of commanding an Empire, such a system breaks down in this sort of scale.
                                                Enter Strategic and Tactical turn orders, which are more abstract and follow their own rules to make managing a government easier. How long these turns last is varied based on the Storyteller’s whims, effectively being ticked over at when would be the most appropriate time. As a general rule, for every 1-3 Tactical turns, there should be one 1 Strategic turn.
                                                In Strategic and Tactical turns, all players and Colonies move simultaneously and resolve their actions simultaneously. It is the main way to keep track and organize Empire and Warfare actions and is organized into phases, explained in depth below, in chronological sequence of events.
                                                The start of Strategic turns are also when all Colonies and Empires regain their Attributes supply. For those of you who played Magic the Gathering, this system was heavily influenced by that.

                                                Tactical Turns are the GM’s way of keeping track of War progress and are used to determine how things are going.

                                                Start Strategic Turn
                                                -Refresh Attributes All Colonies and Empires restore up their attribute total.
                                                -Spend on Upkeep Colonies spend any Upkeep they are required to spend.
                                                -Trigger Abilities If the Monarch wishes to use a Colony or Province ability, she may use it.
                                                Begin Tactical Turn
                                                -Activate Abilities If the Monarch wishes to use an ability tied to Minions, she may use it now.
                                                -Attack Phase All players choose what Minions, they wish to send on an attack and which opponent they wish to attack. All attacks resolve immediately. Count the damage dealt this way by summing up the Power of each Minion appropriately and where it went.
                                                -Defense Phase All players then may choose to negate the damage they received. To do this, they may choose a Minion (including one that has been used in an attack) and have it block the damage, reducing the value of damage received by a number equal to the Minion’s Toughness. If the Minion’s toughness is reduced to 0, the Minion is destroyed any remaining damage is still bled through and still damage the targeted player. A player may use more than one minion, each one at a time. And may use his minions to block the damage taken by another player, important for any Alliance. All units.
                                                -War Score Phase After all of the damage is done. Each player rolls a number of dice equal to the damage they successfully dealt to their opponents. Each success grants them 1 War Score pertaining to a specific opponent they attacked. This is used in the Spoils Phase. If two characters have War Score over each other, the player with the lower War Score removes his War Score and reduces the larger player’s War Score by an appropriate amount.
                                                End Tactical Turn

                                                After All Tactical Turns
                                                Spoils Phase Here, War Score may be spent to specific ends, usually the reason for war in the first place. As described in the War, Simplied table.
                                                End Strategic Turn


                                                War, Simplified
                                                A full, and proper military campaign is far from the abilities of a tabletop game to express. However, War Score allows you to keep track of how much is needed to achieve certain goals or maybe make the war easier on yourself.
                                                Just as a note, War Score costs vary based on the Authority of the person you have War Score over, representing the sheer control and willingness to keep fighting of a higher and stronger Monarch. As a rule. Multiply the costs below by 2 for characters with 4+ Authority, 3 times for +8 Authority. Excluding all Vassalize or Exterminate.
                                                Free/Demand Captive (2) - Your opponent releases a captive or releases a person under her care/custody to yours.
                                                Deny Province (2) - You deny your opponent the ability to use a specific Province as long as you are hostile to him. This lasts until the War is over.
                                                Annex Province (5 per ability) - You force your opponent to cede a land title or possess enough political power to forcefully take a Province from your enemy and keep it. It is now yours
                                                Acquire Artifact (1 per dot rating) - Your opponent releases an item into your custody.
                                                Vassalize Defeated (10 per Authority) - You force your opponent to be your servant. This may only be done to enemies with less Titles than you.
                                                Exterminate Defeated (20 per Authority) - You completely eradicate your opponent and take the rest of his belongings and thralls as your own. This may only be done if your opponent is reduced to a single holding.
                                                Question: Wouldn't Chase mechanics, with a modification that it substitutes Authority for speed and turns some of those aspects you listed into "war" merits in a way that Relentless, Greyhound and other Chase related merits do from CofD be a streamlined and conceptually consistent use of existing mechanics? Just an idea.

                                                Also it seems like some of the consequences of War would seem to be good things to format and use as Conditions, Deny Province is a good example. Give it a Resolution clause and Beat clause and you have a mechanic which is more rich. Indeed the point of the War type Chase could be to inflict War caused Conditions on opponents or self (like Plundered on opponent and Enriched on self) even temporarily empty servant spots caused by casualties based Conditions are totally workable, giving you a chance to neatly define resolving such issues without Xp expenditures.

                                                    Oh thank the dark gods, I thought that no-one else was interested in this carrying on!

                                                    With reference to the mechanics of war, though, I think that something we need to consider is the fact that war has a price. War drains resources on a massive scale, especially when you have to construct not only your soldiers' armaments but their very bodies. Of course, Endless monsters are essentially expendable, but the fact remains that I doubt that Monarchs would like to go to war with one another all that often, especially when they have enemies like the Geodics at their backs. I feel that there should be a significant part of the 'fluff' section dedicated to Monarchs' etiquette amongst themselves and in relation to outsiders. How one declares war, what is required as a pretext, when peace must be held (in response to Geodic incursions, for example), protocols with regards to contact with independent entities in the Underneath or other supernaturals and so on.

                                                    Given that Monarchs have in-built long-range communication with their telepathy, I think that their society would be both widely spread-out and closely-linked together. Quite apart from anything else, I would be horribly disappointed if Monarchs didn't have a wonderfully convoluted series of laws, conventions and the occasional 'party' which is really an excuse for making and breaking alliances.
