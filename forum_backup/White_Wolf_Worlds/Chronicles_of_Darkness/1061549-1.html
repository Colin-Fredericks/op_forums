    So, Rex had just gotten the cabal thrown out of Broken Seal's home but has secured an agreement to get her to return the tass she took. In the meantime, they need to organize some way to put a stop to this permanently.

    ---

    There's not a hope in hell of settling this matter easily but there are some settlements that are better than others. A conversation is struck up and the cabal debates their next course of action.

    It's not an easy decision to make about what comes next. The Valley is exposed, its defenses breached. Spider will not stay put if she thinks her home is no longer safe, and it's only a matter of time until word filters from Broken Seal; that tass was going somewhere and whomever it was going to will want to know why it isn't coming. On the other hand, if they petition the Council and, assuming the Council even agrees to a ban on Awakened seeking the Valley, get a forbiddance on seeking it out, they might as well paint "DON'T LOOK OVER HERE" above the place in liquid light. It will just draw attention to the damned place.

    Eventually, a decision is made: Some protection is better than none at all. They'll petition the Council to declare the Valley off-limits, and seek the Swamp Padfoot's assent in the matter as well. As the largest libertine cabal in the region, with an obvious desire to prevent this from continuing, the Padfoot are their best bet to put force behind their declarations, even if the Council fails to back them.

    (Rolls are called for. Goodfeather uses his political contacts to get word into Consilium channels and Rex uses his position in the local Ladder Caucus to try and ensure word of those official messages gets up to the Hierarch and isn't simply lost. Fox, meanwhile, works to persuade Cavall to speak with the Padfoot on his behalf. 44 keeps an eye out for anyone who might be listening in on their efforts and tries to minimize the noise they make setting this all up.)

    (Multiple exceptional successes.)

    A meeting is called for on the night of the coming new moon, several days away. Rex is biting his nails to the quick, and goes as far as asking 44 to hide out in the Valley and watch for Broken Seal; to make sure the mystagogue actually does as she promised and returns the tass. Good as her word, Broken Seal returns the tass and Spider, in turn, halts the kidnappings. For now.

    When the new moon comes, the Quartz Scales arrive at the Parliament Hall; an old, red stone building sitting on the coast, surrounded by high iron fences and patrolled out front by rent-a-cop security.

    ---

    It was an old building. Not terribly tall - only two storeys - but most certainly very wide, with steeples rising periodically at junctions and corners and a clocktower on its front face. It sat overlooking the rocky shore of an inlet, surrounded by trees on its sides and a fence on its grounds, with no other buildings lining that side of the inlet. The place bespoke wealth and exclusivity, and the small docks below and attached boats made many Sleepers assume it was a private club. In a sense, it was. That night, some of the windows were lit; the members of the cabal arriving at the appointed late hour at the front gates. Approaching the front entrance where security guards stood watch. Sleepers, probably. The sorts of people who wouldn't actually be allowed inside the building and, hence, were confined to the yard.

    Past the white-shirted guards and through the metal detectors, the quartet finds themselves inside. Heralded through halls lit by dim tungsten bulbs and into the Council chambers. The room is a semi-ovoid; the flat rear wall with the risen platform for the Council and Hierarch, and the benches for the lay membership surrounding them on all sides save the rear. The ceremonial mace, normally front and centre, is not in attendance at this meeting.

    Up at the back wall, only two of the Council seats are filled; the white oak chair of the Obrimos, where a rather tired-looking Qadi sits and scribbles in a journal, and the throne of the Hierarch, where White Sands resides and discusses some matter with a red-headed woman.

    "Welcome to the Parliament Hall, lady and gentlemen," greets White Tooth Shining, "I'm sure it goes without saying, but there will be no casting of spells or other use of magic in the presence of the Councilors tonight unless specifically asked."

    ---

    White Tooth Shining - or Tooth, for short - is a Sentinel and the acting guard. He's built like a rugby linebacker and wears a collection of shark teeth on knotted cord around his neck. Many of these teeth are much too large to belong to any species of shark alive today, but they are not fossilized. He also has a very large sword.

    ---

    44 throws a lazy smile in the Sentinel's general direction and lets her gaze linger before tipping her head in a concession of politeness to the Council. She skulks close behind the rest of the cabal, in much the same stance.

    "Wup!" Tooth interjects. He steps in and places a hand on her shoulder; stopping her skulking away and turning her to face him. He extends a hand, palm flat, and gives her a hard stare. "Hand it over."

    In her defense, 44 does a decent job of looking surprised. A brief pout, but then her smile returns, intense. From seemingly nowhere, she puts a knife in her hand, and offers the handle to Tooth. "Sharp eyes as ever. Be sure to bring it back~"

    ---

    This is about as close as 44 comes to flirtatious.

    Goodfeather is exceptionally creeped out.

    Once the cabal has disarmed and dropped all spells, the quartet walks inside. Rex approaches the bench and politely addresses the Hierarch. The two of them make pleasantries, her trying very hard to not show that she has absolutely no idea who the hell he is, him trying to hide his contempt for that a meeting was even necessary. He's a former politician, she's a current one; they make a good job of it.

    Also in attendance that evening is about a dozen or so Sleepwalkers. This meeting is private, so most of them are clerks, security, and members of the entourages of Mages present. One of them - the red-haired woman White Sands was speaking to - is a Proximus named Epona. She quietly wolf-whistles at Fox as she passes him, despite being blind enough to require a cane.

    ---

    After settling in, the cabal has time to prepare. It's not long, though, before the Padfoot arrive and make themselves known.

    ---

    There's time to mingle. To talk. There's people in the room besides the Councilors and the Sentinel. It's hard to tell just who most of them are but there's at least a dozen of them. They talk and go about business until, suddenly, there's a loud interruption to the Parliament; the insistent whooping of an alarm.

    It's brief and intermittent but loud when it happens, sometimes in rapid succession. The Sentinel moves to stand and block the entrance but doesn't make look like he's expecting trouble, per se. Into the Parliament they file, one by one, halting before him. Rifles over all of their shoulders. The Swamp Padfoot, aiming to misbehave.

    The narrow hallway leaves them confined to three abreast. Cavall to one side. Barghest to the other. The latter is truly enormous. She has to be at least six and a half feet tall and three or even four hundred pounds. She looks like she's smuggling pigs in her arms, and her face has so many hard ridges, you could make a topographical map of it.

    Between them both, though, stands Black Shuck. Head of the Swamp Padfoot. Dingo's top dog.

        Shuck is a tall man. Not as tall as Barghest, but he might be close if he didn't hunch. His dark hair is long and greasy, his body quite lanky, and it's hard to tell if he's nothing like the sort of person you'd expect to lead a band of rogues or exactly that sort of person.

        He, and every member of the Padfoot, is packing heat. Rifles on every shoulder, just as Cavall had with him when he first came to visit the Scales' sanctum.

        Tooth steps in the way and blocks the hall. No-one is allowed into the hall carrying weapons or wielding spells. That's the law of the Consilium.

        The Padfoot, on the other hand, have sworn an oath to Dingo and Dingo's Law. One of those laws is to never allow themselves to be disarmed; always be ready, always be a threat.

        The result is an impasse. There's more at stake here than simple obstinance on the part of either party. The Parliament Hall is a sacred space and no weapons - aside from the ceremonial mace, which has never been used as a weapon - are allowed inside. To allow the Padfoot to bring in their guns would be a desecration of that sacredness and a violation of its sympathies. The Padfoot, on the other hand, cannot disarm themselves because to do so would be to break faith with their patron, and nobody breaks a deal with Dingo.

        Trying to intervene and produce a compromise, Fox steps in first; flattering them that they're never really disarmed, but ultimately it falls on deaf ears. Where that changes is when Rex joins in and throws himself into the arena, and offers them a chance to prove it.

        ---

        "And Dingo knows you got more teeth than those," Fox interjected, inordinately amicable for wandering between two lines of armed guards and survivalists. "We all know you don't need guns to fight, mates."

        The situation doesn't look like it's going to end well. "The Hall is a sacred space. There are -no weapons- allowed beyond the atrium." Tooth grunts. All the Padfoot show is apathy, at least until Fox interjects. Attention falls on the bright young lad trying to calm things down, and Cavall gets a few eyebrows quirked in his direction. Everyone knows who he is.

        Rex follows behind Fox. His smile falters for a brief second as the Padfoot enter, but it's right back on his face by the time they reach the cabal. "If you'd like to prove it, feel free to take a swing." He gestures to his smiling face. "Tell me this isn't the exact sort of face you'd want to lay one into." He nods to Tooth, a sign to let it go.

        Tooth, of course, doesn't give a damn; he doesn't work for Rex. But Shuck? Shuck considers the offer. "Awright," he nods, "take him at his word, Barghest." And that is all the warning Rex has before his world sinks into blinding white pain.

        ---

        44 is trying not to burst out laughing. Goodfeather is staring, mouth agape.

        (Rex takes five Bashing damage, because Barghest hits like a freight train.)

        ---

        Rex... Needs a moment. He probably doesn't notice that he actually gets applause for not being knocked right the fuck out and managing to agonizingly struggle to lift his head up from where he is laying on the floor. "Good lad." Shuck nods. "Padfoot, weapons down!" He shouts over his shoulder and, one by one, the Padfoot unload their rifles and set them to the sides of the corridor. Shuck's hand tussles Fox's hair as he passes.

        "Your friend doesn't get out of the city much, does he?" Cavall adds quietly to Fox as he passes, patting the boy on the shoulder while carrying on into the chamber.

        [...]

        Rex struggles back to his feet, a little shakey, but his finds his footing. It's not the first time he's been hit so hard to possibly have given him a concussion, and the ringing in his ears wasn't as bad as the time one of the mechanical training dummies had malfunctioned and knock him five feet. He feels his numb and aching jaw. Thank goodness he made sure Caduceus had a good dental plan.

        When he finally steadies himself, he calls after them, "'Oy, Barghest!" and as she turns her head, he winks. It worked. It would've been nice if his word had been enough, but sacrifices. He moves his jaw up and down, making sure it still works well enough to talk.

        Barghest looks back over her shoulder. She sees Rex wink and her thin lips draw to the edge of a smile. The hand that socked him - that meaty hoof - has a faint redness from where it contacted Rex's face. She lifts it and gives him a brief thumbs-up before turning her attention forward. Rex is sure he hears her murmur "Monkey can take a punch."

        ---

        The ice (and Rex's jaw) broken, the meeting comes to order. Shuck starts it off with immediate faux-respect; delivering a mocking bow to the Hierarch, which White Sands has the grace to brush off. She calls on the Quartz Scales to talk.

        Rex speaks while his face is swelling up, ever the dedicated spokesman. As the cabal's Farseeker, this is his territory. The initial discussion breaks down relatively quickly, with everyone agreeing that Spider's kidnappings are a problem but no-one agreeing on the best solution. The Padfoot would rather just outright try and kill her - a challenging proposition, to say the least - while the Hierarch is more in favour of trying to cordon or even bind Spider and capture her Hallow for the Consilium. The Scales, representing a more moderate stance, want to honour their patron's old bargain with Spider. As representatives of the Rainbow Serpent, it behooves them to act in its interest and try and undo the damage done by restoring the sanctity of the Valley.

        This is Rex's domain. This is his area of expertise. He expertly plays both sides off one another until they are exhausted and aims to position himself as an acceptable middleground between the two extremes.

        (Three-way social-fu occurs in the form of a Social Chase. Rex wins with an Exceptional Success.)

        Eventually, it is agreed: The Council will grant the Quartz Scales the rights to pursue an agreement with Spider that ends the kidnappings. If they do so within the next lunar month, they'll retain the title as guardians and enforcers of that agreement, with the Hierarch only giving the subtlest of winks that this comes with a personal request to get Spider to agree to become an object of study.

        The Padfoot, meanwhile, agree to back whatever agreement the Scales come to, so long as it ends the kidnappings. They agree to the Council's timeline; if they can do so within the next lunar month, they'll provide forceful backing of the agreement and put that to Dingo. If not, they'll take matters into their own hands. What's more, Rex gets the Council to agree to a prohibition on other Awakened accessing the Valley without divulging its location; hopefully putting a halt to this for good. It's everything he hoped for.

        ---

        But the success is not unqualified.

        While all this is going on, 44 notices something odd; a woman in the stands she doesn't recognize. Now, there's a lot of Sleepwalkers in the area, but 44 has a good memory; she's a Guardian, after all, and trained to keep connection maps of people in her mind. This woman is dressed like one of the clerks but isn't involved in the bustle of the meeting; she's sitting in the stands and, by the looks of things, taking notes.

        44 slips out of the group and wanders over to have a look, but she gets a bit too creepy and the woman notices. Spooked, the stranger gets up and leaves the meeting in a hurry.

        (44 failed on a Stealth roll to try and be nonchalant.)

        Before the woman vanishes, though, 44 does notice one thing: An identifying tattoo. An apple with a Roman numeral through it. She returns to her cabal members - the ones not occupied by the negotiations, at least - and murmurs her findings; asking of they know anything. (Un)fortunately they do. The tattoo is the sign of the Children of Eden, a cult that appeared in the 30s, rising from the embers of the Theosophic movement. They have a history of unpleasant associations with nationalist and skinhead movements, and there are at least a few actual mystics among them too. Fox knows them more intimately than most; he's been punched in the face by one of them before.

        The question is raised, but there isn't yet time to answer it. Rex gets the deal he's after. The cabal races to return to Spider.

            The cabal races to return to Spider's Valley, there to offer the new deal to the mighty creature. Spider, however, is skeptical; already planning to leave. She explains that, even with their protection, she faces a risk. The Awakened are curious and, inevitably, one will try and slip past her defenses. How much must her children be put at risk? Fox and Rex do their best to soothe her fears and explain that, like it or not, the Valley is the best place for her and her children. If they leave, they'll just be found somewhere else, after all. If the Scales can find her, anyone can.

            Eventually, Spider agrees to the pact; she will do as she promised and keep her children home. In exchange, the Scales will protect her. Rex tries to nudge for the possibility of creating a false burrow (Spider is a trickster, after all) and meeting curious Mages there. It'd put her at risk but it'd keep her children safe. She is... Modestly resistant to the notion, but agrees to consider it.

            The bargain is agreed and sealed. Spider signifies her agreement by gathering mist on webs of secrets and handing each of the Scales a veil made of her silk; a thing to signify their agreement with her.

            (44 analyzes them and finds the silk is enchanted, magically concealing the wearer; granting a +2 to Stealth rolls)

            The quartet starts to make their way to leave. Spider vanishes back into her mist-shrouded hidey-hole and Rex is already planning for the biggest spell of his life.

            ---

            It comes like a bolt from the blue; this sudden overwhelming, oppressive sensation; like gravity had amplified and was trying to force them to their knees. Unseen senses are opened wide and all four see it; a great white electric pillar, slicing a jagged hole through reality and casting forth arcing lines of force until the whole thing resembles a luminous eye the size of a man. In the panicked moments it is there, Rex and Goodfeather both try to hastily analyze it. It's there for all of a few seconds. Then it's gone.

            Time enough for Rex to realize just what the hell it was, though: A sympathetic spell targeting him, cast by his former friend, Noah Lachlan. A Seer of the Throne.

            It takes a moment to process it all. Feelings wrack Rex; fear, betrayal, uncertainty, as his old friend uses their connection against him. He sums up these emotions with a primal scream of "NOAH!"

            Before Rex can fly off the handle and react too strongly, Goodfeather chimes in too, though. That spell was one he's familiar with and recognized the nature of: It was a gaze backwards into the past. Whatever Lachlan did, he hasn't actually done it yet; they were receiving a glimpse of the future.

            (The spell was Postcognition.)

            Unwilling to act on it just yet and give the game away, Rex focuses on the immediate. Spider's Valley is momentarily secure. He has a chance to ward it now, so that's what he will do.

            The group leaves, thoroughly unnerved but glad in their success.

            ---

            An epilogue follows. Rex spends the subsequent days petitioning his Caucus for training in Prime magic and, when he is ready, pulls Goodfeather from his desk and the two enact a grand ritual; Goodfeather blessing Rex's quest by anointing him with oil from the tip of a feather, and Rex circling Spider's Valley from the cliffs above and performing a great working to bar all entry into the Valley, before concealing its magical nature beneath a Veiling spell. The act of casting them nearly kills him. He keys the spells to his personal ring and relinquishes the spells; turning it over to Spider, so that she alone may decide who to grant entrance and audience.

            Relinquishing the spell costs Rex dearly, but sacrifice is not without its rewards. With the Valley barred, they have done services to their patron; fulfilling the Rainbow Serpent's mythical role.

            As reward for his services, Rex's soul is filled with the Serpent's light; an act which replenishes his lost Willpower dots. This is an act surely to have no consequences.

            (A custom Condition is being worked out for the start of the next session to reflect that Rex has a god's essence in his soul while it fixes him, something a mortal frame was not meant for.)

            Fox is the one who did the negotiating on that bargain and, being a canny negotiator, he manages to get a little more in the way of extra prizes for the effort. The Serpent is magnanimous. For now, the boon promise sits in Fox's pocket, waiting to be called upon.

            And as politics do as they do, a frenzy occurs in the Consilium at the new pronouncement. The Scales go to ground for a time; waiting out the initial frenzy and furor until things have calmed down enough to actually start talking about it. Rex begs patience, but it's clear something will have to be done to bleed things off. But for a few weeks or months at least, there will be peace.

            ---

            And that's the end of Chapter One! The Spider is quiet again, silent under a tenuous peace. The Quartz Scales come out richer in experiences, knowledge, and magical wealth. There's unanswered questions, though; like who was that lady at the Consilium? And how are the Seers going to get involved? The fuses are lit and only time will tell if they lead to fireworks or dynamite.

            Stay tuned for the next chapter in the Quartz Scales' story: Buried Memories!
