    What do players actually do?

    10-06-2015, 11:33 PM
    I've always been interested in Changeling but I've never been able to run a game because of a major issue. I'm not sure what players are actually meant to do. In pathfinder you act as hero protecting worlds and cities, in werewolf you keep the balance between the spirits and mortals, sin-eaters fix hauntings, PTU you battle gyms, etc.... But I don't really understand what changelings are suppose to actually do other than just try to live and stay away from the True Fae.

      Changelings have a lot more difficulty adapting to "mortal" life because they have literally been in a madhouse with variable laws of physics for at least half a decade. That's true for Mummy and Promethean as well, of course, but it's worth noting as a major source of possible conflict. It plays in with the idea of the Fetch.

      Court politics is raised repeatedly in the core book as a potential chronicle direction. When you've got a bunch of people with the power to enforce agreements absolutely, but very different value systems and logical pathways, politics can range anywhere from deadly to a literal joke. Although I haven't read the Vampire books, I thought that was the main gist of most VtM and VtR chronicles as well.

      Honestly, there's a LOT of weight on interactions with Mortals. Pledges are far and away the fastest and most versatile way to gain skills and merits, and it can be fun to roleplay both their creation and their fulfillment. Being someone's fairy godmother (or counselor, or hacker, or assassin, or...) ends up filling up the time that the Court intrigue leaves free.

      Of course, my players really just spend most of the time collecting stuff and shoving it in their Hollow... but that's probably most players of most games, haha.

          Reclaim their place in the human world.

          That's fairly vague (which is fine, because it should be personal). Some changelings are going to want to be as close to what they used to be. Some are going to take the chance to be who they always wanted to be. Some are going to just want to find a new persona that meshes who they were, and who they are.

          Everything else, from good things (like Gobline Markets, Freeholds, and Courts), to bad things (Privateers, Loyalists, Huntsmen, Gentry) are tools to make the journey of reclaiming their agency in life by escaping, and then actualizing it by finding a new place in the world, interesting and difficult.

                  Certainly there would be Lost who would love for that to be the case, but so would many other splats. Think about it: Why don't werewolves just stay human? What's forcing them to keep the balance between spirits and the flesh? What about the average Mage? Sure they could go investigate that mystery or whatever, but they've got bills to pay, they've got mouths to feed, there ain't nothing in this world for free.

                  Basically, the Lost want to 1) Acquire new life and 2) Maintain that new life, which means handling relationships and juggling responsibilities, sure, but they also spend a lot of time looking over their shoulders because Loyalists, rival motleys, court enemies, human hunters, and just regular people who might be investigating the weird urban legends springing up around the character.

                  Some Lost devote themselves to a more proactive life though. They're Sheriff Bigby Wolf, keeping peace in the Freehold. They're the Inquisition, finding Loyalists and thwarting the machinations of the Fae. They're dungeon-delvers, going into the Hedge to find treasure and loot for profit. They're assassins, taking out Fetches for the Lost that can afford it.

                  Lost is pretty expansive, really.

                      It depends on the goals of the group. Frequently, themes include regaining your life, dealing with your Fetch, defeating Loyalists, exploring the Hedge, potecting humans from the Gentry, and possibly at high Wyrd battling Gen try themselves.

                      My game with Actual Plays on the forum Malibu Dream House (which you can feel free to read for inspiration/plot ideas/NPC ideas) focuses on running a Burlesque House, dealing with Court politics, and dealing with Loyalists, as well as exploring the PCs relationships and psyches. My previous game Beyond the Sea focused on dealing with supernatural evil in a small town full of secrets and an unusually hostile Hedge.

                      Changeling has a lot of flexibility with themes.

                          My two games with Changelings had very different themes and things to do. The first one was on a more epic scale with a more....railroaded chronicle, but it was mostly politics and trying to thwart destiny itself. Players all had different agendas and tried to complete their own goals while the world was slowly collapsing around us. This is an example of a "Pathfinder/D&D" game with Changelings.

                          The other game, Of Light and Shadow, was much more personal in nature. My player just got out of the Hedge, lost and unsure of what happened. He tried to go back to his family, which acted like he never went away - hell, he even had a job and a girlfriend. He tried to get his life back, somehow succeeded, but then the politics happened. The Freehold wanted him on board, no matter the court (the Monarchs could later-on try and recruit him), the Loyalists were on his toes, he stumbled upon investigative work and the game ended sooner than expected.

                          But during that time, he never asked what there was to do. As stated, he was always watching over his shoulder, trying to patch his life back together, trying to keep his new friends sane (a hard task amongst the Lost), trying not to get mad himself, etc...

                              Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                              So it's just them trying to come back into living a life.... That sounds.... boring for a tabletop game.
                              That's kind of like saying, "D&D sounds boring because you just go into caverns and try to get as many shiny rocks as possible, just to do it again."

                              Changelings have spent enough time (from their perspective) for the human world they once knew to be as alien and difficult to adjust to as the Arcadian realms they escaped from. Fetches have often claimed their old lives, with all sorts of consequences. The fae world wants to drag them back, and the human world is incapable of fully accepting them; only the tricks they picked up as fae beings gives them a chance to trick there way into a new life. Which is a purposeful dichotomy, the only way to be human is to use the powers of the fae world to hide the fact that you're not really human any more.

                              What's the point of combat abilities, why do any contract stuff at all, why go into the hedge, why do any court politics?
                              Being able to defend yourself is an important thing. Whether that's personal combat prowess, mystic powers, powerful Hedge-touched stuff, or the Courts.

                              Things are out to snatch up escaped changelings. Things that can see through the simple Mask that hides them from normal humans. When a Huntsman comes to town with a motley of Loyalists, what do you do? You can try to hide, but if you don't invest in powers they'll have more powerful detection abilities than you'll have ways to hide from them. You can fight them, but without something to fight them with, and preferably allies to beat back their numbers, you'll probably lose.

                              Do you just get a job and buy an apartment and then nothing.
                              Consider how hard this is in the real world for an illegal immigrant. No legal ID. No way to start bank accounts. No telephone or address. You can only work for people willing to break the law to hire you, and they have you by the balls. You get paid enough that if you share an apartment with six other people, you'll have enough money to buy food. Oh, and INS can come calling and deport you any day because one person let slip a group of illegal immigrants were around.

                              Changelings basically have all these problems and more. The "...and then nothing." only happens if the ST doesn't provide threats and risks. The world a changeling lives in is supposed to be dangerous enough to inspire them to join a Freehold/Court/Entitlement/Motley for protection. Those relationships lead to obligations, deals, and duties. The upside is they give you a chance for that normal life. Though downside is that they mean you never get to just "retire" and do nothing.

                              Consider you're playing Werewolf, and the ST never has spirits, Hosts, Pure, rival werewolf Packs, mortal hunters, or Rage come up. What do you do? Well... nothing really. Your pack patrols it's highly secure territory and nothing gets out of hand. Stories need antagonists because they help motivate action. Sure, some players will have character goals that general dramatic conflict without the ST needing to inject it on their own, but ultimately it falls on the ST to make sure there's conflict in the game to have something to do.

                                          It's called sarcasm.

                                          If I may speak on the front of not understanding, I want to get the following story out there.

                                          A few years ago, one of my friends wanted to play Mage the Ascension, and I got talked into playing a familiar. Never played Old World before, had no idea what the differences were, or even what Ascension was really about, and I was pretty sure playing a familiar was not something you normally did. Still, it was a new group, and I wanted to make a good impression.

                                          I cannot tell you the number of times I laughed, cheered, was shocked, and wept bitterly in that game. Easily the best experience I've ever had with a game. But I didn't learn what the game was about, really, or even the rules for my own character, until about two thirds of the way in. I just did the best I could, and this new group explained things as best they could while keeping things going. Kept thinking it had 10-again, turns out that's not true. But it didn't matter.

                                          Which comes back to one of the big things I harp on about with people here: you can't know what a game is like until you play it. All the forum conversation and reading in the world won't help you until you play the game. I've found it to be true time and time again.

                                          So, ignore my early-morning-grumpy self. Give it a shot. You might not know what you're doing. That's okay. That's what your friends are there to help you with. And you just might have the time of your life figuring it out. I did.

                                                      There are a lot of different things a group of Changelings could focus on: Freehold politics, exploring the Hedge, Oneiromancy, etc. Changelings do all of these things to protect themselves and others from the True Fae. They can't hope to maintain the new lives they build or the old lives they reclaim if they're not safe, and these things make a Changeling safer. Politics because a strong and alert Freehold is the best protection Changelings have from the Gentry, and a Freehold that doesn't like you or know who you are might not notice you go missing or care enough to come find you when you do. Exploring the Hedge so that they know the best escape routes when Privateers come for them and can find Goblin Fruits to heal their wounds after fighting Loyalists. Oneiromancy to protect the dreams of mortals from the True Fae and look for omens that might foretell one of the Others approaching. They might also do those things to gain power, or to advance some personal goals, or for Altruistic reasons to protect others, or just because they're curious. It's likely different motives for different members of the Motley. There are likely some Changelings who are content to just try and live their lives, and they're probably the first to get taken by the gang of Loyalists that's set up shop in the city or to be lured into a trap by a loved one who's dreams have been invaded by the Gentry. Changelings who just try to live their lives undisturbed, ignore the fear and paranoia as best they can, are often the most vulnerable. They should probably also be NPCs. Playing a character like that would be like making a blacksmith in Pathfinder and refusing to leave your smithy. Part of the onus is on the players to create characters that would be motivated to do interesting things, that are curious, or ambitious, or proactive.

                                                      For your first story, I'd recommend picking one thing that you're comfortable with and focus on it. Preferably after discussing it with your group. Maybe your players' characters are intrepid Hedge explorers that the Freehold might send to rescue Changelings stolen by Privateers, clear out nasty Hedge beasties, or find rare Goblin Fruits (probably the most familiar option for a D&D/Pathfinder player). Maybe they're super interested in Oneiromancy and focus on learning all about dreams and the creatures that inhabit them. Maybe the Freehold in your chronicle is falling apart and they have to overcome political feuds before agents of the Gentry take advantage of the weakness that comes from division and poor leadership. When you're up to it, it's great to include all of these things in a single Chronicle, probably with different characters focusing on different areas, but to start out I think it's fine to just focus on one thing. Add in other stuff as you get more comfortable. Don't necessarily worry too much about theme when you first start running it; a lot of the main themes of the game are baked in and you can emphasize them later on and add your own themes when you feel confident (and they might just develop naturally as you play).
