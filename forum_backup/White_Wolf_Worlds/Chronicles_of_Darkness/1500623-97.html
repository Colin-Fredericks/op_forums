    Originally posted by quelsec View Post
    Whats the point of unveiling practice? Lets say we have a spell to scan a living organism based on the knowing practice. If the user encounters a veiling effect on the subject it would still be a clash of wills to determine with effect is stronger would it not?
    Unveiling is about extending your sensory perception, while Knowing is about receiving information about a phenomenon directly. So, for example, an Unveiling will let you see ley lines you would not normally perceive, but a Knowing will tell you about what Resonance one has. An Unveiling will let you follow the ley lines to an interception, a Knowing will let you know the strength of the Hallow, if it's tass is volatile or plentiful, etc. An Unveiling will let you know something is tass, a Knowing will let you know if it has any unusual properties.

    Both Clash against a Veiling, but what you find out from winning the Clash will be different.

                Since Unveiling affects your own senses (or subject's senses) it allows you see things where ever you apply those senses (rather than just knowing something within a given spell area).

                So a knowing spell might be withstood by the subject you want to know about (Know Spirit is withstood by Rank, though Know Nature, the Mind equivalent, is not withstood, and nor are an other examples Knowing spells). But an Unveiling spell is cast on yourself - or another subject whose senses you want to enhance... So Unveiling spells would usually not be withstood (though Mental Scan is, telling me something is wrong, and that it is not actually an Unveiling spell... Likewise for the Death Spell Soul Marks)

                Meanwhile, the subject matters, you leave a signature Nimbus on your subject and a weak sympathetic connection between caster and subject. So Unveiling leaves less trace if you cast the unveiling spell on yourself.

                Lastly, with the Forces example spells, we have Tune-in (Knowing) and Reciever (Unveiling). Both let you gain information about signals (radio and infrasound signals respectively) but the knowing effect tell you what the signal means, so you don't need to translate, decrypt or use Mind's purview of communication; whereas the Unveiling effect just let's you hear whatever is there... So you might know there is a Whalesong, but not what the Whale is singing about.

                                                Originally posted by orathaic View Post

                                                Pretty much every increased spell factor makes a spell harder to counteract in some way and Scale is the least durable factor of the ones that exist beyond the moment of casting; Scale is the least durable of those three spell factors and the portion of the game's design goals you're talking about were to reduce the expected amount of interdisciplinary expertise just to affect a given type of monster (e.g. being clear that you don't need both Life and Spirit to affect a werewolf the way you would anything else with a body made of flesh).

                                                Dispel Magic's default effect in 1e is a Reach effect in 2e and it contends with a much flatter defensive trait. The need to be a magical generalist to most safely erase any spell you come across is not a new feature and doing surgery on a spell to pare down who it's affecting is in line with other things Fate does.

                                                But I think I would rather not have "random hidden rock" as a way to defend a spell, like by default, Dispel Magic is cast at touch range, but just touching one subject should allow you to affect the entire spell (rather than needing to touch every subject) in the same way touching the edge of a building allows you to cast a building sized AoE spell on an entire building.
                                                I have been humoring you on the "random hidden rock" scenario because the concept of keeping an essential vulnerability out of the way is a well-established trope for supernatural framework, but the notion of targeting a Subject-Scale spell to something that the spell will specifically not have an actual effect on just to make it harder to dispel is the kind of thing basically any ST is either going to say no to or remind you is also a valid tactic for NPCs to employ if you abuse it.

                                                The default for every spell is that it affects one subject at touch range. The default is also that spells last less than thirty seconds and can't affect an unwilling target with peak human [Resistance Trait], but those latter two get freebies that scope or headcount don't, and the obvious primary use-case of Dispel Magic is for getting rid of a discrete magical effect that you can look at in its entirety. The conjunctional Fate effect exists for roughly the same reason Warding Gesture is an explicit flexible exemption clause for your AoE effects in contrast to, say, shaping the set boundaries of your AoE spells to avoid the immediate location of things you don't want it to affect.

                                                Being forced to account for the number of subjects, without actually touching each one seems enough to me.
                                                This gets into the other end of the problem, where you can shut down a master's long-standing defenses because they happened to cast them on all of their apprentices at the same time for efficiency's sake but you still need Fate to only strip the warding off of one of them. A spell cast on a platoon of soldiers should not be able to be removed just by getting into contact with one of those soldiers.

                                                Analogy to Imperial Practices is a nightmare because propagative effects like those in Dynamics and Entities ultimately don't seem like they engage with Scale and Seekers' spells are exempt from most methods of elimination, but there's a reason Quintessence makes a qualitative distinction between what you need to do to cure one person's vampirism and what you need to do to wipe out a whole bloodline of vampires.
