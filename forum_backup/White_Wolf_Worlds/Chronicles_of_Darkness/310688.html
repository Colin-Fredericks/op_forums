    Mamma mia! Here we go again…

    11-12-2014, 07:41 PM
    Preface:

    It is a pure coincidence that made me write this post. See, I have one player who really digs into Ancient Egypt/Mummies themes. He really loves first 2 “Mummy” films, has 2 latest “Tomb Kings” Armybooks for WhF and a half painted army of Necrons for WH40k. It is by him (and pure chance) that I have learned about Mummy the Curse.

    Honestly – I was quite indifferent for the cWoD Mummies to the point of “Meh…”. I wasn’t expecting much about this new iteration of the game either.

    But what I wasn’t expecting at all was this vaguely Egypt themed gameline’s treatment of Russia.

    I was told repeatedly on this and other forums (and IRL by a few of nWoD enthusiasts) that New WoD is more “realistic” and “de-camped” then the original one.

    After reading this one passage in MtC core I don’t believe in that any longer.

    Stuff:

    So, in “Showcase: Prokneft” I, Russian from Russia living in Russia have a “pleasure” to read this:

    For centuries, the Shchavichev comprised one of the most potent Arisen cults in the region of what would, in time, become Russian territories
    The first thing that “Arised” was the question – “How the hell do you pronounce Shchavichev?”. What authors of this book know about Russian naming conventions? Whatever they know – it is apparently not enough to make some real Russian (pronounceable by both Russians and foreigners) name. Have they even Googled it before hand, just to check – is it a real last name at all?

    So this book failed where all its others predecessors failed also – it failed to make a real Russian name. And the further one reads this one particular sidebar the worse it gets.

    With the rise of the Soviet Union and the militarization leading up to the second World War, the Shchavichev recognized that they would not be able to keep their existence, or that of their tiny community, secret for much longer. They needed allies in high places, someone who could protect them from Stalin’s military and secret police.

    They found one in Jaska Prokhorov, the patron of a family powerful in Soviet military and industrial circles. Through a series of marriages over the course of the following generation—some of which might have been “helped along” by the necromancies of the cult’s mummies—the Shchavichev tied themselves inextricably to the Prokhorov line.
    {Sigh}

    Really? Have these unknown authors ever learned the history of USSR?

    Well lets start with the obvious. They (again) botched their Linguistics roll (probably without considering Google-checking before and after the roll). There is no such Russian name as “Jaska”. No! It doesn’t exists! Look, I just searched it in Wikipedia and it found only this. Because I’m not a complete idiot I presume that the name this Nameless Authors tried to insert here was “Jakov” (like your English Jacob), whose diminutive/shortened everyday form is “Jashka”. I know that – I had a grandfather named Jakov. But we are talking about a proper name here, a name used officially, in documents and by strangers. I can’t be a diminutive.

    Next – Nameless Authors show their total lack of knowledge about the Stalinist period of USSR history. They (and this Siberian family with unpronounceable last name) apparently have no knowledge how the Soviet Union functioned under Stalin – and. What is more importantly, how repressions worked here. First of all – putting all your eggs in one basket and betting on one individual higher up in the Soviet bureaucracy was tantamount for mass suicide. It was precisely this category of people targeted by StateSec as potential traitors and spies. Not only a person in question would be charged with crimes and swiftly prosecuted for them – his entire family, relatives and close associates would also be “taken notice of” by GPU (later - MGB). If lucky, they would only be “blackballed” by Agencies, hindering their prospects in career advancement everywhere. Should they commit any crimes themselves then their former association with “enemy of the people” would be resurfaced damaging their already meager chances of getting a light sentence. At worst – all associates and relatives of the person repressed would get 20 years in labor prison camp/exile with confiscation of the property. Good luck for these cultists in helping their immortal masters while in Magadan or the Central Asia!

    Next – authors do not understand the way power and influence worked in various circles in the USSR. For starters – there were no “industrial circles” here. At all. Have the Nameless Authors heard about such “minor” event as the October Revolution and the following nationalization of the industry? Next – “military circles” (whatever the Nameless Ones understand by that) were completely decimated during Stalin’s purges. Like – extremely. It is not some arcane knowledge – the info about the scale of Stalin’s repressions against his own military are well documented and readily available in the net.

    Finally – I don’t know what these Unaussprechlichen Cultists thought about when they planed to create a dynasty of people in power in the USSR. It just didn’t work that way. The fact that your parents belonged to the elite meant only one thing under Stalin – that you will lose literally everything the moment you parents will become target of GPU/MGB scrutiny. There were no political dynasties like in the other countries. One had only to look at Stalin’s treatment of his own children to see that.

    So, this cult just makes no sense to anyone who knows Russian history. This weird cultists basically staked their entire future on a man already wearing “Line against the wall and shoot me” on his back.

    But this is not the lowest point, oh no! It is this:

    When the Soviet Union fell, the Prokhorov were among the various families and businesses whose wealth and influence skyrocketed. Today, their primary business identity is Prokneft, a major Russian oil company. Based out of Novosibirsk, Siberia, this company maintains the family’s profits and wealth…
    The current chairman, Makari Prokhorov, is a veritable genius in international, political, and occult affairs. He worked, during the last decades of the Soviet Union, as a KGB analyst, and while he has zero field experience, his knowledge is substantial.
    This is so bad, so bad… Nameless Authors don’t know not only USSR history – they don’t know the history of Modern Russia as well. I ask them to name just one “family and business” (dating back to USSR, as they imply) “whose wealth and influence skyrocketed”. I can wait! Not that I expect them to find any…

    Another teeth cringing moment come in the form of “Makari Prokhorov”. It is so bad on many levels… Lets start that he belonged to KGB. As I have noted in my earlier post, WoD authors have some unhealthy desire to lump all Russian NPCs into KGB, probably without even knowing a thing about the organization in question. This is a tiresome cliché that only demonstrates Nameless Authors narrow-mindedness and propensity to resort to stereotypes while writing about “exotic” setting, instead of actually researching the subject.

    Next – this very same authors decided that “third time is a charm” and come up with “Makari Prokhorov”. They bothched it. And even English Wikipedia won’t help them here. There is no such name as “Makari” in Russian language. If not via Wikipedia then using Google-fu they might found out that there are no Russians named “Makari”. OTOH, there is name “Macar” (with pronounceable “r” at the end). It was fairly common before the Revolution but now is very rare in Russian. I’ve met my own share of people with “old-fashioned” and just plainly odd names, but none of them was “Macar”.

    Finally, this “Makari Prokhorov” persona, CEO of the big Oil company in Russia (read – “an oligarch”) looks like a real life Mikhail Prokhorov’s alter ego. Nameless Authors – this is just plain lazy and unimaginative for you. Yeah, he is not as “impressive” (read – “clichéridden”) as this… thing you’ve “imagined”. He is ¼ Jewish, born in Moscow and even never served in army (let alone had not connections with KGB). His international travels were noted primarily by his escapades with prostitutes in Swiss Courchevel. What was the point of ripping off a real person? Oh, while we are at it - when shall we expect the supernatural versions of Lady Gaga and Silvio Berlusconi?

    Just one Sidebar spoiled my whole of entire book. Before that I had at least some interest in game and even tried to imagine the whole thing as actually playable. And then – this. I had to ask myself – what other crazy and done to death clichés and national stereotypes are lurking in other nWoD books?

                      The thing is “Макарий” is not a proper “Russian” – it’s a Greek name adapted literally by the church members. After the ceremony of taking holy vows of the Orthodox Church the person abandoned his or her name and took a proper “Church” name, nearly universally – a Greek one. Common people have adapted Greek names to sound more Russian, when they decided to give them to their children. So, there is still Russian named “Alexey” (Алексей), but the proper name for a clergy person is “Alexy” (Алексий) like the late Patriarch.

                      So, if they wanted to give their kid “old-fashioned” name it should be “Macar”, not “Makarij”. Besides, in the Soviet Union it was actively discouraged to give the children “pre-Revolutionary” names. Really discouraged.

                              Speaking about supposed "de-campiness" of NWoD, how can one ignore the "Carthians" book cover? Cossack's shashka, kindjal from Caucasus, oversized Hammer & Sickle on a Red Star badge, black leathered "chekist's" kozhanka, and Mauser pistol. The only things lacking - a giant dirigible saying "Kirov reporting" and Ushanka wearin', balalayka playin', vodka swingin' and unicycle ridin' bears.

                              But back to this particular mess in MtC!

                              Here are some suggestions (from the IMHO category) on how to make it more realistic (and to prove, that I'm capable of not only destructive criticism):

                              - First of all - do exactly opposite of what the Nameless Authors suggest. The most sure way of surviving Stalin's time wasn't in clinging to exiting power structures on Party, Army or GPU/NKVD/MGB, but in keeping a low profile. They are just one remote village between Tomsk and Novosibirsk? Excellent! This means that they won't be affected by Collectivization and Industrialization of that period. They are not city dwellers either. All these factors already give them good chances of surviving 1930-50s unscathed. Trust me. My own family originally hails from small town of Artyomovsk, later moving to slightly bigger Alapayevsk. They were of "proper" worker-peasant background, without any connections to either Czarist officials or the potential "spies and traitors". No one was repressed. No one.

                              - OTOH, 23 of my relatives took part in the Great Patriotic War. Only 2 survived to its end - my grand-grandfathers maternal uncle, who was a common soldier, who miraculously survived Stalingrad battle with only one wound, and another one, who was the Northern Fleet officer on some gunboat protecting Allied convoys heading to Murmansk.

                              So, GPW provides for them an excellent opportunity to (safely) enmesh themselves into the Soviet power structures. The obvious way here - is joining the Army. Siberia provided a lot of volunteers, who were often crucial in turning the tide in many major battles, including (but not limited to) battle for Moscow in Autumn-Winter of 1941. It wasn't unheard of for people to make a military career during the war. Cultists from that village may be especially lucky to survive all the war and finish it in Berlin "helping" in cataloguing and packing trophies - including the ones from Berlin's Historical Museums.

                              - After the War there would be more opportunities open for them and their relatives. Some may decide to continue pursuing military career (given that Stalin won't be alive soon they had better chance of surviving all those politicking and backstabbing). But there is also another golden opportunity - Soviet academia. They may become geologists and civil engineers, later sent to their little corner of Siberia and making sure that local development goes well (and avoids as much as possible their home village). Or they may become ethnologists and folklorists, studying Siberian native tribes, local Starovery or some old customs still practised in remote villages. Again - only to ensure that it will be them who reports anything about their home village, and reports only "unclassified" data.

                              - Or a few can become involved in Mummy affairs more directly. In this scenario, a chosen few cultists will become a noted Egyptologists of the USSR. Given rather friendly attitude of Abdel Nasser towards USSR, it will be easy for them to not only travel to Egypt but also to gain access to its historically important sites. And if not members of intelligentsia, then military-connected cultists might find their way to the Middle East region as "instructors" and "cultural attaches" engaged in a different kind of skullduggery.

                              - Finally - about Perestroika and "Wild 90s" period. The people who capitalized the most during this period of chaos and ruin wasn't top-level party-connected established elites, but low-key functionaries and literally "random people from streets" who had will (and lacked principles) to carry out large scale "privatization" (most of the time - illegal) and get away with that.

                              Enter Makar Prokhorov.

                              His family was distantly related to this "mysterious village" but lost any ties to the cult. By the whim of fate (or not) he starts buying land and industries for few rubles in the later days of USSR in Siberia. He needs local management and general people whom he can trust. Enter some distant relatives from the village Savicheyevo. Makar can't believe his luck - it turns out he has well placed relatives throughout all former USSR (and, of course, in Israel as well) among whom are geologists and engineers (which will come handy in upkeeping and upgrading his new Oil Rigs and in searching new sources of oil), former officers of now defunct Red Army and "Agencies" who lost their job and position (which will come handy in organizing "private security" and just plainly surviving "Wild 90s"). All those "newly found" relatives no matter how distant actually wanted to help him and prove themselves highly capable individuals. Their conditions are unbelievably mild - to help financially less fortunate relatives connected with Russian academy circles (and particular interest in Egypt and ethnology) who suddenly lost all governmental funding to carry out their research.

                              One way or another, but thanks to his relatives (hailing from "simple, remote Siberian village") and who prove themselves extremely helpful, Makar Prokhorov not only survives the 90s, but actually prospers and becomes one of the Russian oligarchs.

                              Enter Vladimir Putin.

                              The way how he "dealt" with Khodorkovsky and his oil company "UKOS" was a clear and very unsubtle message to all other oligarchs, that rules of the Game have changed. Some panicked. Some fled. Some cave in. But not Prokhorov. Oh, surely, he considered all three options one night a decade ago. After this one night of "consideration" he found himself with a terrible hangover in a village in the middle of nowhere. Thankfully, his chief of security (a distant relative from this parts), CFO (also from somewhere in Siberia) and his Orthodox Confessor (ditto) were with him here. They had a Talk, followed by a Deal. As a result, Makar not only retained all of his unjustly acquired fortune (as if there were any other in the 90s!) but even retained a (relative) independence.

                              Now, ten years later, he strikes a rather untypical figure for a Russian oligarch. He is not an eccentric spender (his yacht is not as large as Abramovich's and he don't own any foreign sport club - only local one), in fact he spends a lot of his own money on local schools, museums and operas. His days of orgy-style "corporatives" with excess of drugs, alcohol and prostitutes are long in the past - he is a model husband of his former CFO and a father of 4 now. He doesn’t own any estates or palaces abroad - only a number of simple and functional condos all around the world, which he allows his employees to use while he is away. And, more importantly given Russian realities, he doesn’t even think about financing any oppositionist movement or taking "suggestions" from the US State Department. In fact, he (after some consultations with his closes advisers and board of directors) offered to the Russian government his company to serve as an intermediary during the negotiations with new Egyptian regime.

                              All in all, Makar Prokhorov is a happy man. Now he only rarely have a nightmare about a night in a distant Siberian village and an unspeakable thing, whom his "relatives" served all this time.
