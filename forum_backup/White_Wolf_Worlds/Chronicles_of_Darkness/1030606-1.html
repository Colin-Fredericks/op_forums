    It's worth bearing in mind that breaking free of a grapple is a reflexive action, meaning that armed characters have a chance to disentangle and use their weapons if their strength + brawl pool isn't likely to win subsequent rounds.

    Still, it's an exceptionally effective way to lock down an armed target. Defense isn't that great of, well, a defense because grapplers have very little incentive not to spend a willpower and all out attack to initiate if they're confident they can win the subsequent rolls.

    Also, given that we're already talking Hurt Locker merits in this thread, it's hard to imagine a character investing in grappling as a skill and not taking Clinch Strike, the one dot merit with minimal prereqs that allows you to deal damage with the same roll that initiates the grapple.

                                The rules for moving to grappling actions specify that they occur on the turn (yes, I know what it means) subsequent to the previous turn, as I quoted from the CofD core. The only exception is the ability to use a grappling move if you score an exceptional success. The function of a grab is to otherwise begin a grapple, but the grapple doesn't actually impose itself on both parties until the turn after the grab.

                                I mean I'll tell you right out: Grappling doesn't work right if you immediately establish a grapple upon a grab, and back in 2003, when we were fretting over this rule in its playtest (it carries over from the old rules), grabbing someone did not establish a grapple on the same turn. Game design being what it is this might have mutated over the intervening 13 years and WW rules sets are notoriously subjective (for a reason) but in this case I strongly recommend that, well, you do it the way I say it.

                                                It's a bit more complicated than "what would you do?" because not every Weaponry based fighter is built the same way.

                                                The optimal way to avoid getting grappled is to pick up Merits that allow you to boost your Defense and apply damage to your opponent. Armed Defense 3 + 4 with a decent damage weapon can easily stack the odds in your favor (at least until you run out of WP) since you're going to be insanely hard to hit and you have good odds of doing lethal to in return. It even can resist multiple opponents for a bit (and if Beaten Down is in effect, it's pretty simple to apply the Tilt to all of a group of attackers and then try to wait them out).

                                                The other primary option is to do what most people that engage in life-or-death violence on a regular basis do: diversify. Anyone expected to be ready to fight in a serious situation is trained in multiple disciplines. Cops don't just use guns. They learn to wield various weapons and how to fight unarmed. Same goes for soldiers. Pretty much every fencing/sword-fighting manual from history clearly shows that training to use a sword also included training in things that would fall under Brawl in the CofD system.

                                                If your character only bothers to learn how to shoot, or how to swing a blade, or whatever focus, they're vulnerable to tactics designed to shut that down. That's why people that have to fight try to avoid being one-trick ponies.

                                                    Avoiding being grappled is pretty important for other types of unarmed styles too, for that matter. All the fancy Martial Arts moves do nothing if you're locked in a grapple and lose the roll. It's less of an issue for mortals, since the things that divide grappling from other styles are largely merits that increase your options after a successful roll, rather than boosts to said roll, meaning there's a greater chance for the characters to have roughly even odds. When you get to supernaturals though there's quite a few ways to get that pool crazy high.
                                                    Luckily, the alternate Martial Arts style in Hurt Locker has a great maneuver called Inch Force which can break a grapple before it begins.
