    On alchemists and insanity. I find it rather interesting (at least to me) that the Conditions that the alchemists suffer from are Addiction and Obsession. They're like miniture mages here, just powered by Pyros instead of the Supernal. Its like... mages are obsessive, mad creators who give metaphorical birth to flawed creations. Meanwhile, Prometheans ARE the flawed creations that have issues with their obsessed creators. A rather interesting dichotomy, akin to vampires and werewolves, a cycle that is eternally destined to butt heads against each other because they're really flip sides of the same coin.


    From my personal perspective, the difference between the so-called "nice" and "bad" alchemists are like the divide between the Pentacle and Seers - in short, Wisdom to know when to not exploit living creatures for your own profit and benefit. Both mages and alchemists are basically trans-humanists that magically enhance their bodies into something more than humans. Both push themselves into grand magical conspiracies. Honestly, the only real difference to me is one relies on pyros, and the other on mana. By the same token, if you made pyros and mana the same thing, then you could run the game with mages replacing the alchemists!

Anyway, 100% agree with you. The wider problem is, Promethean has a love affair with the surface trappings and aesthetics of alchemy but not the actual philosophy, so it ends up being pretty janky if you've actually studied perspectives on alchemy. They get some stuff right, but the OP's devs irrational dislike of transhumanism and immortality get in the way. Historical alchemists were very much in favor of immortality. They were basically transhumanist utopians- they believed they could fix society by helping people live forever and curing all diseases. Saint-Germain, perhaps the archetypal alchemist, was famed for his supposed immortality, and was seen as a highly moral, principled, and altruistic man who used his extended life to help others.

                                            Promethean and Mage toy with gnostic themes, but they're afraid of taking them too far. For example, notice how Dave comes down like a sack of bricks on anyone who suggests that mortality might be part of the Lie.
