        Originally posted by Tabanese View Post
        What is the relationship between vitae pools and feeding scenes?

        I ask because I reread them to refresh myself on the RAW and am confident I misread them the first time.

        I don't understand your question. Are you asking how much Vitae an average human contains?

        A human has an amount of Vitae equal to his Health boxes. If you take the last Vitae from a human, he either immediately dies or is in critical condition and will die without serious medical intervention (depending on the Storyteller).

        Are you asking how the current amount of Vitae the player has affects his feeding scene? Once more, this is up to the Storyteller. Being hungry (4 or less Vitae) or starving (2 or less Vitae) is a frenzy trigger, and the moment that sweet red nectar enters your vampire's mouth, the Beast might try to take over, trying to completely drain the human to the last drop of Vitae. This could result in a Resolve + Composure roll with a -2 (hungry) or -4 (starving) penalty if the player character wants to fight the urge to drain the human.

        Are you asking how much Vitae you can take per feeding scene? Again, depends on the ST. Some Storytellers allow you to choose how much Vitae you take from the human, and afterwards describe the medical condition the human is in after the feeding (completely fine, a bit woozy, knocked out, dying, dead, etc.). Other Storytellers want to do feeding scenes very intimately, going through the scene on a turn by turn basis, and describing what's happening each passing turn, until you decide to stop sucking blood.

        I'm personally a fan of having my players roll a die to see how much Vitae they gain from the feeding, and I let my players decide which die they wanna roll ( a d4, d6, d8 or a d10, choosing a higher die will potentially yield more Vitae, but it also increases the chance you drain the human dry, killing him in the process), but this is by no means RAW.
        I combine this with randomized health for the humans to make feeding more risky and more unpredictable. Not all humans always have full health. Some are sick, some have been in a fight, and some have already been fed on. For humans likely to end up fighting, getting sick or getting fed on, I roll a die to see how much bashing/lethal wounds they already have at the start of the feeding scene, usually a d4 or d6. I always give my players an small indicator of their prey's health, and they are allowed to roll Wits + Medicine checks to further diagnose their prey before going for the bite.
        I don't always do this though. Sometimes I do feeding scenes "down and dirty", allowing my players to make a hunting roll and the amount of successes on their roll is the amount of Vitae they gained, without really going deep into the scene. Sometimes I just don't want to waste time on feeding scenes.
        Again, this is by no means RAW, I'm just sharing this to give you some ideas on how you can handle feeding scenes.

                That's how it can be done (as I said in my previous post, I sometimes do it that way if my players want to feed quickly, "down and dirty"-style, in between major events), but by no means how it should be done.

                What Satchel said is right. RAW the hunting roll is merely to establish whether a feeding encounter happens and if it happens on the vampire's terms or not. After the roll, the feeding scene is played out, and either the player chooses how much Vitae he wishes to take from the human, or the Storyteller chooses how much Vitae the player gains, or the scene might be handled in a turn-by-turn basis, gaining an amount of Vitae equal to your Vitae/turn limit for each turn your're feeding, as the Storyteller describes the progression of the scene on a turn-by-turn basis. Or, you can do it my way, and have the players roll dice to determine how much Vitae they gain.

                And no, I don't think the feeding mechanics have changed in any significant way, but my memories on the 1e rules are a little fuzzy and I don't have the book with me.
