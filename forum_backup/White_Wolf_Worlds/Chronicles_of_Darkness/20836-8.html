        Another problem with Kaiju, is that...what about Miasma? What about the secrecy of Oroboroi? Does a Kaiju have Miasma? How can they manage to be so unseen?
        I've thought about that earlier today and the solution is actually quite simple. Over the course of modern human history as we know it, the Nidhoggr come infrequently. Most of the time, they're asleep or in other realms and prior to humanity getting connected to the internet, communication was pretty damn hard (Lord knows how many primary sources have gone up in flames). A Egyptian town could be attacked, it's resident Oroboroi's heart torn asunder, and the Nidhoggr would vanish back into the sea before the rest of the empire would realize something was up. At best, they would chalk it up to bandits, a natural disaster, or even the Gods just getting pissed at them (ironically, the last wouldn't be too far off).

        To be completely honest, the destruction of Pompeii would make an great historical setting of where a Nidhoggr is waking from Mt Vesuvius and the residential Oroboroi have to deal with it.

        In short, a good reason why the Nidhoggr haven't been discovered/messed with is less having to do with the Miasma and more to do with the fact of the long periods (Centuries long) between both their attacks and the rest of the world learning about the town's destruction which in all honestly would be chalked up to a natural disaster of some sort.

        Though now the world is connect to the internet so I would guess that would only be a matter of time before a Nidhoggr awakens and heads to a major city.

        On that note, I would suspect San Fran would have a Nidhoggr buried within it's depths. The "Big One" is overdue after all.

        If secrecy was required of the Nidhoggr, I would think they are more than capable of shape shifting to a human form and managing an human organization but they aren't subtle creatures by nature. If they are planning secrecy, it would be isolating a town's communication network, shutting down the power, and then hunting the residential Oroboroi down in the guise of a natural disaster.

        edit- A more modern take would be the eruption at Mt Saint Helens in which a Nidhoggr awakened, killed everyone in the area, honed in on a Oroboro's heart who was caught in the blast, and then vanished.

        God only knows what lies in Yellowstone..

                                        Okay, so to recap:

                                        We've got two leading antagonists at the moment, the Nidhoggr and the Shayatin. Here's a summary of what we've discussed about each.

                                        Nidhoggr
                                        Nidhoggr, or the Elders, are natural-born dragons that survived the usurpation of the Oroboroi by fleeing to the corners of the world and the corners of reality (the ocean floors and the depths of the Treasury). They are giant, they are powerful, and they are PISSED! Nidhoggr have one goal: to reclaim the stolen Hearts of their relatives, and they even possess the ability to sense such Hearts from great distance. When seeking the heart of a relative, a Nidhoggr gains +3 to the Heartbeat perception and may sense the Heart even from beyond the confines of the Oroboros' lair.
                                        Nidhoggr attack like any Kaiju would, by knocking down buildings and generally squishing everything in their path. As natural-born dragons they are completely in control of the Typhoon effect and use it to their advantage by hiding their actions under the veil of natural disasters. Any humans who happen to notice them during the torrential storms or terrible eruptions are subject to the Miasma just as if they had witnessed an Oroboros; no one will believe their story.
                                        A sufficiently powerful Oroboros might be able to do battle with a Nidhoggr, but they are usually too large to confront directly. These terrifying antediluvian beasts need to be attack en masse if there is any hope of driving them back to the corners they crawled out from.

                                        Shayatin
                                        Shayatin, or the Forgotten, are beings abandoned by reality, clinging to their last tethers of warmth that grant them meaning. Lacking memory and personality they are little more than objects to be tread upon by the world around them. But the Shayatin desire form, desire an identity on which they may anchor meaning to their actions. They are filled with the dark power of void and are willing to trade this darkness for the shining treasure of potential.
                                        It is believed by the Oroboroi in particular that the Shayatin were once great gods as the dragons are themselves, but their pursuit of power stripped away everything that defined them until they were little more than empty husks, squirming like amoeba in the vast expanses of the Treasury. In this way they are beings worthy of pity; a grand divinity reduced to a bottom feeder by hubris. However, Shayatin are rarely satisfied with their position.
                                        When a Shayatin makes a bargain with another being (usually a mortal or an Oroboros), it offers to unlock the power of that being's own darkness in exchange for a piece of their reality: a sense, a memory, a defining event in their past... anything the Shayatin might use to construct a new identity. The bargaining being gains the malevolent might of their own darkest desires, while the Shayatin gains one more piece of warmth it may use to cobble together a viable identity. As a Shayatin makes bargains it receives form as payment, filling its empty skin with ideas and limbs until it is a veritable horror of form and rage. These beings are never satisfied, however; no matter the number of bargains they make they always desire more form, more identity to fill the gaps in their perforated soul. Any who encounters one of these beings must beware, lest they too fall to the same empty fate.
                                        An empty Shayatin has much power to offer, but is relatively easy to bargain with. As the Forgotten collects more identity they become increasingly talented negotiators, demanding more and more for less. Sufficiently filled Forgotten may even simply take what they want by force.

                                                    Well, what you were describing made me picture other Dragons, actually, before I realized you were talking about human beings. With another dragon, you either compromise by forming a Den with them or you risk them stealing your Assets when they inevitably overlap. With mortals, though, you keep them happy or they revolt, damaging your assets in the process. Both are worth exploring in the Storytelling and Antagonists chapter, but I don't think we need another supernatural entity to fill that slot, at least for the core book.

                                                    As for the B-Splats, we currently have four:

                                                    Dragonborn: Akin to wolf-blooded, the Dragonborn are mortals with Oroboroi heritage, be it parents, grandparents, or great-grandparents (though no further than that, the usurper potency does not last many generations severed from its power source). Dragonborn start with some extra talent as described in the merit and they benefit from the prospect of gaining higher Furnace upon their coronation (based on a roll of the merit dots, again as described in the merit). They are otherwise normal human beings.

                                                    Brood-Bonded: Similar to a Vampire's Ghoul, a Brood-Bonded consumes a piece of the Oroboros Heart and gains the ability to learn Ablutions that his dragon benefactor knows (or rather, he learns how to borrow Ablutions from her). Being Brood-Bonded will extend the mortal's lifespan but also spells death (or at least serious medical complication) if the Heart changes owners. Oroboroi that undertake the ritual share degeneration/breaking points with their bonded mortal.

                                                    Pilot Lights: Pilot Lights are just like Brood-Bonded except all of the drawbacks have been removed; they are born with one ninth of a dragon Heart in their body. As written, they require the joining of two True Heart Oroboroi (which may not be in the game come the closure of the third development phase, so if we're keeping them we may need alternate criteria for the creation of a Pilot Light). The only major drawback a Pilot Light faces is that some dragons wish to cut them up and use their Heart fragments to cobble together a new Heart. I'll discuss ideas for this B-Splat later in the post.

                                                    Spartoi: Spartoi are sown men, they spring into being when a dragon plants one of her teeth as if it were a seed. Spartoi have above-average talents and can make use of Edicts; they also serve as a mouthpiece through which their creating Oroboros may proclaim her own Edicts. Outside that, they spring to life fully grown and thus have no credentials, credit rating, social security number, or identity of any sort. Physically, Spartoi are close clones of their creator.
                                                    Alternatively, the Spartoi can be taken more figuratively (as proposed by Frogs), sown into the 'soil' of an expectant mother to bless the child with draconic talent. I'll discuss ideas for this B-Splat later in the post.


                                                    Okay, so we've got four B-Splats and most game lines only have one, and some of them supply more value to the game than others. Time for some constructive pruning.
                                                    Starting with the Spartoi, I know exactly why they didn't turn out well in the CIJ half-book. I was trying to make an "Edict" equivalent to the Brood-Bonded, but it just doesn't work in practice. Edicts are significantly more powerful than Ablutions are, and their thematic stance as a dragon's stubbornness bending reality should not extend to third parties (Dark Heroes are of course the exception, but let's not get into that now). The more I thought about them as an entity the more I realized THEY are actually just another form of Dragonborn. In fact, all Dragonborn are just the result of a dragon planting a piece in soil (yes, it's an uncomfortable metaphor, but it's a common one in Greek mythology). To that end, I propose that the Dragonborn merit absorb the Spartoi's origins (but not its functions, as it is mechanically unbalanced) as a possible explanation for owning the Dragonborn merit (That is, a Dragonborn could be the result of a human-Oroboros union or could be completely unrelated to the Oroboros, her power planted within a mother before the child was born). We could call the merit Spartoi (to create a little distance from TESV Skyrim) and we might even keep the 'proclaiming Edicts through the child' option as a drawback to characters taking this merit.

                                                    As for the Pilot Lights, they're just so... well, overpowered. I was thinking about it, and perhaps Pilot Lights should be the result of combining the Dragonborn and Brood-Bonded merits: if a character uses all seven of their starting dots to purchase Brood-Bonded 5 and Dragonborn 2, they are actually a Pilot Light, born with a fragment of a dragon Heart and possessing the ability to learn and manifest some Ablutions. This change has several advantages: first it lets us rate Pilot Light at a much higher Merit rating than the system usually allows, second it eliminates the need for True Heart Oroboroi to be involved in the creation of a Pilot Light (They just happen sometimes from a dragon-human union or a Spartoi blessing), third it gives Players a motivation to actually conduct Spartoi blessings, and fourth it folds in some duplicate mechanics to save space in the second chapter.


                                                    So, if we take those two points into consideration the number of B-Splats goes down from 4 to 2.5, with one being the 'kinfolk' style B and the other being the 'retainer leech' style B. Does anybody have any opinions to share on these? Is 2.5 Bs still too many? Or is it too few? I'm eager to hear the opinions of others on this matter.
