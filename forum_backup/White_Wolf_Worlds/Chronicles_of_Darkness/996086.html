      I think the problem is that the first three levels of Auspex have no duration. When you ask if anyone is using Auspex they've already gotten their answers and isn't using the power anymore.
      When it comes to Auspex 4 it's instead a matter of what limits the ST has put on Auspex. Are you able to ask about what disciplines other vampires have active at all if they're not external? It's up to the ST, but I'd disallow it.
      In our group we use an updated version of the devotion Arcane Sight (Auspex 2, any Blood Sorcery 1) to both reveal supernatural effects with Auspex 1-3, and target supernatural effects with Auspex 3.

              Hm, using Auspex is instantaneous, true. That said, I see no reason why level 2 couldn't allow you to determine if someone possesses Auspex. It woudn't necessarily allow you to know if they used it (although it's usually probable) or what level they have in it (although why not a general indication of whether they're good at it or not), but detecting an Auspex user doesn't seem absurd. It'd probably have to be a precise question, not just "what kind of powers does this guy have ?" but more like "does that vampire have Auspex?".

              Or you could roll for the level 2, asking what disciplines they have and each success identifies a discipline. To avoid making it overpowered, it'd be better to play on the vague nature of the visions. Less subtle disciplines like the physical ones would be more obvious (vampire looks totally ripped from a certain angle, their skin looks rough like stone, their movement feels too quick to be natural...), the rest less so. The more exotic disciplines would give weirder info making them unidentifiable if you don't know it.

                              So first of all, no, there's not really a way to detect if someone is using Auspex 1 or 2 by the rules as written. That said, you could always make something up, like a Devotion or Bloodline Gift that allows it. Maybe the Keeper of Elysium has a special Devotion involving Auspex and Animalism that pings her spidey senses whenever someone uses a Discipline in her territory.

                              Second of all, it sounds like your primary concern is with characters finding out NPCs secrets "too soon." But how do you define "too soon?" What makes this particular Elysium gathering an inappropriate time for the PCs to be able to learn this information? What is the appropriate time for them to learn it, and what makes it appropriate?

                              In my opinion, there's no such thing as information being revealed "too soon." I am creating a story with my players, not for them, and the moment they use their characters' abilities and their own ingenuity to uncover a piece of information is precisely the moment they were "supposed" to discover it.

                                      That, basically.

                                      Plus, I guess that depends on the GM, but to me, Auspex 4, when it comes to digging memoeires, is only useful if you kind of know what you're looking for. You're usually not gonna get big secrets by looking randomly into their mind. You're just as likely to stumble upon the memory of their last meal or that they came close to frenzy yesterday when they stuck their little toe while being low on blood.

                                      The more passive aspect of monitoring their mind is more problematic, but not hopeless either : "...the vampire can discern the victim’s precise mood and intention, and some snippets of her current motivation and considerations." Obviously, if the players are being total douchebags and, say, plan to use auspex 4 on every important vampire in the elyseum for half an hour until nobody's left, they might stumble upon something interesting. Without that, they would have to be at least a little lucky to get really sensitive information.

                                      As a security measure, smart vampires might only want to socialize superficially in the main parts of the elyseum, but get behind closed doors when they want to discuss private or important business. An important elyseum will also have security. Some of that security might be vampires or ghouls with auspex that make sure nobody's using telepathy to ruin the party. Whether this is made public or not is up to you (vampires might not trust the security to be neutral and suspect them of fishing for their secrets), but maybe the vampires the players want to spy on are on good terms with the owner of the elyseum and would be warned if the players were caught looking into their brain.

                                      Finally, a rare Auspex + Resilience devotion could justify a higher resistance to Auspex 4, and if you want something kinda legit, there's the Ordo Dracul merit series "mind of the..." which, at level 3 makes it harder to do stuff to your mind. You could also have Indomitable, but if you read the merit, it's only a penalty to powers that "influence" your mind, but if you want to broaden it to telepathy, eh, why not I'd say.


                                      So no, there is no good solution since you can't ban something if you don't know it's being used, but imaginative vampires will try to limit the risks by being careful, laying traps, having allies to have their back, etc.

                                              I think part of the solution is to dial back expectations for Auspex 4. If it can casually function as a deep mind-reading power then why would vampires gather at all? As S. Brechen points out it is keyed to surface thoughts, so if you wander through the Elysium with it on then you'll pick up whatever people are thinking about at the moment, especially stuff like "that Shadow's looking at me again, probably reading my mind--F you little bitch!"

                                              But the natural impulse of the human mind when asked a question or reminded of something is to spring to a related memory, even if we don't say it immediately and can control non-verbal reactions. You want to get the best good out of Auspex? Use it while springing a trap on your target, poking them to remind them of something or asking a question with double-meaning. It takes a lot of will to not even think of an incriminating response to "so, when will you stop beating your ghoul?" (Or, whatever.)

                                              But asking these questions socially, out loud, means playing the harpy game and opening yourself or others up for analysis. That's part of the fun. If you're not asking them socially then you've got your target in a controlled environment--possibly one they cannot just leave from, and you can afford to probe repeatedly until you get the deeper memories you want.

                                              [Talking to handcuffed and duct-tape-gagged subject.]
                                              "So, glad to meet you--my name is Buttercup. Shake! No...?" (I'm Niell)
                                              "Ok Niell, let's relax while we're waiting for the boss. Where do you work?" (The F--- get out!)
                                              "Sex work Niell? The wife approve?" (<flash of an office>Barbara!)
                                              "Don't worry, you'll be with Barbara soon. So what do you do for the office? Handle money?" (<papers, folders, pictures>No!)
                                              "Where do you file them, Niell. A trash can? Haha. No--you do important work. They go in a safe, don't they Niell." (<safe, trashcan, safe, keypad!>)
                                              "What is the frequency, Kenneth! Sorry--Niell, there are numbers on the safe. Safe numbers. You like... 7!" (What? Safe... nine.)
                                              "Nine. Seven." (Nine three two nine, idio.... shit.)
                                              Whips out smartphone, tapping a text. "Thanks Niell."
