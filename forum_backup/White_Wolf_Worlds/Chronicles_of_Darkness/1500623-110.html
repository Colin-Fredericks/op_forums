                A default spell is
                duration 1 (~3 seconds),
                range: touch,
                casting time: ritual duration(up to 3 hours),
                potency 1 (eg 1 damage/healing for a typical attack or healing spell),
                scale: 1 subject up to size 5 (a normal person, so a Werewolf shifted into a larger form may be immune)
                ---
                You can take a reach to increase (most of) these. Casting time -> instant is 1 reach, and entirely necessary in combat.

                Range -> sensory -> remote viewing (probably only need the one reach for combat, unless you are casting from behind a CCTV, or through live web feed (from a mobile phone) or a scrying window...)

                Duration -> 1 scene (most buffs or similar will need this to be useful in combat scenes).

                So since you only usually have 1 safe reach (with the most powerful spells you can cast), you are talking about over-reach is you go Instant casting *and* sensory range (which avoids the subject's Defence). You are immediately risking paradox when you cast something, and each paradox risked in a scene adds another die to the next paradox roll. So in a combat scene you do really want to keep track of things... One big spells which ends the fight is ideal, but risks failing if they have some magical defences... But throwing spells (if you have the athletics/firearms) or using touch range (if you can beat their Defence*) can save on that reach...
                --

                To your question, you can spend multiple turns using yantras/spending mana to prep a spell, but then you need to cast it immediately on the turn you roll it, Including either touching the opponent or throwing the spell... concentration only matters if you used the concentration Yantra, or if you are using the rules from SoS for being interrupted durings ritual casting.

                    "Throwing" a spell is a reflexive action that happens on the same turn as the instant action of casting it. It's also a straight roll of Gnosis + ranged attack Skill penalized by Defense unless you have Fast Spells, surprise, or other workarounds.

                    it is assumed that once a spell is cast its ready to go right? Or would it be "if you get hit, the spell dissipates as you lose focus needed to maintain it"
                    Without Adamant Hand or other Merits or mechanics to allow you to use appropriate (instant) combat actions as reflexive Yantras, spellcasting takes up your turn in combat, and every Yantra you use after the first in instant casting requires an instant action to use otherwise. You can still benefit from your Defense, but Dodging deprives you of your normal action for the turn.

                    So the issue is more that, unless you are a practitioner of the martial techniques of the struggle-exalting Order or have some other trick up your sleeve in a similar vein, casting a spell during combat directly trades number of Yantras for frequency of casting, and even the biggest conventional Yantra bonus only gets you two full extra spell factors before you start reducing your base dicepool.

                    Rotes are worth their weight in gold for this and other reasons (see again "mages cannot master most Arcana without learning from Masters" re: free Reach), but if you misjudge your target's Withstand score or rely on roll-based means of getting the spell to affect them you run the risk of having wasted a turn even without using multiple Yantras; you can try and make up the shortfall in preparation-based resources by overReaching instead, but then you have decisions to make about how to handle the risk of Paradox.

                                            Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                            Should touching in combat range be minus the subject's Defence. Since the Defence rules speecifc applying defence when the subject is aware, or is it not an attack?
                                            It's a little confusing:

                                            The version of the mechanics presented in Changeling and onward specifies that Touching An Opponent is a simple action (neither contested nor resisted) in the same way as a ranged attack and furthermore is a type of attack action.

                                            Deviant's primary attack power Lash has four options for adjusting the roll the attack uses, for which the "Defense does not apply at all" option is exclusively for ranged attacks and the melee-exclusive "the Brawl/Weaponry attack uses Dexterity instead of Strength" option still subtracts Defense regardless of whether the attack is a type that does damage (and attacks that don't deal damage apply their effects on a target with any number of successes regardless of Armor).

                                            The Combat Summary Chart in the Rules Appendix included in Demon, which is also included in Beast and Promethean, specifies that while armor may or may not apply to attempts at Touching A Target, Defense does apply.

                                            The 1e mechanics specify that Defense always applies to the Dexterity + Brawl/Weaponry dicepool used to touch a resisting opponent, as does Armor against touch effects for which it would make sense (though in 1e Armor worked different mechanically).

                                            For sanity's sake it's likely prudent to subtract Defense from attempts to touch a resisting opponent as before, since Defense and Dodging are the only way to make that harder and "Defense is a measure of your character's ability to mitigate harm to themselves" doesn't really scan as an argument against using it when grappling counts as an unarmed attack.
