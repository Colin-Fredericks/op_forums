    Vent0, you cannot seriously believe that curiosity and obsession with the unknown are only inherent in those who aren't bogged down with the everyday struggles of survival?

    All humans have a curiosity for the unknown, and when confronted with the unknown all
    humans have the capacity to become obsessed with it's mysteries. This is regardless of class, vocation, ethnicity, geography, or mental faculties. All of them have the potential to see through the Lie and Awaken. Not all of them will choose to, instead preferring normalcy to the unknown, and those who try to Awaken don't always succeed (although most failed Awakenings are the result of intimately personal reasons for the Sleeper, or the result of outside tampering with the Awakening), but none the less anyone can Awaken.

    As for the whole "Sleepers are all equal in Mages' eyes" counter point you made, your argument falls flat under your own examples. The class schema you mentioned is Order specific, highlighting the Hubris and still very human belief systems that color Mage Society. Interestingly enough though, the fact that they exist at all indicates that Sleepers are given careful consideration in their organizations hierarchy because all Sleepers have the potential to become Awakened like them. If they truly didn't see Sleepers as anything but second class citizens then so much effort wouldn't be put into either incorporating them into their ethos or they wouldn't spend so much time trying to control who Awakens in order to swell their own ranks. We have a great example of a splat that doesn't consider their Sleeper equivalents to be anything but second class citizens, it's Genius the Transgression.

        Well.....actually, Awakening often does have it's mages look at Sleepers as second class citizens. Sure, the Seers, Ladder*, and Mysterium are the worst of the bunch, but they all do it to some degree.

        The difference is that Mage frames this (subtly) as a problem with Mage society, where as Genius, for as cruel as it's language is, never really seems like it expects you to disagree with it. Genius validates it's casual disregard for mundane humanity where Mage keeps up the dissonance. Both of these games can, in various ways, be seen about playing 1%, to borrow from the writers and developers of Awakening-but where Mage asks questions about that position and ultimately remembers that it's characters are monsters, Genius just default assumes it's character (and to an extent, it's players) are the victims in a cruel world and ultimately pins the blame outside of the characters.

        Some might argue that both succeed, since Mage is first and foremost a game about the horror of humanity writ large, while Genius supposedly is secretly a game about cosmic horror. And you know, I might agree with that-but cosmic horror is a bullshit genre anyways. It doesn't help that Geniuses** are larger than cosmic horror really need.

        *Sure, the Ladder is insanely humanist and highly encouraging, but they also have a literal caste system they dream of attaining, and sleepers follow under mages at all times.

        **Long standing question answered-Technically genii is the most gramatically correct version, but common parlance has changed enough that it's barely in use, likely to disappear, with geniuses filling the void.         That's how deep Genius's disregard for people is.

                                    Vent0, I stand by my statement about mortal curiosity in the Chronicles of Darkness. This is because mystery and danger permeates the world to a degree where it's nearly impossible for a mortal civilian to go their entire lives without interacting with the supernatural or unknown in some meaningful way. I bring your attention to the questions for mortal character creation involving setting Integrety Breaking Points, in particular: "What has your character forgotten?" [might not be the exact question, but point is that if a question about what experiences has your character forced themselves to forget about or convince themselves that it wasn't real then the supernatural is decidedly prevalent to every being in the Chronicles of Darkness.]

                                    As for my examples about Mage, I was being more metaphoric with my statement about some won't choose to Awaken. No Mage during their Awakening will have any idea what the hells going on. Some might be aware of an Awakening, like Sleepwalkers in the Orders or Proximi, but here's the thing: Supernal Magic is insidious. Even if you somehow knew it was coming, you mind would be altered by the Mystery Play to make the secrets hidden behind the Lie tantalizing enough to seek them out. No one signs a Watchtower knowing that they'll gain cosmic powers, that's not what an Awakening is all about...or at least that what the Otherworld want's you to think. Aaaaand clever lead into the next paragraph:

                                    There are actually some great examples of what makes Mages monstrous, so much so that it had a noticeable impact on how the Orders have been called out on their ideological bullshit and how Banishers have been reimagined as either botched Awakenings or active realizations on the insidious nature of Supernal Magic in 2nd Edition. The reason it's difficult to find is because it's not in the Mage gameline, it's Witch Finders from Hunter the Vigil. Gives Mages a once over purely from a mortal perspective, highlights just what makes an antagonist Mage in a Hunter or Mage game, highlights how there are Mages beyond "Supernal" Mages and how they're just as deadly a threat as the prideful practitioners of "Supernal" Magic, and exposes the insidious nature of Supernal Magic and how Mages justify using an otherworldly power that bends reality to it's own laws while reality desperately tries to eliminate all traces of it from within it's bounds. It's fantastic.

                                    Side Note: Actually, thinking about Hunter the Vigil, Genius would greatly benefit from having a Mortal Remains-like entry regarding how Hunters see Geniuses, and just what interesting stories could be made with Geniuses as antagonists. Every Hunter installment has both broadened what each gameline is capable of, and has much needed mortal perspectives of the supernatural to inspire new ideas and interesting questions to bring up during Chronicles for those gamelines.
