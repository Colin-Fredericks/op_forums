    Originally posted by Newb95 View Post
    I agree that it makes sense for the supernatural but not the hostage argument, lets be real here, if someone put a 9mm at your head at point blank range and pulls the trigger, you will almost certainly die, we can pull out all the freak cases we want from real life but the vast majority of people can't survive a point blank shot to the head. I understand the need to prevent a pc or npc to be executed like that but there are better ways than pretend he survived a bullet to the brain just because.
    Again, the rule isn't just for a 9mm, but for every single instance imaginable. And it isn't for reality, it is for fiction. Those characters have a right to be freak cases if the story so demands.

    The freak cases, by the way, aren't so freak. The same way a disembodied head takes two minutes or more to actually die, a gunshot to the head may instantly stop a person, but they actually take time to die more often than not.

    Not only that, the actual rate of survival with current medicine is of 10% of all gunshots to the head in the US alone. This without considering a fictional setting where the players may have access to superior technology or magic healing for any number of reasons that can raise it significantly.

    Most of the time, instant deaths are a legend. Which is actually horrible, but is true. In any case, the fiction itself justifies any deviation from reality that improves the story. If you don't like this and don't want to use this, that's fine. But is not a problem with the rule, it just isn't for you.

    Realism is a complicated thing because it depends too much on who the reader is. When you search for actual data on common knowledge, you may come out surprised with how life actually works. Or don't work. The more you learn about any subject, the more your personal perception of what's realistic changes in unexpected ways (after all, if they were expected they wouldn't have reason to change). Because of that, even creating more realistic situations may cause people to perceive something as unrealistic.

    So chasing realism isn't a good design strategy. This is a game of fiction, not a reality simulator. And a rule may serve purposes that don't exist on your chronicle, and yet are common in another.

                                Originally posted by Newb95 View Post
                                99 times out of 100 you are going to kill them, this is the simple reality of things and what I have been told by multiple storytellers (one of which was an EMT btw) when I asked them about using killing blow in similar situations.
                                The human body is an amazing combination of resilience and fragility. A lot of things will kill a human, but almost none of them will do so with any immediacy.

                                A Killing Blow in those circumstances isn't a guaranteed kill, but it's close enough if you just apply the bleeding out rules and apply appropriate penalties for any attempts to first aid rolls to such complicated wounds. Be aware that a human can survive with the majority of their brain gone (though not without neurological issues); it's the intracranial bleeding associated with such traumas that lead to death.

                                Edit: Dealing full lethal is pretty easy using the killing blow rules (especially combined with aiming and/or WP expenditure). If the victim is beaten unconscious, however, you only need to deal lethal damage to half their health since the previous bashing will wrap around and upgrade the remaining bashing to lethal.
