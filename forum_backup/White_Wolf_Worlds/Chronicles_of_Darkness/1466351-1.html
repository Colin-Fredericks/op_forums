        Originally posted by Super_Dave View Post
        That's a VERY detailed hierarchy! Thank you so much, I feel like that really helps how the Church's various officers and agents would have operated in this era. However, with eight dots available it changes the equation slightly.
        That's more to understand the actual hierarchy than to represent it with their individual dots. In actual game play the Pope, as an example, is likely to not even be relevant unless the game takes place near his lands.

        I recommend that you make a number of changes and simplifications. First of all limit the Merit to what it is. Status is about official power, not underhanded influence, and use of the institutional machine instead of just its political influence.

        A) Forget about wealth. Status is not wealth, Resources is. By equating the rank to wealth you're giving an erroneous representation of what it actually entails. And as much as church authority could be and were used for wealth accumulation, members on all ranks at the time could be rich or poor;

        B) Unless you want to be very lenient, learning Latin pretty much is a requirement for acquiring dots beyond the first. Indeed, it is the most likely distinction between a lay member and a priest in training;

        C) Drop the orthodoxy ball. Although unorthodox ideas may indeed affect face and whatnot, they hardly lead to serious consequence at the time even for the clergy themselves. Instead, they are just quirks or lead to the formation of orders. At the time only very serious heresies that effectively threaten the social order are hunted down;

        ​D) Just as Errol216 said, also drop the blackmail and other unhanded control methods, this is not how Status works. If you have this, you actually have a Retainer or something, and then that character has Status. That's because Status always means work through the official channels of the organization;

        E) Give something for female Status holders to compensate for the things they can't do. The actual church is sexist, the rules shouldn't, as the player pays the same amount of points. And remember, a woman can reach the role of Abbess or other Mother Superior, or may work as direct secretary to an Archbishop. So although the title "stops" at Mother Superior, there is no reason to limit Status level;

        F) Let fine differences be represented by other Merits if the player want them, like Staff, Fame and others. Especially roles as Cardinal or Pope should be left outside normal Status, as they carry less weight by themselves and more by the circumstances around them.

        Suggestion for an alternative:

        ●: You are either lay officer of the Church or training for the clergy. Though you cannot perform any of the Holy Sacraments, you have limited access to Church grounds, buildings, records, relics, and personnel.
        ●●: You are an ordained minister of the Church. Males can perform the Holy Sacraments: baptism, confirmation, communion, penance, holy orders, and anointing the sick or last rites, but cannot enter female-only grounds without being specifically authorized by the Mother Superior. You may live and eat on church grounds for free, although you may and will be required to work.
        ●●●: You are the main priest of a parish or abbey. You have administrative control over church properties in it, direct authority over its clerical body and spiritual authority over its populace. Alternatively you may be a priest directly working for a Bishop, holding greater influence but less authority, or an Auxiliary Bishop (a direct secretary to a Bishop) with minor influence.
        ●●●●: You are a bishop, exert control over a diocese (a collection of parishes), and may perform the most restrict sacraments, the ordaining of priests and the excommunication. Under Canon Law you're now fully equal to an apostle in spiritual authority, being able to settle theological matters for your jurisdiction. In areas where secular authorities are absent you may also assume their role, and even where they're present you usually have the power to indicate judges for both religious and secular disputes. Alternatively you may be an Auxiliary Bishop with great influence or auxiliary to an Archbishop, or you may be an Archbishop with little real influence despite the title.
        ●●●●●: You are an Archbishop. While it is technically only a title of prestige, your diocese is of exceptional importance and serves as center of an ecclesiastical province, a big collection of dioceses. At the very least those other bishops pay you a huge amount of respect, but in practical terms you have considerable oversight on them. Alternatively you may be a part of the high administration of the Holy See and still be called an Archbishop even without a diocese under you. The Cardinals, the Roman Curia and the Pope are all Archbishops.

                    Originally posted by Super_Dave View Post

                    Then how do we square with the fact that medieval women couldn't gain much status in the Church, but modern female players (and males playing as females) will probably want to?
                    I figured that was the goal, and I agree that the dots should be available to female characters, but I don't think the way you went about it succeeded.

                    Instead, I'd suggest writing a sidebar on the subject. The way I see it, a Storyteller has two options:
                    1. Screw historical realism for this specific aspect. Do they actually care about having historically accurate sexism? If not, then just declare that, yes, a large chunk of the clergy is female, even at the upper echelons. It's simply not important to keep the sexism unless you're specifically telling a story about that sexism. Obviously, this won't work for tables where people care about historical accuracy, so...

                    2. Provide some guidance about how to change canon law. I.e., how would your female PC become the first female priest, bishop, or pope? What would they have to contend with? Maybe they'd have to convince the Pope, or a specific subset of attendees to an ecumenical council. What would that look like? In other words, the PC can buy the dots, but only after changing the course of history. Which I think is fitting for Genius.

                    This way, the Church Status merit can simply reflect official rank and resultant powers and not worry about trying to capture half a dozen other details at the same time.

                    Neither of these options mean that you have to lose overall sexism, either. You can have female bishops and also still burn women at the stake on trumped up charges of hexing, for instance. Sexism is flexible, and needs no help to exist, so if a table wants to fight the patriarchy in the Dark Eras, they still can. All you have to do is not have any rules against a gendered character buying a dot in Status.

                    This Wikipedia page on the ordination of women is probably worth a read.

                        Originally posted by Super_Dave View Post
                        So females might have unofficial power and influence within the Church, not tied to their actual role within it?
                        If you read carefully, my suggestion isn't about women having unofficial power and influence.

                        The nun or sister that acts as direct secretary to a Cardinal isn't at all in an unofficial position or role. She follows all the rules: she can't perform sacraments, she doesn't directly oversee rituals or theological matters, her position in the "spiritual" hierarchy is below a Mother Superior.

                        Yet, she is the secretary to a Cardinal. Her level of access, administrative power and respect are all official and yet considerable enough for Rank 4, or even 5. Any priest is theoretically above her in theological matters, yet a lesser Bishop will probably defer to her wisdom. Her influence over the Diocese or even the Ecclesiastical Province is considerable, especially if the Cardinal is too busy with the added responsibilities of the title, even if it isn't direct.

                        By all accounts her power is indirect and inferior. Yet, it is 100% through official channels. Does that means it isn't actually a sexist environment? Not in the least. She probably worked harder to attain that position than any man in a similar situation, and most women in a similar position don't have the same actual Rank without constant effort to stay relevant.

                        As a rule of thumb, for sexism reflecting on status, I suggest the rule of up to 2 below. A woman in a given official position in a sexist organization of any kind usually has less leverage than the position nominally concedes. You reflect that by giving a character a lower Status Merit for the position, as she is nominally X, but men right above and below her frequently just ignore her, and even people far below know her pull is limited and will be overruled by her superiors if she really tries, so her actual Rank on sheet is X-2.

                        Why not make it the default? Because personal skill, effort and luck play a role in changing things. That goes with Status in general, but even greater for such women. Sometimes they have every bit as much power as a man could dream with. So it stays as a descriptive option, no need to charge anything on the player. If a player wants a woman with as much power as her position can afford, just go with it, if the player wants to roleplay the struggle of being unrecognized just let them buy their effective Rank but occupy a nominally higher position. When creating a woman NPC, do as you see fit.
