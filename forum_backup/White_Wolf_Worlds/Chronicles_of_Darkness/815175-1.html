                                                Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                As far as incorporating Chambers with extraneous Traits into one's own Lair goes, I think it's a reasonable guess that the manifestations of those Traits just diminish in activity or intensity unless you add them back in later or you have a broodmate with an appropriate Trait to piggyback off of.
                                                That's sort of my read on it, too. Setting elements like storms, burning buildings, riots . . . are all preserved by the Chamber (particularly if they contributed to the sanity break), but are "nerfed" if the Beast doesn't have the appropriate Lair trait.

                                                For example, taking a look at the "blizzard" Chamber idea, let's say the real world version was generated by a werewolf hunting a kid through a snowstorm. Lost in the snow, tripping and sliding through the snow, unable to see the path anymore . . . the kid's fear generates the chamber.

                                                Before a Beast claims the site, and applies any Lair traits, the original Chamber would be something like this:
                                                • The Chamber itself is roughly circular, with a usable area that has a diameter of roughly 200 feet or so.
                                                • The Chamber always feels cold, with icy winds blowing through it. It snows constantly, but not hard enough to obscure your view across the Chamber.
                                                • While unpleasantly cold, and the average person wouldn't want to hang around without a coat/hat/gloves, a human can hang around indefinitely without suffering frostbite.
                                                • There's roughly 3-6" of snow on the ground, spread unevenly across the floor, forming into drifts, etc.
                                                • Despite the constant snowstorm, the amount of snow on the ground never changes. The falling snow fills in footprints, covers up areas that people try to clear . . . but a careful observer will notice that while the drifts slowly shift, the overall amount of snow never increases or decreases.
                                                • Individuals who try to leave the Chamber by walking out into the storm (rather than use a Burrow) will eventually hit a "wall" of fierce winds and blinding snow. Regardless of how good their sense of direction is, or how skilled they are at tracking, they will eventually stumble back out into the usable area of the Chamber. Mages (et al) who use their magic to track their progress will find that its not possible to walk more than five or ten feet past the usable area.
                                                If a Beast wants the Chamber to be dangerously cold, or for the snow to come down hard enough to blind people, or for the drifts to be so deep they actively trip people, or . . . then they need to buy the relevant Lair traits. Otherwise, the Chamber is neutral, full of "dream" snow and "phantom" cold. Just an illusion, replicating the look and feel of the original site . . . but without the real world impact.

                                                Similarly, you could have a "burning house" Chamber full of "dream" fires, or a "riot" one where a "phantom" mob forms the walls (shoving people back out into the usable area of the room), or . . . A Chamber can be as narratively active as the player wants, and the original circumstances justify. Its one of the perks of hanging out with other supernaturals - the chance to find a Chamber where it perpetually rains blood, or one made out of candy, or that's the inside of someone's mouth . . .





                                                Edit: It would also be reasonable to argue that an unclaimed/wild Chamber would have all the Lair traits you'd expect it to have (dangerously cold, blinding snow, et al), and that the act of claiming it as part of your Lair "nerfs" them. Might give the Storyteller some extra setting options (sending Beasts/mages/etc into an unclaimed Chamber for some reason).

                                                Hmm . . . it might even be possible for a Beast to add a Chamber, and not "nerf" the wild Traits . . . but then they'd be just as dangerous to the Beast as to any uninvited guests . . . Have to think about that possibility some more.
