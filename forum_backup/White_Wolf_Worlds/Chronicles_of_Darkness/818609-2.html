                    Originally posted by Firanai View Post
                    Ok, let's start with something simple:
                    What are the things that you think didn't work in the first edition and why?
                    What new things do you want to include in the second edition and why?
                    What's the new theme?
                    Things that I think didn't work: the Wonder Building system is a tad cumbersome and needs a lot of streamlining. Stupid Loserman said in the previous Genius thread that the tone focused on humor to the detriment of drama, and I agree. A balance between the two would be great. The Baramins' ideas about the correct future were a bit vague and confusing at times. Lemurians might work better as disorganized nutjobs who can't work as a team for too long. If they are yet another shadowy conspiracy, Geniuses of the Peerage will have an easier time telling themselves that they'll never stoop to such a level. But if Lemurians are more down to earth in scope and show the occasional glimmer of open-mindedness, they seem more nuanced and members of the Peerage can have more varied opinions on them. That's not to say that there can't be trends and alliances between Lemurians. I just imagine them as being similar to low Integrity Heroes: their thinking is dangerously black and white, which always leads to a lot of internal conflict, even if they have more resources than the Foundations.

                    Things I wanna see in 2ED:

                    1) A more nuanced approach to the Peerage and Lemuria as mentioned above.

                    2) More details on factions for both of them, with special Merits and such to make each faction distinct. Aspects like hierarchies, recruitment, regional variants, resource distribution, internal conflict and so on deserve special attention.

                    3) A Wonder Building system that is simpler and more free-form. Milo has a point about the Axioms being rather restricted. There should be as much room for customization as possible to make the players feel more like inventors without memorizing loads of crunch. Borrowing bits and pieces from the other 2nd editions is OK, but should be kept to a minimum.

                    4) More Catalysts, Bardos and Manes.

                    5) An unique story catalyst, like the Strix, Idigam and Huntsmen. An updated version of the Illuminated was suggested in the previous thread, and I couldn't agree more. Lemurians and Unmada might be dangerous jerks, but at least they are still human. The Illuminated should either give a reason for Lemurians and Peers to team up, or cause an every-man-for-himself. They should have freaky advantages that go beyond Wonders, like perhaps some personalized Conditions and Tilts they inflict by just being in the scene, sort of like an Abyssal Verge pouring through a humanoid gateway.

                    6) More cross-over options, story seeds and signature cities. It'd be fun to see how the Inspired might interact with Beasts and Deviants.

                    7) Minimization of overlap with the themes of Mage, such as alienation and responsibility. This includes giving Obligation its own feel and impact on playing a Genius.

                    The new theme should include "trial and error" and "reconciliation of vision and reality".

                    Bye.

                            One more thing: I'd like more incentive for Beholden PCs. As it is, the only benefit they get from interacting with Geniuses is getting a worldview back, which Geniuses take away in the first place! And being a Beholden with no master is WAY too harsh. You can't recover willpower AT ALL and you die in less than a year. Even ghouls get better deals than these poor bastards.

                            My suggestion is to treat this lack of a worldview as the initial stage of the Beholden. As they learn more about mad science and the rest of the strangeness in the CoD, they develop new identities and can even increase their chance of becoming Inspired. Having henchmen to do your dirty work is undeniably useful, but the lack of differing opinions can get boring and aggravating. In fact, I'd make the development of divergent Aspirations be the most common method of starting a Beholden's own Breakthrough. Geniuses who fear the growing independence of their Beholden can try to keep them mentally subservient in any way they can, but should the servant break free and Catalyze, there will be retribution. This is a game about Transgressions, after all, and limiting Beholden to NPCs takes away a source of good drama. It's similar to the enmity of ghouls and their vampire masters, sure, but what else can you really expect?

                                                    In terms of "what doesn't work", we desperately need better reasons why Geniuses don't break the Masquerade. In 1e as its written, there's nothing stopping a Genius from, say, building a giant robot, having it smash a bunch of buildings, then filming it with his iPhone and throwing it on YouTube. I actually had the idea that Wonders interfere with mundane technology, meaning that they always look blurry and fake if you try to film them. And, fitting with the theme of futility and the struggle to actually change the world, I had the idea that people can see wonders just fine, but their memories cover them up.

                                                    So, say a giant robot rampages through a city- people try to film it, but most cameras just glitch out. A few capture blurry images of a giant shape towering over the city. After it's over, most people swear that it was just an earthquake. The few people who do remember probably keep it to themselves, realizing they have no proof and no one would believe them. And that's how you can get away with giant robots rampaging through cities and still have a masquerade.

                                                    On an earlier thread, I also mentioned having Manes be Twilight entities similar to Spirits, as another Masquerade measure, and I believe someone actually wrote out rules for this. To keep Mane Geniuses plausible, maybe we could say that Catalyzing gives a Mane the power to manifest?

                                                    And, finally, I think Genius is rich and complete enough that it doesn't need to be a ChroD fangame. I would completely support creating an original set of mechanics and releasing it as an independent product.
