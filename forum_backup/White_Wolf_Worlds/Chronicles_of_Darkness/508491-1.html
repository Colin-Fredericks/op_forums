    Originally posted by BunniRabbi View Post
    Hmmm, I think I have to weigh in on the soul issue, from the point of view of my-clerical-aspirant-self;

    The soul-concept most commonly referred to in most WoD books, and in most of western literature, is the Cartesian Soul, while the soul-concept used in Demon is apparently the Narrativist Soul. These are distinct concepts.

    The Cartesian Soul is what most people mean when they say 'soul'; a supernatural object which is naturally attached to an individual by some vague means. This is the kind of soul that can be stolen by a mage, and which Prometheans lack. It is the soul-concept predominant in modern Christianity, modern Judaism, and ancient Islam (there is theological debate within Islam as to the applicability of the Cartesian concept, as it applies inconsistently in faqi, which is Islamic jurisprudence). It is not the source of an individual's identity in the Abrahamic religions. Nor in WoD is it the source of identify, as demonstrated by the fact that a soul-less individual does not cease to be recognizable, and Prometheans still have an identity. Animals are not supposed to have Cartesian Souls according to most theologies that use the concept (with the notable exceptions of Norse paganism and Egyptian paganism [partially, as animals in Egyptian paganism do not possess all of the soul-parts humans do]). I'm reasonably certain that it says somewhere in some of the WoD books that animals don't have souls (not that I can name one at the moment). It seems reasonable to assume they mean the Cartesian Soul.

    The Narrativist Soul is a metaphysical abstract, as opposed to a supernatural object. It is synonymous with identity, and thus it is the soul that Demon appears to use. The Narrativist Soul is the soul-concept used in Buddhism (see the Doctrine of The Carriage), and as a non-supernatural concept of the soul it is endorsed by some groups and individuals that endorse an anti-supernatural understanding of the world. Richard Dawkins refers to it in his TED Talk "Our Queer Universe". The Narrativist Soul is something shared by everything that has an identity, so animals and buildings have it as well.

    What all that boils down to is that it is easiest to work out how soul-issues work if you just realize the word 'soul' means one thing in one place and another thing in another place. In other words you can barter with a Mage for the (Cartesian) soul he has stolen, but you can't use it for Cover, because it's not the same thing as what you build cover out of (identity). Likewise, if you find a mortal who is missing his (Cartesian) soul, you can still make a soul-pact with him for his (Narrativist) Soul. Ditto for Prometheans. It also means you can totally make a soul-pact with an ape for his soul, provided he knows sign language, and you teach him to sign his name first. Both of those sound like options for really patient demons.

    In theory, you could make soul-pacts with any animal you could communicate significantly with, provided they could write in some limited capacity. Since many beings in the WoD can communicate with animals, you could make pacts with them provided you had some help. Also consider that writing may not be as big a limitation as one might think, since there is no qualifier for the quality of the writing. If your dog puts a paw print on the paper, it might count.

    As far as inanimate objects, well, is there anything in the WoD that can talk to them? I tend to think there might be. Many machines could produce some kind of writing or other self-identifier. Their blood might be a limiting factor, but their is no real definition given for blood either. I would generally say that any fluid vital to the operation of the machine qualifies.

    And yeah, the Cover rating would be low, but I could see some advantages to these crazy covers; In dog-form, you can get away with a lot just using Cause And Effect, or imagine you take the soul of the printer/fax/copier located in an office-building infrastructure. Every document they print, you send an identical fax to your local Agency. Even with a low Cover, who is actually going to accuse the printer of being the Mole?
    This is easily one of the most informative and interesting posts I've read online in a long while, BunniRabbi. You deserve a Like not only for opening my eyes, but for also inspiring new ideas for my games. Keep it up and you'll be an amazing cleric.

    Bye.
