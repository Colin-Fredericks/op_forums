    Benedictions?

    01-12-2014, 05:25 PM
    So Benedictions raise a lot of questions, some of which work fine as in-setting mysteries; but some of which I've been wrestling with from the perspective of setting coherence and conspiracy histories.

    - How did Malleus discover/gain-access to Benedictions?

    - What keeps them solely the territory of Malleus? What's stopping the Catholic Church from performing public resurrections?

    I have some ideas for my own game, a couple of which are based on the fact that the Cainite Heresy doesn't grab me as a whole Conspiracy, but several interesting traits of it are raidable for Malleus (originating from a Blood Cult bound through vitae-tainted Communion fits with the liberated Ghoul at the Conspiracy's head...)

    * Steal how Rites of Denial work. Malleus uses "holy blood" to perform Benedictions. This limited resource keeps access to Benedictions strictly controlled by Cardinal Ambrogio.

    *As above but not as extreme - Benedictions don't use said blood, but before one can wield them there is an ordination ritual of anointment that uses said blood.

    *Not stealing from the Cainite Heresy - the spiritual patrons (Saints?) of Benedictions are who limit access to said rites' miracles - and they're only interested in granting access to Hunters. To thematically enforce this point, I wouldn't let things like Hands of Saint Luke or the Boon of Lazarus work on anyone injured or killed by "natural causes" - they are solely for the Hunt.

                                      That's not WoD mortals, that's actually people. Think about kids at magic shows-either they are completely invested in it or become more jaded as they observe it. A "Miracle at Demand" sort of thing does much the same-and in fact, the "at Demand" part will do a lot to jade people to it. Running with Christianity here for a second, because why not, even though Jesus did do a lot of miracles, why do you think a vast a majority of his time was actually spent just preaching and talking to people? He often performed miracles at tipping points.

                                      People are prone to skepticism. Miracles don't build faith, they build a case that two plus two equals four-and what's more, it's two plus two in the hands of man, not some higher being.

                                      This ties into the second point. Have the Pope go in front of everyone and raise the dead and claim it a miracle from God? Okay. A Changeling who wants to challenge that can arrange his own sort of show, do much the same, and then casually point out "if God's responsible for that, then he's a bastard, cause here's how I can do that". A Demon willing to risk compromise can do that as well, and if a mage can overcome paradox and disbelief(A stretch), that also works. A Mummy can resurrect someone from the dead and quite frankly go "Praise be the Judges of Duat". And from there, you start a magical arms races of people who can do this, that, and other. And all the while, there are people looking at this going "Okay, this guy claims it's all God, and this guy is pointing out that it's just really advanced technical application of physics/mystical contracts/applied will". People will be more prone to the relatable material, and without significant consequences otherwise(most consequences actually balance out to about even, actually), they're going to take their world into their hands, and ta hell with God.

                                      Some people might be all for trying to counter argue that those magics are lesser or of unsavory sources and shouldn't be used.... which creates an issue when you don't exactly feel like it's God working on you when the Catholics are doing their hoodoo. Lots of people are used to the idea of masks, particularly with charlatan priests and magicians and when actual magic hits the stage, you have no choice but to rely on what you feel. It can have all the saints days benefits and the morality bonuses it wants-magic can wear masks too, like a conman in a frock. At the end of the day, it comes down to individual perception, and Benedictions have regularly been described as having the look of the Christian Divine but feeling...wrong somehow.

                                      In the middle of this, there is going to be an arms race of bigger and better miracles to prove one is more right than the other, and Benedictions just can't keep up. Vampires and Demons can make it rain blood, Changeling can vastly flood/whiteout/freeze/drought a place quickly, mages do what mages do, and everyone else does their stuff, and Benedictions just can't hold a torch. It'd be a fight, sure, but sooner or later, the elements combine and Catholicism-hell, Catholicism in particular with how unpopular it is-would be crushed.

                                      From the individual faith of people to the mass rejection of the validity of Catholic power, performing public Benedictions is just inviting the entire downfall of every domino in existence. Miracles will not lead the way for the church because it's too easy to swing in any way. Only Faith can build up the people and power the Church needs.

                                              2) Really really hard to learn.

                                              Sure a Changeling might be able to bring back the dead (I don't see that Contract, so lets say the Changeling can heal). Well to learn that contract you must first be taken to a horrific realm of torture in Arcadia, and then escape. That's pretty damn hard. To learn Benedictions you must take a training course in the Vatican. Mage's have to Awaken, Sin-Eaters have to die. Vampires have to be vampires and Sons of Phobos power their magic by cutting out hearts. To put it simply, in an arms race the only people in the cannon WoD who can match the MM for magic that can easily be taught are other hunter conspiracies and Vampires. I don't think I need to explain why the public will prefer the Church to Vampires.

                                              As to your second point. That Benedictions "feel wrong". What do you think the other magic feels like? Changeling magic is tinged with madness, Supernal magic triggers disbelief in your Soul, Mummies are surrounded by an aura of existential dread.


                                              So I think if it came to a maical arms race, the Church will probably win. Of all the magic that is easily taught and doesn't require hideously unethical power sources theirs is the best.
