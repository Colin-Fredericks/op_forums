        How to make a monster interesting, I think, can all be summed up in one sentence included in the original Ravenloft. A sentence that changed RPG games.
        "Strahd is a genius, so play him like one."
        ​The key to making a monster interesting isn't a cool ability or twist to its appearance, the key to making him interesting is having him do something interesting. Jason chopping up random teens quickly gets boring, requiring increasing gore and tits to keep teenagers watching. A monster with a foreword thinking plan and the ability to react intelligently to obstacles is far more interesting.
        The ability to speak also helps.

            It seems to me there is a degree of arguing past each other going on in this thread. The OP is basically saying that it's possible to "kite" a lot of monsters if you're a mage. That you can do something like turn to shadow and kill them off at your leisure. Like running around the Big Bad in a computer game peppering them with arrows from out of range. The OP is asking if that's really the case and if so how you can deal with it. They're also wary of things that feel like the GM just nerfing the player's ideas / abilties and coming across as saying "nuh-uh, I've decided that this monster can hit you whilst you're smoke just because I don't want you doing that anymore". And all of that is valid. I don't think telling the OP they're wrong in this helps because it's kind of true.

            But on the other hand what you have is a lot of people saying that's not really the point. That you shouldn't BE trying to challenge a mage with things like this. That there are lots of creatures and situations where this wont help.

            I think the connection that might help bridge the two view points is to say this should be established from the outset in a game. If you start with gnashy-clawed monsters and that's your game for a while and then suddenly PCs figure out they can Smoke N' Kite once they get the power, and THEN the GM suddenly starts throwing in creatures that can bypass the PCs, then yes players probably will feel that you're out to get them. Or if all of a sudden monsters are hidden and ambushing all the time - same thing. What you need to do is establish before you get to this point that the game is one of mysteries and finding the monster or figuring out the key to defeating it. If you want to scare the players by having loved ones in danger, you need to get those in early and build up their relationship, not just throw in a girlfriend one adventure and then threaten her.

            A lot of this thread reads like the following loop:
            OP: This is busted.
            Forum: We don't care.
            OP: But it's busted.
            Forum: But we don't care.
            Repeat.

            OP is right - it is hard to straight-up Scary Mysterious Monster the PCs in a mage game. Respondents are also right - you have to work around that. The missing part is helping bridge the expectation gap. How does a GM stop the players feeling like the GM is nerfing them. My answer is that you have to establish in advance and early what the nature of a Mage game is, not just knee-jerk once they discover how to turn into Shadows. And if it has got to that point, then you have to address it explicitly with your players and say "I realized with the powers you have that the game isn't going to keep working the way it has done so I want to try a more puzzle / social / whatever approach to adventures.".

            Anyway, just some impressions. They could be off-base.

                    Pretty much what Mrmdubois said. Mages have the ability to know a lot and to figure out a lot, but, they do not know everything about everything. Sure, if they had the luxury of time and circumstance to be able to comfortably study whatever monster that tickles their fancy that week then sure, they could probably learn a lot about it and take away that unknown factor, but so could a vanilla mortal with a dot or 2 in Occult. If you have the luxury to do so then just about anyone could discover a great deal with enough time.

                    So the answer that has been given is to take that luxury away. Throw and unknown at them and give them a reason to confront it then and there. Don't give them time to sit back casting every Knowing spell in existence and then some. And again, whatever they're watching doesn't have to be attacking them specifically, make it so that there are other people who could be saved if they acted as quickly as possible, and give them the choice on staying back to cast more spells and learn more or to go out and save lives. If they choose to charge in anyways, they face the horror of the unknown, if they choose to sit back, observe, and let whoever it is being attacked die...well then we've got ourselves a completely different type of horror story now don't we?

                                            Originally posted by knasser View Post
                                            OP is right - it is hard to straight-up Scary Mysterious Monster the PCs in a mage game. Respondents are also right - you have to work around that. The missing part is helping bridge the expectation gap. How does a GM stop the players feeling like the GM is nerfing them. And if it has got to that point, then you have to address it explicitly with your players and say "I realized with the powers you have that the game isn't going to keep working the way it has done so I want to try a more puzzle / social / whatever approach to adventures.".
                                            I think a two disclaimers would fix this problem of expectation:

                                            Evolving ecosystems and economies (supernatural or otherwise) do not permit unfilled niches or power vacuums for long.

                                            In a setting with many mystery solvers, any unsolved mysteries must be unsolved for a reason.

                                            These act as reminders in setting design for the storyteller while providing a backbone to his narrative style, and cautionary warnings to player that their characters exist in a world that is not an empty abyss but rather a place were things go on outside the limelight which follows them through the world.

                                            If Kiting is possible within the setting then it should be addressed in the setting as if it was a thing possible. This isn't The Invention of Lying where the players are the protagonists and thus the only one to come up with an obvious, semi-obvious, plausible, or even outlandish idea. In real life, a world of people spend all day trying to think of ideas to get ahead, assuming the PCs are the only proactive dynamic thinkers in the game universe is hugely condescending to the rest of the story setting.

                                            EDIT: Actually thinking about it the above assume a High Prep Storytelling play style because of forethought required to simulate the change of consequences found in complex ecosystems and economies. A more improvisational approach is as valid but relies on a completely different set of strengths and has different weaknesses , but is compatible with the above suggestion with storytelling legerdemain and chicanery. As such I'll add a disclaimer to my purposed solution: Your Mileage May Vary.

                                                Cogitohazards, things that harm you by knowing about them. These types of things ruin mages, because they are so good at knowing things and mages ignore the common sense that somethings were not meant to be known, because of their hubris. You need the Lovecraftian theme which means certain monsters are the source of mental degradation if it is known, for the best monster horror in Mage. Cosmic horror linked to a creature not-meant-to-be-known.

                                                Case in point...

                                                Slenderman
                                                Mental Attributes: Intelligence 2, Wits 3, Resolve 3
                                                Physical Attributes: Strength 6, Dexterity 3, Stamina 5
                                                Social Attributes: Presence 4, Manipulation 1, Composure 10
                                                Mental Skills: Investigation 5 (Trailing), Medicine 3 (Removing Organs)
                                                Physical Skills: Athletics 5 (Chasing others), Brawl 3 (Breaking limbs), Stealth 5 (Blending in the background)
                                                Social Skills: Intimidation 5 (Lurking dread), Empathy 3 (Comforting children)
                                                Merits: Fame 3, Mystery Cult Initiation 5 (Slender Man Cult), Trained Observer 3, Relentless 1, Greyhound 1
                                                Potency: 6
                                                Willpower: 13
                                                Virtue: Patient
                                                Vice: Fitful
                                                Aspirations: Watching cult members, Abducting innocents, Entering Scenes of those with Slender Sickness
                                                Initiative: 13
                                                Defense: 8 (10 When Reality Stutter)
                                                Size: 7
                                                Speed: 19 (Species Factor 10)
                                                Health: 12
                                                Weapons/Attacks:
                                                Type Damage Range Dice Pool
                                                Featureless white hands +0B Melee 10
                                                Evicerating Shadow Tendrils +1L Melee 10
                                                Bans: Never injure a child
                                                Banes: Have no one know about it
                                                Dread Powers:
                                                REALITY STUTTER
                                                The creature’s presence sets reality stuttering and convulsing, allowing it to flicker from place to place. By reflexively spending 1 Willpower when it moves, the creature can translocate itself to any location it can see up to its Speed in meters away, seeming to observers to just spasm in and out of reality as it goes. Doing so adds +2 to its Defense for the turn.
                                                SURPRISE ENTRANCE
                                                No matter how secure its victims think they are, by spending 1 Willpower the creature may suddenly appear in the scene. It can manifest out of sight (either of a being or device) in any scene where someone has the Slender Sickness Condition, and then REALITY STUTTER past any barrier. Any character witnessing this entrance must succeed on a reflexive Resolve + Composure roll or gain the Shaken Condition.
                                                If the characters have actively taken appropriate measures to keep the Horror out (barring and locking the door, running recording equipment, etc.) it will take the creature one full turn to break through, giving the characters time to react. If not, it’s just there as a reflexive action and can take one more action before anyone can react.
                                                JUMP SCARE
                                                The creature appears suddenly or lurches into abrupt action just when you thought it was down for the count. By spending 1 Willpower, the creature may resolve the Shaken condition on another character, choosing which action will automatically fail. The character still earns a Beat as normal. Alternately, the creature may spend 1 Willpower to turn the automatic failure caused by a character resolving the Shaken Condition into a dramatic failure. In this case, the character earns two Beats (one for resolving the Condition, one for the dramatic failure).
                                                IMMORTAL
                                                The creature isn’t bound by mortal laws of life and death. It’s not indestructible, per se, but if it’s killed or incapacitated by anything other than its Bane, it just comes back in a later scene like nothing happened. Even if it’s destroyed with its Bane, in this case eradicating all knowledge of it, it’s never really gone — all it takes is someone learning of its existence to bring the creature back for a sequel story.

                                                ---Side Bar---
                                                Where's the Telepathic Powers?
                                                Slenderman is known for his powerful telepathic mind control. People blame Slenderman for possessing them, for mind controlling them, for forcing them to do the awful things they do in his name. The tragic truth is he has no such power other than his frightening presence and he never even makes requests. His presence is supernaturally terrifying, and having Slender Sickness disrupts normal thinking but does not rob its victims of agency. The horrible things Slenderman followers do they decide for themselves. He stalks, he lurks, he harries and terrifies but ultimately his victims do the most monstrous things all on their own.
                                                ----------------

                                                SLENDER SICKNESS CONDITION
                                                Those with this Condition are valid targets of Slenderman's SURPRISE ENTRANCE Dread Power. Also at the beginning of each session the player of the character chooses one of the follow conditions to apply at some point during that session: Frightened, Lost, Shaken, or Spooked. The Storyteller chooses when that happens in the story with a fleeting glimpse of Slenderman. If the character doesn't have a player associated with it mechanically (meaning she isn't a PC, nor does she represent a Merit, Touchstone or similar mechanic), the Storyteller chooses the Condition.
                                                Cause: Those that actively investigate the truth that Slenderman exists and believe it gain this condition. It is not enough to be told, the person with this condition has researched and earnestly sought out proof of the rumors and urban legends and found it. Mechanically this can be handled with the Clue system in CodD page 81, requiring 3 Clues (or for Mages the use of Focused Mage Sight with a Mystery Opacity of 1).
                                                Resolution: Gain the Amnesia Condition concerning everything they know about Slenderman (if they lose that Condition they regain this one)
                                                Beat: Have Slenderman use SURPRISE ENTRANCE Dread Power to enter a Scene with the character. (The other Conditions gained by this Condition grant Beats normally).
