    Alternate history question.

    05-04-2026, 01:19 PM
    I'm working on a campaign set in an alternate early 1940s where The US stayed out of WW1, which lead to all of Europe from France to Russia becoming socialist.

    One aspect of this world is that the Catholic Church is split between a 'Red' and a 'White' papacy. So my question is, what happens to the Malleus Maleficarum? Do they suffer a civil war before splitting in two? Do the majority join one side or the other? Or is it still business as usual for them?

                                      Honestly, with a popular socialist movement spreading across multiple socialist European states, independent from the Russian vanguard state socialist model, and not pursuing an active state atheism, it's sounding to me like the white papacy would be a distinct schismatic minority, with red Catholics still represented even outside the European states. I'd give the red church the witch-hammer, and if the white church can and does still maintain an active monster-hunting subset, it's more likely something between a compact and a conspiracy, making do without the established infrastructure for training witch-hunters. There are probably old witch-hunters who've schismed to the white church and taken their Benedictions with them, but it's gotten harder to teach newer priests, and priests may just generally be in short supply.

                                      The witch-hammer continuing to operate as one organization between both papacies is also a valid angle. They are a conspiracy, operating in secret to most of the church hierarchy, after all. Being concerned with monsters found across the world could easily bring them together more tightly than the political schism pushes them apart, resulting in red and white witch-hunters continuing to cooperate and share information and resources with their brothers, under the belief that the Vigil is the most important holy work to be done, and the schism could eventually be resolved. I like this angle. It combines simplicity with added conflict.
