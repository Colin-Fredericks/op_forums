        One idea I had for a memento vehicle (which eventually spawned into its own Horror) was that they could use plasm as fuel instead of conventional gasoline or diesel...but only if you left it in a low spot, or the underworld, which may or may not give it some unusual quirks. Giving it plasm from yourself while it's running could improve its performance for a scene...while the next scene it has a detriment to its function (say, only able to go up to safe speeds, if not lower). I don't know how balanced that is, but I feel like the larger the memento, the more odd it should be when death energies are involved. Again, the core book gave errata to that effect, but the mechanics of it never gave any indication that these could happen.

        I like the idea of investing psyche into mementos though, given that "psyche" literally means soul.

        Hmmm...what if mementos that anyone handled only gave minor effects, whereas mementos that are "bound" to a player (aka paid for with experiences) offered a much more profound effect (such as the aforementioned vehicle boost)...or vice versa, even, if you want mementos to be creepy gear that still provides some benefit to non-Sin Eaters using them. A bit like tokens in that regard, actually.

                        One thing I wanna bring up, since it seems relevant, is that the whole of the Chronicles of Darkness is written from a very humanist perspective-some games more than others, but all of them have that thread in there. Humanity is a tough thing to be, but it's uniquely celebrated through the adversity.

                        You want a game where it sucks to be human, go play World of Darkness. But as much as Chronicles is a game about monsters, that monstrosity is a giant toast to humanity, a bittersweet party mix played at the graduation wake. In this world, there is something to humanity, and if you remember it long enough, you just might get it back.

                        Sin-Eaters are monsters, as every gameline is, and there is much wrong with them and their niche of the corner. But fuck it, they ain't getting any deader, and there's Mexican Coke and Jack Daniels just waiting to have rough sex in their mouth. Eat, drink and be merry-you've been to the bottom of the world, you'll be back some day, and the only thing to do is to keep going up. You might even find what you lost along the way.

                        Not to say that there couldn't be more in the book to support the Mourner's, Reapers, and Pilgrim's arguments. But humanity is pretty cool, and if it takes making a deal with a fucked-up has-been god to get close to it again-well, eat, drink and be merry-how much more fucked can you get?

                                    Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                    Isn't that abit.... narratively mundane though? "I will bring you back to life.. and in return you must live a good life....?"
                                    If that was what the has-been god was saying, it might be. Does that musty old masquerade mannequin know what it wants? It forgot the taste of it's lovers skin when its tongue took on the thousandth layer of dust, what the shotgun spray of sunny celluloid color illuminates in the dark place of the head when it's eyes fell from their skull, what the thrill of the car crash is on ear when they clawed it out to keep from the screams ages ago. No, it doesn't ask for life. It doesn't remember. It asks for comprehension, it asks for sense, a dream it forgot when the world became dark.

                                    But it comes from the corpse that remembers, the bitter joke that's got legs again. It comes from the ghost who knows, the flatline that remembers. It comes from a human who knows things now, the one who has heard the raining rebar and echoed could-have-beens of the World Below. It comes from someone who has been to the bottom of the barrel, but remembers what sex and movies and hot dogs and never-ending red lights are. It comes from the person who had nothing and has come to realize that every pain, every pleasure, every frustration, every stupid, mind-numbing cry of hounds and telemarketers, every stupid, cringe-wringing pun from drunken slobs tripping on their own shit, every mosquito bite, every brain freeze, every gross fart-all of that is not the loss, the oblivion, the scouring of being. It is not that ocean, quiet and placid, that one slowly drowns in until there is nothing left.

                                    No, from the Geist, such a cry is meaningless-they don't even have a clue what they're talking about anymore.

                                    But from the Sin-Eater? The one who still has perspective?

                                    Life is it's own reward. Every fucking stupid, dumbass, exasperating bit of it. Eat it up. Drink it up. Live this entire fractured, fractal cesspool of delusions and every sensation and emotion you can wring from it to the hilt. These asinine, mundane things are the only holy and real things that matter. Life, in all it's god damned shades, is the only thing that matter.

                                    Eat it, drink it, and live it-because you've been as fucked as you can get, and you will be as fucked as you can get again in future, but neither is this day. And that makes today a good day.

                                    And one day, maybe even Death itself will remember what Life was like.

                                        I still think the game could play up how the Geists are supposed to embody different forms of death, and those forms directly impact the impulses/requirements placed on the Bound. If they are just "you live life and I'll sit back and enjoy" with no muscle/dark-side behind the Bargain, the whole idea that they embody different forms of death end up ringing kind of hollow.

                                        Not that I'm saying their desire to taste-life shouldn't be part of the mix at all! It should. But I think a flaw in 1E is the lack of muscle behind the Bargain encourages players to ignore it, unless they're specifically playing a Reaper, or something else where "well that's what I wanted to do anyway". I think that should be a major part of the "personal horror" of this particular game.
