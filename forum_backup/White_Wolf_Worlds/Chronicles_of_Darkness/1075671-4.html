    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post



    No, because again; the fire isn't operating under the boundaries of scientific observation. The fire is behaving in the fashion that it does simply because the mage in question wants the fire to do so. They will the fire to burn hotter, so it burns hotter, they will the fire to burn without fuel, so it burns without fuel, they will it to hold a specific shape or consistency, est. It's impossible to quantify, because it's only limitations are those of the mage's own imagination and personal training.

    They could use science and technology in order to simulate the spell, but it would not be the same thing as actually using magic.


    Take Dungeons and Dragons, for example. The spell "Burning Hands" allows the mage to expel a cone of flames from their hands. Now I could, in theory, strap a portable flame thrower to my wrists and shoot fire in that way. But I'm not casting Burning Hands, I'm using a machine to simulate the casting of Burning Hands.
    They aren't talking about trying to copy spells with mechanical devices. If a mage can create fire with their mind, that's technology. If the mage can repeat the event by creating fire with their mind again, you can apply scientific process to it and study it. Because if something can happen, it isn't magic by definition. You can call it magic, but the second it exists then things happened to make it exist. It's just might be using laws we don't understand yet.

                                    Originally posted by Vent0 View Post

                                    Does your version of magic have any limits or rules? Looks like "will" is at least one. Others?


                                    Actually, yeah. The way I view magic is that when two or more magic users clash, it's not a matter of opposing forces, but rather a contest of willpower. A mage's spells are only as strong as the willpower behind them. A defensive mage who allows their focus to waver can have their defenses penetrated. Regardless if they're trying to block an oncoming fire spell with a wall of smothering dirt, or a zone of pure oxygen.


                                    Likewise, I also use the rule that a mage's magic can be developed through constant practice; but this is once again due to the power of their belief. They have cast the spell enough times in the past that they are confident they can do so again when they need to. Attempting to cast a brand new spell, especially one that is being improvised in the heat of the moment is technically possible, but it may not be as potent because the magic user is having personal doubts about their abilities. This also is used to explain why the most powerful spells are so difficult to cast. There's not a whole lot of opportunity to practice them; so there aren't any real experts on their usage. You don't whip out a nuclear bomb as casually as a hand grenade, you save it as a last resort.


                                    When it comes to casting magic, any use of incantations, spell components, symbols and the like are essentially a placebo effect. By themselves, they do not give the mage any actual power. But the Mage can trick themselves into believing that they do, in order to make it easier to cast their spells or to improve their effectiveness. This would also be used to explain why normal people or the undisciplined can not wield such artifacts properly. They don't have the proper experience to focus their minds; so it seems like the artifact is worthless to them, or that is functions improperly from its intended purpose.
