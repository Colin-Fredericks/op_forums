                                        Originally posted by nofather View Post

                                        I really like this idea.

                                        I resolve a lot of social rolls that are not manuevering or contested (I don't like contested rolls that much honestly, too much time consuming and often too random) using following method:
                                        A simple roll of Skill+Attribute+Equipment, with penalties:
                                        -1 for each dot in appropiate Resistance Attribute (it's easy to come up with Res Attribute even if you literally don't have the NPC statted up)
                                        -1 for 1st, 3rd and 5th dots of appropiate "counter" Skill (so if you try to lie to someone with Subterfuge 3, they will reduce your pool by 2)
                                        - "called shot" penalty to achieve whatever flamboyant result you want.
                                        - circumstancial penalties that often are similar to "when to increase number of doors by x"

                                        This way, it works a lot like combat; you want to have around 3-4 dice left in your pool to have a good shot at your social roll, and utilize any leftovers for weaving in cool effects. It also allows me to reward social specialists with more interesting tools, and gate certain results behind relatively big penalties that will usually put you at chance dice at best. This way, when a player gets +2 to social roll from their clothes, they can actively use it to declare something like "I want to impress him!", and because it might be -2 to do so, clothes feel like they are "directly" responsible for doing it.

                                                To drive the point home: what if someone showed up to SWAT-raid level combat situation in a tuxedo? At best they'd be ridiculed, at worst completely barred from participating until they changed into something more appropriate and started treating the situation with the seriousness it deserves.

                                                Same reasoning behind showing up to a social event in a kevlar vest. People are at best going to snark at you, at worst you'll be told you're not allowed in until you change to conform with the dress code. No shirt, no shoes, no service.

                                                My game only had one character who routinely wore armor, and that was the police officer Geist who'd been killed by her own service weapon (so her never forgetting to wear her vest again made perfect sense.) Then again, the crossover group also included an Obrimos Mage who handed out Mage Armor like it was going out of style, and Bound had the Shroud Manifestation as well. But basically, mundane armor pales in comparison to the kinds of armor supernaturals can get. Vampires are intrinsically pretty damage-resistant, and loading Resilience on top of that generally means kevlar is a less-attractive option. Also, the main draw of bulletproof armors is the reduction of gunshot wounds to bashing damage, something vampires have just by virtue of being vampires (and better than a bulletproof armor does, if memory serves.) The reduction of damage tends to be minimal, again if memory serves. It could certainly save your unlife in a heavy combat situation, but in most situations it's probably not worth it, except for those wanting to squeeze every last ounce of efficiency out of their character builds. I'm not saying "armor's only for munchkins," just that there are probably better investments for those Resources dots.

                                                    Yeah, Kevlar Vests have torso coverage, reduce all incoming damage by 1 and downgrade the first 3 points of damage from firearms attacks to bashing. Since vampires already downgrade damage from mundane weapons to bashing, the only actual benefit they get is the 1 point of soak. Reinforced Clothing has the exact same benefit for vampires, extends the coverage to arms and legs, and can be more easily passed off as fashion. It's just straight up a better option. Other armors provide better general armor, but are more conspicuous. Generally, a vampire's natural resistance to mundane weapons, Vitae healing, and Resilience will be more than enough to protect them. Armor is really more of a fashion statement for vampires than actual protection. And the statement that is made by going around with unnecessary amounts of armor in everyday situations is not one most vampires will think highly of.
