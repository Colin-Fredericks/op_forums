        Originally posted by Reaper Verse View Post
        Oh, how I longed for this day to come.

        In my own honest and possibly uninformed opinion, there is kinda a general sentiment that magical girls, and in turn Princesses, cannot be portrayed in a negative light; after all, their powers are always used for the greater good and how could they possibly be antagonistic when they talk about love and friendship, having the confidence to fight against the darkness, never giving in to despair, etc.
        Not the case in Princess at all. The three Twilight Queens (my favourite of the antaognist splats) each represent a moral failing that all Princesses are prone too.

        Tears represents putting the people closest to you first, even to the extent of hurting others.
        Storms represents letting your anger at the monsters you fight turn into self-destructive hate.
        Mirrors represents clinging to your ideals even in denial of reality.

        However those are flaws that the Radiant Princesses are aware of and make efforts to avoid; they're just not always successful.


        A lot of the recent series that play with the idea of magical girls not nessacarally being good hinge on a shocking twist that changes the fundamental nature of Magical Girls, Madoka being an obvious example. For Princess I'm trying to avoid that, and I'm trying to avoid anything that makes it inevitable that a Princess will go bad. In Princess magical girls are good by nature, but they have a very hard job to do and some of them crack under the pressure, but others of them manage to live up to their potential.

        In other words. You don't need to change the fundamental nature of magical girls Madoka Style to do a darker take on the magical girl story. You have young people fighting monsters, portray that realistically and it's automatically dark.


        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
        Also, reading past the part that describes Whisps and I have to ask: is the part about a Princess having her emotions and feeling muted to the point of feeling empty if they are low or out of Whisps still a thing in the later or most updated version of the game. Because...well, shit. That's absolutely horrifying. The idea that the Blossoming effectively gives you the power to make your dreams a reality at the cost of hollowing you out definitly paints the "Inner Light" in a waaaay more sinister manner.
        That's still there in the latest Dream version. There's a specific condition for it.

        I wouldn't say it has sinister implications though. Thematically it represents "exhaustion". If a Princess is careful about managing her risks she need never experience this drawback. However if life throws problem after problem at her until she's out of Wisps then the Condition gives a little mechanical weight to how hard it is to balance school, social life, and fighting evil by moonlight. But you can't really blame the Light for the fact the world doesn't give Princesses the time they need to refuel; and it's not like the Light cursed Princesses with this drawback, any more than I would "curse" you to have to regularly by gas if I gave you a car.

        Originally posted by Master Aquatosic View Post
        Giving a literal child mental illness symptoms and telling them that they can't just try to cope with them the normal way is a pretty sinister thing to do for the Queens.
        The Queens don't create Princesses or otherwise choose who becomes a Princess.

        They do however teach Princesses how to cope with their symptoms. I always saw them taking a genre savvy approach. I want to tell this 14 year old Champion to stay home, train and let older Princesses handle it until she's 18 but I know nothing I say will stop her hunting monsters, so I better make sure she knows how to fight and has emotional coping skills.

        (Not least because somebody is going to want to play Sailor Moon and she's 14).

        P.S. the Darkness has a weakness to jade; that rule exists specifically for the benefit of Hunters.

                                            Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                                            However, I think we can both agree that it gets much more dangerous and sinister once you add both Sensitivity AND Belief to the mix.
                                            Dangerous sure, but to me sinister is casting a question upon the Light's motivation or ethics. For that to be true would first require that the Light has enough conciousness to possess either motivations or ethics, and secondally it would require that the Light had the ability to design the Princess' powerset and chose to include Sensitivity.

                                            Neither of those two requirements are confirmed. The Light might just be a natural force with no conciousness whatsoever.

                                            Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                                            That get's me wondering, just like for the Begotten how much of that is cultural traditions and dialogue?
                                            For Princesses it's all cultural traditions. If a bunch of Princesses decided to retreat into the mountains and build a utopian secret village they'd have a very easy time of it. They aren't going to start getting painful cravings to go out and do good, nor will they set of an eternal winter, or have the "Light Mother" turn up to scold them. The only reason hidden Princess only utopias aren't a common thing is that the kind of person who would choose a hidden utopia instead of sticking around to make the world better is not likely to Blossom.

                                            In other words, you Blossom because you've found and followed a Calling. Blossoming does not force a Calling upon you. The distinction is most obvious with Onceborn where the requirements for Blossoming are much stricter.
