        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
        Genius: the Transgression, like it's progenitors in the One World of Darkness, is a Urban (Science)Fantasy game with Horror Elements.
        I don't think that Genius needs an accuser splat to be horrific. Geniuses are supposed to be their own accusers, in their moments of lucidity asking themselves how their attempts to cure cancer ended up with harvesting orphans for parts or accusing their ecconomic supercomputer when it decides the best way to create new jobs is to build a wall on the Mexican border.

        It requires a skilled group of players and Storytellers to consistently make decisions that lead to their own downfall but I do not think that it is wrong for a Storytelling game to target itself at skilled players.

        Besides however horrific or un-horrific Genius is. It's no more of an urban fantasy game with horror elements than Geist: the Sin-Eaters.

        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
        Dilution is a problem. A game about magic users has enough space for prophets and physicists. To say a game about mad scientists can model prophets as well as physicists sounds off.
        I'm know a lot of people who think Werewolves being "spirit world border patrol" feels off. That doesn't mean it actually is off.

        In the introduction to Frankenstein Mary Shelly says she was inspired by Shakespear's the Tempest. Compare victor Frankenstein hidden on the Orkney Islands with his monster practising his secret art to Prospero on his island surrounded by mythical creatures and practising his secret art. Prospero in turn is inspired by Merlin - who is of course a famous prophet.

        Frankenstein is also an alchemist, not a scientist, and in this he is from a long and established literacy tradition of alchemists that draw a distinction between bad alchemists who (like Victor) peruse worldly knowledge or worldly power and good alchemists who use their art to bring themselves closer to god (the other definition of prophet).


        The literacy tradition of the mad scientist has increasingly diverged from it's historical origins in the spiritual, but the legacy remains. Prospero on is island with spirits, became Victor on his island with the monster, became Ozymandus in Antarctica with Bubastis and Syndrome on his island with his robots.

        To say that it's "off" for a game about mad scientists to acknowledge the historical origins of the mad scientist archetype and include characters modelled on those earlier archetypes (common in the sitting's backstory, rarer as the setting passes the point the mad scientist archetype diverges from it's roots) is frankly baffling to me.

        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
        Genius tells me it's about Mad Scientists
        No. This is factually wrong. Genius tells you it's about visionaries in the introduction.

            Errol216, Thank you for putting a link to that video as a response. It's a fine example of how to build an environment of horror. I do believe Genius really tries to do that too. It's just that rather than making it about the dark, terrifying, and wondrous world of the Chronicles of Darkness fighting back against a perfectly sane and brilliant mind trying to unlock it's mysteries and push it's boundaries, it instead tries to make...Quiet. It tries to make Quiet from Mage the Ascension. There, I said it. But you see, here's the thing. Quiet is a horror element in Ascension because it represents the Consensus punishing a Mage's hubris for trying to make it bend to their beliefs. It's response: give the Mage a piece of their own medicine by bending their perception of reality to mirror the Mage's hubris. In Genius though, Unmada is no outside force punishing a Genius for their transgressions. It's just a Genius' brain getting fried on Mania juice because they thought they could handle it. It's more of a drug overdose rather than opening Pandora's box. That's why it falls flat horror wise.

            The Kings Raven, I can definitely agree that Geist is certainly the most Urban Fantasy oriented of the CofD gamelines (alongside Vampire of course) but it is definitely a Horror game. The reason for that (and a reason Genius certainly could benefit from) is the inescapable Other in the form of the Geist itself. A Sin-Eater's life depends upon the Geist, and it's an alien entity that feeds on death. No matter what, there will always be a shadow over the Sin-Eaters shoulder, a homage to Wraith the Oblivion's Shadow Play. Genius has no such tension, a Genius lost to Unmada is driven by certainty. There is no horror in certainty. There an be dread and despair, but not horror. Horror doesn't come during moments of lucidity where you ask yourself how your attempts to cure cancer ended up with you harvesting orphans for parts. Horror comes from discovering that the world you live in is nothing like you innocently believed. Horror lives and breaths in the moments where you wonder what's better: knowing or not knowing.

                Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                The reason for that (and a reason Genius certainly could benefit from) is the inescapable Other in the form of the Geist itself. A Sin-Eater's life depends upon the Geist, and it's an alien entity that feeds on death.
                I was actually pretty disapointed by how benign and passive the Geist came across (especially in the games I played).

                While the Dem Bones SAS did a really good job of creating a unique and horrific Geist. I don't think I've ever seen a Geist cause trouble for a Sin-Eater in any of the games I've played. You don't even loose Synergy if a moral Sin-Eater refuses to kill people when their Geist Murder McSlauhgter asks them too.

                Genius very much does have in inescapable Other in the form of Inspiration. A Geist (who died in a car accident) whipsering in your ear to cut the break lines when you walk past a parked car is very similar to Inspiration pushing you to upgrade the engine (which would cause a car crash when it goes 0-500 mph in seconds). Inspiration isn't portrayed as a singular entity you can talk to like the Geist, but it is definately an inescapable Other riding along in your head.


                I've certainly seen more Geniuses pushed into making bad decisions by Inspiration than Sin-Eaters pushed into making bad decisions by their Geist. That might just be a coincidence of course, but I suspect that it might be more interesting for players to create giant death robots and attack the university than to cut break lines.

                Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                Horror lives and breaths in the moments where you wonder what's better: knowing or not knowing.
                I don't think Geist asks you "what's better knowing or not knowing". Take one of the biggest mysteries in all of human existance: what happens after death. Geist answers that question and it pretty firmly says Sin-Eaters are better of now that they know. Even with a particularly nasty Geist in your head, you still get to a point where the knowledge is good and the Geist is bad. I cannot think of anywhere in Geist where it says Sin-Eaters would be better of for not knowing about the truths of death.

                Meanwhile that question, "what's better knowing or not knowing" is the very core of Genius: What's better blissful ignornace or knowing that the world is so chaotic that nothing anyone does will ever go according to plan?


                I think your view on horror is rather narrow - but given your views I can see why you wouldn't see Unmada as horrific. If you take "what's better knowing or not knowing" then from that perspective Unmada are the people who say "blissful ignorance is definately better" and dive right in. From that perspective, of course choosing blissful ignorance is going to be less horrific than not choosing blissful ignorance.

                From that perspective the fact nobody punishes an Unmada is missing the point. For the choice between blissful ignorance and painful knowledge to be meaningful you have to have the ability to choose blissful ignorance. Punishing Unmada would just be taking that choice away from Geniuses and deminish the possibility.

                Being an Unmada isn't horrific. Being a non-Unmada Genius and having to live every day with facing the truth or giving in, becoming Unmada, and becoming part of the problem is horrific.

                                        The Kings Raven, Good morning. For what it's worth Geist is the closest of the CofD gamelines to being Urban Fantasy, and as long as you had a great time with it then I'm glad.

                                        Right, now back to the thick of things. I believe what Charlaquin was trying to say, and please correct me if I'm assuming to much, is that horror is nullified if the mystery behind the horror is transparent. It would be like walking up to an enclosure at the zoo and seeing behind the glass window a black and roiling void that seems to pull you in while simultaneously trying to expand and escape it's enclosure vs. that exact same scenario except there's a display name on the glass window that says: Paradox Spirit, avoid eye contact.

                                        Ok, sorry. That was a bit of an extreme example, but here's the thing. If a horror game is going to be about nihilism, then nihilism needs to stalk the players like a hungry wolf, always at the treeline gathering it's pack until the player is at their most vulnerable. Then *Blam!* it goes in for the kill. Here's the problem with that, if there is no win scenario for the players, then this is essentially a one trick pony. Because if nothing truly matters in that game then there's no reason to pick it up again except for cathartic wanton abandon.

                                        I mentioned that the best horror stories make you ask questions once the story is over, this is because at it's heart horror is about terrifying agency. It's about examining the steps the characters took to get to the end and realize mistakes and empathy along the way. I ask you this: Was "What do you do when nothing you do is going to matter?" the only question Genius left you with? If not, then there is more nuance to the game then it let's aware upon examination. If so, then why isn't there?
