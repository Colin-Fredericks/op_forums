    Abyss Inspiration: The Dreams of Ruin

    10-22-2017, 10:57 PM
    Basically, someone on RPGnet pointed me at this free download that is essentially a major antagonist for a Horror and Science Fantasy themed OSR game; the titular Dreams of Ruin, which is actually called that because it is essentially a self-aware, self-casting suite of illusion and reality-altering spells transmitted by an occult fungus.

    I thought it was really good inspiration for what happens when an Annunaki is successfully summoned, maybe one of Theumiel's patrons (I seriously like the old bastard). See, when a Dream-spore gets through, it feeds on entropic energy and dimensional fabric, inducing bad luck and evil deeds to get the former and opening rifts in dimensions by way of the latter. That's only part of what makes it a threat-a mature Dream is effectively an ecology, using the gathered energy to not only spread its mycelium but to spin an entire Forest of Woe into existence, complete with black trees that feed on sorrow and goblin-spiders that serve as its priesthood. As it grows, it starts recycling bodies as a servant force of puppets/food for predators, and draws bandits, serial killers, and monsters into it where it becomes a perfect lair for them, inverting its normal flow of eating magic to empower them. It even exudes a mental field that causes everyone nearby to become meekly superstitious victims unable to consider directly opposing or even acknowledging it. At later stages, it even summons a race of malicious forest fae whose wild games help sustain its growth even more.

    That sounds pretty Abyssal to me, replacing normal reality with its own ecology of thought and dream, where there are no true competitors and yet there exists predation that feeds the whole forest. Sound like it to you?

              Funny story, they actually invoke King Arthur in the book.

              Since I may do this for Halloween, have my interpretation of one of the major monsters:

              Bakegumo

              Quote: <sounds of moaning, sistrums, and cracking branches from no apparent source>

              The priests and shamans of the Forest of Woe, bakegumo are technically independent gulmoth of the Forest of Woe, but unlike murdersprites, their reproduction is actually totally dependent on the nightmare ecology-in fact, it is their parent. Much as how certain insects have specialized organs for growing symbiotic bacteria, bakegumo are nurtured within Twilight womb-chambers generated by the maturing Dream, and birthed into the world purely to serve the parasitic ecosystem-without it, they will quickly die off barring some quick thinking on their part to replace a flow of nutrients, but unfortunately bakegumo have one thing that differs them from the mindless soldiers that are the puppets of ruin-they're smart, they're individually sentient, and they're naturally curious. More than once an orphaned bakegumo has not only adapted to the Fallen World but grown to thrive in it, transforming their life force into the semi-Astral form of new acamoth, eagerly using their investments to lead their parent-god back to the Tellurian.

              In the legendary that describes Old Woman Crow and her proto-Scelesti working, the bakegumo are referred to as her husband's animate hands, and it's easy to see why-bakegumo do not look very much like the goblin-spiders they got their name from. They look like giant, man-sized severed hands, withered with age, at least until dissected; their sensory organs and brains are contained in their middle fingers, the other four gnarled digits being legs. Even their abdomen completes the illusion, looking nothing so much as a wrist seemingly torn off its arm, with white bone sticking out. In fact, the "bone" is their other three legs, tucked tightly around their disproportionately tiny abdomen; the sole purpose of those legs is to help weave both their sticky web and their spells of illusion with somantic mudras, allowing the rest of the fingers to serve as legs.

              By far the longest-lived of the Dream's servants, the bakegumo can be compared to "officers" of a sort; unlike the short-lived puppets and the kamikaze murdersprites, bakegumo have something of a survival instinct and enough of a biology to survive for more than a couple years. In fact, given how violent life in the Forest is, most still live around 3-5 years, but a cautious and clever one can and does live much longer-20 years in the Forest itself, indefinitely if they've had to adapt to other environs. Also unlike either of their cousin species, bakegumo have an intellect suited for more than just war and rote ritual; they actively manage the ecology of symbiotes that make up a mature infection of the Dream, and happily recruit cultists (via dreams; they know how horrifying their appearance is, and thus do not show themselves until the cultist loves the forest as they do) to help the spread of the Abyssal woodland. Of course, when it comes down to it, the Forest of Woe still feeds on pain, despair, and chaos, so all bakegumo have a sadistic streak a mile long. It's how they initially manifest-when it first spawns them, the goblin-spiders do not have enough energy to manifest physical matter, instead being bound to Twilight...but they can see and use their distinct ghost-flames to interact with the material world, hypnotically drawing victims to a place where they can literally terrify and burn the poor sods to death. As they do so, the more physical they become-and the more numerous. A mature node of the Forest of Woe can have over a hundred bakegumo spinning their webs and spells within it at any given time, with their replacements just a second away from full gestation.
