    [Advice] Public displays of supernatural creatures

    04-25-2022, 07:18 PM
    Hello!

    I'm start DMing to my group various CofD games this time lately. I have two 1v1 plays, one of Mage [very crossover heavy] and one of Mummy. With a third normal play with the rules of Hunter but in the SCP universe.

    I really have fun, and when I meet this place I fund a lot of insight and helpful homebrew, specially for Mage. So this is my first post and as a not native english speaker, I have fear.

    Today I have a question I not see in my travel for this forum, maybe I'm blind.

    How do you manage public displays of supernatural creatures/power?

    This is a question I have from the moment I read Mummy. The status quo can be broke with a single Utterance, rain of blood and frogs and monstruos swarms of carnivoruos locust.

    The fact is not even that only mages have "laws of the universe" as their "Veil", with Paradox and the Abbyss, but that even the art of the game have images of horrible monsters walking on streets, terrorizing cities and being confronted with a lot of normal people.

    Do you play that kind of scene or are they a "don't do this or the play ends" kind of deal? What happen if a Mummy wants to say "fuck it" and become the king of a city by power of Utterance? What happen if a Vampire takes control of the streets with their ghouls and stablish martial law?

    I really want to see what are your takes on this.

    Thanks!

      There's a sort of sorting algorithm of efficiency when it comes to dealing with problems.

      • It's less of a drain on resources to endeavor to halt offensive maneuvers at vital points than to halt offensive maneuvers at every point a threat could approach, because replacing the things you use up to halt maneuvers takes energy.

      • It's less of a drain on resources to evade an offensive maneuver or force it to miss than to try and halt it outright, because you don't have to replace something you don't lose.

      • It's less of a drain on resources to hide a point of vulnerability from threats than it is to have it evade threats or force those threats to miss, because it's easier to avoid threats if they don't know where you are.

      • It's less of a drain on resources to prevent threats from learning a point of vulnerability exists at all than it is to prevent them from learning how to get at it, because they're less likely to find you if they aren't looking for you.

      • It's less of a drain on resources to prevent a point of vulnerability from being learned of by people in general than it is to specifically prevent threats from learning of a point of vulnerability, because the less people know about you the less chances your enemies have to find about about you.

      Monsters with staying power stay hidden as often as they can and take pains to ensure that people who discover that they're monsters don't spread it around, whether by bringing them into the conspiracy or killing the witnesses or leveraging memory-wipes. People with reasonable fears and some amount of self-preservation tend not to talk about the supernatural horrors they've encountered to avoid inviting monsters to take those pains while a semblance of normal life can be maintained.

      It's a bit of a tired thing to trot out at this point, but: mortals vastly outnumber monsters, and they collectively bring something to the table that most monsters can't wholly discount: it is significantly more trouble to exert power openly in the long term, whether because giving everyone a reason to try and kill you means they'll either wise up and get coordinated or just roll the dice enough times that they get lucky, because murdering them leaves invisible consequences that know where to find you, because you need them around to live and killing them or giving them reason to leave is counterproductive, or because those big dramatic displays of power are taxing to your reserves and you'd get better returns mugging tourists in back alleys or breaking into museums after dark or holding seances in church basements.

      Vampires and mummies run into this the most directly, being the two splats with a central timer limiting their power, and it's not a coincidence that they're the ones whose special location Merit can easily be represented as "I and/or my crew pulled some strings and got this secret room with secret passageways built into the floorplan of this building so I can come and go without anyone being the wiser."

      Tapes can disappear. Photos can burn or be corrupted. Explanations can be contrived, and most people know enough to nod along and corroborate the version of events that doesn't imply the world is coming to an end as long as the world is not visibly coming to an end around them.

      In the moment, most people will have more important things to focus on then the possibility that Nothing Will Ever Be The Same Again, and so a lot of parties have a vested interest in ensuring, by whatever means they deem effective, people don't cross that threshold after they're exposed to an overt supernatural occurrence. The Men In Black show up, and maybe they flash a penlight in your eyes, maybe they jam a syringe in the back of your neck, maybe they just give you a meaningful look and say that it sure is a good thing that you survived that freak traffic accident, huh? and tap their earpiece and expect you to take the hint.

              And what I'm getting at is that the majority of people trying to maintain their Integrity by avoiding the supernatural and acknowledging the mundane explanations over the more truthful account means you would have to do a lot of sustained open supernatural activity in a way that they cannot escape, which is both generally beyond the scope of a single character's capabilities and specifically the sort of thing multiple groups in-setting work to keep from happening at a scale grander than a cabal of monsters ruling over a small town off the grid.

              Trying to tear down the veil tends to escalate things, and escalation tends to make things more precarious for a monster in the spotlight, and that tends to mean they don't survive long without going to ground and letting the heat die down, at which point the veil promptly begins to repair itself. Disinformation machines spin up their gears, conspiracies pull on their puppet strings in society, and almost everyone starts trying to convince themselves that things did not happen in quite as mind-breaking a fashion as they seemed to now that they have a moment to breathe and they're not panicking at what they surely only thought was a monster or a magic spell or a flagrant violation of How The World Works.

                  I've only read the 1e corebook, I need to buy the 2e one soonish, but the general rule is that your average CofD mortal does know that the supernatural is there on some level. World of Darkness runs on the assumption that monsters are just that good at hiding, but Chronicles of Darkness relies on a widespread willfull ignorance combined with few knowing anything specific.

                  If your average CofD mortal notices the supernatural they deal with it in whatever way they can, try to go on with their lives, and in two months only vaguely remember what happened. They don't take a video of a mummy walking down the street, don't tell their friends that they saw a werewolf (who also have inbuilt, but imperfect, masquerade protection), they're not even going to mention the weird creature carved from glass and metal that has their face.

                  If you don't do that, then there's a gameline about that kind of person. There's even at least one Compact dedicated to tearing down the veil.

                  As for why it never gets that far? Very few supernaturals can get the power to operate openly or play king without getting killed. A kindred uses their Disciplines and ghouls to rule over a city? Unless they're the only vampire in town there's likely six others just looking for an excuse to off them, not to mention the hunters they'll create and any other supernaturals running about.

                  Blue is sarcasm.

                      As TwoDSix said, currently CofD works mostly based on the idea that the world chooses to ignores the supernatural than the idea that secrecy is of utmost importance. In fact, the setting does not break even if you tear down the greater secrecy entirely, you can play in a city where Vampires, Werewolves and Mages are common knowledge, and still have most characters living in secrecy and people avoiding paying too much attention.

                      Something is inherently wrong with the world. Some supernaturals are in a position to notice it more clearly, some not so much. Some have theories to what's going on. Demons say it is an effect of the God-Machine, Mages suspect the Abyss or something intrinsic to the Fallen World. Regardless, while specific individuals may detach from this effect and become more aware, humanity in general chooses to ignore. It sweeps away evidence on its own, alternative explanations are accepted promptly, and when no one is looking they deal with whatever they actually know by trying to forget and learn as little more as possible while trying to fade back into true ignorance.

                      But you don't even need this much. Individually the supernatural is rare. In a world where people know vampires exist, they still don't know you're one of them unless you show off. As Satchel explained, secrecy is just good policy regardless of a more general awareness. You'll have an easier time doing your stuff keeping a low profile, and your enemies will have an easier time doing theirs, too. And if one decides to live in the open? Let them be. As long as they don't bring attention to others that means nothing. And if it does, then just shut them down and after the smoke is settled people will go back to their own business.
