    Sorcerous Societies [New/Revised Sects/Orders]

    06-03-2023, 09:01 AM
    This is an idea that has been buzzing in my head for quite some time. In general, I've always liked to ponder about how to hack settings and rebuild them, and one of the things that I always liked about the CofD is the occult organizations that it tends to feature. This is probably the reason why Hunter is my favorite gameline, as not only that it feature such organizations, it also actively encourage you to make your own homebrew. Mage, on the other hand, was a significantly more structured (especially in its early stages) due to the metaphysical Truths which the orders are meant to represent. Later Mage products, and especially 2e, have made shaken things up, but the game still remains on the cohesive society side of CofD games.

    Now, don't get me wrong- I don't think that having a cohesive, structured society is a bad thing. Between Legacies, Nameless Orders and Factions, players and storytellers have a wide set of tools if they wish to expand their setting, while the setting gains a lot from having a solid, organized structure that maintains the themes that the game wishes to tell. However, the itch in my brain keeps on itching, especially after Tome of the Pentacle have dropped all while introducing a in depth history of the Awakened, the local views of mages around the world, and the Lost Orders. Now, while scratching the itch could be a good enough reason to start pondering around with the setting, without a defined purpose it would be (a) harder to focus on what exactly this project is meant to be (b) help others understand the goal of this project and whether they are interested in it beyond a general "homebrew setting lol". As such, I think that I should start with some well defined goals of what this project is meant to achieve, so both you (and I) will know better how to finally get rid of that annoying itch.

    1) As Above, So Below: Imperial Mysteries is, like, a really cool book imo, adding a whole new level to the game by going full blown Cosmic, and one of the interesting features it adds is the introduction of the Ententes- those divine alliances between godlike entities who struggle against one another over holding the fabric of reality. This concept is nice considering how it parallels the Sects which compose the Awakened society.. to a degree. I mean, we have the Exemplars, who represent and support the values of the Pentacle Alliance, the Tetrarchs, who stand at the head of the Iron Pyramid, and the Aswadim, who explore the depths of the Abyss for possibilities found outside of the Truth and are viewed as gods by the Scelesti. However, that's only half of the Ententes, with the others being not as easily mapped into the Fallen World. The Alienated, who worship the Old Gods of the Supernal, sound like something that could be easily developed and distinguished into its own pseudo-Sect, in a similar manner to the Obelisk- but then we have the Siddhas, who seek to embody the Arcana themselves, or the Bodhisattvas, who serve the Oracles and seek to progress humanity towards Ascension which both overlaps and is very different from the goal of the Orders? Those groups are not easily mapped into a distinguished Sects, and tying to Supernal Truths to the Fallen Lie sounds like something would be very satisfying to me.

    2) Lost in Translation: Awakening has a very distinct identity which differs a lot from its predecessor, Ascension, to the point that one could probably make a setting with the two different types of Mages coexisting in the same world. However, while the two are indeed different, we can clearly see Ascension's fingertips over Awakening, such as the Tremere, the Ascension War, the Nefandi, Aswadim and even the Technocratic Union. Now, a lot of those resemblances are merely shout outs, using same names in a very different context- but that only makes me think how we could harvest other features from Ascension in a manner that would still feel unique and fitting to the Awakening setting. The Translation Guide offers some help with such adaption (honestly, in an ideal world I would have loved to get more of that series, but oh well), which further makes me interested in taking the elements from Ascension and port them into Awakening in an (hopefully) interesting way, including some of the Traditions and Factions of the game as Sects and Orders (not necessary correspondingly).

    3) What if: Isn't this an interesting question to ask- what if? What if the Awakened history did not take the same turns and twists in did in the canon? What if the Great Cults were to maintain their own distinct identity over the philosophies of the Awakened? What if the Mysterium were not as unified as it is in modern time, still allowing both the Keepers and the Pancryptiates their own distinguished identity? What if the Great Refusal were to not be as effective as it was in the canon? What if the Vedmak were to be accepted as an Order and were to avoid the Tremere trap? What if Nassau were to survive the joined attacks of the Seers and the Ladder? In a setting of false histories, erased pasts and rewritten reality, the multiverse and the questions it rise are something hidden right beneath the surface, and the Tome of the Pentacle emphasis it in a very clear manner, speaking about events which never were in order to expose the Lie which the Sleepers call as "history".

    4) Blurry Lines: there is something a bit, let's say, "funky" in how Sects are presented in the setting. I mean, in early 1e, we had a very clear division- mages were either a Pentacle or a Seer. Easy and simple. However, as the setting evolved and developed, things became much less easy to define- the Pentacle was a modern creation from the get go, and the separation between the Diamond and the Free Council became more and more apparent as time progressed, to the point that in the current state, it is more true to say that we have 3 Sects- the Diamond, the Council and the Seers, with the integration of the Pentacle being a "work in progress" as we can see with the crumbling of the Diamond Astral Realm (which may be replaced with something else... or not. Who knows?). This is better reinforced by the Free Council having its Assemblies and the Seers having their Ministries, which help both feel large and present enough in contrast to the Diamond and its Orders... however, both the Council and the Seers are also Orders, having a singular Order Merit, even though those ones are easily adaptable to many personal communities. Still, it creates a situation where it starts making you ask "when does a Order becomes a Sect"? After all, the Diamond Orders have their own Factions, very different ideologies and do not always work together in harmony. What does it take for a Order to become a Sect is a question that has been bugging me for a while, and that I plan on trying to answer for myself in here.

    5) Remnants: not everything that was presented in 1e has been equally adapted to 2e. The most glaring example for this is the way Banishers are presented between the editions, moving from a secret pseudo-Order into a phenomenon + a few random Legacies. Note, I don't think that the transition was bad for the setting as a whole. The material given about Banishers in the new edition, such as Harrowing Awakening, is really good, and as the setting started to move from Atlantis as a actual entity lost to time and more towards treating it as a symbol, the "role" of the Banishers in the setting have changed. So I do agree that looking on the final product, the change was probably for the better- but I can't deny that I really liked the Banishers lore in 1e, including the Timori, Locust Eaters, their treatment as a pseudo-Order and so forth. As such, porting them, together with potentially other concepts that were left scattered around in 1e (such as Magical Traditions or the Blood of the Dragon) and have not been fully realized in 2e, sounds like a subject of interest in this project.

    6) Unified Occult Society Theory: so as I said in the beginning, I like the occult societies that CofD has to offer, and for a long time I have hacked and revised all kinds of ideas about how to adapt them all under a singular paradigm. Organization rules, obviously, are helpful mechanically, but I am less interested in mechanical unification and more in a thematic unification. Up to this point, I have mostly used Hunter's perception to do so, restructuring the Tiers and redefining types of organizations in order to fold groups from other gamelines into a single unified setting- something which felt natural for Hunter considering, again, its flexibility in the subject. However, through the use of Sects and such, it makes sense that once I start doing some rewrites to how Orders work in Mage, I would feel the same itch to try and use this setting to fold other occult societies under the same umbrella, especially ones that have some very strong interaction with the gameline- such as the Sons of Phobos from Vampire or the Shield and Spear from Hunter. That also includes trying to adapt the special sorcerous methodologies of other gamelines, such as Blood Sorcery, Sekhem Sorcery or Alchemy, into a singular system to present the methods of "foreign sorcery" (much to the Tremere's delight, obviously).

    7) Welcome to Atlantis: both a mirror project and a motivation to this one, one of the projects I have in the pile of "things I need to do" is the Atlantis Shard that I have been working on for some time. Basically, it is a reimagining of the setting from the Atlantean point of view, presenting a vision of Atlantis as a utopia of magic and wonder on the verge of failure, all while reflecting the Fallen reality that the Awakened live in. As such, it requires some good correspondence of the different factions into Atlantean symbolism- including enemy factions and hostile entities. While it can relay solely on the current setting, as it draws from all kinds of places in the setting, including Imperial Mysteries, it also creates a feedback loop that further demands changes in the core setting to completely fit the vision I have for that shard. Basically, we have a feedback loop in here. As such, every entry in here will not cover how the Order and Sects work within the "standard" setting but also how they work within Atlantis. Expect the use of many dragon parts in this one.

    So yeah, this is basically a 7-part explanation for the motivation (very Tremere appropriate). While I thought to actually start tackling the project in this post, I think that it is enough for people who may be interested to get a grasp of what it is meant to be and comment appropriately. Instead, I will post a outline of my current plans for the project, just so you will know to where we plan on going with this and comment accordingly if you have ideas or suggestions:

    Outline

    Broken Diamond: alternative Diamond Orders and the Mysterium and Guardians as Sects.

    Humanity's Best and Worst: Free Council restructured

    Top of the Pyramid: Seers analysis

    Shattered Obelisk: Scelesti analysis

    Gnawing at the World: Tremere analysis
    ​
    War in Heaven: Verbena, Celestial Chorus and the Traditions

    Shadow of the Council: Technocratic Union

    Those Left Behind: Nameless Orders

    Death of Magic: Banishers and Hunters

    Ashes of Ascension: Bacchanal (Marauders) and Nephandi

    Thieves and Tricksters: Promethean Brotherhood, Cheiron, Faithful of Shulpae and Hototogisu Oh My

    Watchers From the Outside: non Supernal magic and dedicated occult organizations



    Well, that's it for now. I hope you will find this of interest. I can't commit that things will be posted according to the order of the timeline and that things won;'t change as the project progress, but feel free to already give suggestions and your own ideas about the project. Hope you'll find it of interest!

                          I feel the need to point out that the unifying element of the Diamond sect is the Elemental Precept "The Awakened are One Nation", also known as the Diamond Precept. The Silver Ladder lobbied hard for that and the Seers are notably lesser in numbers than the Pentacle. The Diamond Orders weren't called that because there are 4 sides to the shape, but because the fundamental shared/agreed upon concept (that we can do this better if we work together as our own society) was associated with the gemstone in the philosophy of it's most vocal advocates.
                          I say this because the first section ("Broken Diamond") spends a lot of time kind of missing that point.
                          Yes, from a meta standpoint, the Diamond was about the 4 orders making it up...but it was from a meta standpoint that no longer applies with the newer source material. At the very least, it was untrue by the time 2nd edition came out. It means something else now.

                          Originally posted by LostLight
                          I mean, what stops the Silver Ladder, the Guardians and the Arrow from breaking apart?
                          The Silver Ladder spends more time than it likes making sure they don't break apart. Heck, I'm pretty sure Consilia​ exist to lessen the pressure to just run off and never talk to one another again. which is why the Pentacle recognizes Assemblies as Consilia with a different structure. Because they exist to keep the peace and channels of communication open. Knowing this, in my opinion, is kind of required to running the Silver Ladder in games. In this alternate setting, they failed to hold on to either of the Mysterium's foundational Orders after the Corpus Mysteriorum began circulating, if I am seeing where you are going with this.

                          Another concern I have before I hit the next section is that you mention the "dragon" element of the Orders as Atlantean...but in Tome of the Pentacle, it officially came out that was an element the Tremere contributed to the Diamond during their tenure as members. I feel the need to point out that the Orders are constructs of Fallen World history and direct heirs of physical Atlantis. They arose When Great Cults met and people saw their teachings, mysteries and Truths mirrored amongst strangers. Then time passes and new ideas circulate, building them up little by little. They don't spring fully formed from the Time Before anymore.

                          Now the concept of "Mages are the embodiment of Truth" as Atlantean...Atlantis is much more divorced from the reality in 2nd ed. It exists as philosophic stand-in for what was lost in the Fall. It never existed in their timeline, and the times it did, it probably wasn't called Atlantis, (or Shangri-La, Ubar, Irem or any of the other names that the Mage line has called it over the years). Atlantean is not a single definition, Secrets of the Ruined Temple spends a lot of wordcount explaining that very well.
                          I think the word you're looking for is Supernal. Because claiming that magical symbolism comes from a common "ancestral" culture that developed under the divine realms of Truth, is a LOT easier to sell to other such groups if you agree ALL the names are a little wrong. The Supernal Realms are a common enough touchstone to work in that regard. Especially when the argument is "we are all expressions of the same Truth, let's compare notes and work together".
                          To be clear, it is less that statement I disagree with, and more the idea that the Orders tie themselves so strongly to "Atlantis" as a name.

                          Honestly the fact that you tie the Orders to virtues in a bizarre, twisted fashion of Plato-inspired philosophy...works rather well for Mage, with it's pseudo-imperialism and patronizing separation of Sleeper beliefs and Awakened practices. This I can get behind. Justified nonsense is exactly the kind of thing I expect to see in a game about wizards with freeform magic and any half decent framework can generate interesting ideas here. If this is how they wishes to sell the Diamond Precept in this setting, I can buy it.

                          The Atlantean Diamond is next, but not right now.

                                          Originally posted by Phoenix853457 View Post
                                          The Core Belief and Symbolism of the Forge of Vulcan and Watchers of Fortune in detail?
                                          It is hard to go too much in depth as it is not written (and this project already has more flesh than I intended), but in short-

                                          Forge of Vulcan: the Mercurian Manuscripts are texts comprising the philosophy of the Forge where everything can be described in terms of trade, transmutation and production, with magic being described as the resource which needs to be measured, calibrated and distributed. Think a philosophy which combines elements of economy and alchemy through the eyes of the producer, so a lot of "law of equivalent exchange". Their Symbolism, Products, refers to them using tools which are both objects they personally crafted, the very act of manufacturing, bringing use to others through their actions, fair trade and work tools. Even giving charity to those in need or taking wealth from those who hoard it so it would bring use are part of their symbolism. A lot of focus about fulfilling the needs of the collective.

                                          Watchers of Fortune: the Will of the World is the idea that there is a "natural way" according to which things should happen, and that the Awakened most not disrupt that natural flow but work alongside of it. There is a lot of emphasis about "being one with the world" and not acting without understanding the consequences of your actions, and trying to align your own Will with that of the Supernal to make sure your actions serve the Truth, as the Supernal embodies itself within the Fallen and by reading the signs correctly one could fulfil their role and achieve Ascension. Their Symbolism, Patterns, focus around the idea that magic manifest itself through events which seems random, but hold meaning when looked at context. The flight of birds, the spilled entrails, the roll of dice- all of those are not random, but ways through which the Awakened can read the path they should take. That basically means OCD level of obsession in many regards, giving meaning for personal patterns and avoid actions until the events "fit", as well as using astrology, geomancy and other methods which mean to "balance" or "align" the spell with the natural patterns of the world.

                                          Hope it helps!
