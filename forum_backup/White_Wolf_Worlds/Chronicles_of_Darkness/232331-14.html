                                Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                That sounds more like an Elemental Render; it's so impersoanl. A Hunterheart, no matter the seeming, "hunts".
                                The wildfire that chases you through the woods, cracking twigs and splitting trees manic and cruel laughter. The tornado that descends like the feet of an angry god and rampages across your homes like a toddler. The sandstorm running slipshod across your encampment, trampling like a herd of horses. The thun'drous stalker who follows silent and invisible, detectable only as a charge of static and scent of ozone before you are struck and electrocuted as if by a descending hawk.

                                Where there is humanity, there are stories. Where there are stories, nature has been anthropomorphised. Where nature has been made manlike, it can be made animal-like. Neptune's frothing white chargers, say.



                                *




                                I believe, until a writer comes up with gold, it's best to work with them as separate. The key difference between the two is that a Beast and an Elemental have different narratives. Identical construction of kith, seeming and build will still fundamentally point to different perspectives.

                                A hunter heart elemental is a predatory force of nature. A hunter heart beast is a reflection of human nature. It's the difference between "the mountains don't care. If you're out after dark, you will freeze and die" and "the wolf must hunt". An elemental hunter heart could be lightning which strikes the tallest, cutting all things down. A beast hunter heart could have spent it's durance thinking it was a thunderbird. Identical build and thematics, very different stories.

                                E:
                                Originally posted by Murder-chan View Post
                                &quot;be a shark (or a fish or an octopus or seal&quot; is only different from &quot;be a mermaid or a selkie&quot; in the seeming-related flavor. If you've decided to &quot;be a shark&quot;, but as a Fairest, that would be like a fierce warrior mermaid with a shark tail and sharp teeth, really.

                                I'm going to slide in with the &quot;Don't push Beast and Elemental into the same role&quot;, because, like Heavy Arms said (which is actually a pretty good answer to Dataweaver's question? So it seems like it got missed by some people, I think), Beasts and Elemental are operating on a much different difference than just &quot;animal&quot; versus &quot;human&quot; -- Elementals are practically defined by how they can't relate to normal people, or even sentient things anymore, while Beasts are defined by how much more alive they are than the people around them.

                                The real problem, mechanically speaking, to me is that Beasts get a bonus to interacting with living creatures -- they get 8 again and a free specialty to interacting with animals, and can spend glamour to increase dice pools based around Presence and Composure. The only people who are better than Beasts at dealing with people are Fairest, and this is actually stated in the Beast write-up for Winter Masques.

                                Meanwhile, Elementals are actually worse at dealing with people than Wizened are. They don't get the ten again on rolls involving Manipulation, Empathy, Expression, Persuasion OR Socialize unless it's to with the elements. People are talking about how the proposal for Hunterheart Elementals are too impersonal, and not hunting enough, and that's the thing about Elementals. They ARE impersonal. A hunterheart would be the blizzard no one could run from, sneaker waves from the ocean, lightning that doesn't need to strike twice because the first strike left it's target dead. The scary thing about it is that when it's on your scent, you can't shake it and you can't beat it, you just have to hope it get what it wants and you manage to survive.

                                Leave the &quot;personal&quot; to the beasts -- Elementals just get the job done.

                                EDIT: Woops~ I knew I should have finished reading through the thread before posting. Got too excited, but I'll probably edit further once I finish reading AA's post! ^w^;;

                                Double Edit: Even after reading that, I still don't think I like conflating Beast and Elemental, but it does make a lot of sense. . . . I might have more to say later, though~
                                Right. The difference is between a Wolf as a sexual predator metaphor with his animal magnetism, say, or the utter implacability of an elemental with the same seeming.

                                    After reading all the 15 pages I somehow still can't see merging Elemental and Beast into Primal as a good option. Seems to have more cons than pros tbh.

                                    I would probably integrate Beasts into Ogres, and maybe even the other way, as the "Beasts" is a more generic name. They're both focused on physical attributes, both have animal/monster characteristics. It's like the only difference is that the beasts are "sexy monsters" and the ogres are "violent monsters" and that the former are focused on real animals and latter on fantasy creatures. With the main focus kept on kiths anyway I see no harm in putting them into larger category containing all wild, dangerous, physical and animalistic things, both fantasy and mundane.

                                    Then for more clarity I would split Elementals into Nature Forces and Inanimate constructs. Former are primal forces, elements, flora, weather, energy. Latter are objects, constructs, machines and items. They're too different to work together.

                                    I can't make any more clear thematic distinction between those three.
