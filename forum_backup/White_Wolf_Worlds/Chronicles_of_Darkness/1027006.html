      Beasts have access to a power called Wings of the Raptor which lets them glide, but it also details rules for making dive-bomb attacks. On page 132, it says that it's a Strength + Brawl - Defense. The weapon bonus is 1 bashing per 10 feet dove beforehand, and if the attacker has a means of dealing lethal damage, like claws or a knife, it's lethal instead.

                      I once wrote this thing that might be useful. Based on 1E but if you like it then feel free to adapt accordingly. Wall incoming cuz I don't know how to spoilers.

                      =General=
                      Combat & Movement:
                      It's hard to maintain combat in the air, mainly because for one thing few people are capable of doing such, and for another; aerial combat is very reliant on staying on the move whether you want to or not. To this end, there're two types of aerial combat: Flying and Free Falling (see below).
                      Just as in regular combat, the Flyer uses either Brawl or Weaponry— as appropriate —but aerial combat levies an additional restriction. If the Flyer's Brawl or Weaponry (depending on which is being used) is lower than his Dexterity, the score for Dexterity + Athletics is used instead with the Untrained Penalty for Brawl/Weaponry applied, even if the Flyer has dots in the Skill. If the Flyer has no dots in Athletics, the Untrained Penalty is doubled. Although this often makes it more difficult for the Flyer to hit an opponent, the sheer force of the blows that do land more than compensates for this lack of accuracy.

                      Movement
                      In flight, a being is capable of using their full Initiative, Defense and Dodge and are free to move amongst the physical plane as they see fit. Beings with wings (such as the Fallen) gain a base Speed template of 8 which is further boosted by their Strength and Dexterity as normal. Their drawback, however, is the hard time positioning themselves when not otherwise on the move, and as such they require a full turn to reposition themselves once they've stopped moving for any reason. Repositioning is counted as a reflexive action; though no aerial maneuvers such as Gaining Momentum can be used during that time. Winged Beings need a Speed rating equal to half their Size rounded up to remain flying or they start gliding (which follows a variation of Free-Fall Combat rules, see below) or falling instead.

                      Beings flying without wings, such as via mounts and vehicles or through magic/psychic power are not under the weight of these rules (exception towards magic aimed at growing regular wings; those are counted as 'winged beings'). Their base template is counted as 5 by default and may change either for the better or for the worse depending on the means by which they fly (for example: a Mage using Forces to fly would rely on Forces + Gnosis to determine their Base Speed Template). Under most normal circumstances, they can still apply their full Initiative, Defense and Dodge as normal.
                      All flying beings worth their salt are capable of using one aerial movement maneuver:

                      Gaining Momentum
                      For every turn the Flyer spends simply flying and gaining as much speed as possible, she can increase her Speed by +1. When the Flyer stops using the maneuver, any excess Speed above the normal rating drops by 1 until it's back at its normal rating. Any actions taken besides Gaining Momentum will decrease it by an additional amount equal to the Speed Decline of that move.

                      For example: Flyer #1 is a winged being with a general Speed rating of 12 when flying (Strength 2 + Dexterity 2 + 8 Basic Speed Template) and a Size rating of 5 (meaning it needs at least Speed 3 to keep flying. Having made several attacks over the course of several turns, Flyer #1 has suffered some major Speed setbacks and paid for their attacks with Speed Declines that hampers Flyer #1's momentum in the air when it strikes at its foes.
                      Flyer #1's current rating is 7 Speed. As to not lose too much Speed and momentum in the air, it decides to spend the next two turns simply flying in the air and flap its wings to gain some momentum going, using the Gaining Momentum maneuver and gaining +2 Speed for the two turns it spends on the maneuver before it feels content enough to begin attacking again.
                      If we alter the number and say Flyer #1 had its full Speed Rating of 12 and still spent two turns to raise it to 14 with the Gaining Momentum maneuver, the rating will decline to 13 the turn after it stops using the maneuver and then down to 12 where it will remain (as Flyer #1's natural Speed rating from the above dicepool) until spent by making combat maneuvers in the air.

                      Action: Instant
                      Roll: None
                      Speed Increase: +1 per turn

                      Flying Combat
                      Flying combat is only available to animals, or supernaturals capable of flight, needless to say, but can be learned over time if a creature learns how to fly and has enough time to practice. There are three basic combat maneuvers available for all beings who can fly and fight in the air: Swoop, Tackle and Grapple.

                      Swoop
                      Diving out of the air, the Flyer swoops past its opponent, slashing with a weapon or simply striking with a fist. The point is simply to add the force of the dive to the blow and then to climb for another strike.

                      Action: Instant
                      Roll: Dexterity + Brawl/Weaponry - Defense
                      Damage: Base Speed Template + Weapon Damage
                      Speed Decline: -1

                      Tackle
                      Unlike other maneuvers, this one is based on Speed alone, as the Flyer is basically just aiming the path of its flight to intersect the opponent's path with his body. If used against a mounted opponent, the target must roll a Dexterity + Drive - Flyer's Successes and obtaining equal or greater successes than the Flyer did in hitting to stay on the mount.

                      Action: Instant
                      Roll: Speed - Defense
                      Damage: Speed in Bashing Damage
                      Speed Decline: -3

                      Grapple
                      Everyone has seen some form of "grab and drop" maneuver in media being used by beings of flight. While a viable tactic, this maneuver can be used just as simply as an offensive tactic as it can be used to carry someone out of harms way. Or stealing their sandwich while they're on the phone. You bastard. Either way, this maneuver is counted as a grapple action for all intents and purposes and is done mid-flight, as a result the target will take 1/4th the Flyer's Speed in Bashing if they aren't careful enough to reduce their incoming. If they simply grab something from the target, the object will not take damage, nor will the target take damage if grappled in the air (that's what Tackle is for).

                      Action: Instant
                      Roll: Dexterity + Brawl - Defense
                      Damage: 1/4th Speed in Bashing Damage
                      Speed Decline: -1

                      Free Falling Combat
                      During Free Falling combat, for all intents and purposes, speed is not effective during combat on the horizontal plane, but it still applies in falling downwards. All Initiative rolls by the free-fallers are halved. While free-falling, a human cannot use their Defense nor can they attempt to Dodge. A human may "run" from an encounter by making themselves falling faster to earth in what some call "the hail mary" position, though this automatically increases the damage they will take when hitting the ground by one.

                      Falling:
                      While falling from the air, you have a certain velocity that you can travel before it becomes terminal velocity. You can fall up to half your Size rounded up in feet safely, after which terminal velocity applies and you take +1 Bashing Damage when you hit the ground per half feet you fall. If your speed increases to twice your Size rating in the air, the amount of Bashing increased is +2 rather than +1.

                      Altitudes & Winds:
                      Wind and coldness at high altitudes can grant Penalties to actions and make it hard to grip on anything not attached to the person. Like in water tilts or diving, the wind can carry a person away from another, but it usually takes extremely strong winds in order to do this. Flying against the wind brings a -1 Penalty to Speed and any maneuvers attempted in the air while flying with the wind grants a +1 Bonus to Speed, though not necessairly to any maneuvers.

                      Gliding:
                      When gliding the Flyer nolonger needs to worry about their Speed rating nor do they accumulate Bashing Damage as they move closer towards the ground. However they cannot control their direction much nor use aerial maneuvers. To start flying again whilst Gliding, the Flyer must pass an extended Dexterity + Athletics roll or whatever variation is the most fitting for their type of flying with a goal of 5 successes. Failure makes them lose altitude while Dramatic Failure makes them fall. Maybe hit something for good measure.



                      In addition to this, I also made a Style Merit for my homebrewed version of DtF that might or might not be worth your player's time. Simply adjust it away from the Fallen and according to your liking and gameline:

                      Combat Style: Aerial Combat (• to •••••)
                      Book:
                      --

                      Prerequisite:
                      Apocalyptic Form Trait: Wings

                      Effect:
                      An Angel's wings is one of their more common and noticable traits, and the Fallen don't tend to be an exception to this rule. Flying into combat was the chief means of warfare of the Fallen against the Heavenly Host during the War in Heaven and though much time has passed, a lot of them still rely on aerial manuvers to combat their foes. Angelic or otherwise.
                      Just as in regular combat, the Fallen uses either Brawl or Weaponry— as appropriate —but aerial combat levies an additional restriction. If the Fallen's Brawl or Weaponry (depending on which is being used) is lower than his Dexterity, the score for Dexterity + Athletics is used instead with the Untrained Penalty for Brawl/Weaponry applied, even if the Fallen has dots in the Skill. If the Fallen has no dots in Athletics, the Untrained Penalty is doubled. Although this often makes it more difficult for the Fallen to hit an opponent, the sheer force of the blows that do land more than compensates for this lack of accuracy. See the Aerial Combat Rules document for more details.

                      Flying Affinity (•):
                      The Fallen are creatures to whom flight is nature to their being; they're as adapted to it as humans are to walk. The Fallen whom have not been stuck in the Abyss or neglected their Angelic nature and hidden within their Hosts for too long have an easy time moving about in the air and accounting for wind and altitude. Relocating or moving against the wind nolonger Penalizes their dicepools. They can also aim their Grapple attempts as to avoid damaging the target, thus inflicting no damage on the person they pick up even if that person is on the ground.

                      Drop Dodge (••):
                      This maneuver involves voluntairly dropping mid-flight and using the fall momentum to their advantage and pick up speed. The turn they engage in this maneuver, it's counted as a Dodge action for purposes of counting Defense. For every turn that the Fallen uses Drop Dodge they gain twice the Speed that Gaining Momentum would normally bring them until they start flying again. If they start fighting while dropping, the rules for Free Fall Combat applies.
                      This maneuver can be used while relocating, which cancels the relocation.

                      Action: Instant
                      Speed Gain: +2 per turn

                      Drawback:
                      Needless to say, the Fallen can only drop for so long before the ground approaches; and when they want to start flying again they must succeed a Dexterity + Athletics roll to do so. One should be very careful if they plan on flying in the last moment; needless to say if the Fallen actually does hit the ground, the rules for Falling applies. Additionally, the drawbacks of Free Fall apply as well (see Aerial Combat Rules).

                      Flying Thrust (•••):
                      This is a special maneuver, used only when the Fallen has a long weapon, such as a spear or a longsword, in their hands. The point is to impale the target with the weapon and leave it imbedded within, severely hampering the opponent's ability to continue fighting. If the Fallen succeeds, the impaling weapon is driven through the target's body. If the target does not die, he takes an automatic -2 Penalty to all Dice Pools (in addition to wound penalties) until the weapon is broken off. Removing the shaft instead of breaking it off automatically causes another Lethal Damage to the target.

                      Action: Instant
                      Roll: Dexterity + Weaponry - Defense
                      Damage: Weapon Damage + Speed in Lethal Damage
                      Speed Decline: -2

                      Barrel Roll (••••):
                      Do one! I mean, by swiftly counting the distance and speed of their target, the Fallen can swiftly use their wings to flap themselves out of harm's way in just the nick of time. During the turn they do this maneuver, counted as a Dodge action, their Defense is trippled for that turn only. The Fallen can only do this maneuver while their effective Speed rating is at least 6.

                      Action: Instant
                      Roll: Dexterity + Athletics
                      Speed Decline: -3

                      Seismic Toss (•••••):
                      With this maneuver, a Fallen can grapple a target, and then begin flying full speed towards the ground under the same rules as Gaining Momentum. The Fallen doesn't cause damage per say, but they may add their Speed to the target's speed for calculating damage of terminal velocity, potentially inflicting huge amounts of damage onto the target while they themselves take off from a safe distance enjoying the speed gained.
                      If a target capable of flight is grabbed and tossed, they can contest the Fallen's roll to regain flight before they hit the ground. Grappled targets capable of flight may not contest the Fallen's roll while they are grabbed. If the Fallen holds onto the target all the way until they hit the ground, they may add their Strength rating into the dice pool, but must also account for their own terminal velocity unless they possess the "Immune to Bashing Damage" Apocalyptic Trait.

                      Action: Extended
                      Roll: Dexterity + Athletics (+ Strength; optional) - Defense
                      Speed Gain: +2 per turn
