                        I feel like using the words sick or ill to dance around the issue of other words like "mad" or "crazy" is going against the spirit of the idea, if not the letter. I don't have an answer for the question of how to do it right, but I think the root of the problem has a lot to do with intention as well as the specifics of the language, and using coded language for it (much as we see in other domains) may ostensibly solve the short term problem but fails to address the long term one.

                        This isn't to say to just keep using those words, I just mean it's probably good to meditate on whether the problem is for one to call a group of people "crazy" or if the negative implication of divergence in the use of language like sick or ill renders their usage not much better.

                            The protagonist factions would wouldn't consider a cure a bad thing because it removes "special-ness", and are unified in their belief that a Genius should not think of themselves as having "special-ness" because that leads to hubris and tragedy.

                            However the protagnoist factions think that a cure is impossible and thus they aren't looking for one. Though individual Geniuses in the Peerage are bound to try anyway.

                            Originally posted by TidyGamer View Post
                            And don't mortals observing mad science in action run the risk of going insane themselves or becoming igors who risk death if they don't attach themselves to a mad scientist?
                            The risk is actually small.

                            Firstly you need to spend Mania equal to their Willpower, so simply demonstrating your cool jetpack won't do it. It takes either a rather large demonstration of mad science or prolonged exposure to mad science. One of the suggested modifiers for the roll to create a Beholden is "the subject has gone on some "grand adventure" with the genius". That's an entire "grand adventure" before you even roll. It's not as simple as taking one look at the robot and becoming Beholden.

                            Secondly a genius needs an Exceptional Success to turn an unwilling mortal into an Igor, and it's a tough dicepool to increase (Inspiration + Presence). On a regular Success, if the mortal doesn't want to be Beholden they just spend a point of Willpower to resist it. (As for why a person might want to be an Igor, just watch Doctor Who. With the exception of Rory Williams, River Song, and possibly Donna Noble all the Doctor's companions are Beholden)

                            The Peerage encourages Geniuses to play it safe, because, well why would you take the risk? Telling your family is fine and can even encouraged because they'll keep you grounded. Letting them into the lab is discouraged.

                            That said it is entirely possible for Geniuses and mortals to work together, for example it's mentioned that large Skafoi wonders often have specially built compartments for mortals to stay in.

                                    On nihilism and the failure to be anything

                                    You have a very strict definition of how to do nihilism (a bit ironic isn't it?). “you have to establish the illusion for people to believe in first”, then you tear down that illusion.

                                    You also say Genius doesn't ask any questions. Both statements are wrong.

                                    In Genius nihilism is the question, not the answer. “You live in a vast meaningless cosmos, what are you going to do about it?”. Genius is so up-front about it's nihilism because it's a starting point, not an ending.

                                    So if you'll forgive me for being blunt. Your section "But Arc, you don't get it-that's the point!" didn't give me the impression you understood what the point was. The point wasn't “the world is nihilistic”, it's “what are you going to do about it”?


                                    On science fetishism

                                    There's two questions I want to answer here.

                                    1) Why aren't Geniuses scientists?
                                    2) Why do Geniuses act like scientists.

                                    If you de-construct the mad scientist as an idea it's not actually about science in the first place. The mad scientist trope represents the fear of new ideas that challenge the established order. There's nothing that says those ideas need to be scientific or technological in nature. Jesus Christ challenging the religious orthodoxy at the time provokes the same fears as modern artificial intelligences who challenge our ideas about what it means to be human.

                                    Moochava recognises that and allows you to have mad theologians, mad alchemists, mad playwrights, etc. A Genius can practice any field of study where scary new ideas are born. It doesn't have to be scientific. Heck Doctor Frankenstein himself was an alchemist, not a scientist.


                                    Secondly, why do they act like scientists. Simple: Because nihilism is the question. Geniuses live in a vast uncaring cosmos and, being human, they hate that. Thus they're drawn to and arise from fields that promise the power to explain and pronounce meaning on the cosmos. In modern times science is the hip new thing, but go back in history and Geniuses use predominantly occult or theological aesthetics.

                                    The self-aware Geniuses know it's just a comforting lie, the doomed believe they are actually practising the fields they claim to, and the best Geniuses rise above the need for comforting lies and make their peace with a nihilistic universe.


                                    The in character astehtics of the Inspired community is one of my favourite things. The history of the Inspired follows the history of the mad scientist character archetype. Just as the modern mad scientist is descended from the mad alchemist character archetype through Dr Frankenstein, modern mad scientist Geniuses are descended from mad alchemists, occultists, theologians, etc like the pre-ARPANET Scholastics, Oracles, Numinologists, and Dharmists.


                                    On Failure

                                    Why do anything if you know you're going to fail? Why do failures matter if the stakes are so low?

                                    Why, in real life, do I bother doing anything since I know I will eventually die and my Self will cease to exist and nothing that happens in my life will matter to me beyond that point. Why do I care about failure since death is inevitable and my failures will no longer matter to me after I'm gone?

                                    Why do anything? Why care about anything? Why not just lie down and wait for death?

                                    The complaints you have about Genius' views on failure are what make it so relevant and interesting to me. Answering the question of why do anything is the whole theme of Genius, it's also basically the biggest question I have about in my life.

                                    Also look at Rick and Morty

                                    Rick destroys the entire Earth and buggers of to an alternative dimension with no consequences for himself (and two episodes later Morty is recovered and giving a great speech about accepting nihilism to Summer).

                                    Despite that Rick is a deep, interesting and relatable character. What gives him stakes is not the potential consequences for his failure, but our hope for his development along the way.




                                    And that's as far as I got. I'll read the rest later when I feel like it.

                                    But seriously, if you want to grock Genius you've got to grock the idea of nihilism as a starting point or question rather than nihilism as an answer.
