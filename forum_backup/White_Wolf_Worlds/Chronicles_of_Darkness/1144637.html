    Proposed Fix for 2E Toxicity

    09-28-2017, 01:30 AM
    I tried to make a post about this a while back, but I was a condescending douche and nobody understood what I was saying. I'll try to be more respectful and coherent this time.

    I don't like the 2E update to the Toxicity rules. As a quick recap: in 1E resisting poison was Stam + Res - Tox and success was winner-take all; in 2E, the roll is the same but successes reduce damage by 1 each from damage = Toxicity. I understand this was partly due to a desire to make Resisted rolls uniformly "successes matter" rather than "success matters." Further complicating matters are Armory and Hurt Locker ailments, which use their own variations of the 1E system despite being published in different editions.

    2E's update is a pretty extreme increase in lethality. That's mostly because each extra point of Toxicity requires 4 dice worth of expected successes to counteract (1 for the penalty, plus 3 more to reduce 1 avg damage). Basically: the 2E rules make mid-range poisons deadly to average-stat charactes and top-range poisons completely unstoppable. They also make higher dice pools and resistance bonuses pretty useless.

    Originally posted by TL;DR Math
    I did the napkin math using a dice probability table for Resist Poison dice pools of 2-13 (up to max for unaugmented human) and Toxicity 1-8, finding that 2E increased average damage by 50% (average DPT 2.37 vs 3.56). At 13 Resist dice, 2E poisons are almost 5x as deadly as 1E poisons. At 9 resist dice (basically 2x 3 stats + willpower), 2E poisons are almost twice as deadly.as 1E poisons. To explain what I mean by "higher dice pools are useless", 2E poisons do average 4.375 DPT vs a dice pool of 2 and 2.42 DPT vs a dice pool of 13 = the best possible human spending Willpower resists only 80% more poison than the worst possible human spending nothing. For a difference worth 32 xp.

    For a practical example, the book lists a typical alcohol poisoning with beer or wine as a Toxicity 3 event with 8 iterations. Let's say you have a typical character with 2 in all stats, that means they have a dice pool of 4 and can use +3 Willpower 4 times. 1E rules have that char taking 0.7203 * 4 = 2.88 damage over the first 4 turns with Willpower and 2.1 * 4 = 8.4 more over the next 4. If that's 11 bashing, then that player will be REALLY hurting for the rest of the week if they're lucky or comatose if they're not. If that's 11 lethal, then they still probably won't die but they'll most probably be comatose and REALLY should be hospitalized.

    The same example with 2E rules? 17.52 damage. If that's bashing, you're over halfway through aggravated. If Lethal, you're full aggravated in 7 hours. Sure, drinking is bad for your health, but that's really freakin' extreme. An average American male weighing 195.7 lbs binge drinking 2.5 standard drinks per hour for 8 hours is at about .338 BAC, which is nearly lethal but survivable in most cases.

    I really don't want to get bogged down in arguments about the merits of binge drinking, so please don't get hung up on that. It's just that it's the *only* clear example that the core rules give other than heroin overdose (for those curious: Toxicity 7 for 8 - Stamina hours = 1E 37.8 damage vs 2E 41.4).
    Here's my argument: if 1E's resisted winner-takes-all rules make outcomes too lopsided and 2E's resisted damage-subtract rules are too punishing... how about you keep damage-subtract but take away the penalty to Resolve + Stamina? Stated more clearly: Resolve + Stamina vs automatic Toxicity damage, successes subtract. So if you roll Stamina 3 + Resolve 3 and get 2 successes, then you subtract 2 from whatever the Toxicity is for the actual damage.

    The result flattens out the damage curve and eliminates "I rolled a 10 on my chance die so take no damage from VX gas!" while increasing average damage by a modest 5% over 1E's rules. If you care about the binge drinking example, average damage is 10.72 and it's 30.02 for heroin. All the math is on my Google Drive if anyone wants to check it.

    What do you think? Has anyone had much use for Toxicity rules for characters and NPC's? Stories of success and failure? Any workarounds anyone else has tried? Reasons to love the 2e system? References to fixes or homebrews better than mine?

          It's not a bad alternate, but in my experience the 2e RAW don't match with your points. As well, your math seems off, that the linked spread sheet has the expected successes missing 10-agains, which is certainly going to devalue higher dice-pools (also the highest dice-pool without supernatural powers is 16, Stamina 5, Resolve 5, Hardy 3, Willpower).

          The whole "bigger dice pools are useless" just flatly isn't mathematically true. Someone with 10 base dice against a Toxicity of 3 eight times is clearly mathematically better off than someone with 4 base dice. 7 dice 8 times averages 18.67 successes, leaving just 5.33 left over (I'm aware I'm simplifying a bit here as it's probably that 7 dice will over roll and 'waste' some of those average successes). That's not enough to put anything capable of throwing 10 base dice into anything other than the need to take it easy for a bit.1 die 8 times averages 2.67 successes, leaving 21.33 left which... is going to kill anything with just 4 dice as a base.

          Of course the decrease in effectiveness of higher dice pools is more drastic the higher you go compared to 1e, but that's just baked into moving from an all or noting system to a per success system. That doesn't mean higher dice pools are "worse" or "useless" in the 2e rules.

          The whole "average DPT" part seems completely obfuscating the math instead of illuminating it.

          If I have a base of 10 dice:
          Toxicity 1: Average result 3 successes, zero damage.
          Toxicity 2: Average result 2.67 successes, zero damage.
          Toxicity 3. Average result 2.33 success, 0.67 damage.
          Toxicity 4. Average result 2 successes, 2 damage.
          Toxicity 5. Average result 1.67 successes, 3.33 damage
          Toxicity 6. Average result 1.33 successes, 4.67 damage
          Toxicity 7: Average result 1 success, 6 damage
          Toxicity 8: Average result 0.67 success, 7.33 damage

          Just saying that the average is 3 is skipping a lot of information. It's not even being compared to the average damage without reduction (4.5). Hell, you can't even call that an average, because it presumes you'll face each toxicity level at the same rate which just isn't a viable assumption in the game. If you face Toxicity 3 to 5 95% of the time (I rarely see less because it's usually not enough to really matter, and I haven't seem more come up much outside of PCs trying to max it out via supernatural powers), the reduction at higher dice pools vs low dice pools is pretty significant. Your analysis is so focused on how much harsher the new rules are compared to the old ones, that it never seems to get into how deadly the new ones are in general. This also generally ignores any medical attention. The RAW make it so that you either need to be a tough SOB or really lucky to survive ODing or severe chemical exposures without medical attention... which makes sense. If you can walk away from most things without even needing first aid, Medicine and healing powers aren't really much use.

          I also really don't like that idea of making Toxicity 8 less deadly than 1e; even as an artifact of the math.

                  Sorry that the sheet's a bit of a mess: the blue table was my first attempt and I screwed up 10agains, which is why I added an accurate probability table on the 2nd tab. It only makes high Tox poisons slightly less deadly for mid-range characters because they can shave a point or two off of damage instead of just rolling chance dies (which I think is more fun anyway).

                              Taking a completely different tack on poison rules, here's a variant I've been tinkering with: All poisons deal 1 damage per round (assuming combat or similarly high stress situations, use longer intervals where appropriate) You roll stamina (+hardy if you have it, do not add resolve) as an extended action, making one roll per round. Success(es) on the roll downgrades the damage from lethal to bashing for that turn and adds to your total. Toxicity is the target number of successes, when met you stop taking damage from the poison.

                              It's definitely more forgiving than the current system, I'm still looking at tweaks to keep poison dangerous but not have it be the death sentence it is under the current RAW
