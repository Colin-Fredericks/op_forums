        All this concern about self created deviants seems a little much.

        For one thing, deviants that can create more of their kind is quite possibly something that will already be addressed in the book, infectious superpowers is hardly an unknown trope after all.

        Secondly, deviant scientists are hardly going to be able to make a small army of minions/operatives/meatshields without ST permission. Creating deviants probably takes more work than going down to the local radio shack or wal-mart for equipment, so creating an army of deviants is a bit much for someone running/hiding from a conspiracy. Even if a deviant has access to such resources, using it might set up red flags that make it easier for the conspiracy to find them.

        And of course, that assumes that the scientist who is now radioactive or a 6 foot tall insect thinks making more of himself is a good idea.

        So if a deviant starts a singularity, it's because the ST thought that would make for a fun game, in which case, no skin of off anyone elses nose.

                My main concern is that if the deviant created himself then part of the tension evaporates. One of the main horror factors of the deviant is waking up in a body that it's no longer completely human, and having no idea how or why it happened. A deviant who created himself does know, even if it was an accident or something unexpected happened, at least he understands some of it. That takes away some of the fear, and allows him to get a better hold of his new condition. However, like some people said, he could get amnesia, or maybe the accident or results were so completely unexpected that he truly doesn't know what happened to him.
