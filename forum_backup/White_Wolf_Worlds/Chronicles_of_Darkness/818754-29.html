                << You make them want it after the surgery is done. So they will spend a willpower to keep it. >>

                Under the current rules as written, it's not possible to control the thoughts or feelings of a Dominated victim. I'm not even sure it's possible to directly order someone to spend willpower in that manner since is an out-of-character mechanic.

                "When changing memories or creating new ones, the vampire can create the factual content of the memory, but the victim’s thoughts and feelings remain out of her control. She can create an environment that makes him predisposed to certain reactions, but she can’t force him to love a son she’s just invented." (Blood and Smoke p. 132)



                Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                In 2E I find nothing about suicide being beyond the powers Dominate.
                I never said suicide is beyond the purview of Dominate. It's most certainly possible to get someone to commit suicide using Dominate. If the command is disguised as something innocuous or irreversible and the act does enough aggravated damage in a single turn. It might even be possible with Possession or Conditioning (I wouldn't try it with the latter though because of the inherent ability for its victims to randomly ignore commands.) But 1. It's kind of hard to issue elaborate instructions when you are limited to four word sentences. 2. It's also difficult to do enough aggravated damage to a vampire in a single turn to kill them outright since they only take bashing damage from the vast majority of conventional weapons.

                What I said is that nothing in the entire description of Dominate is going to make a victim want to destroy themself without a devotion. Such manipulation of a person's feelings, memories, and self-preservation instinct is utterly beyond Dominate's ability even at level 5. The book says as much.


                << Even Mesmerize should be able to do it since the condition only resolves after the action has been performed. >>

                Nope. Mesmerize is resolved if the victim takes a single point of Bashing or Lethal damage or if they suffer a breaking point fulfilling the vampire's command. It also wears off naturally after a scene. You can't cut open someone's skull or rib cage without doing damage and Mesmerize goes away at the end of the scene.

                << My group has house ruled that it requires more advanced supernatural powers (I think Conditioning might be enough) to mind control someone into trying to destroy themselves. >>

                By default, you have to get around the highly restrictive word count, vitae expenditures, time limits, 3 different resist checks at minimum, and damage dealing limitations in a single turn. (Or you just use Majesty 3 and get around the damage dealing limitation) Conditioning gives the victim the opportunity to automatically ignore any Dominate command with willpower expenditure. So it might not be a good idea to use that on a suicide bomber who's last ditch act of freedom is to grab you while detonating himself on your instructions who at that point has nothing to lose gambling on a slim chance of survival in exchange for being utterly free of your control.

                                                    Well, using Dominate to gather secrets has a lot of restirctions to it. For one, Auspex 1 can be used on crowds of people, Auspex 3 is basically post-cognition, Auspex 4 can communicate non-verbally, Auspex 5 is sort-of like long range clairvoyance. All of these are things that are beyond Dominate's ability. In theory, Domiante can force someone to reveal all the same questions as Auspex, and even remove all memories of quesitons asked, but that requires there to be privacy to ask in the first place, meeting the eyes in the second place, and third place is succeeding on your roll against resistances - and that might include your victim falling into frenzy or riding the wave as a defense (I know some Wyrm Dragons that use this trick, ST approval pending). But Uncanny Perception has none of these restrictions - it has an equal chance of working on a powerful elder in frenzy surrounded by allies as it does on an isolated, weak-willed fledging or a mortal from a distance and no one ever being the wiser.

                                                    So, there's really no worry of overlap. There are clear advantages of using all levels of Auspex instead of relying on Dominate.
