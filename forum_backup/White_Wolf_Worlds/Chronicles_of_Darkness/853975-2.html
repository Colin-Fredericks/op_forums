                I really like the more detailed Miracle rules! Some comments:

                Manipulation miracles should use Manipulation/Dexterity/Finesse, IMO. Shaping something physically should call for Dexterity/Finesse, and shaping something socially or mentally should call for Manipulation/Finesse.

                Protection miracles should use Resolve/Composure/Resistance, to me.

                Transformation is pretty much the same as Manipulation, so they should be merged. Same with Animation and Creation.

                You might also want a Veiling-equivalent, unless you intend it to fall under Protection.

                Otherwise, looks pretty good.

                    I'd would like to explore how Demigods interact with some of the other fan-created groups, particularly Irin, Leviathans, and the Demons from Demon: the Return, each which overlaps in an interesting way.

                    Irin are based on the stories of angels, but one group themselves to be gods; Demigods are based on polytheistic accounts, but some may percieve themselves as angels - could their be some overlap or interact between the subgroups which adhere to a particular theology? Might they actually be the same kind of being on a some fundamental, but for some reason some become associated with the Divine Fire, while others grow associated with the Empyrean, the expression of human virtue and the opposite of the Inferno? If so, it might be possible for each to have a Z-type which represents evolution toward the other kind of being.

                    In particular, this group would be nice to combine with Leviathan, representing the type of supernatural (or one of the types) that empowers the Empyrean Theurgists. Halysis could be changed to distinguish it more from the Wake of Leviathans, for those who (like me) would like to play up the contrast between the two types of divinity. To contrast with the brainwashed cults created by the latter effect, I would reverse the effect of Halysis in part: instead of causing mortals to lose their own opinions and adopt those of the Demigod, mortals caught up in Halysis become more filled with their own ideals and virtues, but attribute these to the Demigod (As in this study from the Ontario Consultants for Religious Tolerance: attempts to assess divine will almost invariably confirmed the worshipper's own beliefs). Naturally, this will make creating a stable cult very difficult, since the tendency will be for any such organization to fracture into warring groups (each using the same Demigod as a figurehead) based on the true beliefs of the members. The Disciple merit would represent the humility to look past one's own beliefs and towards what the Demigod is actually saying. For mortals that are effected, it might be interesting to encourage a more frenzied pursuit of preexisting virtues mechanically. Maybe they can now indulge virtues more frequently for the full willpower benefit, but automatically lose a few points of Willpower each day, which becomes free-floating faith either consumed by the Demigod or influencing the environment/other people in strange ways.

                    The Erinyes sound as if they might be the Demigods' interpretation of the new Paragon fangroup that is being developed on this same forum. It isn't automatically clear the themes overlap quite yet, but both have a CoD take on mythology, which could be the basis for interesting crossover as well.

                    Not the daimon, just a namesake.

Hm, maybe they're actually a little better at understanding the Drasil than demigods are, part of them having been born there?

                                EDIT: Actually, thought of a common role in myth: Guardians and enforcers (the demigods provide authority, the [lesser template] adjudicates it). This fits with divine monsters being living punishments for breaking moral law and protecting that which the divine rules over. In practical terms, the [lesser template] deals with things the demigod doesn't have the resources or interest in managing-and more importantly, they don't radiate Halyasis, allowing people under the Empyrean's gaze to remain people.
