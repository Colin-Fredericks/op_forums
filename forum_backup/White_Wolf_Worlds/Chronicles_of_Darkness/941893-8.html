    I haven't seen anything to suggest what level of sensation an ephemeral being has when manifesting Possession or Claim. I had to read through half the book to find a line buried in the text to suggest geists feel human and desire it. Assuming this is not an embellishment by a single writer it is used to explain why Sin-Eaters don't fall unconscious from wounds not given as a reason for wanting to enter a Bargain. The reason repeatedly given is geists want to affect the mortal world and desire the most powerful vessel to do so. They give up control for power. The geist can't force the bound to act according to its will but a player's geist shouldn't be crazy enough to not have the Bargain include working the geist's goals.

    Mechanically, it would seem a Sin-Eater's geist would be represented by Aspirations. When a player chooses to defy a geist aspiration the character suffers a breaking point. Whether these breaking points are listed in the book as standard or optional and a ST decides to remove them or the breaking points are absent and added by a ST, it seems to me the game is the same either way. The game differs in a minor, although perhaps significant, way on a table by table basis which literally is every game that comes out. Nobody makes games that everybody thinks are perfect out of the box.
