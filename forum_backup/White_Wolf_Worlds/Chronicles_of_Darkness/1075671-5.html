                Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post


                That's how MY version of magic works; which is the point I have been trying to clarify from the start of this discussion.

                In MY version of magic; it can not be quantified or studied because the variables are ever changing. Magic functions in the manner that is presented because the mage using it wants the magic to function in that fashion. It has nothing to do with scientific reasoning or physics. It's concentrated desire forced upon the world. Anybody with a comparably strong willpower or opposing desire could effectively cancel out their magic. Not because of the nature of opposing forces, but simply because they wanted their desired outcome more strongly than their opponent.
                Quick rant as relates to my tastes and understandings:

                The rest of what you describe is not in accordance to the bolded, nor should it, because it describes magic as a non-repeatable affair, neither a thing you can replicate or anticipate and therefore not a thing you can take advantage of-magic just happens when it will. Now that's cool if magic is a setting thing rather than a character thing, and in fact I'd be down for a game on that premise-but if you're in a game where you can use magic, a character has to at least figure out how it should be able to be invoked in even your most insane of space-time-circumstances confluences- magic might only be a thing you are able to invoke on the 66th half moon of the this current cycle of the world at the crossroads of three empires when surrounded by the blood of ewes this one time, but a character has to be able to figure that out if it's going to mean jack-and/or-shit at all, which supposes a discernable system by which will is manifested into the miraculous action. Discernable phenomenon can be cycled down into the development of understanding and precision knowledge of hows and whys, albeit as it goes on, and thus goes on from just "weird shit happens" to science and technology. If you can take advantage of it, there is a science and technological place for it.

                Let me put it another way-the only game I've seen come close to having their special shit act the way you describe it is, ironically, Genius the Trangression, and that's....not good.
