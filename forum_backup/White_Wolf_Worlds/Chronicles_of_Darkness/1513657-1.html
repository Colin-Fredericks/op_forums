            Originally posted by xela_nut View Post
            1. There is no kinship. Dark Masters don't have an innate connection to the other creatures in the Chronicles of Darkness world that makes them more likely to like them.
            This is one of the more oover hyped things about Beasts. They get a one positive shift on making a first impression for social maneuvering. So, when a Beast first meets another supernatural, they can smooth over initial hostility, or get a fairer shake to an honest good faith offer. Everything else from there has to come from longer term relationship building (or failing to do so). Beasts can easily squander that first impression. They can just outright negate it by not immediately leaning into soft leverage (even if it's just RP instead of rolling).

            2. Beasts seem to be under the impression that they're the good guys, or at least that their actions are excusable because they teach lessons.
            Beasts don't think they're good guys, they think they were forced to be the bad guys. Some are fine being the bad guys, some aren't, some try to excuse their actions, some don't. Lessons are explicitly something Beasts make up as an excuse; not something that has any real validity to it. They know they have no supernatural imperative to do this. They're preference for it, stems from Sages and Oracles (what preceded the ego-corrupted Heroes of modern nights) using their existence to teach lessons.

            Basically, the big difference here is that humanity shaped the Beasts into what they are, because humans wanted moral narratives with good guys and bad guys, and the Beasts got shackled to being the bad guys by humanity instead of some more distant creator deity. Humans wanted greedy dragons sitting on piles of gold so heroes could conquer both a literal monster and a figurative moral ill. Humanity lost control over the narrative, so Beasts are left to either take control of the narrative for themselves, or at least reinterpret it for their own benefit, because the creators that abandoned the purpose they shaped Beasts for are constantly right in front of them. That conflict is always immediately in their face.

            1. I don't mind that the Lairs in Beast aren't in the physical world, but the Dark Master dungeons are in the physical world. This is, in essence, to put them in more constant danger. They have to worry about far more threats attacking them than the Beasts do.
            Ultimately though, the CofD are set in the modern times. What, exactly, stops Task Force Valkyrie from just using some drones to bomb a physical dungeon into rubble before they send in troops to finish it off? One of the reasons for Beasts (and not just Beasts, lots of other supernaturals can do this too) to create bases that aren't in the physical world is so that they still matter to modern conflicts. Even hunters with "mundane" tech can't simply be forced to go through a "dungeon" like you're trying to emulate if it exists in the physical world in a meaningful way. You'll either need to create a magical reason why enemies can't skip rooms to the point where "exists on Earth" and "exists in a pocket dimension," is mild flavor, or Dark Masters are going to have to be even more narrow in concept to be able to construct dungeons that work in a modern reality.

            3. Dark Masters do not create heroes or harm people with their mere existence. Instead, they have a very strong urge and incentive to act evil with it even making them more powerful.
            The same is true of Beasts.

            As for the Beasts, the True Fae respect and fear them and their goddess is fully on their side.
            The Gentry have no unified opinion of Beasts. The Dark Mother doesn't have their back in any fashion that really matters. She's not swooping in to help them out of bad situations. She's not sending them dreams of warning of imminent danger. They aren't her only children, even if they're the ones she offers the most direct power to. She expects them to care for themselves.

            6. Dark Masters have grand ambitions as a core part of their gameplay. I've seen gamelines (I think a fan gameline, Genius the Transgression, but I don't remember for sure) where a fourth aspiration is added in. What I did was change that Aspiration into their Virtue equivelant (their Megalomania) because it's something that can never be truly satisfied. Basically, Dark Masters persue their own personal goals and such.
            Beasts get Legend in the same place... Legend is more in context with the meta-narrative nature of Beast, but Beasts are driven to become monsters that are talked about for ages; whether they die or are still stalking humanity.

            A lot of general CofD fans are also not going to like going back to the "real world mental health conditions as character traits," thing. Derangements were dropped for a good reason. More narrative terms like Legend avoid such things.

            7. The Dark Masters aren't easily set into types. For player versatility and based on feedback I got for a previous fan gameline attempt, I didn't include an X-Splat, just a Y-Splat. Each Dark Master is hand-crafted by a god and can be fairly unique.
            Each Beast, Changeling, and Deviant is fairly unique, despite having multiple splats. X-splats don't stop high levels of customization in character design. Two splats actually helps make each character unique because each combo pushes characters in different directions on top of all the mechanical options. Splats can vary wildly in scope.

            9. The Dark Masters are fundamentally meant to be final bosses or commanders of dungeons. They are anything from Sephiroth to Sauron to Ainz to the Overlord from the Overlord games to the Dungeon Keeper. They are not meant to be the monsters of myth or nightmares.
            The problem is that the CofD is a setting about monsters of myth, both ancient and modern. Roles such as "final boss" or "commander of dungeon," are things that are drawn out of unique takes on classic concepts. Forsaken is a game about werewolves, that has a unique take on werewolves and human-spirit hybrids that are defined by their need to hunt, which expands out into the kinds of concepts the game encourages. Coming at it from the opposite direction feels like a problem. Trying to make a game about "spirit hunters" is going to be hard to pull off because even if you have some solid inspirations, getting the scope of what they do, how they do it, and why they do it to mesh is going to be much harder than starting from "how do you take this archetypal monster, and put a distinct spin on it."

            I like the idea of using merits. Maybe the Dungeon linking could be a merit where the Dark Masters have the option to make their dungeons right next to each other or to use a portal or some other long-ranged transport with benefits to both methods? I'll look at Beast to see if there are ideas for that, but I hope that the basic premise of a merit for that is sound.
            Beasts just... link their Lairs if they want to, that doesn't require a Merit, esp. because it's a bit of a mixed bag to charge people for. If a Beast doesn't defend their Lair well, and is linked to the rest of their Brood, the rest of the Brood now has to contend with invaders getting into their Lairs too.

                Since Satchel and Arms handled the misconceptions, I'm gonna bring it back around to the project with the second major thought I had when reading it.

                I think the concept as a splat is a little anemic, but one idea that might really work is to instead craft the concept as a sort of sub-template(s) that you can append to other splats, taking ques from Deviant's Forms, Imperial Mysteries' archmage template, and Contagion Chronicle's Vectors*. You can actually take a lot of your setting conceits for the Dark Masters and rework it into gameline agnostic sources of temptation and power, and even break up the overall package into particular packages to offer more "Final Boss" powers and tools to characters where such may not readily be apparent-Beasts, for instance, might not need dungeon mechanics, but the options for increased survivability might be appealing for them, where as a Promethean Dark Master has plenty of good reasons for wanting to connect his various Hovels and Safe Spaces together and have a "quick escape" clause at the ready for them. THis also makes it less work for you in terms of needing to justify a whole piece of setting and metaphysics, because this sort of idea fits the amount of "space" the idea of a Final Boss can occupy.

                *Getting specific in the explanation, the way that Forms change any Deviant that's more significant that Variations and Scars but less significant than Origin or Clade, the archmage template as a general "amplifies what you're already doing" sort of thing, and Vectors as being operable sets of powers that aren't reliant upon any one game's internal metaphysics.

                                Right, so this is a long one, and most of it was made before the idea of them being a sub-template was tossed around, but I personally like the idea of them as a full template better so I'm posting this anyway. Most of it was made as I thought of it, but I did try to organize it at least somewhat by topic.

                                I would recommend against requiring too many rules from books other than the corebook unless completely necessary, such as using Hunter the Vigil 2e for the minion creation system, as it means only people with that book can use this one properly.

                                On that note, I would also recommend a greater emphasis on minions and their use, as it would make goals such as the conquest of a city seem more viable if the minions seem important and useful, and would also be something that really helps set this game apart from the others in the Chronicles of Darkness. As such I would recommend a minion design system that is distinct from Hunter, as it would make them seem more unique to this game and a more important part of it.

                                I like that you have different levels of minions, but I would suggest something more akin to a Tier system, with a Tier 0 acting as Minions useful for logistics work such as building or keeping track of things or moving stuff around, but which are generally useless for combat and would get beaten by a typical mortal with little effort, a Tier 1 able to generally take on most mortals in a fight, Tier 2 being more akin to other supernatural creatures, and Tier 3 being powerful even by the standards of most supernaturals. Tier 0s would be easy to get and without much of a limit, Tier 1s would be plentiful and still fairly easily made, Tier 3s would be more difficult to make but with a decent number being possible, and Tier 3s would be difficult to make and very limited (mini-bosses, essentially). Minions would have powers that could be taken representing their abilities, with different powers being locked behind certain Tiers. The lower the Tier, the more loyal they are and the harder it is for them to disobey.

                                One idea I would recommend is for it to be possible for minions to, through sufficient effort, restore a destroyed Dark Orb, to touch on the trope of the evil overlord being resurrected by their servants after supposedly being killed by the heroes (naturally making way for a sequel in the process). As far as Dungeons would go, I would recommend both having Dungeon traits that represent things about rooms, similar to Beast, as well as Dungeon Merits, as others have suggested.

                                You've brought up the idea that all Dark Masters are created as unique entities by the Dark Gods, however I feel like simply not having an equivalent to Clan or Auspice makes them feel incomplete rather than unique, so I would recommend either A: adding in general archetypes based on how the Gods intended them to punish humanity, or B: go all in on the unique aspect, adding in traits that alter how they work (things like being immune to an element such as heat or cold or electricity, having a mind that can't be read or controlled, having the natural ability to enter Twilight, the ability to regenerate bashing damage each turn for free, etc), almost similar to the Deathbed Transformation abilities but weaker and which apply regardless of form, the idea being that they were simply designed that way.

                                I would personally choose option B, as it seems more interesting to me and seems like it would fit better with your original idea. If going with this idea, I would also move some of the weaker seeming Deathbed Transformation abilities to this new type of ability, such as breathing underwater, and either removing them from the Deathbed Transformation list or buffing those ones up to make them stronger (maybe instead of just breathing underwater, they gain the ability to do that and to swim fast and maneuver easily underwater, for example), as well as buffing some of the Deathbed Transformation effects in general. I would also suggest removing the ability for some Dark Masters to always be in their Deathbed Transformations in favor of using this system to represent less human ones instead, as that way the Deathbed Transformation still seems more special.

                                How powerful do you want individual Dark Masters to be, minions aside? Like, are we talking "can equal most other Templates" kind of powerful or "can take on a pack of Werewolves solo and stand a chance at winning" kind of powerful? To be frank some of the powers seem a bit weak even for that first option and I may have some suggestions for them, but it depends on how powerful you actually want them to be. I would personally recommend going wild with it. You've said that you wanted them to be final bosses, and the Chronicles of Darkness doesn't really concern itself much with balancing splats against each other anyway (if they did then Mages wouldn't be able to eradicate someone with 5 dots in an Arcanum while also being able to make the Spell not Withstood using an exceptional success), so there isn't really much of a reason to not make them really powerful, given the idea that they are supposed to be attacked often and also do things that invite attack often.

                                Another idea that could help with the final boss aesthetic is to have it so that at certain Dark Power ratings a Dark Master gains additional transformations, allowing for more powerful ones to have more than two "stages" to their battle, representing more dangerous bosses. Something that may help with the idea of making each Dark Master unique is to give Deathbed Transformations less specific powers; instead of "Extra Legs" which grant a bonus to speed, they can have "Enhanced Speed" which grants them additional Speed and then presents a number of suggested features that could provide that bonus, such as extra legs, the ability to simply run faster, a kind of dark engine that propels the Dark Master forward with a dark shadow trail left behind as they move, and so on. This could help a lot when designing unique forms.

                                Personally I would tone down the apparent fear of Mages in the stereotype sections, as it kind of goes against the idea of their arrogance and power, and it is also generally absent for Leviathans, many of whom would likely be more terrifying and powerful than the majority of Mages because they gain power earlier by just having access to Apotheosis and the high level powers that come with it. In particular, if a Dark Master truly had a nuke that they had stolen from the government, a Mage wouldn't be any more of a threat in the face of a nuke than a Leviathan. As well, given the idea that they are supposed to be final bosses, they probably should be able to take on Mages (as well as Leviathans, for that matter) as well, in addition to being confident that they can.

                                As far as the Gods go, I would once again recommend giving them more unique systems as opposed to using the systems from Hunter, as it would make them seem more distinct and important to the game (and could also allow for making them more powerful and threatening), and also making them less likely to get directly involved, instead sending primarily their servants. If a conflict with a God does occur, they should be extremely powerful but able to be beaten, and there should be great benefits to be had from doing so. Basically, anything that should be important to the game (Minions, Dungeons, Gods) should likely have their own systems instead of directly using ones from other gamelines, as it will make them seem more important and unique. Another good step would be fleshing out the society and culture of the Gods and making them seem less uniform and more unique. For one thing, the idea of them all getting together in a meeting and deciding that humanity should worship them for nothing would probably be better if reference was made to a council of their leaders coming to the decision, as it once again makes them seem less homogeneous. Also, an extremely minor adjustment, I would suggest replacing the reference to spirits of Rank 4 and 5 in the Gods section with spirits of Rank 6 and higher, as they are the entities more commonly described as being god-like in nature.

                                Reading some of the other posts, I personally find the idea of them being a sub-template far less interesting, and I think it's fine to have them as a full template as long as some things are fleshed out (Minions, Deathbed Transformations, Dungeons, as well as the Gods and some of the Dark Master abilities and powers.) Overall, I am quite interested in this. I don't have much experience with sub-templates, so I can't help much there, but if you do decide to make them a full template I would be willing to try and help out with it, though I can't promise I'll do so especially quickly as I tend to be a bit slow on these things, but I can do my best as long as life doesn't get in the way.

                                    I like that you have different levels of minions, but I would suggest something more akin to a Tier system, with a Tier 0 acting as Minions useful for logistics work such as building or keeping track of things or moving stuff around, but which are generally useless for combat and would get beaten by a typical mortal with little effort, a Tier 1 able to generally take on most mortals in a fight, Tier 2 being more akin to other supernatural creatures, and Tier 3 being powerful even by the standards of most supernaturals. Tier 0s would be easy to get and without much of a limit, Tier 1s would be plentiful and still fairly easily made, Tier 3s would be more difficult to make but with a decent number being possible, and Tier 3s would be difficult to make and very limited (mini-bosses, essentially). Minions would have powers that could be taken representing their abilities, with different powers being locked behind certain Tiers. The lower the Tier, the more loyal they are and the harder it is for them to disobey.

                                    One idea I would recommend is for it to be possible for minions to, through sufficient effort, restore a destroyed Dark Orb, to touch on the trope of the evil overlord being resurrected by their servants after supposedly being killed by the heroes (naturally making way for a sequel in the process). As far as Dungeons would go, I would recommend both having Dungeon traits that represent things about rooms, similar to Beast, as well as Dungeon Merits, as others have suggested.

                                    You've brought up the idea that all Dark Masters are created as unique entities by the Dark Gods, however I feel like simply not having an equivalent to Clan or Auspice makes them feel incomplete rather than unique, so I would recommend either A: adding in general archetypes based on how the Gods intended them to punish humanity, or B: go all in on the unique aspect, adding in traits that alter how they work (things like being immune to an element such as heat or cold or electricity, having a mind that can't be read or controlled, having the natural ability to enter Twilight, the ability to regenerate bashing damage each turn for free, etc), almost similar to the Deathbed Transformation abilities but weaker and which apply regardless of form, the idea being that they were simply designed that way.

                                    I would personally choose option B, as it seems more interesting to me and seems like it would fit better with your original idea. If going with this idea, I would also move some of the weaker seeming Deathbed Transformation abilities to this new type of ability, such as breathing underwater, and either removing them from the Deathbed Transformation list or buffing those ones up to make them stronger (maybe instead of just breathing underwater, they gain the ability to do that and to swim fast and maneuver easily underwater, for example), as well as buffing some of the Deathbed Transformation effects in general. I would also suggest removing the ability for some Dark Masters to always be in their Deathbed Transformations in favor of using this system to represent less human ones instead, as that way the Deathbed Transformation still seems more special.

                                    How powerful do you want individual Dark Masters to be, minions aside? Like, are we talking "can equal most other Templates" kind of powerful or "can take on a pack of Werewolves solo and stand a chance at winning" kind of powerful? To be frank some of the powers seem a bit weak even for that first option and I may have some suggestions for them, but it depends on how powerful you actually want them to be. I would personally recommend going wild with it. You've said that you wanted them to be final bosses, and the Chronicles of Darkness doesn't really concern itself much with balancing splats against each other anyway (if they did then Mages wouldn't be able to eradicate someone with 5 dots in an Arcanum while also being able to make the Spell not Withstood using an exceptional success), so there isn't really much of a reason to not make them really powerful, given the idea that they are supposed to be attacked often and also do things that invite attack often.

                                    Another idea that could help with the final boss aesthetic is to have it so that at certain Dark Power ratings a Dark Master gains additional transformations, allowing for more powerful ones to have more than two "stages" to their battle, representing more dangerous bosses. Something that may help with the idea of making each Dark Master unique is to give Deathbed Transformations less specific powers; instead of "Extra Legs" which grant a bonus to speed, they can have "Enhanced Speed" which grants them additional Speed and then presents a number of suggested features that could provide that bonus, such as extra legs, the ability to simply run faster, a kind of dark engine that propels the Dark Master forward with a dark shadow trail left behind as they move, and so on. This could help a lot when designing unique forms.

                                    Personally I would tone down the apparent fear of Mages in the stereotype sections, as it kind of goes against the idea of their arrogance and power, and it is also generally absent for Leviathans, many of whom would likely be more terrifying and powerful than the majority of Mages because they gain power earlier by just having access to Apotheosis and the high level powers that come with it. In particular, if a Dark Master truly had a nuke that they had stolen from the government, a Mage wouldn't be any more of a threat in the face of a nuke than a Leviathan. As well, given the idea that they are supposed to be final bosses, they probably should be able to take on Mages (as well as Leviathans, for that matter) as well, in addition to being confident that they can.

                                    As far as the Gods go, I would once again recommend giving them more unique systems as opposed to using the systems from Hunter, as it would make them seem more distinct and important to the game (and could also allow for making them more powerful and threatening), and also making them less likely to get directly involved, instead sending primarily their servants. If a conflict with a God does occur, they should be extremely powerful but able to be beaten, and there should be great benefits to be had from doing so. Basically, anything that should be important to the game (Minions, Dungeons, Gods) should likely have their own systems instead of directly using ones from other gamelines, as it will make them seem more important and unique. Another good step would be fleshing out the society and culture of the Gods and making them seem less uniform and more unique. For one thing, the idea of them all getting together in a meeting and deciding that humanity should worship them for nothing would probably be better if reference was made to a council of their leaders coming to the decision, as it once again makes them seem less homogeneous. Also, an extremely minor adjustment, I would suggest replacing the reference to spirits of Rank 4 and 5 in the Gods section with spirits of Rank 6 and higher, as they are the entities more commonly described as being god-like in nature.

                                    Reading some of the other posts, I personally find the idea of them being a sub-template far less interesting, and I think it's fine to have them as a full template as long as some things are fleshed out (Minions, Deathbed Transformations, Dungeons, as well as the Gods and some of the Dark Master abilities and powers.) Overall, I am quite interested in this. I don't have much experience with sub-templates, so I can't help much there, but if you do decide to make them a full template I would be willing to try and help out with it, though I can't promise I'll do so especially quickly as I tend to be a bit slow on these things, but I can do my best as long as life doesn't get in the way.







                                    ​
                                    There are definitely a lot of good ideas here and given by other people. You raise the point that they should be able to take on mages, and that is a good point. And they should be able to have at least a chance of beating a pack of werewolves, otherwise they couldn't serve as a final boss for them. One of the biggest things that worried me about it was that the Dark Masters weren't powerful enough or maybe too powerful. One thing that is definitely becoming clear to me is that I can't do this on me own or with bits of advice like this. I need something far more focused, methodical, and bit by bit than we can reasonably accomplish in these forums.
