        Originally posted by VoidDrifter View Post
        Huh, really? I figured at the very least the karma meter would probably get in the way of doing something like that. Thank you for correcting my false assumptions.
        Maybe the new Progenitors can give a benefit to members that allows them to ignore Obligation checks for simply creating sapient Wonders (which is a "sin" against Obligation at 6 dots)?

        You know what, 1ED Obligation seriously restrains possibilities for any Genius that wants to experiment more while keeping sane. I think we should throw out the concept of "sin", like the other Integrity analogues did in 2ED.

        Another thing that I'd change is Favored/Non-Favored Axioms. They work more like speed bumps than anything else, and do not emphasize your proficiency with an Axiom very well. How about we scratch out the speed bump of Non-Favored, and add these benefits to the Favored:

        1) Every time you buy a dot in this Axiom, you gain an additional Postulate and a free Wonder (whose Rank can be lower than the one you currently have*).

        2) Instead of +1 only to the Wonder creation roll, you gain an Interdisciplinary Specialty with Area Of Expertise regarding the Axiom, which can be applied to rolls such as repairing, modifying, creating, sabotaging, aiming, etc. You may ignore the prerequisites these Merits normally have.

        *By picking a more basic but versatile Wonder, you would get a bonus of +1 to +3 on activation rolls, which would stack with benefit number 2 mentioned above for a cool maximum of +5. For maximum effect, you may allow the 9 Again and Rote Quality of Professional Training on rolls to utilize Wonders and any other relevant actions, except creating them. For bonuses on the creation roll, you need resources like Laboratory Equipment, Blueprints, Beholden/Inspired assistants and Larvae.

        This way, Geniuses can create advanced Wonders earlier, or experiment more with less advanced ones and other combinations of Postulates before moving on to the bigger projects. Come to think of it, that would be a cool name for mad science related Plans: Projects.

        I would also change Jabir. As it is, the penalty is not that harsh and increases very slowly with Inspiration. Rather than just changing it to a fixed -[Inspiration/2 round up], I think it should be a Persistent Condition that interacts with Social Maneuvering fast and loose. It could add Doors and worsen Impressions, but if you keep your Obligation in check (not necessarily high), get the right Merits/Equipment and plan ahead, Jabir should be possible to temporarily cancel out or at least mitigate (the Temporarily Sane Condition posted eariler would represent this well). However, Jabir should always be looming over the Genius' head, tempting him with outrage, self-isolation and so on.

                                            Okay, so, I wanted to try and be useful, but here's my own random ideas on the matter. I'm not caught up in all the nuances of the game or in what's happened in the past few pages of the topic, so apologies if I'm reinventing the wheel with any of this stuff.


                                            I really would move away from the whole "consensual (un)reality" explanation for Mania. It really feels, as other posters have noted, like a hamfisted MtAscen fanboyism/anti-MtAwak effort. I think that "why does Mad Science work for us, and not for anyone else" should be one of the big mysteries of the game. Maybe Geniuses are somehow acting as nodes for the occultic infrastructure of the God Machine. Maybe their gadgets are nothing but shells for some kind of metaphysical energy. Maybe Geniuses are walking rifts in reality, their brains attuned to alien otherworlds where the laws of physics work differently, and they can shove aside our reality to let their own come through. Whatever they really are, they shouldn't know, and they should try to explain that with many different theories.


                                            The Sanity Meter desperately needs an overhaul. I've always been a fan of the more alien morality meters in 1e - Harmony and Synergy - and a huge supporter of how 2e has drastically changed the klaptrap "Alignment and yet not" system that was Morality. And to me, at least, it makes a ton of sense that Mad Scientists should be truly alien in nature.

                                            When I think of a Mad Scientist splat, I think of Blue and Orange Morality. To be a Genius means that you inherently don't think like everyone else does. That's why you're a MAD scientist. Even the sanest and most stable Genius is still viewing the world from a mental paradigm that mundies would find unnaturally askew. This really builds on that trope about intelligence causing social isolation, and that's what the Genius Sanity Meter should play to.

                                            I picture a "High Sanity" Genius as being like the New Series Doctor, or certain incarnations of the Classic Doctor; friendly enough, wanting to do good, and usually tries to remember that other people don't think the way he does, but still tending to be... quirky at best. There's always that barrier between the Doctor and whoever he interacts with; his way of looking at the world isn't their way. Think of that episode for the...10th Doctor, I think it was, where Torchwood is founded because Queen Victoria, after seeing the Doctor's attitude towards the werewolf he saved her from, declares he's insane and as dangerous as any monster.

                                            "Obligation", then, should cover just how well a Genius can bring themselves to remember that their minds are broken in comparison to human minds... and, more importantly, how well they can bring themselves to care. As Obligation goes down, Geniuses should becoming increasingly dismissive of humans as actually meaning anything; human opinions, human morals, human lives are increasingly devoid of consideration as a Genius spirals into the all-consuming light of Mania.

                                            Essentially, I think is close to what was literally posted as I was writing this; "Obligation measures your Fucks Given About Human Views/Problems". Which is actually a pretty good "Obligation basis", in my eyes. The Harmony style, where your karma/sanity meter tends to flux wildly over the course of even a given game, feels much more mad scientist to me than the "hanging onto your soul for grim death" style of, say, Humanity - you can still try to play your Genius clinging to high Obligation, you're just not screwed over if the Storyteller decides that you shouldn't have yelled at that kid for not running out of the way of your Mobile Fortress even when you told him to.


                                            I'm not a huge fan of the "force Geniuses to get out with mundies" parts of the 1e Obligation meter, either. It just doesn't make a lot of sense for Mad Scientists. In all honesty, I think it makes more sense that Geniuses actually have a Torment esque mechanic, where interacting with mundies too much or on too high a level is bad for them, in that it forces them to try and mesh their broken worldview against the human worldview, until the clash drives them into destructive outbursts of frustration. This is where you get your "They called me mad! I'll show them!" berserk tantrums from.

                                            Doing it this way also enforces the masquerade; not only do Wonders break down from human contact, but Geniuses themselves actively avoid mass human contact, for the sake of their own definition of sanity.

                                            Besides which, mad scientists usually hang around with only a small handful of "normals" in such works, anyway; look at the Doctor and his small but ever-changing number of companions, or Girl Genius where this is a grand total of ONE major occurring character who isn't either a Spark or touched by SCIENCE in some way - I'm talking about Moloch, specifically.


                                            I vaguely recall that at one point, it was mentioned that the current need to put dots into various levels of Axioms rather hinders players. Perhaps we can examine a new way of handling that?

                                            For example, rather than sufficient dots in Axiom = specific building options unlocked, what if we go for the idea of a more "universal" power level? Omnidisciplinary Scientist is a trope applied to mad scientists for a reason, after all. With this, your general "IQ Rank" determines what kinds of stuff you can build, and you can freely access all of the Axioms. In this case, specific affinities based on X/Y Splats translate as a boost to "IQ" when you're working with a specific Axiom.

                                            So, essentially, all Genii can make use of Wonders that use the Katastrofi Axiom, but Grimm are far more capable of unlocking its full potential.

                                            Sound like it could work? Or is this too out there, even compared to Beasts and their Satiety/Avatisms/Nightmares methodology?
