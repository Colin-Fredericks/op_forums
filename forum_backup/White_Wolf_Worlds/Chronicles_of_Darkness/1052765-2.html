                            Hurt Locker does have mechanics for torture, they're just in the Empathy section. I already pointed them out earlier in the thread. You force Breaking Points on the subject, each Breaking Point they fail opens a Door, and when all of their Doors have been opened, they gain a Persistent Condition, starting with Broken. Once they have Broken, they become unlikely to refuse your demands. Then you can ask them whatever questions you want, and being unlikely to refuse your commands, they will most likely give you what ever answers they think you want to hear.

                            Honestly, it's a pretty decent mechanical reflection of the realities of torture. The criticism I am most sympathetic to is that "use the same system we gave you for Empathy" feels a little lackluster. On the other hand, the Empathy section had three different mechanics anyway, and the one it recommends using for torture models long-term abuse, which is exactly what torture is.

                                            Originally posted by K9ine View Post

                                            That is what an anti torture advocate would say, but the professionals that do it would of course disagree. I am inclind to believe them, and there are logical ways to filter back information out of a victim.
                                            Even if said professionals are right, that's honestly overridden by the intent of the Game Designers. So let's start from there. Let's take these points as held by the game designers:
                                            1. Torture is not a source accurate information; it's impossible to tell if what you're being told is accurate or not.
                                            2. People still torture because they believe it works or because-
                                            3. Torture is an "excellent way to instill terror," and that "Obedience and pliability are the goals here, and whatever details you might in theory be trying to extract from someone are incidental at best."
                                            4. There are technically rules for torture because this is a book of violence and people are going to bring up torture if we don't so we might as well have some rules for it. (I don't take this point as given, but this is the sense that I get.)

                                            I feel that part of the problem is that we kind of already have a subsystem for torture - the Breaking Hearts subsystem for Empathy. Here are two possible routes: Adapting said system to base it on Intimidation, or use Intimidation for something else entirely.

                                            Intimidation: The Hard Way
                                            (Everything already in the Intimidation section, up until "Mechanically,")
                                            Mechanically, torture is the process of breaking down a person’s Integrity systematically by subjecting them to breaking points in a controlled situation. It’s similar to the rules in Empathy, above, except that a torturer is usually uninterested in learning about their target personally. Instead of ascertaining the target's breaking points, the torturer subjects their victim to a variety of practices until they find (or create) breaking points for the target. Locating or creating a breaking point is an extended Attribute + Intimidation roll, with a number of successes required equal to the target's Integrity. The Attribute can vary based on what the subject is subjected to, such as Intelligence + Intimidation for gaslighting, Manipulation + Intimidation for psychological torture, or Strength + Intimidation for blunt, brutal beatings. Each roll requires a scene of interaction with the target. This is the process of subjecting the victim to various tortures and seeing what "works." Naturally, access to the target’s relevant records, if any, will provide the same information given sufficient study. Supernatural beings, naturally, differ psychologically and physically from the masses of humanity, and it will be up to the Storyteller to decide if a particular tactic will be effective on them (or to rein in players who are carrying this too far, whether or not the target is supernatural.
                                            A torturer is unlikely to stop at the point where the Broken Condition is applied. Further trauma might inflict Conditions such as Amnesia, Fugue, Madness, or whatever other Conditions may be appropriate. Healing these wounds, if they ever do really heal, will take years of therapy at minimum.
                                            Was it worth it?

                                            Intimidation: Tell Us What We Want to Know
                                            (The stuff already in the Intimidation section, but restructured so as to focus on the common "uses" of torture - extracting (usually false) confessions and other (also usually false) statements regarding plots/accomplices/etc.)
                                            Forcing someone to talk is an Investigation whose scope is usually confirming a preconceived scenario, involving one or more targets as a source of Clues. Using Intimidation repeatedly to uncover Clues does not incur the normal cumulative -1 penalty, and a target who fails a breaking point roll in the process of being interrogated uncovers a Clue immediately, but any Clue obtained through Initimidation or breaking points gains the Coerced Tag along with any other tags as dictated by the results.

                                            New Tag: Coerced
                                            Your character has forced someone to tell you what you want to hear. If a character has access to a Coerced Clue, they get a -2 penalty for any action in the investigation which could uncover a Clue contradicting it. Your character may have multiple instances of this penalty; additional instances are cumulative. Their elements may only be used to grant dice to other rolls to uncover Clues through Initimidation. If at least one clue with the Coerced tag is used as part of an effort to solve the final investigation, the Storyteller may add complications such as "the culprit is innocent" or "there is no such plot" to the investigation.

                                            It's entirely possible to force the truth from a victim, or to browbeat them into making a false confession or implication to save themselves. The characters (if they care) are unlikely to know which they did or what the facts are until long after it's too late. In trying to get the truth, the characters are very likely to destroy it, condemning an innocent man while greater evils are free to go unchecked.
                                            Was it worth it?
