    Aaaaaaaand.... thread caught up.

    Let me add something (hopefully) new and (hopefullyer) fun to the list of ideas!


    Relic: the Borrowing

    The world is scattered with artifacts of bygone eras. They hide in attics, or sit on display in museums. Some few of them are compulsively collected. Some are discarded and forgotten.

    But some few of them collect and discard their owners, rather than the reverse.

    The Bones of Saint Examplename rattle in their glass reliquary whenever the supernatural draws close. Their bearer somehow always manages to survive the creatures of the night, only to retreat from the world as mute hermits shortly after.

    Maru Marumaru was borne into battle for the last time in 1944, lost, and collected my the advancing American Navy. The katana has sat, sleeping, in a display case in a retired Admiral's study ever since. But now he has passed on, and the sword will do anything to return to its home temple.

    A peculiar silver coin has been in circulation for over two centuries. It has been won and lost in many gambles, and used nearly as often to decide a heads-or-tails call. In all its years, it has brought extreme streaks of luck - wondrous and terrible - to its wielders. It yearns to find the one person who can always call its face when it's flipped, and in searching, it's discarded so many hundreds of owners.



    Relic: the Borrowing (RtB) is a Storytelling game of magic items. These are not the plain old "+1" sword, nor the ever-cooperative artifacts, weapons, gizmos, doodads, or enchantments hiding behind points of Merits.

    A Relic possesses its own magic, motivation, history and destiny. However it comes to be, it is called to some purpose. What a Relic lacks, however, is a body to wield it and a mind to guide it in the short term.

    Bodies can be borrowed, and minds can be steered, but at a cost. Every wielder of a Relic risks their health and sanity, and often even their soul, to carry it just a little longer. A Relic may choose its wielders carelessly and wrack them voraciously, leaving behind a trail of the broken and mad. A more careful Relic may chose carefully and shepherd their wielders along with caution.

    Both paths risk Disrepair, the condition which costs a Relic a bit of its own power or durability. Disrepair may be staved off with use matching the Relic's or wielder's motivations, or with careful Repairs.

    A careless Relic that does not mind its own purpose and disregards its own destiny runs the risk of becoming Jinxed. A Jinx is a dark stain on the Relic's history, throwing off the entire nature of its own magic. Some Relics fall to their Jinx entirely, becoming one of the Banjaxed. The Banjaxed sometimes associate with Gremlins - beings of a darkly paradoxical nature, who seem to be drawn to the presence of a Relic out of its own time and place.

    The cold, cruel end of so many Relics is to become an Antique. Antiques have fallen so far into Disrepair, or been so badly Jinxed, that their peculiar destinies dwindle away to nothing.

    A very fortunate few among the Relics succeed in following and protecting their destinies, to become celebrated as Icons. Icons are Relics that have become touchstones of their era, cementing their history in a world where even the past can sometimes be all too fluid.

            People with innate mental disorders, can often conjur up things that never happened, and aren't likely to. Often you are your own worst enemy. Having a real panic attack, over a whole lot of nothing for the first time... An interesting thing about Malk's is there ability to impose this on others.
            Ok, lets divide mental illness into three unscientific categories:

            Traumatic mental illness. We seem to agree Changeling does this.
            Chronic mental illness: These are the things like personality disorders, depression, developmental disorders, etc.
            Degenerative mental illness: These are things like Alzheimer's, dementia, etc.

            The issue with chronic mental illness is that it isn't a good exploration of them to have them be an inevitable progression towards worse symptoms. Most chronic mental illnesses are relatively stable after they mature. Even if to external observers they look irregular and sporadic, to those with these conditions they are usually a constant presence that is usually not visible to others.

            The issue with degenerative mental illness is that while it follows a progression, it is coupled with a loss of practical faculties that would make for a questionable play experience where your character loses the ability to meaningfully interact with the setting over time rather than having increased capacity to do so.

            Both groups of mental illnesses also have major issues with being respectful to the real world conditions in question and maintaining a playable game. CofD 1e is a great example of how this can go awry.
