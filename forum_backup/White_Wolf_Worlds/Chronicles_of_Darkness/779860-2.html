                                    So, let me explain better what I was thinking; There would be three steps in creating a new Music (Now that we like the Idea of powers coming form music we should rename Streams to something else, I would go along with Partiture, Musics, and so On).

                                    ​1 - Choose a Target (Single target, Multi Target or Area), each would have a diferente cost in Breath and XP to buy. something like: Single cost 2 XP, Multi cost 4 and Area 6 (1 XP extra to widen the área)

                                    2 - Choose a Propertie (Sense, Control, Transform, Invoke, Destroy), Each also costing differently: Sense = 2, Control = 4, Tranform = 4, Invoke = 6, Destroy = 6

                                    3 - Choose a Factor (Rain, Ice, Lightining, Sound, Fear, etc.. The ST would be in charge of deciding if a Factor is valid), the cost of these should be an agrément between the player and the ST. But, let's say Lightining is a 6 at cost.

                                    EXTRA = The character may spend more XP to "Refine" the music with an Instrument, meaning they receive bonuses if they use the chosen Instrument to play the music (Didn't thought about the costs yet, but for more bonus the character would pay more maybe 1 = 1 to a max of 3?)

                                    So we would have: A music that invokes a lightning to damage a Single target would cost = 14 XP, but if I want to do that while a play a Violin that gives me +3 in a test it would be 14 + 3 = 17 XP

                                    I still need to think how to stipulate the cost in Breath and the Rolls, but that's what I have in mind.

                                                I actually didn't expect for Siren to be one of the resurrected fansplats, but I have to say that I am pretty hopeful that it works through.

                                                My main concern is that there is enough reference material to build up the idea of the Sirens.Granted, the game is about mermaids and there is quite a bit of lore that is available (the main one I see being used is the Greek origins of the Siren [which are more harpy-esque than fish-like but that's more of a matter of what is currently associated with sirens]) but, at least in the original forums for Siren, things were much more touch-and-go "Wouldn't it be cool if" brainstorming; the cosmic axis was scrapped for a philosophy axis based on water and such.

                                                As for references to build up the Siren repertoire, I'd like to establish some:

                                                MANGA: School Mermaid (or School Ningyo) is about a journal that reveals a spell that summons mermaids to your school (the setting is set around a middle/high school). If you kill a mermaid and eat its flesh, you will be able to make the person you are attracted to fall in love with you unconditionally. The mermaids themselves are girls that wear swimsuits with a letter on the chest and are able to phase through solid materials as if everything were composed of water. You need to match the letter with the first letter of the last name of the person you want and you need to do it before sunrise. Spoilers in the box below.
                                                If you fail to consume the flesh of a mermaid before sunrise, the mermaids will them come after you and forcibly "drown" you in the walls of the school, tearing off your clothes and skin to reveal a swimsuit. They have turned you into a mermaid, forced to be hunted down by those who wish to gain love. If the person you are attracted to already loves you, the mermaids become filled with jealousy and will transform, gaining bulging fish eyes, piranha-like teeth, and scales. They will then hunt you down for having the gall to hunt them.


                                                VIDEO GAME: Mermaid Swamp features a legend about a mermaid and the man who fell in love with her. The legend goes that the man was so enamored by the mermaid's beauty, he built a fish tank large enough to support her and brought her to his mountain home to be with. However, because the water in the tank was swamp water, the mermaid became ill and her beauty faded. Her melodious voice became howls of pain and she eventually died, cursing the man's mountain home so that young women would disappear. The main characters in the story have ended up stuck on the mountain and are haunted by dreams of being drowned and seeing horrifying, bloated creatures. The game also mentions the Shakespearean drama Hamlet, specifically the character Ophelia, who dies by drowning herself. Spoilers below.
                                                In all actuality, the reality behind the mermaid legend is much more horrifying. The main characters speculate that the story goes more like this: a man falls in love with a beautiful woman, most-likely a diver, and kidnaps her. Because he is so attracted to her beauty underwater, he forces her to live in a large tank full of water until she eventually dies from health complications and bloats up. The man grew to love this form as well and he, along with his male descendants, began to forcibly transform and preserve young women as bloated corpses.


                                                FAIRY TALE: The Little Mermaid is basically the precursor to all mermaids of modern times. However, this is not the Disney interpretation. The Hans Christian Anderson tale follows the titular Little Mermaid as she falls in love with a man from the mainland who she saves from drowning. However, her grandmother warns against this as the humans live shorter lives and will go on to Heaven when they die as opposed to the merfolk, whose life lasts three times as long and will dissolve into sea foam when they die. The Little Mermaid longs to be with the man and makes a deal with the Sea Witch by cutting out her tongue (and thus removing her beautiful voice) in exchange for a pair of legs. Of course, the new appendages cause her to feel constant pain and the mermaid-now-human still lacks a soul, which she can only gain if she makes the man fall in love with her. In short, he doesn't and she dissolves into foam. What a shame.

                                                FOLKLORE Iara of Brazilian mythology is a beautiful woman-spirit who sits in a river and sings an enchanting song that attracts men. When she finds a man coming for her, she brings him underwater where either A) she drowns them with impunity, or B) she makes them one of her lovers for the rest of their life. The rusalka, a river-spirit of Slavic mythology, are said to be the ghosts of young women who commit suicide by drowning and, similar to Iara, would drown men who were attracted to them. The nokken, male river-spirits of Scandinavian mythology, also follow the same modus operandi, although they tend to target women and attract them by playing the violin. Continuously similarly are the morgens of Welsh mythology.

                                                TOYS: At the risk of stretching this too far, there is the Mattel toy line of Monster High dolls, which feature a surprising amount of horror and mythological creatures as described as teenagers in a monster high school. Yeah. Of the selection of creatures, Monster High notably divides Merfolk and Sirens as two distinct creatures. The merfolk are unmistakably fish-based, as the character Finnegan Wake demonstrates by having a tailed bottom, requiring him to sit in a wheelchair when he is on land. The sirens are allegedly bird-based, though the character Madison Fear features two finned legs and no discernible bird-like traits. There is also the half-mermaid, half-ghost character Sirena Von Boo who can pass through solid objects and floats as if she is swimming in the air.
