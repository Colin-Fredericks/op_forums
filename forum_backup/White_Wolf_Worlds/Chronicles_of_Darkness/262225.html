    Irem and the Nameless Empire Discussion [SPOILERS]

    09-21-2014, 12:38 PM
    Share your thoughts on the Nameless Empire!

    Going from bits scattered around the core, its borders extended from Asia Minor in the north, to Mesopotamia in the East, North Africa in the West, and down into Sudan and Ethopia in the South. That's big.
    It ended around 6,000 years ago. More specifically, around 3,832 B.C., the rough date of the start of the Sothic Turns. Interestingly enough, this was also around the time the 5.9 Kiloyear Event occured. The 5.9 Kiloyear Event was an intense period of aridification and desertification in North Africa. More specifically in the land that is now the Sahara Desert.

    They weren't the only magically led civilization of their day. Ubar took some effort for even the Shan'iatu to bring down.

    While it seems like most of their conquests just shipped resources back to Irem and didn't really get much out of the deal, the fact that there are still loads of Iremite relics lying around seems to indicate that everything wasn't entirely centralized. Also the fact that the Arisen even had tombs at all that weren't swallowed up when the city took the elevator to Duat (although that may be a side effect of the first or second turns, we still don't really know how the first resurrection went down).

    The early bits in the player's side of the book say that Irem brought agriculture and civilization to the savage proto-Iremites and their conquests. I'm pretty sure that's mostly propaganda. While yes, they probably did teach early civilizations lots of things about math and architecture and sacrificing nations to elder gods, the fact that the Shan'iatu were imperialistic as all get out really colors that view.

    Anyone else notice or come up with anything interesting regarding Irem?

      I've always kinda rejected a lot of what the book says about canon Irem. For one, I consider the various Guilds to not be connected at all to Iremite Guilds, but rather created in the first Sothic turn as Mummies struggled with just tiny glimpses of Irem, way less than what the book says they had, to recreate meaning. My idea of Irem is also both larger and vaguer. The Iremite empire covered the whole mediterranean, from Spain and southern France, including Italy, the Balkan coast, Greece, all of Anatolia, the Crimea, as far east as the Indus, as far south as the source of the Nile and even including the Niger river valley in Western Africa. I also prefer to think of "Irem" not being known to be centered in the Nile river valley, but rather could have been situated anywhere within that Empire. So instead of being an Egypt-o-centric empire, it becomes a Near-East empire, where the Mummies could have been originally from the Indus or Caucasian from the Crimea or Iberian or proto-Greek or whatever.
      I have Mummies remember much less of their past, so their identity isn't based on who they were in Irem, but rather is based on bits and pieces of their Descents that most resonated with them and that were defining moments in their life, and their face reflects that. A Mummy who helped redirect the Yangtze river to free an ancient artifact may look very Chinese and FEEL Chinese because of the intense connection they made with the Chinese people in that monumental task.

          Personally, the most interesting thing left vague about Irem as an Empire was how long it lasted?

          I mean what do we really know about Irem's timeline?

          We know a few things about the time scale involved from the corebook. That it took the Shan'Iatu about 100 years to get the "first tribes" they subjugated (which presumably only took a hand full of years) ready to march out and wage war and form the Empire in the first place.

          We know that around 3,832BC they started performing the Rite of Return during "Sothic Turn 0". How long it took them to perform all those rites is not known, but the fact that Guildhalls talks about certain Mummy's remembering it going on for a while (First of the Arisen being a title given to one of the Scribe NPC's for example). Likewise we know they experimented for quite some time beforehand during Irems warring years on slaves and captured prisoners to try and get the Rite of Return to work properly (resulting in the Shuankshen).

          From the tid-bits we have about the Deceived we know that the Sixth Guild was destroyed before any Rites of Return started to happen. So that suggests their was a period while the Empire was established proper that no Rites of Return were going on and the Guilds were important enough to be active, fully formed social constructs.

          The enactment of the Shan'Iatu's final plan for Irem must have happened not long after they finished off performing the various Rites of Return on their servants.

          As stands you could say that Irem stood for little over 100 years (say 120ish) or you could argue for a much longer period (maybe as much as 1000 years, though there is no real evidence at current for such a long time-frame) of time had passed between the "first tribes" being conjured and the Shan'Iatu's final plan coming to fruition.

          Personally, in the games I have run I had Irem last about 500 years from inception to disappearance from around 4,300BC to 3,800BC. Making its end about 800 years before the foundation of Dynastic Ancient Egypt. This also makes Irem comparable to early Sumerian/Akkadian settlement (of which Ubar was one) but keeps it early enough that the Wheel is pretty contemporary invention and most places in the world think the plough is a cutting edge farming invention (generally Neolithic cultures around the world were getting into proper farm animal domestication and cultivating rice and sugar cane between 5000-4000BC). So this keeps Irem as "the most advanced" civilization of the time, while still giving them some other societies to interact with as is mentioned in the fluff.

          I expect Book of the Deceived to be a bit more exact with the timeline of Irem and answer a lot of these questions to be fair. So I suspect my person picture of the Empire and how long it lasted will change quite a bit after that book.

          Geographically I keep it about as constrained as cannon has it, mainly North/Central African (Southern boarder in line with Modern day Niger/Sudan/Chad, Western boarder at Algeria/Mali) with a bit of the Middle East to the East. In my last game I also included Turkey and Saudi Arabia in the Empire. Normally I stop North-Easterly boarder as being in Syria, Iraq and the Red Sea.

          I don't see the Empire as the largest the world has ever seen, that's never really mention in the fluff and I'm sure its something the Arisen would make a bigger deal out of if it were the case. Considering a fair bit of the fluff is about how the Empire was more technologically advanced (they had bronze!), magically powerful and well...first! Also, just due to world population at the time, controlling much more land then that would have been a really pain, especially once the Sahara started getting larger.

          "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny

                  It's also notable that a starting Mummy remembers nothing about their own life in Irem, merely that they did live in Irem. The bits and drabs they can recall are impossible to separate from what others have told them about Irem, and so it's all a bit unreliable.

                  Personally, I've not done anything special with Irem in my games. It's largely confined to North-East Africa, no bigger than what's described in the lore. Absolutely no Mummies with Caucasian features at all, unless they're Twice-Arisen. I'm not really interested in the scope of the empire, but rather who the characters were back then and how they lived. The day-to-day experience is what interests me, because that's the stuff I'm going to be bringing up in flashbacks.

                  With that in mind, one of my favourite tidbits about Irem comes from Cursed Necropolis D.C. Seb-Hetchet, speaking about what he remembers of Irem, says, "Before I went to die, I walked the streets without seeing slaves. I saw them emptied of the people. What did our leaders do?

                                                          Judging by contemporary translations into other languages (including greek and akkadian) and the fact that the name itself is thought to be onomatopoeia, it's implied that that sign order was more of a cosmetic consideration. Which is a thing, the egyptians were fond of stacking long/small signs whenever they could get away with it, and the sign for w/u (a quail chick) is not easily stackable while the signs for "n" and "p" (the squiggle-line water symbol and the box) are. With the jackal determinative, the thinking was that everyone knew to pronounce it the right way anyway regardless of how they jumbled the signs. As transliteration into english tends to go for pronunciation rather than sign-order accuracy, anpu is probably wrong in the way that literally phonetically pronouncing a lot of english words is wrong.

                                                          Also, while "no written vowels" is technically true, their idea of what a vowel is was a bit eccentric. From our definitions, signs like w, i, j, ˤ, and 3 (3 is totally a letter, I swear) are basically vowels. They're just not *all* the vowels.
