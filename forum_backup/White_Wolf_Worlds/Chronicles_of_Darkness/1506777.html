    If OPP couldn't make new CofD content, should they make a new Urban Fantasy line?

    03-28-2023, 03:28 PM
    If OPP were not greenlit by WW/Paradox to make any more CofD content would you support a new Urban Fantasy line? What changes would you hope to see?

    My Mechanical requests:
    • Replace Extended Actions with a simpler system that takes less time.
    • Improve on Social Maneuvering so that it works with the Skill's system better.
    • Improve the Investigation system so that it works with the Skill's system better.
    • Include Touchstones to all Splats.
    • Codify Horror rules across all Antagonists types with Templates that differentiate how each Antagonist behaves from one another. (Like how Stix have Shadow Form, Pandorans can go Dormant, Cryptids have Aether sense, etc...)
    My gameline requests:
    • Codify faction and sub-type rules. Especially Geist/Sin-Eater whose Factions aren't factions, but Krewe types which makes them feel like they are missing faction depth.
    • Give Demons a culture that allows them to interact with one another outside of Agencies.
    • Rework Beasts to be more about supplementing their urges to do harm into positive change; if we are sticking with "Lessons" there needs to be a society that enforces these ideals. Can't just have a line that states Beasts frown upon killing prey, but then have a line that says leaving dead prey in public spaces is seen as good because it "sends a message".
    • Codify the Cosmology in a way that allows each Splat to be separate from one another if they so choose.
    • Codify what Dreams, the Astral, the Primordial Dream, and Bastions are. So far it seems like synonyms for one another without a rhyme or reason.
    • Give Vampires enough starting Disciplines to match other Splats that start with around 4-6 dots worth of Abilities.
    • Allow for the origins of Splats to be obfuscated from them in lore and hearsay.
    • Clarify the mechanics of Mages in a way that is less up to interpretation than MtAw currently is. Its tough to run a game when a system is so free form that a basic rules call like when to call for Acts of Hubris or Paradox changes with whoever is Storytelling the game at the time.
    I probably have more, but I think this is a good place to start.

              They have that. That's what the Agendas and Tempter associations are about. Lots of demons don't trust Agencies because organized gatherings feel like tempting fate, but the Unchained are generally willing to extend enough trust to interact with other demons on at least a passing basis.
              Codify the Cosmology in a way that allows each Splat to be separate from one another if they so choose.
              I do not understand what this is supposed to mean. The setting as-presented quietly operates under the assumption that any given chunk of the supernatural is not ubiquitous enough for it to have to be sharing territory with any given other chunk of the supernatural, hence why most of the books that aren't multi-splat focused make little to no reference to other monsters in their sample city sections unless it's a story hook.
              Codify what Dreams, the Astral, the Primordial Dream, and Bastions are. So far it seems like synonyms for one another without a rhyme or reason.
              I do not think you have read any of the books these terms come from, because these terms are codified. Dreams are dreams. The Astral is the inside of the soul, which dreams touch the shallows of. The Primordial Dream is the part of the Astral between the soul of humanity as a collective and the soul of the rest of the world; the Horrors of Beasts dwell there. Bastions are the physical structures through which dreams can be accessed, which bleed some of their contents into their surroundings along the Dreaming Roads, which are hidden within the Hedge.
              Give Vampires enough starting Disciplines to match other Splats that start with around 4-6 dots worth of Abilities.
              Disciplines are built as scaled ladders of monstrosity whose starting powers were explicitly designed to be The Main Thing The Power Does, and they're selectable kit for a splat who can universally make people addicted to them, boost their Physical dicepools, and shrug off being shot multiple times/instantly heal damage it takes multiple days for mortals to heal. Even the Disciplines that aren't clan-specific include free kit like always-on stat boost, psychic invisibility, or making animals do stuff for you.

              Mages get six dots of Arcana because the first dot of any given Arcanum is very limited in what they can do, the second dot seldom adds much more, and their base spellcasting pool is built solely from Gnosis + Arcanum with very limited space for Yantras at low Gnosis. Specializing in an Arcanum to start at three dots means you have relatively little you can do with one of your other two, and spreading yourself thin to start with dots in five Arcana means you have one Mage Armor and a bunch of Compelling effects to complement your suite of investigatory spells.

              Demons likewise start with four Embeds because Embeds are very limited tricks that only scale in the sense that they serve as prerequisite for Exploits, which demons get as starting kit only by trading starting Embeds and semantically linking them to Embeds they know; all their obvious tricks burn Aether, risk Willpower, or shred Cover, and their subtle tricks are mostly there to help them move among mortals more easily

              Changelings start with four Common Contracts and two Royal Contracts because Contracts also don't scale more than the moderate/major power dichotomy (besides Goblin Contracts being more specific) and are operating under the same "this is one trick" model that Embeds and Exploits use; also literally none of them are unconditionally free to use and running out of Glamour makes them start to starve.

              Mummies start with four Affinities for the same "this is a basic trick" reason and have their Utterances constrained by their Sekhem and the cost in Pillars, but also their default assumptions of play are that they're the core of a cult serving a punishing demon-god that will pull the plug on their Sekhem if they start getting ideas above their station; their template combining undead resilience with supercharged regeneration along with a cult to help them and a tomb to be secure in, plus the fact that they need a specific Affinity to not risk wigging non-cultists out, exists completely divorced from their selection of starting powers.

              Prometheans get two powers with four effects each that cost three Pyros to fully use, and they lose those powers when they change focus unless they spend their primary Main Goal currency to keep them. The only way they get more is by completing story beats in the same supercategory of archetypal roles.

              Werewolves start with three Gift Facets and enough dots in Rites to learn the rite they need to keep their Harmony in check long-term, and being part of a pack is the main safety net to justify spending those dots on something else.

              Sin-Eaters start with three dots of Haunts, some Ceremonies that let them do their job and induct krewe members, and some other Ceremonies that they have to expand their krewe to unlock.

              Hunters start with some Tactics their cell is especially good at, plus two dots in Endowments or two unscaled Endowments, if they're playing characters with access to Endowments; they don't even get the extra starting Merit dots supernatural characters get.

              The Deviant template is literally just their Variations and the ways their powers fuck them up.

              A case could be made for putting some space into the Vampire template for starting with a Devotion or two, but Requiem is one of the gamelines where the PCs aren't assumed to be pooling resources on a shared project or serving otherworldly masters or pursuing occult knowledge — the core vampiric drive is survival as a human-shaped predator-parasite, and Disciplines are very good at that as-is.
              Allow for the origins of Splats to be obfuscated from them in lore and hearsay.
              This is already the case.

                  The opening post managed to jump right past it's actual premise and into "What should a Chronicles 3rd Edition do?" so hard, I feel like I need to offer a reorientation, so to that end, I think some questions that need to be answered for this are as follows:

                  1) Does the market at the time seem like it has the space for a new broad-scale urban fantasy/horror with monsters as the protagonist RPG to really flourish, or is now a bad time to introduce such?

                  1.5) Is the market hungry for an RPG that is Urban Horror first, Urban Fantasy second, or do they want that order of operations flipped, or the setting to hew strongly in one direction or the other?

                  2) Is there still an active interest in a shared RPG universe of strongly thematic gamelines that nevertheless share the same root systems and setting to allow for an "Avengers"* style crossover appeal, or is the market more interest in more hyper specific gamelines or settings that treat it all as soup? Is the World and Chronicles model too intensive of a model to follow in today's common buying practices, or do we think audiences are still receptive to that sort of investment?

                  3) What do we think the form is for this hypothetical urban fantasy/horror RPG that is discernably distinct from both World and Chronicles to warrant it's creation? What are it's overarching ideas and themes that inform different interpretations that aren't just repetitions of the prior two?

                  4) In terms of it's base system (and just it's base system, the basics of result determination), what are some assumptions that could be challenged or otherwise be chopped out or instituted that could arguably better facilitate the urban horror RPG as we understand it now versus both in 2013 and 2004? Are those concepts still versatile enough to work with mutliple distinct gamelines in a shared RPG universe?

                  5) How married to the particular expressions of each gameline should this thing be? Should there be a vampire, werewolf, wizard, fairy, ghost, hunter, mummy, and demon game, or should that format be re-examined and reconsidered?

                  Because if we want to explore the question of if Onyx Path should do it's own version of these things, I think we need to get down to the bones of the question, rather than play around in the craft and structure level where, if direction were to be spun from, would only yield pale imitations.

                  *Yeah, okay, fine, Monster Mashups have been done before the MCU Avengers took four genre strong movies and threw them in a blender, but more people are aware of that, so it's an easier comparison.

                                          A minor example that is basically the issue in a nutshell is the complaint that you can't play Harry Dresden in Mage: the Awakening. It comes up a fair amount, and the take always misses that it's actually super easy to do that-most of Dresden's allies are supernatural entities of various scale who operate in similar fashion to the way things work in Awakening, most of his clients aren't on the scene for his work, and the closest exception to all of that is Murphy-who's not impossible, but whose concept is basically going to have their arc in the books done at a speed run. But, and this is the important part, it's easy to accept the small scale adjustments to the Dresden Files if you understand the work as leaning the entire series towards a horror interpretation. If you're just playing Mage as a flat way to get your fix of Dresden FIles style storytelling, then yeah, it's unsatisfying that your Murphy-alikes reach where she's at in Battle Ground in half the time, and it feels like you can't explore that sort of relationship (and I mean that beyond the burgeoning romance and more in the way that the supernatural and the human can interact).

                                          Stuff like that comes up all over the place-"How do I play a good vampire?""Does my Durance have to be absuive?""What if I want Primordial Heroes to be motivated by goodness?", so on and so forth. A lot of people play Chronicles expecting it to be, at the risk of being really reductive, Dresden Files but Not Directly, and then run into problems and even get upset when it doesn't do that. Ordering a coffee and being upset it's not hot chocolate.

                                          So, if we're going to ask "Should Onyx Path do it's own version of Chronicles?", I think one of the questions that is owed to ask is "Is that really what you want?" and, more importantly, "Is that really what the RPG buying audience wants?"

                                                          Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                                          So, if we're going to ask "Should Onyx Path do it's own version of Chronicles?", I think one of the questions that is owed to ask is "Is that really what you want?" and, more importantly, "Is that really what the RPG buying audience wants?"
                                                          It's a bit of an unfair question though, because the audience isn't a monolith. I think forums do tend to skew things though. When I play with my normal RPG groups, or even back before COVID and I was a convention regular, there isn't nearly the same level wanting something non-horror. Forums tend to amplify negativity towards the core premise (not just of CofD) because you get a mix of people that like something enough to want part of what is being offered but want to change other bits, and older hands that have played to core conceit enough times to start wondering about tinkering with it for novelty.

                                                          The old "Superheroes with Fangs" thing with VtM when talked about online could make it sound like the vast majority of people only played VtM for the dark power fantasy, but VtM is still a horror game all these decades later, and is still selling despite dark power fantasy games being around if people want that more.

                                                          From a marketing perspective, it doesn't even really matter if some of your customers don't really want a horror game as long as your product is close enough to what they want that they'll buy it and cut out the horror parts.

                                                          The CofD isn't being soft-cancelled because there aren't enough people buying it to be financially viable. Between the people that want it for what it's trying to be, and the people that buy it because it's close enough to what they want that they'll put in the effort to fill in the gaps, it sells as a horror franchise. Hell some percentage of sales are probably just people that buy anything Onyx Path does. From some of the crowdfunding reward tiers that get taken, it's clear there's a few whales in the community.

                                                          I think it's worth thinking about something were the horror is more optional if Onyx Path were to serious consider their own product to replace the profits from making CofD books, but I don't think some reoccurring forum complaints are enough to scrap the horror focus completely by themselves.
