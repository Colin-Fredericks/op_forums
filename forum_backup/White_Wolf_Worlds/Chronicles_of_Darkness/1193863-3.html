        Hm this makes me think of a change from 1e to 2e that i liked.
        See in 1e there was this big push for the dichotomy of carthian/invictus with the carthians being democratic and modern and the invictus being medieval and ... not democratic.
        This association (to me) is plainly wrong as there is nothing modern with democracy and that the middle age did not have an absence of democracy.
        The Holy Roman Empire was a big influence in the functionning and formation of the Invictus and it may have been a monarchy, but it was an elective monarchy. Not only that but throughout the middle ages, several political, civic and national functions were democratically elected.

        I much prefer the distinction in 2e (which i admit to pushing farther than what the books present) where the carthian is not about democracy (not necessarily) but about an experiment in forming a functionnal kindred community and the Invictus is a collection of elders working together to maintain the masquerade through control of mortal society.

        Well thats my take on it at least.

                    Originally posted by Malus View Post

                    I'm pretty sure the experimental attitude of the Carthian movement was implemented in their Covenant book, back in 1e first.
                    Oh, i agree. Sorry i always have trouble explaining myself well in writing. Uh, let me try it this way. I dont see a clear cut change between the 1e book and the 2e book. Its more a spectrum of change going toward what 2e becomes. At the point of the covenant book, the carthians have definitively started going away from democracy as being their whole shtick and became more about the social experiment in all its form.


                    Originally posted by Ventrue Life View Post

                    Could you elaborate on how you do that? I'm very curious.
                    Sure.

                    I push the carthians in my game as being the covenant that tries to form an united kindred community. To do that they actively try to integrate with the other covenants. While any vampire can belong to several covenants (you can have the status merit in more than one covenant) the carthians actually are the covenant where this is the most common. To the point where most carthians are part of another covenant, with some of them part of several covenants.

                    The big thing of the Invictus in 2e is that they are the covenant concerned with maintaining the masquarade. In that sense i have the importance of a member of the invictus based on how much ownership (and control) they have on mortal society. They want each member to oversee their own domain entirely how they see fit (in that sense they act closer to medieval aristocracy). With the traditions and social pressure being all the law that they need.

                    Rereading myself, i think i'm not diverging as much from the core as i was thinking. Maybe toning down a few things (like the whole rar rar fight the powa part of the carthians or the invictus as the leader of society) and focusing more on other (like the carthian unity thing and the invictus as keepers of the masquerade).

                                                    I have no frikkin clue why people think D&D is about "good vs evil." Its primarily about the Swords & Sorcery genre, which also inspired White Wolf (Elric is known as the White Wolf and that style of moody antihero with poorly controlled powers based off parasitism, dependence or deals with eldritch beings ought to feel right at home across multiple gamelines), like virtually all WW/OPP games, tends to be more about gray vs black. Most groups seem to be a mix of alignments that average out to be neutral. The only cofd lines I can think of where you aren't opposed by antagonist splats of people who are painted as unvarying utterdark villains would be Beast and Demon (even then, heroes and hunter angels are almost always painted in a dark shade of grey).

                                                    In Vampire, the PCs are generally shades of grey (sometimes white and sometimes black) vs other shades of grey and a few pitch black guys, and you suffer early onset dementia as a punishment for being "too bad."

                                                    The way cofd is set up is not only that the world is customizable, but each city is as well. Without houseruling of any kind, or so much as coming up with ideas that aren't published, the CoC may be utterly fluffy and cuddly with regards to treatment of the living (Cruac's still 'rough' but that's not a big deal when you can regenerate), the Lancea Sanctum apparently includes at least one kind of Belial's Brood 2.0, Carthians have totalitarians, fascists, and democrats alike, the Invictus includes some very net savvy types, etc.

                                                    Re: a change between 1e and 2e is that while both had utterblack factions, the strix being front and center make the grey vs black theme more obvious. Its no less there, but "PvE" often has a greater emphasis across 2e.
