            Originally posted by moonwhisper View Post
            Perhaps it is meant to have more granularity, but in practice it achieves the opposite.
            Other Integrity traits range from 1 to 10, starting from 7, and cause a dot loss with a single Breaking Point failure.
            Compare it with an average Clarity track of 4-6, which gives less room for smaller effects, not more.
            Don't forget that Icons increase the Clarity track too, so more granularity can be achieved. And characters will increase attributes over time, and certainly can start above 4-6, especially with +1 from Seeming Favored Attribute. I can make very feasible character builds that start with 7-8 Clarity without being weird.
            On top of that, I've never played a Vampire game where anyone had more than 7 Humanity at any point, or a Mage game with more than 7 Wisdom, so the 8-10 range is very, very theoretical to me...

            I agree, there shouldn't be a Comatose condition when filled with mild damage, at the very least rolling to stave it off seems a good solution.
            Also:
            - severe damage should only happen as an upgrade of mild damage, not from a severe attack (or at least it should only be allowed in extremely rare cases
            - successful attacks should make at least 1 damage (similarly to how breaking point failures automatically cause a dot loss)
            - there should be more and easier ways to heal single mild damages, perhaps automatically healing a bit over time

            I'm wondering if it would be better to have a fixed Clarity range of 1-10...
            Here are the house rules I'll be trying out:
            - Successful clarity attacks always deal at least 1 Clarity damage to the Changeling. Successes on the damage roll add to this damage.
            - An exceptional success on a clarity attack does not cause the attack to do severe clarity damage. Instead it might inflict a Tilt, such as Insensate, or cause the Changeling to Incite Bedlam involuntarily. Or other things like that. (On that note, during Involuntary Bedlam, I always count them as rolling 3 successes to eliminate differences between characters, and I don't let players choose to do this, as I found they were too "easy beats" for too much disruption in play.)
            - Characters only gain a Beat for clarity damage in the 3 last boxes.
            - If all boxes are full with mild clarity damage, the character does not gain Comatose. They gain a different Clarity condition as normal. The character is mentally unstable, and must roll Wits + Composure every scene or suffer 1 Clarity damage. As soon as they heal or downgrade any amount of Clarity damage, they stabilize for the moment (even if they're still full on mild damage) until they take more Clarity damage again, so long as they're full on mild after that damage of course.
            - When all boxes are full with mild damage, additional damage rolls over into Severe damage.
            - If all boxes are full with Severe damage, the Changeling gains the Comatose (Persistent) Condition, and will likely die if they don't get help.
            - Meaningful interaction with a Touchstone (attached or not) during a Chapter lets you roll Wits + Composure + (number of attached Touchstones). You may downgrade a number of Clarity damage equal to 1 + successes rolled. You first downgrade any severe damage to mild, and then any mild damage to fully healed.
            - Touchstones no longer reduce the dice in the damage rolls.
            - When you regain 1 Willpower from your Thread, you may downgrade 1 Clarity damage, starting with Severe.
            - When you regain all Willpower from your Thread, you may do the same roll as if you interacted with a Touchstone, and downgrade that much Clarity damage.
            - Healing from Clarity conditions stays the same.

            I think that should do it.

                - Eating nothing but goblin fruit for a full day.
                - Having someone break a mundane promise to you.

                    Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                    [SNIP]
                    I don't really follow here.

                    It isn't non-viable to move on with your life. You simply make new friends and get contact with new changelings. Nothing in that list says you need to fix your old life, at all.

                    Messing with situations close to your own Durance should be hard, that is a core theme of the game. The "witch of the wood" archetype is pretty clearly more of a True Fae archetype (it would be an excellent Title), balancing Clarity is about balancing both sides of your true nature, "witch of the wood" essentially rejects all links to the things that keep you grounded in Ironsides, not just Arcadia.
                    Remember that Changelings aren't just "people with cool fae magical abilities", they walk on the knife edge of sanity, balancing their attachments to both worlds.

                    "Death of a family member." is in there because Changelings can become 4 times as old as humans, and also because it can be a shock to discover what happened in your absence, I guess. Remember you can always use your own judgement on these.

                    I'm having trouble seeing how your players go Comatose so fast. Wyrd 5+ Changelings would surely have recovered a few Icons, and should have enough experience that if they had weak Wits + Composure to begin with, they could have shored that up by now. So I'm assuming 7-8 Clarity would be about the lowest in the party here.

                    They could easily have at least 2 Touchstones each, but certainly have 1 each. Let's also use 3-dice Clarity attacks as a baseline since it's smack in the middle.

                    3 dice for attacks means 34% of attacks fail straight off the bat. The remaining 66% then trigger a 3 dice or 4 dice clarity damage roll (depending on touchstones)
                    1 touchstone = 4 damage dice = average of 1.33 damage
                    2 touchstone = 3 damage dice = average of 1 damage

                    That means an attack on average deals 0.88 if they have 1 touchstones, and 0.66 damage if they have 2+ touchstones. In other words, they should be able to make it through 7-8 clarity attacks without falling unconscious, on average.
                    If they're taking more attacks than that it's probably common sense that they'd start dropping, they're putting themselves under strain they're not supposed to.

                    Also keep in mind they get clarity conditions for the last 3 boxes, and resolving those instantly heals some clarity, and most are easy to resolve.

                    If they get more overwhelmed, zipping back to Ironsides for a bit to recover is sensible.

                    Your easiest house rule would be to not use Comatose for mild damage, but let mild damage roll over into serious damage and only inflict Comatose once they're full on serious damage. That way they'll take close to 20 clarity attacks on average to get to Comatose, plenty of advance warning before they need R&R; and the amount of rolls mitigates the chance of bad luck somewhat.

                                Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                                Part of that its because they are trying to use clever wordplay to get something good from bargains and promises, but most normal people don't tend to remember specific details about little promises. Also, they are dealing with secret societies, that tend to lie a lot to cover their secrets. And I already said that they should just try to make simplier bargains. But my table was looking for a "I'm a half/fae with powers, fleeing from some eldritch horror, I'm going to go loud if needed". and not "Please don't lie to me, it hurts my feelings and I can't cope very well with this".
                                Why can't they simply spend some time with their Touchstones here and there? It heals all mild Clarity damage right away after some meaningful interaction.

                                Clarity is not about "hurting your feelings", it's about shaking the foundations on which the character is trying to reconstruct a stable view of reality. Remember that the tagline of the game is "beautiful madness"; Changelings have trouble with slipping sanity. When they find out they've been lied to, it calls into question everything they think is real, and the problem is they can't remember very well what is and isn't real because they left so much of themselves on the Thorns.
                                Even the death of the family member is not about emotions, it's about a stable part of your life up to that point suddenly being gone. We identify by our family members to some extent.

                                If you're using "this does/doesn't hurt my feelings" as the baseline for what should and shouldn't cause Clarity damage, you might be portraying it in a way that is counter to what it is actually meant to be. Which is your prerogative of course, but it might be part of the reason why you feel you need individual breaking points that differ between characters.

                                Note that the "eldritch horrors" they're fleeing from are also not good at outright lying, relying more on wordplay, since they're bound up in these rules even tighter than the Changelings themselves are.
