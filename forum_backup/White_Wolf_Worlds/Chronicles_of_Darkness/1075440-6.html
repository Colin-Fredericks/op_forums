Comparing the intelligence of humans and tomatoes is not usefully analogous to comparing the intelligence between chimps and humans.

    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Sure, but you would not say that humans got a nerf to strength and agression from chimpanzees because chimpanzees did not become humans.
    Right. While you would have a far better case in saying Requiem Ventrue got a nerf to their disciplines and a buff to their Clan bane from Masquerade Ventrue.

    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    You're talking about a different kind of continuity here. I'm not saying Requiem didn't evolve from Masquerade, I've quite consistently called it a spiritual successor and conceded that despite my personal distaste of the comparisons I understand why they are commonly made. When I say there is no continuity between Masquerade and Requiem I am talking about narrative continuity. Masquerade's narrative continuity ended with Gehenna, though it is soon to be advanced again much like when Doctor Who came back after having been ended for several years. Requiem's narrative continuity started from a nebulous toolbox and gradually extended backwards to flesh out the setting's history, and at no point do the two meet (at least not by default assumptions. A Storyteller could of course create narrative continuity between them because toolbox, but people could they create narrative continuity between The Flinstones and Star Trek if they wanted.)
    Narrative continuity is not the only type of continuity that should count when relating things to each other. The creative team, the game line, the publisher, the mechanics (See Maina's list) - those all have their own continuities themselves. And lets look at the Lexicon - where do you put that? Maybe you quibble and say its a literary continuity and not a narrative continuity, further narrowing the definition of narrative continuity to only the IC timeline. Just because there isn't a made up historical event or NPC that exists in both gamelines simultaneously doesn't mean (I would argue) you get to discount all the other continuities.

    Any decent ST can connect any two franchises, that's true. But you're gonna have a far easier time doing that for Masquerade to Requiem than for Masquerade to Star Trek. And remember, this is exactly what many tabletop and LARP groups did in 2004 - they transferred their games (which included their narrative continuity) from Masquerade to Requiem. Not every campaign ended or rebooted entirely. You still see Masquerade disciplines and mechanics being converted for Requiem on these boards, while I don't ever remember any threads converting Masquerade mechanics to a Flintstones RPG.

As far as being a "direct continuation" of each other, I would rather say NWoD was built on the remains of the OWoD. All I will agree with is that this is a complicated case and that I don't see why it should be officially wrong to say that CoD is the continuation of the oWoD as it was when it ended.

        I consider (in particular) Vampire the Requiem 1e to be reboot of (and directly inspîred by) Vampire Revised. I am counting out 20th anniversary edition and whatever name the upcoming editions will bear, because, yes, that would be incorrect to say that Requiem 2e comes from Masquerade 20th and that changes were made between those two games since the branching already happened.


        Now as for the chimpanzee argument that is filled with intentional misintepreting to keep things going, here :

        Masquerade Revised = Lucy (let's say Lucy IS the common ancestor even though she actually isn't)

        Humans = Requiem 1e

        Requiem 20th Anniversary = Chimps (yeah, for this contrived comparison, I equate Requiem with chimps, sue me)

        D&D 3574000th edition = Star Fruit

        So no, Humans don't have changes compared to chimps, but they do have changes compared to Lucy. Of course, that doesn't mean you can draw parallels between chimps and humans, but that hasn't been my point here. I've been talking about the relationship between the NWoD and the OWoD as it was when it ended with Time of Judgement.


        So what, was Requiem 1e "Awlays meant" to be a gloriously independent game from Masquerade Revised ? Seriously, in all the writers involved in ALL the books from that (Re 1e) line, would all agree to those exact terms ? Even if they did, would all players wo made the transition agree to those terms ? So they don't have inside information, big deal, they're players, death of the author takes a whole new dimention in RPGs.

        Sure, Masquerade and Requiem are different in lots of ways. Anyone can also see that the authors of Requiem 1e read Masquerade revised extensively, liked the Ventrue, bossy assholes, and Nosferatu ugly sewer mafia, and shifted one thing or two and came up with the totally original clans of Ventrue, bossy assholes, and Nosferaty, creepy sewer mafia, for Requiem 1e. They saw a bunch of disciplines and copy pasted most of their names and general areas of powers, sometimes to the precise type of power. Sure, they took Presence and renamed it Majesty, but it's kinda the same. Then, there was the Malkovian debacle, etc. That doesn't strike me as things people who wanted to create a completely original work would do. Maybe they were told to do it because otherwise it wouldn't sell to gamers who wouldn't recognize anything and be confused, but I don't know about the totally original game that didn't get made, I know about Requiem 1e.

        Now sure, there are differences, some big, some small. The mood and themes ? Yeah, I guess. Requiem wanted to avoid big fuck off elders controlling the world from the shadows and have a more local and more street level setting to focus on personal horror (ha ha). Of course, just because the game states that theme obviously means all players only did campaigns of "personal horror" and those who didn't just played the game wrong or something.

        So my point, really, is that there's no ONE correct way to see this issue. Charlaquin, you said you didn't like people talking about Requiem and Masquerade as "editions" (that's your prerogative, although I don't have to share it), but then, you also gave the example of the different versions of D&D and that nobody compared D&D 3rd edition and D&D 4rth edition because they're recognozed as different games, which is fair, except they are indeed called, in this case, "editions", so to go back to one of my previous question : What, exactly constitutes an edition, if not an arbitrary word used by a given publisher to sort out a bunch of books for a commercial purpose, so that when a gamer comes to the store and asks for D&D 3rd edition, they get what thet asked for ?

        And so, what is a "game" ? Well, I don't know. I'd say a game is, the same way, a way to refer to a certain set of rules as they are presented in a certain book or series of books. So, if you as me, Requiem 1e and Requiem 2e are two different games. There are differences between the two, they're not HUGE (although they are big enough that you can't take a lot of 1e things for granted), but they are there. Similarly, Requiem 1e and Masquerade Revised are different games. The differences are pretty big, although there are a good deal of similarities. Also, Masquerade Revised and Shadowrun 5e are different games. They have almost nothing in common, but they are both games and they're different, so they're distinct games I guess.



        This is my point, you can take those words and make them mean anything you want, because there is no deep inalterable truth here. I've never been made aware of commandments on the stone tablets that go "Thou shalt only refer to editions if the games share the same themes and have a few rule changes, and don't ask me to explain how much is a few, thou shalt also know that and stop bothering me.", so really, we are just arguing about what terms we feel are appropriate and what comparisons we feel are worth making, and well, yes and compared Werewolf Revised to Forsaken 1e, because I feel those two games are close enough that I could make that comparison. Don't like that ? Well, I didn't do it to make anyone feel bad, I'd like to apologize if I did. Still don't feel it's a problem, though, but I should have shut up after that because that became an argument of epic proportions compared to what is a goddamn vocabulary preference non-issue.

                Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post

                If you're talking about the frenzy roll then you misunderstand. It's not about what the character feels. It's about the unpredictable reaction arising from that situation.                                         Rice vampire books were a heavy influence on R1. I think the emotionally dead stuff is about the "Claudia effect". Vampires were unable to feel any new emotions. When someone would normally get angry, a vampire wouldn't feel angry. Instead the vampire would remember how it felt to be angry. It meant child vampires could grow intellectually but would remain emotionally stunted. No mechanics, just a roleplaying suggestion. I believe it might have evolved more into existing as a vampire can mess a person up so that even if one can do well at pretending to be human one still moves away from thinking like humans. The emotionally dead thing was an oddity.

                                                    For what it's worth, I'm a bit of an apologist for...some...of the things people don't like about first edition Requiem, and I never liked the emotional deadness thing. Like, from a purely narrative standpoint, it's great. Tragic and horrifying...if you're writing a vampire novel. From the standpoint of "this is improvisational pseudo-theatre that people have to act out", it doesn't work nearly as well for creating and playing an active character, especially given that so much of the game's aesthetic draws from Gothic media, which is, ya know, a branch of Romanticism. It's hard to bemoan your fallen state when it's more like, meh, kind of a drag, eh?

                                                    That's not any kind of dig on the first edition writers; I totally see what they were going for, but that's what second editions are for...

I pretty much had the rules for vampires memorized before 2E came out, and I'm notoriously terrible at remembering rules at the gaming table.

                                                    Obviously, I'm on board the second edition train, but I'd absolutely play a first edition Requiem game if someone invited me to (partially because Vampire is my favourite and I never get to play it). Conversely, I probably wouldn't want to play a first edition Werewolf game, even though Forsaken Second Edition is among my top five for CofD games.
