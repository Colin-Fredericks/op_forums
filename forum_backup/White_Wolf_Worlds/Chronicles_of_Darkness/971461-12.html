        Not attempting to serve as a moderator, but could we please drop the personal discussions and get back on track, discussing possible hacks to emphasise (as in, above and beyond what's already in the setting/system) the ambiguity and horror in Mage?

        My personal suggestion, born of 20+ sessions where the highest Paradox dice pool was 6, before mitigation (also, it had a +4 bonus because they were targetting an Abyssal entity directly):

        Paradox is a new Advantage, consisting of a rating (0-to-10) and a pool (0-to-10), like Willpower is. Casting any spell within your safe Reach (read: that wouldn't risk a Paradox roll) adds a Paradox point. Casting a spell above your safe Reach adds all Paradox points as a bonus to the Paradox dicepool, and resets it (so if you have 6 Paradox points and risk a Paradox roll, the roll gets +6 and your points go back to 0). Your base Paradox dicepool is equal to your Paradox Dots (the rating) or the base pool derived from your Gnosis, whichever's highest, and whenever you get 10 Paradox Points, they go back to 0 and you gain another Paradox Dot. Also, once you have any Paradox Dots, all spells risk a Paradox pool equal to the Dots, even if they're within your safe Reach.
        The only way to get rid of Paradox Dots might be taking Resistant Aggravated damage, on a 1-per-1 basis, with a Paradox Condition that would persist for as long as the damage does.

        This makes it so that no matter how careful you are, sooner or later, something awful will happen. It's still manageable, if you can handle absorbing the Paradox within your own pattern, but that comes with its own problems.
        Thoughts?

                            Thoughts?
                            To address Dave's "fiddly" (quite legitimate) critique, you might consider simplifying this into a couple or a few categories that you can represent with Conditions. A progressive set of Paradoxical flaws could work.

                            Off the top of my head, let's say, by way of example, four levels: Normal, Running Hot, Burdened, Scourged
                            You get the first by attempting but failing to contain a paradox sufficiently poorly. You might upgrade on any exceptional Paradox success.

                            If each level imposes an increased penalty to safe Reach but with a bonus to spellcasting, it would make casting increasingly prone to slipping out of control. In fact, it could improve the probability of the casting, just with similar boosts to the Paradox roll.

                            Since Paradox now targets others in 2e, this also happens to help magnify the horror of mages, per the original topic.

                            Grump, grouse, and/or gripe.

                                Posting my own perspective here: Unlike other splats, Mages have choices. A Mage has a choice on whether he'll blindly follow his Obsessions into the rabbit hole. A Mage can decide never to overreach. In fact, with the exception of the Long-Term Nimbus (which has no mechanics attached to it besides "Your ST should make something up"), a Mage can decide to live as a Sleeper.
                                And again, personally? I think the occasional "saint" does wonder to highlight the depths some Mages fall to, so I like the system as-is. It works, it does what it's supposed to. It's just neat.
                                But then, I get to the table. Now, I know my players, and they're enjoying the story I'm presenting them -- it's definitely a Mage story, but it isn't a horror story. Why? Because Mage is all carrot and no stick, and my players are allergic to carrots. Seriously, after 20 sessions, not once was a Paradox voluntarily released, actions that could cause Wisdom loss have been avoided like the plague (even after reminding them it's a good source of Arcane Beats), and so on. If it wasn't for the external horror I bring to the story (Lovecraftian entities and time travel, mostly), there would be no horror whatsoever.
                                So I understand why Gecko made this thread. I believe that, for some tables, the system needs more teeth.
