    So many windmills...

    But to replies:

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    But as Arc spends plenty of time on, the question of, "what are you going to do now?" is smoke and mirrors because the book is already telling you that no matter how you answer it, you'll fail sufficiently that trying had no point.
    Which isn't actually true... The Failure talked about is regarding the big, transcendent goals many Genii aspire to. Individual accomplishments are fully possible. And, as TKR stated, realizing that is an important growth step for a Genius.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    The question the book poses is not, "You live in a vast meaningless cosmos, what are you going to do about it?" but, "You live in a vast meaningless cosmos, what are you going to do about it keeping in mind that all answers are equally stupid because none of them do anything that matters?"

    It's a meaningless question given the rest of the context.
    Not accurate, see above.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    Arc addressed this pretty fully... because failure of an action isn't failure of the whole.

    I have a marvelous streak in my (brief) scientific career: zero experiments that supported my hypotheses. In essence, they all failed. It's kind of frustrating even if I'm OK with it. Do I feel like a failure as a scientist? Nope. I showed that a species that's commonly used to estimate the health of specific biomes is not indicative of specific conditions (which is important, because you need to know what your tests can and can't tell you), and expanded two analytic techniques other people can use to do more science with even if what I was measuring the forced me to come up with new protocols didn't pan out.

    Others can learn from my failures (and I pretty much took experiments doomed to fail because I don't have a career riding on getting published with successful experiments to worry about), and even if they never know it was me, someone somewhere might discover something important because I cobbled together a different way of looking at things and told other people about it.

    That's legacy. That's meaning. That's why people do things, even if they know failure is the most likely result. That's why eventually dying doesn't matter, because contributions to the species and civilization outlast one lifetime.
    And you've reached the same answer here. It also applies to Genius.

    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
    But that's the problem with Genius as described: failures can't leave a legacy, robbing them of any point.
    Except, thing like the Axioms exist. And Lemuria, and the Peerage. And Foundations. Etc. Genii leave lots of Legacies. And that's just to other Inspired. It is beyond scope to mention how all the individual lives were altered by plans, failed or successful. You aren't going to herald a new age of space exploration (the Failure), but you efforts to do so can shape lives and outcomes.

    If you want the inability to leave a lasting legacy, that's Leviathan.

    Malkydel: "And the Machine dictated; let there be adequate illumination."
    Yossarian: "And lo, it was optimal."

                                    Thank you for the review. I admit to being surprised over the vemon this subject engenders, but hopefully you feel a bit better now. Sorry that it kind of broke your mind a bit.

                                    Two quick comments that are only tangentally related to your review. First, could you tell me what anime that little "Okay! This pisses me off!" pic came from? It looks recognizable, but I can't place it for the life of me.

                                    And two, a note about mad scientists and wizards being antithesises of each other. That's kind of an idea that I find to be uncomfortable. Modern western culture does suggest that science, the arcane and the divine are all separate things. But, if we dig into the past a bit, or outside? There are no differences. They're all the same thing - priests-wizards who advanced human knowledge prior to the advent of the Scientific Process. Alchemists and herbalists who were labeled as dabblers of the occult. Ascension tapped into all three of those pillars, as well as attempting to tap into some from outside of Western Culture. These aren't antithetical of each other - they really are the same thing, and as much as I don't like a lot of Awakening, this is one part that I think they got right. Everything is blended together to the point of being indistinguishable. Artificial divides are artificial.
                                    1) Madoka Magica. Kyoko Sakura is best girl, but that's just, like, my opinion man.

                                    2) It is true that the divides are artificial, but Genius wants it to be a point, and honestly, it's not a point I care to take away. Hell, I sort of used it a center point for the idea of the better world, whether it's usable or not.

                                    I mean, it doesn't have to, but then Genius runs the risk of relying too much on Mage-isms, and I'm fairly sure Genius fans are tired of the comparisons.

Only, the purpose of Inspiration is to Inspire and the split between the Antagonist and Protagonist factions is a ideological split on how that inspiration should take place. Antagonists take the stance that the best way to inspire the masses is to set themselves up as the masters of the world where they can control and shape how technology and science advance. They want to bottle and control inspiration to take it to undreamt of heights of possibility in a controlled manner. The protagonist factions take the stance that Inspiration inspires by it's very existence and that trying to control it will do more harm to human creativity than good.

                                                            So i read your TLDR and, well it wouldn't be Genius if you did that.

                                                            Ultimately genius is a very personal game where the characters' learn that they're powerless before a vast cosmos and make peace with that fact. Or they fail to learn that lesson and suffer the consequences.

                                                            Your TLDR made it sound like a game of scientific heroes who tie Chuthulu up in chains formed of newly created scientific laws, before blasting him, then publishing a cure for cancer on the flight home.

                                                            I'm not saying that's a bad game. Heck you'd arguably get something very similar if you took the Marduk Society I wrote for Leviathan and made a whole game about them. I'd probably play it.

                                                            I'm just saying that's not Genius and a Genius 2E should be an even better portrayal of the origonal themes rather than their opposite.


                                                            P.S. Genius can and do Break the World. Take Karl Marx (not Inspired, but a great example), he's a failure in the sense that he never built that worker's paradise. But he broke the world by accidentally fathering a Stalinist dystopia.

                                                            P.S.2. Genius' history is basically a retelling of the history of scientific methodology from Hasan Ibn al-Haytham to somewhere between Victorians and 1960 world. In the real world that history does focus on the middle east and Europe; but at the same time, I like how it's based on the real world. Rather than redo the entire history I'd just add a few Dark Eras and a city book or two set outside the Anglosphere. I'd definitely like one set in pre-European contact Polynesia focusing on shipbuilding and exploring Bardos (there's a really cool Maoi shipbuilder in the sample Antagonists).
