    Heart-Devouring Ritual

    01-22-2022, 12:47 AM
    The Begotten tend to take the concept of the Devouring for granted: The Horror rushes in from the depths of the Primordial Dream to consume the soul of a sleeping mortal, creating a new synthesis between spirit and flesh in the form of a Beast. Whether this happens on its own or at the instigation of another of the Children, this is the way of things.

    A Devouring, however, can take many forms, some more visceral than others, some not requiring the cooperation of another Beast, a nascent Horror, or even the Dark Mother at all.

    Cannibalism is a very old fear, indeed.

    Why would a person want to become a monster? There’s the matter of power, of course: all that power running through the veins of walking nightmares, fire-breathing dragons, titanic-strengthed cyclopes, face-stealing noppera-bo. One would-be cannibal might want to become the world’s greatest free-diver, another sees the primal mind-tricks of the Begotten as just the right key to rocketing up the corporate ladder, while a third simply wishes revenge on everyone who’s ever put them down. Jealousy can also play a leading role: Why should some lucky few be granted the mantle of dreams while the rest of us poor saps are left out in the cold? They’re just squandering their potential, anyway. You can swim through lava, fly like a falcon, pierce any deception, and you, what, act like a pathological asshole? What’s that even about? Couldn’t be me. You’ve proven yourself unworthy of such precious gifts, and the scale must be rebalanced. That’s what they call restorative justice, see.

    The monstrosity itself can’t be discounted, as well. Some people are predisposed to view themselves as demons, whether they’re we-live-in-a-society-type anarchobuffoons, lifelong worshippers of a dark power demanding grave sacrifice, or unfortunate victims of irrevocable soul-mangling at the hands of an unwise sorcerer and who now see this as their chance to set things right. Maybe they’re just really attached to the idea of having tentacles. A few see such a transformation as their ultimate destiny, the fulfillment of a prophecy uttered over their gene sequence centuries before their birth, glimpsed only ever half-lidded in crumbling petroglyphs, garbled folklore, and apprehensive impulses toward a nightmarish arete.

    Although hardly commonplace by any stretch of the imagination, legends of devourers make their way into a handful of Hives, recorded in Obcasus grimoires or passed down from wise-monster to pupil over generations. Sometimes it’s the Kin who remember: an immortal who was there the last time some would-be usurper of the Begotten rolled into town, or a werewolf pack keeping meticulous archives on every permutation of the Sacred Hunt they can sink their claws into. Cross-disciplinary occultists sometimes wonder if there’s any greater connection between the acts of these heart-eaters and other instances of supernatural cannibalism, such as the Amaranth, the rites of the Faithful of Shulpae, or the workings of an obscure sect of wizards devoted to an entity they call the Seventh Dragon. Maybe every time one sentient entity consumes another, a single drop of power is transferred to some still-fetal godling curled inside the womb of Hell, nourished on transgressive predation, biding its time to be born into a world so acutely seeded with suffering to be harvested in the last great true feast of creation.

    But hey, that’s just a theory.

    Systems

    No special occult underpinning is required for a mortal to become a devourer, though the process does necessitate the execution of a particular ritual whose steps are not exactly easy to uncover. This also presupposes some knowledge of the Begotten, no matter how incomplete or distorted it may be. Researching the rite can be treated as an Investigation with a Scope of ten Clues, representing hunting down snippets of lore or internalizing lessons received from some grim tutor. Elements from these Clues may be spent on performing the rite, contesting the supernatural powers of the Begotten, or even tracking down and restraining the Beast to be sacrificed.

    Generally, only mortals can benefit from the rite. A small touch of darkness such as possessing a Supernatural Merit is fine, but anything that would reclassify a person as a type of monster, such as a ghoul or stigmatic, causes the rite to fail. Those lacking a soul should also steer clear, lest they meet a more grisly fate. The one very interesting exception to this is Heroes, who may still perform the rite to completion and become a Beast, if they can hold their noses long enough to. Begotten scholars sometimes wonder whether this was the Dark Mother’s intent for these slayers all along. The implications trouble.

    The outer form of the heart-devouring rite varies according to time and place, transforming as it passes from master to student, culture to culture, but the core component always involves the ritualist consuming the heart of a Beast. Like, literally. The celebrant cuts out the heart from their still-living victim, usually tied down, sedated, or both, and eats it fresh. Sometimes incense is involved, or chanting, or doing a bunch of speed. Regardless, completing the ritual requires a roll of the lower of Stamina or Resolve, plus the lower of Medicine or Occult, with an additional -6 penalty. As mentioned above, Elements from investigating the rite can be spent to add bonus dice to this roll. The ingrained penalty can be further offset with the inclusion of rare and elevating ritual trappings: Paraphernalia costing at least Resources 2 reduce the penalty by one, Resources 3 by two, Resources 4 by three, and Resources 5 by four.

    Success: The ritualist completes the rite, devouring the victim’s heart and keeping it down. At the end of the scene, they transform into a Beast, gaining their victim’s Horror in the process as it transfers over to their own soul.

    The devourer-Beast starts with a Lair equal to half their victim’s Lair rating (rounded up), with appropriate Lair Traits, and Satiety 1. They begin with two of the victim's Atavisms and two Nightmares; purchasing other of either that the victim once possessed costs half the normal Experience amount. The ritualist also gains the victim’s Family, Birthright, Hunger, and Legend. The player decides the new Beast’s Life.

    Most other traits the victim possessed, such as Merits, are lost, with the curious exception of Obcasus Initiate along with any rites the victim knew. The victim’s active Family Ties are transferred to the new Beast as well, though the bearers of the Condition are not made aware of the usurpation. The new Beast retains no memories from the old, and has no knowledge of extant Family Ties until they first lay eyes on any given Kin bearing them. Other inherent abilities of the Begotten (Under the Bed, Skeleton Key, etc) require either research, communication, or trial and error to uncover.

    The new Beast’s Lair begins disconnected from the local Hive, any Burrows connecting it to those of Broodmates swiftly collapsing. The devourer may reestablish connection if desired, if the others will even accept it. You can imagine that many do not. As for Chambers, only the Heart remains.

    A mortal without a soul who successfully enacts the rite finds the captured Horror enraged at the lack of anchoring meal in the meat of their murderer, taking their fill in a very different kind of synthesis: The new Beast immediately undergoes the Merger.

    Exceptional Success: The heart-eating ritual culminates in the new Beast gaining a measure of their victim’s memories. Take the Informed Condition relating to the victim’s life. In addition, the ritualist experiences flashes of knowledge of any Kin with whom the victim possessed Family Ties: faces, names, general monster-type.

    Failure: Nothing happens. Nothing spectacular anyway. Maybe the ritualist vomits up the heart, or the Horror misses the mark and speeds off to parts unknown. Regardless, the would-be devourer is left with a sense of disappointment and a gorey mess to clean up, not to mention a Breaking Point to contend with.

    Dramatic Failure: The ritual draws the attention of the Dark Mother, who demands a heart for a heart for the devourer’s presumption. The stage becomes shrouded in impenetrable shadows; when they clear, both celebrant and victim are gone, with only a few errant bloodstains remaining behind to tell the tale of the occultist’s hamartia.

    Cannibals and the Family

    Naturally, most Begotten treat heart-eaters with wariness if not outright hostility. If they consumed one Beast, what’s to stop them from doing it again? In return, most devourers aren’t in it for the sake of community, preferring the furthering of their own goals over any kind of camaraderie with fellow monsters. What a bunch of freaks anyway, right? They think they can organize freedom? How Scandinavian of them! They come off as pretty dysfunctional and sociopathic, anyway. Best to stick to the kind you know.

    Hmm, except for when those noxious Heroes catch your scent. That’s tricky, and maybe you aren’t above asking for help. And, you know, that guy that guy murked was kind of a bastard in his own right, so maybe this is a second chance for his Horror in a new body. You’ve seen stranger, probably done worse after all. And as far as anyone can tell, the Dark Mother doesn’t exactly seem to disapprove of the whole grisly shindig, treating those who strongarm their way into her fold just like any of her other Children. Like the adage about strangeness and beauty, unorthodox alliances might be forged in all sorts of dire straits.

    The devourer could also try moving to the other side of the country and starting over somewhere else. It’s not the worst idea, but something about the ritual stains the aura, like the eyes of a killer: Everything else being equal, the Impression level of a heart-eater with normal Beasts is reduced by one step.

    Some very ancient scraps of lore recorded on Mesopotamian clay cylinders and Mesoamerican glyphics suggest that this act of supernatural cannibalism was once much more common, perhaps even as a way for an old and powerful Beast to pass on their lessons to multiple recipients, each partaking of one portion of the body, and thus its respective accumulated wisdom. The secrets to this are lost to our modern age, but perhaps there are river-fed ghosts who know more than they let on.

    Permutations

    The heart-eating rite can be performed on certain Inheritances: namely, the Beasts Rampant and Unfettered. Both require their own modifications, and such prey provides only a lesser feast. Instead of becoming a full-fledged Beast, devouring one of the Rampant grants one of its inherent benefits, and the Unfettered, one Influence or Numen. Any costs levied by such powers are paid in Willpower instead. Determine dice pools as appropriate for the individuals involved. Either way, the boons of this consumption fade at the end of the next story. At least such monsters’ inhumanity seem a little more willing to give up their power: Decrease the ritual penalty to -4. Good luck catching either, though.

    Against the Beast Diverted, eating the Horror-half grants one Atavism, and the human-half, one Nightmare. Use Integrity in place of Satiety when called for, and the character’s lowest Power Attribute in place of Lair. Eating a Beast Erased bestows Willpower points equal to the pauvre bougre’s remaining Satiety (which can take the ritualist over their normal maximum), while devouring one of the Inverted grants the ability to place a single Anathema, determined by the nature of the victim, like a Hero. As above, these powers fade at the end of the next story.

    The effects of these lesser feasts stack, though performing it on another non-Inheritance Beast merely supplants the old Horror with the new. Once the devouring ritual is learned, it can be performed time and again, though one might suppose that it’s possible for, say, a changeling to slip into a cannibal’s dreams and slip a knife through the ribs of the Eidolon representing their knowledge of the rite.

    The Incarnate cannot be fed upon by the likes of mere mortals. Not like this, anyway. The bones of the presumptuous line more than one mythic Lair. Devouring a Hero provides no benefits beyond what eating an ordinary human would. Their hearts do tend to taste of exquisite honey, though.
