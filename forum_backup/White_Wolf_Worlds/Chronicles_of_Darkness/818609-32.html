    Originally posted by Firanai View Post
    There's one aspect of Genius I wanted to discuss: Funding. Being a genius is expensive since they rely completely on technology to do their thing. I like how the game acknwoledges this, but as some people have pointed out in here a player shouldn't be worrying about balancing numbers and businesses. As such I have come with a system that I hope will balance both aspects. Please, feel free to share your opinions and suggestions on it.

    -Funding.

    In game terms, funding refers exclusively to the characteristics and origin of the resources (Money, personel, tools, materials, buildings, etc...) that the genius needs to create and maintain the infrastructure that allows him to build wonders. Each type of funding will have advantages and disadvantages that the player will have to deal with at the discretion of the storyteller.
    In game mechanics Funding is a way for the storyteller to create drama and adventures for the player character by introducing complications. It's not intended to be a hassle or another thing to worry about. It's just another way to add fun to the game. The storyteller, with the help of the player, is the one that decides when the complicatons enter the game and always with the intention to enrich the adventure.
    During character creation a player character must chose between one of the following fundings, or come up with a new one with the approval of the storyteller. (These are just some examples. If you have another one, or think one shouldn't be here, please feel free to say it)

    - Trust fund.

    Someone in your family or close to you was very rich, liked you a lot, and recently died. As a result, you have inherited a vast amount of wealth that you're using to fund your research and wonders. However, there's an obvious catch: it's limited. Sooner or later your funds will run out, and many young geniuses underestimate just how expensive the live of a genius can get. This type of funding is great for players who don't want to worry about funding, at least in the begining. As the campaign advances, and depending on the character's actions, the storyteller can tell the player their funding is running low and they need to find an alternative source of income or risk running out of cash at a very inconvenient moment.

    -Freelancer.

    The genius offers his skills and inventions in order to fund itself. The genius is already a stablished freelancer with a list of regular clients. It's important to specify what type of client and jobs they take since it will affect their reputation and the kind of clients and jobs they will get in the future. This type of funding offers the most independence. The big catch is that, even with regular clients, it's a very unstable source of income. Also, taking into account how chaotic the life of a genius can get (and let's be honest, their creations are not famous for being very safe or stable) planning becomes nightmare, with projects often delayed or destroyed, and there's the risk of angry clients coming back for the genius.

    - Business.

    You own a business that provides you with a stable source of funding. The player has to specify what type of business it is. It's large and succesful enough that it runs itself, so the player doesn't need to worry about management. The main advantage is that it allows the genius to be independent and gives access to the resources of the business. However, apart from the regular problems that may assault any businesses, the genius has to deal with the simple fact that his or her business is ill prepared to deal with what enemy geniuses may throw at it, which makes it a very vulnerable point of attack. Here's an example, batman and iron man are used to battle supervillains, but the people that work at wayne enterprises and stark tech are not. A genius must be prepared to defend their business.

    -Organization.

    A big and powerful organization is the one providing the funding. The player has to specify which one (Government, corporation, etc...). Working for an organization has the advantage of not having to worry about anything except the research and building of wonders, the organization provides anything they may need. The main catch is that the genius has to justify their expenses, answer to the organizations demands and help it reach their goals, whatever those may be, or face the wrath of their superiors. In conclusion, it's a secure source of income and security in exchange for independence.

    Well, that's it for now. If you have more ideas feel free to share them.

    Hmm. Would you see Funding as something that might be worth tracking, almost like Beast tracks Satiety? It goes up and down as you get new income streams, or spend vast resources on Wonders and research?

    Or just keep it to a more elaborated (and separate) Resources?

            My thought was to keep funding and supplies as RP-based as possible. Basically, the idea was to have Wonders require specific components instead of needing abstract Resource requirements. So instead of worrying about funding for your death ray, you just need to worry about the giant diamond itself, and how you get it (purchase with the normal Resource/Availability, theft, finding it in some Bardo, etc.) is up to you and the Storyteller. That reduces the number of new systems we need and still keeps Wonder contstruction as a character action rather than just rolling in downtime.

                                Originally posted by Firanai View Post
                                There's one aspect of Genius I wanted to discuss: Funding. Being a genius is expensive since they rely completely on technology to do their thing. I like how the game acknwoledges this, but as some people have pointed out in here a player shouldn't be worrying about balancing numbers and businesses. As such I have come with a system that I hope will balance both aspects. Please, feel free to share your opinions and suggestions on it.
                                Many many pages ago in this very same thread I had a post suggesting a similar switch of Funding from a Resources derived merit into its own system. I've had more time and practice to come back to a Funding or Budget conversation.

                                I'm of the philosophy is that if you try to create too many parameters for a single merit, it should instead become its own system. Although many do not want to factor a 'Budget' into how their Genius creates their Wonders, it ties back to one of the game themes of desperation and it is a fitting limiter to what a Genius can do at the start of a Chronicle versus the end. Reworked as a new system, it would have to tie back to other aspects of the game.

                                So my revamped pro funding platform:

                                Get Some Perks from Funding
                                Who doesn't like perks? If a Genius has a certain variety of Funding they also receive a small circumstantial boon, its bonus tied to the rating of that Funding.

                                Reduce Axiom Penalties
                                Trying to create a Wonder beyond your current rating of Axiom? Now you can with the right Funding. The rating of applied Funding will reduce the penalty you get for Wonder creation tied to the rating of Wonder you are trying to create. However this caps all the other uses of Funding for the session, or maybe a story. Instead of requiring a Budget for everything, the Funding now instead smooths over the times you face too many penalties with your Wonder creation roll. Although it can only do so at select times.

                                Funding Upkeep as Story Hooks
                                Each Funding would require some kind of upkeep, such as time or criteria or a dice roll. Even if these are not required, use Funding as a way to introduce or influence plots. Working your 9-5 job to get the cloning budget doesn't have to be only a time sink, you can use it as a storytelling medium for things that interest the Genius or for plots along that sphere of Funding.

                                Tie Funding to Obligation (2.0)
                                This is the biggest point that could of had a lot of potential in the 1.1 edition of Genius yet it was a missed opportunity. As much as a Genius is a fount of ideas and a conduit of Mania, they cannot create in a vacuum. If Obligation was meant to relate how stable a Genius remains in relation to the society around them, Funding is where this should occur. Funding and its ratings would serve as an ideal 'Touchstone' for breaks of Obligation. Risking a drop on the meter yet you upheld a tenant of your Funding? Have it as a bonus to the roll. On the flip side, if you've spurned Funding on an Obligation lowering act the Funding becomes a penalty. Only a single source of Funding could affect Obligation breaking points at a time. Yet it can't be one you've already tapped out for other advantages.

                                Encourage Different Play Styles
                                Does your Genius get involved with a lot of ventures, having a wealth of various 1 dot Fundings? Soak up the perks and incur few drawbacks when you invariably step away from some of them. However your bonus from any single Funding will never be that great and that's a lot of upkeep to keep spinning. Or you can be a Genius that defines and expresses themselves from a single Funding at a high rating. It gives a mighty bonus yet hurts all the more to step away from when its high rating becomes a penalty. Additionally, Funding types could be exchanged between stories and other such points between sessions in a Chronicle.

                                Fix Some Merits
                                With Funding as a separate sub system, other Genius merits can be renovated with appropriate mechanics and themes. The Tenure Merit, besides mitigating Failures with Wonder creation, could also preventing a single type of Funding from dropping lower than 1 dot.

                                    Since Orphaned Wonders are still a thing, I think it would be a good idea to make the original, always-becomes-a-monster Orphans their own thing; they become functionally antagonists only when they figure out how to upgrade themselves (perhaps they have their own term to distinguish them from "glitchy but normal" Orphans-perhaps something like Vital Orphans, because at that point they match the biological criteria for "alive", minus maybe reproduction and only because of lack of motive, not ability). Which leads me into the intelligence scale, and fixing that quickly:

                                    Zombie: The Orphan is active, but it's still debatable whether it's actually alive or not; it evolves, it reacts to stimuli, it can even reproduce, but it's otherwise mindless in all other ways; it acts based on a predictable program, more video game NPC on a fixed loop and attack pattern than any actual thought. That makes it predictable and hacked easily enough by a Genius who figures out the internal logic gates, but that also makes it completely relentless; many Genii regard zombie Orphans as actually the worst kind of Vital if the Wonder that became it was powerful enough, as it doesn't comprehend stopping. Zombie Orphans cannot have an Intelligence or Manipulation higher than 1, nor do they have [Virtue] or [Vice]; they just don't have the proper emotions.

                                    Animal: The Orphan may not be able to talk, but it has a personality. An animal Orphan has well and truly developed a mature ability to think, if not precisely reason; on a raw intelligence scale, it's developed the brain power of a very smart dog or perhaps a dumb chimpanzee. It isn't capable of discussing philosophy, but it has an answer to the meaning of life; eat, remain safe, sleep, breed, repeat. Some Genii wonder what the difference between this and a zombie Wonder actually is, all of whom have never owned a pet; animals may not decide on much beyond where their next meal comes from, but they're quite capable of exercising judgement, gauging a situation for danger, or learning from experience; an animal Orphan who escapes Vital hunters will remember what they look like, and the tactics they used. The good (or perhaps awful, if one lacks Animal Ken) news is that Orphans are domesticated by default; as creations of humans, they do not inherently fear hominids and will react well to being treated nicely. Then again, wild dogs have been known to eat people precisely because they don't fear them. Animal Orphans cannot have an Intelligence greater than 2, but some Wonders originally meant for gauging social situations can have a Manipulation up to 4; instinct is a crafty thing in social animals.

                                    Sentient: Both the most and least dangerous of the Vital, sentient Orphans are capable of communicative cognizance and the full capacity for logical thought. That doesn't make them smart, but if every Intelligence 1 person was a complete moron, people would stop hiring them as muscle due to forgetting what they were doing in the middle of their jobs. No, sentient Orphans have that lethal combination of instinct plus the ability to have ideas that aren't bound by dumb instinct, one that is still largely motivated by finding food and shelter. Which is to say, they aren't inherently alien to humans, though a sentient ray gun is going to have rather different priorities than a domestic servant bot, simply because that's how it's built and what it can do. Thus, it's not actually that easy to communicate with the more alien Orphans (to some solitary Vital, it's quite possible they only understand language as "big humanoid things make noises at each other, change behavior" without getting why-they've never had to come up with a language of their own), but it's entirely possible to talk to one if one gets the concept of talking (insert sidebar for "Teaching Language to the Speechless" here), and from there come up with a deal that benefits all sides. Of course, that also means sentient Orphans can network with others not of their own "species"; anyone who's multilingual has pulled off a similar trick. There are no particular limits on the Attributes of sentient Orphans, though many of the less humanoid ones have bizarre Skills due to their nature.
