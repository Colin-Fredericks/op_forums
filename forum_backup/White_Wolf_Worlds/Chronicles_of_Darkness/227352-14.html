    It was left out, because it was assumed to be a minority occurrence-the PCs were not playing as spineless cowards, were not playing as people with Geists that were so disturbing or anoying that you couldn't function with them, and were not playing as Wretched. The three of those are the places were it's said that having a Geist is a problem, but none of those are PCs.

    Like I said, if you want more WoD life Sucks, include it as things external to the Sin-Eater: Instead of making the Sin-Eater Condition inherently Sucky, make them have to deal with problems that sometimes suck.

    sin-Eaters are, at the time, the only Splat that has no inherent Super Natural Angst: Yeah, you've got a head guest that can be annoying or disturbing sometimes, but for the Most Part you can ignore it or negotiate it down to something else, and besides, you're alive and you have Super Powers, and that's better than being dead.

    Introduce major inherent Drawbacks into the default Sin-Eater condition, if you introduce something that would honestly make more than a minority of Sin-Eaters regret making the Bargain, then you introduce Supernatural Angst to the Splat, and Supernatural Angst , that is, "Woe is me, my awesome powers make my life suck" is utterly incompatible with "Yeah, sometimes bad things happen, but in general, life is good"

    So, if you need more of that WoD life suckage, making it completely External to the Sin-Eater-The Sin-Eaters doesn't have anything inherently Sucky about their nature, but sometimes they have to interact with Sucky things or make difficult decisions.

    If you want more incentive for a Sin-Eater to interact with Ghosts, use carrots, not sticks.

    But don't introduce Supernatural Angst to the splat, especially not by making Minority Cases default PCs, because "WoD, now with less Supernatural Angst" is the selling point of Geist: There's no Supernatural Angst, and even with Weird and Creepy stuff going on, in general life is good.

            Sin Eaters do not live in happy unicorn land where everything is just perfect for them. Geist is an extremely Nietzscheian game, where the point is staring at the ultimate fatality of the universe and smiling. The trick is to add those grim dark elements but in a way where people can still laugh at them and have fun. Yes, you see a literal timer clicking down over your child's head and it's much shorter than yours. Other people would mourn the sudden loss of their child. You have the chance to make every second on that clock matter. Do you?

            Unless the Geist's motivations are something to completed I think the best place for them is Virtue and Vice. Instead of whatever the character was before, now relaxing and affirming oneself enough to get willpower involves cowtowing to the Geist. Through playtesting, determine whether or not Sin Eaters should regain willpower through sleeping. Include a Virtuous / Vice-Ridden equivalent merit. People with Geists whose Virtue and Vice are things like, "Give your victims enough time to beg before killing them / sucker punch orphans in the face" are obviously going to have a harder time than someone with tamer and more common ones, representing the particularly crazy Geist without making it mandatory.

            Another good place to introduce drama is the Geist equivalent of Touchstones. One subversion I simultaneously like in concept but feel was underutilized was the fact that Sin Eaters really don't need to fear death. It's simply off the table. However, if we are going down that path then we really need to highlight something else as what's at stake. Making Touchstones attached to integrity similar to Vampire puts them at stake, with the concept of permanently losing them by dying enough times on the table. The trick here is being very careful about what the "sins" are.

            On that note, you know what would be cool? Taking on a Ghost's Anchors and Unfinished Business like Demons do with pieces of someone's life to patch in parts of their Cover. Maybe grant "Finished Business Beats" for both taking Unfinished Business on and for resolving it. Maybe the Sin Eater can only keep one batch of "Unfinished Business" per dot in Psyche. When you gain someone's Unfinished Business you gain some plasm and merits or something to represent the passions of the Ghost helping you and the world strangely accepting that you are the person with that specific unfinished business. When you resolve it, you lose the benefit for having it but completely refill on Plasm. Could replace Ectophagia.

                            Something to keep in mind is that none of the other supernaturals get a choice in the matter (save for Demons, arguably). Mages have the veil ripped from their eyes, Werewolves and Prometheans (and Beasts) are born this way, Vampires, Changelings, and Mummies have it forced on them. Even many Hunters answer the call out of necessity rather than personal desire.

                            Every Sin-Eater chose to be a Sin-Eater. Every one. So right from the start the tone of the story is going to be different from the others, because every problem you run into because of your supernatural-ness becomes a price you are willing to pay. You don't need to have everything be awesome and great and your Geist is your best bud, because that would undermine what is otherwise a pretty strong theme. That regardless of the problems you'll run into down the line, life is worth it.
