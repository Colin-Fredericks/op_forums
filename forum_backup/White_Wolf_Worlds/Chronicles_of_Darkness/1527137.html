      I'm going to exclude hunters and "lesser" templates because otherwise the list becomes very long.

      1. It's the mages. A few of them use magic to twist themselves into things that are horrifically inhuman, by following obsessions born of very human desires. Most of them engage in schemes and projects that are horrifically human in motivation.

      2. Second place: changelings, who drink dreams and walk on feet of fire, but whose experience of the world is ultimately grounded in relatable connections and needs, and who have a healthy enough perspective to choose whether to stay attached or let things go.

      3. Third place: deviants. Their physical transformation varies from divergence to divergence, but the crack in their soul that they all share severely damages their ability to relate to the world on an even keel. Their need to anchor their self-image by their relationships to others swings them between clinging need and deadly venom in ways that overlap with extreme ends of human behavior.

      4. The bound, who are two ghosts in one walking living body. The concerns and motives of the recently-living half are relatable to the average human, moreso than deviants' are, but the psychology of being fused with a geist that has taken in some of the elemental nature of death means they're getting to a similar end point from a more distant starting position. This is where the list starts getting into distinctly inhuman psychologies.

      5. Mummies have an eternal curse of otherworldly gods suppressing their human sentiments and memories with a torrent of raw power and brainwashing decrees intended to make them as close as possible to undead automatons.

      6. Vampires are either all possessed by an alien will without human intelligence, or suffer atavistic urges so overwhelmingly powerful that they dissociate and ascribe them to an external persona. Their sense of relationship to human norms withers without active and constant reinforcement.

      7. Prometheans are like vampires without the sense memory of having once been human. They're dead bodies whose life and consciousness is a byproduct of the reactions of occult humours, also subject to overwhelmingly atavistic urges when those humours fall out of balance, and construct an image of human behavior starting from almost a tabula rasa.

      8. Werewolves have transforming half-spirit bodies which passively snap bones and tear skin in order to painfully reshape the flesh into healthier configurations. They feel a restless, instinctive need to hunt prey and dominate territory, use smell as one of their dominant senses to experience the world, have an inborn understanding of how to convert their bodies into extradimensional spirit-matter and tunnel through to an alter-world, and experience psychological and physical symptoms from failures to balance their lives as intelligent humanoids and devouring spirit-beasts.

      9. Demons have extradimensional minds that experience the world through artificial bodies that fold in on themselves into multiple configurations, who have minute and unflagging conscious control over what are for human beings autonomic bodily responses. They are used to perceiving and manipulating abstract concepts as if they were physical properties, such as their ability to deliberately think thoughts and hold beliefs that are honest or dishonest independent of whether they honestly think or believe those things.

      10. Begotten combine the two-in-one double-identity psychology of the bound with the animalistic instincts and outlook on life of werewolves. At any given time they simultaneously have two independent but interconnected bodies and minds, one in the physical world and one a dream-form in the collective unconscious, except when they use the resonance of their location-heart to fold the collective unconscious on top of the physical world. They experience psychological and physical symptoms from how much trauma they have inflicted on others, which they perceive in a similar manner to how humans perceive eating food. Their power is on some level defined by the rejection of conventional humanity and elemental association with ideas of monstrocity and the inhuman.

          Yeah, hunters with actual legit powers (like the lucifuge) win hands down, with mist minor templates in a clear second place (I'd put Wolf-Blooded as the second place). Ghouls are weird though, I think I'd actually rank them as 'inhuman' as a lot of major templates. That's not to say that major templates aren't human, but that they present a more focused view of humanity.

          So my actual answer is 'no supernatural is more human, it depends on what part of humanity you want to explore'. Vampires are ambition without connection, werewolves are emotion without restraint, changelings are dreams without sanity (at least those are my readings, others will likely have different takes). Depending on if you focus on what they amplify or what they downplay the templates can seem anywhere from incredibly human to inscrutably alien

          Blue is sarcasm.

              Like SL I'm going to ignore Hunters and minor templates. I think I would go:

              1) Sin-Eaters - Yes you've died. Yes you've come back. Yes, you aren't alone. Yes you're now the Ghost Whisperer. But you're also still human, you still live your life (probably).

              2) Deviants- Humans that are broken in a fundamental way and gifted powers. I think their scars, alongside their lack of flexibility (compared to Mages) make them more human than the next splat. However your life is more disrupted by your powers and your scars and your burning need for vengeance than the life of a Sin-Eater.

              3) Mages - Mages have too much power, IMO, to truly be looked at as human, but do not possess the otherworldlyness or inhumanity of those that follow.

              4) Changelings - Changelings, like Deviants were taken and changed and like Deviants have entirely lost the life that was theirs before to this process. However, unlike deviants the changelings have left the world, are fundamentally tied to things beyond the world.

              5) Beasts - Beasts are drawing to a close on the living things that are akin to humanity. Their souls have been devoured and replaced but they still get wet in the rain, still stretch out in a bed to go to sleep, still enjoy a nice medium rare steak.They are only behind changelings because changelings at least have a part of their soul remaining and because Beasts motivations and hungers are more inhuman than those of changelings.

              6) Vampires - Like changelings and deviants vampires were born ordinary every day humans and snatched away from that life and transformed. Unlike the other two they're now dead. We're at fully dead things walking half way through our list.

              7) Mummy - Speaking of dead things walking, oh right, Mummies are the exact opposite of dead things, they're things from which the concept of Death was taken. Like, as weird and as foreign as vampires are, they can still die. Mummies lack that most common unifying trait of everything below them on the list.

              8) Prometheans - Prometheans are a bit of an odd ball in this list. Obviously they start off completely inhuman, have never been human, and their whole gameline is about discovering what it is like to become human. A promethean at the end of his pilgrimage is obviously more human than anything on this list, this position is more of a starting promethean.

              9) Demons - Demons are not human, have never be human, will never be human and the closest they will ever come is playing pretend.

              *) Werewolf (Wildcard) - Werewolves are a wild case in every way. Some werewolves are born what they are, some are born normal humans and transformed. Some are high harmony and thus very human, some are low harmony and incredibly spirit. A high harmony werewolf I would say is probably a 4 (bumping Changelings and everything else up) where as a low harmony werewolf is probably a 7 (bumping Mummy and everything up) and a medium harmony werewolf would probably rank around 5 (unsure how I would rank them vs beasts).

                  You can't rank each splat as if they have one place.

                  Prometheans - depends on where they are in their pilgrimage.

                  Vampires - depends on how much humanity they maintain.

                  Mummies - depends on how much Memory they have.

                  Changelings - depends on their clarity.

                  Mages - depends on their Wisdom.

                  Demon - completely inhuman ranked below all of the above.

                  Deviants, Beasts, I don't know.

                  Bound - all too human even if they happen to be able to talk to the echoes left over by humans. Ranked above most of the above

                  - so on a scale of 0 to 10 -

                  Prometheans at the end of their Pilgrimage ~10 - they have become human, they are have created a soul and become more human than some (eg low integrity humans who have become detached from their humanity).

                  Bound with any Synergy ~ 9 - they are still human, and only technically dead. They feel love and joy and community. And their Geist is an inscrutible companion not themselves.

                  High Clarity/Low Wyrd Changeling ~ 9 - their body and mind has been twisted into something else, but they still grasp what it means to be human, find their tribe and form a family with those experiencing the same trauma.

                  High Wisdom Mage ~ 8, Obsessive beyond mortal norms, and powerful, but with the power checked by their wisdom.

                  High humanity Vampire ~ 6, still in touch with their humanity, eats blood and fakes some human behaviours but can't really experience.

                  High Memory Mummy ~ 6 - they remember being human, but they are not living in the world the remember, those connections to humanity are severed, their experience of time is dramatically altered and their relationship to death and their body is fundamentally altered. Even at their most human they can never regain what they have lost.

                  Bound with Tryanny ~ 3 - they have become entirely focused on control and detachment from their Geist, embracing the darkest part of Death and sacrificing their humanity.

                  Low clarity/high wyrd Changeling 3 - broken mentally and distracted from reality by their deep attachment to something else. They are Lost and may never find their place again.

                  Low humanity Vampire ~ 2 - inhuman to the point where they see humanity as nothing but food and playthings. Even other vampires are pathetic for playing at humanity instead of embracing their nature, yet still driven by human urges to eat, control, seek power.

                  Enraptuded Mage 2 - while still human in some technical sense, they have become entirely consumed by their Fault and detached from the experience of humans.

                  Lich 1 - a Mage who has made themselves something else. Still has some memory of what it was to be human but has actively given that up.

                  Low Memory Mummies 0/1 - Brainwashed undead automotons.

                  Demon 0. Not human, will never be human.

                  (Not sure about werewolves, deviants, or others).

                      Again, including main splats only as all others do-

                      Mage > Deviants > Mummies > Changelings > Vampires > Promethean > Sin Eaters > Beasts > Werewolves > Demons

                      Reasoning:

                      The defined criteria is "how human is the soul or equivalent", considering that a lot of the magical funky stuff, the mental stability and relation to humanity comes from the soul and its expressions- and besides, I like a measurable criteria. Mages has a mostly human soul- it is Awakened, sure, yet at its basis it is still human, as all souls has (in theory) the potential to Awaken. We can see it with the connection of Mages to the human part of the Astral and the fact they have Virtues and Vices, like mortals. Deviants, on the other hand, have a mostly human soul, yet it is scarred/broken, making them unstable. Depending on that stability defines the distance between them and humanity, yet in general their soul is still essential human. The Mummies also have a human soul, yet it is bloated with Sekhem- which is the lifeforce of civilization, which is a human product. They might be slave gods of a forgotten empire, but they are still human as their Memory shows. Changelings are transformed by their experience through the Hedge and Arcadia, sure, but iirc they still have what you would call a soul- a scarred soul bound by contracts with the principles of the world, which transforms their forms, yet a soul nevertheless. The reason they are below Mummies (and not by a huge margin, it is pretty close) is because their soul seems to be more alien, so it was distorted to a higher degree that the Arisen- something was added or removed, while in the case of the Deathless, it is the same essential aspects, just one of those getting emphasized.

                      Moving to the stranger territory, as far as we are concerned Vampires still have a soul or something close enough to it- they are able to cling to Humanity in a manner other supernaturals can't. As it seems, it might be that the Beast is the decay of their own soul- still the same essential components, yet it is quickly degrading if not kept together. Prometheans arise already with only have the raw materials to produce a soul, yet they could only achieve one through their Magnum Opus. Sin Eaters are the point where we definitely don't have a soul- yet their replacement is "close enough" for all purposes- what they have is their own ghost claiming their body after a synthesis with the minor death god, and ghosts, while not being souls, seems to be products of it, or at least its remnants. Beasts, on the other hand, have their soul fully devoured by a astral entity and replaced by it- their body is still essentially human, yet the thing that replaced their soul have no direct relation to humanity. However, goetia are still a product of the Astral, and they seem to reflect the collective human fears as reflected within the collective soul- so goetia are a product of humanity, even if not part of any singular human. Werewolves are the point we simply no longer have a true "soul" equivalent- their spirit and flesh as all one hot mess, which while it could be separated somehow it is still something that you are born with, and is born from the Old Gods of the Shadows where the Pangaeans mixed their Essence into a human Flesh- hence why "acting human" is as bad for them as being too "spirit like". Finally, we have Demons- they have no relation to humanity whatsoever, and they only wear human souls like masks.

                      Now, let's have a bonus round if we are already at it- let's add some major antagonists and secondary splats in here-

                      Hunters > Sorcerers > Atariya > Dreamers > Stigmatics > Occultists > Skin Thieves > Possessed > Infected > Lost Boys > Mage > Alchemists > Heroes > Immortals > Psychic Vampires > Eaters of the Dead > Fractals > Children of 7th Generation > Dhampirs > Deviants > Everlasting > Mummies > Deceived > Changelings > Vampires > Shuankhsen > Promethean > Fetches > Sin Eaters > Absent > Beasts > Reapers > Purified > Claimed > Werewolves > Tremere > Demons > Insatiable > Slashers > Geryo > Carriers > Strix > Hosts > Huntsmen

                      Hunter is pretty self explanatory, they are not entirely human but they are "close enough"- probably having their soul scarred by the supernatural but not beyond that. Sorcerers might practice strange rites which manipulate Sekhem, but Sorcery still seems to be a product of humanity and as such its property. Atariya might be blessed by Luck, which modifieds their fabric to a degree, but it seems to be less of an alien intervention and more of a blessing of some sorts. Dreamers have their minds messed up by some supernatural manipulation, but the soul itself seems intact. Stigmatics more directly have a scar upon their soul, which probably changes it to a degree but their soul still resembles more the common Sleeper than the averaged Awakened. Occultists are in a similar situation, yet their soul was manipulated more directly by a spiritual entity instead of a mark which allows the soul to develop its natural capabilities.

                      Skin Thieves, again, are marked by the supernatural, even though to a larger degree than the previous forms as the transformation to beasts seems to shaping their mentality. The Possessed still host an entity within their body, which considering the origin of Infernals seems to be relatively human (fully understanding Vice), which means that their taint is heavy yet still very human. The Infected seems to bear some sort of a Contagion strain which modifies their bodies, minds and souls, but they are still human enough as the taint is external to them instead of a complete alteration- but still bellow Stigmatics and Occultists as it is more of a gaping wound instead of a scar. Lost Boys, or the "child friendly" version of Deviant, might be radically altered in terms of their physiology, yet their soul seems to be maintained stable (otherwise, they would have gone Renegade, and the distance between a "heavily altered body which makes the soul strain" and a broken one is skies and earth)

                      Moving below the Mage threshold, we finally come upon entities which have their soul deviated from the mortal scheme to a huge degree frim the mortal state. First we have Alchemists, who might resemble Mages in many regards, yet the fact that they are driven by inspiration to realms beyond what humanity should access means that their souls must have been heavily modified (as it is the goal of alchemy). Heroes come next- they are definitely touched by the Astral, but they don't experience the same Devouring and displacement- so their souls "come out different", yet they are still "human". Immortals are up next- unlike sorcerers, those guys heavily modify their soul to rely on Sekhem, transforming their existence to survive beyond the human boundary yet without reaching the limit of Decreeing their soul like the Deathless. Psychic Vampires seems to have strayed far from humanity, being the closest to a "major splat" among the Hurt Locker members, yet they still relay heavily on a human connection and do not seem to have replaced their soul in any manner. The Eaters of the Dead seems to come up next- again, they have modified their soul structure in order to eat ghosts and express Dread Powers, which is why among the immortal beings they are very far, but still they are human to a degree.

                      Moving to the next tier, we have those children of the supernatural who are born different, yet seem to be human- at least partially. Fractals are the children of the Unchained, so while they are humans they clearly come from an unnatural origin. The Children of the Seventh Generation are close after- where it is important to note that yes, the Lucifuge are monsters, and without joining the conspiracy they quickly develop Dread Powers after the Becoming. Dhampirs are below Cot7G because they are born from the dead, and all of their powers are based around how unnatural they are to the world and they can degrade into the Ale which shows how much of a potential horror they are. Just between Deviants and Mummies we have the Everlasting (which I would like to remind the world that they exist). Little is known about those Immortals, but which is known shows that they are definitely more inhuman than Deviants, having their soul heavily tuned to Sekhem, yet while they proclaim themselves as gods they seem to be less modified compared to the Arisen existence. In between Mummy (human soul heavily modified by the divine) and Changeling (dramatic alteration to the soul by eldritch powers), we have the Deceived- who are like the Arisen only with the shard of an ancient god clinging to their soul.

                      Now we are starting to move to the very inhuman category- Shuankhsen might have remnants of a human soul, but the vast majority of it was devoured, leaving them with less "soul components" than Vampires. Fetches are below Prometheans, as while they are created from humans they are basically echoes and shadows of the human existence- making them similar to ghosts, but more "accurate" than the distorted images caused by death, putting them above the Bound. The Absent are, well, ghosts, yet without a body- so they are less human than Sin Eaters yet closer to humanity than the astral aggregate of human concepts which is the Horror. Reapers are still ghosts, yet by donning the deathmask they lose a lot of their humanity and become similar to goetia ion concept- a collective definition of death, and without a body they drop below Beasts. Purified outright transforms their souls into being spirit-like, so while they have attachments to humanity they become very detached from it after the ritual, just above the Claimed who have their souls fully melded with inhuman spirits (who, at best case scenario, give a Sitzen Uzu like scenario where the human mind still has some control, and at worst is the common Claimed horror).

                      Moving to the completely inhuman part of this chart, Tremeres are soulless beings who constantly consume souls- yet the fact that they have a human body puts them a bit above Demons, by a small margine. Insatiable lose their soul completely and have it replaced by some Astral deviation which does not represent a human nightmare as much as a violent territory which actively manifest itself within flesh- so at least the Unchained have a soul to grasp on and Werewolves are part human, they are far from it. Slashers are even further down the line- their bodies has either killed or ejected their souls, and they have nothing but a void and desire for murder. Geryo are the union of Pangaean Essence and human nightmares that do not need the human flesh to function, and are even corrupted by the Contagion- so at this point, they are clearly straying from humanity. Carriers are clearly inhuman horrors, yet they strongly believe themselves that they are humans, so we could say that it puts them above some of the other horrors at the bottom of the list. The Strix are very much inhuman- yet they can make vampires and have some understanding of human experience, allowing them to wear human flesh like suits, so it puts them as somewhat being able to understand human desires- which is not much, but better than the Hosts, for they are simply programmed by an urge to take upon human flesh and fulfil the desire of a shattered god. Yet at the end of the list, we have the Huntsmen, who predated humanity, despise humanity and when forced to exist see each moment as lingering pain they just want to get over with.

                      While I am relatively sure about the "primary set", there are ones in the secondary set where I am less sure where to put them, so I may change my mind later (and potentially add a few other critters to this list because why not).
