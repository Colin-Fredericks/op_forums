                I missed the early part of this thread, so some or all of what I'm saying here may already be covered.

                I suspect that 2e will be redefining the origins of geists; first, put a stake in the heart of the "geists are ghosts that ate spirits" theory, because the 2e notion that ghosts and spirits exist in separate Twilights makes that idea nearly unworkable. If you must keep it, shift it from "ghost who ate spirit" to "ghost who ate chthonian"; but my suspicion is that 2e's geists will be Rank 3+ ghosts who are evolving toward an inhuman state of being, and who's part of the Bargain is to reconnect with humanity: from the geist's perspective, his bond-mate serves as a Touchstone.

                I wouldn't mind the player's geist having its own Aspiration; I wouldn't mind if the Archetypes get recast as a third Anchor alongside Virtue and Vice and representing the geist's motive in offering the bargain. I wouldn't even mind if the geist provides one or two Synergy Breaking Points that stem from the mortal host doing things that the geist vehemently opposes. But that's as coercive as I'd want to go. Sin-Eaters aren't the Possessed from Inferno; the relationship between geist and mortal is a lot more... mutually beneficial, and cooperative. Symbiotic.

                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                I'd like to see more done with the boundary of life and death. What is agency; astral projection, akasha, etc. With the concept of ghosts, I'd like to see a focus on the duality of Fetters vs Liberty. We are bound by our fetters, and that's not a bad thing. Fetters keep us attached to reality. Sensation is a fetter, Sanity is a fetter, as are people, places and beliefs. But we are not honest about what binds us. We seek unlimited freedom, but are we honest about what makes us free.

                                Could I pick up the fetters of a young girl ghost, taking her place in the world until what still binds her to world is freed?
                                Can the powers that I gain be expressed in the terms of freedom - free to walk through walls, free to ignore the need to breath/eat, etc.

                                            Speaking as the guy who wrote the Twilight Network section in 1e, yeah, that was all it was ever really meant to be. There was a period, I think, where the old nWoD was really into old 1930s hobo codes, and the Twilight Network was sort of an outgrowth of that. 2nd Edition will talk a little bit about ways Sin-Eaters might leave secret signs or messages for her, but don't expect it to be a major element with a Fancy Proper Noun. Like most things in Sin-Eater culture, it's ad hoc, invented whole cloth, forgotten, rediscovered, forgotten again, and independently reinvented later.

                                                Originally posted by Rater202 View Post


                                                The lack of supernatural angst is a feature, not a bug. Don't fuck with it, that's the appeal. "You made a deal. The person making the deal is so desperate that the deal is stacked heavily in your favor. You don't die and you get awesome powers. In exchange, your head guest would like it if you occasionally do things for it, and you're totally allowed to tell it to shut up and think of something else if you don't want to do what it asked you to do.
                                                I've never looked at the Bargain as inherently stacked heavily in the favor of the Sin-Eater. I've always thought that it should vary from character to character. Sure, one gets the advantage of a Geist who is content to tag along and live vicariously, but I see that as the exception rather than the rule. Others want more. I see it as less, "Please, Mr. Dying Mortal, don't die on me yet. I'll give you another chance at life, and super powers, just please, let me tag along with you. Please, accept my offer. Pretty please?" I've always seen it more as "Ah, here you are, Mr. Dying Mortal, about to shuffle off this mortal coil. I hope you did everything you needed to in life... Oh, you're not ready yet? You still have things you'd like to do? Unfinished business among the living, perhaps? Well, that's just fine. You see, I can give you the chance to take care of that. I can give you another chance. In exchange, you do something for me. I scratch your back, you scratch mine. Now, here are my terms. Take it or leave it." I've always looked at it as the Geist holding most of the chips in the Bargain, and that it is the lucky Sin-Eaters that get ones that only want to ride around in the mortal world for awhile. I think that the Bargain should have consequences beyond just a second chance for the Sin-Eater. I don't think that a Sin-Eater should get to come back to life, hear the Geist say, "Okay, now it's your turn to hold up your end of our Bargain," and have the Sin-Eater respond with, "Fuck off, I already got mine, so I don't need to hold up my end, and there's not a thing you can do about it," to which the Geist responds, "Crap, you're right. Umm... Please..." I never really ran it that way. For instance, one of my characters was a lazy stoner who made an agreement with a Geist called Idle Hands who had been a mass murderer in life. In exchange for being allowed to live again, he agreed to the Geist's Bargain, which involved killing one person a month. After he was back, he blew off the Bargain, and went back to doing what he had been doing. At the end of the first month, Idle Hands took control, and forced his end of the Bargain. After that, the Sin-Eater realized that his Bargain was actually binding, and so now, the world's laziest man has become a Reaper, desperately searching for someone who deserves it to remove from the world before his Geist takes matters into its own hands and picks someone at random. If the Bargain has no teeth, then that concept would be totally invalid.
