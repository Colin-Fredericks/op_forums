                            And if that really bugged you (it doesn't at all bother me, but I could see it bothering some folks, or being problematic in crossover play), here's a quick house rule for you: use the supernatural being's Tolerance Trait as its Withstand trait, or the usual Withstand trait, whichever is higher. This still gives more potent supernatural beings a better chance at resisting, though it may be unnecessary—chances are, higher Supernatural Tolerance means higher Attributes anyway, but just in case.

For the Master of Mind, an entire world of thinking beings is within her grasp. Power? No Sleeper knows true Awakened power. The world's most powerful leaders know nothing of the sort. An assassin kills a target. A supernatural monster eviscerates a target. A Time Master can shatter that target's history or cast it through time into the future. The Master of Space creates not barriers but instead rips you from space entirely, leaving you unable to interact with the world from which you were removed.

                                        There is power, and then there is magic.

                                            Originally posted by branford View Post
                                            That sounds deliciously sociopathic, inhuman and monstrous. If the entirety of Mage 2e fully conveys this mood and is truly backed by the new mechanics, I have no doubt the book will exceed our already high expectations.
                                            People have often asked "Is Mage a horror game?" Yes. It always has been. 1E did it, too, but 2E's changes help reinforce that, in my opinion. The horror of Mage is a slow-burn kind, the slide into decadence as far removed from the life of the average person as is the life of a blood-drinking immortal, or a lupine lycanthrope. Where you realize that you've become the thing you hated, working your fingers to the bone for a heartless tyrant of the office—a creature only concerned with his own influence, his own well-being, using others like puppets to serve his own ends. You'd have done anything then to keep your family fed. Now you've all but lost contact with the ex and your kid. You use people for what little you need from them, heedless of their petty little lives and petty little concerns. Of what importance is a struggle to make ends meet when you grapple with the secrets of the universe? It's easy to forget where you came from when you literally wield the power of a god.

                                            Not that Mage is solely a game of horror, or that every master becomes an uncaring asshole. But the potential is there. The sheer power in what you can do distances you from mortal life just as much as the Embrace, the First Change, whatever. So yes, you could use Mage for that, and yes, it will exceed your expectations. This thing is a work of beauty.
