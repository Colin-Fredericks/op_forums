    Tips for creating a great character

    08-21-2014, 06:29 PM
    I'm a huge fan of Mummy: the Curse but haven't yet had the privilege to be a player in a Mummy Chronicle. This inexperience as a player led to an awkward moment the other day when I was trying to sell one of my friend on Mummy and they asked me the million dollar question: "How can you make a fleshed out character when they can barely remember who they are?"

    I tried to explain how an Arisen's Decree, Guild, Judge, and Cult all hint at their true nature but they're convinced that they just reinforce the character's archetype and not their background and personality. I was about to hit the old Core book again but though it might be quicker and more thought provoking to ask the forum their opinions on the matter. Can anyone give some advice about creating a great character for Mummy: the Curse so if nothing else I can at least show my friend you can make a character who isn't just an archetype in Mummy?

      Well, they still have a Virtue and Vice: that will determine a great deal of their personality alone (have your character pursue their First Task, Decree, Virtue, and Vice single-mindedly, and simultaneously). There's also the Cult. A Cult's rituals, behaviors, and general attitude toward a Mummy will shape what kind of person the Mummy is at low Memory. Granted, a lot of that will probably be ego-swelling what with all of the worship and kowtowing.

      Finally, and this requires Storyteller help, how does a Mummy react to Sekhem loss and resets? Especially Sekhem loss due to certain behavior. That will help them understand their Judges' plan for them, but what if they don't like the direction the Judge seems to be pointing? Say a Mummy does something nice for someone, but then suffers Sekhem loss. How would that make the Mummy feel? Would your Arisen take the opportunity to help somebody again in the future after that?

      Finally, remember that Mummy is a game of discovering who your character was. Starting the game, you won't have a good idea of who your character is. In that way, it's sort of like every Descent is a chance to be a new person, rather than the one you were.

      Personally, I'm of the opinion that a truly great character only manifests during play, and no matter how interesting or well-written the backstory, it's a poor substitute for characterisation.

              At Memory 3, a character remembers their past Descent. Players should be fine to decide what happened there. When pitching it to a player who really likes to design their character's history, ask them to think of it this way. Your character has lived many, many lives. You can decide what the most recent one was like. The rest are a mystery, but you still effectively have a single Arisen lifetime (so to speak) to write up. Every dilemma, event, emotional crisis, and defining moment that could be used to create a great character normally can easily be set into that most recent Descent, especially when coupled with the eternal and never-changing elements like the character's Decree and Judge.

              So, basically, think of it less like losing control of your backstory, and more like the GM creates background elements to layer on top of a backstory you get to create. You do have holes in your memory, most important of those being that you can't remember your origins, but it doesn't mean you have no sense of who your character is. That previous descent can be used to create all the depth and complexity you could ever want in your character, and then you get to begin play.

              Thankfully, I didn't have much of a problem with this aspect of the game when pitching it to my group. Most of my players rarely write more than 2-3 paragraphs of background information for their characters anyway, and only one of my players found the loss of control over their character a little bit troubling. They came around pretty quickly with the above explanation. After all, it's how you actually play the character that counts, and in Mummy the GM has the ability to challenge you on a roleplaying level by not just presenting you with a crisis in the present, but also by showing you how you dealt with such events in the past. And if your present character disagrees with his own past decisions... well, that's just when the real fun begins, right?

                      Affinities are built into a Mummy's soul and so presumably carry over from Descent to Descent, but as written have plenty of reasons to go dormant, be liquidated, or otherwise disappear.

                      Utterances, on the other hand, are rote magic that have to be studied. It is presumably quite easy to have and later lose access to an Utterance.

                      In either case, there are a variety of reasons why a starting character might "only" have the powers they begin with, and lots of fun reasons for why a character might demonstrate abilities in flashbacks that they don't possess in their current life.
