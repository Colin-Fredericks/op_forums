    Finally finishing Tappan's character sheet:



    Note that this character sheet only applies to Tappan when the Twin is in control; the real Arkwright is a normal human who is more likely to pass out in a physical conflict (which, unfortunately, also gives Twin an opening to overtake their body).


    Attributes: Intelligence 6, Wits 5, Resolve 7,

    Strength 2, Dexterity 4, Stamina 3,

    Presence 6, Manipulation 3, Composure 3

    Skills:

    Academics 3, Crafts (Edomite Rune Magic) 2, Investigation 3, Occult (Edom Lore) 5, Science 2

    Athletics 2, Brawl 2, Stealth 1, Weaponry (Ritual Knife) 2

    Empathy 2, Intimidation (Misanthropy Incarnate) 4, Streetwise 3, Subterfuge 3


    Merits:

    Encyclopedic Knowledge (Lore of the Kings) **, Interdisciplinary Specialty (Edom Lore) *, Relentless **, Mentor (Vulshoth) ***, Resources ***,

    (while he is the High Priest of Vulshoth, the Twin does not have a proper organization to have Status in, as the worshipers of the Kings are too disorganized, at the moment. What powers he would have gained from Mystery Cult Initiation are covered by his Dread Powers.)

    Potency: 5

    Dread Powers: Ban of Power (refreshment ritual, grants 2 Dread Powers), Beastmaster (Least Edomites*), Blast (Dissolution of Matter), Drain, Influence (Qliphotic Energies ***), Implant Mission, Madness and Terror, Maze

    * Actually, the Twin can summon much bigger things that last longer in our universe, but these require specialized rituals that, in fact, much of the Twin's waking hours are dedicated to preparing and carrying out. The creatures summoned by Beastmaster are the few species he can manage without all of that mucking about with rituals and alien chanting, a chaotic arrangement of animal-like monsters that resemble mutated versions of vermin with redundant organs, tentacles, and the like. They vanish after about a day.

    Ban: The Twin must drink a specially prepared serum of pulped brain matter every month (counting the time Tappan is in control) or lose his access to Dread Powers until he does drink the serum. The preparation, naturally, being a ritualized murder in which he slices the donor in seven sacred spots with his ritual knife, but Vulshoth isn't picky about the choice of victim, and the ritual only takes about ten minutes if the victim is fighting back.

    Ban: Tappan is somewhat aware of what his sibling does (not the least because the Twin bullies him into living a normal-esque life to hide his activities, or occasionally helping when the Twin needs to work in a constructive social manner to get a particular resource), and fights back. Whenever the Twin is knocked unconscious, tired, or frightened (the last of which is not easy to do, seeing as how the High Priest has generally seen the worst any universe has to offer), Tappan roles his own Resolve + Composure pool of 6 versus the Twin's own. This means that the Twin can be active for days given how his pool is nearly twice that, but even Tappan losing to the Twin (assuming that the Twin does not roll an exceptional success) inflicts a -2 dice penalty for the rest of the scene to the Twin's actions as he fends off his brother's control. The Twin's loss (which he is known to do on purpose when laying low), means Tappan rests control and the Twin sleeps...at least until Vulshoth calls his servant once more...

                        Here's his first write-up
                        Wormflesh
                        The worms crawl in and the worms crawl out
                        They'll eat your guts and spit it out
                        Put the blood of your enemy in the worms' den
                        and we shall see what happens then






                        Most musical historians place the Hearse Song as we know it as originating from at least WWI. Some more fragmented reports of the story attribute the song to British soldiers in the Crimean War. There's even another song they won't admit is the same emerging from the 1810s. .

                        But the song is even older than that.

                        The true song is known to few people (and we can thank many brave hunters for that), but its original form is a description of the ritual to summon the great beast, Wormflesh. The details can get tricky, but the gist is that you must find at least a mass of earthworms equal to at least your own weight. Then, you must give them the richest compost you can find and mix in the concoction of sacred, or perhaps infernal, "herbs", before adding the final, and most important ingredient: the blood of your worst enemy.

                        And as you sing the song and the worms make their gruesome feast, the worms will learn to taste your hatred, bathe in it and form the monster. That monster's first victim will be you. But that's not where it ends.


                        Virtue: Hateful; Vice: Trustworthy
                        Aspiration: Destroy everything their target loves, eventually kill target
                        Mental Attributes: Intelligence 5, Wits 8, Resolve 6
                        Physical Attributes: Strength 3, Dexterity 7, Stamina 4
                        Social Attributes: Presence 10, Manipulation 8, Composure 3
                        Ban 1: must flee sunlight
                        Ban 2: Can only use Contagious on rotting corpses
                        Bane 1: soil untouched by any life for 10 years
                        Bane 2: The blood of someone whose trust he has betrayed
                        Skills: Occult 3, Crafts 3 (trapped passages), Athletics 3, Brawl 2, Larceny 3, Survival 2, Weaponry 1, Animal Ken 3 (scavengers), Empathy 3 (Vices), Intimidation 5, Persuasion 4, Subterfuge 3(spreading rumors)
                        Merits: Striking Looks (Revolting, 00); Safe Place (00); Staff (00; Wormlings Spies [stealth] and Trap Builders [Crafts]); Grappling (000); Pusher; Spin Doctor; Fast-Talking (0000); Double-Jointed
                        ]Potency: 8
                        Willpower: 17
                        Size: 5
                        Initiative: 10
                        Armor: 0/0
                        Defense: 10
                        Speed: 15 burrowing / 13 above ground
                        Health: 9
                        Weapons/Attacks
                        Grapple 0B Melee
                        Dread Powers: Numen(Price of Power(Hallucination, Implant Mission)), Discorporate, Swarm, Hunter's Senses (target and immediate sympathetic connections), Numen(Price of Power(Mortal Mask, Aggressive Meme)), Contagious (transmits through forcing worms into orifices for at least 1 sustained minute)*, Dead Flesh (00)

                        *This version of Contagious creates the brief nightmares known as wormlings, vaguely humanoid mounds of worms slavishly loyal to Wormflesh. Here's a single entity
                        Best At (5 dice): spying unseen, suffocating victims, creating underground lairs
                        Worst at (Chance die): fine manipulation, bravery against their bane, communicating with anyone other than Wormflesh
                        Aspiration: please their master, frolic in fresh corpses
                        Willpower/scene: 2
                        Bane: worm-killing pesticides
                        Dread Powers: Swarm, Unbreakable, Innocuous

                                                        Re: Wormflesh

                                                        Looking over your write up - here are a few notes -

                                                        Stats for the Horror

                                                        I stick primarily to the format for Horrors as presented in CofD (pg. 140 - 148). I while it does not explicitly say Horrors start with 1 dot in each attribute, I still use that as my starting point.
                                                        As far as Mortal Remains and Dread Powers, the format is somewhat different between the two sources. CofD treats Dread Powers like Numina (to the point that they are mostly interchangeable), with each Dread Power having a specific effect and only a few of them having ranks. With Mortal Remains rules, Dread Powers have varying ranks and costs, and operate more like Merits. So they are not quite compatible, and you may have to do some tinkering to get the powers to work correctly. Feel free to go over this this thread and snag any of my home brewed Dread Powers if you see any that would work for this creature.

                                                        How much Blood?

                                                        I don't think you need to be as specific with the details of the summoning ritual. Stick to the main points - Compost Rich Soil, The blood of a Hated Foe, and the Summoners weigh in worms. Try to make the text evocative enough to inspire plot hooks.
                                                        For the stats itself, the main considerations would be how will it oppose the player characters. Is it a physical bruiser that pummels and drowns in a squirming tide? Is it a subtle terror that warps perceptions and drives it victims mad? Does it have human patience and cunning, or is it a near-mindless thing? So on and so forth. Once you know what its main gimmicks and tactics will be , the selection of skills and abilities will fall into place.

                                                        Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)

                                                        Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
