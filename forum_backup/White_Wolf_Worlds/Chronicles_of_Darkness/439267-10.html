    Originally posted by Pierrot le Fou View Post
    To continue with the above example, another concern is that with merits like it and Know the Territory mental and social characters now are able to "double up", so to speak: i.e. using their non-physical skills and abilities to become combat powerhouses. This feels more apt for a game like DnD or Exalted (switching out your attack skill, using certain special martial arts) rather than NWoD to me. While there have always been things like Fighting Finesse (though that was with another physical attribute) and supernatural activation rolls, I think these merits are much more extreme. Whereas before if your fast-talking con man wanted to be useful in combat he had to pick up a gun like everyone else (with the appropriate expenditure of dots and experience if he wanted to be anything like good at it), now he can take a three to five dot merit and with just the attributes he undoubtedly already has be perhaps the most, and definitely one of the most, invaluable members of the party in that sphere.
    I disagree. I believe there should be ways for mental and social characters to help in a combat situation that do not involve actual combat, such a planning, dirty tricks, intimidation, etc., so I really like Merits supporting that kind of play. Physical character are also good at certain non-combat tasks, so why should non-physical character be bad at combat?

    Also, I don't think "Know the Territory" steps on the toes of actual combat characters, as it actually increases their combat capability, creating situations where it is easier to win. It is not as if the Merit lets you substitute mental for physical skills to let you inflict damage with the Academics skill, enabling the librarian to overshadow physical combat characters. If I were playing a combat focussed character, I would really like to have a party member with this Merit.

    Finally, I understand your concern for balance, but some of these Merits are balanced by that fact that not everyone should logically have them. If one if your characters is a military tactician it is no wonder that he will increase combat survivability. But when a player wants to buy "Know the Territory" for a character without military, policy or other tactical background (gang warfare?) and no other good reasons to get it, I would not allow it.

        Originally posted by Penta View Post
        I disagree. I believe there should be ways for mental and social characters to help in a combat situation that do not involve actual combat, such a planning, dirty tricks, intimidation, etc., so I really like Merits supporting that kind of play. Physical character are also good at certain non-combat tasks, so why should non-physical character be bad at combat?
        For one, non-physical characters should be bad at combat if they do not put their points into skills or attributes related to it. This is a horror game, and the mechanics that enforce limitations and sacrifice even during character creation are excellent features to the system. Secondly I feel that intelligence or charisma do tend to have a place everywhere in the hands of a creative player. I support the idea of mechanics to that end (so that unlike many systems non-combat options are more than just left 100% up to the player), but the simple fact is that many of these merits have direct combat utility that, by the investment of a few merit dots, turn a social character into a weapon. Cohesive Unit and Defender both give your team and yourself massive boosts in combat capability that lack a sensible in game source. In a combat scenario the librarian might well outshine the soldier, if only to the mass of dice he adds to an scenario.

        Why not just add in a basic stunting system? Tricks. taunting, misdirection, etc. would already work off of basic skills. The social tank would use a turn to trick someone and gain a bonus while the soldier just gets two turns attacking (for example). It's like aiming, already part of the system, but tied into skills so other types of characters can be useful. At the same time it creates that utility without requiring devoting merit dots. Doing that decreases utility in some ways. Now the ONLY way a social character can be good in combat is by dumping all his merit dots down one tree.

                                                            A few additional things I discovered:
                                                            Zanshin (Iajutusu 1) is a one dot merit that gives +2 to +5 to avoiding ambushes, compared to danger sense which is a flat 2 for 2. Sure the prereqs are a lot stricter but that's still a huge buff. Also, combining it with Defensive Combat (weaponry) allows a character to benefit triply off the one skill.
                                                            Trained Bite (Kino Mutai 1) adds quite a bit of damage. It's bashing, true, and you have to initiate a grapple first, but there are easy ways to avoid both drawbacks. Specifically, Clinch Strike to bite on the first round, and any type of supernatural with a lethal bite or just taking the fourth dot of the merit.
                                                            Stronger than You (Strength Performance 4) At first I thought this was just a nice alternative to Martial Arts 5, but on closer inspection it seems like it applies to attacks made with weapons as well, making it vastly more powerful. Unarmed a character with this merit can deal a lot of damage, give him a fireaxe and it's a minimum of six lethal on a success.
