    Originally posted by Sikander View Post
    I can't tell if "wrapping pieces of it as a mantle around her" is supposed to be a procedure or a poetic flourish.
    Almost certainly not a poetic flourish, given the verbiage is "except by" rather than the more ambiguous "without" — it's a thing they have to do rather than a thing that happens.

    Does it take an action? Does it show? Is it more like a crude bio-hazard suit made of leaves and junk, or more like an aura of "special effects" perceptible to those who can pierce the Mask, like a Court Mantle?
    It presumably takes as long as it takes and looks the way it looks using the method the Queen uses and is made of physical pieces of the Hedge, albeit with the broadened definition of physicality available in the Hedge. Whatever the result, the functional outcome is as subject to the Mask as any other fae thing and means you will not be seeing a Goblin Queen out in the world in a form that is visibly inhuman from a distance.

    Could the Queen form the mantle - however that is supposed to work - in the Hedge proper, enter the limen, stay there indefinitely, and still have her whole Wyrd score in hours of survival in the mortal world beyond the limen?
    My read would be that each constructed mantle works for one visit outside, and whatever normal rate of decay applies to the materials within the limen, but yes, they could linger in the limen just as one can hang out in the front entrance of their house with their coat on without going outside.

    And what actually happens when that clock runs out?
    My impulse is that the Hedge pulls them back in through the pieces they covered themselves in, in a similar phenomenon to Hedgeways that are more events than physical thresholds, given that there's no described effects for a Goblin Queen being outside of the Hedge without a mantle

    If a Goblin Queen just moved to a limen and stayed there, I assume her castle wouldn't just grow in the limen, but would her hobgoblin retainers try to pack it up and move it there? Could they succeed?
    No. Possibly they or her subjects would leverage the obligations of her station to wheedle her into dealing with her business back home — remember that the station does not exist in a vacuum.

    I also find from The Hedge that anyone can potentially become a Goblin Queen, and the two examples are a vampire (which is fine) and a mage. That second case raises a question, because mages don't track Touchstones any more than blue-book mortals do, so how did that happen?
    Possibly a bad choice of example that you can reasonably ignore the implications of unless you feel like making something of it. Possibly Fate shenanigans leveraging the same channels that allow a mortal child to become a Goblin Queen to transfer the Destiny-adjacent mantle of power to a mage.

    If a something-else becomes a Goblin Queen, is this a case where we should construe "Wyrd" strictly, or should strange Queens be able to use their Supernatural Tolerance to go home for a visit?
    The same sidebar that has those two non-changeling-non-mortal examples explicitly says to use the character's Supernatural Tolerance in place of Wyrd where appropriate.

                Originally posted by Bernese View Post
                what exactly are true fae anyway? they don't really fit with anything else in chronicles of darkness.
                In practical terms, they're the guiding intelligence behind the set of Titles, Regalia, Contracts, and other assets that collectively seek to acquire as much of reality as possible (with specific preferences varying by Fae), which leads them into conflict with changelings, hobgoblins, mortals, other monsters, and each other in the pursuit of their desires.

                In the "standard model" of the wider Chronicles, mages who've studied the Hedge identify it with the barrier they need to overcome to meditate from dreams or conscious thought into the deeper realms of the Astral, and its ability to lie to Mage Sight and apply Goblin Debt for released Paradoxes indicate it has some conceptual overlap with the Abyss, which of course suggests some conceptual overlap between Arcadia and the other part of the cosmology where mythic bastards bought their way into heaven, but for the most part the True Fae are the current rulers of Arcadia, who according to legend are a colonizing force who took over the land that gave them an endlessly complex collection of rules, manifested their greed as a hungry bramble laid over the land where dreams are found, and allowed them to pour their destinies into the natives to help fulfill their desires.

                They're gods who traded for their place in the land of miracles. They're a society of extremely powerful magical beings who each have enough influence on the force of reciprocity empowering them that exploiting the times when they make a poor decision is the best way to deal lasting damage to them.The ultimate nature of what they are is up in the air, such that most of the terminology for the core continuum of otherworlds in Changeling is self-referential and only descriptive at the level of end results — Faerie as it exists doesn't serve a singular purpose except insofar as it serves the individual wants of individual Fae, which are frankly all over the place with the only unifying thread being that every Fae wants something. The Strangers don't intrinsically represent or reflect anything except their own egoes

                        Originally posted by Sikander View Post
                        Should a strange Denizen subtract Supernatural Tolerance from the successes required for a Hedge navigation chase? I assume non-Denizen strangers don't.
                        Yes on both counts.

                        Does a changeling who becomes a Denizen stop subtracting current Clarity from the Hedge's Initiative? (Hedge Denizen says that the changeling is no longer a changeling, which is why Court Mantle is suspended while the Condition persists.) If not, should a strange Denizen subtract Clarity analog from the Hedge's Initiative?
                        Depends on how you want non-native Hedge Denizens to work in your game. If former changelings ought to be distinguished from hobgoblins who used to be other things or were always what they are, then sure, let them keep the minor advantage; if you intend to have former changelings be indistinguishable from other hobgoblins, then don't. Given the way Hedge Navigation works, the practical outcome amounts to whether or not the character qualifies for an additional 1-3 successes off their required total on the outside chance that subtracting their Clarity (or equivalent) from 10 results in a number below their normal Initiative.

                                                        Originally posted by Horodrigo View Post
                                                        Just finished scanning trough some threads but still didn't find much about the following:
                                                        I am reasonably certain that Pandora's Gift is not exempt from the universal drawback of Contracts and that the reference to being able to kill a Huntsman is an artifact of a previous draft of the game where Huntsmen worked differently.

                                                        I see no reason for the material not to count as food in the case of a food-based product, though it's worth noting that the verbiage of the Contract indicates that, like the 1e Contract of the same name, Pandora's Gift can create things from nothing more than the target's desire — the thing the target has to touch is a material or tool that the changeling will use in the crafting, and if a power that removes the need for tools is used then the target need only touch the changeling.

                                                        Also, is there any way for getting more Chrysalis forms? Like new ones or replacing old ones for new ones?

                                                        I kinda feel that all that falls under homebrew territory, but would love suggestions anyway.
                                                        Firmly within the realm of per-table decisions. This is the gameline to allow for various means of trading off alternate forms or buying weird trinkets to add on; a relatively inexpensive Merit to allow one to e.g. take a point of Goblin Debt X times per story to change out one's alternate forms wouldn't be out of the ordinary, nor would a seeming bonus to allow workarounds (e.g. None More Dark Publishing's Book of Seemings provides an Elemental bonus for Chrysalis allowing them to use the highest traits and their choice of Size by combining their two animal forms).
