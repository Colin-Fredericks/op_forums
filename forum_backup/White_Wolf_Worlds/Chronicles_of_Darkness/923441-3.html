        Technically all the city states are independent. Philip and Alexander are merely the leaders of the League.

        Anyway, I'd think the AKD would probably be in both cities. Networks of Greek aristocrats weren't restricted to individual city states. I mean, a lot of what we know of Spartan culture comes from Athenian aristocrats who had friends in Sparta.

        If you're looking for a Spartan tradition for inspiration, I'd look up the Crypteia. There's some dispute over how real it was, but the idea at least is that annually young Spartan men would be sent out into the countryside at night to attack the Helot population (basically the serfs). That sounds not totally unlike a vigil.

                so- about the Three Kingdoms of Darkness:

                The time of the Three Kingdoms is a violent Era. People fight and die in the name of heroism, as warlords wage war against one another. There is a reason why the hunters of the Era are described as "bands of brothers" tied by bonds stronger than blood. The Mandate of Heaven rules above all, and those who serve it thrive while those who abuse it fall. That civil war is not done for one's own sake- but in the name of Heaven, and people do some terrible thing in the name of personal survival and heroism as long as it may be justified by the Mandate. In many ways, such an era of selfish heroism could describe as one of the aspects of what an hunter must deal with during the Vigil- and that takes it to the extreme. Even in modern times hunters wonder how far the Vigil takes them- just where the lines are crossed? Just when something is right or wrong? Using the Vigil as a justification for one's own terrible deeds is how most of the hunters survive today- but now, if that Vigil is not just a man-made concept but is a duty granted by Heaven itself, who are you to judge yourself for actions Heaven decreed that must be done? Yes, this is a violent time, heroic time and a very, very Vigilant Time.

                For the hunters of China, the Vigil means operating under the Mandate of Heaven- as long as your actions are done according to the will of above, you are just. Where modern hunters debt if killing a child witch is moral or not, the hunters of that Era may butcher an whole village and without blinking, mourning afterward for what they had to do in Heaven's name. This is a time of treachery, as people may betray one another in the name of personal gain- but hunters challenge that concept. The Vigil can't survive without trust- yet you can't truly have faith in anyone, forcing you to always sleep with one open eye.

                That Era has two, big players in the shadow world- Changeling and Sin Eaters. With the fall into a civil war, the ancient contracts and protections of the Lost are torn apart, allowing the Huntsmen to flood the land, yet they keep following the Mandate of Heaven as their heroic story tell itself. With so many dead with regrets being left behind, someone has to put the souls back to rest- not to mention the many who seek for a second chance in life. Monsters wage war against one another under the cover of the personal battles and warring warlords- yet so many innocents are stuck in their way. Someone has to rise and put those monsters back in their place, for this is their role under the Mandate.

                With China being separated from the world Alexander has shaped, we can be sure that no familiar face would show up in that land- but there are others who would take their place. The Shi (converted by Reighnhell) seem to have been around since the time of the Shang Dynasty- and while the civil war tears the whole world apart the mystical monks did survived. The Tianxia Order talks with spirits and gods under the Mandate's authority and make sure the Shadow would follow Heaven's decree. The Theater of Tragedies also exist in that Era under the guise of the Wu Keng, a classic example of search after selfish glory as they make pacts with the demons who defy the Mandate- only to betray them later. With versions of both the Wu Nung and the Wu Keng around, a translation for the Wu Lung is also expected, as those chosen by the dragons pull the strings of Fate and commune directly with the Mandate of Heaven through sacred bureaucracy and holy laws written in calligraphy, targeting Yaoguai and their agents. Talking about the Yaoguai, this is also an opportunity to present another Strain of Cursed blood- the beast shifters descendant from the Fae of China, who hunt after those who are touched by their demonic influence. With so many dead, the world is out of harmony- and those who work with the mediums of the land are a Taoist group who use the eight point diagram in order to being the world back to its natural state and clean the taint of death. While I would have loved tying both the Red Envelope Society (by Caitiff Primogen) and Hotel Mascaron (be Reviled) to the Era, both would originate only during the Tang Dynsaty- but maybe some of Reviled's other ideas which are detailed in the Hotel writeup could be developed, as detailed bellow:

                the reincarnation police of the Avichi Sutra and their obliterative mudras, the kukri-wielding soldiers who make up the Royal Kauda Corps, the anarchistic sorcerers called the Seven Wanton Horns, the gangsters of the Diyu Tong who cover themselves in squirming tattoos, the ritualistic thugs of the Feringhea Phansigar, the penniless master-killers known as the Guild of Assassins, the psychomanifesting spirit hunters trained by the Extranormal Society for Special Execution, the loyal communist investigators of the Wang Jun Xiu, the Taoist arms dealers dedicated to the Blood Principle, and more besides.


                Some are of course too modern for the Era- yet we could undoubtedly expect some to show up, and that is without mentioning which hunters did the people of the steppe brought with them.

                This is an Era of war, heroism, treachery and survival.

                There is not better time to uphold the Vigil.

                                    The Shield and Spear - Guardians of the Wise

                                    Having braved the Supernal World to Awaken to their lost birthright, the Wise stand as champions of the People against the shadows that lurk beyond the light of the hearthfires. Each speaks on her village's behalf to the spirits that are deeply intertwined with their way of life, and each is ready to work magic alongside her Circle to fight those who would prey upon the People.

                                    But even the Wise cannot stand alone for long.

                                    As they carry out their duties and pursue their Mysteries, the Wise are beset by enemies on all sides. Spirits roam the Land in abundance, quick to kill or Ride any who approach them in ignorance (or, worse, arrogance). Uratha emerge from the wilds to raid villages, seeing their residents as nothing but prey to their fangs and claws. And as the world trembles with their coming, even the gods themselves may turn on the People in pursuit of their own goals.

                                    The Wise must also watch for more mundane dangers. They find themselves increasingly at odds with the hunter-cults of their communities, and barbarian outcasts who shun their protection out of fear of their power. The latter group works to undo everything they have built by slaying the Awakened priests of the village and - if possible - shattering the god-hearts in every village they plague.

                                    Faced with such numerous threats, and in spite of the powers they wielded, the Wise would have fallen long ago on their own. However, even before the Bird and the Bull gave their gifts, they came to rely upon several of the People for aid. These men and women lived free of the Sleeping Curse's touch. Their souls were not truly illuminated with Awakening, but they could bear witness to the magics of the Wise without succumbing to madness or the fogging of memories. These "ember bearers", as the People called them, were therefore valued not only as assistants and acolytes, but also as guardians and protectors.

                                    When the People prospered from the blessing of the Omphalos Stones, the Cult of the Shield and Spear was formed from the most dedicated ember bearers among them. These dauntless individuals swore binding oaths before the Omphalos Stones, pledging to defend the Wise from the many dangers that would lead to their fall. And in the pursuit of this task, they were armed not just with the hearth magics of their villages, but vessels blessed with pure Supernal fire as well.

                                    The Shield and Spear faithfully carried out their duties for generations. They have guarded the Circles of the Wise in their journeys, clashing with spirits, werewolves, and even those among humankind who saw their charges as enemies. Some would even sacrifice their lives to ensure the Awakened safely returned to the villages. So great was their loyalty to the Wise that they came to earn their own place of honor among the People.

                                    Recently, though, the cult has had its faith shaken. Its members accompanied the Wise in their quests to seek new Omphalos Stones from the gods, and many took part in the battle that ended with Fox's death. They find themselves complicit in the Lie given to the People, even as the gods withdraw their favor and grow more hostile with each divinity murdered by the Wise. Most continue to stand with their Awakened champions, convinced that their actions are for the greater good of their communities.

                                    Yet as the world begins to change, as the violence of war plagues several villages and bull-shifters migrate from the east, some begin to fear they must act before all that they know is destroyed.

                                                    ok, another week has passed- and this time, Vikings!

                                                    The endless waves on invasion finally start to die out, and the nations of Europe finally dare to rise their head. The world starts to change, forcing the proud Northmen to either adapt or die out. Old traditions are destroyed as new ones take their place, and the northern pirates are slowly vanishing from the horizon. They stretched too much, they grew too much- and now, they fall under their own weight. However, while their glory in near its end, the story of the Vikings is not over yet. Through all of Europe, their presence was felt- and their legacy would carry own even after the wild times would end. For the hunters of Europe, the time represent both a danger and a opportunity- new lands are discovered, and new threats show their face. Some hunters would cling to their old ways when dealing with those new threats, while others embrace the new world and adapt to the upcoming changes. For years, the hunters of the Northmen rode the waves and fought the native monsters of the lands they took for themselves. They stood at the front of the fight, carrying the candle with pride and unwavering resolve. But as the Era comes to its end, the hunters become more and more tied to their local community- but the local hunters of the occupied lands won't forget the harms they suffered from them. And so, instead of working together against the monsters, it is more than likely that those hunters would leave each other to the dead.

                                                    Still, for hunters, the main theme in this Era is exploration- new lands await for the brave, and the hunters would fight their way to Valhalla. The most basic fear always was the unknown, and those who dare to fight the monsters who hide in that darkness have their place promised as future warriors of the gods. They follow the footsteps of their own heroes, searching after giants and dragons and the dead who don't stay in their grave. Unlike the hunters of other nations, the Viking hunters has a complicated relationships with their own monsters- witches are to be scared of, but they are also respected. Werewolves are terrible enemies, but are also proud warriors. Sin eaters talk with the dead and guide souls to the halls of Hel and Valhalla. Vampires of a small clan known as Grettir prey upon the Norsemen and doom them to exist as draugrs- but even those wild vampires has their uses. When the hunters of Scandinavia hunt their own monsters, they do so for glory and the chance to die honorably in battle- but when they hunt foreign monsters, it is to protect their people and gain the necessary resources for their people to survive. All hunters are warriors- but the hunters of the north are the greatest among their kind.

                                                    The major players of the Era would be, of course, a Norse organization- and the Valdaermen (temp name) would fulfill that place. Carrying silver swords and covered with magical runes, the hunters of the conspiracy has traveled everywhere their ships has led them. Following the culture of their people, harsh divide of gender roles has left its mark upon the conspiracy, as the women of the group weave complicate runes to enchant the weapons and equipment of their people while the men use those weapons to fight monsters and other threats. There are two great enemies for those hunters- the dead and the werewolves, as the former being constant threat from souls who cling to their former lives and are try to escape their fate in Hel, while the others are seen as the spawn of Loki who would one day end the world. While the divide between the gender roles of the conspiracy is harsh, some dare to challenge it, gaining both the respect and loath of the rest of the group. Shieldmaidens storm the battle alongside the greatest warriors, while males are branded as Ergi for delving into the knowledge of sorcery. After years of controlling the northern sphere, as Christianity marches on the conspiracy would fall and die out- yet its secrets would remain carved into stones, waiting to be found by modern heirs of their glory.

                                                    Those hunters, of course, are not the only ones around- with so many dead left to the cold winds and the Bound rising from their graves, you could be sure the Ebony Fangs would lurk around under the service of Hel, the half alive queen of the Underworld. As the Great Heathen Army rush through the lands of England, the current form of the Knights of St George would stand against their heretic witchcraft, as they start their tradition of using Christianity as a mask to their true nature, denying the existence of magic one hand and fighting its threat in the other. However, foreign witches are not their only target- for the Shepherds of Annwn travel the Welsh lands, being forced to hide from the Knights as they put the many restless dead to sleep so they won't call the Hounds of Hell. The Varangian Guard offers opportunities for not only the Uratha, but also for the hunters of Byzantium, becoming a power which would stand strong until the empire's upcoming fall. Frankia is now under the Carolingian dynasty's rule, and their own Vigil works more or less with harmony with the Norse hunters as they operate under the blessing of the Pope. Finally, against all chances, Dublin stood strong, unbowed by the endless invasions. However, it has its own problems- the slave trade is prospering, and the city was built unknowingly (probably) around a gate to the Underworld. The screams of the slaves echo into the dark pits, turning into a song which lures things from the pond better stay hidden. Some may think that this song has to be stopped- but others wish to harness its power to fight the monsters. For the Aided (temp name), the song of death is nothing modern, for they sang it by themselves for centuries- and if so many souls yearn for it, it is only fair to grant their wish.

                                                    Ride the waves, fight the unknown and die in honor to enter Valhalla.

                                                    This is what it means to be a hunter among Vikings.
