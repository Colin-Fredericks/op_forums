            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post

            As a longtime staunch opponent of crossover on the grounds that the themes of each line often get muddled, I'm really excited to see some strong advice on this.
            In my experience that's a problem with free-range crossovers, but you can do very strong thematic narratives where characters are limited to two or so splats with a game exploring some hook of their interaction/overlap.

            Also there are certain combos where a different supernatural type makes for a strong antagonist for a different game line. I'm particularly a fan of using Mages as antagonists for game lines where the hook is that theMage is more overtly powerful in a given domain but understands it less well than the domain's "natives" and is dangerously mucking with powers beyond their ken. The most obvious candidates for this dynamic are Werewolves vs Spirit-Mage, Changelings vs Fate-Mage, and Sin-Eaters vs Death-Mage.

                                        So we're dealing with these Chronicles of Darkness where a diverse population of monsters are hiding in the shadows, conducting their affairs away from mortal eyes…

                                        …and said supernatural entities cluster together, each into its own shadow society that exists in parallel to the others and largely ignorant of each others.

                                        Right. That seems rather unlikely. What seems more likely is that you'd end up with troupes and organizations with mixed populations but shared themes. I'd like to see advice on how to set up a unified “shadow government” in a city that polices all of the Night Folk living there, rather than assuming that vampires police vampires, werewolves police werewolves, mages police mages, etc. I'd like to see advice for putting together, say, a group of changelings and Sin-Eaters (a “motley krewe”). I'd like to see advice for “intertype y-splats” that can be used to help fight the thematic muddying that crossovers tend to cause, by gathering in one place subgroups of all of the supernatural types who share common themes.

                                        I'd like to see more crossover-based elements for each supernatural type: Bloodlines and Disciplines that riff off of the animistic metaphysics of the Uratha or the cosmology of the Lost; Lodges and Rites that allow one to maintain the balance of the ghostly denizens in the Packs Territory; Legacies based on the Were-Hekau with Attainments aligning with the Arisen Pillars; Entitlements and Contracts by which changelings can contact the Moire and Anachronisms of the Arcadian Witches; and so on.

                                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                If anything, I'd like to focus crossover on a primarily thematic axis. Say you're looking at blending Promethean with Vampire and/or Mummy; the game becomes a reflection on what humanity means to those who covet it and to those who have left it behind, and that's the kind of thing you can build your chronicle out from. Demon and Changeling both share ideas of being hunted, living behind masks, and paranoia, so how can that inform a cohesive story as a structure. It's easy for obvious fits like those, but my mind kind of boggles when we try to do, say, Promethean and Beast, or Mummy and Demon.

                                                This might also be a lovely chance at some updates to 2e and some new content; the Dead Wolves bloodline or Lord Sages of the Unknown Reaches entitlement have few better places to see conversion than in a dedicated book for it, and things like a fae-focused Lodge would be welcome additions.

                                                    *cracks knuckles* Promethean and Beast would be about being caught between extremes, at least for the Promethean. Defy your nature or embrace it? The Beast outlook might make a Promethean consider whether or not their state might actually be "natural" in some way and not innately harmful to the world. Of course, the natural order espoused by Beasts is a fairly dark and brutal one, so there's another line of contention. I don't really think Mummies have left humanity behind as much as Beasts have, after all: Mummies don't tend to revel in their condition the way Beasts do, since theirs can't really be described as anything other than a curse.

                                                    Mummy and Demon would be about discovery and mystery. Each side would have the experience of realizing that the world is much larger and much stranger than you ever imagined: an experience that's much more impactful when you're an ancient supernatural being. Mummy's theme of "realizing that you're being dicked over by ancient malevolent forces" has a fair bit of overlap with Demon's, and the techgnosticism of Demon can represent the future shock and time displacement that frequently comes up in Mummy: the Arisen dealing with an incredibly strange and confusing world that often seems to have left them behind.

                                                    And yes on the crossover splats. I've already mentioned Kiasyd, but there's so much more.

                                                        I've actually run a crossover "chat" before that was based around the typical organizations not being present, and instead a sort of 'council' of various supernaturals existed, mostly to make sure the Masquerade/equivalent was in place and that nobody fucked with each other too much. Then, to give people reasons to actually interact, there were two 'crossover splats'.

                                                        The first was called 'Influence', and it was the realm or supernatural focus your character had. If you took Influence (Shadow) you had some bonuses dealing with spirits and their realm, AND you could recognize others with the same influence (It was a merit).

                                                        You then had Guilds, which were more of an organizational type thing, obviously, and focused on the same sorts of things your typical y-splats deal with, like you had those that focused on mortal interactions, a guild of police-y types (obviously worked closely with the council), a mysterium-like organization, and a few others. Obviously, the typical y splats for those that had them based organizationally weren't much present, making it so if you wanted support, you joined a guild instead. (Vamps could also access blood magic more easily, but generally only a single type)
