    [2E] Running Rank 6+ Ephemeral Beings - Ghosts, Spirits, Angels, Supernal, etc.

    03-29-2018, 08:09 PM
    So I wanted the place to talk about those Gods of Twilight and other supernatural states - and how to run them as ST in games.

    Lately, there was some interesting sub topic in What We Know So Far: GtS 2E thread. I will copy here main posts from it, to spring what I have in mind to talk for.

    Originally posted by nofather View Post
    I'm with Tessie on this. 'They win' isn't a plot device, it's an end statement and doesn't explain anything. It's cute to stamp on a character sheet but is otherwise a hollow gesture. We have Rank 6 and higher entities in the setting, active and doing things in the world. The Gurdilag, Rank 6 idigam, defeated by Max Roman. And we have Rank 7 entities in the setting active, encounterable, and doing things in the world. And it goes on, the Underworld isn't the only place one can encounter Rank 8 entities. It's not a matter of omnipotence, because these things are limited by their Influences and seem hindered when acting in the world.

    This is just an offshoot of the further explanation Influence needs. It's not a character sheet we need, but some description of capability, scale, and limits (even if they're scant). Ghosts especially have had shaky descriptions regarding what their Influences are and would relate to and a book about people dealing with them should do more than copy and paste the same thing that's been in the other books (though they don't seem to have only copypasted, each seems to edit some to deal with the specific ephemerals they're likely to deal with).

    I appreciate that this is the Geist forum, but while Geist might not be designed for it, it's clearly something other games were meant to get into. There's a Hunting Ground in Werewolf that involves one of the Firstborn sauntering around Basra and actually collecting the same kind of characters you're told to make throughout the book. And Mage's LA has people being possessed by Goetia, with seemingly no help from mages, and Goetia do not have the restrictions on Rank other ephemera do. These are both clear examples where players are encouraged to interact and deal with these things, and encourage storytellers to do similarly.
    Originally posted by Satchel View Post
    Yeah, it's worth noting that even before the statless tier, a Rank 5 entity shares its preceding Rank's specificity of ban and bane, has double the Essence capacity of its immediate predecessor when before then the scaling was strictly linear, imposes the largest singular penalty the system affords to attempts to bind it, and has casual access to base dicepools that equal or dwarf those that exist at the limits of mortal ability even outside their areas of focus. There isn't much higher for the system to go without getting into the "bucket of dice" problem that it's been historically fairly clear the game isn't supposed to actually model, which is why Rank 6 is the point at which an entity generally doesn't leave its home realm and starts being able to form Royal Avatars as specific expressions of its nature as a global-scale actor.
    Originally posted by nofather View Post

    No one seems to be asking for the latter part.

    Even if they did, 'But if we describe them, people will beat them up,' hasn't stopped people from positing ways to destroy the God Machine, Exarchs, Luna, the entirety of the Abyss, and everything else that's described as ambiguously as possible. Posters and developers can respond to these people on the forums as much as they want, but it's just a fraction of the community. If you're labeling things, people are going to handwave their destructibility, even if you assign a developer to go with every book and pdf sold and have them tell people it's not how it's supposed to be.

    Why should that stop these entities, which are already described as active setting elements within the games, from getting some sort of clarification beyond an ambiguous, 'It has complete control over Drowning and this location.'
    Originally posted by Taidragon View Post

    You note that no one is asking for a rank six entity to beat up...and then list people beating up various entities that are, by definition, all but impossible for player beings to destroy. Isn't that a paradox?

    To quote Spoony on this, do not stat out Chthullu; it will just result in players wanting to kill it...and then he comes back angry.

    But if you want a scale, look to Influences. A Spirit divides its Rank among its Influences, and the level of its effects and duration is limited by the level of its Influence. A Rank 5 Spirit thus can only Mass Create something for one minute, or permanently Strengthen a small area of its purview. Logically speaking, Rank 6 allows it to permanently manipulate something to be more to its purview - this is a small change, but given it is permanent there's nothing stopping a Rank 6 entity from slowly warping something until it entirely embodies its purview. It can Mass Create for 10 minutes at a time, can control for days at a time, and single things it creates last hours, rather than the single hour a Rank 5 Spirit could only hope for. Even if the Spirit doesn't have all of its Rank in a single Influence, it will have greater capabilities and more than enough spare Numina that one could shave them off to grant it more Influences, possibly to the point of having two Influences at 6 dots.

    These are not minute things, and it's also not taking into account how high its Essence pools are growing - as Satchel noted, the mere jump from Rank 4 to 5 doubles its Essence pool instead of merely adding another 5, so hitting Rank 6 is not liable to make the pool smaller. It is going to have at least 46 dots of attributes, and may well go above 55. Its Numina will vary, but it may well be above 13 (and as noted, this can be lesser for more Influences). If all that doesn't give it a good reason for being a force of plot, then I have to question just what kinds of things your players are getting into without attracting a lot of attention from other sources that would want to reign them in.

    And to cap it all off, read all that again...and bear in mind it gets worse the higher up the Ranks you go, and this isn't getting into the Numina. This is supposed to be the Chronicles of Darkness, not the chronicles where a Spirit has the stats to permanently control a person's emotions, blood and or bones without the players giving their okay on it.
    Originally posted by nofather View Post

    I was referring to this thread, or at least the last few posts. I already said that it's not a character sheet we need, but even if we were given one it doesn't matter if you stat out Cthulhu, people will want to kill it anyways, and do it. The 'it's a plot device' works both ways. Developers aren't there to tell you things can't be done, storytellers aren't obligated to show antagonist character sheets to players as they sit at the table, and that wouldn't stop people from doing it anyways. Having some clarification, even brief, on these things would do nothing but help storytellers.


    Influence and its ambiguousness is something I've mentioned already.

    Nearly everything has Influences. This is a big thing that seems to reach across all these entities. And it's not limited to Rank. But aside from that:

    'Influence is measured in both scale and duration.' is an introductory line to the abilities of Influence, with no further explanation of scale beyond 'Create/Mass Create.'

    Why would it have to make a small change? Do you know how far Manipulate can go in a single usage? Do you know its scale? Can only one thing be Manipulated? And this Rank 6 entity doesn't seem very powerful using several turns to transform one thing to another. Ephemeral Entity with Influences: Drowning and This Place. Can it Drown a ghost or other thing that can't be drowned? If it's a ghost can it just expand the size of This Place? Is it based on line of sight, or perception? These things aren't even touched on.

    Another thing is the Numina. Because these things all use the same Numina from the various core books, but you look at something like Unwashed Isaac, a Rank 5 angel, and he's got nothing special or unique to him. Sure, he could have some spare Numina that would be

    Do you know what the Maximum Essence Per Turn is for ephemerals? Even rank 1-5? Because it's not mentioned, like so many other things. And have you seen these games? Every one has a chart for your Supernatural Attribute to go up to 10. And are these 'other sources that would want to rein them in' be any less ambiguous. Because as you describe it, you're just bringing more big dicepools and the same limitations as any other ephemeral.


    If a Rank 6 or higher entity flaunts the rules in a way a Rank 3 creature doesn't, then how the Rank 3 thing works should at least be clarified. If it's just bigger dicepools and stronger Influence, that's fine. If it's something different, then that should be explored. Because as I pointed out, these things are already active and antagonistic setting elements in the game.
    Originally posted by nofather View Post
    You said this: "I believe there’s a distinction to be made here, between “Rank 6+ entities should be engaged with in a story as active setting elements” and “Rank 4-5 are chumps on paper, so let’s stat up Rank 6+ for more things to beat up.”"

    Well lets make a further distinction. Wanting information about Rank 6+ entities does not mean I want to have a character sheet so PCs can beat them up at my table. Because I already said It's not a character sheet we need, but some description of capability, scale, and limits (even if they're scant). And I don't appreciate this being attributed to me. I'm not saying that your argument is 'Rules need to be made more unclear' am I?

    I did not say Fenris-Ur should be statted up. I did say he's an active and engaged part of the setting, though. He's literally calling Blood Talons from across the globe and driving them mad. This is the kind of thing players would want to get involved with, or be driven to getting involved with. It's the kind of thing they are going to engage with in something that goes beyond 'Well we're in Basra now, I guess I better kill all not-Blood Talons.'

                  I'm gonna go challenge an assumption here.

                  I can think of a bunch of good reasons why statting up Rank 6+ entities is inadvisable, or at the very least something that falls into the category of "this is a nontrivial problem that is probably not worth the word count to do in detail in a systematic way".

                  But I'm honestly mystified at the argument that you shouldn't do so "because then your players will try (or worse, succeed at) beating them up".

                  I mean sure, I'd be pretty concerned too if my players' first reaction to seeing something powerful is "Let's beat it up", but that's because I think player characters should not default to violence as their base interaction with the world, rather than the idea that this particular type of violence is verboten. When Acrozatarim statted up Fox the Pangaean, my mind didn't go "wow, he's strong. Let's kill him!". It went "wow, Pangaeans are awesome. I wonder if there's a way to incorporate these guys in my Chronicle!".

                  But the idea that players should, in the fullness of a Chronicle, be able to materially challenge and a Rank 6+ entity does not seem all that weird or radical to me. As Nofather mentioned, Max Ronan did exactly that and his most memorable moment in the fiction (to me, anyway) was him skipping out on Pack duties in favor of meetings, then changing his mind.

                  But, to me, there's really no compelling reason why a sufficiently prepared and powerful group of PCs shouldn't be able to do it, if there was a need and it fit the story. There's no overarching plotline in the Chronicles of Darkness that makes it imperative for this or that NPC to survive. If tomorrow, in my game, Helios gets out of bed tomorrow and missteps, thus breaking His Celestial Neck (I am sure I speak for the entire forum when I say that a more plausible scenario has never even been conceived) and dying on the spot, that doesn't really affect anything other than my game. Ditto if instead of slipping, he wakes up to a Pack or a Cabal or, for that matter, a Hunter Cell and they murder him before he can so much as bat a celestial eyelid. It'd be pretty silly if it went down that way, but it won't have cosmic repercussions (well it will. Just not on anyone's game but my own).

                  So, to bring my rambling to an end: I'm ambivalent about statting up Rank 6+ entities because it's a hard problem to do to my satisfaction, and I don't know if statting them up in the official books is worth the time and effort, but I think that "don't do it because you may accidentally enable your players to beat them up" is not a reason with any merit.

                                      There is another big issue with statting things such as Rank 6+ entities that wasn't discussed before: satisfaction.

                                      As the system stands, with Influences, Numina and Dread Powers, characters who aren't part of the player splats will usually have all the tools they need to do practically anything the plot requires of them and more. These are powers that range from teleportation to granting wishes to literally raising characters from the dead, if not themselves. I have even pondered on creating a TARDIS-like Horror, and it doesn't even need to be all that high in rank to be satisfactory. Yet, as noted in the Geist discussion, there has been disatisfaction with what canon characters have and how they lack anything "unique". Everything is known, and so it isn't interesting because we know all of it. There is no mystery.

                                      That is why I feel that statting a character that goes beyond a particular threshold is a bad idea. By statting it you have defined and locked that character into particular limits. Luna is explicitely a god in this setting, as is the Dark Mother and the God-Machine itself is a being that is noted as being like the ocean in terms of killability. These are beings who are only limited by way of the lore, which allows them to do great things but only in a particular method. This method means they can't act directly, and when they do the storyteller is allowed to do it however they please. They are mysterious, potent and what limits they have are hard to pin down; statting them undoes this. They have defined limits, the mystery is gone because their traits are all listed, and potency is now something that can be challenged. The only way to go around this is to make unique merits, Influences, Numina or Dread Powers to do away with those limits...at which point you've kind of made the whole point moot (and introduces the possibility of players being able to outright replicate them - Show Of Power, as just an example).

                                      The point of including Rank 6+ entities is because they have gotten to a scale that they have transcended some major limitations that Rank 1-5 entities are limited by, be it by way of attaining new power, sources of energy or they simply are blessed with some part of reality that others can't access. They represent things that are so potent that a bucket of dice is needed for any rolls they have. Leaving them unstatted means not only that the Storyteller can have them do things without rolls, they can fulfill any needs of the plot without being limited to a particular character sheet. The challenges, or assistance, they bring to the players can thus be tailored to what the situation needs. By all means, make notes about what limits they may have - the Rank 6 Angel inhabiting the Statue of Liberty may only be able to work its wonders within the monument itself - but as noted, statting the entire thing out feels like missing the point.

                                      ...and that was yet another of my 1am rambles. Hopefully there's something of value to take from it...

                                          Luna does not have a stat block in Imperial Mysteries.

                                          As for Numina...

                                          Well, here's a few I thought up on the spur of the moment. I went for 'flavorful' more than 'powerful', with each power (hopefully) presenting a story hook as well as a concrete ability. Additionally, these may well be suited as Numina for a Royal Avatar (or, if Luna is too distant interact in such a way, a similarly powerful being such as one of the Maslunim).

                                          Moon of all faces

                                          Luna’s presence is overwhelming and all-encompassing, leaking her transformative Essence into the world. Any turn spent in the same scene as Luna grants any present Forsaken a point of Essence as though first seeing their Auspice moon in the sky and, for the next full lunar cycle, the Werewolf acts in all respects as though their Auspice moon was visible, gaining a point of essence each night they see the moon.

                                          The Moon’s power additionally allows him to change the faces of her servants, letting her transform any Lune in his presence into a different choir of Lune with an reflexive action that costs a point of Essence. For a point of Willpower and an instant action, she may instead change the Auspice moon of any Forsaken. It is rumored that the Warden Moon could likewise return an Auspice to the Pure, though no known member of the Anshega has ever come in contact with Amahan Iduth since the Death of Father Wolf, and so these rumors remain unconfirmed.

                                          Trans-lunar barrier

                                          The Warden Moon is a protector Goddess, protecting earth against any intrusion and likewise defending the void between stars from ill-considered ventures by those within. The Trans-lunar barrier is the area around the earth described by the moon’s orbit around the earth. Whenever anything crosses this limit, whether this is within the Shadow or the Flesh - and, at the Storyteller’s option, even in stranger analogous spaces such as the Hedge, Anima Mundi or Underworld - Luna is instantly aware and may target anything within with her other Numina or Influences. Additionally, he may Clash with any supernatural power that crosses the orbit of the moon. It is unknown how many potentially world-ending disasters have been prevented by Amahan Iduth, but her dutiful attention is almost certainly among the reasons the world has survived the attention of the great predators that roam the void between the worlds.

                                          Goddess of Silver

                                          Luna is the goddess of silver, and the material is strongly resonant with her. Silver can never hurt Amahan Iduth. Her own body counts as silver for the purpose of inflicting banes, and he may transform any object up to size 30 into silver with an a touch and an instant action.

                                          Additionally, she may levy a curse on those that displease her, targeting any number of creatures and making silver painful to the touch. This causes the material to deal a point of bashing damage per turn of contact, increasing to lethal damage when the full moon is in the sky - displaying the Silver Father’s most wrathful face.

                                          Quicksilver Changeling

                                          Luna is the the most changing and adaptable of the Gods of the Shadow, finding a new form for any occasion. She may additionally bestow this power or a subset thereof on any creature with an instant action and a point of Willpower, though she is not capable of taking back a blessing so granted.
                                          • Healing any Condition or Tilt not inflicted by her Banes.
                                          • Healing up to her Rank in lethal or aggravated damage.
                                          • Change her size to any number between 1 and 100.
                                          • Any of the changes listed under the Shifting (Werewolf the Forsaken, p. 222) Dread Power granted to Formless Idigam, which is surely just a coincidence.
