    No, Lasting has nothing to do with whether or not a spell can be recast. Lasting is a piece of terminology to describe permanent effects of a spell. For instance, if I use magic to smash a vase it isn't suddenly going to repair itself after the spell is cast (Barring other supernatural effects) so the fact that the vase is broken is a Lasting effect.

    Without looking at the spell you're referencing I assume that damage dealt via passing through the Gauntlet is not the effect of the spell. The actual effect of the spell is probably to place a Condition or Tilt which has that effect. Thus, instances of damage are not a result of the spell being cast again. If that's not how the spell works it's likely not a spell that complies with the actual rules of the game and shouldn't be used as an example for how to do things.

                        In fact I included the Potency bit just because a lot of spells in the core book have it. Since then, I've thought more about the uses of Potency, and removed it almost completely from all spells except those that actually provide/remove dots or inflict/heal damage. My current version of this spell is:

                        Vicious Gauntlet
                        Practice: Patterning
                        Suggested Rote Skills: Survival, Animal Ken, Brawl

                        The spellcasting roll for this spell is always penalized by the Gauntlet Strength.

                        This spell alters the nature of the Gauntlet, adding biting fangs, snarling thorns and vicious claws to its "surface". The subject of this spell is the Gauntlet itself as it covers the area affected by the spell's Scale. While the spell lasts, whenever any entity attempts to cross it or use a supernatural power to reach across it, roll a dice pool equal to twice the Gauntlet Strength contested by Stamina or Rank. Each success inflicts a level of lethal damage to the crossing entity.

                        +1 Reach: Only one side of the Gauntlet is affected (the mage chooses which one).
                        +1 Reach: You may designate a Key that protects its holder from being attacked.
                        +1 Reach: If the entity takes damage from the Gauntlet, it fails to cross over or reach through the Gauntlet.
                        +1 Reach and 1 Mana: The Gauntlet deals aggravated damage instead of lethal damage.


                        You might notice that there is something weird with this spell: instead of Withstand, I apply the Gauntlet Strength as a straight penalty. In my rules I apply Gauntlet Strength as a penalty instead of raising Withstand, so that it is compatible with blue book Spirits and Werewolf.

                                                            Originally posted by Mage: the Awakening, 2nd Edition, Chapter 4: Magic, Spell Factors, Duration
                                                            Duration Duration is simply how long a spell lasts once cast. Standard spell Durations are measured in turns, while using a Reach to use the Advanced Duration chart makes the spell last much longer. If a spell would logically have an immediate effect but is cast with Advanced Duration, the effect recurs at every multiple of the character’s Gnosis-based ritual casting time, until the Duration runs out. For example, a Gnosis 1 character’s healing spell with a Duration of a day heals its Potency in Health boxes every three hours. The highest level of the Advanced Duration chart is “indefinite,” meaning the spell lasts until dispelled or the caster cancels it.
