    Originally posted by Two-Shadows View Post
    I can't help but feel Princess would have been stronger if it just had ditched the Radiant Queens. Don't get me wrong, I really like the Radiant Queens as characters and their kingdoms/courts are very colorful and interesting, but on a whole, they felt like they'd be better suited to a different setting. Personally, just having the Twilight Queens would have presented a more interesting conflict: so you're a magical girl and there are honest to god magic queens and even a whole magic kingdom! The catch? In the face of overwhelming opposition, the queens have all fallen into corruption and now you have a choice to make: will you follow them or will you take a stand against both the queens and the darkness that broke them?
    Contrariwise - and perhaps this is just because about seventy-five percent of my mahou shoujo consumption so far is PreCure - I feel like the Twilight Queens took the focus away from the Darkness as a worthwhile set of antagonistic forces on its own; it dramatically collapses the potential for game-localized motif-sets and Alhambra steals heat that could go into developing the various ways the Dark does its work.

                            Originally posted by Aspel View Post
                            That said, maybe it's just because I've talked with Kings' Raven, or maybe it's just because I like the idea of grimdark magical girls (since playing a Nanoha-esque character was my first RPG), but Princess: The Hopeful kind of grew on me. I almost ran a game of it until I realized that it was kind of unfinished. Although apparently I was looking at a far behind rules set.
                            I think part of the issue is that, unlike the main game line, fan splats are developed in public. As such, people may form impressions about the game that cease to be true as it is constructed. Many fan splat threads begin as simply a concept, and as mechanics and ideas accrete onto the original seed, it is compressed by heat and pressure (I'm using a geology metaphor, but it's perfectly acceptable in a social context as well) and thereby transformed into a completely different entity. For instance, the original concept for Dragon the Embers by Shock and Gepetto is only a distant speck now (Y-Splats, 'Ritual' powers, and most of the interpretation of the thematic goals have all changed), and many of the ideas from the first phase of the development were twisted in the second phase and then discarded altogether in the third (and current) phase of development. People who came into the game during different stages of its development will form varying (and perhaps skewed) opinions about the fan splat.

                            This open development is a double-edged sword in that manner. If you join in during development, you may be able to influence and tailor the game to your preferences. You will, however, be subjected to repeatedly changing rules and thematic elements, possibly souring you on the whole idea. It's certainly a gamble, and not one with good odds. Still, those always have the best payout, don't they?

                                    The lack of an appointed lead developer is one of the biggest hurdles fan games have to overcome. The presence of one individual with final say over vision and direction does a world of good for keeping the game consistent. Princess is a good example of a game that's been negatively impacted by the absence of a lead developer.

                                    It also helps to have tested writers, a full development and editing process, dozens of hours of devoted work, and a team small enough not to get muddled with excess input.

                                        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                        Contrariwise - and perhaps this is just because about seventy-five percent of my mahou shoujo consumption so far is PreCure - I feel like the Twilight Queens took the focus away from the Darkness as a worthwhile set of antagonistic forces on its own; it dramatically collapses the potential for game-localized motif-sets and Alhambra steals heat that could go into developing the various ways the Dark does its work.
                                        I fully agree with this statement, however as far as I'm concerned it's intentional. It's a deliberate thematic specialisation.

                                        The Outer Darkness is there to be a simple and straight forward antagonist that serves as monsters of the week or the occasional evil empire such as the one in Sailor Nothing. The Twilight Courts (and Radiant who've got a few dots in Twilight Invocations - that's very common) provide the more complex antagonists.

                                        Originally posted by Two-Shadows View Post
                                        Think ya hit the nail on the head with that one. When I want neat monsters of the week, I look to the Dark. When I want interesting, in depth antagonists, I look to the Twilight Queens/Courts.
                                        See

                                        ------------------------

                                        One of the reasons I like it this way comes down to the Child Soldiers metaphor that is one of Princess' thematic strands. The problem with child soldiers is not "the enemy might shoot them". If they had better guns, better training, better strategies, and won without casualties. It still would not be ok to deploy child soldiers.

                                        When you're focusing on the child soldier metaphor for Princess; then the Twilight Courts represent: The desire to protect yourself and those closet to you (Tears), hatred of the enemy (Storms), and the desire to just escape the horrors (Mirrors).

                                        These are not bad things (ok, maybe hatred is). They're natural psychological responses. You can't punch them, and you shouldn't punch people just for feeling them. The only correct response is to find a “better way”.

                                        And that's vital. If you're using child soldiers as an enemy the primary antagonist can't be defeated defeated with violence; because the solution to child soldiers is never making the children more effective at violence. It's not using child soldiers (I'm resisting the temptation to add a swear word here)

                                        In short: An antagonist group that's fundamentally evil just cannot work for the child solider metaphor because violence becomes an appropriate solution. (It can't work for the social activist metaphor either).
