    Doc’s Log

    Doc’s Log: 1000 Hours, Friday, September 24, 2010

    I went to bed fairly early last night to keep myself from stalking around the House and terrifying anyone. It really seemed to help. I got up like normal and was able to meditate without issues. That was important given how angry I was last night. After only about a half hour, I came down to breakfast.

    Plans for the next few weeks were discussed, given Chandra and Cupcake are leaving for Paris for two weeks. Rose informed us that she had been sneaking off to the Underworld lately. Not the safest move, but people don’t bitch at me for going on patrol, so I’ll just keep my mouth shut. Anyway, she found a place she wants to claim for herself. It’s apparently a lighthouse out by the Sea of Darkness. If the geography holds, it should be close to Lighthouse Park. Fitting at least. If I had to guess, I think that Rose and the Drowned Bride have grown so close they have to spend time Greyside now. I know that The Dog and I have discussed it before, just not something that comes up all the time. But this lighthouse already has something inhabiting it. Something that sounds like a geist. She wants to try and either flush it out or make a deal with it. Part of the agreement would be to find some way to handle the glowing crabs on the beach. From what Rose says, the crabs each Plasm and can destroy ghosts. They are somehow tied to a sunken ship at the seafloor leading to the lighthouse. Visions of my own Black Pearl came to my mind unbidden. I realize it was completely selfish, but I immediately signed on to this idea when she mentioned the sunken ship. There was even talk about taking supplies down to build something into the Bluffs on the other side for Snowflake. This would need some scouting and we also needed some preliminary reconn on Taipei. Rose told us about the abandoned Buddhist Monastery she was travelling to and thought it might be useful as a base of operations in Taiwan. Guang’s house may still be available, though the sorcerer is certainly keeping watch there. We mentioned trying to get it in order so Rose can move Guang back into his home when this is over. Between investigating our own locations in the Underworld and setting up our own Krewe base in Taipei, it sounds like we’re going to be busy while the honeymooners are gone.

    I’ve come back to the shop to check in. I need to order a few things and make a cheat sheet on the herbs so the girls don’t have to stumble through questions, but otherwise they have done a fantastic job while I’ve been gone. I did notice there were more college guys in than usual, but I also know why that is too. It’s kinda of relaxing to be back here again.

    Doc’s Log: 2200 Hours, Friday, September 24, 2010

    I got back to the House around 1500 Hours and got a text right after I walked in from a TigerKittyMeltdown asking, “Am I your girlfriend?” I realized immediately that I was an idiot. I hadn’t talked to Noriko at all since she went home Wednesday morning. I’m not that kind of guy, so I called her right then. She answered quickly and I apologized for not calling. I didn’t want to sound lame, but Wednesday night was vampires, Thursday night was vampires, and the wedding was going the whole time. So instead of sounding like a player with bad excuses, I asked her if she would like to be my girlfriend. Listening to myself say the words, I thought of junior high, but she was so quick to answer I lost that train of thought. I couldn’t think of a good reason why a woman who could easily take care of herself shouldn’t go out with a guy who’d seen some things. I asked if she wanted to come over and I think she jumped and grabbed her stuff before getting off the phone.

    She made it to the house about twenty minutes later. I smiled when I opened the door and started to greet her, but she pounced on me and kissed all over my face. I got the very real impression it might have been awhile since she had been safely involved with anyone. Once she was standing on the floor again, I gave her the tour of the House. I didn’t just unload all our secrets on her, but I did give her the “What you need to know to safely visit Dark Haven Bluffs” speech. We wandered the House talking and enjoying each other’s company until Snowflake came to herd the Krewe to the rehearsal. Noriko watched the proceeding and didn’t even laugh at all the weirdness. Rose was incredibly warm and happy. It was a little surreal but I guess it was the wedding and Noriko being here. Either way, Rose was positively giddy. Snowflake on the other hand was the most authoritative and demanding I had ever seen him. He was his normal gracious self with Chandra and Cupcake, but with all the rest of us, he was nearly a tyrant. I chalked it up to being nervous and wanting everything to be perfect for the wedding. Lynne came up after dark and watched for a while, but based on the looks from Chandra and Rose, she stayed at the back and didn’t say much. At one point, I walked back and sat with Noriko while some of the others were going over details. She was staring at her phone which seemed to constantly get messages. She looked up at me with a blank stare and said she should have never mentioned me to her mom. She showed me the phone and I blinked a few times before admitting to her that I didn’t yet speak Japanese. Noriko snapped out of her fugue and blushed a little. Getting the translation was like hearing a laundry list of things her mom expected of any man that she went out with. I asked about her parents and learned that they were still in Japan. Snowflake ushered me off before I could get much more conversation going.

    When the rehearsal was (finally) over, I scooped Snowflake up like in the movies and rushed into the bar. Then I put him down and handed him a bottle of scotch. He was catch off guard and wasn’t sure what was happening until the scotch arrived. Then he shrugged and poured a drink. I laughed until I turned around and saw Tavia standing in the doorway behind me. There was no kindness in her large eyes and her features were equally unwelcoming. When she saw Snowflake intact and happy, she half nodded as her expression became more normal. Afterward, she soundlessly turned and walked away. ...That was creepy. Now I’m going to put this away and get to know my new girlfriend better.

    Doc’s Log: 1315 Hours, Saturday, September 25, 2010

    I woke up early this morning after sharing hours with Noriko last night. It was nice to talk and kiss and touch like that. There was something about that it just felt...innocent? Anyway, I slipped out of bed to go meditate around 0600 Hours. Centering was again a fairly simple affair as I seemed to glide along the mental path today. When I found myself awake and centered by 6:30, I decided to be a decent housemate. After getting cleaned up, I went out and get breakfast for whoever eventually stumbled out of bed. I left a note back in my room to let Noriko know I was running errands for the wedding. It’s probably cliché but when I went in, she was catnapping. I saw an eye peeking out from the covers before her hand slid out and reached for me. I sat next to her and kissed her before whispering what I was doing. She nodded and rolled over. Noriko is adorable.

    Quietly, I got my Service Dress Blues to be pressed and I headed out to get a regulation haircut. I got back around 1100 Hours and found Noriko staring at her phone again. She blinked a little when she saw me, but she didn’t seem upset with the haircut. She asked me about the wedding and I realized it was only a few hours away and she only had the shorts and t-shirt she came over in last night. I asked if she wanted to go shopping for something to wear, but she said she had something, she just needed to go get it. I went to talk with Chandra to make sure it would be okay to add a +1 to the ceremony. I think Chandra was a little surprised, but didn’t mind the added guest.

    Snowflake and I mounted cameras around the wedding venue and on the widow’s walk to record the whole ceremony from any angle we could think of. The plan is to edit all the best shots together to have a proper video without having a normal person here to freak out at the ghosts, living doll, etc. Honestly I think we did it so we could both give it to the happy couple and send a copy to Ronin, since she’s still MIA.

    Nightshade and Snowflake were both visibly nervous about their speaking parts when I came back down. Snowflake was assembling images and recordings of speeches so I knew what he was about to do. Thinking it might help, I offered to give Nightshade a little luck with her oration. She immediately jumped at the chance and the three of us headed down to the GMC Room.

    Now we all need to get dressed so Snowflake doesn’t get a whip.

    Doc’s Log: 2200 Hours, Saturday, September 25, 2010

    As weddings that last a full week go, this one was quite beautiful. After the meticulous rehearsal last night, the ceremony was actually very smooth. Nightshade officiated and did a wonderful job, if I do say so myself. Snowflake gave a moving Best Man Speech at the reception and then the dancing started. As I gave away the bride, I got the first dance. Cupcake just seemed to drink in the moment and everything about the day. Then I danced with Chandra as it seemed like the only fair thing to do. Nightshade danced with me next, though she danced with the everyone present in the Krewe except for Rose. And that’s about the time I realized that my dance card was going to stay full. Being professionally trained means everyone wants to dance with you, even if I’m not a professional. After Nightshade, I danced with Noriko and showed off a little, but just enough to make her dance feel special. Then I danced with Karen because she likes to dance at any occasion. Next I danced with Marjorie because I didn’t want her to be left out of something I knew she understood. There was a break in the dancing for a few minutes and I explained why it was a big deal that Snowflake and Tavia were dancing together for a third time. This was clearly a new concept to her, but she nodded and watched the various couples move on and off the floor. She danced with other people as well. Next up for me was Bright Eyes, then Dierdre. I did notice Noriko watching seemingly uninterested and realized just how catlike she really is. Before things wound down, I danced with Grin as well, as he seemed up for it but was turned down by Karl. There were a few cheers as we headed to the floor, but I wasn’t too concerned. We were there to have a good time. Lynne even joined the party. Bright Eyes danced a few times with her, but they also just sat and talked a bit. I noticed that Rose and then Karl disappeared before Chandra and Cupcake announced that they were going to take the yacht out. We all cheered and threw flower petals or blew bubbles at them as they left. The Krewe and all our various +1s had pitched in together to get a full set of china for the shower since Cupcake didn’t have any. We also got a full set of silverware to match as a wedding gift. While Snowflake and I were mounting the cameras, we talked about making a new cabinet to keep all these presents in.

    When things started calming back down, Noriko informed me that she was happy I was her boyfriend. I think that about sums up my day, so I’m going to end here.


    Jasmine’s Diary

    Friday, September 24, 2010, Morning

    I had a particularly good meditation this morning. The Bride and I are very much in tune with one another, no doubt because of our excitement over Alexia and Chandra’s wedding.

    Over breakfast, the krewe discussed our plans and how Alexia and Chandra’s two-week honeymoon would affect them. I mentioned my recent trips to the Underworld and I explained my wish to establish a retreat there where I can go and stay when the urge strikes me. To my surprise, the others understood immediately. I had expected them to scold me unnecessarily about going to the Underworld on my own, but I was pleased to find that my forays didn’t distress them at all. Indeed, the others must also be starting to feel the pull of the Underworld, because Doc and Simon began a lively discussion of the ways they might also establish their own private getaways.

    We also decided that we would spend much of next week in Taipei, preparing things there. It will give me the opportunity to introduce my friends to my father. Unfortunately, it means Jean will be on his own for training next week, but I have confidence that he handle himself for awhile. I considered getting Rita to fill in for me—she is an excellent swimmer—but she and I both decided that she wasn’t quite ready to be left to her own devices with a handsome man like Jean.

    After breakfast, Karen drew me aside to ask if I was okay. She and Natalie have both been worried that I’ve been especially distant and melancholy of late, and after hearing me talk about needing to spend time in the Underworld, she was afraid I had some sort of supernatural sickness. I assured her I was fine, that my dark mood had mostly passed, and that the pull of the Underworld was a manifestation of my deepening connection to the dead, which, all in all, is not a bad thing.


    Friday, September 24, 2010, Afternoon

    Karen and I went shopping for matching dresses to wear at the wedding. Alexia didn’t want us to bother with formal bridesmaid dresses, but we wanted something that would look good and compliment the bride’s clothing. Since Alexia is wearing mostly red (this being an Indian wedding), Karen and I found charming, simple white dresses with red trim. I can’t really speak for myself, but Karen looks very nice in hers.


    Friday, September 24, 2010, Night

    The wedding rehearsal went quite well tonight. There were no major problems, though Natalie seemed more nervous than I expected. I felt like everything was a whirl of activity, and I was rushing here and there trying to make sure everything was just right. But my efforts paled in comparison to Simon’s. He was almost a nervous wreck as he managed every detail, intent on making sure that the wedding will be flawless. And yet whenever he spoke to Alexia and Chandra, his whole demeanor changed. He seemed calm and collected, as if that would assure them that they need not worry about a thing. More than anything, Simon wants the couple to be happy on their wedding day. It’s terribly sweet, even if Simon has been frighteningly manic over the past day or so.

    Noriko showed up to hang out with Doc this evening, so she was there for the rehearsal. I greeted her warmly, glad to see that she and Doc have hit it off so well. I’m so happy to see that he’s involved with someone who really appreciates him.

    Lily and Naomi were very good during the rehearsal. Lily did a fine job as flower girl, and was quite well behaved, and Naomi managed to sit still and not get into any trouble (which is hardly surprising, as most of the trouble she gets into is Lily’s fault anyway). So after the rehearsal, I took both of them into the kitchen for a little treat. Lily got a bright new pink bow barrette for her hair, and Naomi got a tasty cupcake. I had to rely on Doc’s help to destroy the cupcake earlier in the day with a ceremony that would transform it into a ghostly object that Naomi could eat, but it was worth it to see the look of delight on her face as she munched away. I should get Doc to teach me that ceremony in case I want to make another treat for Naomi in the future. I hate to bother him with it with everything else he is working on, like his shop and the van he has been rebuilding.

    I spent the evening after the rehearsal helping Alexia cook for the wedding tomorrow. And now I’m off to bed, though it may be hard to sleep. I’m almost as excited tonight as I was the night before my wedding to Richard!


    Saturday, September 25, 2010, Late Night

    What a wonderful wedding!

    The wedding today was beautiful, full of joy and roses and the closeness that comes from being a family. The whole household was there, including Karl and Gretchen, and Deirdre, Mr. Malard, Angelique and Grin joined us as well. It was small but intimate. Alexia was gorgeous in her gown, which was a fine reward for all the hours we spent helping her get ready. It’s a good thing I was there, because Karen, Tavia and Natalie would have lost everything from hairpins to stockings if I had not kept up with it all. Chandra was dashing as well, though I think she would have looked much better in a nice dress rather than the more masculine clothing she chose.

    Doc was very handsome as he gave Alexia away, and Natalie performed the ceremony with dignity and poise. What she lacked in gravitas, she made up for in warmth, which was probably more fitting for a wedding like this anyway.

    I suspect that Simon was out of sorts making sure everything was just right before the wedding, but once the ceremony began, he became a sea of tranquility, and seemed to enjoy the event as much as anyone else. Tavia was at his side the whole time, which I’m certain helped.

    During the reception, there was a lot of laughing and dancing. Even I laughed quite a lot, which is rare. Lily was energetic, and I think she enjoyed the atmosphere of romance. I danced twice with Karl, and during our second dance, which was slow and romantic, I laid my head against his chest. It was nice to hear his heart beat, and I could sense him relaxing and enjoying the moment. I also danced with Mr. Malard and with Grin. Natalie asked me quietly if I’d like to dance, but I politely refused. I don’t really feel right dancing with another woman like that, though I was certainly pleased to see most of the other women at the reception enjoying dances with one another.

    Tavia danced tree times with Simon, and made something of a show if it. She obviously intends to marry him soon, though I think most of us assumed that was coming, so her antics were not surprise. However, Natalie and Karen also danced together three times. It was very touching and gentle announcement of their intentions toward one another.

    After the reception, Alexia and Chandra departed to the yacht to spend their wedding night there, and the rest of us departed, many to spend time with lovers. Before she disappeared with Simon, Tavia found a chance to tell me that Karl was planning to visit the undersea grotto once night fell, so I resolved to go meet him there.

    Once the sun set, I donned my swimsuit and slipped out of the house. I felt a bit like a teenager sneaking out to meet her boyfriend. I dove into the sea and swam down to the grotto, where I waited for Karl.

    When he arrived, the warm look on his face told me that he understood. Even if we could have spoken underwater, we didn’t need words. We swam together and embraced. As our hands caressed and explored each other, we recaptured the passion that has been lost these last few weeks, and for a time, the world no longer existed. We had found each other again. We made love, and it was simply amazing. I had forgotten how incredible it could be with someone you really love.

    The Drowned Bride is aflutter with excitement and love and wonder and a thousand other wonderful emotions that I can barely explain. But as usual, her feelings echo my own. I’ve taken a big step toward what we both want—need, even. I don’t think she’s ever been this content before.

    Eventually, Karl and I realized that we had to return to the house. Even though neither of us wanted to, we decided to go to bed in our separate rooms. We didn’t want to make a spectacle, not on Alexia’s wedding night and especially not with Tavia’s almost obsessive interest in our relationship. And I think both of us are a little scared to move too quickly. But I think I will sleep well tonight, cozy in the warm afterglow.

        It was a busy weekend. Today was just as busy, but very different.

        Cupcake and Chandra left for Paris this morning. I helped them load the taxi for the airport while most of the others were still in bed. I’m sure they will have a great time...but just in case I made sure to do a little investigative web searching for larger cemeteries around Paris. The maps really helped in the event I need to go get them.

        Jasmine’s Diary

        Sunday, September 26, 2010, Morning

        This morning seemed brighter than any morning I can remember since Richard died, and the early autumn chill was invigorating and inviting when I went out to perform my Tai Chi meditations. Memories of last night lingered pleasantly, bringing an unbidden but welcome smile to my lips. I could hardly wait to see Karl at breakfast.

        Of course, I had to get inside and sort out breakfast myself, since Alexia and Chandra were enjoying the morning of their honeymoon, but I didn’t mind. Karl joined me in the kitchen shortly before the rest of the household made it downstairs, and it was nice having a quiet moment with him before the day really began.


        Sunday, September 26, 2010, Afternoon

        I made a trip into the Underworld to investigate the Lighthouse today. Doc and Simon were elsewhere, but I asked Sara, Natalie and Alexia to join me since it might be dangerous and I didn’t want to risk going alone. I led the way; I’ve grown so used to the Underworld that I have little trouble finding my way around, and I’ve managed to learn much of the area around our Avernian gate.

        We stopped by the diner first. Poor Alexia chose to wait outside; I think she’s worried that she might be too tempted by the Underworld’s food. But Natalie, Sara and I went inside and had a nice chat with Janice over milkshakes. At least, the chat was nice until Natalie asked about Janice’s last name. Janice bristled at that, and informed us that she would rather not discuss it. I told her it was okay, and Natalie helped calm her down, so there was no harm done. I personally suspect that Janice did some very bad things and is trying to make amends for her past by helping other ghosts. But she’s my friend, so I’m willing to respect her privacy.

        I led the way to the sea from the diner. The Underworld ocean always strikes me as ominously beautiful: a vast sea captured in a cavern so large that the opposite walls are lost in darkness. The faint luminescence of a moonlit night illuminates the sea, and something like stars sparkle here and there in the cavern ceiling overhead, all the way to horizon.

        The Lighthouse stands upon a rocky outcropping over the sea, tall and dark, with its pale, ghostly light stretching gently across the waves. We made our way toward it, unsure of what we would find.

        Janice had warned me about the soul-drinking crabs. They were clustered in small groups on the beach around the Lighthouse, glowing softly. Some of them were quite large, easily the size of a dog, and as we approached, they began to scuttle hungrily toward us. I suggested that I should hold them off while the others made for the Lighthouse, but Sara had a better idea. She shifted into her Pyre-Flame Caul and burst into ghostly flame. The crabs fled from her as she ran toward them and did not seem interested in harassing us any further.

        I knocked on the Lighthouse’s door, and a man in a faded yellow fisherman’s coat and hat answered. He stood well over two meters, and he had only one huge eye that beamed with light. His large mouth was filled with shark-like teeth. I suppose he might have been terrifying if we weren’t used to dealing with such otherworldly beings, but the way he was forced to stoop beneath the house’s low ceilings made him less impressive than he might have been otherwise. He was simply called the Lighthouse Keeper.

        The Lighthouse Keeper was very gracious and polite, and even offered us tea after I explained why we had come. I told him that I was looking for a place to stay, an Underworld retreat that I could come to when the need struck me. I asked if we might have a tour of the Lighthouse, and he guided us through the house, though we did not go into the lighthouse tower itself.

        The entry room is rather small, with raincoats and other gear hanging on pegs on the wall or stacked neatly on a bench. A bare light bulb sways constantly in the center of the room. It wasn’t exactly inviting, but the rest of the house was cozy enough, and I’m certain that I can make it more so. Everything was quaint, attractive, and well-kept. There are a few rooms with beds in them, all attached to a central kitchen and dining area. The wooden walls are painted in a faded, dull red, and the wood is slightly warped, as though they had been water damaged at some point in the past. In fact, most of the furniture looks somewhat water damaged, though it’s clean and functional. I suspect that the Lighthouse must have fallen into the sea, which is how it’s echo came to rest in the Underworld. I should research and find out more about it.

        I asked about the entity lurking in the shipwreck off the coast, and the Lighthouse Keeper explained that it was Pup Pid. He is a pirate skeleton made of coral, and he commands the monstrous creatures and the drowned dead of the sea. His victims become drowned things under his power; I suppose I’ll call them the Drowned. I think the crabs also belong to Pup Pid.

        The Lighthouse Keeper operates the lighthouse in part to keep the horrors that lurk in the sea from coming ashore. His efforts keep Pup Pid in check, which is a good thing.

        In the end, I was able to make a deal with the Lighthouse Keeper. He will allow me to move into one of the rooms in the Lighthouse and share the place with him whenever I visit. He rarely comes down from the tower in any event. In exchange, we must try to deal with Pup Pid. We talked with her about Nightshade making it where she had to stay in this room so the entity could not reach her. She agreed so the ceremony was enacted.

                                                            Based on what we learned, our blood snaps these ghosts out of whatever fugue state the entity inflicts on them. This alerts the entity, however, as it seems not to have omniscience within the hotel. Most of the ghosts aren’t experienced with their condition, with the exception of Troy, who exhibits a ghostly variation of my Rage Manifestation. When we cut them off from the entity, they relive their death, usually in their own minds, before becoming aware of the present. Only Troy seems to hold any memories from after death not made since we arrived. Making myself an anchor with the Stigmata Marionette does not give me any ability to keep the ghost manifested if the entity tries to recall them. Cutting the ghosts off from the entity seems to weaken it, based on the reduced pull on our Geists from the basement. The new addition of the Pool Room is safe from the entity’s control, but does not undo any effects already in place. And finally, the entity possesses some form of ability to unlock our dark passions. Seven hours in and that’s what we know.

                                                            Whatever this thing is, it’s keeping an ever growing number of ghosts as slaves for an unknown morality play of some kind. Spectral slavery, forced interaction with the living, and fucking with my emotions are only some of the reasons I will see the end of this thing. Now I’m not sure if we’re sleeping in shifts or what.


                                                            Jasmine’s Diary

                                                            Thursday, October 21, 2010, Evening

                                                            We spent the afternoon and evening continuing to explore the house. Alexia led us on a very organized survey of the entire place, floor by floor, using her Phantasmal Oracle to peer through any illusions we might encounter. There were none, however.

                                                            We encountered Molly again. She warned us about ghosts again, and complained of disorientation and fogged recollections. Doc offered to examine her medically, and though she was hesitant, she agreed. He reported that she was exhibiting symptoms of severe stress, among other things. I quietly suggested that he bleed on her to make her more lucid, hoping that would grant us more insight into what’s going on.

                                                            When he flicked a few drops on her, she began screaming “Let go!” at the empty air, and then, with a horrified look on her face, she exclaimed “No! I’m trapped here too!” Then she seemed to be pulled away, leaving only a reddish silhouette that persisted for an instant before fading. However, when Molly became lucid, we all felt the pull on our geists weaken a bit.

                                                            Doc suggested that the trapped ghosts strengthen whatever is keeping them here. When they are freed from its control, even briefly, it becomes weaker. If we can help to resolve and free the ghosts here, then perhaps we can draw out and destroy whatever is holding on to them. Troy seems to be a linchpin of sorts, and we discussed the very real possibility that he was the entity behind all of this, though our later experiences seemed to prove otherwise.

                                                            We made is significant discovery when we entered the swimming pool room. It was a large, spacious chamber with large windows to let in plenty of sunlight. The pool itself was very old fashioned, and not large enough to swim regulation laps, but it seemed like it would be suitable for a bit of relaxation. Of course, it was empty now. More importantly, however, we all felt the pull on our geists disappear entirely when we entered the room. The pool is of much newer construction than the rest of the hotel, so it appears that the entity trapping all the ghosts is confined to the physical space of the original building. This is good news, as it means we have a safe haven to retreat to if necessary.

                                                            I recommended that we try to bring a ghost into the room. If necessary, and if whatever is trapping the ghosts keeps pulling them away from us, we might use a ceremony to capture a ghost in a vessel long enough to get them there. Natalie volunteered to use the Spectral Captivity ritual to accomplish that, and we might also have a few other options.

                                                            After the pool, we searched the laundry room and music room. There was little of interest in the laundry room. In the music room, however, Doc suggested that Natalie play something from the 40’s on the piano (which was a bit out of tune, I gather). He was hoping to draw one or more of the ghosts out so that he could try binding them to himself as a new anchor. He reasoned that doing so might prevent whatever is trapping the ghosts from tearing them away from us, or at least give them a better chance of resisting the pull.

                                                            Soon enough, Sandra came in search of the music. She was a bit tipsy, and said that she couldn’t sleep. Doc quietly used Stigmata and became a new anchor for Sandra. Drawn to him, she struck up a conversation. It seems she’s from Santa Monica, and the hotel is a getaway for actresses like her. It’s quiet, and nearly as far from California as you can get and still be in America.

                                                            We asked her about Troy, and she seemed charmed by him. She also told us that he would sometimes smuggle things about, using his job as a traveling salesman to do so without drawing much attention. Since she was alive during the Prohibition, she said he mostly smuggled alcohol, and suggested we talk to him if we needed any.

                                                            Doc eventually got her to sing along with Natalie’s music, a popular tune from the 20’s, I think, and she was quite good. While she sang, Sara, Doc, Alexia and I agreed to see what would happen if we made her more lucid like we had done with Molly. So I cut my hand and intercepted her as she left Natalie and the piano. I smeared blood on her as I shook her hand to compliment on her singing, and then she became lucid.

                                                            Like Molly, she immediately began to freak out. She started screaming about Troy; she was terribly frightened of him, and it seemed obvious that he must have been the axe murderer who killed her. At that point, we were even more convinced that Troy must be the entity keeping the other ghosts in the hotel. We tried to maneuver Sandra to the pool room, which was no easy task given her hysterics, but before we could reach it she turned into a red silhouette and was torn away like Molly. It didn’t matter that she was anchored to Doc—his bond to her was severed as soon as she disappeared.

                                                            Then something odd happened. Doc became extraordinarily angry. He’s prone to a temper anyway, but he usually keeps it in check. It frightens Natalie terribly. This time, however, we could all sense the fury simmering inside him, and he turned to look at Natalie with something approaching hatred. Sara and I both put ourselves between Doc and Natalie, but Natalie turned and fled, dashing out of the hotel. Doc stormed stiffly to the pool area to try and calm down.

                                                            We all went out to check on Natalie. She was shaken but okay. She knows that whatever came over Doc had more to do with the hotel than with her, but she is terrified of Doc’s temper. Sara and Alexia helped her go up to her room to lie down for a bit. I went to check on Doc.

                                                            I found him meditating in the empty pool. He was still angry, but he was calmer. Unfortunately, the pool didn’t help calm him like he hoped. Whatever got into his head set him off and that was enough; the rest was all Doc. The entity behind all of this must have some way to draw out our dark sides. It’s a frightening thought; I can only imagine the dark, depressing places my own mind might go if the hotel turns its influence on me. I tried to encourage Doc, but I wasn’t really helping, so I left him to find his center on his own.

                                                            Alexia, Lily and I continued to explore the hotel while Sara sat with Natalie. We didn’t find much in the kitchen aside from a few empty bottles we can use for the Spectral Captivity ritual should we need it. Doc briefly came in looking for large, empty flour sacks or other things he could use to make a punching bag, and once he had what he needed, he left again. Hopefully the makeshift punching bag will help him work out the rest of his anger.

                                                            Lastly, we went into the bar, and found it positively full of ghosts. There was a 40’s-era bartender, 20’s-era Troy Donovan, Wynona Watts, Thomas Wyndham, and Marvin Tommelson. Alexia can be a bit odd at times, and lately she doesn’t seem to care much what ghosts think of her activities, though I suppose the same is true of her attitude toward living people as well. She began rummaging around in the bar while the bartender looked on, a bit put off until he asked her what she thought she was doing. Meanwhile, I struck up a conversation with Thomas Wyndham. He was a very handsome, charming fellow. We talked a bit about the war, and he was of the opinion that Hitler was not the real threat. Our so-called ally Stalin was the truly dangerous one. We chatted a bit about it, and I told him that if Stalin kept supporting the Chinese like he was, things would go poorly in the long run. Eventually I turned the conversation to Thomas himself and asked what he did for a living. He was a bit evasive, but eventually admitted that he worked for the IRS, though he was at the hotel on vacation.

                                                            Sara came down to join us about then, and struck up a conversation with the bartender, Bertrand Stanley. He told her that he had been there forever. She asked about Wynona, and he said she had become fascinated by the hotel after learning about Sandra’s murder, and ended up spending a lot of time there, mostly in her room. She had been spending more and more time there during the late 30’s and early 40’s because she was on the out in Hollywood.

                                                            He did tell Sara something curious, as though he knew more than he really should. He told her that some of the people at the hotel really needed help. “Heaven rewards good deeds,” he said. Perhaps he knows more about what’s going at the hotel, but if so, how and why?

                                                            Alexia and Sara also struck up another conversation with Troy, who was charming as always. They decided to test him a bit, and the conversation turned to talk of the basement. He didn’t seem to know much about anything down there, but agreed to go with them to check it out. He was apparently surprised to find it caved in, and did not seem comfortable with the elevator. He did not behave strangely in the basement, and that was our first sign that he might not be the entity behind this haunting.
