    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    I find it especially ridiculous to think that ocean entities are focused on taking over the land by drowning it.
    That's fine. But it's what at least some of the aforementioned entities are interested in. You don't have to use them but it's what they're about and while you may stand firmly against it if someone's asking about ocean-oriented monsters in the game then I see no reason why we should hide or deny their existence.

    War Against the Pure, p205, "Unfortunately, the apocalypse cult has taken to trying to usher in the consuming wave by appeasing Mother Ocean with violent sacrifices. Animals, humans, maybe even a Forsaken. It’s not without effect. The water elementals that lurk along the shore are growing potent with the sacrifices that are ostensibly in their honor. The sea grows wine-dark with bloody resonance, and threatens to truly rise up and come crashing down upon the shore."

    Pollution and trash are certainly a big thing happening there. If there's a big conflict among modern oceanic spirit courts and whatever pass as Uratha down there, it certainly has recent trash formations and temperature changes among their main reasons. But big large scale changes in the ocean aren't really such a new thing, so from a long scale perspective it isn't a reason to think the ocean needs the Uratha.
    Werewolves, as a whole and at the table, aren't an omnipresent force. What is happening in the Pacific Ocean may not have a direct impact to a pack on Miami Beach. But if things are happening in all the oceans, a game about the pack on Miami Beach can deal with how it impacts them.

    Also because, again, most of those phenomena are far more drastic above the ocean than on them. Water warming is a big problem, but weather isn't so much. While we face violent storms, droughts, typhoons and the like due to climate change, those phenomena have very little direct impact in oceanic life, and less of an impact the deeper you get. It is quite the opposite: a 1ºC change in oceanic temperature that has medium to low impact on oceanic life (it does have some, and may deeply affect some species depending on where it happens) is more than enough to generate a surge in catastrophic weather above water.
    Werewolf has referenced climate change as a source of problems (and ways spirits war among each other) since its inclusion in one of the first books, Chicago.

    Chicago, p273, referring to a particularly harsh winter exacerbated by climate change. "Elementals of cold and water gain sudden power during this time, more so than in a normal winter storm. Manyof them begin to assume dominance over lesser spirits, consuming them or forcing them to perform tasks for them."

    It's Winter. The book points out that to most Urtha the cold is "more a nuisance than anything." But in the Hisil it's a mess, with most sources of Essence (loci and eating other spirits) tainted with cold to the point that spirits of all sorts are coated with ice. And that, in turn, spurs a brood of fire-spirits to decide to sacrifice the entire city of Chicago in an inferno to fight back against the spirits of Winter.

    And the trend continues throughout the books to the most recent one, Shunned by the Moon, where Jeremiah Fury the storm-claimed fueled by climate change represents a different aspect of the same conflict. The whole game is about how issues, even seemingly innocuous ones, can cause massive repercussions.

    Ships and entertainment are meaningless in this regard. Our impact through direct presence is, fortunately, extremely more limited in the ocean than it is in landlocked wild environments. We can cause havoc on coastal areas, but from a oceanic perspective they're less of a thing than they are from a continental perspective. Our biggest ship can barely be noticed passing just a dozen meters below surface, even a submarine is a lonely spec in the middle of a realm where IRL whales and giant squids are already a thing. Are they something the individuals present will noticed? Yes. But hardly will they cause lasting, meaningful changes to the system itself.
    All it takes is one magath to throw an entire swath of the spiritual ecosystem out of balance. Not only have we been using ships to cruise across the ocean and dump trash and chemicals in for over a century now, but we've actively had wars there. We've developed and lost nuclear submarines. Even before modern times, ships were used to raid and plunder and bring war, riches, plague, religion, slavery, and entire civilizations to other lands and occasionally just throwing them overboard.

    And that's just one part of it, just 'the water.'

    The other part is the sea life. Ignoring the pollution and 'islands' of garbage, we've still managed to destroy huge populations of fish and sea mammals. "Ocean fish numbers cut in half since 1970." "Commercial Fleets Reduced Big Fish by 90%, Study Says." This has huge impacts that affect countries in real life, even leading to combat between nations. I'm not sure why it wouldn't have any impact in a world where there's an added, spiritual component.

    I mean a lot of these things if you don't merely have justification for disruptive spirits or other monsters, you have good reasons for heavily infectious Wounds.

            Originally posted by nofather View Post

            I agree, but that was not the question presented. The question was if the ocean Hisil took care of itself or what, and I said it certainly has no need for the Uratha or land-based "spirit police".

            That the ocean will affect the land is a given, actually. Indeed, one of the interesting aspects of the ocean to explore in a game is just how much it can affect the land while being itself unaffected in return. Althou you do have a point about some shallow waters ecosystems that I'll have to address.

            Originally posted by nofather View Post
            Werewolf has referenced climate change as a source of problems (and ways spirits war among each other) since its inclusion in one of the first books, Chicago.
            Climate change is an interesting and powerful example of the above, thanks to the laws of thermodynamics.

            Most of it will never affect the oceans in the same way it affects the land. Indeed, the average rising in temperature is practically the only aspect of climate change that affects it, hence the old term Global Warming. Otherwise, the oceanic waters have weak response to localized and temporary changes in temperature, and practically no response to winds, precipitation or electrical currents, even shallow water life can dive deep enough to ignore those when they occur. So no big impact in the oceanic Hisil except exclusively by the general raise in temperature.

            But on the other direction? The ocean is the single main climate engine of the planet. While mountains, rivers and forests play an important role, it is just without comparison. Due to the sheer mass of the ocean, any slight variation in its temperature packs a huge, almost apocalyptic amount of energy, and air reacts to that energy far more promptly. Less than 1ºC is enough to make a hurricane with more energy than the entire electrical output of the state of New York in a year.

            For the ocean that's basically a hiccup.

            This is the theme I was pointing to, as it relates to the question as stated. The Uratha probably should worry about the ocean if they have any knowledge of how climate works, but from the ocean's perspective things are far different and the Uratha aren't needed. The waters do have their problems and events, but nothing they can't handle on their own, and if they ever need someone else to handle it, it will have to be someone specialized, not some insignificant land meddler.

            And while there are things happening in the land that may affect it, those things generally only do so at the end of global-spanning chains of events that make local-based packs effectively incapable of doing shit about it. The focal intervention the Uratha are used to has little impact on the ocean unless under extremely specific circumstances.

            Originally posted by nofather View Post
            All it takes is one magath to throw an entire swath of the spiritual ecosystem out of balance. Not only have we been using ships to cruise across the ocean and dump trash and chemicals in for over a century now, but we've actively had wars there. We've developed and lost nuclear submarines. Even before modern times, ships were used to raid and plunder and bring war, riches, plague, religion, slavery, and entire civilizations to other lands and occasionally just throwing them overboard.
            As Satchel said, space and movement in the ocean is very different from what it is in land.

            Lets say we have a factory spilling pollution and that generates a Magath. If this happens at a small forest, the spiritual ecosystem affected is fairly limited and the Magath will probably at first want to stay at the forest. A small forest likely don't have a lot of powerful spirits to deal with it, or if it has they send it whining to the outskirts of the forest in a short time. One way or another, the Magath end up in a region of limited space (if big to our standards), and with few enemies with the power to actually destroy it in short order, so it can feast and grow.

            On the oceans there's always more ocean. While the waters aren't actually uniform, its ecosystems spam far larger areas and have a far more robust use of three dimensional space. Here, every place the Magath is expelled from, it can just wander to another not-so-terribly-worse one without any contact with the previous. And it gets moving on and on until it finally pisses of enough one of the continent-spanning elementals to whom Rank 5 spirits are an afterthought.

            Either they die because the ocean at large isn't forgiving, or they end up in some spot where, for all practical purposes, they're a land problem.

            Not to say that after some time the ecosystem can actually absorb some of those new elements into it. We have to ditch the WtA idea of pollution = Wyrm Taint. A lost nuclear submarine will produce Magath? On a sense, yes at first, as the local Court probably won't be accepting of the newcomer spirits, but those spirits will be able to form a society of their own as the submarine remains there, and with time the local court will probably reach some understanding with them, at least as far as spirits do.

            Dumping thrash and chemicals affects far more the coastal areas than the oceans at large, oil spills are the worst but they also affect coastal life disproportionately. The oceans aren't completely immune to those things, but they still work it on the scale factor, either it is out of proportion or the ocean shrugs it with an ease the coast and land can't. Our wars do very little to the ocean, and the spirits they spawned there will be locked and isolated to either perish or reach an accord with the local courts, and that applies to everything else we did overseas.

            Picture it: our entire existence over the seas barely covered a fraction of the surface of a realm that is insurmountably bigger than said surface. Yes, you can have the very point where a ship sunk or a battle occurred be a thing in the Hisil, but is that thing seen as anything resembling a significant problem for the Ocean Hisil it can't solve by itself?

            Originally posted by nofather View Post
            The other part is the sea life.
            This is a sticking point of my argument, though, and you're right: while our direct impact is generally limited, our predations over some lifeforms are another matter, at least to some extent.

            It is important to contextualize this first, though, but I admit I won't close the question of our impacts on those species.

            Our impact on oceanic life is mostly related to big fish and mammals. Those creatures live mostly in the uppermost level of the oceanic surface, rarely reaching the 100 m mark that goes actually deeper than most continental platforms and is only halfway through to the first change in photic zone. The average depth of the ocean is 3.7 Km, so we're talking about a 1/37th fraction of oceanic volume in those areas.

            Even at those depths, those species act far more as consumers for the oceanic biomass transferring it to their predators in land, coast and air.
