                                                So. In regards to fire burning within you, here's an idea that I had.

                                                Condition: Fire of Retribution
                                                Type: persistent

                                                Effects: Vengeance burns within you. Ever since you found yourself in this mess, it has never been far from your mind but now that you've taken your first steps it's more than just a thought. It's an urge. You go to sleep with it, you wake up to it, you eat, drink and breathe it. It gives you strength and resolve to see it through and makes it very difficult to focus on doing anything but exacting retribution.

                                                While you are pursuing vengeance in the most direct way possible, gain +1 to all dice pools and have one less wound penalty (applies only in combat). For example, the dice pool bonus would apply to all combat rolls while trying to put down your target, but also more subtle actions such as preparing explosives for an assault on the corporate headquarters, finding out where exactly your target within the build is located, or even analyzing floor plans to figure out how to get to the target once the chaos starts. However, it wouldn't apply to seducing his colleague to dig up dirt on him, tailing him for days to see his daily routine or making him confess once you get to him.

                                                Additionally, you need to pass a Resolve + Composure roll in order to focus on extended actions that make you stray from following leads towards the target of your vengeance. Having a non-vengeful breakfast or fixing your car is fine. Helping a friend renovate his house might fall into this category, depending on the circumstances and time spent (might be fine for an afternoon if you don't know where your target is, but certainly would require a roll if you know your target's address, you've already seen him home alone and you'd need to spend days working on the house). Stepping away from the target when you have him in your sights would always require this roll. Every subsequent roll is made with a cumulative -1 penalty if no steps towards following your vengeance have been made in between. In more abstract circumstance, the roll needs to be made once a day. Directly confronted with the target, roll is made once per round: you might want to immediately leave your target's presence if you don't want to attack him.

                                                Resolution: Once the vengeance is exacted, the condition is resolved. Other than that, significant efforts would need to be made by the character in order to resolve it peacefully which would best be left to ST and player to agree on for each individual case. Joining a Buddhist monastery for a couple of years might be one way. Having yourself committed to a mental institution for a while would be another. Increasing Integrity should probably be a component here.

                                                Beat: The fire inside you burns so bright and hot, it might be starting to consume you. While exacting vengeance in whatever way, each time your actions cause a Breaking Point, gain a beat.

                                                    You know, its interesting that people keep using the term "limited line" in regard to new lines. I thought part of the Onyx Path practice was that all lines are effectively open (a la the "Big 3"), and so will grow with time, but at a slower rate given their new scale. The full potential of Deviant, or any lines present and future, effectively takes years to unfold deliberately, or else risk running into the errors other products have had like 1. The line has little definition or appeal without more fleshing out (Promethean), 2. Popularity demands more releases for a property, reducing resources allocated elsewhere (Changeling), or 3. Trying to make the venture self-contained, where the premise is engaging but is left to exist in a relative conceptual vacuum (Geist). Allowing the material to be returned to at any time leaves for sporadic but continuing growth, which is a stronger base for a new edition of a game universe than many have a shot for.

                                                    That said, I was not expecting Deviant to be our new perspective into the World of Darkness.

                                                    It seems familiar in theme (at first glance, albeit) to several games which might already exist (in nWoD or not), but I think the most analogous example is found in a fansplat. RPG.net had a number of threads pertaining to fansplats over the years, one of which underwent an in-depth attempt and an abbreviated revival attempt some years ago. I believe these were known as Soldier: the Betrayed and Soldier: the Perfected, respectively. Here are the links: http://forum.rpg.net/showthread.php?...r-The-Betrayed ; http://forum.rpg.net/showthread.php?...-the-Perfected . I'm not stating I believe these ideas are the same, but that the fan creations have themes that closest match what Deviant might try to convey. I hope someone more informed or involved might weigh in on that matter.

                                                    But analogous or no, thinking of Deviant from that perspective gives me some idea for what the game involves. These aren't mummies and prometheans, purpose built to pursue dead values of a deathless empire with life unending, or captured activation in a shell of old lives, seeking to define a more complete existence in their most immediate means. Perhaps the Deviants can be marked in death or can be dead, to a degree, but that doesn't define them. Perhaps they have drives and imperatives to accomplish, old code-words and conditioning that one day drive a grandmother to leave the children for hours so she can sift through dirt in the old woods until she finds wooden carvings of indeterminate make and great value. Or perhaps, so caught up in espionage and subterfuge for years, a change in the flow of the squamous gel where his stomach used to be sends a man out of observation for the first time, scattered and wary as he tries to determine the threat potential of people using library computers. But these shouldn't define what a Deviant must do. Likewise, these aren't changelings or demons, beings stolen and warped by unbound forces that only regain their agency by forcing their aberrance into some mold resembling their old selves, or ethereal occult programs divorced from their designer by building enough inborn contradiction to change their nature as concept machinations to something with comparable agency and grounded, variable capability. It's possible the child dreaming of basketball was abducted, and spent so many years being filled with wire and conductors he can't remember what it was like to have aspirations until he tore the cables out and broke into an old repair shop, crafting some mess out of what used to be fans and circuit boards to keep himself even. And it's possible there's some poor brat born of feeding tubes and fabricators, following endless trails in lights reflected from the mirror mazes that blend with its skin, until one day it finds a path too worn to be reflective and tunnels through the walls behind the mirrors, lurking in old sewers until it gets its bearings enough to finally see itself clearly for the first time through an old, shadowed puddle. Maybe those can define them, but it doesn't define a Deviant's struggle.

                                                    .It's possible that what a Deviant is can best be seen in their utilization of their circumstances. These people, bent on finding their makers and halting their works are agents not because of their making or by their makers, but of their making. A mage dreams-lives an imprinting of the cosmos-symbolized into their souls and attains enlightenment, and a beast recoils from entrenched, matchless fear until their essence is the fear embodied, but Deviants are forged by a group that wants easy revelation by engineering or by forces less enigmatic than the truest terror and the rawest understanding. A sin-eater allows the spirit-ghost-incarnation the bond that binds them indistinguishably to create a living receptacle of all things death and life inseparable, as much the true lack of either for both hosts, and hunters elect to wade in the dark and find all things banal and necessary beyond the rest of his world but within it, but Deviants can only really choose when they've rent their control or at least, when the bargains not so sweet anymore.

                                                    Vampires are made with agency and some even volunteer to become life ended but not forfeit, wresting others from and pooling in their very source of power and waking. Maybe the same decisions can be said of the Deviant or their makers, but they aren't bound to their old humanity and succor like these creatures are the vein, and their curses might well be more "tangible". Werewolves are often born, and even when they aren't all erupt into avatars of union and change, the wolf/moon/human-spiritual avatars of urges-sanctified and boundaries-wielded. Maybe the Deviant is born with the proclivity for their creation or have this foisted upon them unheralded through horrid lottery, but the creation imparts no law for their kind, and they are beholden to no sire of theirs.

                                                    To be deviant isn't a becoming or realization, nor is it the reconciling of the lot given to them. It is mastery: the reclaiming of the self to include what they took - what they earned - when others decided to inflict it upon them. Perhaps they didn't result in the purpose they were forged for, or perhaps they are promising beyond reckoning. But that no longer matters. That power's in the hands of the made, now.

                                                    And the Deviant one will use that for their own ends to play.
