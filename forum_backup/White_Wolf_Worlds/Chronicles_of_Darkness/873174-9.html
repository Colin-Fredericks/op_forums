                                    I've been working on stuff in the background. Since you're fleshing out antagonists and setting, I figured I'd put some elbow grease into mechanics.

                                    I made a second pass of Ablutions and my first pass of Purviews.

                                    You can read what I've come up with in this google document.


                                    Don't mind my terminology: I was trying out some different words for the power types but you can trade them out with a simple find>replace.

                                    Instead of "Purviews", I used "Sovereignties". Purviews makes me think of Scion; perhaps I am alone in that perception.

                                    Instead of "Impositions", I used Mandates. It's such a good word and tied so well to Chinese dragons; it seemed a shame to discard it. The verb is still "proclaim".

                                    "Breath" is "Eupnea" in the document. It is a medical term for "A restful breath" so I felt it was appropriate.

                                    "Finder" is "Delver" and "Trickster" is "Grifter"... call it a holdout from third phase. I think those terms suit the methods better but that's up to your editor.


                                    A few points to keep in mind:
                                    • I've eliminated Resistant wounds (the ones marked with the registered trademark symbol) in exchange for giving the orobori a LOT more Health.
                                      .
                                      • The Health bar is divided into three sections: Divine dots (from ablutions), Corporeal dots (from human traits), and Heart dots (Always 5).
                                        .
                                        Divine dots are the sum total of what is possible for a true form dragon. Players can safely mark and leave these dots due to a mechanic I've introduced.
                                        .
                                        Corporeal dots are protected by Divine dots AND Ablutions; there's a lot of Health keeping an oroboros alive. That said, jade directly damages the Corporeal meter, and this is the one that triggers the (effective) death state when it is filled.
                                        .
                                        The Heart meter is a backwash basin for overflow damage: overflow doesn't begin until the Heart meter pushes wounds away. That said, it's not safe to fill this meter much: at full lethal it starts trying to reject the Oroboros for a new owner and at full agg it is destroyed. Both these conditions trigger fossilization, which erases one health dot each day until death occurs (starting immediately), the Heart is reclaimed and consumed again or--if it was destroyed--a new Heart is obtained.
                                        .
                                        .
                                      • Ablutions now serve as miniature Health meters. Any damage a dragon sustains blasts away her human 'flesh suit' to reveal divine traits: the character benefits from an ablution when it has damage under its dot. This also means disbanding and healing are the same action: the more severe the injury to manifest the ablution the longer it takes to hide when the fight is over.
                                        .
                                      • Breath has been given a boost to how much health it can replenish. It removes three bashing wounds, two lethal wounds, or one aggravated wound. This is to compensate for how much health an oroboros actually has to heal to look human again.
                                        Because of the high demand for Breath/Eupnea, I HIGHLY recommend reincorporating the Asset system IN ADDITION to the current gorging mechanic. I was going to write about it in this document but decided that's content for chapter two. Still, here are my recommendations:
                                        .
                                        Eating something also restores Breath/Eupnea. The amount restored is based on the size of the food consumed (one to one), PLUS Resolve if the food was a living thing (Even if it's been cooked: eating a steak would give 3 Breath/Eupnea: 1 size + 2 Resolve from the cow), PLUS Potency if the food was supernatural (Eating a werewolf would usually give about 10 Breath/Eupnea: 6 size, 2 Resolve, 2 Primal Urge).
                                        .
                                        Eating something inedible adds its flammability to the Breath/Eupnea garnered: one meal should translate to one point of fuel so dividing calories by 250 for the fuel should tell you roughly how much additional Breath/Eupnea it grants.
                                        .
                                        Assets store a fixed amount of Breath/Eupnea based on their rating. They can even be merits. An Asset must be a tangible noun (Person/Place/Thing, NOT an idea). Spending a scene with an Asset replenishes its Breath/Eupnea. Food can partially replenish an Asset, but the most efficient way to do so is spending time with it.
                                        .
                                        .
                                    • Degree of Divinity is gone. Ablutions are strong enough as is. Besides, with the new narrative the character doesn't really become more divine; they just hide it under a flesh suit like Edgar the Bug from Men in Black.
                                      .
                                    • True Form now refers to having all ablutions manifest (and thus being vulnerable to damage against the health meter) and provides no additional benefit. Again, ablutions are strong enough.
                                      .
                                    • I wrote ten Sovereignties with five Mandates each. They all are roughly independent, but it's okay to introduce Mandates which overlap--provided they overlap with Mandates of OTHER sovereignties.
                                      .
                                      • A Sovereignty proclamation has INDEFINITE duration, and is only erased by losing all of its Sovereignty's Authority
                                        .
                                      • A new proclamation overwrites an old one: only one proclamation is allowed per Sovereignty at a time. This is to keep the bookkeeping complexity of indefinite powers down.
                                        .
                                      • The biggest component of this new setup is that proclamations can use multiple powers as a single command. Read the example proclamations to see how this is intended to be used.
                                        .
                                      • Dice pools were significantly simplified: each Sovereignty only rolls its rating and Heartstring to activate.
                                        .
                                      • I eliminated emotion-manipulating powers. While they seem interesting on the surface, it is better to encourage players to invoke emotions by manipulating the environment around a victim, rather than trigger emotions directly. This is why there are only ten sovereignties instead of your original sixteen.
                                        .
                                      • Mechanic overlap is a big problem here, particularly because the mechanics need to be able to scale. Conditions might be a good way to get around this when making more Sovereignties/purviews. Alternatively, it might not be bad to let Mandates from separate Sovereignties achieve the same effect: it'd allow a combination of proclamations to break the hard 5-point limit these powers possess.
                                    Okay. That's what I've got for you. I'm'a go draw more pictures now. ^_^

                                            Originally posted by Killerclown View Post
                                            ​Can I have some advice on how to make their backstory less cliché?
                                            Okay, let's take a look at what you've got.

                                            We'll start with the aesthetic.
                                            Your recruiters have taken on the appearance of the door-to-door salesman (or missionary... or in your case both). In such a profession the pleasant, well-kept visage is a means of delaying the slam of a door. After all, if they were shabby the person answering the door might assume they were pan-handling derelicts or--worse--criminals.
                                            You're playing this concept straight, but it sounds like you want to play up the creep-out factor with it. So let's play with it.

                                            You could defy this idea by making them actually appear shabby. Maybe people do mistake them for criminals, but the charm of their pitch overpowers the gut reaction of fear (which is why Sunstone is so successful).

                                            Alternatively, they could keep the clean-cut look by opening their pitch by posing as security contractors and then explain that real security comes from the acquisition of power: "We'll teach you how to stand your ground".

                                            You could emphasize their instability by having them wear a particularly out-of-place article of clothing despite their "suit and tie" facade. Something pops to mind immediately: perhaps they wear ties made out of aluminum foil. They could even make this a conversation piece:
                                            "Yeah, I used to be a tin-foil hat, but then Sunstone really showed me the way to go. I wear this tie to remind me where I was and keep me tuned to a bright future... instead of aliens *laughs*."

                                            You could turn into the skid by making them VERY creepy. For instance, maybe the recruiters always come in threes, and always stand in a triangle. They move and walk in this formation, blink in unison, and even perform the same gestures (if you hurt one of them, all three flinch). This is because they've actually be Drowned by a Deep One and are really one amorphous monstrosity: they've managed to scrape together some form of illusion to hide their hideous mass, but the magick is finnicky and requires multiple illusory bodies to hide the whole body.


                                            On to motives.
                                            First, I want to know more about this religion of yours that was the original body of the cult before Cottonwood took over. What was their goal in worship? What fears were they trying to allay or what changes were they trying to make to the world? Why was their faith so threatening that an Orobori den destroyed them? People turn to religion when they are suffering, and they choose what to worship based on how that suffering might be relieved or given purpose. Something about these gods gave the monks a reason to worship them: that reason could be a lie but we need to know what the lie might be before we can see the weight of the truth underneath it. Given the old gods are dreaming horrors, I doubt they pitched their goals straight.

                                            The concept of the twin gods bothers me primarily because one has a concept which is open to social interpretation (pleasure has the context to be a positive thing) while the other does not (nightmares are universally bad). I'd recommend making them BOTH gods of death and dreams: the first steals good dreams (with the cult believes are taken from the wicked and given to the devout) while the second eats bad dreams (thereby protecting the cult from the harm they can cause). Though the old gods both represent death, they give promise of consciousness eternal in the sea of dreams, floating blissfully on a gentle current at the eye of the storm, with non-believers dragged down into the swirling chaos. The cult also believes the twin gods will grant them the comfort of death in sleep: they have heard from their leader Cottonwood that the devout always die while dreaming and thus assume that death will be painless (spoiler alert: it's not).

                                            Now, what is the cult's goal on the corporeal plane? Clearly they seek to increase their number. Perhaps they are trying to collect minds under a single idea so that--once adrift in the Dreamtide--they can combine their strengths to build some sort of civilization on the other side of the Ora. Cottonwood may be promising this city will be a utopia but instead be planning to use it as a place to launch attacks against other Orobori, whom he despises. Keep in mind cults are scary when the goals sold to the bottom of the pyramid are little more than power machinations of the peak.
                                            What does Cottonwood personally seek to gain by fishing in the dreams of these two old gods? Is it oblivion for oblivion's sake? Does he hope to somehow steal their power? Maybe he (foolishly) believes he can execute them while they sleep and then fill the void of power which forms (not realizing that the oceanic plate at the bottom of the Dreamtide is comprised of the intertwined bodies of the old gods, and beneath it waits a boiling mantle of terrible energy which if released would obliterate the entire Dreamtide and sleep as the world understands it).


                                            I know that's a lot to chew on. Still, hopefully it gives you some ideas.
                                            Take what you need and make a write-up for your antagonist. Then put it into a team pitch for Raccoon.

                                            Stick with it, you're on the right track! ^_^
