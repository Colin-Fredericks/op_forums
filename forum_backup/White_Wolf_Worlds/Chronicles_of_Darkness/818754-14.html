            It's in a few of them, but if you're looking for the most notable example, it's in the Daeva Clanbook, when a member of the Carthians describes the consequences of Hurricane Katrina for Kindred throughout the United States. All those deaths are felt keenly through Blood Sympathy, and if that's not an indicator of how powerful it can be, I don't know what is. (Though, another obvious one is that you can feel it from your sire from anywhere, no matter the distance). If it has to be called out, the Clanbooks are one of the explicitly called out products to use with the 2nd Edition.

            Also:

            Originally posted by Vampire The Requiem Second Edition, pg. 99
            Sympathy comes into effect automatically any time a vampire’s relative enters torpor or suffers Final Death. The feeling washes over the vampire and she senses her relative’s state in vague, often symbolic terms. As well, characters can initiate sympathy to communicate their states of being to their relatives once or twice removed. To do so, they must spend a Willpower point and make a Blood Potency roll. Each success allows them to contact a single relative; the relative must still make the roll to detect sympathy. Any particularly powerful emotion can trigger sympathy, at Storyteller discretion. This is less a foolproof detective tool, and a more dramatic device. Any time a relative suffers a horrible fate, sympathy may cause frenzies.
            We can observe that Final Death factually causes Blood Sympathy to trigger automatically. So there's no debate to be had about that. I'll bold above the things that lend itself to the conclusion that they can almost certainly sense when someone has been diablerized, but I'll stress that I doubt it'll tell them who did it, because as the last sentence states, this is less of a foolproof detective tool, and more of a dramatic device.

            (The last sentence also probably explains what happened as a result of Katrina, mind!)

            Separate from that, there's a point to be made about the nebulousness of actual canon in the Chronicles of Darkness and Vampire's ambiguous status as the most permeable of the lines in that regard. Check out references to 1e content such as the Icarian Bloodline or the Sanctified Creeds in Requiem for Regina. I think it's deliberate that both in 1e and in 2e, that the core does not state anything like what is said in Blood Red + Ash Grey to allow people to make that decision at their table. I think it contributes to the drama and interest of the game, and that the way Diablerie is discussed, it follows that one should be able to identify diablerie as something that actually occured. There isn't much of a witch hunt to be had if the only way to tell diablerie even occurred (that is, absent the answer of who did it) is to look at the person who did it.

                        Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
                        There isn't much of a witch hunt to be had if the only way to tell diablerie even occurred (that is, absent the answer of who did it) is to look at the person who did it.
                        Err... The whole point of comparing a diablerist hunt to a witch hunt is to point out that there might not even be a diablerist at all, just like there might not have been any actual witches executed at witch trials. It doesn't much matter if a Diablerie has actually occurred or not if roughly a fifth of your population has the power to accuse anyone in the community of a heinous crime based on evidence that the other four fifths of the population can't verify.

                        All it took in Salem was a couple of girls who didn't like their babysitter saying they saw her doing magic. Whether or not it was true, it was the spark that ignited a powder keg of paranoia and hatred. Likewise, all it would take in a vampiric domain is one Mekhet telling the authorities that she saw the stains of Diablerie on her rival's aura. Sure, you could get another Auspex user to verify the accusations, but you're still relying on one person's word against another. Then what happens when one claims the other is in league with the Diablerist? What if she pays off another Auspex user to "confirm" her claims? What about when other Auspex users decide to take advantage of the situation and start accusing people they don't like?
