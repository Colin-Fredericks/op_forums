    If so, abilities like Channel Spite and Ebon Fury are a mug's game. You have a strict limit on the number of times you can use Penumbrae before you lock yourself into the damage-or-breaking-point loop, so each use had better give a major advantage. A scene-long buff or debuff may be worth what you're paying; a single ranged attack, or a few turns of boosted strength, aren't. A Penumbra should be designed on the assumption that it'll see use maybe twice in a session.

    An alternative, if that's not what you had in mind, is that going over the limit just imposes the Darkened Condition, with all that that implies. It's a lot harder to get out of being Darkened, but the Condition doesn't kill you by inches as this drawback would.

                    So, how's this version?
                    Endowment: Cold Resolve (• to •••••)
                    Prerequisite: Status (Cold Guard) 1+
                    Effect: The Guardsman has faced their sins and taken part of the Darkness into their own heart. Only through chilly resolve and determination, do they hold its corruption at bay. They can acquire Penumbrae or Darkness-based Dread Powers, with each one giving them a permanent dot of Shadows. The Hunter can have Shadows equal to their dots in this Endowment plus their Resolve without affecting their Integrity or being treated as a creature of Darkness.
                    Drawback: The corruption of the Darkness can't be held at bay forever, nor invoked lightly. Each use of a Dread Power, and most Penumbrae, gives the Guardsman a temporary point of Shadows. These Temporary Shadows fade at a rate of one per day (or once per Scene, if the Guardsman fulfills their Virtue that Scene). Whenever their Shadows are over their safe limit, the Guardsman is treated as a creature of Darkness, and must take 1 Lethal damage or suffer a Breaking Point. This happens for each new Shadow acquired, as well as once per day spent over their safe limit.

                                                        Obviously my views are going to be biased because I worked on some of these groups, but nevertheless my opinions:

                                                        The Crown Jewels / Free Company

                                                        The issue I have with the Crown Jewels is that they go against a core principal of Hunter: the Vigil: Hunters defeat supernatural opponents by using numbers, tactics, and exploiting their weaknesses. As written the Crown Jewels are the exact opposite. By trying to manipulate and manage Princesses, and compete for the loyalty of sworn they're going against Princesses on their strengths, not their weaknesses.

                                                        The closest analogue would be the Bear Lodge who compete with Werewolves against their strength of hunting; but note that the Bear Lodge do exploit Werewolves' weaknesses with silver weapons, the Crown Jewels don't have an equivalent, and the Bear Lodge canonically have taken major losses. As a decentralised Compact they can afford those losses, but Princesses are much better at sharing information than Werewolves. If the Crown Jewels had a similar attrition rate to the Bear Lodge their secret would be exposed to the Noble community and it's game over.

                                                        The Free Company is an attempt to take the core concept of a hunter group of Sworn who work with and support Princesses, but avoid the above flaws. For example, rather than competing with Princesses for the Sworn's loyalty; I made it a win-win situation if a Princess recruits a member to be her retainer.

                                                        My vote is to use the Free Company.


                                                        The Star of Bethlehem

                                                        The positives: They're a believable group with plausible motivations. And they seem like the sort of group that would naturally exist.

                                                        The negatives: Their motives are incorrect as the Queens don't encourage children to fight monsters, they're just genere savvy and know it's pointless to try and stop them (unless the ST twists the Princess setting to make the Star of Bethlehem right). Their goal of opposing the Queens is also mostly impossible, since they can't reach the Dreamlands, and the Queens are demigods surrounded by entire armies of magic wielders.

                                                        I'm not sure if they'll appeal to players given the above.

                                                        My thought: I wouldn't object to dropping them. I would object to making them an Endowed Conspiracy with a real chance of fighting Queens – it would remove the “ordinary parent in an unordinary situation” vibe that makes the group good in the first place.

                                                        Giving them a more achievable goal could work, but I'm not sure what goal could be: Relatable, achievable, and conflicts with Princesses.

                                                        My vote: Abstain, but I'd vote against turning them into a Conspiracy.


                                                        The Cold Guard

                                                        My views are simple. Any group who outright tries to hunt and kill Princesses needs to have a philosophical reason for doing so, and that reason needs to be solid enough that players can make a convincing argument in character. The Cold Guard don't do this, the replacement groups do.

                                                        My vote: Replace them.

                                                        The Cold Guard (Vent0's version)

                                                        It's still early days so it's hard to say too much. I like the Endowment. I like the philosophical opposition to nobles based on overcoming your sins / managing your sins and drawing strength from them; and I'd like to see their methods of fighting the Darkness thematically embody that philosophy. Bleak Oubliette is a good start (I did say it was my favourite ).

                                                        I'm less keen on the bit about opposing nobles because nobles want to make people dependent upon them. It's not exactly wrong, but I think it's less interesting than “ overcoming your sins / drawing strength from them”.

                                                        My vote: It's really too early to say. But it shows promise.

                                                        The People's Guard

                                                        I very much like that it ties into real world history and a real world philosophical idea that actually would object to Nobles on principal.

                                                        Given that they're willing to kill over that principal, and their origins in the bloodthirsty French Revolution, I suspect they'll come off as fanatics and morally tainted, but at the same time that's not a bad thing. There's plenty of morally dubious Hunters, and it's kinda neat to see fanatics who aren't religiously motivated. I've never heard of an urban fantasy group who object to the way magic creates a privileged class before.

                                                        However like Vent0's Cold Guard it's early in development so it's hard to say much.

                                                        My vote: It's really too early to say. But it shows promise.

                                                        The Church of the Valley

                                                        (The new name for the Sanitation Workers Collective. Link is here)

                                                        Thus far I think they're the only group we have who fit the morally white/grey Conspiracy niche. The People's Guard are grey/grey at best because of their fanaticism, and Vent0's Cold Guard are powered by the physical embodiment of evil and can be corrupted by it so grey/grey at best; their philosophy of accepting and drawing strength from their own sins also strikes me as anti-heroic (I recommend embracing that, who doesn't like a good anti-hero?).

                                                        And I think this group has a lot going for it. They have a philosophical objection to Princesses and a modus operandi that can result in the two groups working together or working against each other depending on the situation. There's a nice “protect the world that looks down on you vibe” to it. They explore parts of Hunter that rarely get explored – which is to say they put a lot of focus on actually acting like a conspiracy, infiltrating the corridors of power and manipulating them. But at the same time you can still run them in the classic hunter style of finding monsters and fighting them.

                                                        Myself and MP also addressed the previous issues, giving them a religious angle to make them more distinct from the people's guard and a more defined origin for their Endowment.

                                                        My vote: Include them.

                                                        Overall

                                                        I understand your worry about going in every direction at once, but I do not think there's a problem here.

                                                        Vent0's version of the Cold Guard, the People's Guard, and the Church of the Valley are all different groups that fill different niches. Rather than think of it as the redevelopment of the old Cold Guard being confused and pulled in different directions, treat it as three independent groups being developed side by side. Unlike official products you have no wordcount limits. I could see a 4/4 spread of

                                                        Compacts: #Ammit, The Light Company, Character Risk Analysis, Star of Bethlehem.

                                                        Conspiracies: Magisters, People's Guard, Cold Guard, Church of the Valley.

                                                        Working fine.
