            I modeled this power after the Air, Earth, Fire, and Water Elemental Dread Powers from Werewolf:the Forsaken 2nd Edition. these powers are either defensive (Earth, Fire), Offensive (Fire), or Movement (Air and Water). Currently I have it so that Lightning elemental is about on par with Fire Elemental power-wise. I would set up the ability to travel through a medium as a separate power.


            Transmit
            The Horror is capable of transmitting itself though space via a specific medium (such as mirrors, doorways, electrical wiring, etc) which is chosen when this Dread Power is selected. By stepping into their chosen medium (or changing into a form that can travel through the medium), the creature will exit 1 minute later from another location where the medium is present, within a range of (Potecy x 5) miles.

                        Here's something: a Thing That Should Not Be in 2E rules (also something from the Champions Universe)

                        Vulshoth, the Eye of the Void

                        One of the infamous entities known collectively as the Kings of Edom, Vulshoth is a native of the "Qliphotic World", a realm of "dying" universes that may or may not be the same thing as (or a subsection of, or something else) as the Abyss mages so fear. As with all his kind, he is a profoundly alien, amoral being with little care for any entity but himself-and like all the Kings of Edom, he was successfully imprisoned by ancient beings of extraterrestrial nature. In Vulshoth's case, he was imprisoned in something fitting his own influence, an extradimensional labyrinth within a metallic shell made of unknown matter. Normally Vulshoth could easily crack said shell, but the entity seems vulnerable to radiation-while the shell is not dangerous to humans, it prevents the Eye of the Void from regaining his strength. Unfortunately, the Kings do not need their power to find cracks in the prison small enough to send dreams through to those who might free them to feast on the life force of the universe again.

                        Power: 4. While Vulshoth is a potent creature, his imprisonment and inability to communicate directly with mortals (he must use prophetic dream and metaphor to translate his desires into something comprehensible) limits his influence on Earth. Hence the scheme of most of his cultists to construct "amplifiers" to focus and enhance his powers over space and enslavement.

                        Virulence: 8. This is why Edomites are so feared; they observed the current universe as it formed, and so they have adapted their powers to be almost part of reality. Vulshoth is no exception-once the Shining Darkness of the Qliphothic World enters the healthy universe, it stays until great sacrifice is needed to purge it.

                        Vulnerability: As with most of his kin, Vulshoth has been forced to accept two universal vulnerabilities that show the Kings of Edom to be of the same class of being. First is the Spider Sign, a crystal lens that has been electrified after a precise relief of an eight-legged sigil has been carved on it forces the Kings and their creations to relive ancestral memories of its initial creators, who imprisoned them in the first place-the only things they fear. Second is the Mordant Flame, the result of mixing five sacred herbs that are found in most ingredient stores (just because, say, aluminum is common doesn't mean it isn't valuable) with the pulped brain of a member of an alien race called an Elder Worm, mixed with accelerant. The result is a purplish-blue conflagration that, while simply stinky to most beings, might as well be the Sun's own plasma to Edomite creatures with actual flesh (even Vulshoth, who is a physical entity). In addition, Vulshoth and his direct creations have a noted vulnerability to any harmful source of ionizing radiation-doubtless Vulshoth could be more resistant at full power, but the architects of the Black Maze smartly did not risk that.

                            Paradigms
                            Paradigm is one's conception of how supernatural abilities should be. All supernatural effects are a product of will, but the psyche isn't well equipped to channel reality twisting effects without ascribing it some rationale. Paradigm is an intellectually consistent framework of why supernatural abilities works that serves as 'insulation' between the supernatural being and the power they wield.

                            In practice, a paradigm has two parts. First, it's a philosophy or methodology, an understanding of why and how supernatural powers work. This can be any known framework, so long as it's internally consistent and follows after mortal metaphysical tradition at least a hundred years old (though pseudo-sciences that old also work). Pseudo-science less than a hundred years old lack some quality that makes them suitable for use as a paradigm, while accurate and 'real' scientific theory fails entirely regardless of age. Increased supernatural powers may be understood as the ability to frame more and more abilities in the context of one's paradigm.

                            Secondly, and more concretely, it's a set of physical actions (formally called methods), locations prepared for the purpose, (called sanctums or laboratories) and/or physical objects (called implement or instrument) that the supernatural being uses to use its powers.

                            Paradigm always reflects a core aspect of the supernatural using it, tied to either its mortal upbringing, traumatic events that introduced it to the supernatural while still mortal, or the deep connection to the being that mentored them changing their Bane, and as such those that successfully identity a supernatural's paradigm gain the Informed Condition in relation to the contesting or identifying the supernatural creature's powers once per chapter. Additionally abilities of a supernatural to rise from the dead do not work if rest-in-peace practices appropriate to their paradigm are performed for them.

                            Each supernatural being with the Paradigm Bane defines what broad belief system they are tapping into to perform their spells or powers. Feel free to use one of the following sample Paradigms that is appropriate to your character or how your character gained the Paradigm bane, or use them as a measuring stick to judge what is appropriate in breadth of a belief system.

Spending 3 Willpower points adds its Potency as bonus Successes on a Stealth roll for one turn, or divides its Potency as bonus Successes across multiple turns. However, the enhanced animals suffer from the same Bans and Banes as the Horror while this effect lasts.

                                        Beastmaster +1: The Horror may conjure animals from nothing. Spending 1 Willpower point conjures a group of smaller animals, or one larger animal.                                                 Tappan Arkwright III

                                                Quote: "Soon I will be I, and sooner, you will be nothing at all."

                                                This lead-in is a lie. Arkwright, the person actually named as such, is not a Horror. In fact, he's not even a bad person by any reasonable stretch of the imagination. It's really not his fault he is host to the High Priest of Vulshoth, and while he can't quite overcome his unwanted passenger's survival instinct, he has been quietly responsible for thwarting his wicked brother's schemes more often than not.

                                                His brother, on the other hand, has no name. In fact, for much of Arkwright's life, nobody was aware that there was a brother-just a freakish birth defect. Quite simply, the Twin is a parasitic one, the result of an incomplete reabsorbtion that, due to Abyssal energies in the graveyard Arkwright was conceived, has a functioning brain despite it being the size of a walnut and sharing his brother's brain stem. Driven insane by isolation and the fear people have of a literal second face at the back of his brother's skull, the Twin came to despise the species he was nearly a part of, and his hate and despair called to Vulshoth's dreaming intellect. In symbolic dreams, the Eye made a deal with the Twin-in return for service, the King would give the Twin the ability to take control of his and Arkwright's shared body. In return for freedom, a place as a fully independent god-king at the Eye's side.

                                                As might be obvious from his title, the High Priest has made good on his promise.

                                                Description: Tappan Arkwright and the Twin share a body, so they share a description. Arkwright is an unassuming white Bostonian in bis 40s, one who is only notable for wearing silk scarves rather than ties in his conservative business suits. He wears his hair long to conceal the Twin's face in the back of his head, a lined two-inch diameter mug that has gained the ability to express since the deal with Vulshoth (it goes back to its original blankness when the Twin is in control, so the Twin doesn't let his sibling go to a barber-however, that does mean the local shops remember, and dread, Arkwright, as the Twin is a horrible and mean-spirited customer). One can tell who is currently driving by their stance and expression-Tappan is withdrawn, slumped, and shy, while the Twin is manic, vicious, and wilful.

                                                Storytelling Hints: Arkwright himself is a victim. He doesn't share memories with the Twin, but is all too aware of what, and who, his brother is. The Twin can't help but gloat, really, giving his "lucky" sibling a taste of the helplessness the Twin felt for decades. He tries to help when he can, but the Twin is wise to his brother's shenanigans and he can only manage manipulating his brother's considerable ego and inferiority complex. As for the Twin, his story may make him pitiable, but the idea of some disgusting human feeling for him is, if anything, even more offensive than the constant jeers he (actually Arkwright, but since when has a crazed worshiper of alien gods ever been known for perspective?) endured. Even beyond the deaths required to supply power for Edomite mysticism, the Twin regularly kills humans at relative random, deliberately chosing a wide palate to confuse the authorities-the police have no idea that the sudden increase in violent deaths is the work of one killer, let alone he's the same person as the Mind Flayer (the recharge ritual requires pulping the sacrifice's brain matter and extracting their cerebral fluid to drink later to revitalize the High Priest's powers).

                                                    Re: Advanced Dread Powers

                                                    I like this idea. I normally balance an Horror as if I was writing blue-book material (ie. with the idea that anyone going against the horror will be mostly mortal). That being said, Horror that go up against Template characters may need a bit more oomph to keep things challenging. However, Generally speaking, I try to avoid just adding more dice - dice bloat just slows down the action at the table. Strive for simplicity of effect. Here are a few of my notes and suggestions below.
                                                    Chameleon Horror +1: I like the overall idea of Advanced Dread Powers and I like giving the rote quality to a specific task. However, adding Potency extra dice to a roll the Horror is getting rote quality on, in addition to the penalties observers take from regular Chameleon Horror (not even considering the possibility of auto -successes) becomes really excessive. At a point, its easier to say "the creature is invisible" than giving it a massive dice pool. Try this version -

                                                    Invisible Horror (prerequisite -Chameleon Horror): The Horror becomes wholly invisible. The penalty the Horror imposes upon attempts to perceive it granted by Chameleon Horror becomes -6 in all circumstances. Supernatural perceptions can pierce this Invisibility, invoking a Clash of Wills against the Horrors Composure+Potency to negate the penalty imposed by this Dread Power until the end of the scene.In addition, the Horror receives Rote Quality on Stealth rolls, and may make Stealth rolls even when moving at full Speed. In most cases Invisibility is a permanent aspect of the creature, but some can control when they are visible. In the latter case, altering its visibility for a scene costs the Horror one point of Willpower. The creature’s Invisibility imposes a Ban - which is always condition where it does not apply (such as infrared light or when observed by children).
                                                    Natural Weapons +1: Offensive Powers are best kept simple - invoking a Clash of Will on each attack is probably too much.

                                                    Rending Attack (prerequisite - Natural Weapons ••): The Horror's Natural Attacks are imbued with power enough to rend even the most resilient flesh. The Horrors natural attacks gain the 9-Again quality and are considered supernatural damage. When the Horror inflicts damage with its Natural Weapons, it may spend a point of Willpower to convert a point of the Lethal Damage it inflicted to Aggravated Damage.
                                                    Beastmaster +1: With the first Beastmaster power, the effect can be streamlined a bit. The second Beastmaster power is not really necessary, as where the animals come from is more of a flavor consideration and not a explicit concern of the Beastmaster Dread Power.

                                                    Dark Menagerie (prerequisite - Beastmaster): The Horror not only can command animals, it can enhance them as well. By spending 1 Willpower, and making an appropriate roll, it may share one of its Dread Powers with any animal it commands that is within its line of sight. The animal gains use of the Dread Power until the end of the scene. On an exceptional Success, the Horror may share 1 additional Dread power with the animal. Any animal enhanced in this fashion suffers from the same Bans and Banes as the Horror while this effect lasts.
                                                    Hunter's Senses +1: 8 again is good, but a +6 dice bonus may be a bit much, and Potency auto-success (even with numerous drawbacks) may also be a bit much. This would be a good place for a special effect.

                                                    Relentless Pursuit (prerequisite - Hunter's Senses): The Horror gains 8 Again on Perception rolls against its favored prey. By spending 3 Willpower, and making an appropriate roll resisted by the targets Wits+Supernatural Tolerance, it may "mark" a chosen target. The target gains the Marked Condition. The Horror will know the distance and direction of a target it imposed this condition upon. Targets with the Marked Condition suffer a -3 penalty to rolls to avoid, flee or hide from the Horror, and any failures become dramatic failures. Attempts to use Supernatural methods of escape or Stealth invoke a Clash of Wills against the Horrors Resolve+Potency. The condition ends when the target either fulfills a "escape clause" or after a number of days equal to the Horrors Potency. This escape clause is typically a distance from the Horror (Potency x 20 miles is common) or location (such as hallowed ground or across a particular body of water).
                                                    Maze +1: I like Advanced version of Maze mostly as is. the only real adjust is its Clash of Will roll. Clash of Will rolls are typically "Power +Potency". To keep that parity, I suggest Attribute + Potency to keep it in line with how Clash of Wills are set up in most of the other game lines.

                                                    Escher Knot (prerequisite - Maze): Attempting to leave the Maze through supernatural means provokes a Clash Of Wills against the Horror's Resolve + Potency. The Horror gains Rote Quality on Chase rolls while inside the Maze. However, the Horror automatically acquires a Ban that forces it to release its captives in a certain situation.
