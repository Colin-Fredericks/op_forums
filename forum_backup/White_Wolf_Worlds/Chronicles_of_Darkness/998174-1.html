                                    Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw View Post
                                    Eh, doesn't matter. No, Abyssal entities aren't kin.
                                    Dave, I appreciate the position you can speak from as a dev and metaphysics guy, but the existence of a Kinship Nightmare that specifically references the nature of the Abyss, the applicability of Thicker Than Water to feral supernatural beings, and the fact that Family Ties is a Condition that the non-Beast party has to be receptive to all kind of point to the more operative issue with Abyssal Kinship being "good luck getting ahold of a gulmoth or acamoth that's pliable to further tying itself to something that partially defines its ultimately undefinable nature by proxy."

                                    If the Abyss's thematic relation to destruction and negation is not a valid source of inspiration and relation for the Begotten despite its constituents' being about as driven as any other ephemeral being, that contributes more to the difficulty of readers in academically understanding what the Abyss is about than it does the clarification of how Kinship works in a fashion that is less broad than the Question of Lineage section's "everything but the Unchained" and its implicit macrocategory of "everything not drawn from the God-Machine."

                                    I can understand not wanting Mage's cosmology getting misunderstood and used inappropriately in the low-level crossover mindset half the fandom seems to operate under by default, as well as the necessity of ensuring that Beasts don't get easy outs for their ties to humanity short of claiming one of the lesser Inheritances, but unless I've missed a substantial chunk of the map here (and I very well could have, between Mage still not being one of the gamelines I have a stronger understanding of and Beast relating to a relatively new element of the cosmology) there's not a readily visible reason for the Children of the Dark Mother to lack even the surface-level shreds of Kinship with beings whose principle ties to the world are the things that they attempt to corrupt and destroy.

                                    It's highly probable that I'm misunderstanding one or more elements of the games' metaphysics here, but if there's been a miscommunication at some point in the process between writing and reading the book it would be great to get it cleared up.
