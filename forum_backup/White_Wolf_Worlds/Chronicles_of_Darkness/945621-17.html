I'm more somber and accepting. Both of my mother's grandmother's died realy, really old. One of them died at 107, the other died at 101. And my mother was and still is extremely affected by it. For me, I was just like, &quot;I lived a good life with them, they lived a good life, I'm not upset, it was time&quot;. Honestly, I don't even cry, and I didn't when I got the call about my uncle. I was somber, and more introspective, and I'm not a crier in general. So I guess I'm trying to understand the position of others who react differently. I'm interested in the philosophy of it.

        (Unrelated side-note, my great-grandmother who died at 107 dated Al Capone.)
        To be perfectly honest: I am genuinely not sure how one can not be scared of the permanent and irreversible cessation of consciousness. In fact, Terror Management Theory says that a great deal of human behavior comes from the human need to suppress the terror of death, because of how disastrous it is mentally and emotionally to fully comprehend it. I've explained it- death means that you can never experience anything or feel anything ever again. And that horrifies me, and I would do anything to put it off or escape it.

        To be honest, if I lived in ChroD, I'd be more willing to die- magic exists there, so there could be an afterlife, and even the Underworld, shitty as it is, would be better than ceasing to exist.

Having Immortality as an Obsession and trying to fulfill it without falling into the pitfalls of making light of a thing I desire? That sounds way cooler to me.

                        Putting Dave aside for a second here, my point is that Mage is a game of the human will and the consequences of it's conquest against reality as it is.                         The inmortality wasnt as important as to how he processed and was affected by said inmortality which was more interesting to us than said "looking for the grail" as an unchanging body meant (plus life magic) a body that never learned which in this particular PC was kinda a hippy version of bojack horseman.

                        So i dont think having inmortality as "easy" like in ascension is flat out boring escapism, there is way to much "conventional wisdom" about the follys of searching for inmortality and not that much about dealing with it...except vampire i guess.
