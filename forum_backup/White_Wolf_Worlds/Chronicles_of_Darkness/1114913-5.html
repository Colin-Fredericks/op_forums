            Food for thought, RE: "werewolves, having access to the Shadow, are at a permanent advantage over vampires who are stuck in regular space."

            1. Yes, absolutely, being able to slip from Point A to Point B without attracting any mortal attention is a powerful tool.

            2. On the other hand, though... access isn't the same thing as control. There are plenty of things that go bump in the Shadowy night, like potent spirits, that have their own agendas and might be disinclined to let werewolves trespass without challenge, especially if they're congregating around vampires as sources of tasty Essence.

            Spirits are an integral part of the Werewolf story, but that doesn't always make them Werewolf assets; they can be, or they can be violent enemies, depending upon the needs of the story and what would make for more dramatic narrative. (And Helions still hate the half-fleshed for what they did to Luna's lover.)

            Imagine: the Prince has been at her wits' end, trying to figure out how the local Lupines have been vanishing and reappearing seemingly at will as they "clean up" her smuggling operation... until one night, Elysium receives a visit from a smiling young man with bloody knives for teeth (read: Spirit-Claimed by an ensih of [pick your poison]). He claims to be an emissary of others like himself, and offers to protect them from the wolves' mysterious back-and-forth travel. In exchange? Why, he just wants them to... adjust their feeding habits. (Read: risk Humanity to generate more Essence of its preferred type.) That kind of choice - risking your Humanity & the Masquerade, or risking waking up to wolves in your haven - is the stuff of compelling drama; why not exploit it?

                On swarms: as much as I like the VtR:2nd corebook, I'd suggest that Onyx Path really nailed the mechanics of swarms more effectively in Demon (pg. 179) and in Beast (pg. 126). If I were running another Vampire game, I'd probably adapt Swarm Form to the following:

                - Radius is two yards per dot of Blood Potency
                - Speed is double normal; move in three dimensions; fit through any opening
                - Inflict one bashing damage per turn to everything in the swarm's radius OR condense on one target & inflict lethal damage/drain Vitae equal to Blood Potency
                - Armor effective against swarm damage only if covering the whole body, and even then only at half normal rating
                - Everyone else in the swarm's radius takes -2 penalty to all actions; may trigger Breaking Points for the entophobic
                - Taking damage reduces radius proportionally (take half damage, shrink to half size)
                - Non-area damage capped at 1 (normal) or 2 (Exceptional Success)
                - On any turn in which the swarm condenses, these caps double (max. 2 normal, 4 Exceptional); easier to squish a lot of tiny things when they're all tightly grouped together
                - Full damage from fire, sunlight, area attacks
                - Celerity & Resilience work as normal
                - Vigor passive adds one yard of extra radius per dot; Vigor active adds to effective Blood Potency for that turn when condensing
                - Can't use Claws of the Unholy
                - Only one physical "active" effect per turn (e.g. if using Vigor active, can't use Celerity active)*

                Edit:
                *Watsonian explanation: each of those tiny, gnashing bodies can't channel the power of the Curse as effectively as the vampire's "real" form, and/or the vampire's power is already taxed by independently coordinating so many moving parts at once.

                Doylist explanation: being a swarm already makes it very tough for most foes to cause you harm. It should be an overall buff, to reflect the XP spent on the Merit, but the combination of automatic lethal damage (Vigor active) + swarm immunity + preemption in case someone brought a grenade or a flamethrower (Celerity active) seems too much; pick two.

                    AmusedByIrony, thanks for that. I recently noticed the size restriction as well recently and was disappointed as I'll need to soon use the form or similar Animalism displays.

                    Anyway, here's a potential fix:
                    Murder of Crows (2 point Merit) [Swarm]
                    Most Kindred use Murder of Crows to dissolve into a writhing, screeching mass of flapping carrion birds, but it can enable any other, similarly sized creatures to be assumed as per Swarm Form. Your ability to dissolve your flesh into a swarm includes larger creatures up to Size 2. Doing so costs an additional Vitae but brings several benefits: the size of your swarm doubles, and if damage dealt to it by a single attack is not sufficient to kill a member of the selected species then that damage is ignored instead of reducing your health per the limits of Swarm Form.

                    I can also easily see a 1-point merit that enables vampires to take non-predatory forms, so you can get your swarms of butterflies or whatnot.
