                        Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
                        Seconding Dataweaver yet again.

                        Further more you are going to run into complications vis-a-vis the theoretical ideal and the practical example of a splat. Mages, for instance, are all but guaranteed to top nearly every category. This will come as no surprise to anyone who knows Mage well, it's a game where power is a key theme, and the general response to "Can a Mage do that?" is "Yes, eleven different ways". Of course, in practical application this is miles from the truth, a real character won't have access to all Arcana, unfailing situational comprehension or a pure utilitarian personality, and that's not accounting for time, scenario and the player simply dropping the ball. But the theoretical Mage-Splat Mage does, and that's what the Tier list will reflect.

                        Similarly, Vampires are likely to rank low in many categories where, in the context of a story, they would probably shine. Combat is a good example, Vampires pale in comparison to Werewolves in white room battles. We had a thread on it, it's not a good matchup...in the white room. Largely because the white room utterly neglects all the social manipulation, preparation, and sheer get-out-of-dodge potential the kindred possess. Throw in context to manipulate and suddenly Vampires will astonish you with their out of the box problem solving. Of course that's true for all splats (especially Demon, the unchained are ungodly good at out of the box problem solving), but that really only makes the tier problem worse.

                        Never the less, if we are to engage in tier making I shall propose a few:
                        1. Which splats are best at getting elected president?
                        2. Which splats are best at sleeping?
                        3. Which splats are best at cleaning up the streets? (I mean literally cleaning them of dirt and litter, not crime. Ignore crime. Except littering, assuming that's a crime where you live.)
                        4. Which splats are best at eating someone's Grandmother, putting on her cloths, and impersonating her when her granddaughter comes to visit?
                        2. Deathless because well....they're mummies after all.

                        On another note. I have found myself somewhat surprised at how the conventions of Mummy:the Curse have help make the concept of power tiers less of a thing for me.

                                                            Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
                                                            You're really overthinking this. Tier lists are based off of pretty broad strokes, and you aren't really expected to come up with the whole thing yourself. Tier lists have always been more of a community effort. The variables will be taken into account, don't worry yourself trying to do it all yourself. It'll work itself out, it always does.
                                                            Traditionally Tier lists are a combination of aggregate data, theory craft and opinion. This works for games like Street Fighter, Starcraft and Call of Duty because of their repetition, sheer player base and the ease by which data (and opinion) is gathered. The same stands, to a lesser extent, for D&D. Where at the very least all the characters are expected to tackle the same challenges in roughly the same manner.

                                                            But CofD? Broad strokes, sure, but strokes look different depending on the canvas. We have no repeatable scenarios, everyone's story is different, with different priorities and different storytellers run the splats in radically different ways. I just cannot see how it is possible, or remotely productive, to create tier lists. Hell, even if we had all the necessary factors, tier lists are so frequently upset, debated and revised as to render them dubious even in the most statistically rigorous environment.
