            Ah I'm starting to see what you're saying.

            In that regards I believe a majority of the newly First Changed end up in the Tribs of the Moon or the Pure mostly for two reasons.

            1. They are found by someone who belongs to one of those Tribes already and are quickly indoctronated to their cause, and let's be realistic in that this wouldn't be that hard a sell too. No matter how pure of heart the individual is half of who they are now is totally into what all of the Tribes are selling (that's just the tragic nature of their dualistic spirit.)

            2. Resources. Pure and simple. Just like the Covenants and Orders in the other game lines the Tribes of the Moon and Pure are way more organized and unified than the individual Ghost Wolves and their scattered Lodges. A single Pack of Storm Lords can bring more resources to bear against their enemies than the most wealthy of Ghost Wolves simply due to the fact that they have a far richer history and network of allies to draw from than the lone wolves of the World of Darkness. (Important to note though that this doesn't make the small Pack of Storm Lords more powerful than this expert Ghost Wolf, just that the hunt of the Tribless will be much more perilous. And according to custom make the Storm Lords respect that Ghost Wolf that much more for it's dedication and fortitude.)

                        Originally posted by Possessed View Post
                        My point is that Ghost Wolf, to me at least, sounds the most logical way for most Urdur to choose for their few first years at the least and start thinking of the other Tribes as possibilities only after they have gone through that transition period where they come to terms with themselves and who they are.
                        I see a few problems with this.

                        First off, "logical" seems a bit misplaced. What makes the most logical sense from an external perspective isn't the same as what makes the most logical sense form an internal perspective. "I'll take a few years to find myself," does not seem like a logical thought for a freshly changed werewolf to have. They just turned into a monster that can, at any moment, flip out and kill everyone around them. The immediacy of their situation doesn't really logically lead to taking a lot of time to figure things out for themselves; unless they are actual sociopaths that don't care that come to terms with themselves in their own time will probably mean the deaths of dozens of people.

                        Second, it seems to assume that the First Change is both more abrupt and less complete than the book presents. The book doesn't portray this as, "one day you were normal, then bam one night you change into a werewolf with a completely human mind, now go from there." The book describes there being considerable psychological lead up to it, as well as significant psychological changes that go along with the First Change. After the First Change a new werewolf sees the world through the eyes of a werewolf, not a human. Their base biological urges change and their psychology with it. The urge to be with other people that understand what you are, and what you're going through, is going to be incredibly strong.

                                The way I like to present it is that most newly first changed werewolves don't immediately join a Tribe. The books do say that the Uratha and the Pure keep tabs on wolf blooded who may be on the verge of their first change and approach them right away, but for reasons similar to yours, I don't imagine that would generally be well-received. In my games, the tribes are well aware of that, and tend to leave the new werewolf alone for a few weeks, to give them time to process what's happened and start getting used to their new predatory urges. Obviously, they monitor the pup closely during this time, so they can intervene if they get themselves into trouble, or if the other side tries to get to them first. During this period, the new werewolf is likely a murder suspect, which is sure to add a lot of stress, and potential death rage triggers. Their whole life is falling apart around them, they're seeing spirits that constantly harras them, and all the while they have a near irresistible urge to hunt and to kill. THAT's when the tribes' representatives come to them, and offer purpose, clarity, and a way to realize those predatory urges without hurting people they care about.
