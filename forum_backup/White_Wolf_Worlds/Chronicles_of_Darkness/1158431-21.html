                                    While we're discussing the Huntsmen, I personally found them a bit...I dunno. Overtly grim? I get that they're meant to inspire fear and paranoia in Changelings, and it's justified here. However, my interpretation from the text from David Hill's version was that a Huntsman was an inevitability, rather than a mere possibility. That, coupled with the fact the that a human dies every time a Huntsman comes through, and that by giving the token for a Huntsman over the Changeling makes the Huntsman return, it meant that any player character could have any number of deaths on her head by association, whether or not they slay every Huntsman that comes after them. That's also not helped by this text for the Token of Reprieve, which in my opinion kind of makes it too bleak: "Possessing one by definition means that a changeling has sold out one of her comrades. That’s an ugly thing, but even uglier is that scant few changelings would do any differently in the bargainer’s position."

                                    I appreciate that Changeling is a horror game at the end of the day, fantastical as it may be, but the Huntsmen felt...I don't know. It almost made me feel guilty to be a Changeling in this game because, no matter how good a fae I end up being, someone completely innocent is going to die because I had the audacity to return to normal life. It would just be an NPC, and I wouldn't know the person who died...but that's still someone's blood on my hands. It's not a good feeling.

                                    Of course, this feeling is probably not what the writers had in mind for the game, and really in Chronicles of Darkness, expecting nobody to die from our actions is rather silly. It would be rather easy to not have any Huntsmen appear in the Chronicle. I just feel that the text went a bit too far in making them the dreaded, and I'm interested in how the current version is going to alter that, if at all. That said, I am "horror-lite" in a lot of ways, and if you enjoy them as they were, all the more power to you.

                                    ...sorry, that's rather rambly.

                                            My interpretation is that hobgoblins cannot retreat from the Summer Court's blessing - just as Spring becoming Summer is an intrinsic fact, so too do the blessings of the various Courts establish rules that the Fae must obey. That is the power of the Courts, where the laws of the blessings are...well, law.
