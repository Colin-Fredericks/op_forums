    As I recall in the 1E OD book, there is a discussion of Dragons who truck with mythos type creatures for knowledge and power. I'd look at that for some ideas. Generally speaking, I think vampire mix with Mythos baddies quite well. As a general rule Vampires shy away from anything supernatural that is not themselves because quite frankly vampires don't like to reminded that they're not the only game in town and there might be bigger badder games in tow.

    So for example lets say a cotiere is out looking for a poacher and they corner him and suddenly his shirt rips in half and he's got a foot long vertical mouth on his torso full of teeth and two tongues, thats waaaaaay beyond normal for vamps. Even with protean, vampires don't do that. So automatically their dealing with shit that should not be and assuming they survive, they have to be wondering 'what the fuck did that?'.

    Likewise if they run into a lovecraft ghoul, at first they might assume that they're dealing with an case of Nosferatu but overtime with it starts wielding more and more magic that deviates further and further from Cruac and Theban sorcery, they start to think "Uhmmm...what exactly are we dealing with here guys?"

    I would say if you want to bring in the mythos is to slowly turn up the heat on the water. In lovecraft anything supernatural will freak out PCs early on. With vampires they will be making all sorts of assumptions that will get them into trouble or lead them in the wrong direction in the beginning. Work with those assumptions so when the reveal happens, that "Oh fuck" moment is that much sweeter.

                        Originally posted by Live Bait View Post

                        Vampires are the only supernatural that need humanity and therefore the only one they can truly rely on to keep them safe and numerous is my opening argument should anyone claim humans would be better off wiping them out.
                        Indeed. If vampires are the worst the world of darkness contains, then that world is not dark enough. And if it does contain worse, then the vampires existing in the shadows between the light and the outer dark as they do, are naturally the first line of defence against that darkness. Whether they wish to be or not. I like to try and create the feeling for the players that they're standing at the edge of an abyss, that they've stepped from the world they knew to the lip of some great darkness that should terrify them. It's one of the things I really like about Blood and Smoke - the Vampires may be powerful and pull many strings, but there are things like the Stryx that are unknowable to them. Vampires and a lovecraftian feel go together surprisingly well. I tend not to go for the vast Cthulhu scale stuff - more the small strangeness. Goblins scratching at the windows and something hidden beneath the waters. But it's good to hint at greater terrors.

                                    Mehh, I'm a bit hesitant to say vampires are part of the Lovecraft hierarchy, since they all pretty much agree that discovery of their existence would lead to their extinction. The horror of Lovecraft isn't that there are alien monsters out there, it's that there are invincible alien monsters out there. Humanity is completely helpless to defend itself against such creatures, and we should thank whatever gods exist that they deign to give us the bliss of ignorance.

                                    But when it comes to the Chronicles of Darkness, it seems like humanity is the one holding the cards. If they found out the supernatural existed, they'd go to war, a war that most splats seem to agree they would be victorious.


                                    If you want a more Lovecraft feel, I'd say that Mummies and Geists are more appropriate. Mummies because they're can't be permanently defeated and have cults of mortals dedicated to them. And Geists because they've seen what lays beyond death and decided a second chance at life was worth cohabiting a body.
