    LGBTQ+ in CofD games

    06-27-2015, 06:09 AM
    We have written 3 games corebooks for 2ED CoD ( or 4, depends on how we look on Beast ). Very nice for the Onyx Path is that they push inclusion in their games in small, nice references in games about non-heteronormative characters. This topic is about collecting them all, for the LGBT+ players and STs glory - because it's Pride Month!

    So I will start with two mine favorites:
    • Beast: The Primoridal - Character Concepts - p. 87 - “IT manager by day, drag king by night” - It hits me very hard, in positive sense, as myself bisex bear IT Technician. Also, this lady will have hands full on - work on day, drag by night and Horror in the dreams?!
    • Werewolf: The Forsaken 2ED - Touchtones, example - p. 87 - "Example: Picture the dedicated police officer married to his job. He wants to propose to his boyfriend, but it’s just not fair. He’s never home. He’s always in danger. And it’s not like he can just give up the job; it’s who he is. The boyfriend is close enough to touch, but too far to hold. " - It's just so good love story idea, highlighting the topics of Werewolf. With a very nice, gay spin. I love it!

                  Beast actually has quite a few LGBTQ references in it... which may be part of the reason why it got the "SJW Front!" backlash from some morons... anyway. Taking from the 99% review doc, but I don't recall seeing any of these edited out in the revamp drafts on the Patreon page:

                  * Page 50: Sample Nemesis Jess is a genderqueer Eshmaki Nemesis.

                  * Page 51: Patrick & Ahmed are a homosexual male couple of Namtaru Beasts (one Collector, one Punisher).

                  * Page 134: The example of a High Satiety Hoarder feeding mentions a male homosexual Beast trying to find a very specific sort of man to be his lover.


                  Some Mummy examples I've noticed:
                  * Book of the Deceived, Page 123: Najaulla-Sen The Merciful One is a female Deceived mentioned as having spent her last incarnation as a male, during which time she suffered from gender dysphoria.

                  * Book of the Deceived, Page 124: The Deceived Kefershaitain Stained Hands is genderqueer due to indifference.

                  * Cursed Necropolis D.C, Page 56: Hebeny The Bull of Bennu is a FtM transsexual who struggles with the fact that low Memory allows his sahu to show his true gender, whilst higher Memory forces him back to his birth gender.

                                                  On a related note, as lots of my current GM writing has been taken up with the creation of villains and adversaries for my Mage 2nd Ed campaign, I've been considering the ways in which homophobia and other such related human ills have their place in the game. Some of this relates to issues like how to portray homophobic NPCs and whether homophodic PCs should ever be allowed, etc.

                                                  Other issues I've been considering relate to how the homophobia has a place within the Fallen World. Obviously, Mage is a world of supernal symbols and their mysterious interactions within it, so homophobia is obviously going to be a potent supernal symbol along with tyranny, oppression, etc.

                                                  Hence the question is: does homophobia have a place within the Exarch's plan for humanity. I like the idea that homophobia, transphobia, and hatred for the different and deviant are ways in which the Exarchs divide and rule humanity; one of the myriad ways in which they keep humans at each other throats, reduced to a state of constant strife. There may even possibly be an Exarch/Seal devoted to the symbol itself, perhaps something like 'The Purity' - a symbol of unrelenting Natural Order and the Purity,Stagnation of Natural Law, Its Total and Unchanging Dominion Over All Life - the Eradication of the Deviant and the Unclean. Obviously this symbol has echoes in Eugenics and racial purity, and nasty connections with Nazism and the like. (As you can see, I like my Exarch and the Seers by extension as villains pure and simple, at least conceptually). This raises the opposite idea that the LGBTQ movement and its symbols are also powerful supernal symbols set up by the Oracles!
