    Adamant Hand fighting style

    07-15-2022, 05:04 PM
    The Adamant Hand in 1ed had a very flavorful writeup, but mechanically some maneuvers were very situational, plus it required a high XP investment. The version in 2ed is a lot more accessible, but feels underwhelming when compared to the power of other Order specific merits, such as Masque, Egregore, Lex Magicka and Prelacies. So I tried making a version with comparable utility, fleshing it out into a fighting style specialized for the Awakened. Design notes will also be included at the end, feedback is very welcome.

    Adamant Hand (● to ●●●●●)
    Requirements: Adamantine Arrow Order Status (●), Athletics/Brawl/Weaponry/Firearms ●●●

    The signature style of magical combat of the Arrow is rumored to trace its roots back to Atlantis. The Adamant Sages of the time were said to have developed its systems by observing the dragons. These beings were seen as paragons of honor, elevated principles, magic and civilization, as well as tremendous warriors. By imitating and adapting their movements, the Arrows developed a combat form that blended occult knowledge and martial prowess. In so doing, they created a discipline whose purpose is to allow practitioners to conquer themselves, drawing closer to the Supernal symbols of the ideal warrior.

    Soaring Stance (●)

    The first thing a student learns is a series of katas, these incorporate stances resembling Atlantean runes into the combat rhythm of their weapon of choice. They must be replicated precisely in a multitude of combat scenarios. With practice their true purpose is revealed, to teach the student to use their connection to the Supernal to intuitively grasp the myriad forms of Awakened combat, transitioning between them with crisp movements.

    Description: The mage may use a combat action from any of the merit's required skills as a reflexive Order tool yantra, adding dice to a spellcasting roll on subsequent turns or to a reflexive spell cast on the same turn. The bonus is equal to half their Gnosis, rounded up.

    Drawback: The utilized skill must be at 3 dots or more to qualify for use.

    Adamas Scales (●●)

    After grasping the fundamentals, the next step is to refine one’s internal flow of mana (or Ki as some instructors prefer calling it). This is usually done with a mixture of a carefully balanced diet with qigong exercises. The novice then learns how to focus mana into the middle dantian, which is then distributed and used to reinforce the body against the most grievous harm, if only for a few moments. Through rigorous training, they internalize this technique at an instinctual level.

    Description: The mage may spend mana reflexively to reduce damage from all sources for a turn, at a rate of 1 to 1.

    Drawback: The mana used counts towards the mage's per turn limit.

    Supernal Breath (●●●)

    The breath of the dragon draws forth the Supernal, giving it purpose and directionality to vanquish their enemies. Adepts have learned to achieve this through mastery of an Awakened variation of pranayama. Mastering these breathing techniques lets them enter a state of relaxed focus, a type of mindfulness that blurs the boundary between the combat styles of the mundane and Supernal, allowing one to flow into the other with fluid ease.

    Description: The mage can perform a physical action in combat and cast a spell on the same turn. These spells can benefit from the bonus of Soaring Stance.

    Drawback: Costs one Willpower per turn and applies a -1 dice penalty on the spellcasting roll.

    Watchful Gaze (●●●●)

    Masters of the Adamant Hand are always prepared for combat, remaining aware of their surroundings while delving into the Mysteries, maintaining combat discipline even when focusing on the metaphysical nuances of the battlefield. The training borrows principles of constant awareness from Krav Maga, regiments to attain zanshin from Japanese martial arts, as well as many others.

    Description: The mage does not suffer penalties to perception rolls (such as detecting ambushes) or rolls involving one of this fighting style’s required skills when using Mage Sight, even outside of combat.

    Drawback: The utilized skill must be at 3 dots or more to qualify for use.

    Sundering Claws (●●●●●)

    The warrior must learn to see their weapons as an extension of themselves, allowing them to manipulate them with deftness. It is a difficult thing to teach and can only be grasped by first hand experience. Grandmasters take this one step further, leveraging martial technique to refine their spellcasting aim and channeling their Awakened will through their armaments, using them to deliver spells with decisiveness and precision.

    Description: When using an aimed spell, the mage can choose to instead roll Gnosis + Brawl / Firearms / Weaponry to hit. In addition, should they have a weapon of the chosen skill at hand, they can add its to-hit modifier and any associated dice tricks to the roll.

    Drawback: The utilized skill must be at 3 dots or more to qualify for use.

    What were you thinking design wise ?

    The Order merits in 2ed have some strong and flavorful entries. The Lex Magicka is one of the most powerful tools when investigating anything related to upholding the Pentacle’s law. The Prelacy a Seer obtains gives them a fierce competitive edge in their Exarch’s specialty. The Masque allows Guardians to have a great amount of stealth and even a bit of breathing room regarding Acts of Hubris. Egregore is the perfect tool for an international adventure archeologist and to keep the secrecy of the Mysterium. Then we have Techné and Adamant Hand, which are very underwhelming. This is an attempt to revise the Arrow's merit into something that reinforces their specialty as the masters of magical combat.

    The first step is to review what already exists, to capture theme and intent. First let's look at the 1ed version (found on pages 53-54 in the Adamantine Arrow book), which is treated as a fighting style with a preference for close range:

    Adamant Hand (1ed)

    Requirements: Adamantine Arrow Order Status (●●), specialized Arcanum (●), five dots of fighting style merits and at least one of them must be up to 3 dots

    Observation: This merit is purchased per Arcanum, conferring its benefits only to spells that are primarily of that Arcanum (hereafter referred as the Style’s Arcanum). This potentially makes it a heavy XP sink.

    Thunder (●)
    Description: The mage becomes adept at perceiving flaws in defensive spells, reducing magically increased defenses by 1 against the mage’s spells of the Style’s Arcanum, as well as Brawl and Weaponry attacks.

    Drawback: The caster must make physical contact with the target to benefit from this maneuver.

    Diamond (●●)
    Description: Increases the defenses of the mage’s Shielding spells (Mage Armor in 2ed) of the Style’s Arcanum by +1/+1 against a single target per turn, but takes a -1/-1 penalty against everyone else. With 3 dots in this fighting style, the mage can make the adjustments be +2/-2 , should they wish.

    Drawback: Costs one point of Willpower per scene

    Star (●●●)
    Description: The mage can cast a spell of the Style’s Arcanum and perform a physical action on the same turn, with the spellcasting roll suffering a -1 penalty.

    Drawback: Costs one Willpower per turn. This cannot be combined with other abilities that grant additional attacks per turn (more of a 1ed concern).

    Blood (●●●●)
    Description: The mage can take aggravated damage to gain bonus dice on spellcasting roles of the Style’s Arcanum, the conversion being made at a 1 to 1 ratio. They can choose to make the damage resistant to gain 9-again. They can sacrifice up to [Resolve] points per use.

    Drawback: Costs one Willpower per use.

    Abyss (●●●●●)
    Description: The mage never suffers Paradox for spells of the Style’s Arcanum that are delivered through physical contact.

    Drawback: The mage must initiate a grapple to use this maneuver.

    Analysis

    Thunder is an incentive for attacking at close range, but unless mages prepare extensively, it's not the safest place to be or the most efficient use of their spellcasting. It is also highly specialized for mage combat. It provides a slight bonus for melee fighters, but nothing game changing.

    Diamond is probably made to synergize with Thunder. It allows the mage to focus on a priority target, having an easier time beating their defenses while also being more difficult to hit. But since the lowered defenses from this maneuver stack with the loss of Defense from multiple attacks, it means they need additional spellwork to make this effective, otherwise you might fall to a death of a thousand cuts. This would normally be a semi useful ability, but the willpower cost makes it have more drawbacks than benefits. Having a few defensive maneuvers included in the style is an interesting idea.

    Star is pretty amazing, it's perhaps the most useful and widely applicable one of the entire fighting style. The Willpower cost and spellcasting dice penalty can be managed, and are a nice tradeoff for the sheer efficiency this grants. This makes facing an Arrow in combat terrifying, they are able to multitask with their unflinching discipline.

    Blood has far too many drawbacks to be useful. Aggravated damage can cause you to lose limbs permanently, it takes mana to heal, it's far too little benefit for a single bonus dice. Upgrading it to resistant, making each point require a week of rest to heal, is simply not worth the 9-again. Combined with the Willpower cost make this a terrible maneuver. However, having some kind of sacrificial moves could open interesting combat choices.

    Abyss is very specific to mages with heavy investment in a grappling build to be consistent. Not suffering Paradox can be incredibly powerful, allowing you to end combat in all sorts of ways (not just lethally) when combined with Star. It's weird that the capstone is so highly specialized and drops the support to melee weapons, it feels out of sync with the Third Phalanx: Adaptability Is Strength.

    Overall, the merit seems to require too heavy an investment for two powerful abilities and three that are situational at best (although the writeup and thematic justification are very flavorful). Five dots in Fighting Styles, two in Order Status, plus one in the Style’s Arcanum are required to learn this 5 dot merit. Plus you will likely need to make additional investments into close combat competency and you need to repurchase this merit for each Arcanum. It has interesting ideas but is way too rigid in its execution, like an attempt to port the Akashayana's Do combat style to Awakening.

    The first thing to address is to reduce the requirements for this merit, having to repurchase it per Arcanum is also excessive. I like the idea of making it a 5 dot fighting style, plus the efficiency Star affords and how Abyss requires some setup on the material side of fighting to be effective. But it also needs to give more emphasis to how the Arrows are highly adaptable.

    Now we will look at the 2ed version of it, which is much briefer. It can be found on page 99 of the core book.

    Adamant Hand (2ed) (●●)

    Requirements: Adamantine Arrow Order Status (●), Athletics/Brawl/Weaponry/Firearms ●●●

    Observation: The expansion of the merit that includes Firearms was done in page 26 of Night Horrors: Nameless & Accursed.

    Description: The mage chooses one skill between the required ones and learns a version of this merit specialized to it. It can be purchased once for each specialization. This allows the skill to be used in combat as a reflexive Order tool Yantra, adding dice to a spellcasting roll on subsequent turns or to a reflexive spell cast on the same turn.

    Analysis

    The bonus from an Order tool yantra is fixed at +1 and this allows it to be applied reflexively. In other words, with a compatible Shadow Name, it gives four dice or 2 extra spell factors while requiring no extra time, the same applies to Willpower expenditure. When combined with some defense spells with reflexive cast options, like Exceptional Luck and Turn Momentum, it allows the mage to have a bit more consistency when dealing with surprise attacks. It has far less demanding requirements, incentivizing its wider use and it's a way to incentivize Arrows to mix some mundane skills into their approach to combat.

    The downside is that the bonus is minuscule and cannot be accumulated. Generally it's less efficient for a mage to use mundane weapons rather than spells, unless those are heavily enchanted or they are facing multiple Sleepers (a situation they can probably escape with a good Veiling spell).

    Analysis

    Similar to 1ed, the Adamant Hand was made into a full fighting style. However, the pre-requisites were lowered to allow for more widespread adoption and favoring a design philosophy where characters can expand the style’s utility by investing in relevant Skills and Attributes. The maneuvers were also designed to be useful regardless of what combat range the character prefers.

    The first thing that was modified in the fighting style was the name of the maneuvers. The four initial ones were named after a similar train of thought to the Elemental Precepts of the Silver Ladder, each representing a higher form of the 4 elements. That would leave Abyss as the more refined form of the fifth element, quintessence, which is a baffling choice.

    So in order to diversify the names, focusing on dragons seemed interesting. In Atlantean lore, they were exemplars of magic and inspired each of the Orders to the point where they equated themselves to parts of the dragons. Drawing from a similar train of thought as Shaolin Monks, it seemed intuitive that the warriors of the Arrow would try to develop their own magical fighting style by observing beings as powerful as dragons.

    It also draws some inspiration from the dancing dragon firebending form in Avatar:The Last Airbender. The maneuver’s names each correspond to one of the Orders (with the Free Council corresponding to the previously empty slot of the scales), the claws (symbolizing the Arrows) representing the final skill learned in the path of the warrior.

    Soaring Stance was made by revising the 2ed version of the merit. Utilizing half of Gnosis as the bonus allows it to not be too powerful early on, while steadily improving alongside the mage and providing a powerful combat centric bonus when they reach high Gnosis. The necessity of having skills at 3 dots to utilize it incentivizes variety.

    Adamas Scales was made based on one of the most useful survival abilities in the setting, the Resilience Discipline of vampires. However, this was made to be far more resource intensive, providing an emergency response, but being a lot less sustainable. This allows mages to have some leeway against traps as well, since it's not specified to be only usable in combat. Utilizing the mana per turn limit ties it to Gnosis, meaning it can potentially absorb more damage than a vampire, but progresses much slower and remains resource intensive. This also means that Banner-Bearers with a charged mana battery have a bit of leeway as the first line of defense, which is one way the mage can decide to build upon its defensive aspects.

    Supernal Breath is based on the Star maneuver from 1ed, which has far too many thematic and interesting applications. This allows things like a mage laying covering fire and using an AoE spell for further crowd control. The Willpower cost remains to make sure the mage can’t use it as often without either pausing to recharge Willpower or have prepared some way to replenish it.

    Watchful Gaze is based on a house rule by Freemind. The idea is to give more emphasis to the role of the Arrow as a defender of the cabal, allowing them to remain aware even when everyone is focusing on a Mystery. This also allows them to study a target while dodging or chasing them, making them much more effective in situations where it's not a good idea to just stand around studying a Mystery. It also allows them to be much more aware of the battlefield, allowing them to dodge aimed spells while remaining ready to counterspell or respond with a mundane attack of their own. It also synergized well with Supernal Breath.

    Sundering Claws allows the mage to have a lot more accuracy with aimed spells, while also providing for cool scenarios where they punch, stab, sword beam or snipe distant opponents with their spells. This allows them to further build upon the style by using enhanced weapons to increase accuracy. It is not even remotely subtle, but aimed spells were not subtle at all to begin with. It also synergizes well with Fast Spells.

    When all the abilities are put together, they make an Arrow mage a nightmare to fight, channeling powerful spells via sword beams, martial arts attacks and tactical shooting, surviving deadly blows with relentless endurance, simultaneously wielding spells and all the combat prowess of a well trained warrior, not missing a beat when using Mage Sight and keeping their guard up even while examining a Mystery.

    Hope you enjoyed reading this and thank you in advance for the feedback.
