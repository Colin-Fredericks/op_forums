                                    Okay, so I have to admit I'm not huge in the Supernatural fandom. What they do with myths just isn't my particular cup of tea. Then again, I come from a background not particularly steeped in Christian mythos. I had a stepfather once who had a bible, but that's about it.

                                    However, I've pulled up the Supernatural wiki, so I'll see what I can do. Mind you, I'm just skimming for now, because the wiki is a little hard on my eyes. I'm also working primarily with the Kickstarter copy, since I haven't had a chance to purchase the full book yet.

                                    For his original incarnation, before falling, I would say either Messenger or Psychopomp are likely jobs for him to work at -- I'm leaning towards psychopomp, as it fits a canon talent for putting things together, however messenger would be ideal if he was doing this to people rather than supplies. Messenger would also suit his "suave businessman" concept. After the Fall, I would say he's driven to experience humanity in it's fullness, and connecting with humans, although is ways of doing so may not be entirely appropriate to the perspective of someone who was raised in a normal, functioning household. I'm going to go ahead and go with Messenger here after all; as a Messenger, he was able to interact with people to the extent that it did what the God-Machine wanted, but he craved more interaction (I'm basing this off of: "In Sacrifice, as Sam cured Crowley of his demonic nature, he starts to reveal the reason he does what he does is because he just wants to be loved. It's possible, based on his reaction to his own confession, that this is true and his time being tortured in Hell twisted his desire to an megalomanic need for power and respect. He also displayed some regret for his actions, asking Sam where he could even begin to ask for forgiveness while discussing Sam's own confession to purify his blood. "), and so he fell when he started using his Messenger powers to make people like him.

                                    Feeling even more isolated and alone, he became more and more destructive, but never quite started living solely for destructions sake. He still wanted something out of it. He wasn't some mindless monster with a baseball bat.

                                    That said, I'm going to go with Messenger/Tempter, possibly Messenger / Tempter & Saboteur if double agendas are still a thing. As for Exploits and Embeds, I'm not going to sort through them too hard. Sorry!

                                    Combustion is an obvious choice of embed, based on his fire powers, while Cool Heads Prevail makes sense for his character, as does Devil's Advocate, since it seems he likes arguing. Plus, people arguing with each other aren't arguing with him! He has healing powers, so On The Mend would be a good choice.

                                    Alibi would be good, as would Authorized. and since he can manipulate memories in canon, Homogenous Memory. Never Here, Without a Trace, Heart's Desire, and Trick of the Light might work very well with another Voice embed as an interlock to allow him to mimic voices, or you could achieve something similar with a glitch.

                                    The red eyes effect could also be a glitch.

                                    Affliction would be a good exploit for him, as would Four Minutes Ago, and Incendiary.

                                    Electrical sight could fit his demonic form, and again, to suit the red eyed demon of the crossroads thing, they could be filled with red sparks of electricity. Inhuman strength, Sonic Acuity (maybe he has large, batlike ears), Tough As Stone, Blind Sense, Electric Jolt (electricity sparks between parts of his body that get too close to each other, like the crook of his elbow or between his fingers), Electrical Resistance, Fire Resistance, Mind Reading, Phasing (he looks like smoke when he's not going through things), Teleportation, Memory Theft, Rain of Fire, Wound Healing.

                                    Interlocks can be used to fill in powers that haven't already been covered, but I think I got all of them but mimicking voices.

                                    The rest of Crowley is literally all up to roleplaying. It's his personality, his choices, his goals. As you should hopefully be able to see from some of my notes on the Demonic Form, he wouldn't need to look like a machine. Biology is a pretty good resource.

                                    As for demons being more human than humans, I think looking at the psychology of child development could be very rewarding. A newly fallen demon is a child who hasn't learned anything beyond the basics of human interaction. They know how to walk, talk, they might know how to say please. They know what money is and what it does. They probably don't know what an abusive relationship looks like. They probably don't understand prejudice and hate crimes. These are all things they learn over time. It's not things they're incapable of.

                                    I've found a lot of the times when "human/inhuman" variables come up, we're underestimating the sheer variety of humanity there is. It's pretty amazing, actually! Humans can be jaded or naively optimistic, they can be cruel or kind, they can "get" other people, or they can be antisocial. They can be complicated or simplistic. A great example is Nick Ryves, from the Demon's Lexicon (which is a great book. A lot of people who like Supernatural also like this book. It's YA though, so might not be your cup of tea). Throughout the book, other characters are horrified by how he is, because the only things that he likes in life are violence and his brother, who practically raised him. When asked about his friends at school, he mentions that he doesn't know any of their names, because he just doesn't care about people. The people around him are quick to assume he's the way he is because of what he is, but the way he's been raised might very easily have had an affect on how he grew up. Did he learn to favor violence over people? Most human beings in his shoes would have.

                                    I'm sorry this is quite a long post, but to finalize, I would put Castiel as a Messenger/Integrator. He definitely wants to go back to God. The reason he doesn't is because he's terrible at it, not because he doesn't want to, and Agenda =/= personality, skills, or predispositions. Agendas are literally what the character wants to do re: Falling from grace.
