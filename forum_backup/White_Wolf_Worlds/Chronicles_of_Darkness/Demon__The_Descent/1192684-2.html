    - Ataraxia: "oh, she's looks nice. Kinda"

    All in all, I do really like how the angels are presented in the book, and how natural their Bans feel like when written into their character (the same goes for the glitches of demons in the previous chapter). I really liked the mention of Marco Singe and Avarice (which I now need to write down as a part of my "Nine Parts Mechanism"), and I loved the reference to Thelema, the Blind Men and Verney (not to mention the Contagion). I must wonder why would Morgue's fall would cause such a destruction, and I do hope that Wong would Fall. The Gardener is still my favorite in the chapter, I think.

    And as for Exiles-

    - Carta: "poor thing", also "what if.. ATLANTIS?!"
    - Angel Maker: "yeah, that's terrifying"
    - Damocles: "another poor Exile, in a fashion"
    - Dosiel: "yeah, that's scary"
    - Lavadiel: "yeah, that's a Night Horror"
    - Lutzow: "oh, my divine machineries..."

    I must say that Dosiel reminds me of a concept I have for Raziel, the First Exile and keeper of the Machine's secrets, so I'll probably connect them together in some way (currently, I think of Raziel's very existence as being the said secret). Also, yeah, I do love Carta (like, I don;t know, everyone? She is a very lovable character), and while it was probably not the intention, with all the emphasis around crossovers I do imagine that the First City would be Atlantis. It's funny how Exiles pretty much divide between adorable and poor beings and terrifying monsters which scare the shit out of you. I'm kinda surprised there was no mention of Silvers in there, I think.

                        Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                        I must say that Beelzebub interests me the most, as I've had similar thought about "demonic cryptids" which eat demons for Cover- even though the execution was really different.
                        Now that I'm finally on an actual computer instead of my phone, I can finally address this.
                        As you can no doubt imagine, I was quite excited when I was hired for this project. Before we even were given our assignments, I was brainstorming and pitching ideas for possible entries. It was while doing this that the first seeds of Beelzebub were formed. Since I didn't have my assignment yet, I wasn't restricting myself to the cryptids section, and I originally pitched him as an unnamed Analyst that had been created with a swarm-based form to research demons by infesting their demon's bodies. After his fall, he would have kept himself alive by claiming the bodies for his own and stealing their Covers. When we were asked which sections we'd like to write for, I put the cryptids as my first choice, mostly because I wanted to make certain that some cryptoflora made it in.
                        When I was assigned four cryptids, I was delighted, and immediately began pitching ideas to John (my partner for that chapter). One of those initial ideas was a swarm of locusts that could take over or devour a demon's body, because the idea of a swarm of tiny creatures crawling between the spaces of a demon's form to control it or rip it apart for spare parts seemed like it would be horrific enough to make people's flesh crawl. Whatever I did with my critters, I always wanted to make certain that something about them screamed "demonic", so that they would fit both with the Unchained and with more mainstream conceptions of the demonic. I had initially gone with locusts for that whole "Biblical" feel. I also considered whether I wanted to make it claim the identity of an entity from traditional demonology. It was about this time that I started thinking about the possibility of using biting flies rather than locusts, so that I could use the Beelzebub name, although I'd had other potential ones that I'd been considering, and those would have probably left them as locusts if I'd chosen one of those names instead. I decided upon the flies in the end for a couple reasons. The first is that it separated my critter further from the locust Hosts in Werewolf: The Forsaken, which had been a bit of a concern. The second and more important reason was that I'd learned about cerebral myiasis, and the idea of brain maggots making people into temporary "Sleeper agents" for the swarm was just too horrific not to use. The fact that Beelzebub was the most famous demon name I was considering was just icing on the cake.
                        So that's how Beelzebub came to be one of my critters for Enemy Action. I could go on a bit about what I was thinking while writing him, but I might have rambled on more than was desired already, so I'll leave that for if there's any interest.
