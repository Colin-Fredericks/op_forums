                        Originally posted by Azahul View Post
                        As for only showing up when someone flubs a Compromise Roll, what about the Angels out and about on missions unrelated to Demons? I would call such beings as some of the most valuable assets in a Demon Storyteller's arsenal. Adding a Psychopomp or Messenger Angel who doesn't give a fig about worming out Demons lets you have scenes other than, "An Angel appears, fight/flee!" and Angels other than the Destroyer Archetype.
                        My apologies, I was using hyperbole to express my frustration with angels becoming mooks, not describing how they show up in a chronicle.

                        Critical to the theme of DtD, and to any espionage fiction, is the fear of being caught, of having the protective lie of cover (or Cover) stripped away from you.

                        Angels are the manifestation of this fear in DtD. The threat of angels has to have teeth, dealing with angles has to be a source of tension. Angels have to scare the players, no matter what the programming and operational parameters of the angels in question, and no matter what Rank the angel is. In-game fiction has demons terrified of angels. Mechanically... this isn't always been represented in play, especially as players climbed in power. A demon with enough xp can eat a Rank 1 or 2 angel alive, especially if the demon in questions is willing to Go Loud, which is more of an option once they have Burn covers that they aren't as invested in as their main Covers.

                        My personal experience has been that angels with form abilities, even rank 2-3 angels, make a stronger impression on my players than ones where the form abilities are mechanically abstracted. In addition, it's helped me portray angels as materially different from other ephemeral beings, and describe the bio-mechanical horror lurking underneath a human mask.

                            That's interesting. I've had the total opposite experience. My group is terrified of Angels as written. A few members of the group have brought up the fact that they actually resent the fact that, from their perspective, they have basically no way to beat an Angel in a fight. Let's remember that a Demon can't even touch an Angel without either knowing its Bane or the Angel using the Materialise Manifestation (popularly referred to in my group as "The GM's going easy on us"). There is the only one power in the whole of Demon that allows a Demon to force a straight an Angel out of Twilight for a straight fight, Reality Enforcement. Even it is not quite foolproof as it tends to require the Demon to get the drop on the Angel or for the Angel to be a melee combatant. Then there is the sheer strength of Numen. Awe, Rapture, and Strike Blind are all nasty things to throw at a Demon who isn't ready for them. The Possess Manifestation is basically an automatic win if you can contrive a way to stick the Open Condition on a player. Or just use the Image Manifestation effect and throw Blasts at the Demons who are helpless to touch you until they keel over.

                            Rank 1 Angels are, admittedly, a bit on the weak side. I only have one written for my Chronicle, and I have it designed as something barely above an Imperative. But my players feel that any Angel in the Rank 2+ range is something they should avoid rather than fight if at all possible. Planning to fight an Angel is invariably a big, big deal for them, and something that will require the resources of the entire group.

                            I should note that I do give my Angels weapons and armour, sometimes fluffed as "natural weapons", when it makes sense for them to have it. So it's not entirely a bare knuckle fight. And I certainly don't shy away from using descriptions (or pictures) of the Angelic form to ram home the biomechanical horror side of things. I just haven't had much need for any kind of additional game mechanics beyond the Ephemeral Being rules.
