    Demon Grooming

    12-18-2014, 10:19 AM
    Ok, the great thing about a soul pact is that you can take it when you want. You can pact them money and power and make them the person you want them to be. Then you take over once the work has been do. Cool.
    But underneath the role play there are rules. Now if I wanted my demon character to go from resources 1 to 5 I would have to spend xp, from pacts or from earning it in play. The thing is it is cheaper to super power a moral giving them merits and skills. Now skills are a bonus not dots which logically will go away once the soul is taken. What I want to know is, if I gave a mortal a True friend, Contacts, Money and fame and I took the soul, wouldn't that be part of the cover.

    There are 4 ways to resolve that.

    One is put the player in xp debt until they pay it off. They have the dots but are not safe as they have not spent xp on it. The True friend wife falls out of love as her husband hasn’t changed but there is some reason she can not connect with her husband anymore

    That you do not get the merit dots but have access through spoofing

    That the cover has these dots as well

    As it is a soul pact, you are going to get from around 6 to 9 dots of cover. You can convert the dots you would give to xp for them to spend on merits as they wish as they spend more time making certain parts of the cover more real. As having a cover of someone rich doesn't put millions in the bank and just gives you the props to act as a rich guy (big mansion with nothing in, fancy cars with no fuel in)

    As I am still running a demon game I would like to know how this should be run, as I want to say true to the themes (selling a should is never good for the seller, that it is very much what a demon would do. Shape the cover you want before you put it on).

    I would say demon meddling would not help make the cover more solid (better to find someone rich than make them rich). Think of it as the gift the demon gives as hollow, they give what was asked without the back story. Taking the riches of a woman who her money has been passed down from generations is better for a cover than the guy who just now has loads of money.

                  Remember that technically a Soul Pact is not turning a person into a Cover, but destroying the person (body and soul) only to replace them with a facade created to replace them. Therefore things that you have artificially imposed on the universe, things that would disappear when the pacts with that person were destroyed, would cease to exist. The pacts would end, as the pacted mortal no longer exists, and therefore the benefits you placed in their lives also no longer exist. To keep those assets, you'd need to improve and modify the cover.

                          I would rule it this way (and intend to with my players):

                          The aspects of the life are there, the don't just disappear, BUT they are not safe from storyteller interference (no Sanctity of Merits) AND they do not necessarily have their specific mechanical effects. Resources would still be there but the job can be lost, the wife is there but is not a True Friend and can fall out of love or whatever due to story. The player could use Legend to gain the effects of Merits the Cover should have (when it makes sense) and can spend Experiences to solidify the Merits.

                          Run this way, work that the Demon puts into grooming a Cover is still there, they get something out of the Willpower DOT they spend on the Soul Pact but its not a bunch of free Experiences.
                          The player could have done the same in-game themselves using a poor, single Cover by getting the job, courting a woman, etc and then solidifying it with spending Experiences. The Pact just makes it happen diffrently.

                              If your question is: Do characters that have merits that then get destroyed and replaced with a Demon's cover mean that the demon gets those merits? The answer is no; the demon is expected to purchase those merits before they dissolve on their own.

                              If your question is: Do characters that are enhanced by Pacts that a Demon has made with them retain those enhanced characteristics? The answer is also no; the mortal is gone, the contract is destroyed, the universe rubber-bands back. (As a storyteller, I'd even say it'd be a compromise.)

                              On the note of cover merits. To my understanding in the base system it is unappealing to give your Demon merits attached directly to their Cover. My house-rule for this was as follows:
                              Social merits, which normally affect a cover, can be purchased by the Demon for the Demon with experience (for those individuals that know you're a Demon, like your pet Stigmatic). However for the Cover itself you cannot buy merits for it with experience, instead you use Cover experience. Therefore Cover experience you gain via pacting becomes more useful and allows you to make your covers unique (or the same) at your leisure. (It's still 3 cover experience for 1 dot of Cover)

                              In this system if you pact with a Mortal in an effort to "groom" their identity so that you can get a good identity when you inevitably annihilate them and replace them then (after you've taken that compromise roll for screwing with the universe) you'd simply spend cover experience to improve your new 7-dot cover with the appropriate merits that you'd given then as part of their pacts. You have reason to modify it now and so wouldn't need another established reason (such as taking wealth from another persons life) to improve the cover.

                              In this house-rule though it is fairly imperative to encourage players to avoid adding characteristics (especially merits that need -people-) out of thin-air just because they have the Cover experience to spend. If the Cover experience was earned by taking away someone's best friend, then they can patch that in, and use that Cover experience to buy a new merit (such as Ally). But they should avoid buying Allies, Contacts, etc, out of nothing.
