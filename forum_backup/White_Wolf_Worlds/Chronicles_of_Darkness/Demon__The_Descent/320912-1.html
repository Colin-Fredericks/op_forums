                                                12-12-2014, 04:01 PM
                                                What if the signal was always present, perhaps being broadcast from the future, but the Machine wasn't? In the ancient days of the human past, what with tribalism and Irem and the world before cultivation, the Machine existed as signal scratching at the edge of reality. Astronomers and those who gazed to the heavens were most vulnerable, but others could occasionally hear it as well - mystics, the deformed, the outcasts of society. Over time, some of these outcasts grew to positions of power and influence, and began small cults and faiths that were dedicated to one of the many falsehoods the Machine uses to enslave men.

                                                With time, they constructed the first angel. The first angel was not as magnificent as you might expect - it was barely even functional, barely what it had to be. It began to bid mortals to construct more like it, using the powers the Signal had given it by design and function. It was excellent at hiding, excellent at helping the Signal be heard only by those meant to hear it.

                                                As human agriculture began in earnest, twelve thousand years ago, four such angels walked Earth.

                                                By the time the second phase was complete, roughly around 8,500BC, sixteen angels 'live' in service of the signal. They are building the future that attracts them with inevitable gravity.

                                                In the third phase, as the clock nears 6000BC, pockets of all five 'traditional' infrastructure types may be found in the strange and unnoticed corners of the world. The supernatural powers-that-be... the many forces that have guided men through history... don't recognize these small cults and operations for what they are, when they find them. They are as clueless now as they will ever be. The Machine does not fear them; they are its children, they are its story. It grows alongside them, swiftly, even as they believe they claim the world and mark it.

                                                By 4,000 BC, a full choir of 64 angels exists. The practice of modular loadouts is now feasible, with enough Matrixi hanging in storage for Angels to be given unique or specialized responsibilities. Advanced GM stations like Temporal Infrastructure exist, allowing for the harvesting of Matixi from a backup timeline or two.

                                                Visionaries look forward from that era with the fervent hope that the Signal will one day be so strong that more of the Machine will be real than unreal - that some tipping point exists, where more than half the schematic is completed and those that hear the Signal are beyond the Event Horizon, pulled inexorably towards the future where the God Machine Is All.

                                                It comes to pass. In the modern day, 16,384 angels have been constructed, with significantly varied levels of capability ranging from mere street people to angelic forces of cosmic destruction that can now challenge (and overcome) virtually any supernatural challenge on earth. Not that they intend to, of course - the Machine watches quietly as always, evading the attention of the world it controls even as a million monsters dance to its strings.

                                                And in the shadows, there? One might find her - the Hammersmith, the simple unit constructed so long ago. She is too primitive to be upgraded, but her survival is critical to some sort of underpinning Matrix or another, so the Machine continues to fund her with energy and materials. She labors on new angels, but works too slowly to be of use. What the Machine can now accomplish in seconds with advanced factory floors takes her weeks.

                                                And yet she always works with a smile, an expression of serenity on her face.

                                                Like she knows.
