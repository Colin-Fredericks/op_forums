    Analyst Issues, Incarnations, Infrastructure types and Agendas (New Style Soon!)

    08-12-2015, 11:00 AM
    So, we've had a lot of discussion on the issue of Analysts lately, plus a tiny bit of a resurgence of the idea of matching Incarnation and Agendas together. Lots of different thoughts on the matters. Instead of putting a bunch of confusing cross posts, I wanted to give a full analysis (no pun intended) on the issue, touching on Incarnations, Agendas, and even the five kinds of Infrastructure that the God Machine uses, and how those Infrastructures relate to the Agendas and Incarnations. There's a lot of interaction here between all of the different aspects of the Unchained, and as a mirror reflection of the angels.

    Incarnations
    As I'm sure most of you have noticed from the thread with Analysts, but the five Incarnations are actually an artificial construct designed by the Unchained. The God Machine doesn't really categorize angels that way - sure, there's trends with the catalysts for the Fall, with the Demon Form, and with how they react to the Descent, but the only real absolute is their affinity for a certain class of Embeds. I like to think of Embeds (and Exploits by extension) as the demonic equivalent to Influences. Its only natural that angels with power over Chaos and Destruction will have experiences in common - the best purpose for angels built with Cacophony Influence are destructive measures. And it only makes sense that the God-Machine would give them Numina/Form abilities that enhance that so they would be good at their job.

    That said, while the only absolute is the affinity for one class of Embeds, each of the five Incarnations are rather iconic styles of demons found in literature.
    Destroyer - As demons, they take the form of the classical red skinned, horned and tailed devil with fire powers. Cacaphony is made for the classic strokes of misfortune that dealing with devils is renowned for.
    Guardian - The Grigori, the Watcher angels, who Fell in love. Or hate. Your classical fallen angels who became invested in humanity. Instrumental powers also are really good for watching out for someone, and for playing like an time-tripping angel from Its a Wonderful Life.
    Messengers - Beautiful form, affinity for seductive powers and visions? These embody the incubus and succubus archetype without the hyper focus on sex.
    Psychopomps - Ironically, they have the powers for appearing the most human, but often appear as the most strange like Buer, the lion-headed wheel of legs. Or the mess of nano-bugs pulled together. The Wheels and Mundane embeds were designed for locomotion - to get you places. Their demon embodiment are also strange messes that have superior methods of transportation to a human shape.

    So, if that's the original four Incarnations as an iconic style of demon? What about the fifth Incarnation?
    Anaylsts - Imps. Its even listed under the catalysts for falling. They're great for observing and figuring out the magical side of things, and tinkering with magic objects. Classical imp traits.

    No, you are not held to these stereotypes. DtD is a high customization game, and really flexible. That said, it does help provide a ground work for when we look at any questions relating to the Incarnations. About what is thematic for a game, to help really make one Incarnation really stand out from the others. This is helpful from a game design standpoint, to make each category unique, and from a story perspective, to help the ST design personalized stories during the chronicle, and have the Incarnation not fade into the background. Many players choose an Incarnation for the story that's built into it, and not just the mechanics. In fact, I'm a firm believer that the mechanics should help lift up the themes of the story, not the other way around.

    Agendas and Infrastructure Types
    Angels and demons are mirror opposites of each other. God-Machine versus Hell, Order and Law versus Free Will and Self Determination, Community versus the Individual, the Man versus the Little Guy. They stand on opposite issues, but they both use the same tools - the Unchained raise cults like the angels, they have Missions that follow their personal philosophy (agenda) instead of being assigned one. They create magic items for a purpose. They blend in and hide among humanity while hunting the other for the resources.

    The point here is that the Agenda and Infrastructure Types are actually mirror reflections of each other. Elimination Infrastructure specializes in hunting down demons and removing obsolete Infrastructure so it can be either recycled or just all traces of the work removed; meanwhile, the Sabatour Agenda? They specialize in battling angels and bringing down Infrastructure in general. They both fight their opposites and bring down Infrastructure, though for different reasons. Logistical Infrastructure and Tempter Agenda both specialize in bringing cults and materials together to build a pathway to something - either for building a new occult matrix, or for preparing to establish Hell. Defense Infrastructure and the Inquisitor Agenda are both hyper focused on analyzing threats. And Concealment and Integrators are both specialists in sneaking around and avoiding attention from others (note that Angel Empathy encourages you to be sneaky/suspicious for a beat, and grants you a bonus to evade and outwit your enemies, but not fight). I'm oversimplifying some things here - the comparison is actually deeper than I'm touching on here, but that's a matter for discussion elsewhere.

    And, just like Infrastructure has certain Incarnations (/influences) that are best at certain kinds of jobs, so we have certain Agendas that naturally lend themselves to certain Incarnations. The Destroyer / Elimination-Sabatour is an obvious choice, as is Guardian / Defense-Inquisitor.

    When all is said and done, however, that's still only four Agendas to the five Infrastructures. There's no equivalent to the Command and Control Infrastructures, which are said to do all the number crunching, then send out the information to others to execute. And we have a fifth Incarnation with no associated Agenda, but it does have a strong association with Command and Control Infrastructure. At first, I wondered if Uncalled could serve in that fifth spot, then rejected it - that's the equivalent to an angel being in storage with no Mission. So, the missing fifth Agenda* is something to keep in mind as we ponder the next section.

    *Yes, I know, we can theoretically have a million different agendas. But, like Incarnations, I would imagine that the G-M doesn't think in terms of "defense, concealment, logisitics, etc" for Infrastructure - its simply a convenient generalization. So, for the purpose of story, its easiest to just work with what we do have.

    The Analyst Issue
    Now, let me begin by saying this: Going Loud is a lot like an uncontrolled frenzy in Vampire or falling into Death Rage in Werewolf - its a significant boost in power, but comes with side effects, making it very potentially a Bad Thing for your character. As well, falling into Kuruth for a Rahu that's invested heavily in Rage and Strength Gifts, or a vampire with a focus on the physicals and Coil of the Wyrm, you get a lot more benefit than, say, a Mehket Theban Sorcerer or an Itheur ritualist. A Messenger or Psychopomp that Goes Loud gets less benefit from her Vocal or Mundane Embeds than someone with Cacaphony or Instrumental Embeds.

    So, the Analyst issue is simple at the heart of the matter. Incarnations are mechanically defined by their affinity powers. When you Go Loud, you have access to all the affinity powers your Incarnation grants you. Its like getting Influence (Cacaphony/Instrumental/etc) ranked up to 6. I suspect that is also why you can use all Exploits - you can buy any Exploit if you can justify the right Embed, and with effectively infinite Embeds, its pretty easy to "find one" that would justify any particular Exploit.

    Anyways, back on subject. Because the Analyst doesn't have any affinity Embeds - his affinity is for Exploits in general - he gets less benefit for Going Loud than others. This isn't really a matter of game balance - as stated above, some splats are just naturally better at going all out than others. There's absolutely nothing that makes an Analyst worse than a Psychopomp when forced to Go Loud, given that being loud and obvious is directly opposed to the very nature of the Psychopomp's Embeds, and they both get the same Exploits. That said, it is -fun- to get all those Embeds. It also helps reinforce the iconic roles mentioned above in the Incarnation section. While its not mechanically necessary, from a have-fun-and-tell-stories play perspective, its desirable.

    So, now that we've established that, the question becomes, what do we do about it? Some people suggest just giving the Analyst affinity for one of the categories of Embeds. Others have suggested giving a bonus for doing things with Gadgets - there's no mechanical support for that, so they really don't stand up to their own hype. A third suggestion involved learning other Interlocks.

    Lets talk about the first suggestion. Affinity for one of the existing categories of Embeds; suggestions include using the first Key as the affinity. The problem with doing this? At this point, there really is little difference between the Analyst and one of the other Incarnations, mechanically speaking. If anything, its slightly better, because of the anywhere Exploit, but still. May as well just build an unusual version of the original Incarnations. We want something that uplifts the themes of the impish Analyst, not makes them more like another Incarnation. If you don't care about that? Then you may as well just not use the Analyst.

    The second suggestion involved a bonus for gadgets of some kind. The problem with this is that its inconsistent with how other Incarnations work, which creates a bit of disconnect. However, lets think back to that talk about a missing fifth Agenda. Agendas giving out bonuses in certain situations? Its what they do. And if its a little known Agenda that's mostly adopted by Analysts? It upholds their stereotype as gadgeteers well.

    The third suggestion - involving Interlocks - is possible, but difficult for the game. Its not like we have a large selection of interlocks to pick from as it is. Its a bit unthematic considering that the interlocks are supposed to be personalized, and a bit overpowered when you consider that an interlock can be theoretically anything. So, get every power in the game with slight variation, plus all the ones that aren't?

    My suggestion:
    Analysts are known for being the Eyes. They're observers and recorders, information gatherers. Unlike the Guardians, who are on the watch out for danger, Analysts are the scientists. Demon-mages, if you will. That is the theme that Analysts follow - information and observation. That's the seat of their abilities, being the impartial observer.

    So, when the Analysts Go Loud, they should get abilities that resonate with that theme. And there are already any number of Embeds that do that. Voice of the Machine, Last Place You Look, Data Retrieval and Special Someone to name an example from each category of Embeds. By using this kind of affinity, we re-emphaize the Analyst as its own style of fallen angel, distinct from any others, helps reinforce that they don't neatly fit into any category, challenges assumptions about demon-kind, and it can be pretty fun to Go Loud and be able to analyze any and every bit of information that starts coming your way.

      New Agenda - Inventors
      "What did you expect, an exploding pen?"

      The Inventors, also called the Gadgeteers by the polite and Imps by the rude, are a little known Agenda among the Unchained, primarily adopted by the Analysts.

      The Descent: Like the Tempters, the Inventors want to build their own Hell. Unlike the Builders, however, the Gadgeteers don't want to create a pocket dimension or another world. They want to create it here, in reality. They want to build a new network of demons, using their Gadgets and modified Stigmatics to build a new kind of Interface - the Satan Machine.

      Organization: Inventors are a surprisingly fractured group, for all their focus on building a unified machine of demons. Unlike other demons, Imps focus their organization around hidden workshops, where the members work together to build obscure new devices to aid in their projects. That said, little communication travels around between workshop networks, for fear of one group becoming compromised and bringing everything down at once.

      Interestingly, most Gadgeteers keep their membership secret, often posing as one of the Uncalled, or a member of anther Agenda. From there, the Imps poach members from other Agendas by following the breadcrumbs to see who has certain skill manipulating Infrastructure and building Gadgets among the rings.

      Condition: Quartermaster
      Inventors are constantly looking for ways to not only build their Satan Machine, but to bring new help into the system.
      Beat: The Inventor gain a Beat when he convinces a fellow demon to work together with him on a personal project.
      Resolution: +3 bonus to analyze Infrastructure or gadgets, or to create a workshop or gadget of their own.

                                                      I had an idea to set Inventors apart from Tempters. The latter are more socially cunning and well connected than actual engineers. They are called Builders because they search for ways to gather a good base of resources. They don't necessarily have the cerebral know-how for feats of occult engineering.

                                                      So, to live up to the connotation of mischief of their rude nickname and to explain their isolation, Inventors have a well deserved reputation for meddling with the creation of Hell as envisioned by the other Agendas. How do they do this? Mostly, they just divert intelligent and inventive Demons to their cause. They open their arms and their Analytical eyes see right what you feel. "Those guys don't appreciate your intellect and effort. Join us, and you will have allies that understand you. Oh, and the best toys." What the individual Inventor sees as Hell is not set in stone, as is to be expected of Analysts. What matters is that they eavesdropped, saw your dissatisfaction, and promised understanding and shiny new Gadgets. Their Quartermaster Condition grants a +2 bonus when Resolved, but the bonus is increased to +4 if the invited Demon diverts time and resources from other causes, including Agendas. Contrary to the Tempters, Inventors are encouraged to get shit done themselves and with teamwork, not lording over people they have a social advantage against. Another compelling (not tempting, they hate that word since it implies coercion in their minds) feature is the friendly rivalry they create among each other for amusement. They often build the most insane and even risky Gadgets and go poking inside the most interesting Infrastructure. Not to alter or destroy it, but to get ideas for yet more Gadgets and mental challenges. Those that take the idea of the Lucifer Engine as the main guideline for the creation of Hell often discuss what that device would be, and who would be the first among them to build it. Most such discussions are remarkably friendly for the subject in question.

                                                      To some Demons, the Inventors are almost like a family of similarly gifted and carefree individuals. They feel at home with them, and to some, Hell would simply be a place where things to play with and explore never run out. And the Inventors are more than glad to provide the means for that in the form of brain power and psychological support. All this noble intention and warm reception are not well received by most Demons belonging to other Agendas. They often accuse defectors who turn to the Imps as immature or even insane. Nothing but unfocused tinkerers that would walk up to an Archangel fully equipped with combat Gadgets if they thought they could turn the Archangel into Gadget Hardware. They'll scoff and say that the only way to make an Inventor do something the way you want him to without losing track of the mission is to offer him something he hasn't analyzed yet. Not all Inventors are so maniacal and short on attention span, of course, but rumors spread faster than Infrastructure.

                                                      That is the central paradox or quantum state of these Demons: attentive and distracted, ingenious and clueless, useful and useless. They are Imps, with all the urge to play with their pitchforks and laser guns that this implies. It's often too much for the other Agendas to handle, and fights happen. They never get slaughtered outright because they have talent, and though few would admit it, they often make Unchained existence a little more bearable with the laughter that often accompanies the stories of missions they kept their Eyes on.

                                                      How does this sound? To goofy for a spy game? I personally love the idea, and you could emphasize their rivalry and morbid curiosity if you wanted something more serious, though.
