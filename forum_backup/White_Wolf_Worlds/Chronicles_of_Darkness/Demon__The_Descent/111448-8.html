    177. In a neighborhood the ring knows well, the city has started installing large (~3-4 feet in diameter) concrete spheres along and sometimes in the middle of streets. In some cases the placement makes sense as a way to control and direct the flow of traffic (ie turning an intersection into a roundabout, creating a barrier between opposing lanes on a busy thoroughfare). In other places the spheres seem like they're just installed for the aesthetics, and in a few places they're actively a nuisance. They're also some kind of Infrastructure, though the Angel in city government who organized the project has protected itself within a bureaucratic maze. Just what kind of Infrastructure the spheres represent isn't apparent, either. Until one of them hatches...

            179. All right. This'll sound crazy. Just hear me out, okay?

            So you've heard of the Luddites, right? Group of protestors and "machine breakers" in England who feared that better sewing machines would replace and disenfranchise the skilled laborers that once worked the mills. Claimed to follow a guy named Ned Ludd. They'd send mill owners letters warning them to remove their improved machines or they would get smashed at some (usually unspecified) time.

            Who cares, right?

            Well, see...I heard there was a group within the movement independent of the others. Not unbelievable, there were at LEAST three separate groups operating in various places in the UK, and the evidence says they did not coordinate. But these guys were different. They were smashing machines. They were smashing THE machine. Stigmatics attacking Infrastructure hidden in the mills. That alone would be interesting enough. But these weren't just any other Hunter conspiracy. They were being...lead by somebody.

            See...old myths in Wales and England talk about this old king who ruled a city where London is now. Guy was named King Ludd. One of the things this guy did was put a pair of red and white dragons to sleep that were terrorizing the countryside. Two dragons grappling and eating each other. One depiction of the ouroboros features twin dragons devouring each other rather than one. Incidentally, one symbol for the Machine? Ouroboros. Anyway, so many centuries later the dragons get restless and people start sacrficing kids to keep it asleep. Then this one kid convinces them to stop and let the pair duke it out. That kid was named, according to some myths, Merlin. Later legends connect the sacrifice (occuring in the month of May, usually around Beltane) with such folk as Robin Hood.

            Again, who cares?

            Well, picture this. What if Ludd was a real king? What if he had dealings with the Machine, found a way to turn it off a long time ago? And maybe he's still alive. Some legends say England had inhabitants for the humans showed up. Maybe the Machine has an ancient enemy, something representing the land or the wild or something primitive as opposed to the ultratech of the God-Machine. Maybe this enemy pops up when the Machine is done using a group. Think about it. Ludd would represent, at the least, the people on the Isles before the Romans showed up. Most scholars place King Author, if he existed, around the end of the Roman occupation of Britain. Empire is falling apart....and the Machine is already trading in the tools of Rome for the incoming Saxons. Few centuries later, around the supposed time of Robin Hood, its the Saxons turn to be driven out by the Normans. Thousand years after all that a group pops up named after a guy named Ludd, officially fighting against the extinction of a class of worker that are soon to be abandoned to more modern machines.

            What am I saying? If this...entity is real, he seems to enjoy forming bands of resistance among the people and classes right around the point where progress or time or the Machine is ready to abandon them. Here's the thing. These pseudo-Luddites, so the rumors go, are still around. They've grown up with the Industrial Revolution, a bunch of survivalists and machine breakers who still leave messages for stigmatics and Angels warning them to stop serving the Machine or else. And they are still guided by their General Ludd, whoever that is. Only this time its not a nation or a nationality or a worker class that's the lost cause Ludd is backing. Its humanity. The Machine is advancing enough to where it can sustain Itself without needing to piggyback off human constructs. Pretty soon It will have no more use for humanity and will trade it in for something more pliable.

            (Author's Note: One day I'll get around to a full write up of The Machine Breaker for Hunter. I hope. In the meantime, enjoy my weird ability to find bizarre connections in the most unlikely moments in history.)

                180) The End of the World (Inspired by the movie Legion)
                Investigation has led your Ring to a gas station out in the middle of nowhere. The air is thick with Aether, but when you examine the place you can find nothing odd, not even with the application of Embeds, Exploits or whatever else you try. As night falls, though, a van turns up with half a dozen people, one a pregnant woman. They're terrified, paranoid and Stigmatic, although they seem incapable of perceiving their own stigmata and have a wild tale to tell. According to them, everyone in the nearest city they have been possessed and are hunting them, and they only just managed to get away. Any powers you bring to bear show that they believe what they're saying, but if you use anything that somehow shows objective truth you receive inconclusive, nonsensical or no results

                180 b)You don't have long to talk, though. As the night wears on, things start turning up - animated corpses and twisted servants of the Machine, although no angels. Any attempt to leave the gas station runs you into what seems to be some kind of spatial distortion - not matter what you do you seem trapped in a bubble of space where if you leave from one side you find yourself at the other. You defeat these things (and notice that for some reason no matter how egregiously you act outside your Cover it remains stable so long as the Compromise isn't caused by direct witness a supernatural power), although they whittle down the refugees one by one until only the pregnant woman remains. Finally, just before dawn, she gives birth and dies doing so, leaving you with a child on your hands, a very confusing night and a number of human and not-so-human corpses.

                180 c) And then the other shoe drops, in the form of something that looks a great deal like an angel but isn't - a man with wings of steel, superhuman physique and a conviction that he is the angel Gabriel, sent by God to retrieve the child. If you defeat him, his wings vanish, but no matter what he will not tell you the importance of the child.

                180 d) Where do you go and what do you do now? The spatial distortion vanished with the dawn, so you can leave, but this was so obviously a setup of the Machine that it could be no clearer if it were shouted from the rooftops. You were trapped; why did It not finish you off? What if the occult matrix is still ongoing, and whatever you decide to do next or with the child (who may be a Stigmatic, Offspring, Latent, Fractal, Nephilim or something stranger) is simply a continuation of whatever that was the night before? Perhaps most chillingly, what will you find when you return to civilisation? What if the refugees' claims were not some memory-trick of the Machine, but in fact real? Perhaps an entire city has been overrun by angels, spirits or something else. Maybe the world. Or maybe it's all just a fabrication. Who knows? (coughtheStorytellercough)

                                184)
                                The Setup
                                One of the ring comes into contact with a Vampire one evening. Besides the obvious consequences of the discovery of the undead – more disturbing is that the Vampire is setting off your Aetheric Resonance.
                                Meanwhile a person is found dead in their home. Their is no sign of entry into their house, only their window smashed open from the Inside. Their body looks like it was detonated from the inside and something burst out of her chest.
                                Not far from the scene of the crime a homeless person is found shriveled and dead, their body drained of blood.

                                Interchangeable Parts
                                Demons will soon track down that the Vampire had a close encounter with this woman – did they feed from them? Where they a part of their herd? A ghoul of the vampire in question?
                                The person has recently undergone a serious medical procedure, kidney replacement, IVF or Cancer Surgery supplied by a new Hospital in town.
                                Something was left in the Person, and once it reacted to the Vampires interference it… changed.

                                The Hooks
                                How will this vampire deal with the revelation that their food supply may be tainted?
                                Who is this medical company and what is their connection to the God-Machine?
                                Where is the Creature that escaped?
                                How many more people has the hospital done this too?

                                I ran a variation of this hook from the Vampire POV. The secret being that the new IVF clinic in town was using humans as Incubators for new Angels for a long-term project, and the still forming Angel reacted poorly with Vitae being added to its system. It mutated and burst its way out of the mother Alien Style and became a monstrous Angel/ Vampire hybrid who fed on blood instead of Essence. Good for introducing some nasty body horror and crossover to the game while also telling a violent and quite visceral tale with a personal edge. Not quite for the feint hearted.

                                            187: Black Cat Crossing

                                            There has been a rash of people being moved - not simply being shoved from one side to the other, but people vanishing and then reappearing at the other side of entire town in a very short amount of time. The cause has already been identified as a black cat, so black it looks like a shadow even when in full daylight, but with eyes that move like backlit liquid amber. Each moving happens the same way: someone feels something against their leg, and upon looking down they see the brief outline of a cat; the moment their gaze hits those amber eyes, they find themselves in a place without any light, no sounds or even smells, just the afterimage of those eyes staring back at them. Then, after at most twenty minutes, they find themselves on the other side of town near what turns out to be known Infrastructure.

                                            What’s odd is that nobody can figure out why this is being done. Nobody comes away hurt, or even with a lack of memory, nothing goes missing, and even Demons who have been moved have reported no aether being stolen from them. It doesn’t even seem to be fulfilling any form of security on Infrastructure - most of the Infrastructure it appears by have actually featured stronger security than before its appearances, and there are rumors of faceless cat catchers being on patrol. The cat is simply taking people across town without any prompting, without any apparent rhyme or reason.

                                            So why is everyone on both sides so worried?

                                                        This is more of a plot then a plot hook. I apologize in advance for the long post.


                                                        The published scenarios I have seen for DtD don’t seem like espionage fiction to me. They lack that the double and triple crosses, the boss with an ambiguous agenda, and similar uncertainties. Below is a lengthy sketch of a chronicle I’d like to run. I am posting it here for constructive criticism, especially a check on its consistency. By consistency I mean whether or not the different antagonists have far simpler and more sensible courses of action than the ones I give them.

                                                        The players’ Ring is approached by a mysterious demon I’ll call Smith. They are offered a job: there is going to be an auction of powerful Gadgets by humans who don’t know all of the real powers of most of the Gadgets. One item, the McGuffin, is very important: it can greatly increase the probability that an angel will Fall, by sabotaging communication with the GM. The PC’s are to either steal or purchase the McGuffin. They are welcome to obtain any of the other items they wish as a bonus. One in particular is a Aether battery, which would be valuable to any demon PC.

                                                        The auction is held in an isolated and warded mansion. There’s no access to Twilight, and there are other special protections. To sneak into the auction Smith will provide the PC’s with two identities, Brad and Janet. Brad is a multi-millionaire (via Pact) stigmatic and Janet, who is unconscious when presented to the PC’s, is to be his beautiful escort. Brad is a GM cultist who thinks he is helping the GM. (This is a deception the PC’s must maintain). He has the Sleeve Integrator merit, so that one demon can drag on his form. Janet is to be Possessed via a Gadget. Brad has an invitation to the auction, and the PC’s are told to use Brad and Janet to sneak in. They will find a way to sneak in any other PC’s, buy or steal the McGuffin, and get out.

                                                        There will be a variety of antagonists at the auction - one will be an uber-mage, one will be a vampire, there will be a trio of cultists and there will be assorted well-armed bodyguards and alarms. Standard sneaky stuff ensues, such as exchanging a fake McGuffin for a real one. Oh, there will also be one angel, in Cover (call him Fred).

                                                        Deception 1: The McGuffin doesn’t cause angels to fall. It’s a trap by the GM to lure in demons. The McGuffin, when activated will turn into an Aether devouring cryptid. It’s designed to be a demon killer. The GM is testing it out and Fred is there to see how it works. Once it is activated the mansion is sealed off from reality and everyone is trapped. The cryptid is really good at tracking demons and feeding off them.

                                                        Fred will give the PC’s an excuse to activate the McGuffin so that it will transform and eat them. However it will eat the Vampire by a lucky coincidence and change into something far more dangerous. Now it wants to eat everyone, and can attack angels as well. Victims might become new cryptids or something. It’s bad. The GM did not predict this.

                                                        Deception 2: Smith knows it’s a trap. He’s not stupid. However, there are valuable gadgets there and he wants to find out the GM’s plan. Even if the PC’s are slaughtered, he’s simply fooled the GM into thinking he’s gullible. But he’d rather they weren’t, so Smith chose Brad and Janet on purpose.

                                                        Deception 2a: Janet is a mage, who is there involuntarily. At some point if the player pops out of Janet it will be clear she is first disoriented and soon is very pissed. Because she entered the mansion possessed by a demon, she didn’t set off any magical alarms because she wasn’t a mage then, she was a demon. Something about her will narratively make it useful to have her there. (She may be the counter to the uber-mage). She is a hostile ally to the PC’s but they are innocent - they didn’t know she was kidnapped. Now she’s stuck there with them with Eaty McEatFace the cryptid.

                                                        Deception 2b: Smith knows that Brad is someone very important to Fred. Fred was Brad’s guardian angel and was pulled off that assignment by the GM for this mission. Fred wants desperately to protect Brad, and will collaborate with the players to kill the cryptid and any of the other auction participants who get too greedy. If the players are clever, they might find a way to use this to induce Fred to Fall as well. Again, the PC’s are innocent of kidnapping, and might be able to explain that to Fred.

                                                        Deception 2c: Smith has at least one other hidden actor at the auction - perhaps another demon. Will the PC’s trust them? Will they trust Fred? But it might be a non-demon as well.

                                                        Fred won’t drop the wards while the cryptid is alive - it will eat angels and demons and everyone else if allowed to reproduce. The demons have to find a way to kill the cryptid, handle the uber-mage, and still steal the valuable gadgets. Finding a balance so that the threats are severe but not overwhelming will be difficult.

                                                        I want the PC’s to feel that Smith yanked them around, but in a justifiable way so that they are annoyed but not furious. Smith got the PC’s allies and resources at the auction in a way that gives the PC’s deniability. He didn’t know about the cryptid, just that it was some form of a trap being run by Fred. There are deceptions but no soap opera style twists.

                                                        I am not sure what to do if the demons revert to their normal Covers in Fred’s presence, since that means the GM will know who they are - if Fred tells the GM. I have to think of a way to fix this plot complication.


                                                        If you've read this far, thank you! Feedback is welcome.

                                                            189) For Heaven is Empty
                                                            A month ago, buildings and sites all over the world began to activate. Stonehenge rebuilt itself and pulsed with white and ultraviolet light, even as the Pyramids screamed in radio waves. Big Ben tolled out infrared C# while the Burj Khalifa sang microwave hymns to the cosmos. Naturally, panic spread, both among mortals and among the Unchained - what Infrastructure was this? What was its intention? Hasty saboteur-teams were assembled and hits were made, but it's unclear whether they succeeded or failed in disrupting the Machine's plan. The end result was subtly apocalyptic, either way: the structures flared one last time, and then the Machine went silent. No Infrastructure has been begun since, no angel summoned and no cult visited, save by the angels dispatched before the disaster. All remaining angels have become Exiles - those that haven't Fallen, at least. Compromise is much harder to cause; using Embeds and Exploits no longer erodes Cover, and neither does remaining in full Demonic Form (though entering it still inflicts Compromise as normal).

                                                            It's a time of confusion, both for the mortal world and that of the Unchained. As scientists, leaders and religious authorities of all stripes strive to deliver answers to the people regarding the Event, the Unchained all have a single question on their minds: Is the Machine really gone? Is it some elaborate hoax, a trick to lure the Unchained out of hiding? As time wears on and the Machine remains silent, Rings and Agencies are beginning to become bolder, and tentatively look towards a future without the Machine. Infrastructure is being suborned left and right as demons begin to move more openly, while others look further ahead and fear what will happen when all Infrastructure has been claimed and no more is produced. The flood of Exiles are banding together as well, many Falling due to the stresses of free existence, but even among these many remain loyal to their absent god.

                                                            War is coming, and mortals may be a third side in the conflict, given the fact that the ever-present threat of hunter-angel squads is no longer present to keep Unchained heads down. The stage seems set for a war for the throne of Hell.

                                                            Note: This is a plot hook, but it's chronicle-defining in the most basic sense. Also, it probably only works in a WoD where G-M juju is the only juju, or where non-G-M juju is limited to ghosts, psychic powers, one-off Horrors, mortal occultism and that sort of thing. One suggestion I'd make is to have it so that mortals can, unconsciously, tap into Embeds in some way, enabling Matrix: Reloaded-type shenanigans. However you run this, it'll probably end up rather more... action or political thriller than horror-movie.
