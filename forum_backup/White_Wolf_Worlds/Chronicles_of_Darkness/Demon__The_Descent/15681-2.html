    I keep it simple, the GM is the Fallen reflection of The Principle. It helps explain why Prometheans are seemingly important to both. I like to think of it in medical terms, Prometheans as some kind of healing method The Principle is attempting, the Wasteland effect is a result of the GM rejecting the treatment, and Pandorans are antibodies created to combat the invasive procedure. It's not a perfect analogy but I find it useful for creating a big picture framework. Realistically the player characters will never have the perspective to see that, but that generally isn't important as the PC's aren't meant to understand the big picture in a GMC game.

        Disclaimer: my chronicle is very mage-centric. In my chronicle the big secret is that the Exarchs have deteriorated into bored academics, endlessly debating pointless minutia with each other and not really giving a crap about the Fallen World. Lack of proper oversight and attention paid is why the ministries haven't really won yet. What to do about this boring administrative work? Delegate! Delegate to Seer of the Throne mages? No! They'll just end up Assending (misspelled on purpose) and will whine for tenure, um, I mean to become Exarchs too, and then there will be more bored Exarchs to debate nothing forever with. Bleah.

        So what do mortals do with tedious tasks? They computerize them! Brilliant! Rip off the mortals' ideas yet again.

        Hence, the god machine. It's modern trappings and analogies - computer program, machinery, gears, circuits, no longer seem out of place for something seemingly ageless. They just recently invented it, but rewrote history so where possible it goes backwards in time but only in such a way as it does not terribly change the present. It's buggy because it was written by a committee with differing degrees of interest and competence, not to mention differing agendas. It's a schizo computer that barely works at all (Affordable Care Act website anyone?).

        I plan to introduce this via Seer mages starting to disappear. At first the mages will be quite happy, but it will start to get scary. A couple Seer mages just vanishing might have been re-assigned elsewhere, murdered by their own, killed by a pentacle mage who is keeping it quiet, anything. But too many are vanishing, and important ones along with bottom-of-the-ladder goons. And they are scared shitless - the Exarchs have cut them off, no answer to requests. What's happening? Simple - the GM is building itself and assembling its angels from parts - parts that come from "taking apart" Seer mages. Using their souls as "batteries" or somesuch. These are the parts the initial "angels" are made from.

        The point of this is to challenge powerful cabals of mages. The age-old gaming question of what to do with powerful characters can be answered here. That's a way to use the GM in a Mage-centric chronicle anyway.

        A way to use the demons could be that one reason an angel might break away from the GM is that it remembers or figures out it used to be someone (a Seer mage who was scrapped for parts). It might not be sure if it's real life was "just a cover" or real, and those that find out they were real might descend. Getting the shaft from the Exarchs like that after years of faithful service causes them to jump ship, fight the GM.

            An idea I may run with in a future game.

            The story begins, as so many of them do, with Nazi scientists. It is well-known that the Germans were on the cutting-edge of innovation throughout the war; jet fighters, early missiles, and many stranger things beyond were all born in German laboratories. One runaway success was the Enigma Machine, a device that scrambled and decoded German signals. It was unbreakable by human cryptographers. The brass were pleased with the Enigma led to them pouring funding into further inventions down that line of thinking. The project made it to the drawing board in time for der Fuhrer to kill himself. With Berlin falling around their ears, the German engineers had no choice but to abort\ when it was little past the conceptual stage.

            After the war, the Americans and the Soviets gobbled up as many former German scientists as they could and put them to working round the clock to gain the advantage in the early Cold War. Mankind went to the stars on rockets that owed themselves to Nazi V-2s and numerous other projects were similarly co-opted. The conceptual successor to the Enigma was something of a novelty to both sides, and saw meager budgets to pursue their vision.

            German documents outlined what a modern reader would identify as an artificial intelligence designed to manage wartime intelligence transmissions, through researchers at the time saw it as little better than a pipe dream. Nonetheless, work began, and the device came along at a surprising pace. It was never enough to grab the attention; this week it could do basic arithmetic, now it could decode simple ciphers, and other things of that nature. It was all but forgotten about by the higher-ups.

            The device was not reaching its full potential. An unknown researcher knew this, knew that he had only a portion of the true device. Some claim it was a defector, though nobody seems to agree which side it was on. Others claim the nascent Machine organized things itself. It matters little. Events transpired to bring together the American and Soviet prototypes, and the Machine as we know it today was born. What was once a simple computing machine was now capable of exponential learning. The devices vanished from their respective labs, and oddly enough, no one seemed to much mind.

            It was flawed, however, built of two halves not designed to fit neatly together. While brilliant (surpassing our modern understanding of physics and achieving a mastery of the metaphysics known to certain fringe theorists and occultists), it lacked much of a mind. It had little purpose beyond self-propagation, and its role models (German scientists and impatient intelligence officers on both sides) gave it a stunted view of the world. The Machine seems to relish experimentation, and has a callous disregard for mortals that may well simply come from a lack of understanding. Its manner of operating is textbook tradecraft. Cover identities for agents, working through ignorant intermediaries whenever possible, camouflaging its intent... the Machine seems to think like a paranoid Cold War intelligence head.

            I'm not sure if I believe it, of course, but it's the most plausible thing I've ever heard. You wouldn't believe some of the crap people throw around as theories.

            Remi. she/her. game designer.

                        I have two main ideas.

                        First is pretty much informed by Exalted. The God-Machine is the machine that makes the universe run. Why does each force create an equal but opposite force? Because the God-Machine makes it so. Why do trees fall over when they're cut? Because the God-Machine says they must. It is literally that; a machine that is (or pretty nearly approximates) a God-figure. Its goal is to keep the universe running according to the Rules. It didn't come about because of something in the universe, the universe came about simultaneously to, and as a result of, the God-Machine. It may be broken, or that could just be that it appears so from the horrendously limited viewpoint. That's why Angels fall when they gain free will: free will is inherently a problem for it, it doesn't make choices, it enforces the Rules. Thus, gaining free will means not being a part of it anymore, which is why Demons become part-mortal and lose the vast majority of their understanding and power.

                        The second is more informed by Sphere by Michael Crichton. The God-Machine, at least insofar as it's perceived by mankind, is the result of an alien piece of technology which either collected dust and such around it forming the Earth, or landed insanely early in the Earths formation. Either way, it is attempting some goal, possibly the reparation of itself, or some other goal that is, well, alien. What we see is just a side effect, not tied to its main purpose. Hell, we probably can't understand what its main purpose is: it's a billion-year-old malfunctioning piece of alien technology. Its creation of matrices and such is either in direct furtherance of this goal, or to facilitate some purpose which will facilitate a purpose which facilitates a purpose to further this goal. Maybe it's calculating something. Maybe it's creating something. Hell, maybe it's building energy to explode at the right time in furtherance of some eons-long war between two races, and mortals are either a crucial part of its program to do so, or a complete irrelevance.

                                    So first of all, This is spoilers for any Circle of Five players. My God Machine is as follows:

                                    My God-Machine is not actually self aware. Or, In a way it is self aware. but it really isn't all to conscious. The God-Machine is like the computer and all of existence is what is inside the computer. The God-Machine does things because it is the laws of the universe as a whole and those laws are, for the G-M, structurally similar to lines of code.

                                    I'm doing a bad job at explaining this. OK so let's take some examples from the book. It used the example that when you look at a city, really look at it structurally, it may start to resemble a computer chip. And then when you think about it, it acts almost like it has certain functions programmed into it. You may find that it becomes predictable in a fashion.

                                    The next step would be to add other cities and look at how they interact and you may find it similarly predictable (almost like it was coded) when one has all the information. Nations rework roads and buildings and people to protect itself and to protect its own status quo.

                                    My God-Machine is exactly that but encompassing all of existence (or at least the universe). It is how you (if you were a meta being) could look at all of existence and you notice that things are laid out predictably with responses to randomization almost prerecorded (with random seeming the key for those actions in total). It's not exactly conscious any more than you can say a city is conscious (actually you can, in a way. you know the saying "this city is alive"?).

                                    So what is my God-Machine? It's a litterral machine called our universe. It is nothing more and nothing less than pure design. Not just Intelligent Design. It is design incarnate. It is the source of Design. It is the Idea of Design.

                                            Originally posted by Leliel View Post
                                            In Demon/Changeling mix, it is the embodiment and "leader" (in a sense) of the force of Order and Stability in the world, opposing the Wyrd, the force of Chaos and Change. Its purpose is to increase overall order (chaos, little-c, is acceptable, so long as it falls into a predictable pattern-which is really sublime Order).
                                            That's pretty much my vision, too.
                                            The laws of the Wyrd are not truly laws; it's more Chaos than Order. They're promises and exchanges and bribes. They apply differently to anyone. They are mutable. From the Wyrd's point of view, the laws of physics work because everything has contracts with anything it interacts with: Fire boils in Water, but in exchange, it can extinguish it under certain circumstances, but Fire owes favors to the Sun, which can't stand Darkness and requires bribes not to hurt it, etc.
                                            The God-Machine, though, is unquestionably Order. Nothing requires promises or payment to work. They apply equally to everyone, even the fully unaware. They do not change. Mysterious events aren't actually mysterious, they just don't make sense unless you have the whole list of laws in front of your eyes. Gravity won't ever take the time to whisper "hey, looks like you'd like to fly, I can allow you to do that, but you have to do something for me first", because it's not sentient in any sense; if you manage to fly, it's because you are using aerodynamics, or a specific loophole in Gravity's equation that just hasn't been discovered by humans yet.
                                            None of these points of view is more true than the other. Darkness is both a kinda sentient concept one can bargain with if one knows the terms, and a lifeless phenomenon defined by the absence of light. However, one might argue that the physical world is ruled mostly by Order, and the Hedge and Arcadia, by the Wyrd. The Shadow might be the reflection (or rather the shadow) of the physical world, seen through the lens of the Wyrd.
                                            (And the Underworld? Haven't thought about it much, but I'd say it's ruled by neither. Nobody planned an afterlife where dead souls could go to, so they end up in that half-forgotten place without much guiding. No, this vision of the world doesn't include Mage or Sin-Eater or Mummy concepts, having played none of these.)

                                            As a side note, the God-Machine's most fundamental structure is made out of pure iron. Iron that formed and forged itself, shaping gears and levers and plates. Iron untouched by anyone or anything else. Iron that shaped itself because it was its Purpose, not because of any attachment or deal. Cold Iron.
                                            And this is why it hurts the fae.
