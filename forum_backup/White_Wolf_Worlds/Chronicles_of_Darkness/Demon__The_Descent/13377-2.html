    OK, so a bit of insight into my process as a designer:

    1) I don't get one shit about "game balance." I think that, in general, when people say "game balance" they're talking "white room combat" and I think that's so far removed from any actual play experience that it's completely useless as part of the process.

    2) I'm a big fan of killing darlings. If something is assumed to be a rules requirement, I want to know why. If there's something to be gained by fiddling with it, let's fiddle. Sometimes I think it's worth keeping rules principles intact (I was not a fan, for instance, of Mummy having "screw with target number" powers, though I haven't seen that in play so I don't know if it does or doesn't work), but it's always worth examining.

    3) With that said, I think that a useful definition of "game balance" is, "Is this ability so useful, in the context of the game, that not taking is an objectively dumb idea?"

    Now, I don't think we're quite there yet with On the Mend, and I'll tell you why in a minute. Let's instead look at a power that I think is useful enough that it's unbalanced: Electroshock Recharge from Promethean. It's a level-two Transmutation (so, what, 10XP if you're on Mercurius, 14 if you're not?), and all it does is piggy-back on the Created's existing ability to heal via electrocution; it lets them recharge Pyros as well as Health. In practice, as I've seen running the game, it means that the characters are always fully charged. Now, there are in-setting ways to limit that, but it's still so useful a power that you really should take it; there are few uses of your 10/14XP that give you the same value. If/when we do a revision of Promethean for GMC rules, that's something we'll address.

    For On the Mend, I think we're either at that level or approaching it, but not because it provides free healing. I think that there are two things at work here: first, that it provides free healing for yourself or anyone else, which is pretty potent, and second and more importantly, it works better than an Exploit that does something similar.

    What all that means is that I'm going to revise the Embed somewhat before the final version of the book goes to press. Whatever that revision is, it's not going to be some arbitrary limitation that I put in there to "nerf" the power because it's "too powerful." It's going to be because I want the power to be consistent - balanced - with the game around it.

                                        "White Room" balance is useless as an exercise, as it tends to ignore many significant factors in order to test a situation that basically doesn't exist in game: two characters enter and fight to the death for no reason, unaided and under equipped. It's a fairly dumb premise that produces dumb results. It's especially bad when we are doing this across game lines; that's like asking if a 7th level Tiefling Warlock/Fighter can beat an Adeptes Astartes Space Marine - it's just a pointless comparison. Such things can be fun exercises but ultimately shouldn't be used as any kind of game evaluation tool.

                                        What does matter are apple to apple comparisons, such as when a low level power does the exact same schtick as a higher level power, or very close comparisons can be made, such as with say, damage output. There it is a matter of making all available choices valid, and that's the most important thing in RPGs. You must be able to make choices and those choices must be valid.

                                                While it's a good ability, I don't actually have a problem with it, and here's why: I don't think that it fails Matt's "Is this ability so useful, in the context of the game" test. Consider this:

                                                1) Changing into their demonic form shouldn't really be a standard tactic, given the myriad of risks associated with doing so. The consequences of any kind of detection of your true form in Demon is vastly greater than for the other supernaturals, so I would expect that tactic to be much rarer than it is in other games;

                                                2) As a result of (1), the vast majority of combat will probably be engaged in without breaking Cover unless it's really, really necessary;

                                                3) When in Cover, Demons are squishy. Really squishy. As squishy as any bog-standard human;

                                                4) As a result of the above, I would expect most Demons to avoid combat unless it's completely unavoidable. This is, first and foremost, a 'spy' game. In that context, if bullets start to fly then 99% of the time it's because you've already failed and you're at your backup or bug-out plan. This is not a game where combat should be the first (or even second) solution to your problems. Hell, just unwanted attention by human law enforcement is enough to cause demons all sorts of problems that other supernaturals just don't have to worry about (or at least, not as much);

                                                5) Therefore, while a nice ability, I wouldn't expect it to see as much use in actual play as it would in other games, which intrinsicly reduces its value;

                                                6) By taking it (or any other combat power, many of which are also very nice) you are denying yourself access to an alternate power. Many of those other powers have widespread applicability and are, frankly, so full of utility in your day-to-day operations that it's just not funny.

                                                Seriously, just looking at some of the other Cacophany embeds, if you can't figure out a way to use Cause and Effect, Left or Right?, Lucky Break or Sabotage to great effect in combat, you're not thinking hard enough. Plus, all of those have great utility in normal activities as well.
