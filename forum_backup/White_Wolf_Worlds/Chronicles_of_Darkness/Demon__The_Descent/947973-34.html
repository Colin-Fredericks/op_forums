            Originally posted by Stupid Loserman View Post
            It's one NPC, potentially with unnatural cybernetic augmentations or other occult alterations, taking another NPC by complete surprise. Why bother with deliberate mechanics at that point?

            From a theory angle, primarily with an eye towards if you're considering what happens if he hunts the ring or the ring tries to stop it in progress? Probably the closest analogous situation would combine some access to unarmed lethal damage with the rules for a killing blow, where attack dice automatically succeed. A killing blow is an extremely potent maneuver reserved for when one character has another totally helpless, though. It's not an ambush attack; it's taking your time to find the right artery to squeeze while your victim is thoroughly bound or paralyzed.
            I don't intend for this antagonist to pull the same trick on PC's (not without at least rolling for surprise!) and I considered just running it by fiat as you suggest in your first paragraph. However, I just thought that it would be odd if the antagonist then just turned out to be your average street tough. I thought of using some Martial Art but it is hard to do enough damage in one turn to be fatal.

                                                        Can an angel in Cover Discorporate?

                                                        I am wrestling with the Twilight rules and conflict with angels. I want to understand how Twilight does not make angels completely overpowered. There are two cases to consider - an angel in Cover and an angel Materializing. Below are my interpretations of both cases, but I want to know what you think.

                                                        The second isn't too bad. Angel Arthur has a mission. He lives in Twilight, like all angels. He can spy from Twilight, but in Twilight our world seems distorted and speech is unintelligible. Being in Twilight doesn't give Arthur any special mobility. While in Twilight he can affect the normal plane only with those numina that have the "Reaching" quality. At times, to carry out his mission, he uses the Materialize Manifestation, perhaps with Mortal Mask. This can only last for a few hours and various abilities can see through Mortal Mask. If Demon Debby somehow attacks and Arthur is in trouble he can (if he has the Manifestation) Discorporate to escape. So Arthur may now know that someone who looks like Debbie (depending on details of the attack) is an enemy, but Discorporation is described as being painful, so I would expect that Arthur is out of combat for a few days. To me these aspects of Twilight prevent it from being overpowering.

                                                        An angel in Cover can also Discorporate. However, Cover is explicitly created by the GodMachine and not simply created by the angel. Discorporation would require (I think?) the GM to completely re-install the angel in Cover. So while Discorporation would allow an angel to survive, it is basically an admission of failure by the angel. Arthur may or not be prepared to pay that price, and the GM might or might not wish to re-install Arthur. However, if Arthur is not disassembled by the GM, I would expect that he is pissed and Debbie has now made an extra special antagonist.

                                                        So, can an angel in Cover Discorporate? Can the angel return to Cover without help from the GM?
