              Hey, Top Ten List? Top Ten List.

              Demon is the fastest I've ever gotten a top ten list, which is saying something because the other two are Changeling and Hunter(Yeah, I know, Hunter never got put up. I plan on fixing that soon). All the pieces lined up beautifully for pulling together a bunch of songs that people might like. Before I put these out as massive lists with lots of exposition, and I don't intend to stop with the second, but character limits, by necessity, break this list up. That's fine, we'll plan on releasing one or two entries a day. That said, we're going to start on a unique entry, an Entry 0. THis is normally an exception to the rule-either it's so well known that listing it is pointless, it's not that great but I personally feel like it's a number one contender, or a unique issue such as...

              0)The Angel Among Demons by The Instrumental Core

              I favor vocal music for these kinds of lists a lot because pure instrumentals/choral pieces tend to have a harder time conveying a direct theme by simple nature of the medium, but man does The Instrumental Core deliver on some mighty fine Demon songs. I put down The Angel Among Demons as an obvious sort of idea-you flip the title around and you have the idea of the game in a nutshell-but really any song by Instrumental Core delivers on the techgnostic feel of Descent. Powerful choral vocalizations permeate much of their work in celestial majesty, over sweeping chords of dubstep and electronica instrumental that bring with it the feeling of technological order and imposition. There is not a song by Instrumental Core I know of that doesn't carry the God Machine with it, and it's bombast and style, while making it inconvenient for the quiet or stealthy tense moments of a Demon game, is perfect for when the world comes crashing down and the demons must go to war with the angels. It also helps that most of their music is made free to own. The Angel Among Demons was the first one of their works I experienced, and it really sells everything you need to know about them. Putting the powerful struggle of the God Machine and it's children out in full majesty, The Angel Among Demons and the rest of The Instrumental Core's list will be the soundtrack you go to for when the heavens descend.

              Noteworthy Mentions: Shatter the Clouds, Hybridization of Humanity, Let There Be Light

                                  Moar top ten!

                                  10)The Salvation Code by Scandroid

                                  The God Machine is an insidious machine, full of plots and schemes and ideas that act across the heaven's expanse while we go about seeing a galaxy move by. The Salvation Code MIGHT be one such plan. The entire song is a love letter to the idea of god of the internet and of the machine, really, a testimony of the digital gospel delivered with a new retro sound complete with an 80's like cyberpop edge. So it's a God Machine song through and through, promising absolution to the unwitting humans promoted by their angels, right? Well, maybe not. The reason this song gets insidious is that demons work on much the same principle, and many of the lines, particularly the first part, work for humans but much more intensely well when it's a demon-the appeal to not dying, the freedom from a plugged in life, the identification of "this lonely place". It might be interpreted as an Integrator's theme, but it can also be loosely be appealed to as the illusion of Hell, that such salvation for the Unchained exists, that it's calling. It's hard to tell if this comes from Heaven and Hell viewed through the lens of Demon, and that's what's so disturbing about it. Whether it's a cry from the fallen or an eternal ploy, surely answers to the God Machine lie somewhere hidden within The Salvation Code.

                                  Noteworthy Mentions: Scandroid is a new band, and I'm not familiar with the Varien side of things, but Celldweller brings some awesome pieces for a Demon Soundtrack: Blackstar, The Lucky One, Unshakeable, Ursa Minor(Electron Mix)

                                              And the next entry is...

                                              9)End Of All Hope by Nightwish

                                              Where does a demon find hope? After all, a demon is more less born from the corpse of an angel covered in the ashes of their 'innocence' and faith, but can something else fill the void? End Of All Hope is a song that is brutal in its' pace and tempo, playing theme to a demon who doesn't really have the time to ponder these questions as they recover from their Fall. The pervading feel of fatalism never lets up, with instrumentals playing hard and fast and the goth operatic vocals bringing a sense of desperation to the entire affair, and yet because of it's energy it's not wholly an Integrator theme. Firstly, there's a casual sense of suicidal defiance, the willingness to drag the Machine down with you. Secondly, and more importantly, it's not wholly implied that the clarity of purpose, of faith and innocence is lost with the machine. In fact, the birth of all hope comes from the idea that it can be found again as the demon is born as a whole new being. Frantic, powerful, and ever conflicted, End Of All Hope is the flaming road to oblivion demons run on as they search for life after the Machine.

                                              Noteworthy Mentions: Nemo, I Wish I Had An Angel, Planet Hell, Higher Than Hope

                                                      8)God Slaying Machine by RegaP featuring Hatsune Miku (lyrics)

                                                      How far will anyone go for the people they connect to? What could create the drive necessary to break the God Machine, once and for all? God Slaying Machine may present a normal answer for these questions, but It's undoubtedly one of the most Demon-esque songs out there. Sung by Vocaloid program Hatsune Miku, the entire song has an ethereal autotuned feel that captures the alien grace of demons quite well, and the music itself is steadily despondent and defiant at the same time. You can feel the lengths and depths a demon would go through for their beloved human and their hatred of the steel heart of their God as this song moves desolation in beats to soaring guitar riffs as defiance is sung out in almost operatic tune. The key feature is the relationship between the human and the inhuman and the sympathetic connections between them, not just between the demon and her beloved but within the demon herself, the transition from the single track mind of an angel to the more emotive and wider feelings of a more earthly attitude. While it's one note in terms of relationships and much what you'd expect, nevertheless it's a great song for directly capturing the voice of a demon both vocally and atmospherically, showing quite nicely that it's the little things that turn an angel into a God Slaying Machine.

                                                      Noteworthy Mentions: Love is War(Pandora Remix) originally by Supercell, remixed by PandolistP, Dancer in the Dark by fatmanP feat. Megurine Luka, Lie by CircusP feat. Megurine Luka
