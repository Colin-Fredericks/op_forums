                Originally posted by JoeB View Post
                Matt, would you mind previewing the corrected rules on this thread pre-pdf launch (in the event that the KS pdf does not come out this month)? Some of us are already playing and this would be very helpful.
                Oh, sure. Here ya go:
                Success: Choose one: All of the target's bashing damage heals in fifteen minutes, or all of the target's lethal damage heals overnight, or all of the target's aggravated damage changes to lethal. A given target can only benefit from one use of On the Mend at a time, meaning that if the demon uses the Embed to change aggravated damage to lethal, she cannot then use the Embed to speed the target's healing of the lethal damage. Healing a normal human being of aggravated or lethal damage causes a compromise roll, and therefore demons are often loathe to do so. The demon can use this Embed on herself.

                Exceptional Success: As above, except the options are as follows: Target heals all bashing damage; target heals all lethal damage in an hour; target heals one point of aggravated damage per success or per dot of the demon’s Primum, whichever is lower.

                                    Originally posted by The Grim Lich View Post
                                    While it might appear to do so, it looks to me like you'd need to get 6 successes and 6 Primum to heal aggravated the way you heal lethal with an ES (5 of each if your target has Stamina of 1 or is a teenager). Otherwise, you'd probably heal 1-4, possibly 5 if your Primum is that high. You'd still be in the danger zone, and you'd still have a lot of aggravated damage to heal. Aggravated damage that wouldn't benefit from another use of On the Mend.
                                    On the contrary, lets say you've got a character with Primum 3. He's taken 4 damage. If this damage is aggravated an exceptional success immediately heals 3 points of it, leaving him with 1 Agg. Since he's no longer benefiting from accelerated healing at this point he could then immediately activate it a second time and heal that agg at lethal speed (or faster, if he's very lucky). If that damage is lethal he gains no immediate benefit, and ties up further use of On the Mend for the next hour. One hour later he's in peak condition, but that doesn't help if he's already dead.

                                    In other words, in the thick of battle (where quick healing really counts) he'd be in better shape if he'd taken aggravated damage than if he'd taken lethal. It's niche, but weirdly counter-intuitive.

                                            I think the conflict is coming from differences in interpretation. I read "A given target can only benefit from one use of On the Mend at a time" to mean that, once they've set to healing they can't use the Embed again until all the wounds affected by On the Mend are healed. That's a pretty clear line when dealing with bashing and lethal, you wait until the character has recovered all the damage taken at the time they used the Embed. But for something like an Exceptional Success on agg, where the Embed does all it's work in one action, it's less clear cut.

                                            Correct me if I'm wrong, you seem to be reading it as "once the demon has used On the Mend he cannot use it on himself again until he's completely healed." Which I'll admit is what I'd prefer it does (with the kicker that it can't be spammed on the same person more than once in a scene).

                                            Even then what would you say to the situation where the Werewolf did 3 Agg, and the Demon heals himself completely? Would you rule that the Demon still has to wait? Or that, having healed all damage, he can now use On the Mend on himself again.

                                            Note: I like this version, it's just a little weird and ambiguous.

                                            Edit: Actually, my original point still stands. If the Werewolf in your example had dealt Lethal damage the demon would still have 5 lethal boxes filled, putting him ~2-4 damage away from bleeding to death. But because it did Aggravated damage he has 3 more health worth of buffer, meaning the Werewolf has to do ~5 - 7 damage before the demon dies. In this life-or-death situation, having taken aggravated damage improved his chances of survival.
