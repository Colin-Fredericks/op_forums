                                Originally posted by irigi View Post
                                If a mage casts Correspondence over one or more sympathetic links and thus finds another mage with Space arcanum, will the target mage be able to sense the spell, e.g. using his peripheral mage sight?
                                If you cast Correspondence on a subject, and find the link to another mage, that mage won't notice anything because the spell isn't actually targetting them. If, however, you use the Reach option to chain connections and look for a sympathetic connection on that mage, then they'd notice because that mage basically becomes a new subject of the spell (not exactly since the spell isn't cast on them, but effectively the spell takes effect on them).
                                It's like grabbing the original subject's phone and finding the mage in the contacts list (no direct contact), and calling the mage to check their phone's contacts in turn (direct contact).

                                        Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                                        What would be the exact requirements for a mage to cast a turn enemy to stone permament spell?
                                        Assuming 2e rules: Transubstantiation, p. 158. Matter 4, Life 4. Reach for Advanced Duration. Reach again and spend Mana for Indefinite Duration. This is not a Lasting effect, meaning you need to either maintain this spell in your limited active spells, or reliably relinquish it by spending a dot (not point) of Willpower, to make sure the subject remains stone. Dispellation by another sorcerer can undo the transformation, bringing the enemy back to life. Dying without spending the dot of Willpower to safely relinquish will result in a weak relinquishment that will eventually decay and release the enemy in some form.

                                        How to do it as a Lasting effect? This is explicitly barred off as not intended game design, as per the "I Turn Him Into a Frog!" sidebar on p. 127.

                                                    Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                                                    Whats the difference between doing damage with fraying, waveing and patterning?
                                                    Weaving only changes the properties of the subject, which could create new ways to do damage (like adding bullets to a wristwatch or claws to a mage).

                                                    Fraying can only do bashing damage, as it just degrades a phenomenon under its purview, it can only cause bashing damage. Unraveling severely impairs and damages the subject, causing lethal damage, or aggravated with a Reach. Lastly, Unmaking simply annihilates the phenomenon.

                                                    So lets use the Life Arcanum as an example. A Weaving could give the mage the ability to crush a foe like a constrictor snake. A Fraying can bruise their opponents, which means they can just rest up and be fine. An Unraveling can do damage akin to stab wounds, being shot and bone breaking damage. An Unraveling with a Reach can end up destroying arms and legs, necrotizing an eye, causing permanent damage that requires a long amount of intensive care or powerful spells to heal. An Unmaking is simple, you Withand and live, or fail and die instantly.

                                                    Lastly, I recommend keeping in mind the nuances that exist within each Arcana's damaging spells. Time has a great example. Rend Lifespan is better than Weight of Years for damaging a living target. However, Weight of Years is even better than Decay when you want to destroy structures (such as cover) or equipment (like enhanced gear and spell tools).

                                                    Hope that proves helpful.

                                                            Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                                                            Could you give the same example for fire based damage?
                                                            In order to manipulate fire, you need Forces. Fire damage is covered on page 224 of the core book. It's greatest advantage is the continuous damage it does, which most attack spells can't replicate.

                                                            So you would need a source to manipulate via Control Fire, or to transform into fire with Transform Energy. You can also produce fire via Create Energy or producing the Inferno environmental tilt via Control Weather and Adverse Weather. The main challenges are that, without additional preparations or spellwork (such as using Environmental Shield on allies or the mage using the Environmental Immunity Attainment on themselves), the fire could spread to unintended targets, or burn the caster and their allies as well.

                                                            So probably the simplest way to harness it (and least Reach intensive) would be for the mage to have a fuel container of gasoline in their car and carry a lighter, then just use Control Fire to do controlled burns. It would require some coordination with the cabal to pull off in combat, but fire damage is powerful, so it would be worth it.
