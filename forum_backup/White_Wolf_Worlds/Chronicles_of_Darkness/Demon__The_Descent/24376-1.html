            Originally posted by Ephsy View Post
            Supernatural merits would come from an unlocked strain of DNA, and, given that Gnosis is knowledge, and you can impart it with pacts without someone else becoming dumber...
            Both true, but question: How would the player unlock that DNA? How would the player have the Supernal knowledge (Gnosis) to impart? In my reading of the book, ex nihilo creation is beyond even the God Machine, it requires Infrastructure to enact any changes.

            Here's how I think both of our stances can be reconciled: The "balance" is maintained by a debt/trade-off in another form rather than another's loss. An Awakened's Gnosis is increased through inspiration via tutelage by another Awakened. The enlightened Awakened now has whatever debt to the mentor he would otherwise, while the Demon is the one who made it happen. A Forsaken's Primal Urge is increased by his interactions with Spirit Courts, gaining him whatever reputation goes along with that, for good or ill. (My apologies if this isn't how that happens, I haven't read W:tF in a while.)

            About mortals gaining supernatural merits, suddenly they are in the supernatural world, if only tangentially of it. Their newfound merits make them quite useful to supernatural beings looking for disposable resources or resources that aren't nominally traceable to them. (When someone realizes their mind has been plumbed for information, they don't think Sin-Eater or Promethean, and when someone realizes they are being spied upon through projected senses they don't think Forsaken.)

            Do the players get involved in these dealings beyond their "brokering" the underlying changes in the universe? Do they involve the others in their espionage? That could lead in of itself to a great story.

            Once is happenstance. Twice is coincidence. Three times is enemy action. Four times is Infrastructure.

            Rule #2: Never let the game-mechanics get in the way of a good story.

                                    Along those lines, I asked this question in another thread but kinda phrased it badly : Could a human be made into a vampire with a pact?

                                    When you secure bits of cover via a pact... say, a best friend... are you obligated to use the best friend IMMEDIATELY? Or can you 'store' it in paper form for trade later? Let's say you trade somebody for a 'negative' life aspect, like a horrible ex. If you intend to barter that bit of cover to an agency, can you do so without having to have a horrible ex yourself for a while? Does the person who gave up the ex get the 'benifit' of the ex until the deal is 'cashed in'?

                                    Example :

                                    Joe Demon makes a deal with Frank for his disabled sister, who's a quadrapeligic anyway that he feels burdened by. This life-defining sibling is a juicy cover element that can provide the backbone for an entire identity. Joe wants to sell this to his local agency. When Joe presents the contract to Frank and Frank signs his sister away, does Frank's sister 'vanish' until somebody cashes in the contract? Does the sister become Joe Demon's sister, obligated Joe to be her 'nurse' until he can offload the contract to agency?

                                    I've considered lots of different ways of handling this, balanced towards realism and playability, and I'd like to see what the forum thinks.

                                        Generally, no. The impression I've gotten is that you should assume that members of one Greater Supernatural Template can't grant or empower other Greater Supernatural Templates—unless explicitly noted otherwise. This said, as Paddon said earlier, never let mechanics get in the way of a good story. Go with the option you think is best for your Chronicle.[1]

                                        Originally posted by MissMaddy View Post
                                        When you secure bits of cover via a pact... say, a best friend... are you obligated to use the best friend IMMEDIATELY? Or can you 'store' it in paper form for trade later? Let's say you trade somebody for a 'negative' life aspect, like a horrible ex. If you intend to barter that bit of cover to an agency, can you do so without having to have a horrible ex yourself for a while? Does the person who gave up the ex get the 'benifit' of the ex until the deal is 'cashed in'?
                                        Considering that soul pacts are tradable until cashed in, I'd handle it the same way. You can 'store' the pact and the cover element provided, but until then the pact's dormant. To use your example, Frank's sister remains Frank's sister, and thus Frank's burden, until someone calls in the pact and adopts the Cover of "has a quadriplegic sister." Frank gets the other benefits of the deal right away, though, if any.

                                            My issue with this is complexity.

                                            Joe Demon : So, I take the sister, and you get the showbiz job. Done.
                                            Frank : Wait, my sister is still here.
                                            Joe : Yeah. You have custodianship until I cash in.
                                            Frank: When will that be?
                                            Joe : Potentially never.
                                            Frank : Are you gonna let me know?
                                            Joe : Maybe.

                                            Later...

                                            Mary Demon : Hey, you got a sister to trade me?
                                            Joe : I -think- so. Let me text my client to see if he's still alive... ah, shit, he's at the basketball game. Can I getcha later?

                                            Later...

                                            Joe : Sorry Marry, turns out my clients sister got hit by a car a few days ago, so...

                                            In the end, it creates a kinda overcomplicated spiraling web of considerations, particularly if you're dealing with multiple pacts over multiple demons. To a large degree it seems more simple to just 'render' the Cover down into a 'Trading Card' - the cover becomes part of the 'character' of the Contract, and thus kinda vanishes from reality. Then it can be traded much more freely.

                                            That's the solution I settled on, not to suggest it's canon or appropriate or anything, that's just what I did and the reasons why.

                                                        Hmm. I have issues with this.

                                                        Second case ; I dislike the idea that humans can 'cheat' demons by double-dealing and making them fight with one another.
