                        On a slightly related note:

                        I'm writing a Chronicle that takes place in Oklahoma. Rather than map out small towns, fuel transfer stations or creepy farms, I've been using satellite views from Google maps. For example, the characters are fed that something odd happened at a location. Why wouldn't a demon google the location and look at the lay of the land? The images and actual locations help ground the game in the concrete, present. The players can see if a location is isolated or populated, plan their approach to the location or ask about some odd shaped building.

                        For example, the players will be given the numbers

                        35.406334, -99.683020

                        If the figure out that they are map co-ordinates and look in Google maps, they'll find a fuel transfer station. It's actually the location of some Infrastructure, invisible on the map but visible to a who demon visits the location. They can look online and see that the landscape is barren and that a stealthy approach might be difficult. They know how far it is from other locations in the game, and what that might mean in terms of the plot.

                        Of course when they get there not everything will be exactly as it was on the map. Perhaps some buildings are burned down since the images were taken. And perhaps some of the images turn out to be completely misleading. After all, who is better at hacking electronic information than the GodMachine?

                                                #147
                                                09-05-2019, 01:30 AM
                                                Originally posted by ajf115 View Post
                                                Does anyone have any ideas for plot hooks for a Mage/Demon crossover game? I've got an idea that the PCs have banded together for the sake of extra occult muscle and detection capabilities (on the demonic side) and access to the mother of all Mysteries (on the mage side), with both parts interested in trying to puzzle out some of the principles behind the Machine's workings.
                                                It might be fun to present Demons as an Outside Context Problem to the mages. By that I mean that the threat to the mages should break the rules, because interactions with the GodMachine play havoc with paradox and all sorts of rules of magic. One thing I like about Demon is that, as powerful as the characters are, they have to be very, very careful or the GodMachine will squash them like bugs. Add to this the duplicity and ruthlessness of the spy novel and it makes for a very different feel to the game.

                                                For example, mages A and B are hanging out in a cafe and their old friend mage C wanders up to meet them. Suddenly SKReeeehtCH her chest splits open and metallic cables snake out attempting to ensnare A and B. No one else in the cafe even notices - some powerful Force has the incident screened. A and B try to do something and their magic sputters, and Mysterious Stranger D shows up, helps them barely escape from C, and says "come with me if you want to live". (tm).

                                                Mysterious Stranger D explains that there is a Force hunting mages, harvesting them. This Force can detect significant use of magic. The Stranger warns them not to trust C if they see her again. Stranger D offers to help free C from the clutches of this Force, called the GodMachine by raiding the Harvesting Infrastructure.
                                                • Can the mages trust any of their old friends? How can they be sure?
                                                • If they tell the larger mage community, are they just drawing them into more danger?
                                                • Does D really want to save C? If so, why are they bringing all those explosives? What are their true motives? Is D just using A and B for some other goal?
                                                • What level of magic can the mages use and not have the GodMachine detect them? How pervasive is it?
                                                • From the mages PoV, what are the Demons? What is this weird set of identities (Covers) they have? Who are they really?
                                                The Demon characters need the Mages because (1) Demons are pretty squishy when in Cover, (2) they have to worry about cover, and (3) Mages are superb at finding things out. The Mages need the Demons because they don't know what is going on. The Demons have to be very careful with their Covers because revealing too much to the mages will compromise their Cover.

                                                            Originally posted by ajf115 View Post
                                                            While that's great material, one bit I'm not really clear on is how the demons would be able to effectively help the mages without blowing their Covers entirely. I feel like they'd have to interfere beforehand, preventing the meeting, rather than intervene in medias res.
                                                            Demons can tell mortals about their identity, it just risks Compromise. They can tell them "I am not human, and you and I both are fighting a common, other-worldly, powerful entity." That would result in a Compromise roll, but not destroy their Cover. Demons make similar disclosures all the time when they try to get a mortal to sign a pact of any sort.

                                                            You are correct that if the Mage keeps picking at the Demon's Cover, investigating their details, this will result in more Compromise rolls. This could make for interesting game play though. The Demon might ask the Mage to back off, stating that they can't explain why. What does the Mage do? If the Cover collapses, the Demon might switch to a different Cover identity. How will the Mage react to a new face claiming to be the demon?
