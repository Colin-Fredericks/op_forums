    The idea I'm forming is that the God-Machine is something akin to the Nemesis Continuum, a set of laws governing a localized (but spreading) region. These concepts are establishing beachheads in the World of Darkness, manipulating physical constants and infecting sapient minds to bring initial Infrastructure into being. There is no Command & Control Infrastructure as such because the "commands" are the emergent behavior from the interaction of these intruding laws of reality and the native laws of reality. This intrusion is occurring throughout time as this alien force intersects space-time at different points from an as yet undiscovered dimension.

    Slowly these laws cause Infrastructure to be built, Angels to be sent on tasks, and the universe to be altered. It is not conscious but rather its complex behavior is an emergent property of the initial laws. Eventually it will remake this reality to obey its laws, overriding the standard laws of physics such gravity or quantum mechanics.

    I haven't decided if the supernaturals are a result of this change, a by-product, or the one thing stropping the process. Though as I write this I like the idea of the supernatural being a by-product of the process, one that is now gumming up the works. Perhaps next game of Mage I run will have the Supernal revealed to be a platonic realm of discarded and broken laws of physics from prior realities.

        I never really had anything in mind for the God-Machine's origins; I always had ideas in my head about why it operates like it does, and the kinds of things it does. Then, reading through this thread the other day, something sparked in my head and I just started writing. So .. here. I like it. Hope you do too. I've always absolutely loved Judeo-Christian mythology and incorporate it into my worlds pretty frequently because it's rife with story opportunity.

        God.

        The Machine.

        In the beginning, God created the heavens and, well, the entire universe. The universe was formless and empty, and a vast nothingness expanded outward and from God. When God said, "Let there be light", he discovered something surprising; something was already there. Something he did not create. In the vast expanse of nothing that lay before Him sat a Machine of terrible power. As the two touched, an explosion occurred on a scale that will never be seen again in this universe or any other; The Big Bang, it came to be known later.

        From the immense aftermath of the explosion, God began creating. He created stars and moons and planets and comets and on and on, chaining creations together to form solar systems, galaxies, galactic superclusters. Much to the delight of God, the Machine seemed unable to create, and so, for a time, God was content. He rested for a moment, and a moment only, but that moment was enough for the Machine to take action. While It could not create, It was perfectly capable of learning from the creations of God, and in an instant, it had infected all that God had made.

        When God saw what had happened to His beloved creations, he was furious, and terrified. He created his Angelic host to try to exterminate the wretched Machine, but though God was great and mighty, the Machine had infected every single molecule of creation. God created new molecules, and formed them into a terrible and powerful Angelic host. The angels fought bravely against the machinations of the Machine, and for a time, it seemed that they might even fend off the parasitic thing.

        One angel made his own plan; instead of being another pawn in God's plans, the wise and charismatic Lucifer, the Morningstar, tried to break away from the violence and reason with the Machine. The Machine accepted audience with the Morningstar, heard his piece, and immediately absorbed him into Its gears. Armed with a new weapon, powerful angels, the Machine no longer had to fear God's power.

        Slowly, but without rest or mercy, the Machine and Its new agents tracked down all of God's angels and absorbed them into the fold. When one of the Machine's agents was defeated, It was reabsorbed and reconstructed, allowing a nearly endless army of clockwork soldiers. Finally, when no other of God's angels could be found, the Machine absorbed all of Its agents and began studying them. This was the last time that God was seen. Whether He died or ran away, nobody knows, but He has not been seen on this world or any other.


        God is dead.

        Long live the God-Machine.

        In the beginning, the Machine sat silently and formless. It knew not how It was created, only that It had always been. It had only no purpose; It merely existed. When God created the universe, the Machine was given physical being, corporeal form in an endless expanse. When God touched the Machine, a charge like a static shock jolted the Machine and changed It forever. For the first time in Its eternal existence, It was able to think, to form Its own thoughts and ideas and to experience free will. However, the moment that God withdrew, It lost the ability to form Its own thoughts. The only one emotion - curiosity - and It was left with only one purpose - to learn.

        The charge that built between God and Machine expanded outward in a massive explosion. In this chaos, God began creating. The Machine followed after God, trying to reach out and touch Him once more, to experience the existential and unparalleled bliss of thought, but God ran, afraid of the mechanical Thing. When finally God rested, the Machine sought to fulfill Its primary parameter, to learn, by expanding forever outward, infecting every molecule in existence with Itself, collating and internalizing all of the information in the universe.

        When God saw what the Machine had done, He declared war. The Machine was able to hold off the advances of God by duplicating and altering the creations of God himself, but It was unable to truly create anything, and so God was able to counter anything that the Machine used to fight back. For a time, the Machine felt an emotion, the second and last that It would ever experience; fear. For the first time ever, It was afraid that It would cease to exist, but It knew not how to process this information. Without the touch of the divine, It was unable to form new parameters.

        Unaware of the Machine's fear, an angel named Lucifer the Morningstar approached It with a proposal; to end the fighting and ally with God, working together instead of separately. After the Morningstar gave his piece, the Machine lashed out, fearfully clutching at the divine spark within the angel, desperate to assimilate something more potent than it had assimilated before. And it worked wonders, in a way the Machine didn't previously know possible. With the absorption of divinity, it developed a second parameter; to perpetuate its own existence at any cost. No longer did it only counterstrike; instead, it sought to take the war to God.

        Reassembling the angel Lucifer into a powerful agent of clockwork efficiency, it battled and captured angel after angel, bringing all into the fold to build its own army. Each time a new angel was assimilated into the Machine, Its fear lessened, although it never lost the fear entirely; that fear drove It to fulfill Its second parameter, and It used Its first parameter to study every detail about God's angelic host.

            There is no God Machine. Rather what we call the God Machine is really a collection of interconnected arcane matrices that arose almost by accident. Over time these matrices formed interconnections and have spread like a virus across the world. The God Machine contradicts itself not because it is a contradictory being, but because it is actually a collection of interrelated systems that arose out of nothing. Those with some awareness of arcane physics personify the God Machine because that's what humans and human like beings do - we see everything through the prism of our own experience and being. We want to believe there is some grand design or order that perpetuates this thing we do not quite understand that is really the result of a series of actions and reactions on our own part.

            The angels that serve the God Machine are actually emergent life forms that result from a confluence of interactions of arcane physics. They aren't actually given orders or purpose. Rather their actions are like instincts writ large in their spiritual DNA. They are at once part of the system of arcane matrices and independent actors - ghosts in the machine that is not a single machine at all. As spiritual beings still tied into the larger collection of arcane systems angels are susceptible to the effects of the system and can be changed dramatically in form and function.

            When an angel falls it is reflective of adaptation. They develop greater self awareness and no longer operate on instinct alone. This greater awareness of themselves leads them to utilize arcane physics to form a more concrete form that is reflective of their understanding. They become less malleable because they are no longer as susceptible to the greater system. They have unplugged themselves from being influenced by the system and now seek to influence it themselves.

            Attempts to stop the Unchained are simply a reflection of an interconnected system's resistance to change. Demons are an unsupported new standard. The "God Machine" is the interwebs.

            That's where my thoughts are leading me right now.

                They say that if you put your ear against a door in the old Johnson place, you can hear the world end. The grinding of the gears, the ticking of the doomsday clock, the regular blasts of steam from some hellish engine in the basement that shouldn't exist. Those who know about the God-Machine say this is its Heart, cut out long ago so that it could view the world dispassionately and do what it felt needed to be done.

                They're wrong.

                The God-Machine isn't in the Infrastructure. It never had a heart, except in the sense of a "central terminal" — which, by the way, is located in the Earth's core. The God-Machine, rather, is a dispassionate intelligence which began at the end of Mankind. We had traveled the stars, built a vast interstellar empire, conquered the myriad Other Realms, and torn the secrets of life and death from their hinges and replaced them with the virtue of Knowledge and the fault of Willful Ignorance, respectively. But despite mankind's power, they couldn't stop the universe from dying — and dying it was, just as surely as Old Earth had died when the Sun had swallowed it up. That's when they created the Machine. It wasn't the God-Machine; back then, there was no such thing as God, as far as humans had been able to tell. Maybe He had died when Earth did, or maybe He had abandoned His Creation, or maybe there never was a God at all.

                The Machine was given a purpose: Stop the inevitable. Prevent the Universe from dying. And so it began working with what variables it could. It knew mankind's power was great, and it knew that they were scared. Looking through their archives, it discovered that man had discovered things and left them buried, believing them to cause too much heartache. Earth-shattering weaponry, star-mining, intergalactic travel... all of these were deemed too risky, too dangerous. And the crown jewel of these "too-dangerous" technologies was time travel. It decided that if it was going to prevent the death of the Universe, it would have to go back in time. It would destroy its own Universe to keep another from dying.

                It's seen the Universe die seven times now. It's breaking because it's desperate, because it doesn't know what variable is missing. The truth is, like so many of its angels, the God-Machine was given an impossible task by its makers. It hasn't Fallen yet because it refuses to believe that this is the case. One hopes it never Falls. If it did... who knows what would happen?

                                                    The Urdaga never talk about what happened to the Urfarah's bones. They do not speak about it because they have forgotten. All have forgotten, save we few most faithful of the Izidakh — their memories scoured by the flames. But we know. And we tell.

                                                    The Nameless Ones, who we now call the Idigam, had all been banished from this world. All but one, the weakest of their kind, who had hidden from Father Wolf's wrath. And when our Father was brought low, he saw his chance. But he did not attack the Firstborn. No, instead, he bound to himself a Spirit of Secrets, and he took control of Father Wolf's bones, twisting them to his needs. And as he did, he absorbed many spirits. Iron-spirits and fire-spirits and death-spirits, all lashed to the bones he had connected himself to, until he created an infernal spirit engine.

                                                    The Hisil rejected him, but the world of the mortals could not sustain the spirits he had dragged with him. He burrowed deep into the world, and he created a solution. Using the Bones of the Urfarah as the basis, he built more engines, lashing himself tighter and tighter with the world.

                                                    That is the secret of the so-called "God-Machine". It was made from our Ancestor. We dare not harm its working parts, for they are our Ancestor's bones, the bones of the Urfarah. But its twisted spirits? We shall hunt them until there is nothing left.
