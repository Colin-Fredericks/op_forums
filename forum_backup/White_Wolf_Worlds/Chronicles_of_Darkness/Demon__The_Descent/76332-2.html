        No, I feel it is neglected is the right word. Why? Because if its optionally applied.... then they should have been listed as an option for every power. They did not do so.

        Lets say that we have a case of using Bystander Effect. One PC decides that, hey, he wants to kill a human in a bar by using the Embed. There is another PC nearby, who's witnessing the assassination. By the rules, that PC has NO option to resist the Bystander Effect. He's not related to person being killed. This is a concern. There should be some Condition or the like covering this event. It may be rare, but it should be covered in the event it does.


        Most of the Embeds follow in the same vein. Using Cause and Effect against an NPC without a resistance of some kind is fine, but a fellow PC? One person employing Cool Head Prevail when the rest of the party wants to go kill happy?

        The Embeds tend to assume that you're against certain kinds of opponents. And that these opponents generally should be cultist mooks or pawns. Ergo, the lack of resistances. Ergo, Merciless Gunman's main use in mook killing. When used against fellow PCs, or, hells, even major NPC demons/angels? No, that's not right; it is highly unthematic and makes little sense to me. Hells, the whole raison d'etre of Conditions was to cover these very situations! I'm going to stick by my choice of words and say that these situations were neglected.

        The thing about Merciless Gunman though, it is explictly written that against PCs or Angels or any major enemies that won't utilize Down and Dirty Combat, the effect changes to just firearm attack with 8-again.

        See, I do think that since the resistance or subtraction have not been listed as an option for every power, especially because designers intended for some powers to be resisted/contested. If ALL of those powers did not have any resistance options, I would have said the same. But I think the distinction itself means that the designers did intend it, and I think it could be what gives demon an edge. Of course I might be wrong and the designers could have left them out by mistake, but I don't think that's the case as of now.

                                                Skimmed through most of the thread...not sure what the fuss is about. As a ST/GM (whatever you want to call it) I adhere to a simple rule of thumb when it comes to any supernatural powers used on other supernaturals (even subtle powers that don't normally allow resistance rolls) ... CLASH OF WILLS. One of the best mechanics in the game. Consistently keeps players from feeling railroaded. So in all of the examples of Demon A uses subtle power Z in the presence of Demon B ... Clash of Wills to notice. Also, always remember that just because a player notices a power has gone off doesn't imply that they have any info on the source. This principle extends to cross-overs (my favorite way to play). I also like to tinker with game mechanics in my spare time so...

                                                    Originally posted by Xenesis View Post

                                                    The Devs have stated on multiple occasions that they don't feel that the 'white room mashup' is actually important, helpful or useful. They've stated multiple times that they don't think inter-genre 'balance' is important at all, and that if something is bad for crossover but good for the individual game, they'll do the thing that is good for the individual game.
                                                    I have a weird relationship with crossover. I actually really like it, and I like it when the rules mesh. That's why Clash of Wills is useful - it's nice to have a standardized way for powers to relate to one another that an ST can use as a default. So crossover and a certain degree of "balance" is good.

                                                    But "balance" isn't white-room balance. White-room balance (that is, "devoid of any context, are these character types equally capable, usually at murdering one another") is stupid. It's stupid because a) it never comes up in play, EVER, and b) players are clever, and they spot things like the Endless Blood Point Loop that shows up in the Coils of Blood in VtR.

                                                    We're not going to spot all those things. Ever. No matter how good we are or who we have proof, there are *always* going to be things that look "overpowered" because a player reads them and thinks, "oh, shit, I know how to exploit that."

                                                    So, instead, I ask - I insist - that players consider where their exploit is awesome and thematic, or just a way to go "BROKE YOUR GAME LOL" at the Storyteller. If the form, awesome. If the latter, I don't care.
