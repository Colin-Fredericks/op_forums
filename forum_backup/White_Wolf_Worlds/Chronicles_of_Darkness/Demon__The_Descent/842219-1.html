                                                    I think the answer that it might be required by an occult matrix is really the only situation I could conceive of. Otherwise, I'm inclined to agree that the God-Machine would maybe use someone Claimed by a spirit in some circumstances. Bonus points if it's an angel directing that effort.

                                                    EDIT: Also the reason why it's worth looking at the 1E books is not because of some kind of legal precedent for the rules, but rather conceptual precedent. Looking at where certain mechanics were introduced in the original line helps you glimpse the intent of the writers, especially because they're working with the same (though perhaps updated) bibles that informed the writing of the 1e books.

                                                    I think, insofar as Claiming is concerned, that we've seen it discussed in a few books. We've totally seen it talked about with regards to Spirits, a lot. Hell, Claimed are a central element of the antagonism between Spirits and Werewolves, and pretty much everywhere else they crop up (like in the Book of Spirits) it's also discussed.

                                                    I think the difficulty I run into is that I can't really conceive of any situation besides an input for an occult matrix where Claiming somebody makes more sense than using a regular Angel or a servitor.

                                                    To use another example of reading the books closely, we're informed repeatedly that the Machine likes to use existing infrastructure where possible. The way you can synthesize that and other behaviors is that it tries to accomplish its goals as efficiently as possible, using the least resources it can. Creating Angels requires an immense amount of resource, so the Machine is so averse to destroying them unless it has to that it's willing to send other Angels after Demons so it can recycle them into Angels again. We've seen a few instances of discussion where the God-Machine deliberately sets Angels against one another or to Fall and shit, but again it's almost always talked about in the context of occult matrices.

                                                    So, what can be drawn from that is that it doesn't waste Angels unless they have to feed an occult matrix with their destruction/fall. It's got to be important to warrant that. Just the same, an angel Claiming somebody sounds like it's not likely to occur except for an occult matrix. So I'm not sure how many other edge cases can be manufactured.

                                                    I guess as some advice/food for thought, I definitely think the coolest times this kind of Claimed thing should be used in an Occult Matrix would be ones that actually apply to Angels. Maybe this Claimed is the key input to create an even more powerful angel? Or to make something like the Galatea hook in Splintered City: Seattle.

                                                            I'm aware, tangentially, that Claiming is a Big Deal for Spirits and Werewolves. Which is sort of why it became a topic of interest to me. If it's so significant, why is it available for Angels with no word count dedicated to its impact on the setting? Spirit-only effects exist are called out as such and Claimed is not one of them.

                                                            So yeah, the obvious conclusion, which I was able to come to on my own, was that the writers didn't really see Angels Claiming people as a big enough deal to talk about. Nevertheless it's an option and given how integral it is to Werewolf it can clearly lead to some interesting stories. Hence this topic.

                                                            Now, personally, I feel there are a few more options than just "Occult Matrices" as an explanation for why the God-Machine may seek to manifest an Angel with the Claimed Manifestation. But first of all:

                                                            Why, exactly, is this seen as "wasting an angel"? The Angel still exists. If its body is destroyed, it can move on and Claim a new target. It loses access to Numen, sure, but it still exists, it can still contribute to future projects, it can still follow the God-Machine's orders and use its Influences and has a new unique skill set unlike many of its angelic siblings with which to carry it out. It can even abandon its Claimed body if its Ephemeral form becomes more useful to the God-Machine's next mission. So why this idea that dedicating an Angel to Claiming a target is equivalent to destroying the Angel?

                                                            In terms of reasons to Claim, the obvious ones that leap out are:

                                                            -Infiltration. The Angel can access the target's mind, and be the target in every way once it has been Claimed. Obviously, this works best when the Claimed Being is one whose physical alterations can be discreetly concealed. In Demon, certain changes could be passed off as glitches/partial transformations.
                                                            -Motivation. Getting a Saboteur to give up all their secrets is a whole lot easier if they become a loyal Angel, merging their personalities so that at least part of them wants to help.
                                                            -Special skill sets. The Claimed being can develop Supernatural Merits. These can be combined with the Angel's influences to create abilities that Ephemeral Angels couldn't possibly possess. Combined with the massively boosted attributes and long term nature of Claimed, this could be a lot more useful to a variety of projects than a Possessed or Materialised Angel.
                                                            -Efficiency. At least in terms of Essence. I mean, it's certainly clear that the God-Machine has no trouble producing Essence, but as it strives to reach results in the most efficient manner it could be that a being able to operate without Essence for prolonged periods of time could be attractive for it due to the corresponding reduction in supply costs.
                                                            -"God moves in mysterious ways". Yeah, this is really just the Occult Matrix argument, but a little broader. No one understands occult physics. Maybe the Machine only had the resources to manufacture an Angel with the Claimed ability. Maybe there would have been unacceptable side effects to specialising the Angel in a different direction. Who knows?
