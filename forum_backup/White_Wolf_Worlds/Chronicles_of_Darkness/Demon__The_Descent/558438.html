    Demons and the God-Machine elsewhere?

    06-17-2015, 02:00 PM
    I have two questions, and this seemed the most appropriate forum for them, though I may be wrong:

    a) What all books besides the original blue book, GMC, and Demon: the Descent reference or discuss the God Machine?

    b) DtD core mentions that some DtD-type Demons appeared in Promethean. I admit I never quite finished reading all the books for that line (I love it, but got distracted), but I'm having trouble thinking of anything that would fit except maybe Mr. Gold and Ruby. (who are nominally Qashmallim, but actually seem a weird fit for that, in retrospect and could be reimagined as Demons with an interest in the supernatural without too much fuss)

      a)

      Keep in mind that the God-Machine was radically reenvisioned for game design purposes from the GMC onwards; it seems Rose had some of the seeds of ideas that would become Demon: the Descent early, but a lot of the pre-GMC references don't quite neatly fit into the context of the post-GMC presentation. That may be a drawback or a boon, depending on how you take your inspiration.

      Several books have opening fiction pieces referencing the Voice of the Angel from the blue book. Most of these are recollected in the God-Machine Chronicle Anthology. They include, among others, Mysterious Places (that's a good one), Urban Legends (I believe the first appearance of the Deva Corporation), and Asylum.

      Saturnine Night concludes Promethean's sample adventure path by confronting the throng with a Zeka who has discovered a great nuclear god-machine of grinding gears underneath Detroit.

      Danse Macabre presents the Holy Engineers, an esoteric covenant of Kindred who worship Death, angel of the God-Machine, as their patron, and receive prophetic transmissions by transmitting prayer-signals to what they believe to be the God-Machine somewhere in the vicinity of the Orion asterism. Cool stuff.

      Rose seeded a few proto-demons and early DtD style God-Machine phenomena in a few supplements. Midnight Roads contains Mr. Blue in an encounter sidebar, and Hunter: the Vigil presents Mr. White as a sample greater demon. These characters seem to be more focused on ideas of recompense and retribution, especially punishment of vice, than the Unchained eventually turned out to be. Midnight Roads also has gremlins, whom Rose has said she considers to be possibly malfunctioning lesser angels of a sort. Glimpses of the Unknown's Promethean section contains The Cat, whom Matt McFarland has used in his games as an exile.

      Hunter, of course, devotes a chapter in its Mortal Remains supplement to the creatures of Demon: the Descent as viewed through the lens of hunters on the Vigil.

      Various books have had shorter story hooks and teasers sprinkled here and there. Off the top of my head, there are mentions and brief hooks in Tome of the Watchtowers, Imperial Mysteries, and the second edition Vampire and Werewolf corebooks.

      b)

      This remains a confusing point, I think. Matt, who developed the Promethean supplements following the corebook, has said that he doesn't know of anything in there that would refer to. I think it's more that Mr. Gold and Ruby drew upon some of the same inspirations that later influenced Demon: the Descent as a whole.

              In terms of good books with inspirational material that you don't already have, I'll recommend Danse Macabre and Imperial Mysteries as both solid books. Mortal Remains is a mixed bag, one of those books that varies in quality and presentation of subject matter from chapter to chapter, but the Demon chapter is the strongest one of them in my opinion, and enriches and expands both Hunter and Demon while doing neither of them a disservice. Danse Macabre is the equal and opposite in the sense that aside from the Holy Engineers, relatively little of the book is relevant to Demon or the God-Machine, but it's filled with lots of strong material covering all sorts of different topics relevant to Vampire.

              Maybe like half or so of the Anthology reprints short stories already in other books, but the other half or so is new material. Of the new stories, Stew Wilson's "Go Back" is my favorite. It's a sympathetic story about a protagonist who goes through emotional hell but is empowered over the course if it in a way that is appropriate for and resonant with player characters in a game, and it captures the themes of uncanny facades hiding bizarre distortions of everyday life in a strong and evocative manner.

                  The thing about the god-machine is that quite a bit can be viewed as connected but is left ambiguous.

                  For example, antagonists has places that make zombies, frankensteinian technozombies, a living webpage, a sapient memetic virus, occultism/folk magic, and zombie plagues in it.

                  One so inclined could see all of those things as occult matrices or infrastructure or the results thereof, but if you don't want to, then that's okay too.

                  Magical traditions has "machine elves" that "aren't spirits." GM angels? If you want them to be.

                  And so on and so forth.
