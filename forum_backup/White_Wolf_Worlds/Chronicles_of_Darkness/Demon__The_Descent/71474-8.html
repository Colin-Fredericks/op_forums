    I think the technological angle really limits the game. I personally have no problem with a demon game being neutral and actually would prefer it to not have any references to angels/god in any way. I would have preferred another antagonist (exorcists, demonologists, other demons, etc) instead of angels. When I look at DTD, I just cannot see it as a demon game, no matter how hard some people keeps trying to explain it to me. Just slapping the titles angels and demons to them does not make them so. Instead, I just see the matrix, which I think they were a little too inspired by. Demons are just rouge programs, angels are agent smith, the god machine is just a super A.I. trying to run or take over the matrix. A great game by itself but not really a demon game in my opinion.

    As I said, hard to see demons when there are words like "glitches" to describe circuit board tattoos and wire hair. That really does not help convince me otherwise. Again, I have no problem with a neutral game, but if you title the game DEMON, it really should have been made in a way where you could EASILY put this in any other theology or pop culture reference WITHOUT having to amputate or alter a big chunk of the game design to make it fit.

    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
    For the love of God.

    Okay, GNOSTICISM. Let's talk about it, since it's one of the root words. I hope someone better than me comes along to explain this, but here we go.

    In the beginning there was a single consciousness. For various reasons, this consciousness split itself into two minds, masculine and feminine. Between interactions of the two, a new pantheons of beings called Aeons emerged and continued going about things. At this point, everyone has been living pretty happily in a spiritual world. One Aeon, Sophia, made a mistake(again, ascribed to various reasons) and gave life to the primordial material world, either being trapped there or destroyed there. Out of the material divine emerged the Demiurge, He's proceeds to make the universe as we know it out of the material and seperates it from the rest of the spiritual. Being an incompetent god figure who believes himself all powerful and the Absolute God, he basically traps any spiritual essence in the material, and the soruce of all suffering in the world comes the fact of this division.

    His servants have two names-archons and angels. When pressed on the subject, many people who know more about Gnosticism says that effectively there is no difference between these.

    DOES ANY OF THIS RING A BELL TO YOU?

    Despite Abrahamic faiths possibly having their roots in Gnosticism, it must be said for the God Machine's servants: There were never Judeo-Christian angels, they were always Gnostic Angels.

    I will go back to my earlier argument. If someone has to repeatedly keep using a sledghammer to change other peoples view to see how this is a demon game, then the problem is probably with the game and not the people.

    I like DTD, it is just too limiting on how you play it and what you can do with it. It would have been better if there was more variety of beings called DEMONS from different origins outside of the GM. While there would be a group of demons created by the GM, others could be some created/work like in inferno, and others could be from other myths/theologies/popculture..etc given a chance to introduce a wide variety of beings called "Demons". Each variety of demon has its own methods, motivations, goals that may be similar or widely different. I think that would have been a much better game and still could be if something like this was introduced in supplements.

    On a totally different side note. DTD is missing a few big cultural references to demons. Things like summoning and/or binding a demon. Summoning and binding is practically synonymous with demons.

                On the other hand, I do not think most people have this familiarity with Gnosticism. Nor do I think it should be necessary for people to be familiar with Gnosticism before the similarities between it and the angels of this book start ringing bells. =(
                Gotta say, I emphatically disagree with this sentiment, or the sentiment that the Angels or Demons of a game have to align with a narrow definition of the term.

                We're all familiar with tropes of how Our Vampires Are Different or Our Werewolves Are Different. At this point since there isn't a centralized narrative of what any of those things are, There's a lot of different versions and room for innovation.

                The idea that Angels have a strict canon that needs to be adhered to (particularly a Christian one) seems disingenuous if not outright imperialist. I really hate throwing that word around in this discussion, but I really fail to see how the change in veneer is problematic.

                People make Angels that are dickish or uncaring all the time in fiction. They make some pretty weird angels. They make ones that are people + wings. The Testaments do both, as far as I know. I don't see how putting a few wires or circuits on that make them less angelic than these other myriad weird angels that have been appearing in fiction and in religious scriptures for ages now.

                I also think it's worth pointing out that if you feel like you REALLY NEED to stick a strict idea of what Angels should look like or act like, there's little stopping you from applying a visual palette swap to them.

                Shave the Machine off of God. Call them Miracles instead of Occult Matrices. Etcetera.

                If anything, the miniscule amount of work required to get rid of the techgnostic to just have gnosticism is incredibly easy, and if we're just saying "Well, Gnosticism isn't good enough" then I'm confused about why it was cool in Mage but not cool here, unless we're assigning some sort of hierarchy to our belief in fantastical angelic beings.

                Which I don't think is really a legitimate way to argue the point.

                The Angels and the Demons here play exactly the parts you see Angels and Demons playing in other fiction. If they're n ot recognizable as angels or demons as some kind, it seems to me that you're deliberately not looking at them with the lens of broad fiction (which seems to be the reasonable, intelligent way to look at any line) and that you're decrying them for not fitting a very specific paradigm.

                                                One thing I'd like to add about the discussion of gnosism here, and the one time Demiurge that came up.

                                                Techgnosis doesn't directly have anything to do with actual gnostic beliefs. Techgnosism is belief in that machines can be spiritual and magical, and can relate from shamanism through creationism through actual gnostic teachings. The term itself seems to be coined by the book Techgnosis: Myth, Magic, and Mysticism in the Age of Information, by Erik Davis. That book is listed as Inspirational Material in the Demon core book.


                                                So, Techgnosis is just Promethean alchemy dialed up to modern times. In Promethean, alchemy is (victorian era) science with a spiritual aspect to it. In Demon, techgnosis is modern science with a spiritual aspect to it. If we made a Demon game in the victorian era, there's a good chance that the Unchained might resemble the Created! So, so many themes those two share.


                                                TL;DR: Point is, Techgnosis just means "machines are magic."
