        Originally posted by TempleBuilder View Post
        Is a museum full of fossils close enough to a graveyard to have a associated Avernian Gate?
        Is it a place of Death? Because the Resonance of Death — "this is a place where a lot of death has occurred and/or been been thought about" — is the distinguishing factor moreso than "are there the remains of living creatures kept here?"

        Fossils being many many thousands of years past the point of being actual dead matter, it would have to go to the judges whether the fossils are enough to shift a museum's vibe toward the deathly. Some of the mineralized skulls and bones might put visitors and staff in the right frame of mind, but while fossils are likely to make solid material for Moros Path Tools, petrified wood has been a material for the Obrimos for ages.

        Symbols are tricky, but it's important to remember that "Avernian Gates appear in places with a Resonance of Death" is a fact with narrative weight.

            Unless Geist describes Resonance of Death for graveyards specifically in terms of being a place where it's thought about, I'd even wonder if it shouldn't be about somewhere heavily invested with the ritual of death. A graveyard accumulates the qualities that lead to the formation of a passage to the Underworld because, well, they're where people conduct the rites of quite literally committing a deceased person to their next destination. The creation of the gate is the literal expression of what the symbolic and social function of the graveyard is.

            A museum has none of that, and is arguably contrary to it; it's taking remains and keeping them in a context of preservation, removed from the association of the vessel of the dead that must be committed to a final resting place and into being mere objects for study and observation. There's no passage to the next life innate to a museum, just a superficial association. You might as well say that a Halloween supply store can become the foundation for an Avernian Gate.

                Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                Unless Geist describes Resonance of Death for graveyards specifically in terms of being a place where it's thought about, I'd even wonder if it shouldn't be about somewhere heavily invested with the ritual of death.
                I should clarify: "has been thought about" is me shorthanding "not a lot of people die in graveyards or morgues, but they have a Resonance of Death just as surely as a battlefield or a hospice does" — that's "a Resonance of Death makes observers inclined to think of it as a place of Death," not the other way around.

                Geist's bullet-point list of the continuum of Underworld areas describes the cenotes/low places in Twilight around an Avernian Gate as "keenly attuned to the energies of death." The Persephone's Return Ceremony, which creates or re-establishes an Avernian Gate, does so from the living world through symbolism associated with death, grief, and mourning, which are also the components to the less codified rituals used to open an existing Gate from the living side (explicitly doing so through the leveraging of things that accompany death if not the significant presence of actual death).

                Geist's explanation for how ghosts are sustained links grief and mourning to the sensation of loss experienced by the living with relation to the things whose destruction formed the ghost — "the instinctive expectation for something to be present while knowing that it won't be" — and describes Avernian Gates as standing in "every place associated with death: sites stained by murders or disease, quiet, lonely places, as well as every graveyard, morgue, crematorium, and even pet cemetery in the world​"; "anywhere that death has marked as its own" is another way it's worded.

                                                        I believe a Vampire under the Blush of Life would show up. Werewolves in human form would count, Mages would count, Promethean pre New Dawn wouldn’t count. Changeling is…hard. I’d argue no. Beasts would, Demons in Cover would, Bound would, Mummies probably get a weird result like Time spells. Deviant is worse than Changeling, but I’d argue no for most cases.

                                                        So I used for my tests two qualifications: Is it alive, and is it Human? I’m also giving a bit of leeway in either direction due to theme.

                                                        My case for Vampires is that they are fully alive under the Blush of Life last I checked. I think they would also count as a Human too, but that’s a point I can see people having a different perspective. I think it’s fitting that a Vampire can be human, if they want, but it’s temporary, comes a definite cost to those around them, and doesn’t truly counteract any of the downsides of being a Vampire. But I can definitely see room to quibble.

                                                        Werewolves are truly Human while in human form. I’d argue any other form wouldn’t register.

                                                        Mages are Human and Alive in general, so easy yes.

                                                        Prometheans aren’t human, and arguably not alive, so easy no.

                                                        Changeling are scarred by thorn and nightmare. They never come back from Arcadia unchanged. They are alive, but I think they wouldn’t be human to a Supernal understanding. So hesitant no.

                                                        Demons in Cover are Human as far as the Supernal is concerned. Unless they are in cover as a chair or a cat or a vampire or something, but Cover genuinely turns them into the thing they pose as.

                                                        Sin Eaters are both alive and Human, them also being a ghost and having a second ghost a soul doesn’t change that. So they registered.

                                                        Mummies are both alive and dead. As I understand, they are like if every moment of their Life and Death are compressed into one moment. So, yes, I think they count? Would also count as a corpse, if using Death.

                                                        Beasts are alive and Human. Easy yes.

                                                        Deviant’s whole thing is being changed to something other than human against your will, so for thematic purposes I’d say no…but the Remade have a tendency to not fit neatly into boxes. Depends on the Deviant, and the what the Storyteller is using them for narratively.
