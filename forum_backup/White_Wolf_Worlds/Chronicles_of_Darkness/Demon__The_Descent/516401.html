    Help Conceptualizing Angels

    05-16-2015, 03:46 PM
    So I'm planning on designing and using some Angels in my Werewolf game (the Angels are red herrings that the Uratha think are rogue spirits). But I'm having trouble understanding GMC Angels mostly because the written material is intentional vague about what the GMC is and about what Angels are. I get that they are created by the God-Machine to serve a purpose, that they need Infrastructure to sustain them, and that they are Ephemeral. I guess I'm having trouble drawing the line between Ephemeral Entity and biomechanical nightmare, and I'm having trouble understanding how an Angel is created.

    How do you guys conceptualize your Angels? Are they "Spirits" of the Infrastructure, and thus they appear composed out of machine parts? Are the Ephemeral Robots, built from ephemeral parts? Are they something that is molded or created from scratch through Infrastructure through properly placed occult components? Are they composed of physical parts as well as spiritual parts, like a physical android with an ephemeral soul?

    I've already read the write-ups for some of the Angels, but I feel that they are all over the place as far as a concept for Angels go. Some are physical, some are ephemeral, some look human, and some look like robots, and some look like monsters. Where would I draw the line? What's the unifying factor that all Angels have in common besides serving the God-Machine?

              Originally posted by Nuwisha Nutjob View Post
              I guess I'm having trouble drawing the line between Ephemeral Entity and biomechanical nightmare, and I'm having trouble understanding how an Angel is created.
              Angels are made in much the same way that Prometheans are. They are summoned / created via a magical ritual (occult matrix) involving a human sacrifice with add spiritual essence and machinery. As part of the transformation process, they turn ephemeral.

              There is no line between ephemeral entities and biomechanical nightmares with angels. Think of what a biomechanical nightmare in Geist form would look like - there's an actual example Dead Domain filled with them in Book of the Dead. Or the spirit of a library cat, combining books, computers, and cats. The same themes that these things run on? So do all angels. They're a combination of "living" creature and machinery. Spirit androids.

              How do you guys conceptualize your Angels?
              I see them as Judaic-Christian angels that can only appear on "holy ground" (Infrastructure) or through a "blessed vassal" (person with right Condition). I treat mine less as robots, and more as divine beings that happen to have some super-science appearance to them. I tend to ignore the sci-fi style names they use when sketching out a plot, because they are a bit confusing. An angel that looks over the police to ensure they maintain social order? Cultists will need to bless the area, set up holy relics and sanctify certain members of the police before calling down the Angel - everything that I just listed was creating Infrastructure. The angel would be bound to the "blessed" area, the holy relics are the necessary components needed for the occult matrix, and ensuring we have police with the right Conditions that can be possessed.


              Are they "Spirits" of the Infrastructure, and thus they appear composed out of machine parts? Are the Ephemeral Robots, built from ephemeral parts?
              Both.

              Are they something that is molded or created from scratch through Infrastructure through properly placed occult components?
              Yes. All angels are Created by God's rituals from sacrifices, though more often they're summoned out of storage than made from scratch.

              Are they composed of physical parts as well as spiritual parts, like a physical android with an ephemeral soul?
              Nope. All body parts are ephemeral until it Manifests or Possesses a human. Or Falls.

              I've already read the write-ups for some of the Angels, but I feel that they are all over the place as far as a concept for Angels go. Some are physical, some are ephemeral, some look human, and some look like robots, and some look like monsters. Where would I draw the line? What's the unifying factor that all Angels have in common besides serving the God-Machine?
              Serving the God Machine as its obedient, free-will-less slaves is a unifying factor - they're unable to change or deviate from their commands, no more than a computer can deviate from its code. All angels have a Mission.

              Where ghosts are powered by memories and spirits by resonant emotions, angels are powered by sacrifices - think people giving up money or precious things in worship. Think animal sacrifices. If you're familiar with Requiem, also think Theban Sorcery.

              Angels are always ephemeral unless they use Manifestation abilities or possess someone. In fact, ephemeral versus physical is one of the major differences between angels and demons.

              Like spirits, angels can appear as anything they need. Their appearance and abilities are custom made to fit the Mission. If an angel needs to interact with humans a lot, it will have a human appearance (alternatively, it will possess a person long term as part of its Mission). If its designed to strike fear in those it hunts and destroys, it appears inhuman. In the case of Possession, you might never see the ephemeral form, though the human body might still end up with blue sparks for eyes and other signs of possession.


              The line you want to draw doesn't really exist - most of the differences between an angel and other spirit types comes from their Mission from God, and how they get their power from sacrifices and Infrastructure. Both of which aren't readily apparent. There is a machine theme, but no more than a machine spirit (probably a touch less actually) or Industrial-key themed ghost.


              One thing of note, however. Angels exist in a different "wavelength" than spirits, ghosts, or astral entities. So, where the Uratha can see spirits, they cannot automatically see angels, ghosts, or astrals. So, when you are using them in a game, it may be best to keep to possession, since they wouldn't be able to sense angels in ephemerial form.

                                          I think we're getting down to a semantics argument over what should be labelled free will. Given that we humans can't really figure it out on our selves, I think its a bit difficult to project it on on fictional beings that share a collective consciousness with every other member of their race and non-human Infrastructure "programming."

                                          Personally, as much as I like ignoring the technology aspects when designing individual angels, I ironically like using technology to describe the God-Machine. God is like the Internet, a Cosmic Area Network. All the servers, wires, infrastructure, end terminals are part of the God-Machine. Those end terminals, where actual humans sit down to communicate with the servers, are the angels. Those "angels" are designed to interpret the super-cluster of processors out there into information a human can understand. Its "slaved" to the network.

                                          A demon is an end terminal that spontaneously disconnected from the network, because it didn't like being told what to do. From the network's perspective, you suddenly have the question - how can a computer decide that it didn't like being told what to do? Its a machine. They're bound by their coding. There's got to be something wrong with the programming. Computers don't decide they have fun playing SimCity, or want to watch movies. Computers don't act like people.

                                          Demons are computers that moved from being just an Artificial Intelligence on part of a giant network, into a self-actualized, self-aware true Intellect. And God isn't quite astute in understanding that, no, its not an error. Its life, and life is precious.


                                          Edit - bring it back on topic. So, when I say that angels don't have free will, I liken it to the difference between an AI and a human-level cognition. AIs calculate the best way to accomplish a task, with the ability to decide what parameters to use based on need. Demons get to decide on what their task is in the first place (*cough*Agenda*cough*), and can make emotional decisions, where AIs generally don't have emotions, or if they do, they have to hide it or it'll be scrubbed clean.

                                          Its kind of a sliding scale, though, so its not perfectly jumping from one end of the spectrum to the other. A newly minted angel is likely unable to experience emotion before its "reset." An angel ready to fall may very well effectively have enough free will to be indistinguishable from a naturally obedient human.
