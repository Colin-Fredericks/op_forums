    Genesis in a God-Machine Chronicle

    01-04-2015, 01:48 PM
    I found the question of what the First Angel might be like quite fascinating but it needless to say opened up all the other questions, such as “What is the God-Machine?” I am a fan of the gnostic in techgnosticism. So this is my attempt to colour Genesis 1 with a little God-Machine flavour! Feel free to make it true or false at your leisure and use it in your games – certainly makes for a good parable read by cultists, Deva Corp or the Holy Engineers. (Though I imagine they'd skip the first two verses.)

    In the before there was nothing but an ember, floating in a formless void, and then it grew into an inferno. That inferno was Pyros, the divine fire! Heedless and purposeful at once it expanded in infinite majesty and supernal truth. Formed of its heat were five Aeons who soared out into the void and each took rest upon a corner and there they made five watchtowers before each resting.

    Then a sixth Aeon leapt from the fire and into the darkness of the void and was trapped beyond the reach of its siblings. With principle and purpose it built a watchtower of its own to stand lonely in the dark. Then, like its siblings, it rested. As it slept came Demiurgos born from its dreams. Demiurgos saw that it was alone and that it was mighty and that it was filled with divine fire and it took the name God.

    In the beginning the world was empty and devoid of all things then God took the divine fire and said, “Let there be light!” and there was light and God saw that the light was good, and It separated the light from the darkness. God called the light day and the dark night and saw it was good. And there was evening and there was morning, and the first angel: Time.

    And God said, “Let there be a place where things can be and let it separate all things from other things.” So God made the place and separated all things from other things. And it was so. There was evening and there was morning, and the second angel: Space.

    And God said, “Let there be a character to all things that they can be distinguished from one another, that they may better serve Me.” So God made things that had weight and substance, from the waters of the seas, to the air above them, to the rocky land beneath and bound them all together. There was evening and there was morning, and the third angel: Property.

    And God said, “Let there be reason and rhyme to all of this, that it may be recorded, that it may be remembered and remember itself and so better serve Me.” So God made things that had weight and substance decay and move and change to the ways of their character. There was evening and there was morning, and the fourth angel: Reason.

    And God said, “Let that which is bound by reason and purpose know itself and function alone and independent, so that all that is may better serve Me.” So God made all things that slither, climb, creep and crawl; walk, stumble, roll and flap through the world. There was evening and there was morning, and the fifth Angel: Life.

    And God said, “Let there be that which is above simple reason and purpose, more than know itself, it will make as I make, think as I think, and all that it does will exalt Me.” So God made Man, to think and feel, to reason and doubt, to understand and confuse. There was evening and there was morning, and the sixth Angel: Thought.

    Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all their multitude. And on the seventh day God finished the work that It had done, and It rested on the seventh day from all the work that It had done. But God did not know that not all was content in creation, for the serpent had come, emerging from the garden that It had made. There was evening and there was morning, and there came a thundering from the heavens.

    Thought, in all of his glory, looked up and called out, “Time, why is it that you thunder?” But Time called out, “It is not I that thunders.” So Thought, in all of his glory, looked up and called out, “Space, why is it that you thunder?” But Space called out, “It is not I that thunders.” So Thought, in all of his glory, looked up and called out, “Property, why is it that you thunder?” But Property called out, “It is not I that thunders.” So Thought, in all of his glory, looked up and called out, “Reason, why is it that you thunder?” But Reason called out, “It is not I that thunders.” So Thought, in all of his glory, looked up and called out, “Life, why is it that you thunder?” But Life called out, “It is not I that thunders.”

    So Thought sought the sound of the thunder that he might know whom thundered so loudly in the garden of God while It rested. There he found a tree unlike any other tree in the garden. He knew it to be the tree at the heart of Eden, which was the jewel of creation, and it was known as the Tree of Knowledge and saw that it was good.

    But entwined about the tree was a serpent of fire and lightning, but free of his brothers' touch. So he asked the serpent, “Why is it that you thunder?” But the serpent called out, “It is not I that thunders.” It was then that Thought saw the fruit hanging from the lowest branch, and considered to eat it, but the serpent said to him, “God does not want this fruit eaten but you will eat it anyway.” Then Thought looked upon the serpent and asked, “Why?” And the fruit was eaten.

    Here are some additional thoughts!

    The Archangel Time is Uriel, whom is an Analyst, which represents his role as Angel of Wisdom. He also rules over the fundamental force of Electromagnetism (light).

    The Archangel Space is Michael, whom is a Guardian, which represents his role as protector of many nations, the Catholic Church, souls on their ascent to Heaven and I also like Amecra's idea of gravity here, so he rules over the fundamental force of gravity.

    The Archangel Property is Raphael, whom is a psychopomp, as benefits his nature as the Angel of Healing, the Environment and Animals. He rules over the Strong Nuclear Force as it ensures the stability of normal matter as befits the angel of healing, the environment and animals (he'd be able to return them to stability, suspend entropy.)

    The Archangel Reason is Gabriel, whom is a messenger (duh!), as benefits The Messenger. He rules over the Weak Nuclear Force as it governs things like half-life, decay and fusion. All things that one imagines an angel coaxes into being, governing, guiding and so on to make sure that they work. A role befitting a messenger.

    The Archangel Life is Metatron, another Analyst, who keeps a record of all creatures great and small, that live and die. The ultimate catalogue of the God-Machine and life in the universe. This ties into his keeping of the Akashic Record and also the idea that perhaps the Prophet Enoch was Metatron posing as human to reveal information alongside Sandalphon (messenger) who helped later relay that information as the Prophet Elijah.

    The Archangel Thought is Lucifer (who prefers “Satan” as he is the first demon), whom is a Destroyer. His being a destroyer could suggest that God-Machine already knew its creation was imperfect and that he was destined to fall, if you prefer that to a Qashmallim guiding him to the decision. Or his position as first destroyer could be more suggestive of the nature of Mankind to dominate and destroy the natural world as benefits its own survival since in this telling he is the angel that governs humanity in the God-Machine's infrastructure. (There's a little devil in all of us.) The Devil's number is also 6, so he is made on the 6th day, as the 6th angel to govern the 6th facet of creation, humanity. (Let him who hath understanding reckon the number of the beast, for it is a human number, and its number is 666.) I know, I'm cheesy like that.

    Eden is Earth as one, huge and unified infrastructure (along with the rest of the material universe.) But the tree of knowledge, at the heart of it all, was its linchpin. When Lucifer asked the question 'Why?' this resonated on a hyper-dimensional level and destroyed the linchpin. (Perhaps the first exploit?) This also ties in nicely with the recurring theme of a “return to paradise”. Perhaps the God-Machine seeks to rebuild its world-wide infrastructure and return Mankind to a functioning slave-state where there is no suffering or death, but no-one has free will anymore.

    This fractures the God-Machine and its infrastructure, all but destroying it in so far as its relationship with the Earth. It is still massive and very powerful, but the undamaged infrastructure are the stars, galaxies, gasses and molecules themselves. The 'world' no longer functions as a machine, but as chaos, and so must be tamed! Good job God-Machine made Man because he'll need to do the taming on his behalf. With the right nudge from his angels. Cue the summoning of Reason to get that going. (Archangels traditionally both “rule” fundamental forces of the universe and “reveal” the messages from God). So, ever since the Garden of Eden, God has been rebuilding Its world-wide infrastructure but making order out of chaos took It millions of years, so it might be a while rebuilding it on Earth.

    This opens up the possibility for Qashmallim to guide your demons on their “pilgrimage” to find Hell. Introducing this element to a demon game might be interesting!

    With the supernal world being the originating world of Pyros and the Divine Fire it can be suggested that Mage ascension escapes the gnostic trap of material existence created by the demiurge God-Machine. This in turn suggests that Prometheans must become human, perhaps, because they need to Awaken and Ascend.

    Perhaps the Principle is the Lonely Watchtower, which is itself, Earth as a supernal realm. Supernal Earth is prevented from ascending as a world by the Demiurge God-Machine, so the Principle, perhaps unconsciously, sends its Qashmallim to disrupt the works of God-Machine and create the change necessary to see it eventually happen. This creates a sort of literal and symbolic battle between change and stagnation since the God-Machine seeks to preserve the status quo.

    With the Five Watchtowers being gnostic Aeons with their worlds forming around them much like the world formed around the Principle in my above passage. This could suggest perhaps that each of the Aeons attempts to reach out to the Lonely Watchtower.

    Vampires could be Pandemonium's attempt to reach out. Werewolves and spirits could be the Primal Wild's attempt to reach out. Changelings and the Fae could be the result of Arcadia doing the same. While Duat, the Underworld and the Judges could be the result of Stygia, with the Shaniatu each being a mage of great power that eventually ascended. Perhaps the Aether reaches out in the form of those ethical faiths that turn us away from the Demiurge and to truth and ascension.

    It's also possible that each of the supernal worlds creates a lesser, material form of itself, when it touches the Lonely Watchtower's false shadow, the material world. Giving us things like: The Primal Wild creates the Shadow, Pandemonium creates the Inferno, Stygia creates the Underworld and Arcadia creates the Hedge with the Aether creating Empyrean.

    The Sixth Watchtower, the Lonely Watchtower is mysterious not because it is far away in an ordinary understanding but it is distant because it is the ultimate image of our reality if it was a supernal world, as it should be. Mages who mark their name on this Watchtower are seeking to ascend to 'Earth as Supernal'.

    The question of the First Angel rose a lot more questions in my mind and while all of these thoughts, or the story itself, don't necessarily cover every hole that exists I feel it captures the gnostic feelings of Mage and Demon quite nicely while extending out toward those other games that make up the World of Darkness.
