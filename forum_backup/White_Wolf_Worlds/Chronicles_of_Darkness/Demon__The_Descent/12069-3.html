    4) Revolution Roulette by Poets of the Fall

    For all the lack of shock and awe that I, for some reason, can apply my favorite band to a new favorite game, it can't be ignored how iconic Revolution Roulette is of Demon. The entire song is about the God Machine, a highlight of why a demon would fall and how they would live under it and fight it. The best part is that this song isn't just about blatant defiance or hope-in fact neither come up. Rebellion, after all, is a gamble. When you Fall, you have no promise anything will work out-that's part of the reason why Integrators are sympathetic in this game, because they may be playing the safest game, and they're still probably screwed. You may be motivated to move and act, even just trying to live a normal life, but nothing about it really promises yields. If much of the rest of this playlist is about the ideals a demon might hold, this one still plays the game but is honest. The sounds are both dreary and powerful, with a strong beat to get the heart pumping and yet illustrate that you're in the heart of the enemy. This is the oppression of the Machine, the proof that there's no purgatory, no paradise, just the gamble.

    Noteworthy Mentions: Dreaming Wide Awake, War, Delicious, Where Do We Draw The Line, You Know My Name(original by Chris Cornell)

                    Because I'm not yet done and I have restored my faith in writing these, Let's get into the top three.

                    3)Darkangel by VnV Nation

                    This song is almost elementally Demon in ways few other songs are. The techno is powerful and steadily defiant with its' tempo. In fact, let's hone in on that word: Defiant. That's what this song is. The words aren't angry in delivery, but they are brimming with rebellion and disdain. This is the song that says "No More" for demons, the one that says something that needs to changes. It's a natural Saboteur song, but really it so captures every element of the Fall and the Descent in how it captures the need for the system to change that even Integrators can sympathize, even if it's only their defiance demands they be allowed to come back in. This song just describes Demon to a t, covering their entire lifespan and general motives. THe most powerful point, though, is how it comes back to people. The chorus is a testament to the person the singer is speaking to, the entire song a vow and explanation of their alien nature and the danger of their lives. This is the life of a Demon under the observation of a person(not from their perspective, just their observation). It's just-oh, man, how does this song not come with the game? With song playing and book in hand, Darkangel will take you to the darkened skies once more, and ever onward.

                    Notable Mentions: Control, Rubicon, Beloved, and even if it's a Changeling song really, Illusion

                        2)Annie, Would I Lie To You by Iris

                        Nope, I take it back-THIS SONG is Demon, elementally. Or rather, this is the opposite number. If Darkangel is the quintessential song for fighting the God Machine, then Annie, Would I Lie To You is the quintessential song for dealing with humanity, in its' most heartbreaking form. Demons are damn good liars, tricksters, and schemers-but they can't wholly escape the questions that will arise if they do anything outside of maintaining their cover, and even then. They need their covers to stay alive, but there's no denying just how their lies will hurt other people. From simply taking jobs or hair colors, small things that never the less define the person in the moment to so many to actually wearing their face, a Demon living is nothing but velvet pain for others. How do you justify the changes? How do you excuse the way you slip off? How do you keep people from seeing the worst? You never really can-all you can do is lie to them and pray you can get them to trust you. The simplest way to go about this song is to listen to it twice. First time around, imagine yourself as the demon,and the circumstances that would provoke you to say these things-hell, some of it can be because of the person you're telling it to! Second time around, just be Annie, and see how you'd feel if you were told to this. Imagine what it would look like to you. This is Demon, one part cold war, one part human drama. It hurts so much, both you and them, but if you want any kind of a happy ending, at some point you're going to have to ask people the horrible question "[Annie], Would I Lie To You?".

                        Noteworthy Mentions: Endless, Twilight, Saving Time

                                        I think I'd do a fairly broad range of music. Grunge is almost mandatory, but you have to go with the right style. I think Alice in Chains was the best group to come out of the Seattle grunge scene by a large margin, and a lot of their tunes could work. In addition to being one of my favorite songs ever, the beat and grind of Man in the Box and its weirdness works for Demon. Also recommend Heaven Beside You, and Would is especially good.

                                        Also, I'd mix in heavy alternating doses of industrial (I get the feeling Slipknot would feature in there a lot, too), maybe some particularly haunting techno (dance floor stuff won't work unless you're in that kind of scene), and some stuff like Def Tones, maybe some Rage, Static X, Mudvayne, etc., as long as it works. I'm not sure if Waterproof Blonde's other stuff is any good or not, which I should really find out, but I really like Just Close Your Eyes by that group.
