    Clarifying uses for Embeded Gadgets

    07-02-2014, 09:58 AM
    I am still not entirely sure why Demons would create vanilla Embeded gadgets, considering the hefty cost in loss of Willpower dots. Exploit gadgets, I get - no compromise rolls is a significant feature. The new-fangled types - Lambdas, Near-fields, Overclocked, Form - again there are obvious advantages. But simple vanilla Embed gadgets from the core book - not so much.

    The alleged advantages for gadgets are:
    1. No compromise rolls. But most embeds don't cause compromise rolls anyway
    2. Blending in. But, again, most embeds are pretty subtle to warrant the expenditure of time and willpower dots to offer any kind of justifiable advantage
    3. Reliability. But, unless the trigger is specifically chosen to not involve any rolls, activating a Gadget still requires a roll.
    4. Use in pacts and for general bargaining. But a pact is able to manifest whatever the pactee asks out of thin air. Why would a demon expend valuable resources and willpower when Primum can do it much more easily? Yes, there are limits to what a pact can grant but still I am struggling to imagine a mortal agonizing over "I could give away all my illnesses, abusive relationships etc ... OR I can have a lighter that creates a loud bang"...
    5. Equipping your cultists.

    So it seems to me that Embed gadgets are only useful if:
    1. They have a non-roll trigger
    2. As a roll-converter of some sort (i.e. If you'd rather roll Dexterity+Academics on a book instead of Manipulation+Expression on Trivia, for example)
    3. As equipment for your cult
    4. Trading between demons for Embeds you don't know

    Still useful, I suppose, but it seems to me more limited than the core book implies. Is that a fair assessment or am I missing obvious applications?

      Firstly, that assessment as it turns out depends highly on the embed in question.

      Other advantages include: The ability to create an effect that is not normally the purview of the Embed itself, but is close to it. Example: A silk shirt embedded with Just Bruised. It still looks like a silk shirt, but it might provide ballistic armour or defense when worn.

      Embedded Gadgets do not require the demon to recharge them like Exploited gadgets do. Once you've expended the aether, you've got your new toy.

      An Embedded Gadget might be able to activate itself under certain condition, such as: "Wearer is shot." Meaning that those nasty surprise rounds aren't such a problem anymore when your Just Bruised shirt has your back.

      Embedded Gadgets could be used to set up traps in your home to protect you from God-Machine interference such as sneaky cultists, police or angels. Example: The demon installs Combustion into the window-frames or doors of the sensitive areas of their home so that anyone whom climbs into them causes a house-fire. The demon then knows that there is a good chance that any evidence of their demonic self has been destroyed and they can then seek refuge in a bug-out cover until things blow over.

      Embedded Gadgets could be used to set up defenses in your home, rather than just traps. Example: (I can't take the credit for this one!) The demon installs Tools into Toys in the locks surrounding a sensitive area making them nigh-impossible for mundane characters to pick.

But should they be able to just flash a badge and the person forgets them because they've made that gadget? I don't think so.
                                                      Just to clarify what I mean by "purview".. The flavour of the embed is that it is an underlying law of the universe. The write-up of an Embed includes a brief summary of what that "law" is and gives the game-rules implementation for a typical use. Necessarily, the rulebook cannot attempt to cover every consequences of that "law" in the short and concise write up. My understanding of the gadget restriction on using only a part of the Embed's function is that it refers to the "fluff", not the rules text. According to that interpretation, the ID badge is not within the purview of the typical offensive rules-write up given in the book but it is, in my opinion, within the purview of the "fluff". Unperson write up states "a demon with the right expertise can force a person into a kind of “identity blackout” for a short time, making her unrecognizable and unable to identify herself." The rules for gadgets permit only a partial functionality to be installed. Thus the ID badge installs the "making her unrecognizable" part but not the "unable to identify herself" part.

                                                      By that reading, I would indeed consider allowing such a use of Unperson once the near-field gadget is created. Now, yes, of course - there is a risk of the slippery slope scenario here. you can certainly keep pushing the bounds of the Embed effect too much. But I would say there are three safe-guards for that: 1) the spirit of what the Embed represents, 2) the consent of the story-teller to create a near-field effect and 3) the fact that, as you say, once the effect is so far removed from the original Embed as to be a new Embed the ST can require 2 XP.


                                                      Originally posted by StSword
                                                      As for allowing one to essentially learn a new free embed or exploit by making a near field effect, by that logic all demons ought to be able to overclock their powers because gadgets can do that. By that logic making a lambda gadget would allow a demon to combine their powers.
                                                      I don't immediately see a problem with that. Overclocking an Embed is essentially how a demon discovers Exploits. That's what Exploits (by flavour) are: Embeds that are forced with Aether. And I don't see any obvious restrictions to why a demon can't combine Embed effects - I have seen a number of such suggestions on these very forums.

                                                      By that logic demons can change their dice pools on embeds and exploits because demons can do that when they make gadgets.
                                                      This I think is different. The roll used to activate a gadget has nothing to do with the Embed, it's a property of the hardware. Thus it tells the demon nothing new about the laws of the universe; it wouldn't warrant any change to how the demon knows to use the underlying Embed.

                                                      You are free to do whatever you like in your own games, but that sounds like a big can of worms to me.
                                                      Ultimately, I am willing to concede that I am overthinking and overinterpreting the rules. And certainly I can see problems with my interpretation. But I still don't see how else to reconcile the proposal that gadgets can go beyond what an Embed can do with the explicit requirement that it represents "only a portion of the Embed's potential".
