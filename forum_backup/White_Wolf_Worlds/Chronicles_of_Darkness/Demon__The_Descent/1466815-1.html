                            A few ideas I've had about the next edition.

                            1. Powers of the Occult Matrix- give a little more life to infrastructure. Create mechanics for certain Outputs used by various infrastructures. Give Storytellers a place to start in creating their own. As a bonus, this could double as rules for effects Demons or humans could create if they try to build their own occult matrixes. You could expand the system for glitches to include the nasty side effects a juryrigged matrix crafted by a non-divine intelligence will surely have on reality.

                            2. Mechanists- the God-Machine Chronicles briefly alludes to a group of mortals that steal pieces of the god machine and tinker with them. Expand on this. Maybe certain humans have been hacking the god machine for years, writing elaborate strings of divine source code easily mistaken for magical circles and seals popular in the Renaissance. Use the suggested rules above for making occult matrixes to showcase their abilities. Perhaps some of these hackers have "spells" that can induce a kind of Fall in an Angel but with the baked in component that the resultant demon must obey the mechanist instead- this game's version of a demon summoner. Freeing these captured demons without alerting the god-machine might be an interesting story.

                            3. Inferno- Demon the Descent has been oddly shy about talking about the "other" demons in the chronicles of darkness. I feel like the Inferno from the first edition sourcebook of the same name would make a good added element to the game, a secondary antagonist independent of the god-machine. Perhaps the Inferno is the end result of the Satan Signal that has occasionally been mentioned in the game, a cosmic computer virus that 'infects' reality with a whisper that spreads sin and corruption that slowly grows into an increasingly powerful entity. Perhaps these are the Second Children the Pain Prophet mentions in the original story about the god-machine. Perhaps the "demons" of Inferno are the remnants of human psyches that still linger in the cosmic "recycling bin" of the universe's desktop after the god-machine grinds up their bodies and souls to create essence for itself. I especially like that last one, since it makes a third dangerous elements out of the humans that Demons and the Machine both use and manipulate in their cold war.

                            4. Splintered realities- As a general rule I think the more alternate histories and splintered timelines are used in the game the better.

                            5. The Furies- I really, really want more of the Pain Prophecy to play a role in the overall setting, including more exploration about what the Furies are and how they relate to demons.

                            6. Society- Yeah, the old vampire games left me biased in this regard. I prefer my monstrous entities to have well developed secretive societies surrounding them. The Agendas and Agencies are criminally underdeveloped in the current setting. Angels have been falling since (presumably) the dawn of time. They've had a long time to develop factions, traditions and rivalries. If the Agendas are essentially ideologies in pursuit of Hell then it would make sense that each would have sub-factions pursuing more concrete plans in keeping with that philosophy. In this I'd also like to see the concepts of espionage and...well, being a demon explored in more depth. The first one is straight forward- we are told that espionage culture is a inspiration for the game but aren't given much exploration ABOUT espionage culture or how that is molded into demonic society. How do the Agendas recruit? Who serve as the handlers for a new ring? Who are the leaders? Who are the cutouts? What information is each Agenda looking for, what do they do with it, etc. I'd like to see goals for each Agenda outside of their relationship to the god-machine, for instance. Give us more causes to fight for and plots to foil between the Agendas. Multiply the intrigues. What does a demonic safe house look like? What do you call neutral ground in the world of the Descent? Are Demons who have unlocked their Ciphers given any special treatment in their society? Are there any known demons who have achieved hell? Do they have Demons who are trying to emulate them? Are they a threat?

                            7. Personally, I'd like Demon the Descent to feel more...demonic. A personal opinion, of course, but the setting just never scratched that itch for a creature of sin and malice risen from the depths of hell vibe I was expecting. At least not enough for my taste. I'd like to see hints of the kind of ruthlessness and desperation in the Agendas and Agencies that would have inspired the human understanding of what makes a demon a demon and hell hell, if you follow me. Maybe Saboteurs have secret site devoted to interrogation and torture that inspired the notion that hell is a place of agony and suffering. Maybe mortals tempted into acts of depravity and sin are actually being coaxed away from the plot the god machine has crafted for them. Maybe Aether leaves the stench of brimstone behind when it is used. There are a thousand little ways to explore the idea and a wide range of cultures to draw upon for inspiration. Just...keep descending into that darkness.

                                    Setting aside Agencies for the moment,* it'd be good to get a little more detail on squads and associations, since it builds out some of the usual channels of communication between the Unchained and "cross-ring group of demons with a shared Agenda" has the same kind of potential for fun clashing-loyalties/distrustful heist-crew issues as lodges provide for packs in Werewolf 2e.

                                            “3. I think the Inferno being expanded through Demon the Descent is a bad idea. It would dilute the focus on what "demons" are for the gameline and possibly bring up and old argument about Descent demons not really being demons despite all the parallels they share. Not only that, but Inferno demons should stay as separate from the machine. The Inferno sourcebook was amazing, and provides a more classical take on demons. To tie them into the God machine or Descent in any way would be a disservice.”
                                            Shrug. I suppose there is that danger of “dilution”, a problem that is risked when trying to incorporate any established concept into another established one. But I think there are ways to avoid that problem in the hands of a skilled writer. I’ll cite the incorporation of Immortals into Mummy the Curse, first in dark eras and later into the proper release of Mummy’s second edition. One way that I suggested earlier is to let Demon: The Descent be the ‘fallen angels’ of demonic lore and emphasize Inferno demons as ‘damned mortal souls’, focusing primarily on whispers, possessed, and Inferno corrupted spirits and ghosts (familiar but twisted concepts established already in the Chronicles and Descent alike) and leave the more powerful entities discussed in Inferno as shadowed rumors (which, to be fair, is not far off from how they are treated in the Inferno book in the first place). Shrug. First edition has largely treated Inferno like the elephant in the room, going to (in my opinion, of course) absurd lengths in the Storyteller’s Guide to even mention groups like Belial’s Brood, Mastigos, the Lucifuge, etc. I don’t feel like mentioning the Qashmallim in the Core book ‘diluted’ the concept of God-Machine controlled angels, and I don’t think talking about Inferno would do that here.

                                            “6. This clashes with the themes of Demon.”
                                            I could not disagree with this statement more vehemently.
                                            “It's an espionage game filled with backstabbing and liars.”
                                            Yes, it is. And I do not believe this fact in any way makes a more well developed demonic society an impossibility. In fact, I’d argue the opposite. More on that in a moment.
                                            “And demons aren't the establishment. They are runaways out for their own hides. They don't have any good reason to centralize to such a degree, not to mention it would be a nightmare logistically.”
                                            You don’t need to be the ‘establishment’ to have organization, traditions, or a society. Some of the most tightly knit communities on Earth come from immigrants and refuges that found themselves in a strange land and had no one but one another to rely upon. And no matter how cautious or paranoid a single person might be, sooner or later somebody is gonna do the math and realize that you will have more success working with people and watching each other’s backs than you will have going it alone when you are being hunted or working clandestinely. The best spy organizations on Earth by necessity have well developed hierarchies, well established protocols for engagement and conducting missions, carefully maintained channels for information and resources, and so on. I would strongly argue that organization is an absolute necessity for long term survival against something like the god machine. Also, I’d like to think that hyper advanced robotic ultra dimensional computer programs, cut off from the main network or not, can handle a higher degree of logistics than us poor dumb humans.
                                            “The furthest it goes is Agencies (which should be expanded on admittedly), which are ultimately made to benefit individual rings who have SOME trust.”
                                            I don’t even think that statement is true per what we see in first edition. Agencies, from my reading, are left frustratingly ambiguous in size and scope, but at bare minimum most certainly are providing services to and making use of more than a single Ring.
                                            “in short, having them come together in societies not only makes little sense to lore, but undercuts huge thematics of the game.”
                                            And I once again think the exact opposite. A game dealing with espionage and spy culture is, to me, inherently implying a carefully maintained web of secret organizations, working together or competing with each other. We don’t need an elaborate feudal system like Vampire, or the pomp and circumstance of a Changeling Freehold, mind you. I’d imagine Demons are even more focused on layers within layers of secrecy than your average vampire, in fact. I’d propose taking the basic structure of a spy network and reimagining in it in more supernatural terms. A Ring of Demons implies having a handler that feeds them information, resources, and safety in exchange for going on missions. A handler implies a network that demon reports to, which implies a hierarchy with some unknown(s) at the top organizing the overall goals of the Agenda or Agency- a spy master or director. A society composed of face dancer technomonsters implies a need for code words, secret handshakes, and other elaborate systems of identification so that they know you are you no matter who you seem to be on any given day. I see a lot of potential in that alone and I’m surprised it wasn’t explored in more detail in first edition.

The God-Machine Chronicles and Demon both establish that the body of this Fury or angel or whatever was recovered during the moon landing and is now housed by Deva Corporation. Since we’ve already gone that deep into the meta plot we might as well go all in and explore what these Furies really are within the current framework of Descent.

                                                            Originally posted by Ur-Than View Post

Which frankly would be more a change of perspective rather than a tweak because I feel that's already a necessity of their Agenda.
                                                            The corebook writeup describes the Agenda as splitting into roughly three factions based on its differences in opinion on what the final goal of being an Integrator is. The largest faction wants to reprogram the Machine or rejoin the system with their newfound humanity intact — the latter method is not "hoping it'll change the Machine" as "aiming to become an internal reformist," while the former allows for changing the Machine before they rejoin it as an angel. The other two factions are aiming to either get the best of both worlds re: loyalism and the Fall or complete whatever undercover mission they believe they were sent into deep-deep cover for.

                                                            The unifying feature of the Agenda is that Integrators all want to integrate themselves back into the Machine (and thus pick up the skills and development priorities of the Agenda Condition). That's not significantly more dangerous than Saboteurs drawing attention by messing with Infrastructure that can spark disasters or Tempters putting their Agency's lower rungs in harm's way for a goal they don't entirely believe is possible. The Idealists are arguably the second most organized Agenda of the main four, and the highlighted need for most of them to do something more involved than turning themselves in to the nearest recycling station serves the same general function as Inquisitors' recognition that they can't work alone precisely because anything could be a trap — Angel Empathy's Beat Condition may not be a diegetic phenomenon, but the fact remains that Integrators grow faster when they put their fellows at risk and make themselves suspect and specifically in ways that give them opportunities to advance their Cipher.

Cypher and Pacts need a simplification, because I still haven't finished designing the Interlocks for any players and Pacts are a pain in the bip to play with. Incidentely, I also feels that the interaction between Pacts and Covers should be made a little simpler/smoother, but that may just be because my players are just starting to dabble in all of it so we're rought around the edges.
                                                            On the one hand, I can definitely agree with this, but on the other hand I feel that Changeling 2e's more freeform system for pledgecraft is not substantially preferable to 1e's extensive balancing act; the former is definitely simpler, but it does not make arbitration easier.

                                                            The bigger problem currently facing demonic pactwork is that the full spread of systems are currently spread across about half a dozen books (including multiple Dark Eras) and also plug into the systems for gadgets (and the gadget mechanics are an iffy mix of well-defined and fiat-based), which itself runs into the problem of pacts currently being notionally a mysterious ability separate from the God-Machine's installed shortcuts and systems, and being the first Revised Storytelling System game out the door is all I can readily attribute several early Embeds and Exploits' comparative lack of specific mechanical effects to.

                                                            Obviously something like Promethean 2e's Pilgrimage charting doesn't work for Demon without changing one of the assumptions of the game, because not every demon pursues the Cipher specifically because it's a loose thread to chase rather than a deliberately constructed goal for themselves, but like "demons and angels use mostly the same rules instead of the latter being mechanically Ephemeral Beings But Not Really," an overhaul of the splat's mechanical interface to better mess with "things the God-Machine has made true about the world, and their unintended uses" would go a long way.

                                                            The game already incentivizes demons whose main goal is more involved than staying in Cover to pick up a bunch of Embeds both as potential Keys and as routes to developing Exploits, and the sheer breadth of things Exploits can do (including several things that would be equivalent to template features on other splats) makes me inclined to want the Cipher to be part of something stylistically similar to the Sphere Grid from Final Fantasy X or a simplified version of Deviant's conspiracy/Web of Pain mechanics; you can study and/or subvert pieces of Infrastructure to gain knowledge of new tricks that can then serve as the basis for new gadgets or abilities, which in turn can make certain pact benefits less troublesome to provide, which can then make it easier to avoid or mitigate damage to your Cover.

                                                            Given that Deviants run on a somewhat reduced baseline template to account for the granularity of Variation and Scar Magnitude and the inclusion of the conspiracy mechanics, it's possible this might necessitate Cover change to work more like the Resilient Cover hack from the Storyteller's Guide, if not outright replace what the splat's Integrity analogue is like Promethean did, but the core of what I'm saying here, I guess, is that a Demon 2e would benefit greatly from a full ludo-structural overhaul of its powerset to make Demon Powers, including pacts and the Cipher, run in a more unified framework.
