    My number one tip is: Talk to your players about Guilty, Shaken and Spooked. These conditions are the most commonly applied as they occur when a demon succeeds on a compromise check. One of my players had a lot of trouble grasping the nature of these three conditions, playing an anarchistic Messenger Saboteur/Tempter. She felt none of these should apply to her demon. While explanations were given for each, she found them dissatisfying. Thankfully, the book does suggest making new conditions, which I did. I even found a few here on a post about custom conditions, some of which were very interesting. It's a great chance to get a bit creative. Conditions that have more to do with the compromise making the Demon's cover metaphysically uncomfortable, for example, or that detach him from it on some multidimensional level, are all excellent bases to launch from on creating Demon-specific conditions. You shouldn't have to do this too much with failure conditions, though, as these already have plenty of Demon specific options.

                Glitches are cool, but Conditions give Beats, so if you have a player hesitant to take Conditions (if you're giving the option), make it clear that that's the advantage over Glitches.

                As for Compromise, I think each of my players had about 3 or 4 checks a game, and only one of them ever lost a dot of Cover. It was a short game, and that success rate partially had to do with form ability bonuses, but if you have even a moderate Manip + Wits, and a decent Cover rating, it's not as risky as it seems. At least in my experience.

                        I almost feel that providing advice to the great Baron should call for a Humanity check.

                        While my advice may not be the most earth shattering, what I have found since I started acting as GM for my Demon chronicle is to give the players options. I actually grew annoyed at my players when the plot I had put together was actively ignored in favor of my players acting like Celebrant Sin-Eaters. They decided that having escaped the God Machine, they were going to live life how they wanted, and on their own terms, most of which consisted of making covers for themselves to enjoy mundane activities. I actually ran an entire game session where my players took covers for a pizza place including running deliveries, making money, and then going to a Baseball game with their profits.

                        Did Hunter Angels come in and try to destroy them all? Nope.
                        Did the God Machine suddenly create new infrastructure to tempt the players into action? Nope.
                        Did the players have a good time? Absolutely

                        As a result, the focus of my chronicle has shifted drastically and now it plays out like a screening of Rush Hour with Jackie Chan and Chris Tucker

                            The conditions I added especially for demons for my chronicle, as per Ramza's request. They were heavily modified from some conditions I saw in a thread here months ago. So all credit goes to this forum and it's wonderful posters!

                            ARC (Address Resolution Conflict)
                            A demon is his cover while simultaneously being all of his other covers and his true demonic self. Though he has resisted his cover's degradation the Unchained has lost core metaphysical connection with it. All checks made against the demon's Cover are made as if the cover value was one less.
                            Resolution: Your character is forced to reconnect to his cover by force in a moment of panic, usually after his vulnerability is exploited, and chooses to gain a minor glitch.

                            Lag
                            Though their cover allows them to disguise themselves as human beings, the Unchained are no such thing. With vast memories and higher perceptions, they must receive and process vast quantities of information rapidly all while maintaining the guise of humanity. The compromise has damaged the efficiency in which crucial information is understood by the demon, creating latency in his ability to process information and dismiss irrelevancies. The Unchained suffers from a -2 penalty on all perception checks.
                            Resolution: Your character realises the delay in his own perceptive qualities and seeks to repair it, usually after a jarring event, represented by choosing to fail a perception check.
                            Note: Lag could also affect investigation, occult or extended actions in the same way as perception. The player may take a variation that impacts the relevant skill. They must fail the same skill to resolve the condition.

                                            Originally posted by Baron Samedi View Post
                                            Re: Glitches and beats. My thoughts, totally with 0 experiance backing it up, would be you will get a beat from resolving a condition...eventually. However, Glitches you MAY gain a beat, or you may not, depending on if it impedes you somehow, so conditions are more a sure thing for beats, but also more a sure thing for at least minor trouble.
                                            Glitches don't provide beats at all by RAW, same as, say, a frailty in CtL 1e doesn't function like a Flaw. My group found that the compromise rolls were making things a little too harsh for our liking, so we chose to scale them back with a simple house rule: fail a compromise check, and choose one of losing a Cover dot, taking a compromise Condition, or taking a glitch. Cover quality can be used as a sort of ablative defense that's harder to recover in the long term without putting effort into collecting pacts, glitches are relatively minor in effect but can stack up and subject you to further compromises, and are most notably a lot of work to get rid of, and compromise Conditions are brief and harrowing, but more easily and quickly shed if you can stand the storm.

                                            Originally posted by Baron Samedi View Post
                                            So one of my Demons has Essence Drain. I am puzzling how to handle that in game re: other supernaturals. below is my tentative system, but I am open to input, especially from how people are currently handling it:
                                            Aether is a precious resource in Demon, much more precious than most other supernaturals' fuel points, and partial transformation can let you Essence Drain without a huge risk of compromise. If you have a lot of crossover involving supernatural friends or allies, especially if there's a PC of another splat hanging with the ring, this is worth considering first, from a design standpoint. If said allies are inclined to drive a harder bargain, having to strike deals for large gifts of converted Aether without having to adopt full demonic form or snoop around Infrastructure is good because it makes more plot than it removes; similarly, hunting supernatural enemies for Aether food seems fine because it creates plot. If there are supernatural allies who are better served by being generous to the Unchained and cooperating freely, though, you may want to make Essence Drain less easily compatible with the process.

                                            Second consideration: demonic forms are fun because they're customizable. Forget about how Essence Drain is written in the book: what form does your PC's Essence Drain take? Design its effects on other supernaturals, and compatibility or incompatibility, based on that.

                                            After considering all that, if you still want some interesting conclusions to draw, here are my thoughts off the top of my head.

                                            Vitae: Could go either way. I think I'd probably let the Unchained draw the Vitae out without drinking it; demons are not vampires, and the power is not the blood, it's in the blood. Since it's in the blood, though, the process probably requires opening wounds.
                                            Essence: Should work fine, if crossover design concerns above don't get in the way.
                                            Mana: Mana strikes me as the petrodiesel to Aether's raw crude oil, if that makes any sense; Mana is the Essence of the world in its most sublimed form, while Aether is Essence's runoff. Thus, using Essence Drain to catalyze Mana into Aether seems like it would be an inefficient process, and possibly even slightly dangerous if you want to play with minor anomalies, Mage-style.
                                            Pyros: Huge narrative crossover potential. I'd allow it because closing it off seems to get in the way of lots of interesting possibilities for how qashmallim and the Divine Fire relate to the God-Machine. Side effects probably depend on your inclination on which possibilities you prefer, there.
                                            Glamour: No special concerns or ideas I see here. Might as well allow it, again, unless crossover design concerns get in the way.
                                            Plasm: Especially plentiful compared to Aether, I would think, though I'm obviously not the expert here. I like the idea of it leaving a mark on the demon's demonic form until burned out, similarly to the Ephemeral Cover power; it might be colored by the geist of the Sin-Eater whose plasm has been drained. Since plasm is a sort of occult bodily humour, like vitae, it might require opening wounds or have to be drawn out by provoking the use of Manifestations.
                                            Pillars: One of the fuel points that more closely approach how precious Aether is to demons. There's definitely something to be done here by relating the mummy's ability to harness the force of the different aspects of the soul, and the demon's ability to dismantle the soul to construct identities and draw power from the reaction. If all else fails, catalyzing the reaction might produce an especially resonant and abundant reward of Aether and possibly even temporarily wound that part of the Deathless's soul. Have fun making enemies of the Arisen and likening yourself to their age-old Shuankhsen enemies!

                                            Originally posted by Baron Samedi View Post
                                            Also, how many covers are common for PCs in your games?
                                            Our game hasn't progressed far enough to get past Primum 2, but everybody still has only one Cover currently, though we've used a couple facades (weak, brief zero-dot sorts of Cover that can be borrowed via pacts, they're in Flowers of Hell). We're big on character interaction and zoomed-in characterization moments, so it would be overwhelming for us to deal with multiple Covers per character, so if you have a group like mine, I'd expect characters to generally have one primary Cover and maybe one or two auxiliary lives used primarily as tools and utilities. Your mileage may vary, however! Demon is flexible about that.

                                                        That entirely depends on how hivemind-ish you want to present the God-Machine. One interpretation would be to have the G-M know all things that happen to all angels and directly coordinate their individual action. In this case revealing the Demon's identity to an Angel would certainly be a huge compromise. However, that presents the G-M as more "a super intelligent human" than I feel appropriate.

                                                        What I prefer, and find better fits the setting, is that while the God-Machine acts globally, Angels act individually and often without context. So a demon interacting with an individual Angel will not automatically mark them as screwed in the God-Machine's eyes. Indeed the presentation of the God-Machine as something that makes such decisions should, probably, be downplayed. It's an unknowable alien engine after all, humanizing it reduces the horror. There may even be Angels whose entire job it is to act as intermediaries for Integrators.

                                                        (It's my personal cannon that the God-Machine is bad at dealing with things where it doesn't have established infrastructure, and thus employs Demons through Angelic intermediaries to solve such problems.)
