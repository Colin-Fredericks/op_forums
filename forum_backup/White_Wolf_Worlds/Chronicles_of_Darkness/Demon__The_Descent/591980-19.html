                                    Hey, I wanted to say, I appreciate all the support coming from you. I wouldn't say my opinion was "negated" so much as that Arcee's and my views simply clash. To put it simply, Mage isn't really my game- in fact I would go so far as to say it is the game I have the least interest in playing out of anything in the NWOD. So drawing a distinction between Mage Technomancy and God-Machine Infrastructure is...well, its one of those exercises I'm just gonna smile and nod at. To me, a giant castle rolling down the street hiding an intricate and monstrous engine will always be God fodder. Simple as that.

                                    (SPOILERS: Although, if we wanna talk about Utena as a whole, I could totally see the entire Academy being a piece of Infrastructure cut off from the outside world by containment Infrastructure of dream like illusions and allegory, wherein the duels are a part of the occult matrix to bring about "Revolution", although no one involved in the Project quite knows what that output is meant to be. Akio/Dios would be the angel in charge of the whole thing, and Anthy would by the end of the series be a Demon who Fell as a result of watching a human sacrifice themselves for her liberation. Everyone else in the series I would call Stigmata, with the occasional ghost thrown in.)

                                    But onto the book as a whole. I sadly find my opinion fairly mixed here. The New Maps of Hell chapter I found less inspiring than I had hoped- I frankly got milleage out of the equivalent discussion of "Hell as Philosophy" from the Player's Guide. On the other hand, the discussion on the Cipher was all pretty solid and helpful. Sections on Angels wasn't bad either, although I continue to be confused in exactly- it seems like one book will say they are almost human and experience feelings of betrayal or pity towards their Demon counterparts and in another they are intelligent but emotionless robots aligned with the God-Machine. Sure, some of that will vary between Angels (and STs) but I was looking for more discussion on that angle. What we do get is good, of course. The concept of slivers is fairly interesting as well. But the best section of the chapter for me was the other servants of the GM- Echoes are especially interesting. I not often a fan of Storytelling advice, but the chapter on Espionage games was all good.
                                    Urban Legends ranged from mediocre to excellent, leading me to believe its a hodge podge of several writers. Like many people I hoped that the Satan Signal would be something more interesting than it was. I have to say, the fear that Descent would be too much like Fallen with the addition of a "Luciferesque" figure seems...silly. Your game involves a bunch of former programs rebelling against a computer in control of reality. You have already fully succeeded in making your game strikingly different from its previous incarnation. You CAN include more demonic elements in your game as long as you give them a twist- Satan as computer virus would have been fine. This mindset played especially true in the latter section of the chapter- crossovers. First off, to get it out of the way, I don't have any criticism about what was discussed- all pretty spot on and interesting tidbits for the future to boot. What struck me is what wasn't included, the proverbial elephant in the room. No Belial's Brood, no Maeljin or Bale Hounds, the Pentacle gets glossed over without mention that two of their Watchtowers occupy an apparent heaven and hell respectively, no Incubi from Changeling, no Lucifuge from Hunter. I get the idea that Descent Demons are supposed to be a different thing from Inferno and such. But who would be more eager and likely to initiate contact in other supernatural factions than the subsect of a given faction that specifically makes it their business to deal with demons? It feels like a missed opportunity- I wish more time was devoted to reconciling the differences between Descent Demons and demons as they were depicted before instead of avoiding the topic completely.
                                    And then we have the setting hacks- what most of us came here for. This is good stuff, to the point where I want some of it to be folded into the canon for the second edition. Aside from the Nephilim there's really nothing about Giants in the Earth that strongly conflicts with the default setting. With a little reworking you could even make the City the seed of truth in the Pain Prophet's ramblings about the floating city that humanity destroyed. Fear Tomorrow offers an intriguing setting in the form of the Spiral World. It strikes me that this could make a great "canon" location for the Celebrants of Hell from the God-Machine Chronicles. Perhaps a lost Agenda that took the concept of freedom/nihilism too far and fled into the Void of dying timelines and obsolete Infrastructures? Altered State...eh. Perfectly fine. I'm not so sure that it needed to be a shard, however. It seems like a perfectly "normal" operation of the God-Machine more worthy of the urban legends from the previous chapter. Its a cool idea, don't get me wrong. It just feels like the odd man out. In a chapter about ultimate beginnings and ultimate ends this felt fairly routine for the God-Machine. Shrug. Just my reaction.

                                    Overall, a good book. Possibly sullied by expectations of my own that were higher than what was delivered. But nonetheless, a good book even a cynic could like.

                                        Wow, guys, I do not need you to fight Bunyip for me. I mean,, appreciated, but I don't need it. Specially since he has a fair point. Now, for me, the entire thing was kind of idle chatter, and so more or less a non-issue for me, hence non-explanation.

                                        Now if you want to just take the visuals of the mobile Castle Where Eternity Dwells and run with it as Infrastructure, sure, go ahead. For me though, it's hard for me to separate the context of the show from the visuals, because I saw that movie and a young age and boy howdy did it mess me up. Add in that later down the line, Utena would become one of those series that helped change me as a person and as such is my favorite anime series ever(with that eternal debate about it in relationship to Cowboy Bebop and Rayearth), and then add in a bunch of other instincts that relate to CHroD and conversions and such, and you get some of the answer I give.

                                        THe final matter is, taking it in context-Utena is a highly surrealist show. I say Mage, but truth be told, it's hard for me to pin that scene as exactly what it is in therms of ChroD executions because Utena is dense. I could actually make a decent argument that that scene is Beast, Mage, Changeling, Demon, Werewolf, Sin-eater, Hunter, and MAYBE just a little bit Mummy. Really, truthfully though, it is probably best treated as Blue Book Weirdness. For me to sit down and explain and arrive at a conclusion that feels satisfactory to me would involve me having to type up an analysis of the movie, which would in part require me to type a partial analysis of the series, which would be mind-boggling long. Now, I would love to do that one day, because if any series deserves loving analysis and deconstruction, it's Utena.
