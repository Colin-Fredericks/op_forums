    Originally posted by Michael Kenner View Post

    Well I'm not bothered personally since I genuinely quite like the way Demon is presented with the techgnostic elements. However perhaps I can explain why others do not.

    When it comes to narrative themes there's a big difference between science, magic and religion. Each of these three are used in different ways when you're telling stories. I'll boil it down to just one small aspect because I can't possibly cover everything. It's much more than just this one aspect that I'm referring to though.

    The scientist does what works, even if it doesn't make sense, because it gives them results.
    The mage does what should work by symbolic logic as a deliberate attempt to gain results.
    The priest does what they're supposed to because it's right, regardless of results.

    Demon, quite deliberately in my opinion, attempts to use all three in describing the God Machine. However, when it comes to how the God Machine works we almost always only see it done through a process that's scientific, magical or a combination of both. It builds infrastructure to achieve results, not because it's right that it should. The divine and religious aspects of the God Machine are mostly only used for their grandeur rather than being frequently invoked in the setting.

    It's not just this process. The most common aspects of at least Abrahamic religions, faith, identity, morality, community, dogma and personal relationships with the divine are either only used in twisted forms for small cults or excluded altogether. The G-M is only a god in power and terror but in all other ways it doesn't really reflect that mode of storytelling associated with religion. Instead it's a form of cosmic horror like H.P. Lovecraft's Cthulu mythos.

    I'm perfectly happy with this and it's the game that I want to play. However for people who were looking for something a little more Paradise Lost, they may find a lot of clashes with that in far more deep thematic issues rather than a surface aesthetic of cogs and gears. Given that Demon: The Fallen was from more so this perspective and Inferno... well, was somewhere in the middle, it's not surprising that some people expected to find it here in Demon: The Descent.

    I do think it's possible for a storyteller to re-emphasize these aspects and shift Demon back to include some religious aspects to the G-M, but it's a tricky task and I think many people troubled thematically by Demon don't really recognize that this may be the problem.
    Fair enough.

                                        Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                        It's been said by a few people that they dislike the technological nature of Demons and the God-Machine (though I personally disagree, liking how well it fits with Ezekiel). Now, I understand the preference of Demons should be more mystical rather than bio-mechanical beings, as it is a valid preference. But what I don't understand is why the technological nature is hard to remove?

                                        Can the issue be fixed in your personal games by simply restricting access to Demonic Form abilities that are technological? If not, what other things conflict with altering the to your desire? As far as I'm aware, everything else in the game is fitting for more generalized interpretations of demons.
                                        Your first idea is there in your post.

                                        Ezekiel 1:4-9
                                        4I looked, and I saw a windstorm coming out of the north--an immense cloud with flashing lightning and surrounded by brilliant light. The center of the fire looked like glowing metal, 5and in the fire was what looked like four living creatures. In appearance their form was human, 6but each of them had four faces and four wings. 7Their legs were straight; their feet were like those of a calf and gleamed like burnished bronze. 8Under their wings on their four sides they had human hands. All four of them had faces and wings, 9and the wings of one touched the wings of another. Each one went straight ahead; they did not turn as they moved.

                                        Simply paint the technologies of the God-Machine as arcane in design. Here are some examples off the top of my head:

                                        Electrical Sight doesn't need to change at all, but perhaps rename it to: I See The Light or something flavourful like that. Angels, and demons, are luminous beings after all.

                                        Rivet-Arm allows you to get creative. You could go for something such as a cross-bow built into angelic armour or something more weird and unreal like an arm that shoots crucifix nails.

                                        Plasma Drive becomes Messenger's Motion. Then illustrate it's appearance as a glowing radiance flows from the Demon's skin, shining like the sun caught beneath the flesh.

                                        Tether becomes Serpent's Tail. Then illustrate it as a fiery or fleshy serpentine tail. Maybe add a thorn, spine, spade or stinger for effect at player discretion.



                                        Also, I disagree with the assertion that Demon: the Descent lacks any distinct religious undertones. I think that it has them in spades. The God-Machine's primary operations are supported by cults; it manipulates and utilises belief systems that exist independent of it or creates new belief systems whole-sale with the deployment of an angel. A Demon often has to infiltrate these cults, witness the faults and fallacies that faith - an otherwise altruistic trait - can create when bent to certain extremes. Then a Demon, as a traitor and a turn-coat to God-Machine and Its design, has to decide against the workings of God-Machine on a personal, moral level and decide on a per event basis with no knowledge of the possible consequences. This is muddied even further by the fact, as Rose said, the Integrators might be right. God-Machine might be a benevolent being purely working on a scale, as God Himself, and therefore beyond our limited perception. The moral ambiguity of spy play and a need to survive all further feed into a heavily moralistic game. Should you do something because it's right? What is right? If I go back in time and kill a mass-murderer like Adolf Hitler, have I done the universe good by saving his millions of victims or have I created millions more through a chain-reaction which results in the Soviet Union becoming a much more economically powerful and oppressive regime? A Demon has to wrestle with these questions. Is 'good' good because it feels good? If so, why does 'evil' feel 'good'?

                                            This story I wrote for a character I want to play details almost literally removing the gears. I wanted something still befitting the game, but with a slightly more traditional look and trappings. So I created it. The game works just even if you do stuff like that. After all, this is a game in which truths are constantly hidden; in machines, in mysteries, in minds, in madness. Metaphor, veiled truths, webs of lies, all of these things come together to create the game's imagery. A battle between angels and demons might be a clash of celestial warriors and hideous beasts, with light and hellfire filling the sky. It might be a gun battle between mafiosos clad in expensive suits and bio-mechanical vultures with opera masks covering their bellies. Couching these conflicts within religious imagery is probably a favored tactic for both the God-Machine and the Unchained. It lets them hide more easily even as they wage shadow-war, and it lets them turn mortals to their side more easily.
