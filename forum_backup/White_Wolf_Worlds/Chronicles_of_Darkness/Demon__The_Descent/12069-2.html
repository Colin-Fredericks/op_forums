                                            6)Arise by Flyleaf

                                            The Fall hurts. The angel who Falls has crashed, burned, and died, and now a new thing stands in it's place. A demon starts off as a broken thing that is, in many ways, right at the bottom of the barrel. It can be tempting to let it completely destroy them, but demons are what they are for a reason, and that thing is now worth living and fighting a cold war for. Say what you will, a demon's Descent is just another ascent from a different point of view, and that's why Arise is a song that works for them. World of Darkness games tend to have a strong, if sometimes illusory, core of hope, the idea, at least, that some things are possible or that some things will not fall, and Demon is no exception-the Descent is all about finding a reason to keep standing, to keep fighting. It's a dream worth living and dying for, worth bleeding and lying for, or at least they keep telling themselves. This entire song is not really a testament that they'll win, but it is a rallying cry that calls on common experiences and metaphors that demons draw on. It is a plea to keep on keeping on, full of hopeful power and desperate pleading. You don't believe the song because it's promises are realistic-no, they rather feel like the comforting lies you tell to your fellows. You believe it because someone else, the singer, wants to believe it so much that you get caught up in. It seems possible with so much emotion wrapped up in it. You have something you died for, something you fought the God Machine for, even if it was just in the form of a choice. You have the potential to be so much more than you were under the God Machine, if only you'll rise up and take it. Yeah, this song can apply pretty strongly to a variety of WoD characters, but given how brutally every angel falls, it seems most appropriate that every demon born in the inferno find their own song to Arise to.

                                            Noteworthy Mentions: I give credit to Flyleaf not calling themselves a Christian band, but they totally are-maybe more real than some others, but it's still what you would expect and it's still hard to make sit nicely with the iconography of Demon. What I'm saying is I'm not a fan. That said, these are, if not entertaining, at least solid pieces of Demon music, thematically speaking: Believe in Dreams, Bittersweet, Fire Fire.

                                                First off, I need to apologize. If it seems like I emphasize the fight against the God Machine and maybe not enough of the other aspects of Demon, there's two reasons for that. 1)Some themes are easier to find than others. I'm sure there are plenty of songs that emphasize the nature of a demon's casual life-in fact, I know at least two immeadiately, Devil by Stained and Rotten Inside by Earshot. Pacts, cults, covers, the way a demon feels, acts, so on, so forth-those songs exist, but it's not as populous as the "Rebel Against God", at least from where I sit. 2)I'm also a sucker for rebellion, particularly against big, grand things like, I dunno, God, or the poseur thereof. I may not be a Saboteur, but I deeply sympathize, and wouldn't say no if that Agenda came knocking at my door for help. I suppose another factor is that a lot of these songs just fell straight into my lap, it was a quick construct of a top ten list, but whatever. That said, I do still stand by this list. With that said, possibly the most aggressive song on here:

                                                5)Anti-You by Blue Stahli

                                                For all the roots that the main enemy of Demon has in mythological and religious ideas(icons if nothing else), the God-Machine is a whole new being because of it's involvement in our technology and the culture that springs up around it. We rage against God quite a bit here, but many people are quick to note that we are raging against the Machine as well. So instead of going for an obligatory reference to some band that may or may not have gotten big with an earlier techgnostic revolution movie about a not-quite-occult-matrix or something, I'm gonna let Blue Stahli preach some heresy to the Church of Media with Anti-You. Without a doubt, it's still very techgnostic in it's feel, with it's evocation of religion in it's discussion about...well, everything, from advertisement to advertisement media to social networks, the dogma created by a manipulative corporate/government/both driven world and the quick-to-please masses. More so than any other New World game, Demon really gets into the heart of how the world is being played, tricked, deceived, and shuffled into place by it's own conceits, using that as the divine apparel for their steel and wi-fi god. Anti-You is vicious, painting the world that demons must contend with in perverse and cruel metaphor and turns of phrase before burning it all down. Really, the thing they attack is a nifty point-the million media lies are now so pervasive that it's almost impossible, if not flat out harmful, to remove from the world, much like the God-Machine, but demons can acknowledge that and still play counter to it all, despite the risks to their Cover-this song is the anthem of those going loud or those who skirt the edge of plausible Cover. It really captures, more so than anything else on this list, the new face of God that demons spit in. For every could be Integrator song on this list, Anti You raises the middle finger and tells the God Machine quite clearly where it can stick it.

                                                Noteworthy Mentions: Ultranumb, Corner, Metamorphosis(Life Theory Remix)
