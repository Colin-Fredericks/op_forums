                    1)True, but Demon does still largely play to it's own context, since whether or not Changelings exist is a ST fiat. So keep that in mind.

                    2)What happens in Arcadia doesn't provide context for what happens in the world.

                    3)Changelings can't generate 'people'(well, video game NPC's really) to go enact a story, they can make people who live close to patterns become utterly ensnared in them. In a certain sense, the demon can do it with less blood, or at least less risk to actual people. But it depends on the effect you want.

                    4)You are creating temporary people to enact a narrative for whatever reason best works for you, and you can hop into one of these bit roles to go and do whatever shit you want/can as that role. THere's a lot you can do with Urban Legend.

                    5)There's a relationship here that I think you might be missing. A lot of the things in the WoD aren't true because people believe in them, people 'believe' in them because they are true.

                    People also believe a lot of false things. Frightful memes mutate and become something wholly new. Demons can take advantage of that faster that most. (And to be fair[as an example], Slenderman is totally their sort of game, a dark fairytale fueled by modern fears and backed by quantum myth)

                    6)It's like a occult matrix given form by the masses and life by the Demon.

                    7)It's an Exploit. All of them are big neon signs reading "PAY ATTENTION TO ME!"

                    8)EDIT: Consider some of the fiction for WoD, particularly Strangeness in the Proportion, where all things are true, but there's still a pervasiveness of folklore, myth, and urban legends that aren't true. Simon said that out of the thousands of autopsies he performed, none really seemed the result of supernatural involvement.

                    Yes, the World of Darkness is filled to the gills with supernatural baddies, but quite frankly it's still so big that not everything is absolutely covered.

                                Originally posted by Badger King View Post
                                So I was wondering, aside from not being able to use powers that require extended actions, what are the limitations to Show of Power? Could a werewolf's shape shifting ability be used since it only requires a reflexive action? What about Disciplines, Gifts, Rotes, etc? If something like Garau form was available, how would one determine how long it lasts, or would it last the entire scene since it is an inherent ability? Would they be subject to Death Rage?
                                Fair questions all. There would have been literally no way to address all of them in the write-up of a single Exploit, so I'm afraid there will necessarily be some group discussion and ST discretion involved. RAW gives you a lot of leeway, and I'm not going to say "this is the right way to interpret the power and this other way is bad-wrong fun and forbidden." Enough with my standard caveats, though.

                                That said, I originally conceived of Show of Power as primarily a "convince them that I'm one of them" power with a side of "convince them that I'm something supernatural but not a demon." I wrote it immediately after Deep Cover, which serves a similar purpose to a lesser degree. In essence, the intention is for the demon to be able to pretend to be a mage or werewolf or vampire or whatever without being immediately detected because he can't cast spells/shift/use Disciplines/whatever. But it's supposed to be juuuust enough to fake it, not enough to be impressive at it. Thus the exclusion of extended actions, the limit of one success, and the minimal number of factors. When someone says, "You're no mage! You're a demon!" you can "prove" you're a mage using, well, a small show of power.

                                From that perspective, I'd say werewolf form? Yes because how can you pretend to be a werewolf if you can't change shapes? Rotes? Kind of. I mean sure, wiggle your fingers all you want and mumble convincing arcane babble, but it isn't going to give you a bonus to your make-magic-go roll so it's just Rote theater. Burning in the sun? Usually optional, but sometimes the only way to convince the Anarchs that you're not a spy is to spend some Aether so you can get a nasty sunburn when the situation calls for it. Gotta keep up appearances if you want to stay alive in this biz.

                                                    Finally finished the book last night. There's a power called Demon Car that allows you to merge with your car into a demon car. This automatically makes this the best book ever*.

                                                    ...

                                                    Okay, so I think it's actually pretty decent. I agree with an above poster that the first chapter's pretty much just a rehash of information you already knew from the core or could extrapolate from there, aside from a few parts. Decently written, probably useful for those that need it spelled out, but I didn't see as much interesting or innovative stuff in the text as I was personally hoping for. From then on the book gets better and better: chapter two is pretty useful, chapter three is the toybox full of neat new toys (like Demon Car(!)), and chapter 4 is a bunch of sorely needed and incredibly interesting expansion and twists on gadgets.

                                                    All in all, well worth the money. I'll be picking it up in PoD as soon as it launches.

                                                        I realize this is of finite use to a ST looking for guidance, but this is definitely one of those powers that you really have to adjudicate as you go. That said, here are a couple words of comfort? Warning? Something like that.

                                                        First, I will point out that each power the demon "fakes" requires a separate use of this Exploit, and every use of it costs Aether and constitutes a compromise. Nor does access to a particular ability last forever. Even if you spend a year masquerading as splats X, Y, Z, and Q, learning their kung fu, er, powers so you can be the biggest badass ever, simply "powering up" all those kewl muuvs has a good chance of bringing a choir of hunter angels down on your head. Demons already have the option of Going Loud, and a dozen compromises in a single scene sounds to me like just a slower way to do that.

                                                        Another balancing aspect is the demon can't mimic a power he hasn't experienced or encountered first-hand. As ST that means that you have a pretty decent amount of control over what powers that demon acquires via Show of Power. Don't want him to pick up ridiculous amounts of Celerity? Don't bring in vampires with tons of Celerity into the game. Yes, he's going to learn every power you use against him and turn it against the next batch of baddies you bring out, but the ST has considerable control over the speed with which that cold war progresses.

                                                        I totally understand how clever players could find ways to abuse this Exploit, but I also see ways that clever players can be, well, clever with it. I can imagine a demon who has spent a decade or more apprenticed to a mage, carefully pretending to be one of the Wise herself, and has access to a fairly huge range of spells as a result, and I can see the concept as either a horribly twinked out Sam Haight Special (in the worst cWoD sense) or a really nifty character with a complex Cover to maintain (especially as a bizarre antagonist). It's up to the player (and the ST) to determine whether the execution of that concept is awesome or just dumb.

                                                        Hope that helps you somewhat, at least.
