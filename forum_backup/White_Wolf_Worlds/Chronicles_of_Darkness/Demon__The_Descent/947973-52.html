                    The fact that that line is in the Duration section and the two shortest durations for a pact add nothing to the demon's side of the equation feels relevant here — anything the demon can get out of a short-term pact is effectively zeroed out at the end of the pact and "you just have to give up a minor acquaintanceship"/"you'll be the leader of a cult that will form over a couple weeks" is close enough to "free" for most would-be dupes to overlook the actual cost.

                    Second! I seem to recall that when forging pacts the mortal needs to know they are dealing with a demon/someone representing a demon, but I can't seem to find this in the book. Am i misremembering this or am I just bad at finding stuff I read long ago .
                    The terms of the pact need to be laid out truthfully in writing, however difficult to parse out it may be — regardless of the costs in time and opportunity, it must be theoretically possible that, if the mortal reads carefully, they will know the rules of their agreement.

                    You may be thinking of bargains from Changeling 2e, which require the changeling making the bargain to appear before the other party without the occlusion offered by their Mask; even that doesn't require

                    You might also be thinking of the degrees of belief different ratings in the Cultists Merit represent for how in-the-know your cultists are, but that explicitly doesn't represent having pacts with your cultists.
