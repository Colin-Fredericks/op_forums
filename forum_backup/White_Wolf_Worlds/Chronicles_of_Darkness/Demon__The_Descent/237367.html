      Oh my word, the picture on page 62 is fantastic!
      This book as a whole seems far more useful than any other WW/OP citybook. It has a central theme - the time splinters, a ton of npc statblocks, an adventure section, and a whole chapter on plothooks for all the other gamelines. Best of all, not all of those plothooks bring changlings, werewolves, etc into the content from the rest of the book. Rather, they focus on fitting each gameline into the mood of paranoia that suffuses the rest of the book.

      Several mechanical thumbs up for this book!

                  one demon, it forms an impression. It normalizes this idea, gives it ground. Some valid interpretations of DtD, I think, for which the demonic state is an important statement about freedom, suffer as a result. It can also accidentally communicate to Storytellers that this is something that is normal and casually okay to drop on a player character as well, when that decision ought to be more careful and given thought before being done. (I won't say it can't be done well, but you really have to know your player and what they want out of the game to pull that reveal off.) The extension that the God-Machine's plan goes all the way into Y2K's everyday life as a demon today takes this idea into silly town for me; as vast as the GM is, it's hard to swallow that it could keep an accurate forecast that far ahead. It does the things that I said earlier about "forming an impression," but much more so.

                  - The decision not to give the Gerent any stats at all. Stats are relevant for more than combat, and sometimes are referenced by abilities the players use. Some kind of guidelines for what happens if the shit really goes down would be nice too, really. Feels like a cop-out.

                  Okay, when I write it out in that much detail, it looks like it's more than it is. That's three things. Stayin' honest. Here's stuff I did like, not necessarily a comprehensive list:

                  - My God, that cover. Who drew that cover? Give that person more work, it's so well composed and hangs together well. Kudos to whoever designed the title logo for the supplement, too. They complement one another and it's a good logo just by its own merits.

                  - The Luther Burbank Lid is a nice example of infrastructure that is relatively benign. I like that that's there.

                  - The extended treatment of just how stable or real the 1889 world is. I especially love the spot parties. That's a fun way to spin your average ominous-anomalies hook.

                  - Marc Janssen's projects. Good color combined with a sincere-feeling story about how stigmata would affect a relatively normal person.

                  - Local Curiosities. I love these sidebars. Fantastic flavor, a lot can be done with so few words. Do more of these, please. Give whichever writer decided to put these in a cookie. Thin mint.

                  - Pacific Tower. Cool, versatile concept.

                  - Pike Place Flowers. It was interesting up until the last sentence, which is brilliant. There's enough detail that if it doesn't give you ideas Pike Place is still a useful hook, but it's so vague that it can go in all sorts of directions.

                  - Mr. Excitement is cool, and her demonic form description is fantastic.

                  - Bait and Hound. Nice concept made into a great aesthetic victory by both the descriptions given by the writer and the interpretations then made by the illustrator. Look at these guys. Man.

                  - Stray. I like having the occasional genuinely friendly and receptive NPC, and I'm a sucker for talking animals. Great-Grandfather Crow is one of my favorite Promethean NPCs, too.

                  - The idea of the Apocalypse Vault itself is okay, but the writeup takes the exact right approach to it when its first suggestion on how to use in a game is to run a heist.

                      I guess the forum just eats the first part of your post if it's too long? Append this to the beginning:

                      "Really good, densely packed supplement. Here's the stuff I didn't like:

                      - The coffee shop infrastructure feels like it's trying much too hard to be clever telling an old joke. It also feels a little too pervasive in reach and effect, between the wide distribution of the coffee chains and the writeup going as far as to suggest they might put drugs in the coffee to make the populace pliable and manipulable. This hook feels like it would be much more at home in the cWoD as a Technocracy project or somesuch.
                      - The idea that Y2K's entire Descent, from his Fall to his current everyday life, is the result of a grand game by the God-Machine to the ends of monitoring the Vault. This is tricky territory. The idea that the God-Machine might be at cause over a demon's Fall, that it is an intended function of its angels and works somehow to the Machine's ends, is a useful idea to keep in the game. As an idea. I feel the game suffers once it becomes more than an idea, when the gameline presents a demon who is verifiably an intentional product and pawn of the Machine from beginning to end. Even when this is true of only one demon, it forms an impression."

                      I also notice that the credits page tells us the cover illustration is by Michael Gaydos. Very nice, versatile guy too it looks like.
