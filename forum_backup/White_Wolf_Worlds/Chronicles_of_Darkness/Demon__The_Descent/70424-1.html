                    Keep in mind that splinters are a rare phenomenon manufactured by the God-Machine. And for that reason alone do they have some coverage in Demon. Your city might not have a Splinter, your Demon's may not ever experience one. But in the back of their heads that are likely aware of the possibility that such things do exist. It's no different than the reason why the various Agenda's have a blurb about the other splats. It's not that Demon needs interaction with the rest of the WoD splats but if they do, here's a handy guide. Splinters were first introduced in the GMC book. They just go more cover here, and will get more again. I find them interesting, but I would be hard pressed to use one myself. And if I do, it would be later in my game than something that's just looming over the players heads the whole time.

                                There's a situation in the anime Kamisama no Inai Nichiyoubi (loosely translated as The Sunday Without God) that's very much like a splinter - entering a particular door leads to a version of the abandoned town that's thoroughly populated.

                                The characters are led there by the newest member of their group and thus receive a bit of advance warning about the place: on the same day every year, the entire world resets (the character in question has actually tried burning down the town a few times to no avail), rewriting the memories of anyone inside (including newcomers - the town incorporates them on the next loop, as the character discovered when he tried bringing in more people and the town simply grew to accommodate them), and the entire reason the place exists seems to be owed to the apparent fact that God started granting wishes before He went away and the students of a particular class wanted to undo the on-campus death of a classmate.

                                It's very difficult to leave the place and the character who knows about all of this (who is one of two people who aren't subject to the memory-rewrite) has been trying to force the bubble to burst for a couple of years by the time the characters reach the town.

                                It's the subject of an entire arc of the show and it and the Seattle splinters have seemed resonant with similar concepts, though the 1889 splinter's having broken the temporal loop sometime in the last few decades seems like it would cover much of the groundwork for making splinters that aren't time-locked.
