        I'd really like it if the Pact mechanics:
        • Ability to Demons store their rewards from Pacts as bargaining chips. For instance, give someone Striking Looks in exchange for their 4 Dots in Academics then use those 4 Dots of Academics in deals with College Students trying to pass exams. Essentially this is to avoid the "Where does the money come from?" problem that might happen. Demons could still make deals like those, but this would be a safer alternative.
        • "Where does the money come from?" is a great premise and could result in Mortal Antagonists that become hunters or something worse. Imagine giving a person Striking Looks only for a model to become ugly overnight. She becomes obsessed with tracking down what did this to her. It's touched on in the Core books, but these people seem like they'd be the most common type of demon hunters.
        • Supernatural Ability Pacts - Giving someone supernatural abilities like a Werewolf or Vampire, should create a more mundane version of those splats that doesn't conform to those groups.
        • Suppression of Supernatural Ability - While the rule book says a demon can't steal a Vampire or Werewolf's powers as part of a soul pact, I really like the idea of trying to cure people of their affliction, but having disastrous consequences. For example, removing the Vampire part of a person doesn't delete it, Pacts are about equivalent exchange. Instead the Vampire part is splintered off as a physical embodiment of "The Beast". So yes the Vampire becomes Mortal again, but now there is a homicidal Vampire that wants you back. Perhaps to keep this from causing too much damage Demons try to keep pacts like this limited to a short time frame.

                            Originally posted by Verge View Post
                            A full suite of Anchors and Touchstones (tied to Covers) would help underscore the alien mindset of the Unchained.
                            They already have Anchors in the form of Virtues and Vices that are supposed to be alien as to reinforce the alien mindset.
                            Touchstones, while not a bad idea for longterm Covers, are not universally present in Revised Storytelling System (aka 2E) games, and their lack here does not imply that Demon isn't a Revised Storytelling System game (which it is, to like 90-95%).


                            Originally posted by Verge View Post
                            The Cipher should be a means of not only achieving power, but a means of reaching Hell (if it exists), or a means of liberation. I'm reading this edition, and Hell and the Cipher seem to go nowhere. Like, the Cipher could be a backdoor into the God-Machine itself, or a means of hardening Cover against low-level compromises.
                            Supposedly the Cipher is the way to Hell, but it's woefully underdeveloped and completely dependent on the ST to come up with some explanation to what Hell means for your character. Mechanically speaking, though, unlocking your whole Cipher and getting closer to (if not reaching) Hell only grants you the final Interlock.
                            In short, it is underwhelming, too dependent on non-informed character progression choices, and highly discouraging to homebrewing because any homebrew Embeds written up or found during the game will be guaranteed to not be the right Key because the Cipher was supposed to be planned out before any such Embeds were included. Hell, if you start a game before getting the Players' Guide you can be sure those Embeds won't be correct either.

There's such strong questions about Self among the Descent, the Cipher, and Covers, and that's what my suggestions about anchors, 'stones, and Embeds are lurching at.
                                                    Ok - very personal opinion follows.

                                                    I like the CoD products because they focus a bit more on storytelling than tactics (I'm looking at you DnD). You are rewarded for your character making dramatically interesting choices.

                                                    Some of the CoD storylines, I have come to understand, focus on catharsis. In particular, Changeling (AFAIK) focuses on surviving and then thriving after horrible abuse. Promethean focuses on maturation and choice in a world that is actively hostile to your existence, while you are simply trying to become the person you long to be. Geist deals with obsession, commitment unto and beyond death. These can all be emotionally powerful themes for the people playing them.

                                                    Demon, for me, is a fascinating science fiction/horror/espionage storyline that doesn't quite drink from the same well. There's lots of drama in opposing the GodMachine, in spy craft, in evading an overwhelming police state while worrying about who you trust. There's lots of drama, but it's not focused on self-actualization.

                                                    Well, that's because I haven't mentioned Ciphers and the search for their individual "Hell". Those are explicitly part of the game line and are all about self-actualization. But these mechanisms seem at odds with the adventure/spy/horror dynamics1. I think that may be why people see the Cipher as a bit tacked on, since the journey that is supposed to be defined by the Cipher is not as clear as the journeys in the other game lines, and for many PC's may not really be there at all.

                                                    I'm not saying you can't run a great Demon game focussing on a character's Fall, and their internal struggle to find out who they are now that they have rebelled. The machinery is all there to do so. But in many of the stories presented, that existential angst takes a backseat to the tension already present in the espionage/horror them of the game. I can imagine dropping Ciphers from the game without feeling that the game has lost much because of how I see the game played.

                                                    Again, YMMV.
