    Originally posted by Kirby Jerusalem View Post
    Finally finished the book last night. There's a power called Demon Car that allows you to merge with your car into a demon car. This automatically makes this the best book ever*.

    ...

    Okay, so I think it's actually pretty decent. I agree with an above poster that the first chapter's pretty much just a rehash of information you already knew from the core or could extrapolate from there, aside from a few parts. Decently written, probably useful for those that need it spelled out, but I didn't see as much interesting or innovative stuff in the text as I was personally hoping for. From then on the book gets better and better: chapter two is pretty useful, chapter three is the toybox full of neat new toys (like Demon Car(!)), and chapter 4 is a bunch of sorely needed and incredibly interesting expansion and twists on gadgets.

    All in all, well worth the money. I'll be picking it up in PoD as soon as it launches.


    * Seriously, though, Demon Car rules. The only issue I have is that you could have called it "Hell On Wheels" but you didn't. Come oooooon.
    Ah well. Sometimes I miss opportunities. Glad you liked it. :-)

                    Picked FoH up today, and I love it - especially the details on suborning Infrastructure for more than just an Aether source (my Tempter is definitely going to have fun with that).

                    I'm left with a couple of questions, though:

                    First, Laser Cutter. Acidic Spit (also a Technology) does damage based on successes and at greater range, and the damage is Aggravated, bypassing mundane Durability entirely (unless that got changed somewhere and I missed it). I'm guessing, given that it isn't stated, that Laser Cutter's damage is Lethal. Does Laser Cutter bypass the set amount of Durability on magic objects? Does it add its damage to successes, or just inflict the stated amount regardless of successes? (My guess is that it adds to successes, given that the Dex + Crafts roll when using it to break an immobile, unattended object wouldn't make much sense otherwise, but I didn't see anything to support this in the way the form's rules are worded.) Is it even possible for Laser Cutter to penetrate, for example, the Durability 10 bank vault in the base blue book? Is it just strictly worse than Acidic Spit, or am I getting confused somewhere?

                    Second: Would it be unreasonable to conclude that, to free the hand if necessary, a Demon can reflexively engage and disengage Eliminator Cannon as with Blade Hand or Rivet Arm? The Demon would still have to let go of anything held in hand to engage the weapon, as with the other two. I'm asking this one because both of the latter mention under Appearance that the form replaces a hand like Eliminator Cannon (though DtD mentions that the forms' appearances can be changed within reason to fit the Demon anyway), but also mention the reflexive "draw/stow" under system, while Eliminator Cannon says nothing in either direction under system.

                                            Impressions: Really liked this one, and I think is going to be essential material for running a DtD game, as there is going to be a lot of things that will come up in games.
                                            • I'm very glad the developers took the word count to add more inspirational material. The original book was a little scant, considering that DtD is kinda off the walls, more inspiration is always good.
                                            • I'm... not thrilled that they added more dice mechanics to the cover mechanics, one of the strengths of the story-teller system was minimizing dice rolling, but there are some nice story hooks here, such as keeping an abusive ex-spouse alive so he can continue to support your cover. Or Visiting the funeral of a friend to make sure the people there remember you enough to maintain your cover. Gives demons more an incentive to hang around humans.
                                            • Agendas. Nice, but I wish there was more material difference between
                                            • Incarnations. Say thoughts as Agendas
                                            • Infrastructure. If there are any developers reading I have only one thing to say: I WANT MORE.
                                            • Agencies. I always had trouble understanding why a demon would expose themselves to the risk of joining an agency, with so few up front benefits. The answer: ambition, agencies are what happens when a demon decides "go pro or go home."
                                            • New Embeds and Exploits: Mixed. Some are brilliant, some are a little too situational to be useful (Fractal Reality jumps to mind)
                                            • Gadgets: New options, more in-story justification

                                            What I would have wanted (because I'm greedy):
                                            • Options for playing exiles
                                            • Options for playing Cryptids
                                            • Angel Gadgets. You heard me. Get an angel, by hook, crook, or bargain to put a numen in a gadget.

                                                Finally finished with the book! Overall, I'd say it's solid; not amazing, not bad, not even mediocre, but just effective. The redundancy in Chapter 1 was welcome for me, you can't go wrong with new powers to play with, and I don't think it did anything wrong, but it was lacking a few things I was hoping for.
                                                • Sympathetic Integrators: they're my favorite Agenda, and Flowers doesn't do enough to make them feel like a character choice most would want to make.
                                                • Hell: tragically little discussion on Hell, considering how major a goal it is to some. I wanted examples of what physical Hell would look like.
                                                • Agencies: what's there is fine, but I wanted a few example groups! The group that Comrade West runs in Seattle is fascinating, but I had my fingers crossed for examples of Agencies at each of the Tiers, with variable memberships and goals. The guidelines we got felt a little "duh!" to me.
                                                • Demonic Form abilities: my least-favorite bit of Demon character creation, and the options here still felt unexciting to me. Maybe I'm impossible to please.
                                                • Exiles: the cousins of the Unchained probably belong in the Storyteller book, but I'm so damn eager to find out more about them!
                                                • Cryptid Familiars: I think everyone wants these.

                                                    06-29-2014, 12:06 AM
                                                    So I finished Flowers of Hell. On the whole, I'm wildly pleased with the product-it was a legitimate joy to read. I felt like it answered some of my smaller wants, from Going Native answer my idle thought of comparison to Doctor Who to the Gadget chapter placing time limits and stake on mortal usage, which has been the center conflict of a story brewing in my head for a long while now.

                                                    The first chapter neatly expanded demon capability and philosophy and really did give me a great sense of what it was like to live as a demon, both the good, the bad, and above all else the tenseness of that life.

                                                    I have sung praises about the second chapter and will do so again: this sort of thing was the first question I had for Demon The Descent upon finishing the first chapter, and it was delivered quite reasonably-it's big, but not world breaking, risky to do, and story generating. THe discussions on Infrastructure doubled down on this, and the bonus of Agenda creation, while perhaps a little tedious, was an extended bonus.

                                                    Chapter Three was a glorious grab bag, and like I said, a lot of little desires were hit. Some additional ones were Newton's Nightmares, which felt like a great reinterpretation and execution of the Lore of the Fundament from Fallen, Show of Power and Deep Cover for mimicking supernaturals(a great interest of mine since the beginning), Walls of Jericho and Stop for just being brutal, Demon Car and Terrible Avatar for exapnding the Demonic form's usage(and again to Terrible Avatar for hitting a major itch for my favorite demon), Ripple, Trip, Fractal Reality, and Victory at Any Price for perpetuating the momentum of any action scene, particularly combat, Apple of Discord(Want It Need It Spell) simply for making me smile, High of Birth for it's great player-made plot hooks, Knock-off just for being awesome, Open Sesame and Wasted Time for making sneaking in more fun and easy, the entire Mundane list for being awesome as Hell, Imagine for making John Lennon scary and exciting again, The Look for taking me back to younger days(Ruroni Kenshin, not some dark thing), Loose Lips and The Only Word That Matters for making dialogue a knife edge, Detachable Limbs for taking me back to my youth(Power Rangers, not some dark thing), Laser Cutter for having a hydro version that makes me want to read Battle Angel Alita again, Low-I'm just listing the whole damn chapter. Point is there was always something fun in the bag.

                                                    Chapter Four was the hardest one to read for me, but considering I have a story I want to write around this it was all useful, and I'm considering what else I can do with the other things like Form Gadgets and Lambdas for that. The new gadgets are all awesome, and I want to make a Lambda built on Incendiary an Combustion(and maybe another, if I'm cheeky?) that has the activation code "Kieru!" or "Disappear!". Fire that on Overcharge. Speaking of, the sample Newton's Nightmares Gadget almost matches a weapon I gave a TF:V agent a few years ago that he claimed was from Roswell, except that also fractured space around the target point so you couldn't really leave within a certain point. I wonder if that was one of the five second-order Lambdas now. Form Gadgets are an excellent way to expand demonic forms even further, knockoffs and one shots are appreciated-just lots of good stuff.

                                                    I suppose my only complaint with this is something I never wanted to say, given how many people griped about it and I thought it was silly: the language really is moving away from the sense of the divine rebels. It's less Rakshasas and Daevas and Asuras in this book, and definitely more arcane physics/spy thriller stuff. That's hardly a deal breaker for me, and I can always inject more on my own, but it finally did grain against me.

                                                    That's a small price to pay, though. All of this book is awesome, Chapters One and Two in particular. It expands stories I had, gives me new ones to tell, and spirals off into places between. Good job guys-you've definitely secured Demon as my second favorite RPG.
