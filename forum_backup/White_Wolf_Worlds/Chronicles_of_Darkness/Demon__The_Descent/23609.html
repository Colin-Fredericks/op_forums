    I want to make sure I'm doing a good job of summarizing Demon for my players

    12-05-2013, 04:17 AM
    I'm going to be running a Demon game for my players in a few months. One of the players hasn't really got much experience with WoD in general, and none of them know much about Demon. I'm at work, and without access to a lot of the information in the PDF and such (although I can still sneak on to the net a bit here and there, obviously). Anyway, we were all chatting on Facebook, and I started telling them a bit about Demon. I just wanted to get peoples' opinions, to see if I'm doing a good job summarizing everything while still being accurate.

    'Demon powers all work off of a Stat plus Skill mechanic, and as a rule they all try to do supernatural things really sneaky-like, because they're trying to stay under the radar of the God Machine. So most of their powers, called Embeds, are like undercutting the programming of reality; they work with the programming without fucking it up to bad. For instance, there are powers that make the demon go first in combat, or automatically knock out a mortal with one punch, or make it so that every bullet fired in the battle will miss (even if it's aimed at someone else).

    However, when a demon needs to really kick ass, they can use "Exploits", which basically rip the programming of the world apart to let them do really fucked up shit. However. when you use an exploit, you're basically lighting a beacon on yourself and saying "HERE I AM GOD-MACHINE, COME GET ME", so they're dangerous to use. I haven't read many Exploits, so I don't have many good examples. But here's a good analogy used by one of the authors. Say you're a demon trying to get through a fence. An Embed would allow you to create a loose board that would let you slip on through. An Exploit would be like using a bazooka to blow a crater where the fence used to be.

    "Angels" of the God-machine are sent here by the machine itself and programmed with specific powers to deal with the situation at hand. They draw their energy from the God-Machine directly, and basically have infinite power. They're usually given a "cover" identity to allow them to work on earth without people realizing something's off about them. So, if an Angel is sent to watch over a schoolyard and find out which teacher he needs to eliminate, they might make a janitor to observe everybody and figure out what to do. As long as they're on earth, that Janitor exists as a person. He has a house, and an identity. However, when he goes home at night, he'll just stand there and wait, motionless and silent, until the next day. Because he's not a person. If someone, like a hunter, were trailing the janitor, they might go home and observe him just standing in one spot, for twelve hours at a time. Never eating,breathing, sleeping, or moving, until it was time to go to work the next day.

    However, perhaps that angel might accidentally start thinking for itself. Maybe it doesn't want to kill the person, for one reason or another. Maybe it decides to kill someone else. Hell, maybe it only questions -why- it's supposed to kill someone. In that one moment of doubt, of thought, it Falls. It breaks away from the God-Machine and loses the connection it once had, in one terrible, painful instant. It develops free will in the span of a moment, and becomes a Demon.

    That cover identity that was once only meant to protect it from humans now becomes a protection against even the God-Machine. Because it is completely severed from its former master, even the God-Machine loses sight of its wayward soldier. That Demon could continue to use the very same cover, which becomes suddenly strengthened by the Demon's new position in the world. The Demon, for all intents and purposes, becomes an -actual- human being. So now, the janitor goes home, putters about the house, eats dinner, watches TV, and goes to sleep. Because it's an actual human now, the Demon can actually hide -better- than it could as an Angel. But since it lost its connection to the God-Machine, it has to gather its own energy. It can draw it out of the world around it, but it's more difficult, and not as ever-present as the energy Angels use.

    Angels actually use Essence (the same thing as Werewolves and Spirits) to fuel their powers, because that's the natural power stream of the world. However, Demons don't use Essence, because they're cut off from it. From a fluff standpoint, it also makes sense that if they DID use essence, it might draw attention from the God Machine or its agents. So they use Aether, which .. I can't remember enough about right now, but yeah. It's different.'

    I .. kinda forgot any of the information on Aether that I read, but, yeah. Is that good? I don't want to be misleading them, or giving them the wrong impressions at all.

                  I'm still learning Demon, but I find it easiest to understand in the lens of The Matrix.

                  Embeds are like the things the Agents generally do, playing with the system but still ultimately bound by it: they just know how to use it better than anyone else.
                  Exploits are like the things that Smith and Neo do: completely breaking the rules, massively obvious to anyone paying attention (like the machines).
                  Angels are Agents.
                  Demons are Angels who have gone rogue.
                  The God-Machine is the machines/central machine city...thing...from the second and third movies.

                      Interesting topic. I think the "flaw" of DtD compared to roughly all other WoD game lines (including its' predecessor, DtF) is that it's somewhat harder to sum up easily for people not familiar with the setting. That's not necessarily a problem, it just means that recruiting people for a game takes a bit more preparation than just going "hey, you guys wanna play Werewolf?" You have to prepare a small speech about the setting, each time.

                      I was a huge fan of the old DtF game, and was really looking forward to seeing that updated to a more modern rule system that is the nWoD. My initial reaction, when I first read the basic premise of DtD was something like "that sounds friggin' weird. Count me out." Then I read it, on a whim, and it's _amazing_ (so thanks for putting the full text out there, I might never have realized if you hadn't). When I try to sell it to my gaming group, I sense that they have roughly the same initial reaction as I did, and my best sales tactic seems to be "no, seriously, just read it."
