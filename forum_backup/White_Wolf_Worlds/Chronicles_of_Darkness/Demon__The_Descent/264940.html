    Confused about Cover

    09-24-2014, 06:41 PM
    First off, I'm not bitching about Cover. I'm pretty sure I've got the wrong end of the stick somewhere, but despite how much I leaf through the book I can't 'get' it.

    I like the idea of Cover, I do. I even get most of the mechanics. But the more I read it, the more I come away with the impression that it, well, seems to push players into playing stereotypes, bar people from properly engaging with the game, and really doesn't make it clear the line between the Demon and its Cover. Again, reasonably sure I've got the wrong end of the stick here, and would like some clarification.

    First, the stereotype. This largely comes from the idea of acting too far outside of character of the Cover can lead to Compromise. Individually not really too dramatic an issue, but roll enough you're looking at potential trouble. And sure, it's reasonably cheap/easy to fix (ish, I'll get to that later), but still seems like it's encouraging players, with the risk - not a big risk, true, but certainly possible, and given the consequences if that cosmic roulette wheel comes up 0, concerning - the G-M notices, and diverts Cultists and/or Angels to investigate or cause trouble. So the Demon is encouraged to stay within their Cover. Ok, that's reasonable. Except they make the point that the Cover can't really significantly adapt or grow or change, which means the Demons ability to grow, adapt or change, is horrendously limited. You can tweak around the edges, but that's about as far as you can really go. If your cover is an obese computer programmer, you can dial back on the junk food and maybe go for a walk once in a while, but you can't exactly start going to the gym or start seriously addressing your diet. This means that the Demon needs to change Covers, which they make the point is problematic in a few ways, or you end up static and (for the player at least) boring. This also feeds into the second point.

    Second, engaging with the game. Now, a lot of Demons just kind of keep their metaphysical heads down and try to wiggle by. That's fine. Problem is, that doesn't make for a very interesting game, and is not the impression I've gotten for the expected kind of stories. But in the opening fictions, and in the main text, they indicate that many Demons keep their eyes very closely open for any sign of G-M activities, either to destroy/interfere with it, or to avoid it. Outside of fairly specific Covers, eyeballing a lot of Infrastructure isn't exactly in character. Now you can argue it may not necessarily be dramatically enough out of character to warrant Compromise issues, but see above re: paranoid. And what if the Demon, in scanning the news sites, sees something that warrants further examination? Isn't exactly in-character for, say, a stock trader to go poking around a construction site. The line between in-character and out-of-character-enough seems kind of arbitrary at best, and actively discouraging from a certain type of play at worst, particularly if you're one of the Agendas that do make a habit of acting against the G-M. I get a lot of that is supposed to work off ST-player understanding and co-operation, but the vagueness combined with the general tone seems...problem-inducing. Plus, coming up with multiple Covers seems problematic, since most of the characters stats are informed by the Demons first Cover: what if a Demon who was initially e.g. a stock trader picks up another Cover as e.g. a butcher? Either the stock trader has to pick up a bunch of really out of character skills (with attending butchery classes or whatever probably constituting a Compromise in themselves), or the butcher is going to suddenly suck at his job, and have a lot of really out of character skills. This also feeds into the first point, since the most obvious solution is to only get Covers with a similar set of skills, which leads to stereotypes.

    Finally, the line between the Demon and its Cover. Now, I can see that in some cases it'd be blurry, and that's potentially a lot of fun. But, in a similar vein to the above point, if most of the Demons skills, Merits and Attributes are informed/determined by its first Cover, even when riding other Covers, then the character isn't really a separate entity from its initial Cover, is it? Now, I know the old chestnut, "your character are not their stats", but come on: stats are a part of the character. They determine dice pools, which determine capability, which determine how they approach a certain situation, which determines how you play them, which determines how you view your character. We've all seen a player with a character who is good at X roll tank a few X rolls in a row, think very carefully about approaching a situation using X skill, even though that is the most logical approach and the dice have just been particularly mean lately. They even say explicitly that Destroyers or Defenders tend to view their Covers as strictly utilitarian while others really identify with them, but there's no mechanical representation of that, and it really seems that there should be. Even the rating of Cover seems to measure how well-integrated into that reality the Demon is, rather than how attached he is to that Cover, and since it determines a lot of your skills and Attributes and more than a few of your social ties, I can't really see how you could easily model your characters relative embracing of their Cover outside of roleplaying, which as I described above can lead to issues with engaging in the game or forging ties with other Demons and interacting with the other players, since there aren't a lot of contexts where a butcher, a priest, a coffee-shop anarchist and a stock trader can regularly meet up and not be arguably well out of character.

    Like I said, I'm not trying to bag on Cover, I'm just trying to understand, and the best way to do that is explain how I perceive it, so people can point out how I'm misunderstanding things.

      Cover is a morality stat, it's designed just like a morality stat. When Demon's answer their five question's they're answering in relation to that specific cover. Acting out of cover is anything grossly out of cover that would gain great scrutiny from that cover's mortal peers. In the example of your computer tech "What do you mean you don't know what HTML is?" would possibility call for a compromise roll. Remember Demon's are great liars, there's usually away out of those situations. But let's take a Detective, cover breaking would be doing something while on duty that's overly illegal, such as entering someone's house without cause and effect. Cover isn't supposed to be some great mechanical hindrance, it's flavor. And cover's do evolve, check out the Pact system, and read it again. Now one more time. Get it? You can add aspects to your covers by taking them from other people. What a computer genius who's also a body builder, get the Adonis body from a local trainer. The reason why each splat doesn't have a mechanical system for dealing with their cover, because once again, they're just the morality splat to help flavor the game. If your Demon doesn't have a skill that that cover should have, there's an ability all demon's get called "Legend" the point is the challenge, is having to fit in, that's an aspect of being a spy. You are not human, you are not a CEO, but you better start acting like one, slip up too much and the Angels will notice. Learn skills to make it easier, borrow them from the universe as need be.
      Your final line of text makes no sense "since there aren't a lot of contexts where a butcher, a priest, a coffee-shop anarchist and a stock trader can regularly meet up and not be arguably well out of character." People have friend's from all walks of life I'm a catholic housewife whose roleplaying group consists of a Lawyer, a atheist engineer, a Jack in the box manager, and f to m transvestite. People, and their characters will find a reason to bond we do it over roleplaying. Demon's do it over "If you stab me in the back I'm taking you with me" more or less.

      Welcome to the Descent, strap yourself in it's going to be a bumpy ride.

Note also that with the way the Conditions work and the ability to take Glitches, losing Cover doesn’t automatically reveal you to the G-M, but instead represents a marginal fraying of that identity. Play it smart and keep the strengths and limits of Cover and Cover loss in mind and you’ll be surprised how crazy you can get and still keep your Cover rating relatively intact.

By and large, though, most people don’t end up changing -that much-, and besides I’d say that with enough time, effort, and even possibly some applications of patch-job pacts or some Compromise rolls one should be able to shift their cover around, even if it’s glacially slow compared to actual people.                   The thing to remember about compromise rolls gained by out-of-character behavior is that it’s for when you act grossly out of character. It’s not so much for when you go “huh, I wouldn’t have guessed that” when you find out your boss knows a smattering of French, or when you go “wow, I never would have expected you to do that!” like if the previously fat programmer loses a significant amount of weight. It’s for when your octogenarian pacifist takes down five thugs with moves that would make Jason Bourne blush, or when you find the friendly pastor with a bloody machete and a bunch of chopped up bodies. It’s up to the ST and the player to come to an understanding of course, but in general the guideline I go with is that if it causes a hypothetical bystander to suffer some amount of cognitive dissonance, that’s when I break out the compromise rolls. So with that perspective a stock broker poking around a construction site is weird but it doesn’t make you sit up and go “wait a minute, a stock broker -here-? RED FLAG!” As such, demons can get away with a lot of the basic legwork for their operations before they start risking cover on doing things that they blatantly shouldn’t be doing.

In Flowers of Hell there’s an Embed that allows you to take a Legend long-term if that’s what you need. The downside is that you get the Imposter Condition, but if you don’t like that (and I don’t) it’s easy enough to toss aside!

                  As you pretty much say, the relationship between Cover and Unchained is a personal one that varies quite a lot. That said, I want to slightly refocus your statement: it’s not their initial cover that determines their abilities, but instead they were designed with a certain cover and mission in mind.

                  You have to remember that demons are fallen angels, and that angels are built for a specific purpose, not as chameleonic multi-tools; the angel’s assignment (and to a lesser extent the extreme transformation of the Fall) plays an important role in what mixture of skills they have relating to their cover versus what they needed to carry out the God-Machine’s orders. A deep-cover Messenger that’s got its skills mostly tied up in finance and business will probably want to stick to those fields until it branches out; a Destroyer with a flimsy cover built to be a war machine will probably want to stick to covers like cop, war veteran, or bodyguard if he wants his innate talent to be applicable. You might say that this means that a demon is a stereotype, but I’d rather say that Unchained mostly stick to types, and when they don’t they’re operating outside of their comfort zone. On paper this sounds limiting but, well, if different covers allowed demons to do wildly different things, then the Incarnations wouldn’t really work as strong archetypes in my opinion. Put another way: if a player didn’t want to play a combat badass OR experience what it’s like for a war machine to fight against its own nature and try to solve scenarios in ways it’s inexperienced with, why did the player choose to play a Destroyer?

                  That said, you’re playing one of reality’s greatest liars, with the ability to pick up skills at a whim, a silver tongue with which to manipulate those around you, and a bunch of powers to bend or break reality to do your bidding. When in doubt, take a high Subterfuge and fake it until you make it! ^_^

                      I'm liking Demon enough to toss in a few pennies worth.

                      First I'd like to say that after a few sessions, we've noticed at our table that compromise rolls flow like water compared to integrity and breaking points. At the same time, partial transformations, minor transgressions or just being in a high (such as your starting) cover rating simultaneously means you'll 'generally' go unnoticed for a 'long' amount of time... on average. Instead of an occasional check that may or may not break you, you're rolling consistently high pools against a steady erosion. That's the mechanics anyways.

                      Regarding stereotypes it might help not to treat your humans as so human from a player's perspective. In most WoD games, you are a human that one day transformed into something else. But not here. You're not playing a human at all, you're playing the bio and/or mechanical eldritch horror that's piloting the human form. People are your cover; that is to say your mundane identities (especially once you can have several), your paper-trail, all those cardboard boxes that say "nothing to see here, move along, no one under here's an ED-209". You're likely to pick a stereotype the way a mechwarrior picks gauss rifles. You choose to be Bob the comnservative factory worker the way a rogue trader picks a frigate. They're things you put in between you and the god machine, and if you use them really well and treat them really kindly they can last you years and years, but sometimes a Guardian just has to throw "janice the kindly babysitter" in the path of an oncoming hunter angel squadron, Eject or set it to go critical, and hope it can get the flagship out of dock before the smoke clears out.

                      In terms of engaging, I find it's a better 'Geist' in several ways. In (again) opposite to most other supernatural splats, Demons hit the ground running (oft quite literally) when it comes to the "of darkness" part of "world". They may be lacking details, but the challenge is learning to blend in, not expanding their world view. Everything you know is also tainted by probability calculations of God Machine relation and paranoia's at its highest. Anything that happens is "definitely related to you", whether it is or not. You want to keep your head down, sure, but you want to screw GM almost as hard, and every last ping on your radar comes off to a demon as a missile lock. With the right mindset, anything at all that happens will have their first thoughts be "oh fark it's probably infrastructure". Demons are semi proactive; you'll get out of your happy hidey hole only when something shows up as a threat, but everything related or connected, everyone you see or meet there should be viewed the way a cynical, not-particularly-good-aligned three-letter-acronym-director does. Always ask yourself "Can I use this and if so How?". No means eliminate it. Yes? Well, what are you waiting for? That new Gun/Ally/Cover/meatshield/safehouse/falling-angel is waiting for your ability to big-red-button it!

                      Leaving the 'hole' though is dirt-easy. You take a trip. You "fortuitously" obtained tickets, or reservations or a free dinner, or you have a meeting, or you were taking a walk when suddenly... If you remember that cover compromising acts are not actually all that rampant and that you're emulating/driving a chassis known for irrationality and spontaneity, You can ever so easily make your way to whatever it is you want to investigate. Or send friends! Or better yet send enemies! Demons have the abilities (listed and explained, no less) to more or less "metagame" on the fly. A demon has its cover laugh or cry the same way a player states his character does so, and often with about as little emotion off your own (or the demon's) face as someone waiting for the bus at five AM. Thinking 'as a player' can, in many ways, actually BE playing the demon in character.

                      Your last bit about cover was quite right: The rating is how powerfully ingrained in reality it is - tax returns, medical bills, the intense visceral hatred of that one postman on your route from the time you accidentally backed into his cart... It has nothing to do with whether you'd rather nuke this Rating 9 Hobo than the international supermodel at rank 5, and everything to do with how well built that specific tool is right now.

                      Also do go watch person of interest. The two main 'operatives' are spectacular examples of.cold and deadpan killing machines who talk about doing the right thing and how good it makes them feel the way you'd read a vacuum cleaner manual.
