    What does this have to do with Demons?

    02-13-2014, 02:42 PM
    On the one hand, I really like this splat. The world is literally the Matrix and "Angels" are the Agents who patrol and execute the processes of the Source. Sometimes those "Angels" go rogue and become "Demons" like Agent Smith, except that unlike Agent Smith you can barely defeat any other Agents and have to live your life paranoid that a bunch of Agents will hunt you down and recycle you. I love the thematic that they are hacking the source code of the universe, and every single one of them is a super spy who has to maintain his Cover (capital C) or risk his Cover being Compromised (capital C).

    On the other hand, what does any of this have to do with the established mythology and tropes of angels or demons? Demon: the Fallen at least tried to invoke the general mythology with their own spin on things, but this feels no more like angels or demons than a wolf who wears sheep's clothing claiming to be a sheep.

      I definitely see how this feels foreign. Strip away the mechanical/industrial flavors of the game, and you will see that the tropes are still there: Servants of The Creator (God) uphold God's will. Sometimes, however, the servants cannot fulfill their roll or don't want to. They fall, they now shakes their fists at their creator. To hide from Him they use contracts to avoid detection, sometimes even taking a person's soul.

      Now add in the 'flavor' of the game. God and its servants are so foreign to human kind that they project their limited reality upon God and its servants. In ancient times, it was miracles and divinity. Slowly, though, as time went on and mankind developed so to did the God machine. The GM looked like silver and gold rods and alchemical appearances during the middle ages. Later, it looked steampunk-like. Now, it appears to be alien/tech/Matrix-like.

      All that is flavor, however, since the angels are still angels, demons are still demons, and God (a cold, uncaring, distant God like in the old testament- more like a Demiurge) is God.

          It's a game that views a lot of the themes and tropes of fallen angels through the lens of the God-Machine mythos. You have former servants of a vast, unknowable creator who at one time rebelled against the plans and designs of said creator and fell, severing their connection to God and its Heaven.                           on a assumption I"m going to say you haven't yet had a chance to play demon. (Do it.)

                          the problem that I think isn't covered very well in the book is that there are two vastly different perspectives. Demons are part of the machine, they are in the know. to them magic is science, powers are loop holes in reality and The God Machine is an uncaring robot.
                          Humans don't see any of this; magic is magic no matter how many times the nice man inhabiting your husbands body explains it to you. Demons are lairs, perfect bloody liars always trying to manipulate you. Hell they literally want your soul or that's what the piece of paper they keep asking you to sign says.

                          God is control Demons are chaos, cogs and broken pieces.

                          There is an aesthetic triangle of all things Demon: Espionage, Tech, and Gnosticism

                          Espionage: in this case, is how Demons and angels act; Think burn notice, person of interest, mission impossible.
                          Tech: is appearance for infrastructure, angels and demonic forms; think Ghost in the Shell, Terminator, every thing out of the matrix in the matrix movies.
                          Catholic Gnosticism: is the subtext and underlining theme often brought to the forefront to add high weirdness to the world. frankly I don't have any perfect examples off the top of my head but mainly it's just less obvious details. I recently watched some X-files and noticed it there in some of the more religious episodes, How Robocop is Jesus in the first film, Lost, Battle Star Galatica, V is a perfect example, Jericho has it's moments, The First Wave. It's all about religious undertones and questions like what is God and what is faith.

                              Originally posted by Incendax View Post
                              So you were intentionally trying to invoke the idea of demons from other cultures? Is there a culture that displays their demons like this? Or is this a completely new mythology created specifically for the GMC?
                              The God-Machine mythos originates from a short story in the original nWoD core rulebook called "Voice of the Angel" on pages 26-31. After a decade of little easter eggs here and God-Machine related optional things there, the folks at OPP decided to write out the God-Machine Chronicles as an unofficial 2nd edition of the game as well as a default mortal campaign setting that fleshes out the God-Machine for use in your games.

                              Once it was fleshed out, they decided that they would approach the themes and tropes of angels and demons in Judeo-Christian mythology through the lens of the God-Machine mythology, leading to the game of Techgnostic Conspiracy that we have today.

                              So it's not so much that it deals with demons of different cultures as it is that since Fallen was already a game of Fallen Angels straight out of Paradise Lost, they wanted to approach the idea and themes from a different angle entirely.

                                          Well, here's a few things.
                                          One, the idea of the Machine Angel is creeping into pop culture more and more.

                                          Its not uncommon for Angels to be said to lack free will, to be cold emotionless beings.

                                          Another thing with Angels is that they are said to be agents of Fate and Order.

                                          Mix them all together, and you have machine angels, lacking free will or true emotion, that uphold the Order of their Creator. I can't count how many times the books say that the God Machine defends the Status Quo. That's Order, right there, every thing in its place.

                                          Demons are rebels. They're hunted down, not only because they're rebelling and necessary resources, but because their very presence upsets the God Machine's order. They're Angels of Chaos.

                                          Its not uncommon for angels in fantasy or modern occult stories to be ruled by a Council of Angels or a single powerful Angel instead of an all powerful Creator God.

                                          Angel VS Demon is a game about Security versus Freedom. The God Machine is the ultimate in the way of security - the loss of a few freedoms and lives in exchange for the health of the many. That said, it also robs humans of any choice, trying to bring them into machine like efficiency. So, demons are the chaos, the free will.


                                          All in all, the game has a ton of angelic stuff if you just ignore the whole technology bend. Which is, truthfully, a lot easier than you'd think. Demon form can look like an angel, a horned devil, a shining light, a cloak of the sky. Exploits are very much Wrath of Heaven or Infernal Trickery stuff. Pacts. Weakness of being found by heaven's "light" and slain by divine powers. War on Heaven.

                                              Yeah, it's really quite easy to run this as a traditional Demon game. Just replace all instances of "the God Machine" with "the Heavenly Host" and remove the technology elements if you like and you get a nice conventional demon setting. "Infrastructure" then is how the Heavenly Host and its angels influence the world through visions, chosen ones, churches and cults, occult places etc., following a grand design of their own, not fully understood by either mortals or the fallen ones.

                                              I have done this in my chronicle as I really like many of the basic concepts of this game, e.g. the Cover concept.

                                              Characters could be either angels just fallen or can be demons summoned by some mortal fool from the depths of hell who then agreed to a soul pact, not knowing that he paved the way for the demon to remain in this world using his identity as cover.
