    Where is the Infrastructure in your neighborhood?

    11-20-2015, 08:48 PM
    I've been meaning to do so for quite some time, but I finally had the perfect combination of good weather and a free afternoon that this little project demanded of me. You can find the full post on my blog here.

    In short, there are a bunch of random things in my neighborhood that look like the God-Machine's Infrastructure (or lie it has some other connection to the occult in the World of Darkness). So I took some pictures, wrote a brief explanation of what about them made me think "urban horror," and now I'm extending my invitation to all of you to tell me stories about these things or post pictures of the Infrastructure in your neighborhood.

    Here are just the photos and explanations:



    A bowling supply store (those still exist?), a Mercado, and a Masonic lodge (loudly announcing its presence with a huge sign?). Either this is the worst Concealment Infrastructure ever, or the Guardians of the Veil are trolling us.



    I drive by these every day – sometimes multiple times a day. I walked by them multiple times while taking William to the playground. They are clearly visible from less than a block away from my house. I didn’t notice them until I had lived here for three years, which still sort of weirds me out. Someone has spray-painted the letters UFO on the side. Somehow this only makes it even stranger that I didn’t notice them before.



    I’m not sure whether this served a practical function at some point (the building behind it used to be a train station and is now a library) or whether it’s someone’s art project (this is at the heart of the art district). What makes it strange is that you can’t see it from the street from any point within my neighborhood. It is only when you are standing across the river just beyond the border of the neighborhood that it is visible.



    A year ago there was a car wash here. It serves Tex Mex food but is decorated like an over-the-top country club. It has a Ferris wheel and a miniature golf course. A little odd, sure, but did you know that they didn’t demolish the car wash? They actually built the restaurant around it.

    Alright, your turn. Tell me what these landmarks “really” are. Or post your own pictures of weird stuff around town.

      The last two pictures got cut off due to limits on the number of pictures per post. Here they are:



      This picture is a bit fuzzy. It’s a drum store with an anti-theft steel grid inside its doors and windows. This isn’t interesting in itself, but the history of the site makes it a bit more remarkable. In the first three years that we lived here, this building was a graveyard for small businesses. Whenever a new shop would open here, you could be sure it would go out of business within three months. The location was absolute poison for no discernible reason (most of the other businesses in the area appear to be thriving). Then this drum store set up shop, and it has mysteriously survived the last three years. I’m not sure whether the owners are just plain more competent than the previous tenants or whether they just found a good exorcist soon after they moved in. Sorry the picture is upside-down, but I spent thirty minutes trying to get it to display properly, and it keeps showing up flipped the wrong way up. Make of that what you will.



      This is Little Jack’s. Longtime residents of the area tell me it was once one of the oldest restaurants in Minneapolis – an old school supper club that fell into its current sorry state after its original owners died. It has remained an island of blight in a sea of vibrant small businesses for decades. Soon after we moved here, a grand announcement went out to the neighborhood that a new owner had purchased the property and intended to develop it into a commercial and residential center. You can see they even put up a sign to show what it will look like once it’s finished. It has been several years, and the building hasn’t even been demolished yet, much less transformed into a new retail center. I’m not sure what the hold-up is, but it’s almost as if something or someone doesn’t want anyone to tear down the Little Jack’s building.

          Weirdest part is the shift from slow to fast seemed to happen all at the same time.

          May not be what you are looking for but a few of the other workers in the mall know about wod and we joke about the god machine being responsible for that and every other weird thing that happens in our mall (at least something once every 1-3 months). Weird often as something big happens but no one is ever hurt. I could give a list but to be honest after a while you just don't notice. So there must be concealment infrastructure that has numbed our perception within the mall and every incident is but a reinforcement of the infrastructure...

Is that why I and others simply do not dare peak behind the doors or go into its hidden depths? Do we know in the back of our minds to keep our head down and ignore it like the denizens of the world of darkness?

                          I live next door to a small factory. It has exactly one shift everyday (even stat holidays) 8-4. It employs a good number of people for a small factory with only one shift a day, somewhere in the 50-60 range. Then it gets weird, despite living next door to it I don't know anyone who works there. Asking around town no one seems to. Further more no one knows what company owns the factory or what they make. I've tried making small talk with employees on break every now and again but they just brush me off. Also they test their fire alarm every Monday at 8:15, no one leaves the building they just run the alarm until 8:30 and then shut it off.

                          Downtown by the harbor there is a large pole. About fifteen feet tall. On top of the pole is what looks like a wicker statue of growling dog. It is the only one of it's kind in the city and it sits there year round, even into the Christmas season when the rest of the harbor front is covered in Christmas lights and the creepy dog statue seems quite out of place. The lights go right up to the dog but do not touch it directly. This one strikes me more as something from Werewolf or Changeling but it's weird enough that it should probably be mentioned here too.

                          At the corner of 10th St East and 2nd Ave East, pretty much the highest traffic corner in the city there is an unusual storefront. Most of the time is closed and empty but every now and again some aspiring entrepreneur buys it and opens a sub shop. It is always a sub shop, never any other type of restaurant or other business. Once it was a Mr. Sub but it otherwise has never been part of a major chain. These sub shops always fall to ruin in short order. One was shut down after the police found it to be a front for a drug ring. Another time it got shut down for health code violations. The Mr. Sub had it's franchise license pulled and closed shortly after for no known reason. The most recent incarnation got so desperate for funds that a few months before shutting down there were looking for staff as volunteers instead of paid employees.
