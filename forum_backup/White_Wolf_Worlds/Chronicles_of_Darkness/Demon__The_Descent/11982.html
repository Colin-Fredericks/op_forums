          If you're asking "can I make something with as many mechanical options as the Unchained," that depends on how experienced a demon you're talking about versus the angel's Rank.

          If you're asking "can I make a compelling and complex character out of an angel," then the answer is absolutely. I run several different games that are all in the same continuity (so that characters in one city can hear about what happened in another city and so forth), and my one recurring NPC is an angel of sorts. (I don't want to say too much in case my players take a look at this.) For most of that NPC's existence - and certainly throughout our Demon playtest games - she was a normal angel. I didn't fully realize what type of angel she was until I played her a bit. And she's the one NPC that my players who play in more than one group see show up and immediately groan, knowing what sort of trouble is about to unfold.

                                          Am I the only one that gets a bit of an inconsistent thematic from Angels?

                                          They are supposed to be the ever-present threat to Demons. Slip up with your cover, do something obvious, get flagged and the hunter/killer Angel's will descend and you are boned.

                                          Yet in the infrastructure section it really stresses how hard it is to bring an Angel out of stasis and how much harder then that it is to make a new Angel. In another part of Chapter 1 its mentioned several times that Angel's spend the vast majority of their immortal existence in stasis, only brought out of such a state for specific task (via infrastructure). So it's kinda hard to see them being an ever present threat.

                                          This dissidence can be reconciled by an ST deciding that at any one time there are a lot more Angel's around the world then there are Unchained. Yet I don't think that's really all that implied in the core book. Likewise, its kinda a necessary conceit if you are going to explain how an Angel can always get to a compromised Demon in a short enough time that its going to be a problem for any Unchained without a backup or burn cover.

                                          Also the actual threat Angel's present is a bit inconsistent in the book. Sometimes they are presented and talked about as much more powerful, with abilities that Demons can only ape and raw power that even a Demon "going loud" can only just reach. Yet in other sections Demons take on multiple Angels at once and either win or force them to retreat. Which is a bit weird. I always got the impression that fighting an Angel was always a bad idea. Most of the time its more powerful then you and even if its not there is no point destroying it because there are always more on their way as long as the GM can find you e.g. being in Demonic Form.

                                          The second point feeds back into my first point. If Angels are actually pretty rare in the world and hard to even get out of stasis then fighting and destroying enough of them might be a legitimate tactic, as the GM may temporally "run out" of Angels in your immediate area (city). Fighting goes from being a last ditch tactic to a plausible plan to deal with the GM in the area. Which I don't feel fits with the "enemy agent behind enemy lines" feel of Demon.

                                          As a third point. How immortal are Angels? We know they don't age and don't need to eat, sleep and are immune to poisons and disease. Yet if a Demon destroy's an angel with physical might and supernatural power is the Angel dead? As this once again runs into the problem of the "hard to create new Angels" fluff. Demons could likely destroy more Angels then the GM could produce if a large Agency in an area made a push at it. Which once again seems to undermine the themes of Demon quite a bit.

                                          I guess my own thematic dissidence over this point springs from a preconception I have. After reading the Demon Quickstarter I very much characterized angels like the Agents of the Matrix films. They are stronger and faster then you. They have access to senses and information beyond you. They exist as a "core" of programming and essence. They inhabit the form they do for efficiency's stake. If you fight one, you might win through luck or skill or lateral thinking, but at best its a temporary respite. They will be back and the best you can do is inconvenience them long enough to escape and hide again. Demon's meanwhile are "mortal" trapped in the forms they fell in and trapped in the last cover they had. Their transient but immortal selves cut off from the God Machine making them mortal. Much like the exiled programs in the later Matrix films.

                                          "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny
