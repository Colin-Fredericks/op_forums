        Originally posted by orathaic View Post

        What would an Aswadim get from dealing with him?
        Imperial Mysteries offers the novel idea that the Old Man can act as a guarantor of neutral ground for meetings between Aswadim and other archmages (and perhaps facilitate interaction with Travellers that are similarly not affiliated with the Abyss).

        Still, for the most part, I think the point about engaging as archmasters do with any Aeon is on point. The capabilities of the Aeons and their willingness to offer it up does not change. What matters is that, as a being of godlike power oneself, one has a capacity to interact with them as a peer, a more sophisticated idea of what boons from the Aeons could be applied towards, and greater ability to offer goods and services in return.

        For an Aswadim, I'd say that the knowledge, powers, and beings that can be drawn from the Abyss are about the same as they would be for any mage. The most significant difference is in application (since Aswadim have a very specific agenda of using the infinity of the Abyss to reshape the finite to their aspirations). Apart from that, I would suggest that the capabilities and position of respect might mean that the Old Man is more willing to take payment in a form that is external to the mage, rather than inflicting the Abyss through their pattern.

        I'd find it fitting if, a lot of the time, the Old Man wants things that bring complications or obstacles to fulfilling the Obsession that is the point of bargaining in the first place. This isn't done as some kind of monkey's paw thing, or out of malice; it's just an expression of paradox. A lot of the time he might actually be upfront about exactly what they're being asked to spend, the consequences it will have.

        I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                                        Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                        The problem with Sciening it out, is mostly "this is not how magic works".
                                        Magic doesn't have to work on scientific principles, but you can still use magic to affect things in a technological fashion; making a battery that doesn't lose its stored energy over time is less overall effective than simply giving unlimited power to a battery-powered object, but it's still something that can be done.

                                        (Ignoring the fact that the Mage writers seem to think a Locus is a location, not an object).
                                        Because for the purposes of Mage's mechanics, a locus is the area of influence around the focal object or person in which the Gauntlet is thinner, compatible spirits heal faster, and Reaching can be done without the Manifestation of the same name.

                                        Werewolf itself specifies that even if the focal point of a locus is destroyed, unless the Essence it contained is quickly removed from the Resonant area it will settle into a new item in a few weeks; Lore of the Forsaken notes that the heart of a locus isn't readily discernible from the pale around it, and its section on non-standard loci, while partially outdated due to the introduction of the Waystone Wolf-Blooded Tell in 2e (which is nevertheless just a "for the purposes of Reaching" locus), notes that most locuses are part of the location they're in and don't move the boundaries of their area of effect with the relocation of the heart on an immediate timescale, as well as forwarding the concept of a "locus of place" that has no division between its core and area of influence.
