                I've said it before, and I'll say it again; The Protomen are perfect for Demon. They're a rock opera group with a very 80s feel and their music is loosely inspired by Mega Man. The album worth listening to (Act II: The Father of Death) is about a pair of men who create robots in order to make the world a better place. One of them goes mad with power and creates an oppressive dystopia, the other is forced underground and gets shit on repeatedly. The recurring antagonist in Act 2 is a terrifying robot called Sniper that feels like great Angel inspiration, and the whole album is hot-blooded enough to feel like a collection of Saboteur anthems.

                                                Inspirational music thread, I summon you! Rise from your grave to rescue my daughter!

                                                Mind you, this is a bit of a cheat. While the music is a driving techno beat suitable for Demon, the real highlight here is the music video. Warnings: mild robot nudity, extreme violence, epilepsy-very-unsafe.


                                                I don't remember where I discovered Ken Ishii through the music video for his 1995 track Extra, above, and I suspect it was from some thread on these forums, really, but I double-checked to make sure it wasn't this thread in particular. This is an abstract animated short by the director of Akira, and it shows; it's a cyberpunk fever-dream suitable for especially nihilistic, grungy or futuristic games of Demon in particular. Society grows wild and decadent; people wear paper bags over their heads and become faceless; technology is exploitative and abusive behind a facade of excess; rogue machines run rampant and predatory for unclear reasons and yet end up seeming the lesser threat; none of these are new ideas (and they show some nineties fearful exaggeration similar in tone to the Classic World of Darkness if that's your thing), but the visuals are fantastically realized.

                                                In an oddly fitting way, while some of the designs seem suitable inspiration for angelic and demonic forms, the figures in the video that remind me the most of the Unchained are the most ordinary, relatable human figures. The girl on the tricycle and the boy in the goggles are placed in roles that seem to oppose the wild predation of the setting, but they seem to be serving their own goals rather than straightforward self-preservation or banding together; they're free agents who seem safer to be around for any hypothetical bystanders, but not any less dangerous in their own ways. Questions of identity emerge; they might all be the same person, or at least, it seems heavily implied that the girl is not just a little girl but a facade hiding an armed and skilled "player."

                                                Take what you will.
