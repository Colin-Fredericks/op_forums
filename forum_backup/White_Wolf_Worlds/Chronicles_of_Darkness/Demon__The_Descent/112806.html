Are there any other new WOD creature/core books that would be good to use against Demons?                               Your question now has me brainstorming an excellent crossover chronicle which I would love to play/run now: Demon / Changeling (and it'd work well from either perspective) [you'd probably want the CtL core and Autumn Nightmares, though]. It'd make an interesting long story arc (particularly if immediate problems popped up in between) of discovering another group, befriending over common goals, and then devolving into terrible enemies.

                              On the surface, changelings and demons have a lot in common: they're both hunted by otherworldly beings more powerful than they are, they both try to maintain "covers" amongst humanity, and they're both on personal quests toward freedom / self-acceptance. They'd have a lot they can teach each other in these goals as well, particularly because of the key differences. For example, True Fey are a lot more rare to see than angels and live in a separate realm, but loyalists infiltrating a group is a lot more common; their magic (mostly) doesn't attract attention from their enemies, but when they're hunted its much harder to hide.... If demons and changelings were to trade tactics, it wouldn't be a direct translation, but it would help both groups in their respective spycraft.

                              [to help the groups recognize each other initially, I would houserule that Changelings see a Demons' covers as thin veneers of identity, like their own Masks, but with nothing underneath - which does /not/ happen. Demons, watching long enough, would notice changelings trying to maintain a cover while pursuing a supernatural life, and being fairly cavalier about using their "Exploits" and "demonic forms".]

                              This would quickly devolve, of course. Once the changelings learn about the Pacts demons make for Cover, and once demons start thinking of Arcadia as a place to pursue Hell, the changelings would immediately become hostile. And they'd be a super fun antagonist for demons: both groups are sneaky blighters which have to worry about being discovered by their respective Enemies - Changelings have more freedom to cut loose because their Enemy doesn't really take notice to displays of power (unless they're already looking), but Demons, from what I can tell, have more powerful abilities outright. It'd be more like tag or cat-and-mouse than open combat, using tricky shenanigans, crossplans and cats paws, distractions and baiting ....

                              In a nutshell, I'd say Changeling has a lot of potential in Demon as sympathetic villains / former allies, and for heavy tactics warfare. It could require being very familiar with both games, though.

                                          [Edit: Sorry about the format; my Chromebook doesn't like these forums much...]

                                          In terms of running a purely Demon the Descent / God Machine chronicle, vampires as a secondary template probably integrate the easiest; as they're heavily focused on human cities and power structures as part of their own survival and politics, they easily can become huge thorns and powerful (unwitting) pawns to the God Machine. I'd probably use them as a pretty powerful force which require a delicate touch: when the GM is subtle, it can use them to accomplish A Lot, but as soon as they sense a direct hand doing something Not-Their-Agenda, they resist and quite effectively undermine the unwitting human infrastructure, just because they're paranoid and petty.

                                          With werewolves, I would approach from the perspective of "the God Machine wants the world to be Dark" and "the God Machine harnesses Essence for its occult matrices", and relating it to the spirit world. From the first part, the GM seeks to let spirits into the world, makes sure their are hosts and claimed (and werewolves) preying upon humanity and making the world dark, dangerous, and miserable. From the second part, the GM also seeks to exploit spirits and the spirit world for its own designs. I would play the werewolves as mostly unaware that another force is messing with things: the occult matrices don't make impacts on the Shadow and angels leave them alone; but perhaps a pack clues in that a lot of loci/verges keep appearing, the hosts seem oddly well equipped and aided by the city government, spirits sometimes get messages from angels (or kidnapped by them). They could be unwitting elimination infrastructure or a factor of chaos (both to demons and to the God Machine), with the potential of a tight-rope thin alliance, if you can sit down and talk with them.

                                          Changeling is one of my favorite gamelines, though, and I think it has -tons- of crossover potential with Demon. In addition to my above scenario, Changelings have one myth that the True Fey are "half fallen" angels - those that didn't take sides between heaven and hell [and if you make a relationship between Titles and Cover, there may be enough truth to tempt a demon into pursue Hell straight into Arcadia]. Changeling also introduces a force which could potentially rival the God Machine; the Wyrd is the tangled skein of fate, a narrative force which weaves Stories out of peoples' lives. It mostly affects the fae-touched, isn't sentient (we think), and doesn't have any motives which interfere with the God Machine, but it could possibly become a force to rival the GM if a cult of changelings and demons tries the right things - and if it doesn't warp out of control and spiral everyone's lives into madness first.

                                          "Second Sight" I might also recommend as supplemental figures for a Demon game. Off the top of my head, Psychics being a consequence of the GM's workings and its occasional tools; Thaumaturges being able to study Occult Matrices and design their own rituals from it; Cults variably serving beings that could be greater servants of the GM or rival gods that it banished -- those are pretty stereotypical roles, and far from the full potential in that book, but.

                                          Yeah those are the first gamelines I would look at to make a demon game more dynamic and convoluted and expansive. Mummy sounds like it has great potential too; I'm not very familiar with it but Oddness' idea sounded awesome, and I bet the Judges and the GM have a contentious history behind the curtains of the world. For me, though, Changeling wins in terms of crossover potential.

                                                      Sorry to reup the thread, but I saw this a while ago, and wanted to say what I had to say correctly.

                                                      William Wohlforth would call Vanilla Demon: the Descent a "unipolar world". The G-M is the sole superpower in the setting and is without equal. Compare this with Werewolf: the Forsaken, where the Uratha have deal with multiple threats on multiple fronts. (The upcoming Idigram Chonicles takes this and runs with it) There are other threats in the main DtD book (Stigmatics, Cryptids, Sleepers) as well other imported splats that are a possible threat (hunters anyone?) but none are the howling, existential threat that is the G-M.

                                                      Demon is storytelling game. When you add something to a game, you have to within the rules of story and it has to give you new ways to tell the story. If you want a a new enemy, it should resonate with the existing themes of DtD. It should serve as a foil to both the characters, and to the G-M, which is very much a character in the native; the G-M is the whip that drive DtD.

                                                      Themes:
                                                      Technognosis. Mystical insight is the objective. The characters are castoffs thrown into a world that does not make sense. They have to look at the machine within and the machine without to obtain knowledge of themselves.

                                                      Espionage: Tradecraft is the means. The characters are spies, their ability to function depends on secrecy, subterfuge and guile. Why do this? Partially because of the nature of there abilities (lying, cover, etc), but more than that is the disparity in power. even the most powerful Unchained can be brought down.

                                                      Originally posted by miss_minty View Post
                                                      Demon is an espionage game so I don't understand "the party gets used to the combats" considering combat isn't something the Demon jumps for joy over.
                                                      Perfect. Demons operate through stealth and preparation. Our new antagonists need to reinforce this, we want organizations that operate at tier 3, even if the players engage them at Tier 1, maybe Tier 2 for chapter climaxes.

                                                      So:
                                                      Recommended threat 1: The Seers of the Throne. In contrast to the G-M, these "mere" humans. In contrast the unchained, the SotT wield vast (but hidden) power over reality, both through magic, and through vast economics and political pulling strings. And... these guys want it all; there is no enough, only more. This contrasts with Demons' own search for a purpose and meaning, these guys now exactly what they want and are will hurt anyone to get it, and their raison d'etre is to deny enlightenment to others,

                                                      In G-M chronicle, the obvious new goal of the SotT to usurp the G-M. And they just might be able to pull it off.

                                                      Worse, they understand human motivations in ways that the G-M and the unchained can't. The G-M can recruit human servitors, and understands that person X plus bribe Y equals desired outcome Z, but is ignorant of the mathematics that drive that particular equations. The Demons have defected to the human race but they're not human, and often have very different needs and thought processes.

                                                      The SotT play foil to both the Inquisitors and the Tempters. The Watchers information, the Seers have it. The Decadents want temporal power, the Seers have it. Furthermore, the question of how such an organization has been able to operate for however long under the G-M’s nose is something that both agendas will want to answer.

                                                      I recommend re-skinning them when your players run into them, ( say the Illuminati), or better yet give them layers and layers of masks. This is an espionage game, the players should not have find out about this enemy through their sourcebook on drivethruRPG

                                                      Recommended threat 2: Go outside the traditional WoD and steal Nemesis from Jim Butcher’s Dresden Files. Brief rundown: Nemesis is an entity that infiltrates the minds of victims and turns them to the service of wiping out all reality at the behest of the Outsiders. In short, it hollows out souls and wears them as suits. Does this remind you of anyone?

                                                      In addition to that hopefully upsetting bit of symmetry, Nemesis is good foil to the G-M. The G-M is mechanical and seeks to preserve the status quo, Nemesis is organic and seeks nihilistic annihilation.

                                                      It also shakes up the dynamic between Integrators and the other agendas. In vanilla DtD the Integrators are regarded with barely concealed condescension, contempt, and distrust by other agendas. Hell, their agenda beat condition is “Make the rest of your ring distrust you.” Give the game a second existential threat in opposition to the G-M and all of sudden, the other agendas are forced to consider “what if these guys are right? What if the G-M is the necessary evil?” For Saboteurs, their objective is now longer something regarded as a quixotic goal that most of the agendas are indifferent to. “What if they pull it off? What then?”

                                                      Add both of these threats and players are no longer behind enemy lines, they are behind enemy lines in the middle of a war.

                                                      Hope that helps. Happy Storytelling.
