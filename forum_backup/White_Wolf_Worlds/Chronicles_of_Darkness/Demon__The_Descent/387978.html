    Dark Eras: Cold War Berlin

    02-01-2015, 12:18 AM
    I just read the Dark Eras KS preview. No surprise that Berlin during the frostiest part of the Cold War is a shoo-in, and there’s a lot to like.

    The connection between the G-M and the Loyalists of Thule is brilliant from a story-teller perspective. Unlike the primary anti-demon Hunter faction, the Knights of St. Adrian, the Loyalist are scholars, researchers and nobody’s dupes (well… depending on your chronicle…). They have a vast store of occult knowledge, including the basics of occult engineering, putting them on the short list of Hunters who are even remotely equipped to handle Infrastructure or Facilities. They operate in much the same manner as Demons do and that makes them brilliant foils.

    The Loyalist could be allies, of opportunity or convenience if not ideology, to the Unchained. G-M designs and misinformation setup the disaster in 1933, so the Loyalists have a common adversary, if they can be convinced that there is a distinction between the Unchained and their former master. Additionally, an Unchained could sign onto the Loyalists’ vision of Thule, as their personal version of Hell. The Loyalists already have the blueprints, saving the Unchained enormous effort… if they just make sure it’s free of the G-M’s influence this time around.
    Other things I like: listing other RPGs in the inspirational materials. Onyx Path has done this before and other RPG lines are doing it was well, but I’m glad to see more and more cross-pollination in the industry.

    As excited as I am about the Thule connection, I have concerns. The What Has Come Before establishes that a G-M plot started a chain of events that lead to one of the darkest eras (heh) in human history by manipulating the organization that would become the Hunter compact, the Loyalists of Thule. The Loyalist involvement with the Nazi party is established canon on the nWoD, but including an overtly supernatural element like G-M to the Holocaust, even as an indirect actor, is risky territory.

    The Holocaust is still remembered by hundreds of thousands of survivors, many more lost friends and family members in the Second World War. Any and all of whom might take serious offense at the notion that these death were somehow an occult or conspiratorial event. Kenneth Hite wrote a killer essay for Achtung Cthulhu about this risk, some of which he discusses online here (http://frabjousdave.com/creative-col...-kenneth-hite/). A line of special importance:
    “Suddenly their 20 million victims weren’t murdered by humans, but instead were killed by a freak amoral accident, or died of a plague spread by orcs.” It becomes a tragedy, not a sin.
    The Loyalists of Thule are humans making human decisions with horrifying consequences, so their previously established involvement doesn’t ping my radar as much. Also, the writing team avoids of absolving mortal responsibility in the matter, which I appreciate.

    Some things that I hope to see:

    A Rashomon with the Widdershins Windmill. As is, we’re told explicitly that it was a G-M plot. A situation were any and every faction might responsible gives an over-arcing mystery to investigate, one that every party has a stake in (“The Widdershins Windmill was a Saboteur plot that went array and started this entire mess.” “I have verified and credible reports that the Integrators were trying to perform a hard reset of the G-M, try and fix the errors in it’s programming. Went completely south.” “You’re both wrong, an Exile was trying to get instructions from the Machine. It worked.” “I’ve read the Oera Linda Book, all four translations, there's no sign of God Machine interference or involvement”) Give the setting a murky past to complement the unstable present and the looming future threat of nuclear war. It would also defuse some of my concerns with the Nazis + Supernatural tropes.

    More Uncertainty and Paranoia. “Assume Nothing” is a credo that resonates with both the setting of DtD and the Cold War. Mix them together and you should get a wilderness of mirrors where even reality itself cannot be trusted.

    More Weird. Much of the real world information can be obtained on Wikipedia or in any number of novels or history books. Historical information isn’t as interesting or as useful to me as game information and systems are. The vignettes of little bits of strangeness included in each section is pure gold and I want more. What did World of Darkness Cold War looked like? What plots involving the G-m and other beasties looks like? In short, if I can run a bog standard nWoD game with ordinary mortals (not Hunters) confronting unique supernatural horrors in the Cold War, I’ll consider this section an unqualified and total success.

    Splinters and Nuclear Scenarios. Great thing about Demon the Descent? Time travel and alternate time lines. If the G-M wants to nuke a city, or the entire world, it damn well can and to hell with “established history”. The players can screw up, turn the Cold War hot, and have front row seats as Berlin becomes a sea of glowing ash.

    Then the Machine steps in.

    Maybe everyone is recreated by the G-M, including the players’ Covers. Now a real, flesh-and-blood, vanilla mortal is running around thinking he’s the person a Cover pretends to be (Compromise ho!).

    Maybe the G-M creates and repopulates the earth with a completely new batch of people who have no memory of the destroyed world (your mortal friends and family? All dead. Don’t worry though, the G-M has graciously provided replacements). They all have complete histories and memories. They all believe that they existed for their entire lives. They forget the nightmares when the wake up.

    Maybe the G-M can reset the last four days and send a hunter angel after whoever cause the problem (the PCs, because they caused it. They wanted an extra beat so they traded a failure for a dramatic failure during ) the characters now have to correct their mistake while trying to avoid the angel homing in on them. The possibilities are endless, and horrifying.

    The Loyalists of Thule recruited, sometimes forcefully, into the the US, UK and USSR intelligence apparatuses, a la Operation Paperclip. They’re too useful as antagonists, allies and foils. Give the Loyalists access to CIA/Mi-6/KGB resources, send them out against the G-M and Unchained alike, then watch the sparks fly.

    More Cryptids! The Reptilians are perfect for this setting, and desirable mercenaries/slaves/victims to all sides.

    More Sleepers! If there was ever a time when Sleeper agents could cause a demon nightmares, it’s here.

    For the approval of the forum community I submit more resources and inspiration: The Spy Museum Podcast has a number of great episodes for this Era, Ghost Projeckt by Joe Harris and Steve Rolston is not set in the Cold War, or even Berlin, but deals with the legacy of a Cold War attempt to weaponize the supernatural and the atmosphere oozes World of Darkness. Queen and Country: Declassified Vol 1. by Greg Rucka tells the story of a border extraction gone horribly wrong. Cold War Memories: A Retrospective on Living in Berlin: A City Divided--Memoirs of U.S. Dependents in Berlin has great information about living in Berlin during the period, and Black Market, Cold War: Everyday Life in Berlin, 1946-1949 by Paul Steege gives high-level detail on how post-war Berlin became Cold War Berlin.

              Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
              I absolutely adore the setting. I fucking hate that they wrote the God-Machine as being behind the rise of the Nazi Party.
              The God-Machine was not behind the rise of the Nazi Party. It was only one of many factors, and I'm sorry if that did come across for you in the text. Think of it this way:

              There's this bit in Le Morte d'Arthur wherein the forces of Arthur and of Mordred meet under a flag of truce to discuss a possible peace. During those tense moments one of the knights drew a sword to kill an adder that might otherwise have bitten him. All the enemy knights regarded this as a declaration of war, and so a battle was wrested from the jaws of peace. You can interpret this as a tragedy brought on by a single man's ill-considered actions (which it also is), but what it really shows is just how tightly wound everyone there was. If it hadn't been an adder it might have been an arrow accidentally let loose from bowstring or a horse that got spooked and charged toward the enemy line or any of a thousand other innocuous events that could be misconstrued as open hostility. Even if the troops didn't make any mistakes, those negotiating peace might have failed to reach any agreement, and the battle would have happened anyway.

              Now imagine that the God-Machine knew that one specific knight loyal to Mordred (call him Sir Matt) needed to die in order to complete a key piece of Infrastructure. It doesn't care about Arthur or Mordred or anyone else for that matter. As long Sir Matt lives, the occult matrix will never be complete. The God-Machine runs the numbers, analyzes its options, and seeks a way to off Sir Matt while expending as few of its resources as possible. The war between Arthur and Mordred is already underway. The king has already driven Lancelot out of Camelot, and maybe some of Arthur's knights aren't too happy about it and would turn against the king if not for this immediate problem of a red hot civil war. The Machine doesn't pay close attention to all that background, though. It simply becomes aware that Sir Matt is on the front lines of one of two armies facing each other on opposite sides of a battlefield, and according to its calculations Sir Matt has a 99% chance of dying in any battle that unfolds that day. So it sends an angel in the body of an adder to startle one of the knights - it doesn't matter which one. Sir Matt dies in the resulting melee, and the fact that Mordred dies and Arthur is fatally wounded and all of Camelot collapses in the aftermath of the battle is purely incidental to the God-Machine's plan.

              Is the God-Machine then responsible for the fall of Camelot? Kind of yes but mostly no. Camelot was on death's door. The fact that Mordred's open rebellion had been so successful to that point makes that pretty clear. The battle wasn't inevitable per se, but it was was very nearly unavoidable, and a treaty forged on that battlefield probably would not have changed the outcome (Camelot's fall and Arthur's death) in the long term.

              In the same vein, the God-Machine did not create the Nazis. It didn't create the economic hardships and demographic pressures that made fascism so appealing to a sufficiently large and influential percentage of Germans. Saboteur paranoia to the contrary, It certainly didn't establish death camps or order the firebombing of Dresden or trick Hitler into invading Russia. Humans did all those things on their own and in response to their own hopes, fears, and prejudices. Herr Arger's message was meant to inspire the Thule Society to build a piece of Infrastructure and to protect it as though it were their only hope of escape from a really shitty time and place to be living in. He didn't tell them why they were chosen or how they could prove themselves. That came from the Thule Society, and they did some undeniably shitty things in an effort to convince some assholes in a shining city to take them away from it all.

              Would the Nazis have risen to power without the support of the Thule Society? Quite possibly - and if not them, another group like them. I don't want to go all deterministic, though. I'll allow that maybe it wouldn't have gone down that way at all. Maybe Germany would have limped along for ten or twenty or thirty more years and nothing as terrible as World War II would have taken place. But like the adder in Le Morte d'Arthur, nobody can know how it would have turned out - least of all the Unchained.

              Reading about the Able Archer incident we get a glimpse of the opposite side of this sort of moment in history. In that case, one man saw the proverbial adder at his ankles and made a conscious decision to let it bite him because he knew that if he drew his sword it would mean war. History often unfolds this way - a combination of large scale pressures, individual actions, and freak accidents. Throwing in a Machine that occasionally nudges events in small ways to achieve Its inscrutable goals without regard for its effects on human history just adds one more force of Nature to a world that already has plenty of them playing merry havoc on the "predictable" outcomes of history.

              As has already been said, anything we wrote that included a reference to the Holocaust - however appropriate to the era in question - was likely to touch a nerve. I've been to Auschwitz, but I won't even pretend to have the first fucking idea what it must have been like to been there when it was active. You can't have post-war Berlin without references to war, and you can't have references to that war without at least acknowledging the shadow of the terrible events that happened during it. I wanted to have some tenuous link between the Loyalists of Thule and the Unchained (because they're a great Compact), but I tried not to belabor it or reduce it to something as insulting as "the God-Machine made them do it." I hope I have not failed in that too badly, and if I have, I'm deeply sorry.
