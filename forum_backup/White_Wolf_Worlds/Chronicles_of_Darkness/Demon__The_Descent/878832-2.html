                                                            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                            It kinda does, actually.

                                                            Was the hitching of the "mid-level schemer gets dragged by the drones into the reconfiguration chamber desperately screaming about how they can't do this to him" trope to unlucky God-Machine patsies not an obvious tip-off that this efficiency-minded neither-cruel-nor-kind System only displays a humanlike semblance of attachment as a function of subterfuge or risk-management that promptly vanishes the instant it turns out to be less trouble to just scrap that branch of the Plan and find a workable alternative?
                                                            That's one story of the G-M, sure. It doesn't tell us what it was thinking, if it had regret about having to reconfigure that human, or if it felt that the reconfigurement wasn't destruction, but repair. Heck, the G-M having feelings is a great explanation as to why it's so faulty; maybe parts of it feel attachment to its minions and try show mercy, other parts are utterly efficient automata and destroy anything imperfect, other parts take glee in vengence or destruction or memes or whatever. The only facts that're consistent about the G-M is that it is unknowable and that it is imperfect. What the truth behind it is, and what form that imperfection takes, is an exercise for the ST/player/reader/chronicle. For some, it being the ultimate pragmatist is the best story; for others, being a moody and capricious deity is.

                                                            I can't prove the G-M gets attached to humans. But I don't think you can prove that it doesn't.
