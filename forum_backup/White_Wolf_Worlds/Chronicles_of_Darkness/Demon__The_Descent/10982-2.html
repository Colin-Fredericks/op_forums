    I'm making one last contribution to the "should Knockout Punch be toned down?" discussion. Because I have a strict personal limit of three posts in any internet argument about elfgames. Also, I really want to avoid the impression of being antagonistic, because that is really not my goal here.

    First, the fact that you are discouraged from killing the people you knocked out doesn't make it much less of an "I Win Button". You could walk past the unconscious Big Bad and disrupt his nefarious plot. You could just run away and escape from the unconscious destroyer angel who had tracked you down. Hell, if this person really needs to die, you could take his indefinitely unconscious body somewhere else, restrain him and kill him after he wakes up sometime later. On the rare occasions that none of these will do, you could take the hit to cover. It is manageable if only done occasionally.

    @Blackhatmatt: You suggested that the issue might be that you've played with one kind of player group and I with another. That is totally, absolutely the case here. My group includes three players who usually try to build strong combat characters and look for optimal ways to do that. The other three are agnostic about this, they could be good in combat or not. They don't much care. If someone else in the group is way better at combat than they are, they aren't too bothered. I'm guessing most of your players are like these latter three of mine. Cool.

    Your kind of group isn't unusual in my experience, but neither is mine. So probably the best thing to do would be to find a solution that both kinds of groups would be happy with.

    Let's assume that Knockout Punch stays the way it is. Your group is obviously cool with that. In mine, though, I'm going to need to houserule it (and so will the many other groups that have player distributions like mine). I've outlined the reasons above: KP is such a great combat power relative to the others that all three of my combatant types are going to gravitate toward it, whether at first or after seeing it in action. Combat encounters will be Knockout Punch marathons. I'll need to add the resistance roll to put it on the same level as other options.

    But what happens if KP gets a contested supernaturat resistance trait roll added to it before the game goes to press? Well, my group can play without any houserules required, and so can the other groups that are similar to mine. KP remains a great power, but the resistance roll means that it is not quite so reliable against supernatural antagonists. It has benefits and drawbacks. Maybe a given player wants that one, maybe she wants other stuff instead. Diverse character powers are the result. Awesome.

    And your group and groups like them? They are cool with this too! They aren't too bothered about optimal combat stuff, so whether or not KP allows for supernatural resistance rolls doesn't impact them too much either way. They'll take it or not, depending on whether it fits the fiction of the character concept that they have decided upon. Diverse character powers are the result. Awesome.

    So, this being the case, doesn't it make sense to add the supernatural resistance roll to KP? It is the solution that everyone can be happy with.

    Thanks for engaging with the fans over this stuff. We just like your games and want to help out to smooth rough edges when possible. And since Demon hasn't gone to print yet, we feel like this is the perfect time to try to get this sort of thing addressed.

                Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post

                Influences and numina go away after the scene ends (IE knocked out and combat is over) unless he permanently has 5 armor as an innate feature. Compromise rolls don't matter when the person you've killed is dead.
                This is very much A TAKE on that situation. I don't however, believe it to be the default take of Demon (if such a thing exists).

                Firstly, as the ST its entirely up to you if the scene is "over" once combat is over. Sometimes that might be thematically appropriate, other times, not so much.

                Secondly, the fact that you loose cover is a much bigger deal then killing one angel. There are always more angels. You managed to take down a single hunter-killer, congratulations. With your cover reduced the chances that you'll have to face more of them later goes up. Knock Out Punch or no, you can't survive 5 hunter-killer rank 3+ angels descending on your position when you next screw up.

                Thirdly, to my knowledge Angel's don't fall unconscious. So Knock Out Punch does not likely work on them.

                In general I don't think every power needs to quantify every aspect of itself, people can use "common sense" and a general idea of the games thematics to guide them a lot of the time. If something has no unconscious state e.g. Vampires, Geists, Mummy's, Prometheans, Ghosts, Gentry, Spirits, Angels and Demons in their True Form, then it cannot be put in such a state by a power designed to knock-out things out. Things that can be knocked out e.g. Humans, Werewolves, Changelings, Mages can be. Template features always override powers unless otherwise directly stated, for example, a power that negates magical regeneration specifically e.g. Kiss of Apep from Mummy, would negate a Werewolf's healing factor.

                Does this make Demons "better" against some other supernaturals compared with others. Yeah, but none of these games have ever been cross-balanced. Nor should they be, as it would likely hurt the feel and thematic of each.

                Sorry if any of that came off as overly confrontational. Just that when I was reading the embed section of the book I found the preface to it that talks about making powers work with game tone and thematics pretty much cleared up any vagaries for me. With Matt talking about the stuff on here only backing up that interpretation. So I guess I just got a little frustrated that other people seem to not get this idea of "common sense" and narrative function with powers. I know its not everyones cup of tea and I should be more understanding of it, but after it's an argument I see repeated for three pages I felt the need to highlight the point.

                        End of the fight isn't really the end of the scene, although it's close. The scene ends when the characters transition to a new location. That's what a scene is at it's core. If you get into a shootout at a diner and the cops come, the scene ends when the characters decide to cheese it and run to a new location. Aftermath of a fight is just as much part of the same scene unless there is some sort of greater transition. (Notably that transition can be emotional or symbolic rather than physical, so while the characters are in the same physical location the nature of that location has changed enough that it's effectively a new scene. My point still stands though.)

                                @Matt: In many ways you are correct. If the players wish to play Demon-as-unstoppable-monster and the ST is okay with running that type of game, then they should be able to. More importantly, Demon: the Descent is the type of game where that should be possible. Likewise, white-room balance is significantly less important that actual story balance. However, you ask a very insightful question I'd like to turn around. If I am playing the aforementioned rampaging Demon, what effect does taking Knockout Punch have on the story we are telling? How does that effect presented in that way add to the theme of the rampaging demon, or make playing it out more interesting for everyone at the table?

                                As a ST, it is my job both to provide the players with challenges that are outside their expertise or require lateral thought. It is also my job to give the players opportunities to show off and present challenges that are inside my purview. If I have a player who is a rampaging monster Demon, it is important to supply mooks who are eaten and great monsters that are worthy challenges to their skills. What tools do I have to challenge a combat-specced Demon in an interesting way with the material presented? How can I do so without making the rest of the group feel useless.

                                In the case of Demon, this is of a particular concern. Part of Demon-as-presented is this idea that you are being actively hunted by a tremendous physical threat--Angels. A Demon's ability to mitigate that threat has a rather direct impact on the stories told.

                                    To address Epimetheus's point about knocked out enemies -

                                    I don't remember reading anywhere that Angel's get knocked out in general. Hell, even in their cover they don't eat or sleep unless they choose to.                                     Just be absolutely clear to your players when you start the game that Knock Out Punch does not work on things that can't be knocked out. In a pure Demon game that's Humans, Stigmatics, some Cryptids and Demons in Cover which you get the drop on (once again I am pretty sure Demon's can't fall unconscious in their true forms). .

                                    This also kinda deals with one of SoulGambits issues as well. As knock-out punch is no longer useful against Angels then it does nothing to hurt the idea that they are a very dangerous physical threat if confronted.

                                        So, I have no idea if this little anecdote will help or hurt, but I'm gonna do it anyway.

                                        When I was running the SAS, the characters wound up talking to Simon Flannery (the main antagonist). They knew he was a demon, and so they knew they couldn't trust anything he said, because you can't ever tell if a demon is lying, but they weren't sure exactly what he was up to and how much they should get involved. And one of the characters used Freudian Slip on him.

                                        Pause time for a moment, and gaze into the Stygian abyss of my brain as I'm Storytelling.

                                        Should this power work...from a story perspective? Yes, absolutely. The narrative flow of the scene was leading to a confrontation. The pacing of the game was leading to this being the last or next-to-last session (turned out to be the next-to-last). The characters already suspected Simon was shady, they just needed a little more information.

                                        Should this power work...from a mechanics perspective? Not really. Freudian Slip specifically says it doesn't work on demons (though in fairness, I think I added that note after I ran this game). You can't make a demon do anything honestly, even a knee-jerk reaction.

                                        So why did I let it work? (Well, because it was a playtest, but if I were running the game now, I'd do the same thing.)

                                        For one thing, because the player was excited about it. If I said 'no,' I'd have been stepping on her toes. That's bad form as an ST.
                                        For another, because it was the best thing for the story.
                                        And for yet another, because in this context, it made sense. Simon is a low-Cover demon and a dedicated Integrator. He was on edge anyway, and there was so much magic flying around at that particular point in the game that it wasn't hard to imagine that his control might be a little wonky. And, Simon is a self-sabotaging sort of demon anyway.

                                        My point in all of this is not (just) to say "fiddle with the rules to serve the story;" I think we can take that as read. My point is that it's not unreasonable for the Storyteller to examine a situation in context and make decisions on how a power works within that context. It's not unreasonable for a player to say, "Hey, I think this should work this way" and for the Storyteller to say, "sure, that's cool, but it normally doesn't work that way, so don't get used to this." It's not unreasonable for a Storyteller to say, "this power doesn't specifically say it works/doesn't work on vampires, but knowing what I do about vampire physiology as defined in The Blood, I think it should/shouldn't."
