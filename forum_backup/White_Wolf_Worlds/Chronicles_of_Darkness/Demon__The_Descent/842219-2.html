    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    I'm aware, tangentially, that Claiming is a Big Deal for Spirits and Werewolves. Which is sort of why it became a topic of interest to me. If it's so significant, why is it available for Angels with no word count dedicated to its impact on the setting? Spirit-only effects exist are called out as such and Claimed is not one of them.
    Yeah, because it isn't Spirit-only, but rather, a thing that mostly Spirits do. The ghosts or angels that would do it are the kind of one-off edge cases you'd see in a Night Horrors book (which incidentally, is one of the reason's why Satchel's post is a valuable one to think about.)


    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    So yeah, the obvious conclusion, which I was able to come to on my own, was that the writers didn't really see Angels Claiming people as a big enough deal to talk about. Nevertheless it's an option and given how integral it is to Werewolf it can clearly lead to some interesting stories. Hence this topic.
    I don't think the conclusion to draw is that it's not a big deal, but rather that it is sufficiently uncommon that dedicating wordcount outside of detailing that edge-case in the Chronicles of Darkness core was not considered worthwhile.

    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    Now, personally, I feel there are a few more options than just "Occult Matrices" as an explanation for why the God-Machine may seek to manifest an Angel with the Claimed Manifestation. But first of all:

    Why, exactly, is this seen as "wasting an angel"? The Angel still exists. If its body is destroyed, it can move on and Claim a new target. It loses access to Numen, sure, but it still exists, it can still contribute to future projects, it can still follow the God-Machine's orders and use its Influences and has a new unique skill set unlike many of its angelic siblings with which to carry it out. It can even abandon its Claimed body if its Ephemeral form becomes more useful to the God-Machine's next mission. So why this idea that dedicating an Angel to Claiming a target is equivalent to destroying the Angel?
    I mistakenly misremembered Claiming as permanently merging the two, such that an Angel no longer exists. That was my mistake.

    In terms of reasons to Claim, the obvious ones that leap out are:

    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    -Infiltration. The Angel can access the target's mind, and be the target in every way once it has been Claimed. Obviously, this works best when the Claimed Being is one whose physical alterations can be discreetly concealed. In Demon, certain changes could be passed off as glitches/partial transformations.
    Keeping in mind that the God-Machine follows the path of least resistance:

    1. Claiming as it's described in the Chronicles of Darkness core has an effect on the body. Passing them off as glitches doesn't really make sense, since a glitch is in and of itself an attention-grabbing thing.
    2. If it needs to know something you know, it can send an Angel or a Stigmatic or an Imperative that's capable of reading your mind as a mundane mortal, which is certainly less intrusive and noticeable (also things the God-Machine prefers)
    3. Concealment Infrastructure is already purpose-made for this sort of thing. Sleeper Agents, Places that take people's lives and turn them into Covers, all that stuff can do the deed without the time and resource investment of manufacturing an Angel to Claim a person just to infiltrate something.


    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    -Motivation. Getting a Saboteur to give up all their secrets is a whole lot easier if they become a loyal Angel, merging their personalities so that at least part of them wants to help.
    See the above. If the Machine wants to know that Demon's secrets, there's ways that are less time consuming and resource intensive than Claiming him. In fact, I'd bet good money it'd use Infrastructure to recycle him and get his memories. Don't forget that Claiming takes a long period of time.

    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    -Special skill sets. The Claimed being can develop Supernatural Merits. These can be combined with the Angel's influences to create abilities that Ephemeral Angels couldn't possibly possess. Combined with the massively boosted attributes and long term nature of Claimed, this could be a lot more useful to a variety of projects than a Possessed or Materialised Angel.
    Stigmatics provide these talents in a less resource intensive fashion, since again, you don't have to make an Angel to do it. In fact, this is why Stigmatics are called out as valuable to the Machine, because they don't even represent the smallest fraction of the cost of an Angel.

    Angelic Incepts provide a means of boosting the scale of power for abilities they possess that seems again (in an ironic twist) to be less resource intensive/time consuming than making somebody Claimed.

    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    -Efficiency. At least in terms of Essence. I mean, it's certainly clear that the God-Machine has no trouble producing Essence, but as it strives to reach results in the most efficient manner it could be that a being able to operate without Essence for prolonged periods of time could be attractive for it due to the corresponding reduction in supply costs.
    I just fail to see how a Claimed is more efficient than the already existing purpose-built Infrastructure, Angels, Stigmatics and Incepts that cover all of the aforementioned purposes. I'm not saying that it's completely inferior to every iteration of the above, but at a glance, Claiming seems the least efficient way to accomplish these things.

    Originally posted by Azahul View Post
    -"God moves in mysterious ways". Yeah, this is really just the Occult Matrix argument, but a little broader. No one understands occult physics. Maybe the Machine only had the resources to manufacture an Angel with the Claimed ability. Maybe there would have been unacceptable side effects to specialising the Angel in a different direction. Who knows?
    That's completely fair.


    For the record, I'm not at all opposed to an Angel with Claimed. I definitely think it's a very interesting idea, but I don't feel that these explanations provide a good rationale for why the Machine would do it outside of very specific Occult Matrix edge-cases.

    Your last reason, while similar to the occult matrix explanation is IMHO the most compelling, because the question of "Why would the Machine make an angel to merge with a person?" is more compelling when the answer is not something as mundane and (generally) incorrect like "It's more efficient."

    As another suggested thought, I think when considering what weird reason the Machine may have to send an Angel to Claim somebody that the human part of that should be very important. It's not made this Angel to Claim just any schmuck, this person is important to the Machine's plans. But how? Why? What was their disposition BEFORE the Angel Claimed them? What are they like now? Perhaps most interestingly, how might that interact with the potential for an Angel to Fall?

        Originally posted by Azahul View Post
        Why, exactly, is this seen as "wasting an angel"?
        Because Claiming a living being — especially something with as much potential for occult entanglement as a human — is a synthesis that neither component comes away from without picking up bits of the other even after separation. Arc's made at least one fairly extensive essay talking about how the God-Machine and its errant tools are very much built on the notion of being apart. You're looking at the Manifestation as an abstraction that's neater than the practical execution.

        So why this idea that dedicating an Angel to Claiming a target is equivalent to destroying the Angel?
        Regular interaction with humanity is already enough to cause a distressing number of angels to Fall off-schedule and the purely mechanical effects of Claiming are available to the Machine without blowing the esoteric equivalent of hundreds to billions of dollars. Weird bio-psychic engineering can give a mortal supernatural powers that aren't thematically derived from the nature of a given angel and a slavish devotion to a given agenda that isn't vulnerable to the sudden development of independence, and Bunyip's mentioned that angels in Cover essentially shortcut away the entire Manifestation system.

        Unless there's a specific call for such a risk, God has tools at Its disposal that can do most all of the jobs a Claimed might be called upon for without outright merging an angel with a human.

                                                The response to that is so deep and varied I can't even do it justice tonight. So let's go reader's digest here: the angels must be careful about maintaining the purity of their being, which is simultaneously their body, their soul, and their active mind, their thoughts. Claiming anything is to introduce the sort of unclean, unholy deviance to their exist that can cause them to Fall. On the flip side, descending(ba dum ching) to the same level as most living things via Claiming is an affront to the celestial authority and awesome power that is the sphere of the angels-it diminishes them, makes them appear less credible, less absolute. So Falling aside, it just weakens the message any angels conveys.

                                                This isn't to say that claiming doesn't happen. It just has to happen for the right reasons, and in such a way that conveys the particular brand of sacred presence that the God-Machine cultivates, both in the angels themselves and those who come across them.
