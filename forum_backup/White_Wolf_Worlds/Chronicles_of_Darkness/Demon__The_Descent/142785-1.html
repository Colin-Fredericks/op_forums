    Oh, sure. Criminal. Contract killer. Cop — on the edge. Or make a soul pact with a wizard.

    Two more things to note. The compromise rules don't require you to lose Cover on a failed roll; you can choose instead to accept a glitch if the Cover is too precious. These will stack up over time, but hacking into infrastructure (or other means Storyteller-approved) is stated to allow demons to lose glitches.

    Also, keep in mind that compromises occur for acts that are grossly out of character. If a person who knew you in passing would see you do it and know something's not right, that's a compromise. There's a lot of wiggle room there to participate and be useful without running around in Times Square cackling and shooting hellfire out of a shotgun.

    By the way, you can shoot hellfire out of a shotgun!

                            For me "The Matrix" feel of the game fits in perfectly with the World of Darkness and the God-Machine is a perfect aspect for the world. (After all, the idea of Mechanical Angels and a God-Machine were in the original World of Darkness book in the very big early story "Voice of the Angel". From an OOC perspective, it is the mechanism a storyteller can use to bring more modern conspiracy theories and creepy stories into a reality. Making all legends, new and old, true in the World of Darkness.)

                            I perform this thought experiment to get over the machine aspects and realise that Angels and Demons are not robots or programs despite some neat correlations. First, I remove "Machine" from them and see that they follow all the tropes of a fallen angel and a distant God. Next, it is easy to see the Gnostic underpinnings and identify that God as the demiurge (it is actually referred to as this in the new Hunter the Vigil: Mortal Remains book!). A demiurge is as welcome in the World of Darkness as the Circle of Crones pagan gods, or Mage's supernal realms. (In fact, much of Mage was based off of such mysticism).

                            Now we look at things about this demiurge. Such as, Biblical text that refers to entities such as the Ophan or Throne and the Cherub. Such creatures are angelic beings, but are profoundly bizarre and not at all the usual, modern trope of a beautiful person, but of a creature of bizarre physics that defies human ideas of how the universe even functions. A wheel with forty-two eyes, and burning with an inner ring of fire, anyone?

                            Now that we've looked at Ezekiel's Wheel, and see that even traditional angelic beings are pretty bizarre, we can see that this demiurge behaves oddly. Now we can put the Machine back in God-Machine and realise that the machine was always there. It is bound to the principles of the universe, it is perhaps even the "Clockwork Universe" itself. Mechanisms could be invisible, from the seemingly perfect orbits of the solar system, to the calculated movement of atoms, to the horrible amalgamations of flesh, steel and earth that are this demiurge's most trusted earthly servants; the angels.

                            Or so was the run of my mind!

                                Yeah, people get too caught up on the word "machine" and gear motifs, abd forget that the entire universe does in fact run on what are essentially mechanistic rules. That's what the laws of physics are, the deterministic, mechanical rules that govern everything. And on a slightly more personal scale, humans are quite literally biological machines. Now, in the World of Darkness, symbolism is of utmost importance, so it makes sense that the "God" of such a deterministic universe as the one we live in would be a contraption of metal and gears. Not necessarily because it was actually constructed by industrial means, but because that is the only way our limited minds can comprehend it. Just like Pennywise isn't really a spider, that's just the closest thing to what IT really is that the characters can conceive of. The God-Machine is unquestionably a machine, but it operates on occult matrices and infrastructure and principles we could never hope to understand. So, we conceptualize it as a technological device, because that's something we can understand, and it kind of works the same way if you turn your head sideways and squint hard enough.

                                    Originally posted by Topher View Post
                                    I'm still having a hard time getting passed "The Matrix" feel of the game. I've read the quickstart and I'm reading the pre-release of the core book. It just doesn't feel like it belongs in the overall WoD, and since I feel that the God Machine is an unnecessary "update" that muddies the waters of the WoD, I just can't get my head around it. I would really like to play it at least once, so I can have a better idea of how it runs and see if it changes my opinion.
                                    First of all, the God Machine doesn't muddy any waters. That book is basically "Book of Spirits" / "Book of the Dead" / "Inferno," but for angels instead of spirits, ghosts, or demonic entities, respectively. The God-Machine doesn't exist in Vampire or Werewolf by default. You only include it if you want it included, same as bringing in Infernal demons into Vampire, Mage, Changeling, or whatever. The only game where the GM exists by default is Demon. Everyone else can take it or leave it.

                                    The GMC RULES are being used to update all the lines, yes. But the first part of the book, that talks about the God Machine? That's entirely optional.

                                    Two, relax about the whole Matrix thing. People do get hung up on the machine thing too much. Its no different than pushing vampirism as a disease, or genetics determining werewolves, or aliens doubling as Gentry. Super science has always been a thing in the WoD - its just never been front-and-center as much as it is in Demon. But, yes, actually sit down and make a character. Don't even really worry about a game, but seriously make a character in full details. You'd be surprised at how quickly a character feels like a fallen angel. The Exploits and Demon Form especially are thematic - teleporting to magic circles, possession, shadow form, throwing fire from exploits, while wings, horns, tails, hooved feet, red skin, glowing halos and more in Forms. Notice how demons have a weakness to "holy powers" (ie being found / slain by religious followers or angels). Notice how the entire story is a war between Heaven and Hell.

                                    Also, ignore the Embodiment of Human Sin style of demons. That's really more of Inferno's / Vampire's stick. You are a former angel. Think Fallen (or demon the fallen, if you know it). If you're familiar with League of Legend, read up on Morgana's story. THAT is the type of demon you are playing.

                                    The Four Incarnations can be seen as fallen angels, imps, devils, and succubi/incubi. Guardians are suggested to start with angelic features, and their powers tend to reflect spiritual abilities and accessing pure knowledge. Destroyers lean towards chaotic powers, fire abilities and animalistic forms - standard tropes for the devil. Messengers are known for their powers of social influences, beautiful forms, and access to succubus-like abilities such as Addiction. Psychopomps specialize in trickery powers, such as shifting their Covers around as well as messing with memories and illusions; in a nutshell, trickster demons are imps.

                                    Agendas come with four different ways of creating Hell - alternate dimensions where you can "damn" your human friends to, turning Earth itself into your idea of Hell after you kill God and destroy Heaven, or achieving a state of Golconda/Ascension. Or repenting and going back to heaven. All different philosophies for demons you'll find in lots of stories.

                                    Covers represent a demon's shapeshifting ability. Demons are renowned for appearing as whomever they want to be. Covers allow this, and Mundane embeds let you mess with them even more. Spoofing, perfect memories, and lying are also demonic traits, which have returned in full. These all might have a bit of a science theme tacked on their explanation, but the end result is the same - they're perfect liars, can recall anything, and can take any form and you'll never know.

                                    Infrastructure, output, occult matrix are all just fancy words for spell components, rituals, and magic results. God Machine magic relies on being in the right place (geomancy), right time when the stars align (astrology), with the right person (magic bloodlines). WoD books have a tendency to use Big Words to make things sound different or more important than these basic concepts really are.

                                    Check out the example angels - one of them is a being that glows with blinding light, and is weak against weapons forged in absolute darkness. Another is an angel of death, weak to flames. A third is a messenger angel speaking of love, and weak to something dipped in blood. Angels act like angels you read about in stories, and often with a weakness to things we tend to associate with dark powers - darkness, hellfire, and blood magic.

                                    We can talk about the game, and never once have anything related to machines come up. Its a theme, yes. But one that doesn't really inform the game to a great degree.
