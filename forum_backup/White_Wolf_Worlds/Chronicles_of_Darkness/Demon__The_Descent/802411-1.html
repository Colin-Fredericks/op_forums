    Originally posted by MrParaduo View Post
    Here's some rationales I've written down. Lemme know if it works, or I'm full of sh*t. ^_^;

    • Embeds are infallible cosmic truths that demons can enforce onto themselves. Exploits are akin to manipulating the "legalese" of reality, albeit temporary.
    • Terms like propulsion, infrastructure, etc. are simply used to dumb down advanced ideas for "primitive mortals" to comprehend.
    • The God-Machine is not just a collection of machinery, cogs, and gears, but the Spirit of Order itself. Every time a pattern is formed, the God-Machine knows, because they are one in the same.
    • GM is to Demiurge as Promethean Principle is to God(?), perhaps polar opposites, or parent to the other (or both).
    • The demonic form isn't truly made of mundane materials. Rather, a demon is a collection of symbolic truths bound in a quantum state between one or more realities (wow that's a mouthful)--uh, it is a True Name given physical form?
    • The Cipher is a Malapraxis?

    But yeah, I didn't mean Spirit as in Shadow, as more like the GM is sentient symbolism. Software and Hardware are too temporal to equate to something so powerful. The Idea behind the GM, that is what transcends the mundane. It doesn't need circuit boards, not in the conventional sense. Patterns that act akin to circuits, such as leylines touching menhirs to connect with other realms, that is what forms the GM. Transcendental, not material. Ignore the literal idea of a Machine and think bigger, more abstract. It's not a mere robot, but patterns within patterns, made sentient by means unknowable.

    So what's the issue?

    Note: I'm not seeing how the Cypher is at all related to Malapraxis, personally. More of a True Name, I'd think.

                                        Okay, let's be fair here: You can reskin Descent without needing the DTG. The biggest problem comes to what Satchel mentioned, the fact that demons use the language they do to hide from the enemy.

                                        The technological, scientific, and natural law element of Descent's particular strain of techgnosticism encourages to people think of God and heaven in terms that have always been used by people who tie religion and progress together, particularly Christianity, and that includes a lot of sci-fi (One would be surprised, in fact, at how frequently science fiction-like writings and musings have been offered by religious leaders), but Descent doesn't need to rely on the extremes. Consider that technogaianism allows some bending back, creating the pastoral scenery with technological backings. You can bend the subject back or advance it forward enough that the imagery becomes the wholly organic/spiritual iconography they are all used to-Descent already allows for this, see Horse Barn Infrastructure, or hell, even growing a tree to be infrastructure(Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil cough).

                                        Descent has always had the capability to be discussed in non-futurism inclined terminology-this is one of the big problems with saying Descent is not about demons. The emphasis on more familiar sci-fi trappings allows the line to be distinct in ways other games on similar subjects can't be, but demons can refer to Facilities as Temples and still be right. Descent, very technically, does not need to use modern terms to describe itself. It does so, not only for the sake of distinction or for spy association, but because it wants to reinforce the strangest notion religious and spiritual icons throughout the ages have always had-describe their 'heavens' as a utopia that can not only be reached, but made.

                                        When the future has always been divine, it makes sense for the divine to look futuristic. But it doesn't have to.
