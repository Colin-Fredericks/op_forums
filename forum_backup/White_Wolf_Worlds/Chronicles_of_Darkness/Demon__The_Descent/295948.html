    Left or Right?

    10-29-2014, 12:58 PM
    Hey guys if you could and would I would like some help. I have a character in my game that is thinking of buying Left or Right?
    I want to be fair to the player and the power and would like some examples of this power being used and ways it can not. This is one of the flexible powers in game so it becomes more about interpretation so I would like a lock on this power and its limits. I'm not worried about balance but playing the power true to how it should be played.

    I think I should talk about the examples given so you can see what my problem is.
    • Now the person glancing left or right when they enter the room- What if the person was not going to glance at all? if he was just going to look straight. Are you able to force the person into a binary choice? Making someone look right instead of left seems like a binary choice when you look at it like that but there is also extreme right and extreme left. Looking forward or looking behind one self. Is it that you can force someone into a binary choice or is it, if they was going to look left they now look right.
    • Turning a light on and off or the safety on a gun- So you are able to turn machines on and off at will? Let me explain (and sorry for being pedantic) a light can only be on and off. Same for a gun safety. A computer can be on, off, on stand-by or asleep. Would you be able to use this power on a computer? Let me link this with the example of the dice. You can make it odd or even because thats all the dice can be, but the computer has more choice than the two on/off as it has states in between.
    • The dice example. You can not use this power on win/lose. Is this because there is different ways for you to win (which is the way I think it is) or is it because there is more than a binary choice on the dice. I do not think it is the second as there are different ways to get odds/evens on the dice but the fact of the rules of the game.
    So the game Bruce's play your cards right (a game where you get showed a card. Then you have to guess if the card is higher than the first or lower.) You could get it right every time as the cards could only be higher or lower (so you could infact use this power for win/lose).


    Now for some examples my player asked.
    • A lock- it can only be open or closed. (or a door/window etc)
    • An electric lock with a keypad (is there too much possibilities here to be opened)
    • A gun being loaded or unloaded.
    • An item being there or not.
    • A person trusting you or not.



    Wow I am sorry for the wall of text

      As a general rule, just because it's more interesting that way, I'd err on the side of leniency and allow the use of the power if you're not sure whether to allow it or not. As a general rule, it works with any kind of binary state, but it only works with binary states.

      If a person's searching a room, there are more than two places to look, so you can't use it; if a person is escaping and comes to a T-intersection, meanwhile, there are only two directions to proceed, so you can use it.

      A computer is unlikely to be responsive to the power, because most of the states it can adopt are gradients made up of many binary values. You could flip an individual binary value, but that's rarely enough to accomplish something useful, and probably requires a lot of Computer knowledge to know which to flip. I would be lenient enough to allow "somebody flipped the power switch" as a binary state, though, which might mean the difference between off, on, or sleep mode, depending on how recently anybody would have been around to use it.

      Craps doesn't respond to the power because "win or lose" is not a binary state, but rather a category of many possible outcomes. There are lots of ways to roll a win and lots of ways to roll a loss. Odds or evens, similarly, would be categories of different outcomes if you're rolling multiple dice. Rolling a single die probably would permit odds or evens as a valid choice.

      A mechanical lock pretty much only has the two meaningful states.
      A electric lock probably could be deactivated with the binary choice of "did somebody shut the lock off or not?" if that's a plausible possibility.
      A gun being loaded is a grey area. If you want to be really technical, most modern guns would run on "does it have a clip in it?" while a revolver probably would have one variable with multiple outcomes (zero to six, or whatever).
      An item being there or not is a situational call. In most cases, I'd disallow that, but if you have a good reason to believe that an object might have been there, it might be a valid use. If you want to pull out something that's not already highly likely to be there, you want In My Pocket, not Left or Right.
      A person trusting you is definitely a gradient. Trust is way more complicated than yes or no.
