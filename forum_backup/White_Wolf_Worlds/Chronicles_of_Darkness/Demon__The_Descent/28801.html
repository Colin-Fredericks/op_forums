                                  It’s certainly doable. On the old Wiki (is it archived somewhere, or is it just gone for now?), I did a proof-of-concept partial Translation Guide for Changeling; if those two games can be translated, then translating Demon is doable.

                                  The trick is to remember that a Translation Guide involves taking bits and pieces of game X that you like and showing how they can be incorporated into game Y; the goal isn’t a complete translation of everything you’ll find in X into Y all at once — if you like everything about X, why aren’t you just playing X?

                                  So break Fallen into its component parts, look at them one at a time, and ask yourself “how would I fit this into Descent?” Then break Descent into its component parts, look at them one at a time, and ask “how would I fit this into Fallen?”

                                  My favorite part of the Translation Guides, though, is the compare-and-contrast, where the writer isn’t translating, but merely holding both games up side by side and saying “this is where they’re similar, and this is where they differ” — I learned a lot about Awakening by reading the Mage Translation Guide.

                                                          Yeah, I'm loving the Translation Guides too. It's nice to port things I loved in the oWoD into the nWoD (and vice versa), and I occasionally find interesting ideas in them that I had not considered before. I just wish we also would have gotten one for Changeling, Mummy, Hunter, etc.

                                                          Anyway, while the setting, themes and even mechanics of Demon: the Fallen and Demon: the Descent are quite different, there are enough similarities there that I can sort of see how it would work. Especially after reading the preview pdf. One thing that especially jumped out at me early on is how the four Agendas correspond quite nicely with four of the five Factions, albeit modified for flavor and setting. Only the Luciferians are left out, mostly because their entire raison d'être no longer makes sense in Descent. No Lucifer after all. On the other hand, the Saboteurs offer a more PC friendly version of the Raveners*.

                                                          Interestingly, it seems to me the differences between Incarnations and Houses are more nuanced. Some are more obvious than others; the Halaku easily equate with Psychopomps and/or Destroyers. But while the Annunaki might conceptually have more in common with Psychopomps, it would be easier to represent them with Guardians thanks to Instrumental Embeds working with ones tools and surroundings. And of course ANY Demon can become proficient in creating Gadgets. Similarly, before the game came out, I expected Destroyers to map closest to Rabisu (and indeed, Cacophony Embeds feature healing as well as combat effects, which goes nicely for the Lore of the Flesh) but as I read through the preview pdf, I couldn't help but realize how cool the idea of Messengers revealing messages to animals was (via Embeds like Animal Communication). I felt I could easily translate one of my old Devourer characters as a Messenger who revealed things to animals, trees and even bacteria!

                                                          Honestly, adding a Christian flavour doesn't strike me as THAT difficult, if only because so much of it is fluff and setting more than mechanics. Simply swap out "the God-Machine" for God (which happens several times in the pdf by the way) and maybe stipulate that Demons didn't appear on earth until the Reckoning if you want to keep the metaplot. Even the Earthbound aren't that hard to incorporate. The biggest conceptual differences I can see are between Lores and Embeds. Aside from the obvious problem of Lores being progressions while Embeds are singular powers, Lores are extremely flashy and vulgar, while Embeds are subtle. Even the more overt Exploits don't approach what Lores can do. Certainly, as I said above, there are at least some points of overlap there. Rip the Gates goes nicely with the Lore of Paths, while Like I Built It and Right Tools, Right Job at least cover the more basic levels of the Lore of the Forge. But other Lores like the Lore of Storms don't have many cognates.

                                                          * Note that these are just shallow, cursory looks at the Agendas and Factions. In truth, there are multiple points of similarity. The Raveners can be represented by both Saboteurs AND Tempters, while the Luciferians have some conceptual overlap with the Saboteurs as well.
