                Your Dancing Wolf spirit would lose it's immunity from unskilled penalties and have to roll Attribute only, not Attribute + Rank. Why? Because it is not a spirit of dance. An Angel's powers are more broad, why? Because it is not based on a concept, it is based on a task. Which means any intermediary task that it must perform to complete it's mission, it can use this ability on.

                A Ghost must define itself by whom it was: the self. This is going to be narrow, it can't gain this immunity to unskilled penalties on anything it couldn't do as a living person because doing so would violate it's self.

                A Spirit must define itself by it's nature, such as wolf. This is going to be narrow, but not as narrow as a ghost. It can gain it's immunity from unskilled penalties on anything it could do with wolves, but nothing else.

                An Angel must define itself by it's mission, such as "Capture or Kill detected Demon." This is less narrow than the above with the spirit. It can gain it's immunity from unskilled penalties on anything it could do that help it capture or kill a detected demon. (Such as steal a car in order to pursue said detected demon.)

                You seem to think I am saying that, mechanically, they are able to just use an embed? No. They don't need to use mechanical embeds. These are cheats demons made to do the things angels can do. Can an angel replicate anything an embed can do? Yes. Why? Because it is connected to the God-Machine. It can't just do whatever it wants because it has a mission and if it goes off mission it loses all it's bonuses. Not to mention it is probably falling.

                Some embeds are better than others and do things that cannot easily be handled with the angel simply being able to unlock a door it needs to to perform it's mission - such as Just Bruised. In these cases, you don't use a skill, you probably use either an identical Numen or an Influence (such as Strengthen).

                But, if an angel needs to unlock a door to complete it's mission, this directly relates to it's mission as it cannot complete it's mission if it cannot get past this door.

                    That isn't what the word "directly" means. Some thing can be directly or indirectly related to something, or completely unrelated.

                    A Ghost's concept is based on it's former self; a Spirit's concept is based on it's nature, and an Angel's concept is based on the mission they were assigned.

                    All of these have some actions that are directly related to that concept (which receive a bonus based on Rank), some actions that are antithetical to their concept (which would be inconceivable to them), and some actions which are neither directly related nor antithetical to their concept, like a combat-focused being that finds itself needing to dance.

                    There's nothing in the text to suggest that this mechanic behaves differently for different types of Ephemeral Entity. Quite the opposite, in fact.

                    You've argued that the existence of an Embed that allows Demons to download Skills justifies this interpretation, but that would make it the only Embed that all Angels have access to. Why? It's not exactly something that would make thematic or logical sense, and nowhere is it suggested that all Angels had access to this Embed.

                        So, an angel whose mission it was to kill a specific person would be programmed by the God-Machine with certain tools that would help it in that task (Embeds), represented mechanically by being Able to roll Attribute + Rank. Let's say that in order to kill the target, the angel finds that the best method would be to infiltrate his dance studio and leave a bomb in his dressing room. Planting a bomb is a skill the angel would likely be programmed with, as it is directly relevant to the mission. Disguising itself is another ability likely to be needed on an assassination mission. However, if the angel needs to audition to join the target's dance troupe, dancing is not really directly related to assassination. Now, if infiltrating a dance studio was a planned part of the mission to begin with, dancing talent would probably be another high priority on the list of embeds to include. If, on the other hand, the original plan had been to kill the target in some other way, and the whole dance thing was an unexpected turn if events, dancing is unlikely to have been included in the angel's programming unless the God Machine expected it to be needed to complete the mission. So even though in the moment dancing might be helping the angel complete its mission, it may not be directly related, and therefore would not be eligible for an Attribute + Rank roll.

                                I'd take a more impressionistic approach to judging the difference between on and off mission. Is the skill/act something that an undercover spy would reasonably be able to improvise because they're a badass secret agent? Or is it out of context? Hotwiring a car to chase after your objective, even if carjacking wasn't an anticipated need, seems in-genre. Abruptly being a master painter or dancer is much more of a stretch - though Numina to compensate don't seem out of bounds.

                                I dont think anyone's arguing that angels can just use anything that's an embed, but embeds represent use of angelic capabilities, and the two kinds of characters are run on different systems. Some parts of what a demon treats as Download Knowledge can be represented just with the way ephemeral beings use attribute+rank. Some parts are better represented as numina. Figuring out the exact translation algorithm is utterly tangential to actually playing or running the game.

                                            I wasn't going to say anything because who the hell actually wants to argue about logical fallacies, but since you made a point to reiterate it, I want to contend that no, it's not a rule zero fallacy.                                             So, like I said, not rule 0 fallacy. You could argue that it's lazy design, or that the game leaves you hanging, but the fallacy is something different entirely.

                                            The way I see it,the numina as listed have a given power level consistently. So if you are saying 'place any power you desire as a numina to make angels as powerful as they are supposed to be', is a rule zero fallacy. Numina as 'anything goes' is a houserule, in GMC. To make a parallel, I could make a vampiric discipline that has double effectiveness of vigor for the same costs, given that new disciplines can be created, but that'd be way outside design space!

                                            On another subject, embeds have been designed and balanced for the fragile things demons are in their covers. As such, they would make angels quite simply overpowered, given their unnatainable dicepools and immaterial nature. Numina and influences, as listed in the books from GMC and on, are not a very good replacement for 'all embeds from angel type'. The huge dicepools can be something like that, but only in an abstract, stopgap uncrunchy way. We need something else for running angels.

                                            I would very much like a book with more developed rules for angels, as the line develops.
