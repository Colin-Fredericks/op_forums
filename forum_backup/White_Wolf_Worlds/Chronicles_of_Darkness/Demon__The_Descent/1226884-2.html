It's actually a trend they've been doing in Dark Eras and Night Horrors (since the first), where you have a write-up of an antagonist or setting, then the setting relevant mechanics or the antagonists stats or new abilities in the back of that section. Most of the second edition books have tables of contents that let you know where certain rules are, and they tend to be in chunks with other rules. Skipping to the rules section is easier than ever, but these rules play off of the setting.

                                The only actual fiction anthology that also contained mechanics, Interface, also separated those in their own sections after the relevant fiction. But in some cases, for instance the core books or player's guides, mechanics can't be completely separate from the setting. Some people might just want to know what sacred hunt blessing a lodge offers, but without the context of what the lodge is about, it's just mechanical nonsense.
