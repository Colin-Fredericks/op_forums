                                      I've been mulling over this for a while. Some pretty difficult conditions to work with. The best idea I had was the following:

                                      The God-Machine deploys the Broken Clock, an angel that only exists for a single second, twice a day. Local infrastructure embedded in the Town Hall's clock tower then activates to cause that single day to loop over and over, 33 times. The Broken Clock possesses its victims just long enough to perform the sacrifice, whether it be by having the victim commit suicide, murder a passer-by, trigger an "accident", or even wield its own Numen to lethal effect. Each time the day reoccurs, those who died in previous versions of the day are already dead, although their bodies continue to run through the same motions they went through in the instance they died regardless of external stimuli. For the purposes of the universe, however, their time of death was in the second the Broken Clock killed them.

                                      However, as all the killings must occur at the same time, and since the Broken Clock is "right twice a day", after every murder it has a single moment to itself. Its momentary appearances are the only thing mortals remember from day to day, and they lead to subtle changes in individuals' behaviour that cause greater and greater differences between the original version of the day and the thirty-third, made even more noticeable by the fact that the dead entirely fail to act upon any changes between the day of their death and the actual events of the current day.

                                              Originally posted by basketcase View Post
                                              The question is two fold how does the G-M do this? whats needed? what does it look like? whats the linchpin?
                                              And how might a Cell of five (soon to be four) demons stop/hack it? saving that what might they do?
                                              This is fun! Naturally there are multiple approaches. Subtle and intricate or simple and efficient. The God-Machine favours both, so pick what fits.

                                              One important thing you've not specified is whether or not they have to be murdered all at once, within a limited time frame or individually at specific times.If the deaths have to occur roughly at the same time it's far harder, though not impossible, to stop. If they're happening one at a time, appropriately what with the clock thing, it makes it easier to investigate.Currently you have it so that nobody can know anyone was killed - that means the PC investigation will easily unravel the Infrastructure just by finding a body. I'd change that so that the murders are kept secret to prevent he building being closed, evacuated, etc, or worst of all, the show being stopped! I have a glorious image of a director/manager being influenced/controlled by an angel and insisting the show must go on even as PCs try to shut it down.

                                              If you're favouring efficiency,the bodies were dispatched quickly and efficiently and hidden. The MO is likely the same for all of them (maybe one or two fought back but most were killed unawares) and that right there is your big fat clue.

                                              If you favour intricacy, each one died a different way. One of them everyone saw, an old woman who worked at the ticket office suffering a heart attack. She was rushed to hospital, but she'd died on the premise. It wasn't going to close the opera house - you know how money they'd lose? - but some of the staff are visibly upset (clue). Other deaths were probably hidden or disguised in various ways. One performer/stage hand simply never turned up (their body is hidden in a box/the rafters/etc). One of the victims was lured off the street, driven by hallucinations, and killed with nobody knowing they were even present. Their body is somewhere people don;t go - a cellar, an old storeroom, etc. A victim who died in the kitchen (a cook who went on holiday yesterday. but came back late at night because they forgot their wallet, which nobody knew about and nobody realises they're missing because, well, they've got the day off) has been butchered, their remains disguised as other meats and placed in the freezer.You get the idea.

                                              One approach is to have a murdering angel, or angels (though personally I'd have a one angel limit), do the dirty work. of course, The murderer could also be cultists, sleeper-agents or something weirder and more specific. The angel could take a human form, giving you a supernaturally empowered serial killer, or might be weirder. If you're going intricate, they work primarily through apparent chance - triggering heart attacks and accidents that grow ever more brazen as midnight and the final murder approach... and if you want efficiency,they just break necks with a quick and sickening twist. A cult doesn't have access to an angel's supernatural mojo, but they could pull of some carefully orchestrated murders (and some less so) easily enough. Sleeper agents are a great choice if all the murders need to happen roughly at once - some of the audience stand up and wander off to different locations when they hear a trigger phrase/a music sequence that was carefully inserted into the opera's score (which one or two of the musicians noticed, to give you a chance), their expressions curiously blank.Some of 'em aren't obvious patrons of the opera - one's in overalls, for example.

                                              Linchpins might be a specific murder weapon, the sleeper agents themselves, the specific timing/locations required, all of the above...

                                                      Originally posted by basketcase View Post

                                                      The question is two fold how does the G-M do this? whats needed? what does it look like? whats the linchpin?
                                                      And how might a Cell of five (soon to be four) demons stop/hack it? saving that what might they do?
                                                      Well, hacking the pacemakers of the victims seems like the best way to avoid detection early on, though once that happens more than say... twice, people will start getting suspicious.

                                                      You know what? This is Demon: the Descent? We do weird here.

                                                      Try this: the lynchpin is a clock-maker who belonged to the outer circle of a G-M cult and was buried alive in the foundations back in 1872. The inner circle, in accordance with instruction from a Messenger or through interpretation of occult texts, picks this poor guy (perhaps he betrayed the cult in some fashion) and inters him the night before construction of the opera house begins, and since the cult was funding the construction, they were in place to put bribes to look the other way, and to watch over the Opera house for nearly a century and a half. The G-M has planned this for a very long time; play up the horror when the characters realize just how far sighted the G-M's plans actually are.

                                                      Every time one of the murders is committed, the Opera house is regress back in time 12 years, first to 2003, then to 1991, 1979 and so forth all the way to 1871. Any demon will be mentally unaffected, and there are no immediate physical effects ( a person who is 30 in 2015 will still be 30 in 2003, 1991, etc) but any normal mortal in the opera house will think that he has a perfectly normal life in the new era, all of their memories are edited to push them 12 years forwards. Physical artifacts such as cell phones, clothes, newpapers and concessions in the lobby will be replaced with period appropriate items. Family members and companions of the victims will think it odd that their friends are missing the opera, but since they've been bounced back, they don't remember that the missing people were (will be? are?) murdered.

                                                      Attendees will natural focus their attention on the opera, but if forced to realize the inconsistencies in their new life, they might have some sort of a break down or realization (How old are you? 37. When were you in the military? When I was 18. Do you see any action? Yeah, I fought in Afghanistan. It's 2003 right now, Afghanistan, why are you here, 30 years old, and not in the military? Ummm...) Additionally, the Inspiration Embed has the unique situational effect of making a human target realize what's going on, and accepting it with much more calm (they get the Informed condition).

                                                      For the actual party carrying out these murders, I like the idea that our original cult goes to the opera house every 12 years, switches over to the going-in-reverse timeline and murders the selected individual... so if the players run into the cult in 2003, the cult will already have fought them 11 times, with each team of cultist assassins having the notes, received experience and training from the cultists that went before. In some cases, a veteran cultist might run into the players 2 to 3 times. The Ring will also have to deal with the ghost of the murdered clock-maker, who be comes more and more active, more powerful, and less sane as the Opera house goes back in time and is saturated with G-M essence and aether.

                                                      The G-m Gradually sheds the human patrons into different time periods, using other Infrastructure maintained by the cult to integrate them into new lives that closely resemble their previous (future?) lives. To an extent. Pity the interracial couple now living in 1907.

                                                      The G-m's win condition is to regress the Opera house to 1871, one year past the year of construction. The resulting paradox conceals the opera house deep within the folds of time and space.

                                                      To stop this and to destroy or subvert the infrastructure, will need to deal with the clock maker's ghost, and remove his corpse to a proper burial. IF then they have the ability to travel to any of the 12 periods (2015, 2003, 1991,1979,1967, 1955, 1943, 1931, 1919, 1907,1895, and 1883)... as long as they're willing to ritually murder someone at a certain time of day during an opera's performance to activate it. Should be a good way to get some use out of Dark Eras.

                                                      As a final addendum, when the players are researching the opera house, they find that the same opera has be played every 12 years, to the day, all the way back to 1883. Problem is, the opera in question was written in 1891.

                                                          The killer is the opera itself, a magical spell woven by the performers. They aren't even aware that what they're doing has any magical properties, the opera was suggested by a member of the troupe (no one can remember exactly who, the Angel responsible has been scrubbed from their minds) and they all decided it was the best choice.

                                                          At regular intervals throughout the show, the organ is keyed to produce a siren-like electrical trilling, audible only to the sacrifice. Each sacrifice is chosen by the organ based on their seat number (not quite as simple as 1A, 2B, 3C, etc, but an algorithm that makes it obvious that they are being picked at points on a circle). It compels them to descend into the bowls of the building, not noticing that they are walking right into the bloody gears of the God-Machine. Even a Demon is not immune to its influence. The disappearance of these people will hopefully go unnoticed until the performance ends.

                                                          At the climax of the performance, one of the lead performers will collapse on stage as the final sacrifice. The bombardment by electronic signals will cause them to bleed from the nose and mouth, and the blood will drip through the floor to join the bodies of the others down below.

                                                          In terms of lynchpin, the organ's subterranean extensions are the most obvious. The crew had to modify it to create the kind of sounds they needed for their performance, and these modifications are clearly God-Machine infrastructure to anyone who looks at them.
