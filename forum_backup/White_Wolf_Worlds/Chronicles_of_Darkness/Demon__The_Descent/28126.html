      Tommy shrugged off his black leather jacket and stowed it in his locker with a neatly ironed white button-down shirt on the hanger, trading his spiffy duds for a loose fitting gray sweatshirt and atop that a bright thin cloth orange vest with reflectors studding the sleeves, front and back. He looked at the mirror stuck to the interior of the locker door with a sigh that didn't quite convey the bored, resigned tone he meant it to and moved with a rote sensibility as he:
      • runs a pocket comb to slick back his black hair,
      • puts the comb away,
      • looks in the mirror for a second,
      • dab with the tip of thumb and index finger at smoothing his eye-brows and pencilled looking mustache,
      • engage in light ribalding with night manager Reginald Barnes
      "Is tonight the night your mail order bride finally gets here, Thomas?"
      Tommy pushes the locker door shut and turns around, leans back and stares a moment at Mr. Barnes before a grin slides into place, "Well we can't all be married to pastries, Reggie. It's not my fault I'm so damn pretty," finishing with a flourished pull on the hood and walking on the tiled floor toward the door with two steps forward and one to the right, or two forward one left in an L like a chess knight.
      Reginald was about to say something to counter in his dry fashion when he caught the L steps out of the corner of his eye. He looked up from the clipboard he'd been reading, "You're a bit touched, aren't you Thomas. Try not to break any of the cargo when you unload," curtly handing over the clipboard to Tommy.
      "We're shorthanded tonight and it says in your file you're adequate at operating a lift. The containers from Taiwan need to be off-loaded. Client is unhappy, as if we can control the weather-- nevermind: are you able to do this without damaging our charges?"
      Tommy turned around and tipped his hood forward a little like a hat, "I'll be ya huckleberry Reg."
      Reginald would have said something when he noticed powder on his hands and blushed, instead flustered and gesturing at the door.
      The night air felt pleasantly cool and the weather would have been perfect but for a fog blanketing the pier. Tommy looked around for a moment before feeling secure enough to let lenses slide down from beneath his eye lids and a tug at his ear lobes to trigger mechanisms for filtering ambient noise and environmental conditions. He was greeted with mild irritation, "Do you always have to take so long, Y***d? In any event, switch to another frequency and set to alternate every two minutes. With luck, we're done tonight."
      Alright Unit Yesod. You have the manifest in hand- it's crate A2478S34281531-(city name)-UK. It's marked with a fluctuating UV stamp, the signature of the spectrum shifts fifteen seconds each interval. Your accompanying Gemurah partner will arrive the moment the package is en route. Acquisition and delivery is your mission. Do not deviate. Silence herein. Unit Binah signing off.
      Tommy/Yesod pushed the janitorial supplies ahead of him on the way to the forklift. Just because he was pulling the lift didn't stop his normal job from being next on the list. He hitched the cart to the lift and drove at half speed toward the east end of the dock. It wasn't often he could feel the air while between states so he made the most of it. After making sure Reginald or someone else wasn't passing by in the pier or within a half-mile on the block he undid the bolt and walked into the crate.
      As he verified the crate and pulled out the UV marked painting, he could feel vibrations in the air that translated to an incoming phone call which he rerouted to his ear and mouth directly rather than risk the phone as an intermediary to be tapped, the bits of data plucked from the waves and translated to caller ID.
      "Vin-nnny, the ninny, now why would you be calling me while I'm at the docks. You know that Mr. John Marzini doesn't appreciate---let's say initiative. I'm not on the clock with him right now."
      "Wow. Ya know, Tommy, it's funny you mention initiative because it looks to me like you seem to be indulging in that right now in that big old crate with the dinged up paint."
      Tommy/Yesod carefully put down the painting and edged his way from the shipping container. He cast his sight around which earned him a derisive snort from the call.
      "The fuck are ya doing? Ah'm using a high powered set of binoculars with infrared, ultrared, shit the bed vision. And you definitely shit the bed."
      Undeterred by the invective, he continued to search for Vinny until an exasperated caller chimed in, "You're supposed to be some genius forger and clean salesman but you can't wrap your head around a pair of binoculars. Fine. You want to know where I am? Seventh floor of the Thrifton building, southeast window. I'm next to some lady's desk. She's got those metal balls that bounce back and forward and back, a kitten on a tree and I'm givin you a two finger salute."
      "Unit Yesod, I'm picking up a hostile call and the package hasn't been sent. We're preparing an S-Gem to eliminate this interloper. Compromise can come at any time."
      Yesod scrambled his call with Vinny and patched into a frequency to Binah, "Don't do that please...we're supposed to lock down this route for future deliveries. I can talk him down. Don't dispatch the S-Gem." He held his breath and waited. The answer came in the form of terminating the call and patch back to Vinny. He inwardly sighed in relief and tuned his voice back to heavy Brooklyn inflections then looked directly toward the southeast window.
      "You finished being cute? You really think I would be out here and busting into a crate if I didn't know there was some primo art inside that I can unload at Samson or direct to Yuri. That maybe I've already got some chemically treated paints oxidized to appear 132 years old, ready to put to canvas so I can double down and deliver John his 80% after I've moved both? Did any of that occur to you? No it did not. Because you have some toy binoculars and wanted to bust my balls while unloading yours. Talk to him tomorrow and find out."
      He could hear Vinny fuming on the other end, desperate to find a reason to look through a scope instead. Finally Vinny just hung up or he would have if Tommy/Yesod didn't keep the line open on a one way channel. He didn't want any other surprises from an enterprising, pissed off wise guy tonight.
      He went back into the crate and completed verification of the package: a blueprint with ink that scrambled into six different patterns that interchanged with varying frequency. After rolling it up carefully, he placed it into a pressurized tube that he then carefully put in a satchel on his shoulder.
      With that secure, he replaced the painting into its frame and turned it over hoping for something he might make good on. He was surprised to find a Goya staring him in the face. While he was glad to have something that could get him out of the Vinny jackpot, the painting that he now secreted away to the cart did not bode well.
      Putting it out of mind, Tommy/Yesod left the container and bolted it back up. "This is Unit Yesod calling in. Bring out Unit Gemurah now. Something feels off. Nothing to do with the man on the phone. We should be out in five though."
      The air crackled for a moment and like that there was a large man sitting in the carriage of a wrecking ball parked in the pier. He stretched his legs lazily on top of the console and looked down at Yesod with a glazed expression, "Not like you to call early. We've done this I don't know how many times off-hand. Maybe that thug got under your skin.
      Yesod wanted to press the point but there wasn't time. His eyelids flickered as he ran through every subroutine programmed into this iteration and then his body seemed to peel away, scattering like strips that then joined back together and in that moment he was transformed. The satchel had melted away and the tube slotted into the left compartment of his true form: a hovering hour glass turned on its side.
      The tube burst apart and looked like millions of tiny dots. Ribbons of light moved back and forth, analyzing the blueprint for retention of the information while it prepped for transport. There was little that could conceal itself once Yesod took his true form and in that moment he saw two demons. He played the light pattern in a broken hybrid of Morse code and shorthand terms unique to Gemurah and himself to alert him of their company. All hell broke loose.
      One of them started to move toward Yesod with a curious expression on its face. Before it could break out, Gemurah shorn his skin and let loose a surge of electricity, causing the wrecking ball to start abruptly. It slammed into the moving demon and split it into two but not before Yesod saw that it had readied a charge to go on impact. He tapped into the phone line quickly as he heard Vinny wondering what the hell was happening, "Get off the binoculars now!"
      The warning had come too late. A blinding plume of white light engulfed the pier, its effects like concentrated sunlight. There was a scream over the phone line and Yesod knew that Vinny hadn't turned away in time. If his optic nerves weren't fried completely and brain scrambled, he'd have to wear bifocals or something for the rest of his days at best.
      The second demon stationed by the container hatched out of his skin and swerved rapidly on his stomach through the burning light toward Gemurah. He seemed to be all limbs, wrapping itself around Gemurah and constricting the movement, squeezing Gemurah so tight that it would have snapped three bands of steel without pause.
      Yesod kept trying to call in to Binah as it was evident to him that these demons did not care whether they made it back. "I haven't finished the transition but the information is backed up. Let me stop this and assist Gemu...," "This eventuality was not past our foresight and indeed we warned you of compromise. You are not to abandon your position. Secure the package. Gemurah will handle this and if not---there are others."
      Yesod disabled his autonomic defenses to free up processing speed but he wouldn't risk anything more in the pursuit to help Gemurah. The many tendriled demon began to leak acid from the end of each limb affixed to Gemurah. Yesod began to see beneath the chestplate of the behemoth to gears whirring. Gemurah dislodged his jaw and clamped down on the demon's head, a surge of power flowing through the gears, past his throat and lining each of Gemurah's teeth. "We had a good run. See you soon."
      The gears slowed and Gemurah focused everything to immolate himself and the other demon who tried to disengage. He held him fast and in the next instant a red light shot upward piercing through the white flare that was obfuscating everything in the pier. Yesod hadn't quite managed to pull up his defenses in time and the surge buffeted against him. Some of it he deferred into finishing the transport but the rest was burning him as he hovered there.
      New parameters are to collect the cores and eyes of the aberrations and Gemurah. Afterwards, go into the shipping container where an extract will occur. There's too much risk to using you as an asset.
      Yesod used the cover of the corona to move about the pier and collect the cores along with eyes as directed. This mission seemed doomed from the get. And what was with the first demon---it barely did anything. These were the thoughts running through his head as he regarded the eyes on the ground. Confusion bloomed as he began to read an energy signature emanating from the eyes and that's when it happened.
      A flicker of recognition flashed in the disconnected eyes before coils shot out and wrapped about Yesod, breaking through the plating and transport chambers.He was dragged onto the stone masonry of the pier and being rewritten. Every function abandoned him and sound would not heed his call. The white and red light blanketing the pier dispersed in one last blaze.
      He dimly realized that he could see the stars now. The fog was gone, burned away. The shipping container looked like it had been smelted. He struggled to his feet and tried to nudge the bolt. There was no give. Scraps of metal were littered about the ground and somehow half of the cart, which he stood in front of to brace himself survived with miraculously the painting intact and now concealed by him.
      A whisper was registering inside his head that he knew could only be that demon who never lifted a finger. Reginald must have heard something because he was making his way down to see Tommy. Something was wrong with this whole mission: the Goya painting that held the blueprint was none other than "All Will Fall."

          A pale brunette waits at the Indian restaurant next to the city park, thumbing with less-than-rapt interest through several activism-oriented Twitter feeds and Tumblrs on her slightly outdated iPhone. She suddenly goes perfectly still; even the gold-on-green fabric of the sari she wore for the occasion refuses to make the slightest flutter. Green eyes flash to the broad window showing the park and the night sky as her phone slips into her purse. She stands, letting a few pound notes tumble from her hand to the table to pay for her drink as she leaves. "Give the councilor my apologies," she tells the proprietor as she slips a practical black fleece jacket on over her curve-flattering outfit. After slinking way through the light crowd coming in for dinner, the politically active heiress drops her indulgent expression and watches the sky blankly as she walks on in her low, gold-flecked heels.

          Her flat is only one block down - "Much more practical than a mostly-empty mansion," she always tells anyone who asks. It's certainly more practical than a mansion that doesn't really exist. She lets the indulgence of one relieved breath slip out as she closes the door behind her and pulls out the phone again. "Edward, bring the car."

          A few minutes later, Stephanie Marks descends to the street again. Her wavy, dark hair has been pulled back in a ponytail and mostly covered by a black beret. Black turtleneck, black slacks, flat black shoes. A somewhat redundant black scarf drapes around her neck.
          "Milady." Pushing nearly two meters in height, and not much less in width, the cultist in a black suit seems to make even the black luxury car he waits beside seem small.
          "Not where people might hear, Edward," she grouses back at him, having resigned weeks ago to this little ritual. Oh well.           "Where to?" he asks once they're both in the car. "Just drive... toward the water. I'll know when we're closer. Probably." She looks out at the sky again. "Be ready to drive... defensively."

                      The cleaning cart slid backwards with the weight Tommy was applying to stay upright and he fell back to the ground. The stones were slick with oil and other liquids, bringing to sharp relief the chaotic splintering and explosion of metal and melted plastics. Hearing Reggie in the distance, his first impulse was to stand back up but forgot which hand to lead with. The metal dug a little deeper and hit at raw nerve, the pain radiating everywhere robbing him of his voice for a moment and the footsteps drawing closer creating a momentary panic. Then he heard a different voice that elicited something else: anger.
                      "This is just not your day, is it. Man can I relate. All I wanted was to breathe in that salty air and catch a sunrise hitting the water. Me and my associate. Guess we'll figure out some sort of arrangement though, right pal?"
                      Tommy cast his eyes about before realizing no one else was there. Yet anyway. But the damage had to be more severe than he could see to bring on auditory hallucinations. He tried to calm his senses and reach out, willing for a connection back to his handler. Hard as he tried though, it was like trying to jump an ignition with a dead battery. There was no response but worse than that was the feeling of losing where the lock, key and starter are at all.
                      "Yeah, you're not phoning home pal. And you haven't lost your marbles. I hate to say that you're not going to be able to flit out of here---that would be ill-advised anyway. You should get out of dodge though, yes?"

This proved to be an mistake and he recoiled inwardly at trying to touch what was there. The energies that he once relied on without thought and with a cleanness of flow now felt soiled and smudged, like the fumes of gasoline without the gas.
                      Finally he resigned himself to the foreign voice, "You have sullied me. If you think that I'll let any of this stop me though from finishing my mission you are sorely mistaken. The package backed itself inside my memory and that is inviolable. There's at least 15 hours for me to get this to a drop-box. You failed."

More useful items like the toolbox which he was fairly certain had valuable alcohol stowed away thanks to another worker, Lester, who applied the Eagle Scout philosophy of preparedness to getting shit-faced and then there was the cell phone he taped into the receptacle for emergencies---kind of like this.

                                                          Tommy walked up the rear stairs and sat in a little bench on the porch. He ignored Jack's warning and waited until Gaines opened the door. Gaines took tentative steps toward the opening, using a cane to help his steps. He reached out with a wrinkled hand and lightly brushed at Tommy's shoulder.
                                                          "Come in quick, boyo. You can tell me what is happening inside." Tommy didn't need to be asked twice. He stepped in and sat down at the tiny table in the kitchen while Gaines did the padlocks and chains. Rhythmic taps as Gaines walked his wood floor with taps on his slippers and a tap from his cane.
                                                          He gripped the back of the chair Tommy was sitting in, then onto his shoulder, "What in blazes have you got into this time, boy." One more squeeze before Gaines walked over to the stove, "I was about to make some hot chocolate before turning in. Sure you'll say no like usual but---"
                                                          As if on cue, Tommy's stomach grumbled and he realized that he'd be hungry from now on, "Actually that sounds great Kingsley. Would it be trouble to have a sandwich too?" Kingsley Gaines laughed as he rummaged through the fridge, pulling out some provolone, salami and whole milk, "I don't think I've ever heard anything remotely like that come from your stomach. Are you really the Daniel I know?"
                                                          Tommy let the question go by quietly. He pulled a rag hanging from a bar in the kitchen and tied it where the catheter was, tossing the tubing into a nearby trash can. "It's not a social call unfortunately. That bag I left here, clothes and phone. I need it. An hour or ninety minutes tops to get straightened up."
                                                          Three ticks before the pilot light took and the milk was set on the stove with three bits of chocolate melting in, "You don't have to ask for that," he said with the chiding one might get from a family member.
                                                          Tommy pulled the rag tight before resting his hands on the table, leaning back in the chair and sighing loud, "I know, Kingsley. It's just been a very, very long night and it's not over. I need the van too. Key'll be in the wheelwell. I'll let Parker know in the morning." Kingsley reached up to the cabinet and pulled out some rye bread and a skillet. Butter from the fridge and a spatula. "What happened tonight, Daniel."
                                                          Tommy sighed, "It's Tommy, Kingsley. And I---tripped." The bread sizzled a little as it was pressed into the pan with cheese and meat on top, "That's what I said. Don't pull my leg. You're still messing around with those hooligans. Maybe I should have turned you in when you broke into the shop. You certainly haven't changed your ways."
                                                          Tommy stood up and went into a different cabinet, pulling out mugs and plates. His hand brushed a picture taped to a gaudy yellow cup. It was of Kingsley and his son Daniel. They were both laughing or something. Tommy had never asked about what. He didn't want to.
                                                          "I've got something lined up. One good thing to happen tonight. A Goya over at Fredericks Construction." He put the plates and mugs on the counter where Kingsley almost scalded him with food and drink alike. He sat back down knowing this was not what Kingsley wanted to hear.
                                                          "Unless Fredericks has decided to expand, I am very surprised to hear this. He doesn't know from paintings to beans if you asked him who Goya was. This has to do with that business on the pier, doesn't it." The plate clattered as it met the table and a bit of grease hit Tommy on the cheek. Kingsley placed the mugs and slowly sat down across from Tommy.
                                                          To assuage hunger and take shelter from the probing, Tommy took a sip from the hot chocolate. He winced as it burned some even as he let out a contented sigh at the first thing he'd really eaten. Kingsley sighed and reached a trembling hand to grasp at Tommy's hand, "I just don't want you to get hurt, Da--Tommy. You just remind me of him."
                                                          Tommy leaned back until their hands weren't in contact, "Yes. It does. If you need me to go, I understand. I don't have what we agreed on to partner up and these are dangerous people. Just let me clean up and I won't bother you again."
                                                          He retreated into the grilled cheese and salami sandwich, the blood loss and drugs making him hungrier than he otherwise would be and nausea was creeping in. The whole situation made him uncomfortable. It was easy to turn off the old man's stories and kindnesses when he could tune in to another channel and update a detail for a transaction with Yuri or John.
                                                          Kingsley was only a little absentminded. He stood up too fast and his balance was a little off. Tommy made to help him but Kingsley waved him away. He gripped his cane and claimed his mug, turning sharply or as sharp as he could to make his way to the stairs,
                                                          "You're not getting away that easily, Mr. Facilli. You broke a window and had Svensborg clock pieces in that rucksack when I caught you. You have a debt. I can't seem to get you to work it off honestly. Do what you have to tonight and then get that painting. At your commission for sales of purloined treasures, that should give you a quarter of what you owe me. I trust you'll lock up when you leave. Bag is in the closet behind the towels. Good night, Daniel."
                                                          Tommy almost said something but the last word made him quiet. He returned to his meal, then washed the dishes, made his way up the stairs and retrieved the bag with fresh clothes, a little walking money, basic picks, knife and phone.
                                                          He zipped the bag over his hand so the stitches wouldn't get soaked as he showered. The dried blood flaked away and went down the drain. He almost forgot the next step as the hot water came down and then Jack spoke up, "How long were you down here? You've got more than a few people who know you. Very odd arrangement."
                                                          Tommy leaned against the tiles and let the water drip, "The crate was delayed. Before that, I just kept building. Stealing. Kingsley caught me one night, as I'm sure you figured out. The full gaze of my handler was on me as Kingsley walked that slow walk with those tap slippers---I made those because he'd miss a step and I had to keep lending my shoulder to help him ambulate about the store. It was a pain. He doesn't fall anymore really now that he waits for those clicks. Where was--right, the full weight.
                                                          It was like walking the surface of a high gravity asteroid, bones crunched and veins popped. Kingsley sat me on the couch and dabbed my head with a washcloth. He thought I was having some sort of panic attack and said we could work it out. My cover was saved, stronger than ever actually. I split my time between painting fakes and fixing up broken sundries for the shop and the janitor thing too. It was perfect."
                                                          Tommy dried off and brushed his teeth. "Sounds like you were due for ground patrol any day. An Escher angel, looped tumbler. Just be glad your pasta loving friends haven't picked up the slack."
                                                          Tommy unzipped the bag and put on the black jeans, white shirt and gray track jacket hastily with a set of sneakers. He stuffed the various items from the bag in pockets and left without further delay through the back. Key in the wheelwell, turn the ignition and peel out from the alleyway to Fredericks Construction. With almost perfect detachment, "You're probably right."
