    Originally posted by nikink View Post
    If the game rules are there for breaking / ignoring why have them at all?
    Really?

    OK, sure. The rules aren't there for breaking/ignoring. They're there to facilitate awesome stories about Fallen biomechanical angels. If at any point the rules aren't doing that, it's worth trying to figure out why.

    Let's go back to my example with Freudian Slip. Let's say that Amy (the demon who used that power on Simon) had done so one session earlier, on Comrade West, as they were talking about the Demon's Republic of Seattle in Priscilla's apartment (you want context, read the SAS, I'm on the clock, here). If she had, it wouldn't have worked.

    Why? Because West, though he may be low-Cover, depending on the day, is a very much in-control-of-himself demon. The scene wasn't set in a time-splinter, nor was there an inordinate amount of magic flying around. More to the point, it wouldn't have remotely served the story for the power to work in a non-standard way.

    The answer is, you have the rules to provide context, but, likewise, the rules only work in context. It's why I don't like ruling on hypotheticals, and my answer is usually "I don't know, it would depend on what you want to do with your game."

In mine, though, I'm going to need to houserule it (and so will the many other groups that have player distributions like mine).         But what happens if KP gets a contested supernaturat resistance trait roll added to it before the game goes to press? Well, my group can play without any houserules required, and so can the other groups that are similar to mine. KP remains a great power, but the resistance roll means that it is not quite so reliable against supernatural antagonists. It has benefits and drawbacks.         And your group and groups like them? They are cool with this too! They aren't too bothered about optimal combat stuff, so whether or not KP allows for supernatural resistance rolls doesn't impact them too much either way. They'll take it or not, depending on whether it fits the fiction of the character concept that they have decided upon. Diverse character powers are the result. Awesome.

        So, this being the case, doesn't it make sense to add the supernatural resistance roll to KP? It is the solution that everyone can be happy with.

            Originally posted by nikink View Post
            If the game rules are there for breaking / ignoring why have them at all?
            Rules exist for those who are not yet comfortable breaking them. New storytellers and beginner players who love the ideas, but don't yet have a grasp for the system. As well as experienced roleplayers who simply don't have a head for numbers. They exist to help reign in power gamers and overzealous Storytellers, and keep everyone on roughly the same level (theme-wise, not D&D power level). I can't count the number of times having hard rules helped me reign in and refine a character (both PCs and NPCs) who was getting out of hand, and resulted in a better character overall.

            Put simply, every system is someone's first system, and rules are there to help.

                                                As I like to say - game balance is a hobgoblin. What matters is if the players are stomping on each other's feet; if that is not happening and everyone is having fun then the PC group being combat bad asses means the GM is just going to need to keep that in mind and use other types of challenges as bottle necks. Demon, more so, follows on the heels of the GMC rules paradigm. Combat is no longer entirely special, it's being treated as more or less equal to any other game interaction including rules to speed through it. The Demon powers list treats this as the case, and as a result has some combat powers that match "I win" style powers usually reserved for stealth, talking, healing ... errr everything else. If you want combat to be special like before in a way nWOD has decided it should not be that might mean removing powers that treat it like any other player challenge instead of sacrosanct. But citing that as an issue with Demon as it is, well, that might be missing what GMC did.
