            If a condition came with the cover or represents a social problem(or benefit), It applies to that Cover.

            If a condition affects the demon physically(as much as one counts), it applies across covers.

            EXAMPLE: If a demon picks up a drug-addict as a Cover, then he has to deal with the drug addiction in that cover. If a demon starts abusing drugs himself in any cover, then that addiction carries across Covers, because it's demon with the addiction, regardless of his tattered soul clothing.

                I use the philosophy of comparing conditions to tilts in this particular respect. Physical tilts like Arm Wrack are Cover specific - because if you sprain or break your Cover's arm, and you are a sixteen limbed monstrosity from the mechanical depths of heaven, it hardly makes sense that it still applies to you in say Demonic Form (much less any other cover) - so, therefore, the question that must be asked is: Is the addiction physical or mental?

                Most addictions in reality are both. So, I would use ArcaneArts explanation above. If the Cover came with an addiction, and the Demon doesn't wantonly abuse the addiction while posing as the Cover, then the Demon herself would not be affected as she is not mentally addicted to the drug. She is only physically addicted to the drug because the Cover was physically addicted to the drug. (The Demon does not inherit the psychological impulses of the people they steal the lives of, I should think. But they are certainly expected to act like those people if they do acquire the Cover.)

                This creates an interesting premise: The idea of dangerous Covers. In these examples, at first, the Demon would surely not be addicted as it is a physical malady of the Cover. However, as she is required to live the life of the Cover in order to maintain it she would be then forced to either convincingly appear to still be addicted OR to actually take the drugs and risk becoming psychologically dependent (and gaining the Condition across all Covers.) This is made worse as the Cover has the physical addiction and so while assumed, the Demon will crave just as she would hurt with Arm Wrack.

                Now: If a Vampire uses this unique bite mechanic on a Demon, we have another scenario. This is applying an addiction in real-time, which means it is applying a physical dependency on the Vampire's blood to the Demon's Cover, and a psychological dependency on the Vampire to the Demon actively. But, as was earlier suggested by MCN, a Demon is still a Demon even when wearing a Cover and as a result would require the Vampire to expend Willpower to use it. If the Vampire is unaware the Demon is a Demon, then she'll need to act the part or risk compromise. If the Vampire knows the Demon is a Demon, then all she has to do is use that Willpower point.

                Or, so is my assumption.

                        Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
                        Does this apply to other things that state "Mortal" ? I cant think of anything off the top of my head...
                        Check the section in Blood & Smoke, under feeding, p94-95.

                        In reference to the effects of a bite, the word "mortal" applies to humans, ghouls, warlocks, and other things that are mostly human. Vampires, werewolves, mummies, fairies, and other things far removed from humanity are not normally subject to the Assault and the Kiss. The bite is pleasurable or unsettling; just not mechanically so.

                        If the vampire knows she's feeding from something not human, and knows that it is otherwise immune ot the benefits of her bite, she may spend a point of Willpower to give them Scarred or Swooning Conditions as normal. Kindred are aware of this effect, and see its use as abhorrent and degrading. Kindred who rely on it with their own kind often end up ostracized or worse.

                        So, being a supernatural isn't enough protection against the normal "mortal only" Kiss / Assault, assuming awareness on the part of the vampire. It makes sense that being a supernatural isn't enough of a protection against the Addiction power of the merit either. It makes sense, storywise, to have even werewolves and fae beings addicted to the kiss of a succubus. I can see where people would disagree, though I feel that comes down, in the end, to the usual "I want my favorite splat to come out on top of this!" arguments.

                        Almost all crossover arguments come down to fangirls/boys arguing on the side of their favored supernatural. In this case, I think its moot, really, considering that, if a vampire can get the Kiss going, they're also very likely going to be able to slip a Vinculum and Blood Addiction in, which we know apply to all supernaturals now. Being addicted to a bite won't matter if they're also under a three step bond and addiction from just making out in the corner.


                        EDIT: Its kind of important to also note that, in this case, the Kiss seems to be affecting mages by default, since they're "mostly human." So, simply being a greater template, or qualifying as a supernatural, is not enough to be resistant to the Kiss (or the succubus merit either).
