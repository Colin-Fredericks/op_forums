    Well, whatever the issue, unless you need a power that scales in dots, Numina are pretty good for whatever you need. Since Embeds don't scale in dots and we're given examples of Angels who have Embeds in power (not to mention it's said that the G-M created these subroutines for Angels, than I'd say it's a pretty fair fit to give them whatever Embeds you think are appropriate.

    Demon is very straightforward on the notion that the GM can alter things to suit the situation, such as by way of including resistance or contested checks on Embeds that don't seem to have them already. If you as a GM feel like an Embed such as Just Bruised is too powerful for the Angel to have, don't give it to them. I don't see the particular problem with this requiring the judgement of a GM. Hunter is much the same when it comes to designing monsters. Certain combinations of Dread Powers might be too much for your average hunter. Use your own judgement to determine that, and don't hesitate to not include one power or another if that's the case. It's pretty easy to work with that.

    I'm of the personal opinion that if I give them an Embed as a Numina, I'm probably going to introduce a single Essence cost or two. it's a decent limiter. I'd fluff it as given that Angels aren't physical beings like Demons, they need the extra material push of Essence to effect the same backdoors of reality. I'd also probably make them just use Power + Finesse, but if you felt like it you could treat it like other skill rolls for ephemeral beings.

            A lot of people equate "create your own powers if those provided don't suit your needs" to house ruling, and at that point it's easy to see how people could connect that to the Oberoni fallacy, especially because it's often short handed to "it's not broken because I can houserule to fix it". However, the actual fallacy refers to ignoring or changing existing rules, not to creating new ones. A game system can never account for every possible situation, so the fact of the matter is you will always need to create rules to cover situations that the designers did not consider. You can call that house-ruling of you want to, but if you lump it under the rule 0 fallacy than you're calling every TTRPG "broken" because there will always be situations you need to "house rule" to "fix".

                My personal dissatisfaction with the angel power rules is that they are provided within a highly complex and tiered game system of influences and numinae and embeds and conditions and essence and what have you, which are then not elaborated with sufficient examples (this is subjective of course) and are brushed off with the ability to just create powers whenever however.

                If a game system is intended to be reliant on ST fiat and extensive improv then its best to be explicit about that and not to confuse the issue by providing such numerous and complicated rules that you are then encouraged to "ignore" for the lack of better term. One problem with having such a laissez faire attitude is that it keeps begging the question along the lines of "so why bother having rule [blah] there in the first place?" Some people are not very bothered by those questions but others are. It's a comprehension issue. I very much empathise with pcontop's position as I've spent a not insignificant amount of time trying to "solve" the write up of angel powers and figure out when, how and what to use. Mostly unsuccessfully so far.

                I understand *why* we have the write up that we have (compromises, space constraints, conventional wisdoms etc) but it certainly is not a chapter that is easy to use. I very much hope that the Storyteller's Guide provides more guidance on how to use angels.

Feel free to stick with the tricks provided, or to add your own, but the entities are still beholden to the rules of Manifestation.

An Angel's influences are things related to either (or both) it's nature or it's mission. Depending on how many dots it has in these influences, it is able to do more with them.

                                        In the above example, the demons are attempting to get away in a car. The angel is in pursuit. Slowing down the demon's by changing the traffic signs is within the scope of it's mission (It's a destroyer angel sent to chase and kill demons who will, of course, attempt escape). So, it rolls Finesse + Rank and you, as the storyteller simply decide whether success means all lights along the road go red, or a number of lights equal to rank or successes. Activating the sleepers in a nearby car? Power + Rank, each success awakens a mortal as a sleeper agent. (Not all mortals are sleeper agents, but one-shot NPC fodder don't need to be explained, Angels are adept at using whatever resources they happen to have in their local environment). Starting the car? Finesse + Rank, success turns the vehicle on. Finesse+Rank drives it.

                                        These are all solid rolls you as the story teller make and -could- fail on, either to represent an angel's momentary hesitation or confusion, or encountering a situation it does not know how to easily resolve quickly. This general performance in light of its mission affords angels some of the greatest power, and indeed gives them whatever tools they happen to need to perform their mission. Embeds and Exploits represent individual hacks the Demons have created in an effort to replicate this ability along with the more specific abilities of Numina.
