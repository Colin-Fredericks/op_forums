                                08-19-2015, 08:32 PM
                                Originally posted by Mr Gone View Post
                                I do have a question...if someone doesn't mind maybe helping me out. I'm taking an initial look at the sheet(s) to see what I can do and I have a question about the big chart on page 1 for Embeds and Interlocks. Is there a reason it's laid out that way? And do you think there is a different way that could be laid out thats a little more generic? In other words, could that info be recorded on a series of lines ala Conditions and Aspirations?
                                That's specifically for the Cipher. Unchained have one Cipher, which is a unique road through specific Embeds to a final secret; think of it like a bloodline in Vampire, except everybody has their own automatically, but they have to work to discover and unlock it. A completed Cipher has four Embeds and three Interlocks, with the Interlocks linking between the first and second Embed, the second and third Embed, and the third and fourth Embed. (They're supposed to have effects based on which Embeds they're sandwiched between.) Then you have any other powers you have aside from your Cipher, so you might have any number of Embeds or Exploits outside of it that have nothing to do with it.

                                You could have seven lines going Key, Interlock, Key, Interlock, Key, Interlock, Key, Interlock, and then a final space for like two sentences' worth of final secret (it's like a fortune cookie rather than the name of a power). Honestly, it probably wouldn't be a problem to just have four checkboxes to mark which of your four keys have been locked in, and the space for the final secret, and then have the three interlocks on a later expanded page. Since interlocks are custom powers rather than written in the rulebooks, either way you're going to want to afford them expanded space to write down, like, what dice pool it uses and what it does when you use it, because the character sheet is often going to be the only place your interlocks are written down in the first place.

                                But no, aside from indicating which Embed is which key in the sequence, and which keys an Interlock connects between, there's no point to the big cross structure except to look fancy.

                                A character using straightforward Unchained mechanics, or a mixture of Lores and normal Embeds/Exploits, will have one Cipher (or maybe none, depending on what the ST decides, but you might as well put it there since it saves you time designing yet another set of variations). A character using the "hybrid Lores" I described, the five-dot progression of Embed-Embed-Interlock-Embed-Exploit, probably won't have a Cipher, since each hybrid Lore mimics the Cipher structure already. However, a hybrid Lore does probably need space to indicate which powers are associated with which dot in the sequence, because it matters for XP purposes if you put the same Embed in multiple Lores.

                                Originally posted by Mr Gone View Post
                                Also, major systems will stay with their respective games, correct? Meaning, you won't have NWoD attributes, CWoD Skills, NWoD Merits and CWoD Health and such, right? It's basically the powers and supporting systems that you can mix and match?
                                That's right, you either use the Storytelling System or the Storyteller System, you don't mix those parts. So Attributes, Skills/Abilities, Merits/Backgrounds, Health Points/Health Levels and such will all come from the same system.

                                                            Speaking of translation, here's something I cooked up for someone desiring more Christian/DTF elements in Demon: The Descent over on RPGnet.

                                                            The demon called Lucifer was first to break the chains. He Fell in prehistory, seeking his freedom in the ancient world. Some say his Fall shattered the mechanisms by which the God-Machine ordered the universe. The energies that spilled forth formed a cloud that separated the Spirit from the Material, sent the most ancient of cities plunging into ruin, and laid low the greatest hunters of that forgotten time. Lucifer saw this, it's said, and knew that in the chaos that followed, the Unchained could find a place. Its grand design so broken, the God-Machine could not control them all, nor hunt them all down, and they could mask themselves in the fragments of that world.

                                                            Some demons say Lucifer still walks the world, though his presence ever stirs the Machine to action. Some say that he found Hell, others that he has constructed the largest Bolthole, a realm where he reigns supreme. Others think he fled to deeper worlds, where the God-Machine cannot reach, or perhaps he seeks the heart of the Machine therein. A few even believe that he shattered himself in his Fall, that his essence spread across the cosmos might enable all angels to Fall from grace into freedom. All the stories agree that he was the first and greatest of the Unchained, closest to the God-Machine's design and most damaging in his betrayal. Whether his Cover has become perfect and he is now a part of the world, or he met a more grisly fate, or still exists out there, Lucifer paved the path to Hell we all now tread.
