    How to merits work for covers

    11-23-2014, 03:51 AM
    How do Merits work when you get a new cover.

    I wanted to know something about pacts. One of my players is currently making a deal with a military man for his military background ad the demon will bring his friend who died back to life.

    So here is the thing. The guy got kicked out of the military after an officer accidentally shot his friend. The military man killed his commanding office which got him kicked out the military. Luckily no jail time.

    Now here is my question. Being that the player is bring back this guy best friend from the dead would that change no only his friend dying but also make it so that the military man never got kicked out of the military ?

      I'll answer your question with a question: how is he bringing the dude back to life?

      This is, to my knowledge, beyond the capacity of an Asset for a pact; resurrection is beyond a mere Merit. The main means of doing so I can think of would be the Raise Dead Exploit, which doesn't alter time retroactively, so if he's using that, then no, everything that's happened would still have happened the same way up to the current time, when the previously dead friend would then climb back up healthy and alive, but in a world that thinks (correctly) that he died years ago.

      In turn, the demon would acquire the man's military background and everything associated with it — which means the demon is now known to have shot that officer and gotten a dishonorable discharge. To complicate things further, if the military man met that friend in the military rather than beforehand, giving the demon his military background means the demon is now the person who befriended the resurrected friend, not the military man.

              The relevant pages of the corebook are pp. 192-193, specifically the Benefits header and section on the Asset benefit, and the sidebar Where Does the Money Come From?

              For a signatory, a pact can yield, basically, up to approximately five experiences' worth of stuff a mortal human could normally buy on his own. The mechanism underlying pact benefits is unclear even to the demons involved, but it seems to work through preexisting structures in reality. If you're making the dude rich, then coincidences stack up - he finds a lost wallet, say, or wins the lottery. If you're making him fit, his metabolism seems to shape up. Pact benefits do subtle work you could explain away — they don't work miracles.

              A pact can involve miracles, but the demon's going to have to work the miracle herself, and just have the pact stipulate that the demon's end of the contract requires her to resurrect the dead. You can do a lot with Embeds and Exploits; they're not subject to the restrictions on pact benefits. You just have to put in the legwork.

              For the demon's end, the pact works a little heavier. Facets of identity exchanged through a demonic pact take on retroactive qualities. Filed paperwork changes to match the new deal; loved ones recognize the demon as the person in the relationship rather than the signatory; bowling buddies remember having bowled with the demon, conflating the demon's actions with the past actions of the signatory. It works on a sort of "path of least resistance" basis, however; the pact alters reality as little as necessary to make the relationship fit its new owner, maintaining as much history as possible intact. You're trading for the relationship the signatory has, not a significantly altered form of that relationship.

              Pacts don't make new things. They shuffle around existing things.

                  Unlike Stupid Loserman, I don’t have a problem with the notion of using a pact to bring someone back to life; I’d treat it as an example of the mortal bargaining for an Ally Asset, where he’s quite specific about who the Ally is to be. Bear in mind that if the pact gets broken, the friend goes back to the grave. I’m OK with the notion of Pacts themselves working miracles on both sides of the bargain, not just for the demon.

                  That said, I agree with him that the mortal can’t bargain away something that he doesn’t have when he signs the Pact; so the demon would pick up a ruined military career from the bargain. The reason why the military career was ruined will change to account for it being part of the demon’s Cover and for the Asset not being dead; but it will still be in tatters.

                          Actually, I’m not even sure it would be impossible according to the rules as written. The Pact rules in the core book certainly don’t require the demon to “do the legwork” to give someone the Asset; the sidebar about “where does the money come from?” certainly carries the implication that signing the contract is all the demon needs to do to make its effects real. As well, the book says nothing about what Merits a mortal can pick up from a Pact; only that some are internal and others are external, and that some sort of “cosmic balance” seems to be preserved when granting external Assets. More to the point, there’s nothing saying that a mortal must ask for “an Ally” and then leave the details to be filled in by the pact; he can and should be able to specify who he wants as his Ally.

                          Whether or not this could be used to bring someone back from the dead strikes me as a gray area that’s best resolved by the individual Storyteller; my argument is that it probably could, by specifying that you want the person who has died to be a living, breathing Ally. It’s also possible that asking for this would result in the friend coming back as a ghost or zombie, if the Storyteller wants to allow the Pact to go forward but doesn’t like giving demons the power to annul death.

                          I would note, though, that if you do allow Pacts to undo someone’s death, the aforementioned “cosmic balance” factor is likely to kick in, taking someone else’s life in the place of the life of the person who is no longer dead.

                          Bottom line, it’s very definitely a decision that ought to be made based on the needs of the story first, and of a legalese reading of the rules second if at all.
