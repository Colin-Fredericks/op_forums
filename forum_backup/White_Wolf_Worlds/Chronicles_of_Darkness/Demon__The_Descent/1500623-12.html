    Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
    So, I've never, ever read Mage: the Awakening; I've always loved Mage: the Ascension and didn't buy Awakening when I had the chance. I've read a lot of Requiem and in Ascension, magik can overcome anything; so, can mages in Awakening overcome VII's Clan Akhud's curse that no clan-member can betray another one of their clan; even mind-reading won't work?
    2e has the Akhud as a Lost Clan whose Clan Bane manifests as a scaling penalty to knowingly take any action that would harm another Akhud, their mortal descendants, and anyone in Vinculum with one of them, along with an explicit statement that "no compulsion, natural or otherwise," can force the Akhud to take such actions.

    As a point of comparison, prior to the crossover rules from the Player's Guide to the Contagion Chronicle, you could make the argument that the 2e corebook's statement that Disciplines not being able to coerce a frenzying vampire to act against the Beast's desires (instead of redirecting them) could be generalized to any supernatural power being incapable of stopping a vampire from frenzying, but now it's known that with two dots in Death and Mind and enough Potency to overcome the vampire's Composure a mage can cast a spell to end a frenzy. Those same rules also establish that Clan Banes can be studied in general with Fate (as curses) or in particular with appropriate other Arcana (as phenomena relating to the effects of a given Bane).

    Given that the Akhud's bane is described as both a curse and the product of an oath, as well as being framed in terms of knowing intent and and immunity to compulsion, it's theoretically within the power of the Arcana to allow one of the Akhud to overcome the penalty (whether by providing a corresponding bonus, negating the penalty, or messing with the Bane using the standard moderately-high-level spell for messing with oaths and other supernatural binding agreements), but e.g. forcing such an act with Psychic Domination would probably fail in the same way that you can't mind-control an angel of the God-Machine into abandoning its mission. Even outside the context of being a particular interpretation of how VII might manifest in a game, the Akhud are still rare and unusual enough for such a thing to serve as a Mystery in a game where Mage is contributing to the ludonarrative considerations.

    I can't speak to the 1e version of this conversation, because 1e never got into the Awakened's mechanical interactions with VII in any form save that a couple of nods in its general direction inform 1e's version of the expanded backstory of the Tremere liches, which 2e instead generalized/blurred to relate less explicitly specifically to vampires. Barring any other interpretation, it should at the very least be within the power of an archmaster using the Imperial Practices, which involve spells cast using required components whose acquisition takes entire stories due to needing to be difficult to acquire by someone with the resources of a half-Ascended demigod — all of which is to say that the answer is "yes" with the caveat of "though it might only be possible for characters that have passed the point where you can just call them mages."

        Originally posted by Cauthon View Post
        I assume an Attainment giving the Mage access to a Changeling's Mantle is a bit too far outside the norm, correct? Mechanically, I could see it MAYBE being a Fate spell to bestow Potency dots in a given Court's Mantle, but fluff-wise it seems to far afield.
        Some clarity and splitting of hairs on this one, starting with a biggie-Court Goodwill does not grant Mantle, it grants the effects of Mantle.

        Over in Changelingland, Satchel elaborated that Mantle is an accordant change to a changeling's mien, an element of their physical presence remolded (changed, even) by the metaphysical buy in to the bargain made with a Court's patron. Court Goodwill is a sociomystical connection (though not elaborated on in Kith and Kin, likely a dramatrugical connection) which allows the one who has that Goodwill to act as though they were a fairy (a changeling, more specifically) in a roughly correlating position of buy-in, but it's not the same as actually being such.

        A Mage who has Court Goodwill is likely going to see that dramaturgy play out in their Nimbus even going so far as to add it's iconography to it.

        A Mage who forges a Legacy around how deep they can get in with a Patron by way of the Wyrd is in for an interesting time and is likely going to stop being a mage proper* after some work.

        *The phrasing is weird here because of my particular hiccup of not treating mages as humans, which I will stand by, but I will also clarify people without that hangup are more likely to say "is likely to stop being human after some work."

                            Originally posted by Leofwyn View Post
                            New to Mage the Awakening so:

                            Why does having a higher gnosis lead to more paradox rolled on overreaching? It seems counterintuitive as the more a mage understands their magic, the more they should be able to fine tune it to avoid incurring paradox?
                            Gnosis isn't understanding so much as intuiting or self-revelating. While being able to better realize the ideal faster and, correspondingly, in more detail is certainly powerful, it's also means a mage has an increased ability to misconceive their imposition on reality, and it's in those cracks of imagination the Abyss slip through. Think of it like trying to solve complex math equations rapidly in your head-the more you practice at it, the more complex that computation becomes, but it also means you get used to thinking so fast that if you miss a detail or get the wrong conclusion, the more likely you're going to continue along with that error before you realize the mistake.

                            Or, for the Doylistic reason, because the more powerful you get, the more your poorly handled power should fuck up your day.

                                                            I need some help with some rules questions.
                                                            We're running a relatively high power mage game. Players are single arcana masters with 5 gnosis. They have decent stats, but not crazy. Some players min-maxed, some didn't. You know how it goes.

                                                            A lot of things seem unclear. Could the community help me understand some things?

                                                            1. Raising stats with magic
                                                            There are a number of rank 3 spells that allow a mage to raise their skills or attributes. As I understand, these are hard capped at 10. They soft cap at 5 first, which can be overcome with a reach and a mana, and then duration increased to a year for a Mage to have 10 dexterity and say 10 firearms and athletics for the year, increasing their base defense to 15 or 20, and their firearms rolls to 20+ base. This isn't even hard.
                                                            5 gnosis + 5 arcana
                                                            Yantras
                                                            rote 6
                                                            shadow name 3
                                                            high speech 2
                                                            location 2
                                                            ritual 5

                                                            28 dice pool plus Fate 2 exceptional luck to add 10 more, for a 38 dice pool to cast. Make potency the primary so it starts at 5, and then -10 for 5 more potency, and a couple minuses for duration and then split it between two stats for +5 (or more) to each.

                                                            Now the issue. I mentioned the cap at 10. This comes from interpretations I've seen here and the stat cap in the book. The spells are worded as such that they raise the stats up, simulating the character buying them with xp, so would hit the same caps that buying via xp would hit.

                                                            The player is saying that Vigor from vampire adds to strength in the same way that the spell honing the form does, and isn't capped at 10, as a blood potency 6 vampire can have a 6 strength + 5 vigor for an 11. They are being pretty rude about the 10 cap being one rule for mage, and one for everyone else.

                                                            What is your interpretation here? Should honing the form be able to give a character a 15 natural strength indefinitely?

                                                            2. Rote yantras
                                                            In the section it says it is capped at 5, but the Order specialty then adds a die. So if a mage has say 5 in Intimidation, and is an Adamantine Arrow, do they add 6 to their rotes from the mudra yantra?

                                                            3. Exceptional Luck
                                                            The Acanthus is using this to add 14 to their skill rolls, and literally double all their starting spell casting dice pools. This seems...insane. How do you manage this at your game? Do you care or does it affect your game?

                                                            4. Charmed Condition
                                                            If it isn't clear, the Acanthus is pushing the boundaries as much as possible.
                                                            So there is a spell that can grant the Charmed condition. For 3 reaches, it can be cast reflexively. So is there anything that stops him from simply casting it anytime something tries to hit him, you know, past the 19 defense and whatever armor, to grant the Charmed condition every time and then resolving it so the attacks never do more than 1 damage?

                                                            I feel like the distinction between tilts and conditions would limit this to like once per combat scene. I know there will be an argument about why it cant constantly be used, so wanted to understand what others thought.

                                                            5. Paradox on long duration spells
                                                            I read somewhere that when witnesses are present, at the end of the scene or chapter the spell would need to check for paradox if it originally had over reach. I can't find it now. Does anyone know anything about this?


                                                            6. Dissonance and long duration spells
                                                            How do you do Dissonance with 10+ potency over reach spells? if a mage has a huge spell on them with an indefinite duration, do you consider it apparent at casual observance, or only when they do something bonkers?

                                                            My thought is that high potency, over reached spells with indefinite duration 'unravel' when around sleepers naturally, and so if they go out in public just loaded with insane spells, the presence of sleepers begins to unravel their spells. This is something I'm going to constantly be rolling for, but more of a storyteller effect where they can't just super cast in their demense and then count on uber spells to always be up all the time.

                                                            7. Aimed spells and multiple targets
                                                            From my understanding, when a spell is cast with touch distance, it can then be fired at a target using Gnosis + Athletics. If the spell takes the multi target factor, then you just roll full Gnosis + Athletics for each target; you do not take a multi-target penalty. I feel this is true, as the multitarget penalty is baked into the autofire rules, and isn't based on normal attacks.

                                                            8. Armor
                                                            I have players with kevlar jackets and reinforced clothing coats, for 2/3 and 1/0 armor ratings. Do you think these should stack? What about using magic to make the coat a 9/0 armor and then stacking it with Mage Armor?

                                                            This isn't even difficult. A starting mage can get a dice pool to cast of
                                                            3 arcana
                                                            3 gnosis
                                                            3 shadow name
                                                            6 rote yantra
                                                            5 ritual cast
                                                            8 exceptional luck

                                                            20 without Fate, 28 with
                                                            20 with potency primary, one reach for advanced duration, - 14 for 7 more potency, willpower point, gives 7 dice to cast a month long 10 potency spell.

                                                            9. Can't Raise the stat, add to it!
                                                            If number 1 is so, would a creative thaumaturgy spell that added dice to strength dice pools stack with it?
                                                            Say, 1 month duration, and +_9 to strength pools, so the mage rolls 10 strength + 10 brawl + 9 added strength dice + dble specialty 2 = 31 dice to swing a punch? All with 3 life and 3 mind total
                                                            Does this seem right to you?


                                                            ​10. Shadow Name
                                                            In the Shadow Name, it states and gives an example of say Thor as a Shadow Name. Then when the mage does things related to the idea of Thor, say lightning and thunder powers, increasing strength to hit stuff with a hammer, jumping really high, they can use their Shadow Name as a Yantra. Now lets say Thor wants to use Fate 2 to cast bigger spells; would they be able to use Shadow Name as a Yantra?
                                                            How do you then use Cabal Theme in relation to this? Do your players just always have a +4 yantra at their disposal?

                                                            This one is rough. So I saw the clarification that it doesn’t affect the mage who is having the Nimbus effect. Does Nimbus then apply to everyone in the vicinity, friend or foe, if say it has a 9 potency? Does this mean a mage with -1 resolve, and +2 Firearms would give their foes +2 firearms and then what, 9 again on firearms checks?
                                                            This happens every time a mage casts? Does this cause any issues at your table with slowing things down?
