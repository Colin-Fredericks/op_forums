    Second case ; I dislike the idea that humans can 'cheat' demons by double-dealing and making them fight with one another. I'd suggest, rather, that demons DO in fact remove something 'physical' to them when the contract is signed ; such that, future demons could note 'hmm, you can't sell me your sister, you don't HAVE a sister'
    Not if it's not cashed.

    I'm fine with Mortals being able to double deal, because, let's face it. There's plenty of stories where the Demon gets outsmarted. Also, to clarify? If the person doesn't has the sister, it means the pact was cashed in already. If the pact exists merely as experience still? cool. I'd like to see the second demon noticing, at first glance, he's gonna get cheated.

    It makes for a good story, if anything. A Temporal Agency which is giving away more than one contract? Awesome.

    But this does in fact presume rendering down the soul-piece in question to a trading card. Once the victim 'sells' his sister, she vanishes from reality and effectively becomes a symbolic representation until she is given to somebody else or used herself.
    This is not the case. If the pact isn't cashed, life goes on.

    Mortals and demons have to be able to fulfill their contracts, so it shouldn't be possible for mortals to double-deal. Once the mortal agrees to sell his sister, she belongs to the Demon even if it just shelves the contract for a while. The mortal shouldn't be able to sell his sister to another Demon in the meantime.
    Again, the first and second demonic signataries got the rights to the sister until she's collected. Consider that if all your lesser, non-soul pacts should be cashed in immediately, crafting a patch up cover would be a nightmarish exercise.

    I'd much prefer to get several contracts, then cash them in together: say, 15 cover experiences for a 5 Cover new patch-job existance.

                See, the way it's written indicates to me that the only contract type where "payment" is held in abeyance are soul pacts, otherwise it's effects are immediate (or at least in motion, in the case of things that take time to come to fruition like Resources, etc.). You say "money for sister", he agrees, you sign, his bank account goes up, you get a sister and a piece of paper (or other media) holding the reality code that says the demon who possesses this has a sister which he can later trade. Until the moment the mortal signed the contract, she was his sister; from then until the moment you trade the pact away she's -your- sister.

                Yes, that means if you want to have a sister who's still alive - whether or not you plan to trade her away - you best take care of her; same as you would if you were cultivating a cover as Joe the Criminal who an arms dealer friend, Joe would have to decide whether or not his arms dealer friend was worth protecting and cultivating, even if the demon being Joe differs from time to time. Aside from the occasional person created whole cloth for a cover, people live their lives even if they're metaphysically tied to a Demon. If the cover is destroyed, or if you use houserules for things like dormant covers or the like, then reality isn't rewritten that your sister or arms dealer doesn't exist anymore, it's rewritten in such a way that she doesn't have a brother or the dealer doesn't remember Joe.

                I'd rather deal with the complications and bookkeeping of assuming everyone stays alive even if some of their memories are rewritten than removing them whole cloth out of reality and having to dealt with the bookkeeping associated with those holes. It's easier for me to keep track of. Also, I'm not a fan of people disappearing wholesale if they've been contracted away until it's fufilled because that's a way for demons to remove obstacles by dealing with people connected to those obstacles.

                                            Originally posted by Ephsy View Post

                                            That's even more of a headache.
                                            Not at all. Here's how I'd run it:

                                            Let's say a player gets five Cover aspects from various Pacts: Jimmy's sister, Mordechai's rabbi, James' schizophrenia, Alice's totes BFF, and Tammy's job.

                                            When the player bargains for these, the player gets a notecard with "Jimmy's sister" another with "Mordechai's Rabbi", etc., and how much Cover XP they are worth. These are kept with the character sheet.

                                            If a Cover aspect is traded to an NPC, the player hands that notecard to the Storyteller. If an aspect is traded to another player, the notecard is handed to the other player.

                                            If the Cover aspect is folded into a Cover, that aspect is written into the Cover's backstory, the XP is applied, and the notecard is handed to the ST. These held notecards are now plothooks for the ST to use.

                                            Once is happenstance. Twice is coincidence. Three times is enemy action. Four times is Infrastructure.

                                            Rule #2: Never let the game-mechanics get in the way of a good story.

                                                I've been thinking that after something's been bargained away it's just the relationship that goes into holding - the mortal sees immediate change. So if you buy Alice's abusive relationship with her boyfriend Bob, she isn't his girlfriend after she signs the pact. Until that pact is incorporated into a cover, Bob doesn't have a girlfriend. His memory's fairly foggy about it. There's a soft time limit here - if Bob processes that he's single and gets into a new relationship, activating the pact is going to be more confusing and risky when Bob suddenly has two girlfriends. But maybe Bob juggling two girls is believable. The bigger risk is if you buy the care of a dying relative and then don't show up to keep them alive. Or if you buy someone's job, it's not going to be long before it gets filled. Being the second guy to show up for one job is the kind of thing that draws the attention of the God-Machine. So in this setup there are some cover elements that are relatively easy to bank and trade - personal history especially - and some that are a lot harder, mostly involving persistent relationships.
