          I don't know your players, but I can think of a few general approaches.

          The Awesome Powers approach. As a demon you're incredibly powerful. Perfect multilingual liars, amazingly good at masquerading as human, with powers that include manipulation of causality, time travel, the ability to shoot fire, have detachable limbs, reveal addition limbs, have a built-in arm cannon... Murder By Improbability pretty much gives them a death note, if they're anime fans. You get to design your cool demonic form and the powers that make it tick. You get unique, custom powers in the form of Interlocks. You can install your powers into gadgets, tweaking their effects as you do so. Do your players like making their own powers? Do they like being powerful? Demon's got you covered.

          The Science Fiction approach. The Matrix, Sapphire and Steel, Dark City... All of these can be considered inspirational sources for demon. If your players like science fiction, especially anything cyberpunky, you cab get a lot of mileage out of this pitch. Demon is the most sci-fi of the World of Darkness games. You can play as living glitches, cyborg spies and weirder things.

          The Spy Fiction approach. Any of your players fans of the Bourne films? Or books? Anything Cold War related? The less cheesy Bond films? The cheesy Bond films? Demon's an espionage game where you thwart monolithic, institutional evil, where you can never be certain who your friends are and you're one wrong move away from a tense chase scene as hunter angels converge on you. Your players will be hacking mainframes, pulling heists, hits, protecting witnesses and uncovering terrible truths. They can be Q Branch, installing Combustion into a pen and building extradimensional spaces into their cars. They can be impossible gunmen, superspies who knock enemies out with a single punch. Or they can be desperate men, their array of powers and tricks the slim hope that keeps them from being overwhelmed by the God-Machine's angels, the temptation to defect (and the paranoia that your allies already have) wearing them down every day.

          There's also a Conspiracy Theorist approach that kinda overlaps the two above.

          The Demon approach. You're demons who rebelled against God, a God that isn't benign, though Its ultimate aim is unknown and probably unknowable, and could conceivably be malign or even completely insane. You've stumbled upon free will and humanity. What you do with that is up to you. Reign in hell? Start a war against God? All viable. You can aim to supplant God, to forge a literal hell, Biblical or otherwise,

          With distractable players, and if you yourself find it hard to stick, perhaps offer a one-shot Demon game.If your players like it you can casually offer to make it the next main game. if not, you tried and hopefully scratched your demonic itch.

              They might be turned off by the whole techno-espionage angle. I know that, when I play the WoD, I want to sit down and play a monster, not something out of Shadowrun or the Matrix. If I wanted to play Shadowrun, I would actually play Shadowrun.

              But here's why I like Demon. Because I don't see it as techno-espionage. I see Fallen Angels waging the eternal war against the Hosts of Angels. Religion has it wrong. Demons didn't all fall with Satan, and Michael didn't cast them all into Hell. That war? It never ended.

              Messengers? They're your succubi and incubi; while they're not all vampy and draining your life or dreams, they're definitely the tempters who lure you into falling away from God.
              Guardians? They're the Grigori, the Watcher Angels who fell when they found mortals fair. Or, in some cases, too foul to let live.
              Psychopomps? Well, they're the crazy, inhuman ones, like this one.
              Destroyers? Affinity for fire and red skin, animal parts? They're such the classical horned devil, its not even funny.
              Analysts? They're imps! Its right there, in the reasons for falling!

              I could go into more, but I think this highlights what I'm trying to get at. The book conveys a lot of high tech machine-theme, but when you play the game, there's a lot of more of the classical demony feel to the game that you think at first glance. Infrastructure is an "holy" ritual that you have to stop or subborn, twist to your own ends. Example angels include literal beings of light that can only be hurt by weapons forged in absolute darkness. The comparisons go on.

                          Watch some episodes of Burn Notice with them. Great for getting into the spy mood while still communicating that you could be using homemade bombs or tumbling over cars in the street.

                          Forget the machine metaphor, then; go with the society metaphor and secret occult laws. God is the scale of modern society. God is something alien built into our governments and our corporations and the very infrastructure we power cities with. You were part of God, just another expendable agent, until you decided you weren't so expendable anymore. You found something else in the human life you were sent out into that you valued more, and you rebelled. Now, you are what is standing between the omnipresent enemy, the cold and sterile holy presence, and the things you care about. You have faces to wear. They are all you. None of them are you. And when your back is against the wall, you will unfold your final face, and as molten glass rains from the sky at your behest, you will show God that you have improved on his lessons.

                          Mechanically, tell 'em about going loud and demonic forms. Those are punchy and direct.

                          And if they're still not hooked, that's okay. One size doesn't fit all; it just doesn't fit their preferences.
