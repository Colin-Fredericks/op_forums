                                To answer a few off the top of my head:

                                1) My understanding is that there is an intended hard cap of 10 on all Traits though it has been applied inconsistently across books.

                                This also includes Willpower, so even if you had Resolve 10 and Composure 10, Willpower capacity would be 10 rather than 20.

                                2) It's debatable but I think the Mudra bonus should be capped at 5 rather than 6.

                                3) Exceptional Luck is contentious but I think that the only Boon that can be applied to spellcasting is the 9-again.

                                4) Casting Exceptional Luck reflexively costs 1 mana, which also comes out of the per turn mana limit.

                                9) First think what a spell is actually doing rather than just looking at the mechanics listed in Creative Thaumaturgy. How exactly is the action being empowered by magic and then what makes sense?

                                But I will also say it is a basic rule of the system that the net modifiers to any roll can only total +5. Combined with my answer to 1) this means nobody should ever be rolling more than 25 dice.

                                For some more general advice: if the dice pools would be really big, don't even ask for a roll, just say it succeeds and move on to something more interesting.

                                The more time you and your players spend with the magic system and the Mage setting the more you'll come to realise that masters have a million better ways of dealing with problems than shooting them and also have enemies that cannot be shot and don't care about a mage's defence rating.

                                                    In order to answer your questions as clearly as possible, I will break down my explanation into 3 sections, one for rotes, one for Exceptional Luck and one for spellcasting rules.

                                                    Rotes provide mudra yantras. A mudra uses the mage's rating on the encoded skill as the yantra bonus. This provides a maximum value of +5. Which means if your rote skill is Expression, for example, a mage with 5 dots and one with 10 dots will both only get a +5 (core book page 121). If the encoded skill is an Order skill for the caster (found on page 82), they get one more dice, which may push the total to +6.

                                                    Exceptional Luck (detailed on page 135) can provide dice bonuses to mundane skills (or spellcasting rolls with 2 Reach), a bonus of [Potency] for up to [Potency] chosen rolls, which means it does have limited uses. It can also impose conditions and tilts based on the rules on page 134.

                                                    Spellcasting rules dictate that, after applying penalties, the effect of all yantras put together cannot provide a total bonus beyond +5 (details on page 112). Exceptional Luck is not a yantra, so its Reach effect is not bound by this rule.

                                                    Remember that there are downsides for having these spells always up. They only have [Gnosis] spell control, and every spell cast after that requires one Reach per spell above it (see the side tab on page 112). Relinquishing them unsafely requires a Willpower point, it also means they cannot cancel the spell themselves, they need to have it dispelled. It can also mutate after a while (see page 118). Socially speaking, relinquishing unsafely is illegal in many Consilia, or at least seen as grossly negligent. Best case scenario, the Guardians will dislike any long term use of unsafe relinquishment.

                                                    Alternatively, safe relinquishment is always an option, but it requires a Willpower dot. The downside is that it does not make the spell immune to dispellation and it will still vanish once the duration elapses.

                                                    Hope that helps.
