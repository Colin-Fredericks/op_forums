        Regular success will only ever give you one success on the standard effect. An exceptional success on the exploit gives you three successes. You can only mimic instant or reflexive powers, extended powers are verboten to you. It's contested by a pool of Wits + Supernatural Tolerance of the supernatural type you're mimicking. If you fail the roll, at all, in any way, any supernatural who is of the type of power that is being used knows exactly what's up. And every single use costs 1 Aether.

        It's an awesome ability, don't get me wrong (and hilariously versatile) but if you wanted ridiculous power, you'd be better off building around the Demon's own exploits, embeds and demonic form.

It's like the enemy flaw that many systems have that is secretly a bonus.
            G-M attention is nothing to sneeze at, and saying bringing down hunter angels only makes the demon's player happier is a bit like saying a manifestation paradox is exactly what a mage's spotlight-hogging player wants. But leaving that aside:

            - Every use of another critter's power costs Aether. That's gonna run out quickly.
            - It only works on instant or reflexive activation powers (no extended actions).
            - It only does the bare minimum. Mages can accept penalties to make a spell bigger, last longer, affect more targets. Demons with Show of Power can't.
            - Aside from an exceptional success, you're treated as scoring 1 success no matter how many successes you get. Good luck outshining the Forces mage when you're doing...1 damage per turn to one target... On an exceptional success you are treated as having scored three successes.
            - Someone already mentioned the problem where having supernaturals of the same type around contests your Exploit roll, so the more badass the company you keep the less likely you are to successfully spoof their powers. "Getting caught" is really the least of your problems there.
            - Demon form is scary if you build it for an intended purpose. I'm not convinced that I can't one-up a Celerity + mage armor + Garau form if I picked the right form powers, and that would only require one compromise roll, not three.

            Show of Power is a strong power with a lot of utility, but it's the old "jack of all trades, master of none" shtick. Get the book. If you feel like the power is too good or too difficult to adjudicate for the crossover game you're running, don't allow it. It's mainly intended for straight Demon games, with some potential in crossover games where the demon might need to occasionally play "assistant/backup X splat."

                                                            It seems fairly clear to me that if you're in a cross-over game you're already walking through the thin veneer of game-balance anyway. If I'm not mistaken OPP haven't made any cross-over specific books, and Flowers of Hell certainly isn't one of them. The purpose of the power is to allow a demon to mimic a supernatural because when they assume a cover that is a vampire, they are not actually a vampire, but human and so if a vampire tries to determine if they are - they'd see a lovely beating heart and thus be very curious how it is that this supposed neonate is still alive and not among the undead. Show of Power allows the demon character to accurately mimic such qualities as a weakness to the sun, a lack of the blush of life or indeed the ability to consume human blood.

                                                            More pertinent to this string of conversation is their ability to mimic instant and reflexive powers such as Celerity. Firstly: These powers are absolutely limited by that which the character encounters allowing the storyteller to limit exposure in order to maintain balance. Secondly, it will always call for a roll from observers - it says nothing about these observers having to be enemies of the character. Asking your friendly Mummy to turn his back is a bit suspicious... why do you need to turn your back, my supposedly Mummy friend, before you can reliably call your swarm of flesh eating scarab beetles? We've all seen it before, you needn't be bashful, I sent a swarm of flesh eating scarab beetles on a grumpy cultist just the other week!

                                                            If the other player characters know that that character is a demon then there is a clear problem, isn't there? You've allowed that player to completely negate one of the number-one weaknesses of the demon template. A demon must present themselves as someone else to avoid the God-Machine. Therefore, if his demon friends are aware of his true nature, he has to have had at least one situation arise where he's had to roll Cover. If any of the supernatural observers are not aware, he must roll Cover again - even after already having to roll Cover for using the Exploit - because he is now behaving out-of-character for his Cover. The enemies count too. If he isn't acting in observance of what he is presenting, then it's a Cover roll, even if that creature doesn't necessarily know anymore about a demon than he does a mummy - the God-Machine knows.

                                                            So it seems to me that it is less that the power needs balancing and more that you, the storyteller, must take the appropriate levels of due care when mixing supernaturals from separate splat books that were never designed to directly interact with one another. Can it be done? Yes. Should it be done? That's up to you. This is one of the hurdles you face. Another hurdle is you let a player play a mummy in a cross-over game. Those vampires are going to have a hard time living up to the ten plagues of Egypt.
