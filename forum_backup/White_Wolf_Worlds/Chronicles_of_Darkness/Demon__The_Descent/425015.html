    Running a Demon Game for the 1st Time; Questions

    03-02-2015, 08:20 AM
    Hi,

    I've got players who have expressed interest in Demon (subtly but continually for the last few months) and I am looking at it again with an eye toward running it. However I have some questions about how to get things started and going. Can you help?

    1. How does a Ring of Demons form? The are described as very paranoid right from the off - how do four random Demons all fall within similar time frames of each other, all within a similar geographical location and all come into contact with one another?

    The paranoia and mistrust, especially for someone early in their descent, seems to make forming a Ring quite...unlikely?

    Edit: Note, I don't want their Falls and the like to be secretly the plans and plotting of the God-Machine, I want it to be their choice, their Falls and their Descents, not to pull the rug from under them a dozen sessions in with some 'and it was the bad guy's plan all along mwahahaha!' twist thing as I know that would piss me off as a player hugely so why would I do it to them?

    2. Ciphers. Does anyone have examples of these? I'm having a hard time coming up with ideas for them, the Interlock powers you might create from combining them and the idea that i as GM am deciding important personal character 'identity' level stuff for my players while keeping them in the dark about key parts of their characters.

    3. Avoiding 'Plot of the Week'. The game seems set up wonderfully to allow for God Machine Infrastructure of the week to pop up, players tear it down, deal with fallout, rinse and repeat. There's 'making Hell' as an 'end goal' but that's fairly ambiguous and open to individual character's interpretations of it. What have your games had as long term goals?

    4. Any general hints and tips, from hard won experience? Power combinations to watch out for, quirks in the rules, kinds of threats and challenges that are good or bad? Etc.

      How does a ring form?
      Vulnerability. The same forces that give the Unchained a reason to be paranoid also give them a need for support, comrades in arms, and buddies to watch their backs. You can't be in every place at once, so an extra pair of eyes to keep in touch with is itself a great boon for keeping yourself safe.
      Empathy. Demons feel as vibrantly as anyone — perhaps moreso, if just for having their relatively dulled lives as angels to contrast with. The Fall can be a traumatic experience, and it's easy to imagine how the Unchained could have a need for a less logistical, more emotional kind of support. People who know the unique challenges they're put through. People who've been there and know their highs and their lows. Friends and peers.
      Expedience. Some Unchained keep their friends close and their enemies closer. If you don't know whether you can trust your fellow demons, scout them out; do a few jobs with them, get mutual goals accomplished, and in the process, find out what they're really after, whether they pose a threat, and how to insulate yourself from that threat if so.
      Mystery. Maybe there is something greater driving multiple Falls in the same location in a short period of time. The demonic mindset usually doesn't dismiss that possibility. How to find out what caused it, and why — and whether it will be a problem further down the line? Are you being used right from the start?

      The characters created using the chargen rules don't necessarily all have to represent demons fresh off their Fall. Demons before the start of the adventure could have been hiding out and establishing themselves for however long is plausible. The game itself is when the interesting stuff starts happening, and when demons start getting into the kind of trouble that catalyzes fast learning. And with demons' ability to disappear into the crowd, it's not hard to imagine two demons in the same city who go years without realizing the other is around, until one of them makes occult waves that prompt the other to investigate, or a local operation draws the interest of both.

      Once a ring has come together, through whatever means, the simple act of working together for common goals, however immediate in scope, should be able to gradually build a sense of camaraderie and deeper bonds. A ring that comes together out of mutual distrust and aloof pragmatism doesn't need to remain so forever.

      General tips and tricks?
      The spirit rules make for beefy antagonists; even low-Rank angels are nothing for a PC to sneeze at.
      Compromise Conditions are harsh. Don't be nitpicky with the risk of compromise; it's not thwacking the character with a newspaper, it's hitting them with a claw hammer. The feeling of the possibility of compromise, in my experience, is more threatening and oppressive than the actual immediate danger of risking them.
      Not every threat, not even every physical threat, has to be on the level of an angel or other dread monster. Demons are simultaneously very effective but relatively fragile when not in their element. A simple firefight with a couple mortal humans can be plenty tense, especially given compromise can often seem an even more threatening alternative to dealing with bullets.
      That said, know your players' capabilities; the embeds and exploits possessed by a demon can have a big impact on the situations where they come into play. Tough guys with SMGs aren't going to figure out how to get around Check Backdrop for a good while.

          Stupid Loserman has got question 1 covered marvelously.

          Question 2: The Storyteller's Guide will be of use here, but it's not published yet. Here are some threads that might help.
          Making a Cipher. http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...-make-a-cipher
          Making Interlocks. http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...lock-idea-help

          Some quick and dirty tips: Ciphers can be built forwards (working towards final secret) or backwards from the final secret. Does your game have an endgame or direction mapped out? Backwards might be of use. If not, forwards is probably better. If you're really struggling, consider letting your players help. Ever played Wrath: The Oblivion? You could get players to design one of their ring-mate's Ciphers (with your help).

          Question 3: Come up with a big plan for the G-M. It might be a local plan or even a world-shattering one. Consider having a personal answer to the mystery of Its aims and motives. Is it preparing earth for an alien colonisation? Is it shepherding humanity's evolution? Is it entirely insane and just needs to be stopped? All of these could lead to very different overarching plans. Once you have your big plan, be it local, national or global, have your little vignettes and plots of the week hint at and link to it.

          Also consider building a local demonic society - build a sandbox for players to enjoy and they might take a lot of the pressure off for weekly games. They might want to join a local Agency, get revenge on that Tempter who cheated them, help out the demon who needs a Cover who did them a favor when they needed it.

          Question 4: Download and Fungible Knowledge are a hilarious and powerful combo to keep an eye out for. The Look is a bit nasty, though it's no use on demons or angels. Living Recorder can turn human NPCs into nightmares for the unprepared ST. None of these are game-ruining, of course, they just need to handled carefully. Really, this can be said of most Exploits and Embeds. Watch out of the demonic form ability that lets them drain Essence/Aether, too. Again, it doesn't break games, it just needs to be remembered.

          Stupid Loserman hits nicely on one of demon's challenges. Your PCs can use Exploits and form abilities to deal with just about any situation, but they need to be careful when doing so... In general, expect demons to expertly confound, outmaneuver and outfight humans. This is fine. Angels and other demons are another kettle of fish. Cryptids are underused, but I'm a huge fan of them, and they can run the gambit from fun challenge to nightmarish biomechanical dragon. Ghosts are a fun change of pace. Demons just don't expect 'em.

          Hunter angels are very powerful. I generally encourage a 'run' response, with 'learn their banes and bans' as a longer running plot. Don't forget not all angels are combatants, of course. Alexandra Fairbanks and Dizang from the core are good examples.

                  3. Three things spring to mind:
                  • Grow a living world for your players to have stories in, with people, groups and situations that change and shift. Yes, Demons fight the G-M, but they have lives that they need to live. Force them to juggle other obligations. They can do a stake-out tonight, but their son needs to be picked up from school.
                  • Emphasize that Infrastructure isn't something that can be destroyed after work before hitting the bars. It takes time, careful planning, and involves heavy amount of risk. Destroying or subverting infrastructure is something that the players works up to, raising the stakes over the course of a few sessions. In video game terms, make Infrastructure the level boss, not a random encounter.
                  • Give the players moral choices to wrestle with, make them aware their choices have effects on other people. Person of Interest episode "Honor Among Thieves" has the characters sabotage a charity that was set to help thousands of underprivileged children in order to prevent an amoral Artificial Intelligence from gaining a stranglehold on the education system.


                  4. Watch out for over-planning from your players. Bank Heist adventures where the players are endlessly working out plans and infiltration are fun the first time. If every session turns into Ocean's 11, you'll need to find ways to break it up.

                          Also remember that Angels don't have to be Antagonists all the time. Angels work to their purpose and if that purpose doesn't include the Unchained in any way they are unpredictable in their reaction to an encounter with a Demon. They may prove to be interesting foils or allies to greater plots. Not everything has to be the God-Machine (though I encourage the God-Machine to be your primary antagonist, as it's rather the point of the game) and so it can be interesting to have some dangerous events bring Demons and Angels together briefly as allies. If you have God-Machine Chronicle a good example is the angel Kalka. Having an Angel realise it cannot complete its task by itself means the creature will adapt, seek another way to complete that task and that method might be seeking help from its former brethren. This is a great way to present alternative antagonists like powerful Cryptids and is also a good way to bring in the element of distrust. They cannot possibly know if the Angel's leading them into a trap or if it will report them to the God-Machine as soon as its task is done or if it'll just return to storage and never mention them.

                          Here is an example Interlock that I made for one of my players:
                          1st Key – Like I Built It

                          2nd Key – The Map is Not the Territory

                          1st Interlock

                          An Image is a Window

                          Ms. Spider is able to learn the location of something simply by seeing an image of it. By reading the intimate subroutines hidden behind the image of an object or place, she is able to discern where in creation the image represents and how to get there.
                          Dice Pool: Wits + Academics
                          Action: Instant
                          Cost: None
                          Roll Results
                          Dramatic Failure: The interlock functions in reverse, revealing to any nearby hunter angels that there is a demon and where to find it. Roll to check for compromise and gain the Flagged condition.
                          Failure: The interlock fails to activate. Subsequent attempts are made with a -1.
                          Success: She knows the directions to the broad location (such as the way to the house or park).
                          Exceptional Success: She knows the directions to the location down to a more specific locale, such as a room inside a building, or a particular area of a park or field.
                          Note: This power does not allow her to locate the exact position of a hidden object that is secured in this location only that it is somewhere in that location.

                              I too am running a Demon game for the first time and one technique I've found useful is to read spy novels and take notes of the plot twists and bits of tradecraft for reuse. It really helps give things more of a spy flavor.

                              What I did for the PC's Ring, was tell them they were in a Ring and let them figure out how they came to know and trust each other. That generates more interesting content than any backstory I could have thought up.

                              I have also found it fun to create NPC Demons in the setting; it's not like Minecraft, where you can just find a nice piece of Infrastructure and either smash it or leech Aether; there might be another Demon who has been working for the last five years to understand and suborn it to their own purposes, and they're going to be pissed off if the PC Demons just show up and pull the linchpin. Of course, that NPC Demon might actually be an Integrator trying to use that Infrastructure to connect with the God Machine, or trying to blow up the whole city with it; maybe you should smash it.

                              Exiles seem really cool too but I am leery of introducing one into the game.

                                          Best advice for Ciphers IMO - 'cold reading'. Familiarize yourself with a wikipedia entry on it. Read a little Nostradamas. Then write something that could more or less apply to hundreds of separate interpretations, and watch which one the player naturally gravitates toward. Then you can either have them be correct or pull a twist. As an example, I had, 'Thy Questions / Unto Fundament / Are Become / Thy Answers'. Try figuring out what THAT means!

                                          The best Cyphers are vague enough so that Demons can argue about what they mean with each other and not be sure they are right.

                                              Here we go. My caveat is that these are slightly more complicated than an Interlock probably should be. Also, my idea of combination powers is more conceptual than literal.

                                              This is the Watcher's Cipher. Her Covers were information brokers and members of the media. This character was, interestingly enough, originally a Messenger, but when Interface was released, we realised she was an Analyst. In other words, even the Storyteller didn't know! The God-Machine is indeed powerful...



                                              The Watcher

                                              Cipher: Living Recorder (Mundane) → Fungible Knowledge (Instrumental) → Lost in the Crowd (Mundane) → Everybody Knows (Vocal)

                                              Final Secret: “Many eyes. Many knives.” Nothing occurs in a vacuum. The more people see the world as you do, the more tools there are to rework it.

                                              First Key: Living Recorder

                                              Second Key: Fungible Knowledge

                                              First Interlock – Hawkeye

                                              The Watcher can briefly rewire the mind of a human to become the perfect observer.

                                              Cost: None

                                              Dice Pool: Manipulation + Academics

                                              Action: Instant

                                              Roll Results

                                              Dramatic Failure: The Watcher’s eyesight is affected instead, and it’s ruined; she takes the Blind Condition for the next day.

                                              Failure: The effect fails.

                                              Success: The Watcher touches the target and alters her perception. For a number of hours equal to the Watcher’s Primum, the subject of this Interlock gains the two dot version of Trained Observer, and switches the subject’s highest Skill rating to Investigation. If the subject already possesses Trained Observer at two dots, he takes the three dot version. If he has that at three dots, he takes the rote quality. If Investigation is already the subject’s highest rated Skill, she takes the Watcher’s Investigation as a bonus. If the Watcher uses Living Recorder, she can use her subject’s modified Perception pool while reviewing the footage from that Embed.

                                              Exceptional Success: As above, and the subject gains the Merit one step better.

                                              Third Key: Lost in the Crowd
                                              Second Interlock – Ad Hock Op

                                              After using Hawkeye, the Watcher can further modify her subject to become an ideal spy, and direct him to specific targets.

                                              Cost: 1 Aether

                                              Dice Pool: Manipulation + Subterfuge – subject’s Composure

                                              Action: Instant

                                              Dramatic Failure: The subject takes the Blind condition for a number of days equal to the Watcher's Primum.

                                              Failure: Nothing happens.

                                              Success: The Watcher instructs the subject to find or spy on something/someone. For as long as he benefits from Hawkeye, he gains the 8-again quality on Stealth rolls, and does not suffer unskilled penalties, when following the Watcher’s directive. The Watcher reflexively knows the subject’s Stealth rating, and for an additional Aether can bolster it with her own dots in Stealth for a Scene.

                                              Once the effect of the Interlock expires, the subject will report back to the Watcher in whatever way he sees fit. Unless the Watcher specifies otherwise, the subject will remember little of this experience—especially if he’s caught somewhere he shouldn’t be.

                                              This Interlock cannot force a subject to reveal something he himself wants to keep hidden.

                                              Exceptional Success: The subject gains the Watcher's Stealth rating as a bonus at no extra Aether cost.

                                              Fourth Key: Everybody Knows
                                              Third Interlock – Memewar

                                              “Information wants to be free.” With this Interlock, the Watcher can crowd source any problem. Anyone can be her operative. Everyone is her operative. The Watcher thinks of a problem she needs solved, or a question she needs answered. This can relate to a specific person, but it can’t really reveal their secrets…at least not directly. That said, this Interlock can sometimes reveal motive (though not intent).

                                              Cost: 2 Aether

                                              Dice Pool: Intelligence + Expression

                                              Roll Results

                                              Dramatic Failure: The meme disseminates, but it gives the wrong answer. The Watcher takes a –5 penalty when it comes to acting on the problem.

                                              Failure: The effect fails and the Aether is wasted.

                                              Success: For the next day, the victim is compelled to disseminate a particular meme of the Watcher’s design. This can be an image, or text, or even a melody. It does not necessarily need to be an Internet meme.

                                              Anyone who encounters the meme will disseminate it further. But, as memes do, it warps, taking on aspects and shedding context until it’s unrecognizable. In a number of days equal to 10 – (the Watcher’s Primum) the meme will become the answer the Unchained desires. This will usually be nonsensical to everyone else, having become so encumbered with in-jokes and interpolations, but to the Watcher it will be revelatory. Once she collects the answer, the meme will cease to disseminate, becoming just another bit of noise in the collective.

                                              If a more concrete system is required, the Watcher should gain a +5 on any roll made to resolve her problem, or at least a variation of the Inspired Condition.

                                              Exceptional Success: The answer is profound. The Watcher takes the Inspired Condition as it relates to the question.

                                                  1st Key – Like I Built It

                                                  2nd Key – The Map is Not the Territory

                                                  1st Interlock

                                                  An Image is a Window

                                                  Ms. Spider is able to learn the location of something simply by seeing an image of it. By reading the intimate subroutines hidden behind the image of an object or place, she is able to discern where in creation the image represents and how to get there.
                                                  Dice Pool: Wits + Academics
                                                  Action: Instant
                                                  Cost: None
                                                  Roll Results
                                                  Dramatic Failure: The interlock functions in reverse, revealing to any nearby hunter angels that there is a demon and where to find it. Roll to check for compromise and gain the Flagged condition.
                                                  Failure: The interlock fails to activate. Subsequent attempts are made with a -1.
                                                  Success: She knows the directions to the broad location (such as the way to the house or park).
                                                  Exceptional Success: She knows the directions to the location down to a more specific locale, such as a room inside a building, or a particular area of a park or field.
                                                  Note: This power does not allow her to locate the exact position of a hidden object that is secured in this location only that it is somewhere in that location.


                                                  3rd Key – Alibi

                                                  2nd Interlock

                                                  The Angel is its Image

                                                  Ms. Spider is able to use an image of herself to revisit places she's been before by remembering the way the universe formed around her and simply repositioning her molecules.
                                                  Dice Pool: Wits + Manipulation + Primum
                                                  Action: Instant
                                                  Cost: 1 Aether per 1 dot of Primum
                                                  Roll Results
                                                  Dramatic Failure: Not only has Ms. Spider failed to transport herself to the location she desired and possibly compromised herself, she has managed to transport herself somewhere completely new by mistake. She instead reappears at a location of the storyteller's choice (it is encouraged to be somewhat inconvenient) and she must roll to avoid compromise (-2).
                                                  Failure: On failure, roll to avoid compromise (-2).
                                                  Success: Ms. Spider rearranges herself so that all of her molecules appear at the target location exactly as she remembers, though she certainly accommodates for changes in her being since that time. Instantly transporting yourself somewhere is dangerous however and might be noticed by the God-Machine, roll to avoid compromise.
                                                  Exceptional Success: Ms. Spider's mastery over her own design has proven absolute and she has disassembled herself and reassembled herself somewhere else with perfect efficiency. She appears where she desired, does not need to roll for compromise and may remove one negative condition or tilt related to her physical health, with storyteller permission.
                                                  Note: This can result in time-travel. The base Interlock above assumes that no time-travel is involved, but technically space and time are the same, so a location can also account for a time. Indeed, an image is a frozen piece of time in this abstract respect. Storyteller's should be careful when allowing players to time-travel brazenly. Add additional penalties and consequences for using the interlock to time-travel.


                                                  4th Key – Bystander Effect:

                                                  3rd Interlock

                                                  Magic Mirror

                                                  Ms. Spider's mastery of reality and its building blocks have resulted in a dimensional understanding most demons would kill for. It may well be that this understanding will prove instrumental in the creation of a very real and physical hell one day.
                                                  Dice Pool: Wits + Manipulation
                                                  Action: Instant
                                                  Cost: 5 Aether per (Primum x 10 yards)
                                                  Roll Results
                                                  Dramatic Failure: Ms. Spider's attempt to create her own pocket of the world has completely backfired. Not only has she failed to create it but The God-Machine has sensed the interference in its designs and has sent angels to reacquire her immediately. Ms. Spider gains the Hunted Condition (Persistent) and the storyteller should immediately create the appropriate amount of angels that appear nearby to begin looking.
                                                  Failure: Ms. Spider fails to create the splinter and must roll to avoid compromise (-2).
                                                  Success: Ms. Spider has successfully created a splinter that is identical to the area immediately around her. The splinter is populated by any human beings that lived within the area of effect at the time of the interlock's use. It does not however create such copies of supernatural creatures, such as vampires, werewolves, mages and so on. The splinter is free from the influence of the God-Machine. It is automatically connected to any portal in the original location of the demon's choice but once this choice is made, it is permanent. The demon can choose to destroy their splinter at any time but doing so kills any beings inside. The splinter acts as a suborned infrastructure but cannot store more Aether than was expended to create it. The demon can only maintain her Primum in splinters at any given time. Example: A demon with Primum 3 may only have 3 unique splinters created this way in their ownership at any given time.
                                                  Exceptional Success: Ms. Spider has so artfully created her new domain that she may at any time choose to spend a point of Aether to turn any door into the entrance of the splinter. Only one door may lead to the splinter at any time and the change only lasts 1 scene or 1 hour, whichever comes first. Whenever someone leaves the splinter it is always from the door they entered through. This effect only applies to that splinter and no other splinter in her possession may take advantage of this (unless they too were created with an exceptional success.)
                                                  Note: The splinter began when Ms. Spider successfully cast the Interlock, the splinter resets at midnight Primum days (plus 1 day per Aether used) after this. Individuals that reliably enter the area will have copies that reliably enter the area every day. Generic human copies, such as crowds and people who travel the street will also appear. These individuals will neither realise they are copies, or that they are in a splinter, nor is it entirely clear where they go when they cross the edge of the splinter. Crossing the edge of the splinter for any character from outside the splinter, including Ms. Spider, causes them to appear on the opposite side of the splinter.
