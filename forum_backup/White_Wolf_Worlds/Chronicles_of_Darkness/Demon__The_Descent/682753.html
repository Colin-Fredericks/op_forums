      The Bone Clocks by David Mitchell. It reads like a perfect representation of being a mortal in the World of Darkness. Weird, spooky things happen on the periphery of your life but you can mostly pretend everything is normal so long as you don't think too hard about them. There is a shadow war between two supernatural factions playing out in the background of the main character's life, a war with a lot of spy tactics. One group of immortals take on new faces and identities and both sides are constantly prepared for both mundane and magical attacks. Plus there's something in the book that sounds exactly like infrastructure (complete with its own Guardian Angel) and I think it even gets described at one point as an "occult machine".

                  The BBC series Utopia has a plot that goes off the rails fast into some silly stuff, but the aesthetics of the show are fantastic at conveying an oppressive and paranoid atmosphere with an occasional sense of wonder and mystery, and the actions of the conspiracy's enforcers drive home how the agents of the God-Machine don't always have to be plugged into mortal power structures to get shit done, as long as they're willing to just act like they belong. The soundtrack is really good.

                  Burn Notice is a fun thriller series with a lot of asides from the perspective of a professional spy, very useful for giving you ideas as to cool things a demon might be out doing.

                  If you don't mind opaque plots that require you to read between the lines and lack definite answers, the video game killer7 has awkward gameplay but oozes style. You play as a government wetworks operative with multiple distinct identities, each with their own appearance, personality and weird abilities. Curve bullets like in Wanted? More like curve rocket-propelled grenades. At heaven's request we will purge the unwanted. We must get rid of bad trees by their roots.

                          Black Mirror just screams GMC.

                          Try ZER0ES by Chuck Wendig.

                          Edit:

                          Dennou Coil (anime) stupidly hard to find, but essential viewing if you ever want to run an Heirs to Hell campaign

                          City of Stairs by Robert Jackson Bennett

                          Book of Spies Edited by Alan Furst (short stories, so easy enough to mine for individual adventures)

                          For nonfiction try:

                          The 9/11 Wars by Jason Burke for a very complete look at how the 9/11 attacks and aftermath changed the espionage landscape.

                          Near and Distant Neighbors by Jonathan Haslam (Soviet Intelligence. Really, really good)

                          Data and Goliath by Bruce Schneier (any of his works are excellent for Techgnostic Espionage)

                          Future Crimes by Marc Goodman (Excellent look at how the Deva Corp might go about hunting for Demons, and what the Unchained might have to do to stay off the radar)

                          Board games:

                          Tragedy Looper - a board game where a team of 1-3 players compete against the Mastermind (a fourth player). Since the 1-3 have no idea what's going on, and have to deduce what's going on based on how the mastermind reacts to their blind movements, it can really have a very strong GMC feel.

                                              The book Ancillary Justice is pretty good inspiration for understanding what it must be like to go from being an Angel to a Demon. The main character is a rogue AI in a human body navigating a rather religious science fiction society with the intent to destroy something much greater than themselves. Before they went rogue they were connected to the system and possessed thousands of bodies. Suddenly constrained to one leaves them feeling exceptionally vulnerable, particularly in respect to the loss of information they suffered when they went from having a thousand eyes and ears to a single perspective. There are some amusing habits of the main character (particularly their inability to grasp gender and its role in any of the many languages they speak) that would make for great Glitches in a Demon character. Plus there's a fairly strong espionage bent to the main plot.

                                                          Gravity falls - two Latents spend the summer visiting their Great Uncle, a Messenger Tempter who lives in a town full of malfunctioning Infrastructure, cultists, and cryptids. Recommended for running an Heirs/Innocents/GMC chronicle. Great for a scene of Soup Up being used on a golf cart.

                                                          The Black Watchman - An augmented reality game in the vein of the canceled Majestic - 12. It's also a Spinoff of the Secret World MMORPG, which is another good, if flawed, inspiration.

                                                          Deutchland 83 - a German television show about espionage in the days before the reunification of Germany. Nuclear war tension, secrets, all good stuff.

                                                          Subnormality - a Webcomic. Visually inspirational of a world where the mythic and mundane rub shoulders, often without the latter realizing it. The current strip: Museum of the Theoretical would make a perfect piece of infrastructure.

                                                          Heaven and Earth and Potters Lake. Heaven and Earth was an old RPG that tried to do Biblical Horror. Besides the utility of mining ideas from an RPG that delves into maltheist horror, the town of Potters Lake is pure standout of a setting. It's a small mid-western town instead of a major city, but it's every bit as good as Seattle for Demon. It's as a weird place where the inhabitants have no choice but to take it in stride. The town grocery store alone is worth the price of admission.

                                                          Welcome to Nightvale. A podcast. Did we not mention this already?

                                                          Mystery Show. Another podcast about a woman investigating very mundane mysteries, such as how did a video rental store disappear overnight. What sells it how she gets out to investigate, research, and interview. Her rule for the show is that she doesn't use the internet to solves the mystery. Make the object of each investigation an infrastructure of some kind, add a few dead bodies, a cult or two, an albino alligator in the sewers and a sinister hunter angel and any one of her mysteries will make a damn fine DtD story or two.

                                                          Pandemic Legacy. This what I imagine running a tier 3 Free Agency must feel like: global reach, global resources and you're constantly overwhelmed. From the outside everyone thinks your organization is invincible, but you know that's just on paper. Your most capable agents have their own agendas and are the most likely to just... disappear.
