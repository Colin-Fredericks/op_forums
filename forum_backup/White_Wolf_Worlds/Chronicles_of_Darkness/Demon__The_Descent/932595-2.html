            The Black Obelisk

            I've lived in this city since my creation and even after my fall. In that time, I've made deals with dead men who live life fuller then those still alive, I've stole from sorcerers to gain arcane knowledge, I made friends of devils and enemies of angels. I've posted on all the boards and asked others even close friends of mine but known anything about it or can even see it. Every night it feels like it’s getting bigger or its getting closer I can't tell which. Every night at 4 am I awake up with an urge to do something in my kitchen just to see if it’s still there and every night it is. Its blackness blots out the stars in the sky. I will make final preparations to investigate this Obelisk. Even thinking about it give me goosebumps like a cold chill has entered my heated home.

                                                        Claim is a generic Manifestation meaning any entity that can have Manifestations theoretically could have Claim. It's just that Claim is best described in Werewolf (which is the only book that actually lists Dread Powers for Claimed instead of just saying Claimed can have Dread Powers) and spirits (and Idigam, I guess) are the only ephemerals described in Werewolf.

                                                        Edit: Only a few Manifestations are limited to certain ephemeral types. Reaching and Gauntlet Breach are spirit only, Avernian Gateway is for ghosts, angels and death-related spirits, and Shadow Gateway is for spirits and angels only. At least until a book describes a new type (or subtype) of ephemeral entity that also could use those Manifestations.

                                                        Re-edit: The limits are certainly not set in stone. In Mage both Reaching and Gauntlet Breach can be used by either type while Gateway has been condensed to just apply to the entity's home realm.

On the one hand, wordcount is almost certainly a contributing factor to this, but on the other hand, it's pretty clear from Werewolf's treatment of the subject that the Claimed are something a lot more involved than less ingrained varieties of Ridden.
