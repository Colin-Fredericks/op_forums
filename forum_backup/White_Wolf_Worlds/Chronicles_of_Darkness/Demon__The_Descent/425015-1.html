        Established Demons usually find the newly Fallen Unchained as they fall. A fall is a tumultuous event that creates a massive aetheric resonance. While it can appear subtle (though most of the time explosive) to the naked eye it reverberates on a massive scale. It isn't simply hunter angels that appear to locate the new Demon but also other Demons. As Satchel said Facades, burner Covers and Mundane Embeds all go a long way in protecting the "Recruiter".

        Furthermore an "Elysium" might be set up, at your discretion as storyteller, to act as a safe and secure location for Demons to be Demons. One such location is in Splintered City: Seattle in the form of "Hell on Earth", a dingy bar with a bolt-hole re-purposed into a Demon's-Only Lounge. How easily and how often Demons communicate with each other is entirely up to the mood of your chronicle and the themes you're hoping to put forward. Do you want your chronicle to be especially paranoid? Do you want betrayal to be around every corner? If this is the case then a safe, neutral place is unlikely to help facility that atmosphere so don't make one. Alternatively if you're hoping to focus on the more techgnostic factors of the chronicle then perhaps it will benefit from neutral ground.

        In the former case recruiters might be the only Demons that respond to a Fall and search, this process of searching might takes weeks or months after a Demon falls and might represent the beginning of a chronicle. That is to say: The players begin after they've finally been uncovered; not by the God-Machine but by another Demon through the simple process of investigation. This method certainly would capture the feeling of spy-novels. It also benefits for the Demons in this kind of chronicle to communicate through esoteric methods such as numbers stations, spray painting obscure symbols on the walls of key locations.

        In the latter case recruiters are likely to be present almost nightly at your neutral ground. Here your players might find representatives of each of the Agendas seeking to get another body among their cause mingling with the recruiters of more organised Agencies. You could enjoy playing with stories that might evoke a bit of a "Speak-Easy" feel with the neutral ground you've made occasionally coming under scrutiny of the God-Machine and so having to pick up and move. Easy enough, since it's a Bolt-Hole, but getting everyone out without the shit hitting the fan might make a fun event.

        If you've ever played Changeling the Lost then I would recommend considering that Demonic interaction is similar in concept to this. Changelings have great difficulty trusting anyone as they couldn't trust anything in Arcadia to be what it appeared to be. Similarly, Demons can't trust anything or anyone to appear what it appears to be. Their Cover's wife might be another Demon in disguise or a Sleeper waiting for a specific set of conditions to come into play before she kills him in his sleep. But both groups need to trust someone eventually or they're going to be on their own and they're going to die. For Changelings this comes in the form of the Motley and the Courts. For Demons this is the Ring and the Agendas (or the Agencies). Eventually a Demon needs the company of their own kind so they need to look for them. This search might begin on the internet, ordinary investigations into bizarre incidents, and may have them bumping heads with other supernaturals before finally finding their own kind.

        Communication likely happens through the use of special software allowing access to certain chat-rooms on the internet or dead-drop conference like pen-pals. Or it might happen face to face if you have a safe place to congregate. It's probably important in either respect to consider the manners of the Demons, such as the fact that unless they belong in a Ring together Demons are probably going to avoid (if possible) at least initial communication in one of their core Covers.

                Originally posted by Scutarii View Post
                Thanks you two, those help a lot. I'll have to try my hand at it once I get some stuff from my players.

                How do people handle Demon 'society' in a place? As in, the 4 PCs are a Ring and there are some number (I'm thinking 15-20) other Demons in the same geographical area. How have you organised it? How do they communicate between different groups? DO they communicate? How would a Temporal Agency try to attract other Demons to it to buy Covers? How would different Agendas make contact and draw people in?
                Unchained society, in my games, is very much based around niches. You know in movies spies have a network of useful contacts, most often seen when they go rogue? That's how I run things. In my current game, for example, I have a Tempter who'll get you cheap Covers if you're in a bind or want/need a burner Cover, He gets the raw materials from the homeless (most of his Covers come with nasty downsides like criminal records, addictions and mental health problems). There's a demon called the Courier who has a taxi driver and a van driver as Covers. He moves things - objects, people - for a price (but he is emphatically not a getaway driver) in his gadgetted vehicles. Another demon is a gadget-expert, who makes them, unmakes them, examines them and offers guidance making them. One is an information-broker. One offers extradimensional spaces as secure meeting places,

                This keeps demonic society from crumbling into a paranoid, uncommunicative mess. Dealing with other Unchained (sure, it's nearly always transactional) builds relationships. You might pass on tidbits of information (unless you're a particularly selfish Tempter or secretive Inquisitor), telling them about the new angel in town, to watch out for the Exile in the abandoned hospital, that sort of thing. And like-minded demons are eventually drawn together and Agencies begin to coalesce from rings and individuals.

                Agencies that are actively courting demons will, as Satchel says, offer services. Cheap Covers, safehouses (supernatural or mundane), access to Agency-funds, that sort of thing. They'll also market heavily based on their ideology. For example, an Agency that oppose the God-Machine in a sustainable and careful manner will advertise to Saboteurs (we're stronger together!) and Inquisitors (we'll share information for a brighter future).

                For first-time communication, there's an Embed tailor-made for this. Special Message. Demons scrawl graffiti designed for demons to spot saying 'I'm new!' or perhaps 'New guy, we're here to help. Here's an internet key to find our secure ring.' or 'Here's a number for a burner phone. Call it.' A fun way demons can recognise each other is to use their faculty with languages. In downtown New York, how often do you hear someone speaking a mix of Welsh, Urdu and Romansh? Of course, angels are just as good with languages...You might even want a demon who works as a 'greeter' for the local Unchained society. They might be a nice, relatively altruistic demon or a Tempter planning to extort the helpless newbies.

                Also, how do Demons find out that the GMachine is up to something with Infrastructure. Is it just through noticing strange things in the newspaper, or word of mouth or odd planning permission notices? Or is the aetheric resonance attracting them? Or are they just wandering around and just see the gears somewhere?
                All of the above! Some Infrastructure is in the plain sight, if you've got Unseen Sense: God-Machine, though it's likely to be protected or hidden in more mundane ways. (Sure, the Golden Gate Bridge is Infrastructure. What're you going to do, blow it up? No, stop that, put those explosives away...). Aetheric resonance will draw cautious attention from curious demons - is it Infrastructure, a new demon, someone building a gadget? And some demons are conspiracy theorists, which is a reasonable position in a world straddled by the God-Machine. They're the ones looking at strange things in the newspaper, putting pins in maps where people saw UFOs and smiling as the pins form the shape of a wheel. And word of mouth is a thing. While demons are paranoid as a rule and some won't share their secrets, it makes sense to lots of them to share information to allies or even just demons you've worked with (as I mentioned above). The new construction project is hiding mysterious Infrastructure? Mention it, maybe they know more, and maybe they'll tell you. New hunter angel in town? Tell them, next time they might want warn you and save your skin

                By the way, if you still want help with Ciphers/Interlocks, PM me.

                        If these Demons are all trying to hide themselves and are all so expert at doing so how does anyone even begin finding other Demons to start forming an Agency or working with likeminded Agendas at all?

                        Also, how do Demons find out that the GMachine is up to something with Infrastructure. Is it just through noticing strange things in the newspaper, or word of mouth or odd planning permission notices? Or is the aetheric resonance attracting them? Or are they just wandering around and just see the gears somewhere?
                        Here's the funny thing about Agencies. There are four kinds of them, each one associated with a different Agenda. Temporal reflect Tempters building their Hell. Free Agencies share information, and Paranoid demons seek info. Compromised agenda lends itself to a group of Turncoats, who tend to associate with angels anyways. And so on.

                        The simple truth is that most demons avoid Agencies as it is. They don't trust them, since its all too easy for an angel to sneak in, or that Tempter to start using some favors to steal all your Gadgets and claimed Infrastructure. So, demon "society" such as it is tends to be more like werewolf packs. They each claim their own little slice of Hell On Earth, rely on it to recharge their aether and build defenses around that. They run into other demons, get contact information and the like, the more they run around dealing with plots.

                        That's how demon society grows. Word of mouth as you meet people. You know a guy who knows a guy. Demons sense aether, so that's the main reason why they can figure out who each other are in close proximity to each other. Its a slowly growing web of hush, hush meetings.

                        If you need help with running into more demons, I personally suggest letting everyone have Aether Sense always active for free. Demons that want to hide can spoof automatically. Those that don't want to hide stand out more. Aether sense is rather limited in range as it is, and large explosions of power are rare, shy of someone making a powerful Gadget or Going Loud.

                                    What triggers Stigmata is vague, not even interaction with Angels and the God-Machine always results in Stigmata. Usually it has to be some life-changing event that opens their eyes to greater workings of the universe. Joining a God-Machine cult, even one that names their patron correctly, wouldn't necessarily induce Stigmata. While physically encountering an extra-dimensional space in the janitor's closet full of impossible clockwork might. Generally speaking Angels and Demons don't seem to cause Stigmata on their own but inflict it with abilities (there is an Exploit for it, after all). However, I'd say an Angel's fall would qualify and select, particularly explosive confrontations mortals might get caught in between the God-Machine and his fallen angels.

                                    Compromise checks first should be acknowledged, or "seen" by someone (or something, security systems count). Spy-craft is subtle and the general rule of thumb is it only makes a compromise if it's reasonably going to cause an onlooker (either the guy in the security office or a bystander) to double-take. The overweight guy who loves his iced doughnuts deciding that it was time to lose weight and only eating salad isn't cognitively dissonant. It's unusual, but people will slap him on the back, or tease him. The overweight guy who loves his iced doughnuts drinking battery acid thanks to a glitch he's picked up, however, would be a compromise.

                                    Spy-craft should only cause a compromise, in essence, if you get caught. If the player successfully makes their stealth, subterfuge and persuasion rolls to bypass notice then the player probably shouldn't be Compromised. My rule of thumb though, your mileage may vary on what you feel is logical.

                                        It actually does depend on the cover. The rule of thumb ,as I understand it, is to have a general idea on the behavior of the cover determined when the character starts using it as the basis for where compromise comes from. For example lets say your cover was a former soldier who now deplores violence to the point of pacifism .That the cover has decorations for military service and significant combat ability despite being a pacifist still fits in. This person deciding to professional boxer is gonna meet some stares because there's a large gap between "I don't like seeing people get hurt," and "I would like to become champion of a sport that centers around trying to beat the tar out of my fellow human beings." I'm seeing it compared to breaking points a few times in the core, so it probably would be worth cross-referencing the rules for breaking points and compromise to see if it makes any more sense.

                                        As for your Stigmatic question. Well page 168 has an exploit that lets you make someone a Stigmatic. At least one exploit has it as a potential side effect on a success, so the more flashy and egregious exploits might be grounds to do it. I would be more hesitant about it with embeds because embeds are more subtle and don't really have the same potential to shatter a persons view of reality that an exploit would. A blatant use of an embed might cause compromise I would be hesitant about them causing Stigmatics. Joining a cult might have it as a thing that happens, but I don't think just joining the cult would make one a Stigmatic. Otherwise just interacting with a demon will not cause a person to become a Stigmatic unless something happens that causes them to come in contact with the GM. Oh yeah going loud and fighting an angel will probably result in nearby mortals fleeing in terror and becoming Stigmatics.

                                                    One thing I rule is that its not so much acting out of Cover, its that people catching you acting out of Cover that's a big deal. The rationale is that the God Machine is hooked up not only to every Infrastructure and Angel, but to the average person, and can see through their eyes, effectively.

                                                    Think of the Matrix, near the end of the first movie. The agents are trying to chase Trinity and Morpheus. The two of them reach a phone booth. The call comes out, they answer the phone. A bum sees Trinity vanish, and then suddenly, the agents know where they are. They couldn't do a general search without using people as their eyes.

                                                    EDIT - in your example, its not so much the crossing guard doing something out of character that gets them with a compromise check, its someone -finding- them doing something they shouldn't. Then the flag goes up.

                                                    I know its not quite the same thing, but I find it makes things easier game wise.

                                                        The book sure is vague, isn't it? Here are the guidelines for avoiding Compromise I gave to my players. They are intended to foster paranoia and roleplaying without forcing constant Compromise rolls, which can take the whole game sideways.                                                         1) Live your Cover.
                                                        2) Big Brother Is Watching.
                                                        3) A Little Tradecraft Goes a Long Way.

                                                            Pendragon came up with some house rules modifying demon along those lines MCN. I particularly enjoy using many of his rules. One of them is in reference to people being part of the system.
