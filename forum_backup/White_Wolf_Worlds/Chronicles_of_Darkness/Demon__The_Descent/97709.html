    A essay on Covers and their maintenance

    03-24-2014, 04:34 PM
    One question that pops up regularly in the mind of interested players is: “Isn't maintaining a Cover a full-time job by definition? How would that allow a Demon to have a supernatural life while having a decently high Cover? Should my PC be jobless, or have some specific kind of job, in order to experience the game fully? How can I have more than one Cover and hope the one(s) I'm not using is not going to crumble to bits very fast due to lack of activity?”
    Maybe the question isn't actually pondered that often, but it is one that I certainly ask myself occasionally, even as a very enthusiast lover of D:tD. This world – the western world in which we can reasonably expect most of our games to take place in, at any rate – has a special place for job and related administrative existence. Our professional occupations take up a great part of our lives, directly or indirectly. Many employees (or bosses) hardly have any time for their personal life. Very recent and widespread technology has the unfortunate side-effect of virtually and unofficially allowing professional obligations to leak into personal life. The NWoD, until proved otherwise, operates under the same principle.
    That means it's difficult to skip a day of work and similar responsibilities if you want to keep your job, and that is especially true when you look at today's job market. Even losing in competitiveness or productivity may risk you your job.
    So, what do you do, when you have a Demon with an average job?


    Enter Jean-Claude Romand.
    This man was a competent doctor working for the World Health Organization. He had a generous salary. He had cancer. He had a wife, and two children.
    But this was all false. He hadn't attended a single exam in medicine faculty. He was no doctor. He was certainly not working for the WHO. He had not worked for a single day of his life, and relied on the scamming of his acquaintances and loved ones in order to obtain the money he pretended to earn. He had no cancer, and considering this was one of the reasons he got married to his wife, his family was also (based on) a lie.
    It lasted for roughly 20 years. No one suspected anything until the very end (which includes the somewhat ironical case of his wife, toying with the idea that her husband was not a doctor, but some kind of spy; in terms of excitement and hidden depths, the guy's life was in fact on the opposite end of the spectrum). Not even the administration, even though he didn't even have a social security number.
    How did he manage that?
    A combination of factors come in play.
    - He cultivated appearances to an obsessive degree, traveling to his supposed workplace and doctors (for his pretend cancer).
    - His second life was actually extremely dull and spent teaching himself the information he was supposed to possess (so even though he was no actual doctor, he possessed – and still does – an impressive amount of medical knowledge that fooled actual health professionals). He had no real hobby or real life to speak of; he only lived to cultivate his lie.
    - No one among his loved ones thought to ask him any question, about his actual life or their money, because they trusted him and were very impressed by his life and carreer. One doesn't question someone who's gone that far in life, especially when it was obvious this path was the one he was supposed to take. He always was very smart, and had huge expectations put on him; suspecting that he'd failed somewhere was unthinkable.
    - Indifference. He never caught the attention of the administration, through a combination of luck and flawless behaviour from his part.


    That lie was maintained for an inordinately long amount of time. Even when he murdered his family in cold blood before attempting to commit suicide, none of them were aware of the lie, and the truth came as an overwhelming shock for everyone who knew him. (The only person he spared was his mistress – yes, he managed to find the time to have a mistress, I am as amazed as you are – because, one can only suppose, she actually confronted him as the real Mr. Romand. That, or he knew that her death would easily be traced back to him. We may never know.)
    Today, though he is in prison and might still stay there for quite some time, he is reportedly “happy” to have the weight lifted from his shoulder and have an actual, real existence of his own, no matter how poor a life it is.

    Let us think about it. A very mundane man, equipped with nothing but his intelligence, lived a fake life, not in order to live anonymously his wildest fantasies in parallel, not in order to escape an enemy or a past life, only for the sake of that fake life. He was an overcompetent (or overly lucky, depending of your point of view) impostor who never took any pleasure in being an impostor. Yet, at the risk of repeating myself, he had enough of a parallel life to have a mistress, which was not an element likely to improve his lie.
    If this person managed it, can you imagine what a Demon can do to maintain a Cover? A creature who has access to the coding of reality? An entity who can readily trade aspects of their own or someone else's life, and make that exchange metaphysically legitimate? A monster that can lie perfectly, remember all those lies and avoid incoherence, and easily call up skills it doesn't actually possess? A being who has a very concrete reason for having a false existence; something that has to lie in order to save its own life (and not “just” in order not to disappoint their parents, or get the girl they like)? Honestly, a Demon using his methods AND their supernatural tricks would have more than enough time left for their supernatural life and personal drama.
    If your NWoD is lax enough to allow mundane tricks like the ones Mr. Romand used in order to improve a Cover, then, a Demon can combine them with their own supernatural abilities to maintain one very good Cover, or several decently-rated ones.
    As an ST, anyway, I'd allow that. Because those mundane tricks are not perfect, either. And whether you use mundane or supernatural means to fake a life means very little to those who realize it's a facade. You don't have to play out the daily life of a bank employee who's gonna lose his job (and some Cover rating) if he misses one day of work because he has to investigate that new Infrastructure, he just needs to pretend to be one well enough, and do what's necessary, in this era of Facebook and Twitter and decreasing privacy, to avoid anyone finding out that he doesn't actually work at Bank of America. “Go to work”, find an alternate way of earning the money you should get, learn enough about finance to convince your mortal buddies.
    And the God-Machine? Well, if a tree falls in a forest but there's nobody to hear it, does it actually make a noise? In this case, if you fake your (professional) existence but nobody cares to prod it, would anyone call you an impostor? Maybe the God-Machine doesn't, either. Else it would have to send Destroyers after every mortal with a double life, as a precaution. (Maybe, in your NWoD, it does! That would certainly have interesting implications.)

    Just think about it.
    Also, check out about this case if you want to think more about identity, truth, and falsehood, from a point of view that is relevant to this game. It's fascinating.
    If you ever lived in fear of the “impostor syndrome”, this should make you feel better, I know I do.



    (Disclaimer: I'm talking about a real-life case, a man who murdered his whole family in cold blood and showed little emotion or remorse about it. I have no intention to glorify him or his deeds, or even compare him to fictional beings who have much more sympathetic reasons for doing what they do. It'd be great if he wasn't overly discussed about, safe for his methods as an impostor.)

    Part-time table flipper.
