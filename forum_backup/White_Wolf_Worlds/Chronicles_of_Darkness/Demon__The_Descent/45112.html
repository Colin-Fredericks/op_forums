      From what I have read, occult physics basically boils down to "if you do the right thing that's weird enough, then X will happen because the weirdness you just did needs to be one-upped for the purposes of the great universe". So if you somehow banish an evil eldritch abomination by prancing around in a zebra costume on a busy street, singing "Gangnam Style" off-key and sprinkling glitter on the side of the road, all while people are honking their horns to the beat of "We WIll Rock You", then something weirder must be done to banish an even more powerful foe.

      Again, this is just as how I understand it. From what I also know, you would need to do a LOT of research, have a VERY high Occult dot rating and probably focus the majority of a chronicle on getting a mortal to try playing around with god-physics. At that point, too, you might already have the Angels ready to zap you.

                                      Under the rules of Forbidden Lore, mortals have a trait called Reason, which begins at ten dots. When a character comes into contact with Forbidden Lore, in whatever form (an old book, a certain goblin fruit, a song sung by a sentient television), they may sacrifice a dot of Reason to learn a supernatural power, be it a numen, a clause of a Contract, or an Embed.
                                      As Reason falls, the character accrues various penalties of increasing severity, but also some badass bonuses; at Reason 0, though, the character is considered unplayable.

                                                  I've actually touched on this myself, about the time when TGMC came out. My own "update" of it works as follows.
                                                  Mortals that for some reason learn some piece of How The World Works go a little bit weird in the head (-1 to all integrity rolls).
                                                  After that point they can learn things appropriate to that secret (numens for angelic, contracts for fae, discipline for kindred), but at a price. For every piece of Forbidden Lore they learn their maximum Integrity is lowered by one (two secrets means a maximum of eight) and each day, when they wake, they have to roll that many dice. Success results in the character gaining an appropriate "crazy" condition for that day. Some people trend towards the same condition all the time, others vary wildly. In effect they're the mad lighthouse keeper, not even reliable in their madness.
                                                  If they already have the persistent version of Madness subtract 3 from the roll.

                                                      Depending on how you want to parse the metaphysics, all supernaturals already have access to a narrow subset of occult physics: the powers and traits that make them supernatural.

                                                      If a person with this particular blood trait bleeds into the mouth of a fresh corpse Then the corpse gets up, bites club kids, bench-presses cars, and writes shitty poetry.

                                                      That's, arguably, occult physics: a natural, predictable fact of the universe that is nonetheless beyond human scientific understanding or explanation, and in fact departs sharply from what our more mundane understanding of the physical sciences says should happen (namely, that you should have a corpse with a bloody mouth and a lot of really interesting questions to answer when the cops arrive).

                                                      The God Machine just has access to a much bigger (possibly complete?) understanding of all the requisite If/Then statements, and an understanding of the theory that backs them. Where a vampire is like a chemistry student with only four reagents in front of them and an explicit formula written out in a lab book (And somebody's ripped out the part that explains why any of this works), The God Machine has its PhD in chemistry, has won a nobel prize, and has a fully stocked lab at its disposal.

                                                      So the answer is, any mortal with any ability to interact with the supernatural arguably already has some degree of occult physics under their belt. They just don't know the why and the how that would let them improvise more. But that's because almost no one in the WoD has that.
