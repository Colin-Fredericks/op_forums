    The God Machine's Anti-Symbolism

    11-30-2013, 04:50 PM
    Bonjour,

    I may be running a game of Demon soon, which is exciting, but I wish to ensure I am fully understanding the aesthetics of the game. To that end, I would like to verify whether my reading of the God Machine is "correct" or at least cogent.

    So:

    The God Machine doesn't do symbolism. Form always follows function. In this way, it is nearly the antithesis of, say, Mage or Changeling, which rely heavily on symbolism (mystical in the former, narrative in the latter). If a piece of Infrastructure grows a tree that can cleanse the are it is planted in of pollution, it is not because trees are symbolic of nature's purifying power or what-have-you, but because encoded in the universe is some occult law of physics that causes the confluence of that tree placed in that location to cancel out the polluting phenomena. On the same note, though on the literary level that angels are made of machine parts might be symbolic of their conformist natures, the God Machine does not approach their construction with the same eye. A machine angel has those specific machine parts because they aid in its function. Mechanical hearts are more resilient and efficient than those made of meat, etc. The God Machine might not even understand the concept of exegetical symbolism. I imagine it is intelligent enough to recognize symbolism as an integral part of human psychology, and probably plans accordingly if a specific angel is needed for a specific task (i.e. capitalizing on the mythological infrastructure in place in India by giving a Messenger angel meant to amass a cult there the form of Kali or Shiva), but the idea of, say, dressing up a Psychopomp angel as a skeleton in Haitian funerary garb for its own aesthetic sake would probably never naturally occur to it.

    To summarize: Form follows function, and symbolism is (almost always) unnecessary window dressing in the God Machine's electronic eyes.

    Does this sound about right? If so, I wonder what sorts of implications the God Machine has for supernatural entities that do deal heavily in their own symbolism--Might Tale Crafting always fall apart when attempted on an angel, for instance?

      Well....Yes and no.

      THe thing of the matter is that while form follows function, the functions are inherently inherently somewhat strange and abstract to our eyes, and certainly a very large thing to the God Machine. Working off an (ill-remembered) example from Demon, just because you kill people at four clocktowers doesn't mean you automatically have the engine to fold space and time-the form has to line up in some way so that it has occult significance. We think of the Sephirot as a visual guide of Kabbalah's Ein Sof's manfiestations(putting it stupidly simple and somewhat incorrect), but to the God Machine it's just an extremely effective circuit board or something for reality.

      The point is that, yes, Form follows function, and occult symbolism is more a happy accident than a deliberate evocation, but that still doesn't mean the God Machine won't build Infrastructure in such a way that resembles a pentagram or something. After all, it works.

      EDIT: Or putting it another way, the work of the God Machine can be as symbolic or non-symbolic as you want, all justified by the higher knowledge of the Machine. I like to mix things up, use symbols as a base and then tweak it according to city/architechtural/computer board design- mixing the occult with the practical.

          Within the GMC Framework, Occult symbolism is often just a trapping, except when it isn't, and it's actually a functionally necessary part of the equation.

          A God Machine project will be covered with mystic symbology, but only because those symbols are necessary parts of the energy channel, not because of any aesthetic.

          More to the point, within the framework of the GMC, all mystical symbols and trappings stem from the complex laws and backdoors of reality that the god machine uses. So if the God Machine uses the Tree-of-Life pattern we associate with Kabbalah magic, it's not that the God Machine is using Kabbalah to power it's infrastructure; It's that Kabbalah is using one of the God Machine's reality pathways to enact other magic. The God Machine doesn't make Angels in the image of gods, it made Angels in the form that was useful or necessary, and humans who survived or benefited from the projects they were sent to preform either built a religion around them or incorporated them into their existing religion.

              Short answer: yes. Long answer: An occult matrix may incorporate symbols that humans attribute meaning to. But the reason for said symbols is not because of what they represent, but because they are a necessary part of the Sufficiently Advanced science involved in the occult matrix. If anything, human symbolism may have developed from observation of these occult matrices. So the reason we associate trees with purification and life is because they are used in purifying occult matrices, and through observation and cultural osmosis we've become peripherally aware that trees have something to do with with cleansing. But the real reason the God Machine uses them that way in the first place is that there's some actual physical law it's exploiting. We just don't understand the science behind it, but in theory anyone who knew how it worked (and controlled implement the proper infrastructure) could achieve the se outcome by the same methods. Because it's all just science we don't yet comprehend.

                      Thank you for your responses. I had neglected the Kaballah angle, which I think does dovetail nicely into G-M themes. I'm imagining the characters breaking open a computer in an Infrastructure library and finding the board circuits to be arranged into the Sephiroth.

                      Speaking of symbolism in the game, it's hard to miss that the number four is most recurrent: Four Incarnations, four Agendas, four Cipher keys, etc. It's mentioned in the section on Ciphers that not a small number of demons consider the number to have special significance, and the God Machine is also aware of this. I wonder whence the number comes? It's recurring in Christianity/Judaism/Islam (four beasts, four matriarchs, Eid al-Adah lasts for four days, the Tetragrammaton), not to mention the number's importance in other cultures (the Four Noble Truths of Buddhism, four in Japanese is considered unlucky because it's a homophone for death), though all of those could be the result of G-M meddling to give demons something to drive themselves crazy with. On the other hand, four might actually have some occult significance that the G-M merely exploits. I guess for now it's up to the Storyteller to decide. The truth of the matter could definitely make for a decent Inquisitor's Cipher.
