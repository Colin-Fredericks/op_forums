    Originally posted by von Lyttenburgh View Post

    Was there any Western head of the Intelligence service in the NWoD, who'd not only learn about the existence of the supernatural threat, but after his tenure as the head of his Agency, just become an influential regional administrator (Semichantsy was first deputy minister of the Ukrainian SSR from 1967 till 1981) and then retire in comfort, writing memoirs and giving interviews?
    I can't speak to everything you've said, and you seem to have some good points, but I think this is something that we do have a pretty clear idea of: Absolutely. Clarified in the 2nd edition materials (and discussed a bit in the Innocents supplement) is the fact that most people in the World of Darkness ignore the supernatural. They might've encountered it once when they were younger or in some other context, they forget about it, they pretend it didn't happen, they ignore it and move on. People that don't do that become Hunters and/or tend to look like crackpot crazy people.

    No Police Officer who wants to keep his badge is going to say that a vampire killed his murder victim. Even if he saw those fangs with his own two eyes, all he'll get is a visit from IA and paid leave to "decompress." Nobody wants to be 'that guy.' Nevermind the mental trauma of encountering the supernatural for a lot of people (typically represented with Integrity Breaking Points) between stuff like that and the Lie, it's just the nature of the setting. People catch a glimpse of what's real, and they don't go under the bed looking for it. They hide in their covers and try to pretend it doesn't exist. (Protagonists defy this expectation, it's the nature of the story. You might ask, "what if they don't look away?" That's your answer. Those people are the protagonists, the hunters, the monsters themselves, et al.)

    Anyway, to bring it back to your question, I can imagine a regular human being who doesn't want the rest of his life to be hellish, short and maddening going on and ignoring what he might've dealt with when he was doing Intelligence. I'm pretty sure they do that with the mundane intelligence in real life, evident with the real man himself.

    To use an example that demonstrates the principle (I'm not a crazy person!) I'm sure becoming the Prime Minister of <country here> or the President of the United States might entail learning some shocking (albeit, probably not supernatural) secrets about what people are really doing in a lot of murky situations where the rest of us don't know the truth. We don't see these leaders, after they leave office, going off the deep end or blabbing about it, do we?

    I'm not trying to leave this as some kind of definitive answer, but I think this is the sort of approach the writers take when seriously considering these kinds of questions. Hope that idea helps your thought process, at least with regards to that question, since I don't think I can help with anything else.

    Lots of people know a lot of secrets about a lot of things, that could totally fuck up their marriage, their home, maybe their neighborhood, city, country, etc. but they manage not to talk about them. The supernatural is just one more in a litany of secrets that people keep. Might even be easier for a former intelligence guy, since that sort of lying was literally his trade.

                        People say 'The Supernatural' like it's some unified bible of truth regarding the way the world works. Of course, in 2e nWoD, heads of state are briefed on a wide variety of totally inexplicable phenomena. This goes far beyond the major templates and probably doesn't even linger particularly long with them. The president hears about the Dero, alien abductions, Nazi moon bases... in the nWoD, he has a stack of papers that's probably two or three feet thick.

                        Those that report on these incidents and deal with them are an unreliable lot ; often on the brink of madness or overly involved with various occult conspiracies themselves. Some information is reliable, the vast majority is just 'probably accurate' but 'verifiable and genuine'. The picture painted is not one of a supernatural uber-conspiracy that secretly manipulates humanity, but rather a chaotic storm of Twilight Zone episodes, often devoid of context or greater meaning.

                        Which is just how the Machine likes it.

                            Given how much weird shit is in WoD, I can't imagine a lot of intelligence agency people not knowing about supernatural stuff. They would be really bad at their jobs otherwise. The thing is, with so many critters and different sorts of critters, with varying levels of masquerade, for something dedicated to intelligence gathering it would be hard to not stumble over it.

                            As noted, though, the Lie and the Integrity stat makes widespread knowledge unlikely. Adding to that, at least in the past century roughly, there has been a pronounced shift in the zeitgeist away from believing in anything which might be considered supernatural. People get mocked for believing in ghosts, much less vampires. For someone famous to stand up and say "Hey guys? Vampires. Also, Werewolves." in the west would lead to their being roundly mocked and ignored. And outside the west, it is not necessarily gone.

                            And honestly, even in the west, many people believe in Angels and Demons. A number believe in ghosts, despite the mockery. None of this has resulted in government actions against Demons.

                            So none of it streaches believability for me. What I would find hard to believe, in a world with Vampires, Werewolves, Mages, Prometheans, Changelings, Sin-Eaters, Mummies, Demons, Angels, Skinchangers, Immortals, Psychics, and Wookalaars*, if someone invasive as STASI or powerful as the CIA, KGB or FBI had no idea any of that existed.

                            Of course, as noted, knowing does not mean accurate or complete information. Believing in Psychics and Demons may still have someone thinking Vampires or Mummies are silly to believe in.
