    Little bit of a necro, but I remembered this thread while thinking about Demon, and how much I like this idea for an Embed. So I took a crack at it.

    Admittedly, I used the Primum Miles version of the idea and wrote up a version of the Embed. Let me know what you guys think.


    Backdoor

    A door is a portal between two different spaces. Demons who learn the Backdoor Embed learn how to exploit this conceptual relationship and the uncertainty that is inherent in any closed door. Any random door could lead anywhere. Humans may think a door will lead them where they want to go, but a Demon can make it so.

    First, the door in question must be closed and must not have been opened in the same scene that this Embed is used. Second, the destination must be one that the Demon can clearly visualize. Many Demons target supply closets and other infrequently used doors with Backdoor, and it is used for infiltration as often as for escape.

    Dice Pool: Wits + Streetwise

    Action: Instant

    Roll Results
    Dramatic Failure: The demon’s door not only fails to lead them to their desired destination, it spits them out of a random door within Primum miles of their point of entry, disoriented. The demon suffers the Stunned Tilt upon arrival. Additionally, witnessing their sudden appearance through the door will likely prompt compromise.

    Failure: The demon fails to connect the two portals. Opening the door leads to its usually intended destination. Additionally, that door cannot be used to activate Backdoor for the rest of the scene.

    Success: The demon successfully connects the two portals, allowing access to a nearby door that’s within Primum miles. The two doors are connected for one additional turn per success. This effect can be reflexively cancelled by the demon, severing the connection if attempting a quick getaway.

    Exceptional Success: The connection between the two portals lasts until the end of the scene, and can be cancelled reflexively by the demon. Additionally, they can sense anyone who steps through either end of the Backdoor.


    I think I have one concern about my Dramatic Failure. I think it's a really cool last resort option. Let's say you're running from the cops and you approach a supply closet and try to use Backdoor to escape. You fail your roll, just your luck. Either you can sit on your failure and figure out some other way of escaping, or you can opt for that dramatic failure. Then Backdoor works! Only... it puts you at some random door within a mile. The possibilities are endless in a city, and many of them could be bad for your health. One guy might wind up in the sewers and make his getaway. Another might wind up at open elevator doors when the elevator hasn't arrived yet, etc.

    That randomness could be a hook for more craziness. Is it too weird or inappropriate?

        I really liked Leetsepeak's dramatic failure condition on his rendition of the embed. I decided to have a go at my own version of this embed, please let me know what you think!

        Employee Access

        A doorway is a portal, the terms are synonymous with one another. Demons understand this reality on a level beyond the limited human concept, realising that a door has the potential to go anywhere. The God-Machine often uses the potentiality of doors to deploy its angels, it does this via a “location” known as “the hallway”.
        A demon with this embed has potential access to the Hallway. The demon can choose a door that has not been opened during the scene and activate this embed; when he does the demon must elect to open the door to the Hallway or to a destination doorway that he can visualise clearly (such as a place he has been or seen), if he is already within the Hallway. The door the demon used to access the Hallway will continue to access it until it is closed or until the scene ends. Once inside the Hallway the demon must use this embed a second time in order to get a door that enters his desired location. This embed does not unlock the destination door. Note: He does not have to use this embed to leave the Hallway, but where he emerges will be left to the decision of the storyteller if he does not.
        Dice Pool: Wits + Streetwise
        Action: Instant
        Roll Results
        Dramatic Failure:         The demon has successfully accessed the Hallway, but at a part of it that is dangerous to the demon in some way. This may be because angels are using nearby doors or because nearby doors in the Hallway all lead to an inconvenient location. Roll for compromise.
        Failure: The embed fails to function and the door does not change.
        Success: The demon has successfully accessed The Hallway, and may use it to escape the area.
        Exceptional Success: The demon has successfully accessed the Hallway, right where he needs to, and the door directly across the hall from the entrance he used to enter will lead to his desired destination. This means the demon does not need to use the embed a second time in order to get to where he wants to go.

        The Hallway (Sidebar Style!)
        The Hallway is a quantum space similar to a demon's bolthole. The origins of the Hallway are unknown, but the God-Machine's angels (and even sometimes its especially favoured agents) utilise it to move unseen from place to place or to influence the world at a moment's notice.
        Aesthetically the Hallway appears to be a never-ending hallway with evenly spaced doors on either wall. The decoration and trappings of the Hallway are never the same on any visit. Sometimes it can appear to be a long, up-scale Art Deco hotel hallway, with each door being a room. While on another occasion it could be a long hospital hallway on a ward, where each door should lead to a patient's ward.
        However some things are always the same about the Hallway. The doors are never numbered or labelled in a way that makes sense to the human mind. The numbers may be impossibly large or incomprehensible, causing eye-strain and headaches when peered at, or they may be absent entirely. Every door looks the same and each repeated feature is identical – right down to chips, stains and aging on the decorations.
        The Hallway, although it can be accessed by demons, is a God-Machine construct and as such any demon using it runs the risk of being noticed by the God-Machine and it's servants. Demons regard the Hallway with caution, for they don't really know why or where it exists; some even speculate that it may be the access-hub of Heaven itself.

                                            Okay, I built up my version of the Embed, with a generous handful of advice and editing from Satchel:

                                            ​Shortcut:
                                            A closed door is a portal full of unresolved potential, liable to take you anywhere. While it's reasonable to assume what's on the other side ("My closet." "The break room."), the connections between discrete locations are more flexible than they seem. A Demon who knows how to take advantage of this uncertainty can substitute one location behind a door for another, if just for the moment it takes them to pass through.

                                            A Demon lays the groundwork for this power by touching a door and concentrating as an Instant action. This door is now 'tagged' by the character, standing out among the countless other portals that separate places from each other. The Demon can have up to [Primum] tagged doors at once, each one remaining tagged for roughly a month after the character sets them. From any point after this they can activate this Embed while opening a door—any door—to have it lead through one of their tagged doors instead of where it logically should.

                                            Dice Pool: Wits + Streetwise
                                            Action: Instant
                                            Dramatic Failure:​ The door is misconnected, accessing a 'utility corridor' used by agents of the God-Machine instead of the Demon's intended destination. These not-quite-places are backdoors used by angels, cryptids, and other strange creations of the God-Machine, none of whom are likely to take well to an unscheduled encounter.
                                            Failure: The door opens to where it normally would, but the character can try to use the Embed again.
                                            Success: The Demon opens a door, connecting it to any of the doors she currently has tagged, ignoring crude things like 'distance' and 'spatial geometry.' The door remains open for [successes] minutes unless it is closed earlier by hand.
                                            Exceptional Success: The door can remain open for up to a scene or hour. Using the door is a reflexive action. Should the demon find himself ambushed on the other side, he adds his Primum to the roll to react to surprise as well as his Initiative
