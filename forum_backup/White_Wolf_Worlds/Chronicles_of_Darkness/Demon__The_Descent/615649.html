    Compromise rolls in the Invisible Realms?

    08-04-2015, 06:55 PM
    The core book of Demon says virtually all Compromise rolls fall into one of the following categories:

    1) Assuming part or all of your Demonic Form.
    2) Using certain Embeds or any Exploit.
    3) A key fact about your true nature is revealed to humans somehow, and they believe it.
    4) Taking an action grossly out of character for your Cover.

    If you have the "Rip The Gates" Exploit or an equivalent way of entering other planes of existence (e.g. Hedge, Shadow, Underworld, Astral), how might Compromise rolls be affected while you are in them? The God-Machine is less pervasive inside these places, despite apparently preferring the use of ghosts over spirits. I'd say that all Compromise rolls gain a +1 bonus while you are hiding in them, but that bonus should be temporary, especially if you fled because you had the Hunted Condition or something similarly dangerous.

    Since you are very unlikely to bump into any humans while in those places, number 3 is not a big deal. But if by coincidence/the power of plot you meet a human who knows the Cover you are wearing right now, the number 3 Compromise roll might get even worse than usual.

    Finally, what counts as grossly out of character for your Cover in the presence of, say, Geister or hobgoblins? If you are in their turf and you look like a normie, they might think you are either a competent occultist or extremely unlucky. However, if you happen to be in a part of those turfs where the existence of the Unchained is known, initial encounters will be very different. If you admit up front that you are supernatural, that might not cause a Compromise roll at all, since it is normally the humans who don't accept the existence of the supernatural. However, if you decide to play along and they later figure out what you really are, a Compromise roll may not be all that happens. They might keep an eye out for you next time or even follow you back to Earth.

    Compromise rolls in the Astral should be analyzed in a case by case basis, I think. You may be immune to Compromise in Temenos Domains related to ideas of advanced technology and how it would change our world. Compromise rolls might be extremely hard inside a Temenos Domain embodying the paranoid, ignorant fear of all magic and even mundane science as witchcraft and heresy.

    How does the psychoreactive nature of the Hedge react to Demons? I'd say the Thorns are usually horrible for Covers if you stay too long, unless you get a Cover by making a Soul Pact with a hobgoblin or even a Gentry. But what about the phenomena that occur as a response to an Unchained in full Demonic Form? Does the expenditure of Aether somehow make the local Hedge more robotic or mechanical?

    Did I miss anything important?

      Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
      Finally, what counts as grossly out of character for your Cover in the presence of, say, Geister or hobgoblins?
      The same things that count as grossly out of character for your Cover in the presence of human people. Or in the presence of nobody else at all. The presence of witnesses can make a compromise risk more severe, but it's not a prerequisite. Covers don't work on consensus reality; they're about internal consistency. You protect yourself with Cover by maintaining the plausible actions that preserve it, and when you contradict that identity, you damage your own protection.

      There's wiggle room, of course. I would probably count supernatural aspects under 3) rather than 4), meaning if no humans are around you can talk freely. But if you're a quiet family man and you open up with a barrage of murder, that's still grossly out of character, even if you're slaughtering space aliens in Astral Mars.

      Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
      Does the expenditure of Aether somehow make the local Hedge more robotic or mechanical?
      I'm generally slow to associate demon = robots, the aesthetic is there but it's not as simple as a straightforward translation. There are angels and Unchained that don't look especially robotic, and there are particular technological aesthetics that are probably inappropriate for the Unchained. Regardless, though, I'd look at the examples we already have of what happens when things are suffused with aetheric discharge: they mutate. The Fall causes malfunctions in physical laws; proximity to active projects produces cryptids and stigmatics. I'm picturing phantom trods that don't go where trods are supposed to, unusual frequency of distortions in space and time, flora and fauna that manifest aspects of the Wyrd that govern them through contracts, and hobs becoming not quite hobs, developing collective minds or spontaneously diverging from their usual fey behaviors.

          Excellent points. In addition to these corrupted creatures and environmental phenomena, one could explore what the Hedge usually does do visitors, rather than the contrary.

          The main threat the Hedge poses can be described broadly as "twisting perspectives". If a puritanical man with excessively harsh opinions on sex becomes isolated from women for a long time while trapped in the Hedge, he might lay in bed with the hobgoblin, changeling or Gentry that looks the closest to a woman, even against his better judgement. Imagine how horrible it must be for him, a man of faith, succumbing to the charms of these demons and distancing himself from the grace of God. Hobgoblins with huge claws and teeth might be horrifying, but sufficient firepower both mundane and mystical can easily blast them into particulate ash. And the Unchained have plenty of firepower (Rivet Arm + Merciless Gunman + Hellfire, for one). Internal conflict, though? That can doom anyone.

          That brings me to the question: what is considered the standard perspective for most of the Unchained, and how might exposure to the Thorns twist it?

          My first hunch is "trust no one". The second is "always have a plan B". The third is "I can fool anyone, no one can fool me". The Embeds and Exploits that Demons use rely on bending the rules of the hidden, occult physics intertwined with standard physics. Or a similar wording. However, the Hedge runs on a "more powerful" set of codes: the rules of Narrative. Anyone who is hiding must be found, anyone who lies must be unmasked, otherwise there is no story for the Wyrd. This might be interpreted as the Hedge being inimical to the Unchained, since they are superlative liars for both survival and power. But one can hide and lie for good reasons. Whatever it is that you do, the Hedge will alter itself to milk and bleed the conflict out of your decision. The Thorns are one third place, one third entity and one third event. This does not mean that all heroes must be tempted and all villains punished. It depends a whole lot on context. Even though Demons are techgnostic rather than theological, the Hedge cannot help but think in terms of archetypes, usually the more immediate and visceral ones. The more you play along with the Thorns, the more entangled you get. You either give them their fill, or escape. And the more they eat, the hungrier they get.

          In mechanical terms, the inherent Demonic benefits of Total Control, Natural Aptitude and Liar's Tongue do not work inside the Hedge (if you think that is excessive, they lose these benefits only when off the Trods). In other words, Demons start manifesting thoughts and emotions involuntarily, they can only speak the languages which they have spent enough time studying, and they cannot say something and then have it read as their choice of true/false to technology/magic. Unless dictated otherwise by the current situation, Embeds, Exploits, Demonic Form and Gadgets work as usual. There is a rumor that Pacts made inside or near the Hedge have a tendency to put either party involved in more difficult than average situations, but Demons in general don't know if this is unavoidable. The fact is that the Thorns, being the second greatest manifestation of Wyrd in material form, can both help and hinder people who make a deal with the devil. There have been Tempters who tried to bargain with people struggling to resist dangerous or dark urges inside the Hedge. Sometimes, it was even the Tempter who led the person there. The person who sings the Pact can expect greater than usual troubles, but should they find a way to bend the rules to their favor, the Wyrd blesses the person for the great story she has told: she can keep all the benefits from Pact, but can choose what words of their part will still be obeyed, if any. She'd better not push her luck, though. Angry Demons are smarter Demons.

                  Originally posted by GottaGoFeast View Post
                  That brings me to the question: what is considered the standard perspective for most of the Unchained, and how might exposure to the Thorns twist it?
                  By nature, there is no standard perspective. If the God Machine is the embodiment of the Status Quo and Order, then demons are the embodiment of Chaos. Look at a demon's virtue and vice. Where mortals tend to have "good" virtues and "bad" vices, demons can pick any combination they want - a "good" option for both virtue and vice, bad virtue and vice, bad vice and good virtue, good vice and bad virtue. You can have a demon with a virtue of Lust/Hedonism and a Vice of Prudence. Their perspective is based on however they were designed by the G-M and how its evolved since then. The only constant is fear of being caught by the Man and the hope of achieving Hell (or rejoining G-M, you turncoats). Note that Hell can be anything from a place, to a state of being, to a mindset, to wiping out all GM infrastructure in existence; Hell is just as chaotic as the demons themselves.

                  How might the Hedge twist someone? In general, it makes the person more fey-like, which generally means more spontaneous and likely to indulge in their Vice. Your minister example likely had a Lust vice. A demon with a Charitable vice would become, ironically enough, nicer and more caring, while one with a Wrathful vice would be, well, angry.

                  My first hunch is "trust no one". The second is "always have a plan B". The third is "I can fool anyone, no one can fool me".
                  Standard paranoia, appropriate for a spy game. I don't think its what the Hedge would encourage, though. When you're in a realm, you're twisted to becoming more like its natives. Underworld tries to make you more ghost-like, the Hedge more fey, the Hisil more predator-like, the Astral more dream-like. The Supernal turns you into a platonic symbol of an aspect of reality.

                  The Embeds and Exploits that Demons use rely on bending the rules of the hidden, occult physics intertwined with standard physics. Or a similar wording. However, the Hedge runs on a "more powerful" set of codes: the rules of Narrative. Anyone who is hiding must be found, anyone who lies must be unmasked, otherwise there is no story for the Wyrd.
                  Not true. There are plenty of trickster stories of the one who got away. Jack and the Beanstalk for one off the top of my head. Anything related to Coyote, Loki, or similar gods. And, even though the Wyrd will likely try to mess with the Unchained, it'll likely focus on messing with the current Cover. The story will be one of the Jane Doe, the lost "princess" of a mega-corporation's CEO, despite it just being a Cover.

                  Spoofing holds up even in the Hedge. There's no reason for Hedge to be able to see under the Cover and know the demon isn't whom she claims to be. There's nothing innately "more powerful" about the Wyrd as opposed to Awakened magic, God-Machine's mastery of occult physics, the Dark Mother's lineage, Luna's blessings, or the vampiric Curse. These are all different magics that operate under different principles, but one isn't naturally stronger or more powerful than another. And, even if you did create some kind of hierarchy, the demonic ability to spoof and lie overcomes even them. Its designed so that God itself can't beat it. It either holds up, or it fails.

                  Anyways, the point is, there's nothing inimical about any of these powers mixing, unless you consider Demons and Mages (hiding v. mage sight), but I think that mages mess with anyone with a Masquerade. There's nothing about the Hedge that will negate demon Covers. If anything, it'll strengthen them.                   Why? You're attempting to destroy what makes demons, well, demons. Why don't we get rid of a vampire's ability to drink blood, or a werewolf's ability to shift form?

The only way to avoid that is assume Demon Form, which comes with its own increasing Compromise checks. Its a ticking time bomb for demons. Unless you make this barred from God, at which point, you've just firmly established a Hell. Game over.

                              To MCN: huh. Those are all extremely relevant points. I'm kind of embarrassed that I didn't think of them myself. Both your posts get a Like. I guess I was trying to make the Hedge treat Demons like it treats Changelings, that is, I thought it would remove their Mask (Cover in this case). Maybe because there is this trickster method of operation that is common for both splats, and I focused too much on that. So recapping:

                              1) Barring epic powers or miraculous circumstances, the God-Machine can always see you. Its agents might take longer to find out where you went if you fled to another realm, but their resources and skills are many. If they decide that the chase should be postponed, they'll do that, but it won't make you totally safe. Demons are never completely safe.

                              2) Even though the Invisible Realms have certain patterns, there is no rule saying that X must always happen. Heck, even different areas of the same Invisible Realm may have different effects on the same individual. Context is everything.

                              3) To borrow the terminology of theater, it may be said that the Demon is an actor, Covers are roles, Angels and other agents of the God-Machine are the audience, while the God-Machine itself is the script. When an actor does something wrong for his role, the audience might notice and show their disapproval in many ways, but they do so because the script says it is wrong. The script itself is not really a singular entity, it is more like an imperative with precise instructions. The Fall might be described as what happens when you get tired of only watching the actors live so differently and decide to become an actor too. However, that opens you to the same harsh criticisms that you gave them before. Granted, the characters might never find out what the God-Machine truly is, and poor reviews are not the same as being hunted by Angels with fire/plasma swords. No analogy is perfect, as far as I know.

I also assume that wearing the Cover of a vampire or promethean makes you look like you have the Blush Of Life activated or suppressed Disfigurements, respectively. The same goes for the other splats.
