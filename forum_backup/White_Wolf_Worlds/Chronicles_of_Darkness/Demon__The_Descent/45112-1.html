            Part of the problem is scope. While it is easy for a Mortal to see a hundred cultists dropping thousands of origami newspaper cranes in Central Park, it is much less easy for a mortal to grasp that a terrorist attack at exactly the right moment is discovered by 16 people across a city, at the same time, and the sudden release of Fear by those 16 people at the right moment forms a series of interlocking "terror" ley lines drawing power to a source.

            The way I explain it to my players:

            Mortals see the lake.
            Supers see the ripples on the water.
            Stigmatics see the rock that was thrown.
            Demons see the arm that threw it.

            Even the most accomplished Mage is only going to be able to see or sense "effects" of the God Machine's plans. Where Unawakened Mortals may see a Flash Mob in downtown Boston, Magi might sense a surge of power. After all, Magi may be struggling to unravel the underlying metaphysics of the universe, but they're only discovering parts of the system the God Machine wants them to see: the fishbowl they live in. Meanwhile, the God Machine is off at PetCo buying fish flakes, and they probably have no idea (unless that's the scope of your Mage game).

            That being said, if your players are actively wanting to play a "screw with the God Machine" game, they should be Demons and not other supers. That's Demon's bag, and Demon deserves to stand alone as a game. xD

                    It isn't the "Alpha and Omega", no, but then it's really just a matter of scope in that regard, isn't it? But it also understands the fundamental nature of the "Laws" that define the Universe in a level of cognition far beyond the players, and utilizes those laws to perpetuate it's own self-preservation. It utilizes occlusion to hide it's nature from the rest of the Universe, and doesn't interact directly with anything. We know that it exists "here" as many pieces of a larger puzzle, but it has influence all around. It can focus given forces of nature, governed by rules alien to it, to accomplish feats of supreme power (altering time, for example).

                    We know that the motivations of "Hell" for some Demons are to find a "place" where the God Machine has no Influence.                         I haven't read much in the Demon book yet, but GMC notes that most of the God Machine's infrastructure is in the material (and interstitial terain such as the splinters).                                 I would begin by comparing GMC technology to cellular biology. You have machines that use worldly physics, but which use a seemingly infinite level of complexity that couldn't be reproduced by a human just knowing quantum physics and trying to design a machine piece by piece. You have to use really intelligent reverse-engineering to begin to understand what has already been made, and customizing it is more a matter of tinkering with a piece here and there and seeing the effects, or having things built out of automated evolutionary algorithms rather than intentionally putting anything together. If you want to have a character begin to understand it intellectually, I'd be looking for a base of 6+ permanent dots in both mental attributes and skills and, of course, GMC Specialties.

                                Whatever the God-Machine's actual nature is, I'd compare it to Rank 9 entities in terms of its own (notably gap-speckled) intelligence.
