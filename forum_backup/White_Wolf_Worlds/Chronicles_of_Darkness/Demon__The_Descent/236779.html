    Rosie the Riveter - a story of a fall

    08-21-2014, 03:16 PM
    ((Don't mind me, my first Demon fic/story. She uses an embed and her first exploit in that order, in case that isn't clear. The rest is up to you and Sir Money. I wrote this on my phone.))

    There's always a dame in a good noir story. The first time I saw the one I found, she scared the living daylights out of me-I knew she was an angel, one of the enemy, from the moment I laid eyes on her. She wore a sailor's uniform, as in a real Navy officer's uniform. I looked at her bars-an Ensign, specifically. How about that.

    This story is about her in the end, not me.

    She was drinking, nursing her glass properly, just so timing on a loop. It took me a moment to realize that she wasn't paying me any attention at all. Angels are a serious danger and one in my neighborhood troubled me. I had gotten by on a little of God's leavings just fine in this backwater area-but I wasn't any match for a fucking angel, even one alone like her.

    See, I am a demon. I made my first choice to heat up the roulette table and made an absolute killing while my charge got pulled aside by some finer-dressed gentlemen. I got rich and I also got my freedom from that first wager. Not saying what casino, not saying where it was; that's nothing nobody needs to know, okay? I had settled into a less active area and into a steady agency since then. Any demon that lives long joins one and I ain't an exception there. They offer too much to ignore them and they can mean the difference between life, death or erasure. Yeah, I count getting recycled into a good little angel as a worse fate than death.

    I looked over to where she had been looling-all three spots. A couple drunks that changed over but she didn't stop looking there-the bathrooms, such as they were-and the corner outside the bar, where cars went by in a sizzling heat. I fronted as a human the first time she scanned the room, spoofing her systems and God. She went back to nursing her drink without knowing a thing.

    I am sometimes dumb for a demon-no demon is infallible, something some of us demons proclaim loudly and in a real irritating way. The first thing I did with my riches was get piss drunk. Why let it go at being free? You live and I do just that- just one week was what it took to blow through the money I got. I learned what sex, drugs and rock and roll were instead of making sure some wino didn't get his kneecaps busted for cheating. So sue me. I protect assets now and I get paid for it, appreciated even. I have, however, learned *taste* since then and she was a real treat to look at.

    Any angel or demon can have many faces, many names. People call me Mr. Money even now for all the faces and names I have worn-but money isn't the only resource I work with. Her? She looked like a beautiful, pale flower with skin pale white and I realized that I couldn't see her eyes through the aviator glasses she wore, they were so dark-tinted. She wore no makeup, her shoes were simple, short black heels. Her hair was simple too, pinned into a classical updo. Her face looked a little broad, with high sculpted cheekbones, thick eyelashed and full, pouty lips. Mm, mm, mm. I remember thinking *...damn, it would be a shame if that face didn't exist tomorrow.* So I said: "Bartender, a round for the lovely sailor over there. How long she been coming here?"

    Of course, I passed a fair bit more than what the round cost to her. "Ensign T? About two months. Tips alright, never takes a tab. Asks about my niece and has a hell of a pokerface. Real cool customer."

    Isn't it just like a Dick Tracy comic? Angels, demons-we don't exactly show emotions unless we make ourselves. We can read like a liar when we're honest, laugh when we want to cry, sound warm and caring when we want to kill a son of a bitch or seem alert enough when our bodies are exhausted. Our minds aren't in our brains for all that we have human bodies, it's somewhere else. I don't know how it works, it just does but I do know we got demon bodies just as and really, more real than our human bodies. And there I was, like a dumbass, admiring her human shell, covering up the terrible glory of her true, angelic form.

    Yeah, I'm a *dumb* demon-I watched the angel get brought a drink, who stared at the bartender. And then stared at me, blank-faced. Again, angel or demon-perfect liars, each and every one of us.

    She left a half hour after one o'clock AM. To where, I don't know but she downed the drink I'd sent her. That was interesting.

    What sort of angel was this? What was her mission? It boggled me. Was she a Shield? A Wheel? A Sword? No Trumpet would have stared like that, they are much smoother than that even as angels. I go out and I don't see her-maybe she has a cab as part of her cover. Me? I had to draw on my contacts with the agency. I went to a deaddrop and wrote on my way in the most current code I knew. Maybe I overstated her friendliness, maybe it was my attraction. Either way, I had a feeling about her, a good feeling not just in my pants. If I had used my head, maybe things would have turned out differently.

    The morning found someone pounding on my door. I peered through the peephole and saw a very serious and tired looking man with a three o'clock shadow but the smallest smile on his face. "Who is it?" I called, sounding tired and grumpy.

    "Buttercup and Wesley. Now open up and gimme some coffee for fucksake." He called.

    It might seem that the first phrase was the password-nope. It was the whole second sentence. The first phrase just told me he was going to help me. I unlocked it. "Son of a bitch, you been at the sauce. Come on in, bro." I grin and stand aside as he laughs, both of us seeming happy to see each other. "It's been forever..." I shut and locked the door, leading him to a more private part of my apartment.

    "So you saw the angel and she just... sat there, drinking. You're fucking serious?" He shook his head. "Okay. Folks higher up have given me a few clears on proceeding, though this is dangerous and rarely works you know." He warned me.

    "Hey, isn't it at least worth a shot?" I was hoping to assure him. He didn't look much assured. "We can assume though that she's supposed to do something. She has gone to the same bar every night for a week. She's waiting for something and we want to get to her before it comes up. Any problems or glitches that might make her Fall will be edited and calibrated out-if she isn't recycled first." He opened a suitcase and took out a number of devices that had very little to do with one another. "She might want something and these will help us figure out what."

    Ah, I knew what they were-each of them had an embed or exploit installed. One had a baleful red eye moving around on its lense. Embeds are basically loopholes and exceptions in reality that demons and angels can use-any angel has all the embeds appropriate to their type. Demons forget most of theirs when they fall but they can learn applications for these Embeds that angels just cannot. If they are thinking about them at all, they're usually about to Fall. He explained how each worked, each a variation on some Embed that stretched its base assumptions. While not as powerful, installs are damn useful in situations, especially when you don't have those embeds and you really damn well could use them. It's been long enough that I don't remember what they were, sorry. We went over plans and contingencies. Wheels within wheels. We went to the bar and lo and behold, there she was, my pale, pale angel. I hung away, sending a round her way.she noticed me and nodded at me. I wasn't sure why so I smiled at her.

    It is an amazing thing to see a Trumpet in action. He approached her, as planned, a smile on his face and singing sweet songs into her ear. It's always impossible to tell, I have said but I saw her leaning forward, stop looking at her targets. Finally, they both stand up. I follow them put of the bar. They meet me outside. She doesn't fidgit but volunteers speech. "This is still within mission parameters, this is something women do at taverns."

    In a single sentence, she had betrayed so much of herself. Such an old word, such a plain statement of reassurance. "You do nothing wrong, sister Sword. I have not drank-why don't you talk to Cantor here about your prior missions? He is the other half, the shield to your blade." We got into the car.

    She stared at me so intensely through her shades. "May I see your eyes, sister?" I ask and reach for her glasses and she catches my hands. Her nails were painted, I remember that. They were white and seemed shaped into talon-like points but the white was awkwardly done and it didn't really look like nail polish. "Would you like to remove them yourself? Or keep them on?"

    She stayed there a few moments in silence, holding my wrists in warm hands. She let go of my hands, slowly going to the aviators, pulling them off... to reveal closed eyes. She opened them to reveal red-pink eyes. She was not simply pale-she was albino. "May I kiss you?" I blurted.

    "It's not covered in my mission." She spoke lamely.

    So I just kissed her. Just a little peck, really. It was dumb and not just because it was dangerous for me. A short enough ride it was, pulling up before one of the more distinctive highrises in town. A uniformed gentleman walked up to the car and welcomed us to the establishment. No, I am not saying which one. The trumpet tossed the gentleman the keys. "Don't scratch up the Bentley, alright?" He tucked money into the man's hand. "This way."

    She put back on those shades and we both ushered her inside. I didn't know where she was staying, if it was at the base or what but there were suites waiting within, each tailored to one of the four Incarnations: Swords, Shields, Trumpets and Wheels. My Trumpet guide led us into an elevator and rode upwards. It felt strange and I know I expected her to turn on us at any time. I also expected that of my guide but it was a lesser concern. If she turned on us, she was still plugged into God. She could call down other swords and me and the Trumpet would be so very fucked. It is a risk of doing business anywhere as a demon. If you sleep soundly or with one eye open don't really matter, so I sleep soundly as I can. And not take dumb risks. Until something or someone I want comes along. Then I am the dumbest smart demon for many a mile. We got into the suites and I marveled at the setup. There were so many crafts, so many things waiting to be made. She went first to the loom, hand going over the parts. The Trumpet started to explain how to work it before she waved him away and sat down, working it like a pro. I knew I had not made a mistake then. She explored that suite, going here and there and trying out just about everything like a curious little kid. She could not seem to make up her mind what she liked best. No expression a demon or angel makes is unconscious and yet, the attention and care she took with each possible thing to do! She actually fell asleep though with a book in her hands, a bit of dried clay here and a bit of pigment there.

    How adorable she was. The morning dawned and she rose at an unlikely hour of seven thirty in the morning. Tired I was indeed and coffee seemed just the thing to have. Plenty of cream, a bit of sugar, a splash of whiskey for taste-perfect for a cautious enough early morning. What can I say? I looked in the mirror and saw a three o'clock shadow already and decided to hell with it, my cover allowed me to grow a beard. A lounge lizard was expected to be scruffy at times, after all. She woke up and took a moment looking around. "I need to be dropped off somewhere." She said, sitting up, grabbing the book sliding off a generous-enough chest before it could fall to the ground.

    The Trumpet did not ignore what book it was-he held up the laser sight with the bizarre moving red eye and peered through it. The thing made a soft, metallic-sounding sigh as it fell apart in his hands. She dropped the book and picked up the now normalish-looking junk. The metal looked like it had been sawed away, the components melted or scorched though there was no scent of burnt plastic. "Don't worry, I will have a taxi called for you. Come eat breakfast first. It'll take a while for a cab to get here. Come on, now Miss Rose-Eyes."

    Pancakes, sausage, hashbrowns, eggs and toast really picked me up though it was hard to see if she liked it. She did eat all on her plate and slipped outside. "We are poor Tempters if she doesn't have much to consider. We watch now." He advised me for a few things to encourage her into falling; things to say, to do.

    My dame would not fall simply for the chance to create things, little did I know.

    That night, she showed up at the bar, dressed up in a new uniform. She did however bring something new: knitting needles and fine yarn. She talked with the bartender, who was happy to show off pictures of her young relations. I sent her a drink on me and watched her glance and raise a glass to me. I sneaked off to the bathroom, looking around and seeing no her but her knitting left behind. It was then I heard the screech of a car and a scream. I swore with feeling and ran outside, finding a bloody mess in the street and a bundle in her arms, speaking in low tones, calm as can be. That belied when the next second, all the visible streetlights blew, leaving sparks spilling onto the street and sidewalks below it. I felt her fall, felt her disconnect from the God Machine. She looked calm but tears were falling down her cheeks. The bartender came out and began screaming, running to the... dying woman. The pilot put a hand on the woman's shoulder. "He's miraculously unhurt."

    At first, it made me confused. He? The body soon to be cooling was a woman-but the bundle in her arms then moved. A baby! The bartender grabbed and clutched the infant to her, still asleep. She touched the woman's shoulder as the body became a corpse. It is exactly that what happened. My dame though; she made as if doing CPR and the woman gasped. Still hurt. No longer dead or dying. I saw the tracery of what looked like a circuit tattoo appear on both her hands, letting me know she had used one of our exploits to revive this woman. "It's not as bad as it looks. It's still bad, but she will live." She spoke slowly.

    Understanding dawned on the bartender's face and she gave her a one-armed hug. "I don't know how you did it, but thank you. Thank you for saving my sister."

    "Try not to spread that around, alright? Greedy people would beg her to go to just about every grave site and hospital. Which she won't be doing." I told her.

    She looked between us, as if seeing us for the first time. This human's face became one of awe. "You're-okay. I'll keep this quiet."

    I went to the car and found a bloody, unconscious man who stunk of blood, smoke and alcohol. Was this her mission? No. I decided; she wouldn't fall if she had done her mission.

    I still tell myself this about my dame, my Destroyer who weaves, knits, sews and loves to fly.

    I have not seen the Trumpet again that I know of. The bartender's sister woke up in the hospital two days later. I do know their names but nobody else needs to know them. Oh, I know: call them Bea and Sissy. Original, right? Bea I thought might become a problem but she liked Ms. Rose too much. She got herself a gold star in my book when Bea finally woke up. "Nnn. Where-where did I-my baby-!"

    "-is fine. It was a miracle but he was unhurt. This is my friend, Sissy. She is the one who did first aid when you got hit-she saved your life."

    "What's your name?" Sissy asked softly.

    "You can call me Ensign Rose." Good on her for that.

    I stayed around the dame a while as she got on her feet, ran into stuff. What we couldn't know and didn't see was a witness who knows damn well who and what she is. She could turn my friend Rosie over to the angels any time. I just hope it's faster than whatever that Deva Corp bitch will have her co-workers do.

    But that's another story. While the suites didn't cause her to fall, I do believe they served their purpose as a catalyst for her fall. The damndest thing-the Trumpet brought her to the Psychopomp suite, not the Destroyer. I think, anyway. Hard to tell.

    You must be asking me-if I am so hot for a whole lotta Rosie, why didn't I sleep with her? Well. She didn't understand yet that she had choices, that she could tell me no. Maybe I am just an old fashioned sucker for a dame, no matter that I have been one and will again. Who knows-but that, my friends, is how an antimonian falls. Make no mistake-she will kill people when necessary and isn't one to let a punch to the face go unrepayed-but she does not seek death and violence. She has been a good comrade and an excellent help-thus that is why I ask you now to make her a part of our agency. She handled the accidental witness, now a useful stigmatic who is quite grateful, for what it's worth and you know what they say: gratitude is worth its weight in gold.

    Mine weighs a bit more than that, fellows-just a bit. What do you say?
