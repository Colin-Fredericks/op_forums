    I think the problem for me would come from two points: 1) it feels wrong that an extremely potent being like an Elder Vampire should be trivially knocked out and 2) there's the weird cognitive dissonance that, should we get to the point of fighting the Elder Vampire, using the power would feel too easy for the Vampire's narrative role but not using it doesn't seem to make sense as a Thing My Character Would Do. This issue will show up with any form of attack that shortcuts past a big chunk of the combat system, since the narrative idea of "this character is hard to defeat in combat" is encoded into the mechanics.

    I agree completely about where the problem is when people are doing things to ruin the game, but if I have to make a decision where one choice simultaneously looks like it'll make the game less dramatic and fun but also is the obvious sensible choice for my character, that does nasty things to the way I enjoy games.

                                    Knockout Punch isn't that singularly amazing. There's a lot of ways for demons to make one hit fight-ending for the target - Blade Hand can do 5L+ in a single hit, or Cavernous Maw can do 3A+ targetted to a limb. It's a straight pool of Dexterity+Brawl with defense subtracted, which means you have only one skill and one attribute up against GMC defense levels. There's not a lot you can do to inflate your pool, especially compared to using a light weapon, or a firearm (or rivet arm). And every time you miss is a -1 to later attempts in the scene.

                                        Leaving aside combat-specific things, here are my Embed problems:

                                        1. Why does Manipulation get everything? There are 47 Manipulation + powers, 5 Dexterity + powers, and 25 Wits + powers. A lot of them make sense to be Manipulation-based because they're things involving screwing with language nad heads. But then you get Manipulation subbing for anything from resolving binary events (Left or Right?), to pure luck (Lucky Break), to providing raw materials ex nihilo (Raw Materials), or ruining aim (Check Backdrop). I get that Manipulation doesn't contribute to combat like Wits does, or double-up on combat helping like Dexterity does, but it just feels like a crappy limitation in character-design-space when your options for subtle, insidious power use are "be good at lying, also" or else you throw out a little under 2/3 of your options.

                                        2. Why's there an overlap on some things, and why's Manipulation still get the best of it in that context? I'm looking specifically at Raw Materials, Sabotage, and Shatter (pp 186-7). Just three powers in a row, doing similar things, with wildly varying efficacy.

                                        Raw Materials: Manipulation. Breaks the target object no matter what, brings a replacement depending on success, might blow Cover on a dramatic failure.
                                        Sabotage: Dexterity. Shuts down the object until someone gets a few successes to repair it, harder to fix with exceptional successes, forces a compromise check on dramatic failure.
                                        Shatter: Wits. Breaks an object of Size (Yours or lower), risks blowing Cover if the object was above a Durability threshold, can have a delayed effect on exceptional success, hurts you on a dramatic failure.

                                        Why are there three of these, and why is the Manipulation one straight-up the best at actually doing the "break a thing" thing? It's not even the main effect of the power, but it's guaranteed with the added bonus of getting a replacement later (which would be handy if you're banking on something like surrendering yourself into custody or somesuch). The Dex one is the most subtle of them (and the only one that's temporary, really), unless you can get exceptional successes on the Wits one. And the Wits one is the only one to come with so many limitations---can't be above size X, risks blowing cover above Durability Y, straight up damages you on a dramatic failure.

                                        Now, IN A PERFECT WORLD, it would be super-cool if this kind of thing was part of a framework for effectively making almost any Embed "castable" via one of the three Finesse attributes (almost something akin to Geist keys being applied to Manifestations), but in practice it's just...here, which of these three ways of breaking stuff do you want: the one that might hurt you, the one that's only temporary, or the one that gives you an added bonus later?

                                        In general though, Demon's a really cool game idea, the space it occupies for espionage-but-with-powers, angelic/demonic-without-strictly-Judeo-Christian, Matrix-without-being-owoddy-90s is amazing. And a ton of the Embed/Exploit ideas themselves are amazing (pun power alone has my entire group yelling at each other and pointing fingers over who has to run a campaign for the rest of us to play in). I just wish the Embeds were anywhere near as stat-balanced for choices as the Exploits are (they're a much closer 11/12/17 split between key attributes, at least).
