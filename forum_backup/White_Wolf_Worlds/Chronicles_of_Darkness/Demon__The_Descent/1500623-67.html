    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
    While in principle I agree that Knowing spells should provide knowledge that is Lasting, I think there's a degree to which something that is a bit more active and transformative ought to be retained when a spell ends, especially if it's tied to Potency.

    Otherwise, what, can you just pile spells one after the other with Durations of only a turn but that yield tens of enhanced dice rolls because oh it's fine, you remember all of the insights provided?

    I think that especially matches up with some of the themes of Fate and Acanthus, the Tarot image of the Fool, where there might be knowledge but it's a bit more of a tacit, intuitive form of knowledge that could plausibly require a spell to hold up, compared to something that is a hard fact.

    I can imagine a person intellectually retaining what it was that made using Streetwise to socially interact with a librarian feel like a good idea, but something is lacking in the moment of it, some sense of proper timing and coordination, in a way that means when you try to pull it off it just feels awkward and gangly. You're like Bill Murray in the later parts of Groundhog Day, where his attempts to recreate something spontaneous and charismatic ends up feeling forced, despite how all the surrounding details should be exactly the same.
    I jump between both approaches depending on the feel of the spell. In the case of Perfect Recall, for example, I tend to prefer the memory needing a check after the duration expires, as its normally not easy to recall a moment to that exacting level of detail. But in the case of Serendipity, I see it as Fate giving a hint, not terribly difficult to act upon. In cases like the Reach effect of Craftsman's Eye, I prefer saying you retain the benefit, counterbalanced by it only affecting the latest tool you inspected. It all depends on what feels fun for your table.
