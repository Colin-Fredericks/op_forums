        The Sanctity of Merits and Covers sidebar reminded me of a house rule I use: where it makes sense to do so, I allow players to assign individual Merits to specific Covers. The downside is that the Merit is only available when in that Cover; the upside is that you can use to Sanctity of Merits rule to “redistribute” your Cover-specific Merit dots in other Covers (for this purpose, the Demonic Form counts as a Cover). So the businessman has plenty of Resources, while the math teacher has Contacts at the local university.

                            Impressions:

                            Front cover is sexy as hell, pun intended. First piece of demon art I actually enjoyed.

                            Opening fiction is likewise awesome. Thoroughly enjoyed it.

                            After that, we get into the meat of the book. I feel like there's actually two parts to this book, all in all.

                            The first is a series of essays on how to see things like your Cover, Rings, Agendas or Agencies; also, a bit on Infrastructure. There's a few new rules on how to handle things like faking emotions. But, overall, I feel like this section is just a major disappointment. For the most part, it just reiterates the same material from the core book. If you had trouble grasping the idea of the Unchained as Fallen Angels or had trouble wrapping your head around some other part of the game, then this section is for you. If you had a strong grasp of the core game already, then you're just wading through so many words. I feel like this part of the book is more set on pushing stereotypes than broadening different avenues of play.


                            One exception - there's a section on suborning Infrastructure, which is handy. It gives a couple new ways to analyze and get into how demons interact with Infrastructure. It has new rules for how to analyze and adapt to such places, and taking its power for your own. I appreciated it far more than any of the other parts, though it may be because I didn't entirely grasp that part of the core book myself - someone else may find that section to be dull as I find the Agencies.


                            Second half of the book is crammed full of new mechanics to play with. New Embeds, new Exploits, new Demon Forms, new ways to use and abuse gadgets. And, yes, Lambda are sexy as sin; something tells me that a lot of players are going to be jumping at the chance to make them. My only complaint is that it feels like boltholes are becoming less compelling as the demon's Safe Place merit, as you can't actually make your hole into a protected workshop. So, sorry, but no hidden hell-forge for you to keep tucked away in a hidden dimension.
