                            Neither cruel nor kind.

                            The God-Machine is fairly consistently painted as lacking in surplus resources to the degree that the losses a Fall presents - an entire angel, at the very least - are sufficiently problematic that, like Youtube's copyright-claiming software, it's too much of a liability to approach dealing with the Unchained via blacklist methodology - the number of demons it determines are worth more to it free than reclaimed are vastly outnumbered by the number it judges to be in need of repossession, as the Hunted Hunter's anecdote suggests, and free demons are by definition beyond the Machine's known ability to easily check, so we get the better-safe-than-sorry net thrown over the whole lot of potential misplaced embezzled funds that are the Unchained.

                            Being what they are entirely because of the sustained decision to embrace their own free will and separation from their former master, the bulk of the Unchained are understandably resistant to being approached with the very real prospect of being forcibly recycled with nothing they valued about their lives coming with them. When The System keeps trying to make you part of it again despite your strident refusals in every direct confrontation over the subject and your self-determination is viewed as an inefficient use of resources by a System that in many ways is all about efficiency, the relationship between the two forces tends to be antagonistic.

                            I'm fond of the ambiguity as to whether the Machine's designs include the Fall, as a motivational uncertainty. It's reminiscent of the question Werewolf occasionally poses about whether the Lunes speak for Luna.

                                                            Originally posted by torete View Post
                                                            Reminds me how I absolutely hated oWoD endgame books... especially Gehenna. Could not stand those games, even though some of the themes were nifty.

                                                            This is one of the prime reasons I prefer nWod, but I am especially glad that they decided to run with the God Machine. It does provide some of the cosmic horror themes, (nWod games were mostly local, except maybe for Mage, who always seems to be their favorite ) without giving one concrete, definite answer.


                                                            Speaking about Mage though, I thought MtAw was at least as Buddhist as it is Gnostic, or even more. But yeah... Mage was never my favorite game so what do I know
                                                            There are elements of Buddhism in Mage. Mage is based on the whole Golden Order of the Dawn style mystery western occult practices, which borrow heavily from Christian Gnostic texts, as well as bits of Egyptian geomancy and works of Thrice Great Hermes, Arabic numerology, and, yes, some Buddhism. That said, I personally find that Christian Mysticism aspects are really stronger than the buddhist.

                                                            I mean, the Watchtowers is a concept that's core to Christian mysticism - they're the abodes of the Archangels (instead of Mage's "Oracles"), one associated with each element (mage's "Paths.") Atlantis follows the story of the Tower of Babel - ancient advanced civilization that built a tower to reach the Supernal (this term literally means "Heaven"). For their hubris, the Tower was crushed, and the universal tongue of mankind ("High Speech") was sundered, a curse lain over humanity to scatter them so that they'd never again reach that high ("Sleeping Curse.")



                                                            There's more, and I could go on, but I think you get my point at the sheer amount of it all. Personally, I find that the weight of it all makes it difficult to look at Mage through the lenses of other cultures. Its difficult to really make a more neo-pagan style witch in mage society, I feel. Or Vodun. Or jumping over to Asia for Taoism, Buddhism, Hindu, or Shintoism.

                                                            So, I mean, its still there in the books. Werewolf, Mummy, and Changeling are the only two that I really think don't lean that way. Which is kind of inevitable, when you really think about it. The WoD books are -heavily- invested in Western pop culture. Christianity, for good or ill, is a major part of that culture, even if people don't realize just how much and how pervasive it is. Adapting to the point of making the books religion-neutral takes a lot of work, and is commendable for how far they did come.

                                                            I would love to see them push the envelop further as well with the Chronicle books as well.
