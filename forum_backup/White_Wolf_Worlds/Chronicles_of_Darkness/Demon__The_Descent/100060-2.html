                                                    In a thread that is about Demon interacting with either True Fae for Arcadia I find the amount of True Fae vs Luna discussion sorta weird in this thread. However I felt Tatterdemalion King did bring up some good points.

                                                    As Tatterdemalion King points out Luna/High Rank Spirits and True Fae are kinda (if you squint and look side on) similar beings. Also my earlier comment about the "love" True Fae receive is pretty well summed up with ideas like:
                                                    True Fae romancing Luna (because the Fae and Spirit worlds really interact so often in the Werewolf/Changeling books/fluff/mechanics).
                                                    Giving up on fixing the Iron problem because ways they already half figured out to do it (because god forbid a hard and fast aspect of reality they can't fuck around with exists).
                                                    The True Fae can travel to any realm attached to our reality (last I checked they were limited to the material world, Hedge, Arcadia and the Dream sub-realm of the Astral).

                                                    Of course, all this stuff is completely subjective to ones and the ST can do what they want within their game. Likewise, I get that a lot of what Tatterdemalion King said was in jest, fun and with a bit of sarcasm. I'm pointing out some of the example he gave because they are the sort of thing I've run into before unironically that people have stated about True Fae.

                                                    I agree that Imperial Mysteries is problematic. However, I think its only problematic when people try and apply its metaphysics/idea outside of the Mage cosmology/game line. I used to be one of those "Mage fanboys" that Tatterdemalion King mentioned. However, I kinda got fed up with increasingly worse explanations that Mage tried to supply about the other game lines and basically wrote Mages off as not really knowning much more then any other supernatural in the NWoD. Despite my separation from the Mage line for a while I feel it would be remiss of me to not point out that I've noticed the number of "Changeling Fanboys" is at least equal to the Mage ones. Though they are less obtrusive about their preference by about a hairs breadth.

                                                    Interestingly I always got the impression in CWoD that certain metaphysically empowered beings were more powerful then others. That while the argument between fan's could occur, the writers would pretty much decide the relative powers and abilities and determine the victor as need be for that settings metaplot. For example, the Gehenna book pretty much outright states that Tzimisce is more powerful then Giovanni or Tremere because they are younger antediluvian. Likewise Lilith is more powerful then Caine according to least one Gehenna scenario. As for cross-line interactions in CWoD see the Week of Nightmares stuff. Ravnos stomps a lot of stuff including some very, very powerful characters from other game lines. In the end the Mages kill him using smarts and technomagic.

                                                    It might just be my taste, but I'm fine with that. The idea that all supernaturals have an "equal" slice of the metaphysical pie when it comes to the validity of their arguments about the nature of reality, the reason they exist and all have equal "end games"/metaphysical beings looking over them is a very NWoD idea.

                                                    Before I seem to accusatory of any particular view point (I find the Changeling fanboyism towards True Fae annoying but that's about it) I want to say I agree with the general consensus that any of the "god-tier" supernaturals in the NWoD win in their own realm if they get in a fight there. For me this includes the God Machine (or at least its archangels) in the material universe. I'd also say their could be exceptions to said rules, if your using Mage and want Archmasters to go around being the wild cards that shake up the worlds metaphysics then let them beable to storm other realms to do stuff. That's an ST choice though, same as saying the True Fae can realm hope to have adventures for the sake of not being board. Its oranges and apples at that point.

                                                    Ultimately the answer to this thread has been summed up a half dozen times already:

                                                    1)Demons can beat True Fae in the real world if they've for the stats for it or use their powers cleverly.

                                                    2)Demons can't really "beat" a True Fae in Arcadia but might be able to pull some sort of fast one to at least not get insta-screwed or escape. Maybe...that's up the ST at the end of the day.

                                                    3)Demons can oppose True Fae in the Hedge, but likely want to be baseline a bit more powerful (Primum 6+) if they do so.

                                                    One final point I'd like to raise as a final point to my position on the True Fae issue. I find the True Fae "messy" as metaphysical beings go. They are not defined by anything save their agreements with things/concepts/etc and this should give them a fixed nature to a certain extent. It does not, they flang all over the metaphysical rules that all other creatures of equivalent power in the other game lines have to obey without question.

                                                    Honestly its one of the reason (along with the Mage issues I've already discussed) why I moved away from a completely inclusive model of the NWoD. These days I have each game happen in its own world, with maybe minor crossover occuring (say 2 maybe 3 game lines e.g. Vampire, Demon, Promethean).

                                                    Its also why I think I prefer the Demon and Mage (the earlier bits that make sense) fluff in general, it all structured and ordered because that part of the points of the game. In one a giant machine is running the universe. In the other a bunch of evil mega-mages made the universe this way to trap everyone in it and keep their monopoly on godhood. It all structured and ordered and works on rules and ideas that can at least be passed off for a sort of solid metaphysical theory akin to the ones that you might see in a philosophy textbook or high level physics (one again looking sideways and squinting).

                                                    I guess I find Changelings idea that everything runs off of story's and tropes and deals and contracts and "what fate says" is all a little "soft" and odd for me. It's not no absolutes, it all messy and changes on a case by case basis and such. That's not to my taste, it does not make it bad, but its not to my taste and I guess its really, really annoying in cases where people then drag that messy indistinct narrative based metaphysics into other games that have set mechanical metaphysics.

                                                    Its like a Demon player bringing a maths textbook and says:

                                                    "This is Maths, it provides the basis for human understanding and its principles have been infinitely useful to mankind."

                                                    The Changeling player brings an art textbook and says:

                                                    "This is Art, it enriches our lives and gives us a different perspective on how we see thing. It's helped us grow as a species."

                                                    The Demon player then tries to explain why maths is obviously more important and how useful it is. He shows why maths is important, tires to point out the number of things that require maths at their foundation to even work.

                                                    The Changeling player then tries to explain why Art is so important, He tries to explain what Art has contributed to human development in general and civilization in particular.

                                                    The point is neither is going to win their argument. They are basically using different languages and the things that they are point out as so important as points why their thing is so good carry very little relevance or merit within the other thing/subjects worldview or field of practice. Its literally science vs art.

                                                    I may have other thought this issue a bit...​
