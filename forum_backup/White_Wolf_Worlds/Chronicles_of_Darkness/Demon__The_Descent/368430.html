              As flavorful as meeting a demon at a crossroads is, I don't feel like that particular bit of folklore fits the Unchained. You could easily enough work that into an Infernal demon's...whatever those things are called. Summoning rites? Templates?

              The big problem is that having a reliable way to summon a demon is something you can use to exploit them. Not to mention that by necessity you'd be revealing yourself to a mortal, which is a harsh Compromise by itself. Even if you showed up in demon form only (to keep your Covers secure) I wouldn't risk it, especially with any amount of regularity.

              I don't like being the guy who says "You shouldn't do this", but remember that Demon is a game about Techngnostic Espionage, and including crossroads as a thing would be weird.

              This isn't as relevant, but I also never heard about demons at crossroads specifically until I started watching Supernatural. I don't think it's important to the lore.

                  Since my last post was such a negative Nancy, "Stop Having Fun, Guys" kind of post, I'll make this one constructive. I'd implement this as a Merit of some sort.

                  Crossroad Pact-Making (* to *****+)
                  Unchained are not slaves to the legends of demons any more than they are to the God-Machine itself. However, that doesn't mean that they are incapable or unwilling to exploit some of the tales. In this case, the legends of pacts with demons made at crossroads.
                  Effect: Each dot in this Merit gives an Unchained purview over another specific intersection of at least two roads. The Unchained also describes a common ritual that can be performed at each intersection. When a character performs the ritual at one of the Demon's intersections, the Demon is immediately made aware that someone is trying to contact them. She does not receive any information about the summoner unless she has some sort of remote surveillance at the site, nor does she obtain a way to quickly travel to the intersection. She must drive, fly, or use some sort of Embed or Exploit to get there (or possibly bring the summoner to them, perhaps using the Summon Exploit).
                  The Merit does not provide any sort of protection from Compromise checks made if the demon reveals herself to the summoner. If a pact is made at one of the locations, the Willpower point cost of the pact is reduced by one. The Willpower dot sacrifice for Soul Pacts cannot be eliminated, however.

                  I hope this gives you something like what you're looking for. I figured simply having an associated intersection per crossroad wouldn't be worth a Merit point, so I included the Willpower discount. Thoughts?

                      Actually, the crossroads is one of the very first references to the Unchained (though arguably as it could admittedly fit a denizen of the Inferno also) in the setting, as it appeared on page 75-78 of Midnight Roads. (The Dark Man sounds like a facade to me!) I think this is the primary reason crossroads aren't directly referenced in Demon the Descent, as they're something special you'd come to in a traveling chronicle. (There are a few more potential God-Machine related hooks here too, gremlins and an old man on the lam from something.)

                      That said: They do hint heavily at these things. There are many means with which an Unchained can get around and construct a phoney ritual to summon them. They start an urban legend with the help of their exploits and embeds, make sure it spreads like a meme. Then, using their talents for investigation, they simply track whom might be looking into the legend they've begun, track potentials, because appearing in a gout of flame at the stroke of midnight is an easy feat for one of the Unchained; so long as they know where to be. Bonus: Mortals that believe they're summoning something from the beyond to make a deal allow the demon to make use of their powers more freely and openly before them without risking compromise - after all, they're a demon in the mortal's mind here.

                      As a result, I think the whole crossroads demon might just be a nice story a demon started a while back, nobody remembers who, but everybody loves to take that for a ride and make some easy and dramatic deals.

                      One of my players did this very thing taking a page from Hellraiser. She took a rubik's cube and turned it into 'The Nightmare Box'. A gadget which allowed her to facilitate her demonic pacts in a very deliberate and occult way. As an exploited gadget it took on an arcane style and when it was solved, it became increasingly complicated and impossible and demanded to be solved again and again until, finally, the person completes it and opens it. When they do this, they were inflicted with stigmata, but only for a short period. The powers, merits and second-sight into the God-Machine that came with this stigmata would evaporate after 3 days. Since she always took the cube from the claimant after making the deal and always used Tag and Release to keep track of it, she had an easy way to appear. She'd follow the device wherever it might go in her spare hours and when she saw someone had solved it - largely because they began to see how the world really was - she'd scoop them up and announce herself as some otherworldly, hellish entity that would reward them with a bargain. Sometimes she was a god, an alien, or even just a demon.

                      Similarly, I believe, a demon might be able to create a "ritual of the crossroads". Get a cover working a bar or diner on a crossroads and use Special Message on several of the songs that play over the radio. Have the message trigger on criteria such as "a below average guitar player" and have the message promise "their heart's desire given in a devil's deal". Then simply watch the crossroads at midnight and use some other trick to make for a heart stopping appearance when one of the fish bites. Using other embeds, like Earworm.

                      Or do the whole thing the easy way with Urban Legend from Flowers of Hell. It's an exploit that literally gives you control over a story like that, making the principal characters facades you can use when complete, in this case the "Crossroads Demon" (or 'The Dark Man' perhaps?) would become a facade you can wear when someone actively pursues the story. If you lie in wait and use Teleport or use Urban Fluidity, you can get there quick and in a blur or instant; 'summoned'.
