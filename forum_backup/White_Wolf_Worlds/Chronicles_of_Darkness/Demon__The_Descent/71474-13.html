    Originally posted by Charles Red View Post
    That just means they understand cause and effect.
    I am going to go ahead and forward the response of "no":
    Although a demon’s physical nature is dualistic — partly divine, partly mundane — their mental and emotional outlook resembles that of a human. It’s true that demons have extraordinary pasts and think in alien ways that defy supernatural manipulation, but they tend to come to fairly mundane human conclusions. If someone a demon cares about is in danger, he experiences fear. He feels it in his gut, in his throat, in his chest, just like an ordinary human being.
    A demon’s essentially human perspective is reinforced by her human experience of the world. Demons are more sensitive to certain supernatural emanations, but they generally see and hear the world in human ways.
    No matter how alien a demon might be when she first Falls, years of thinking about human things, experiencing the world in human ways, and feeling human emotions has a humanizing effect on them. This doesn’t mean that demons are universally good people — there’s a big difference between “human” and “humane” — but when demons are monsters, they are monsters in a human way.

    Resident Lore-Hound

                                I do think DTD does overlap with MAGE/Atlantis problem. Both have the fixed origin problem that sets the game in stone. All angels/demons come from the god machine, the end. No debate about that, no different possible origins, that is it. I prefer that games leave origins vague and not set in stone with only multiple possible explanations theorized, but nothing guaranteed. While the details may be debatable, the end result does not change that demons from DTD were created by the god machine and that's that.

                                The scifi elements do hurt aesthetically for those who want a more traditional game. All I can see is mechanical robots shaped like a demon, or something that comes across as a cyber-demon that is half organic and half machine. This is hard to ignore when it is described and reinforced again and again in the book. Also, the espionage/spy angle seems kind of weird and not the first thing when I think of a generic demon purpose. It is not bad, just weird and kind of limiting on the type of gameplay potential demon could have. I think a little more horror route would have been better.

                                On a side note and minor peave of mine. Why does every game in Nwod have to tie humanity outlook/motivations into the characters? Usually with the goal to become human, or prevent yourself from losing your humanity. While ok occasionally, it gets old when its every single game. Obviously hunter and mage are humans so they get a free pass, but vampires? They are monsters, they should act it and not cry about it. Someone always say "Humans are the worst monsters" or something like that, that is BS and bugs me to end. Have anybody actually look at the scary stuff that nature has created? The only reason we seem scary is because we have human intelligence and can do horrible things with it. However, give some of the real monsters in nature human level intelligence and we would learn quickly what an intelligent monster with alien motivations can make human monsters seem like misguided children. I only brought this up because we were getting on demons with human motivations and such. I think most of the game lines should be flexible, some characters might want to understand/find humanity, while others have no interest and embrace the creature they are with totally alien outlook on life. It just bugs me that if you choose the later your considered doing it wrong by the books interpretation and often get some kind of punishment because of it. Like I said, minor pet peeve since we were on the subject.

                                Like I said earlier, I think it would have been better to go this route. I would have the GM still be the biggest antagonist, but not the only one. I would have demons be different species and not just ones created by the god machine. The god machine would have created the DTD version, however the other demons were pulled from other places by the GM and altered mentally, phisically, or both. Most have a lot of their memories erased so their origins are vauge have remembered, but one thing they do remember. They fell (escaped) the god machine. So, some of the type of demons:

                                DTD Demons (the created)
                                Inferno Demons (sins)
                                Mage Empyrean (fallen angles)
                                Lovecraftian beings (The twisted)
                                Other Religions (The lost ones)


                                I would say that the GM did something to these demons so their memories of their origins is hazy and it prevents them from returning to their home (wherever that might be). The only ones sure of their origin are the DTD demons. Some remember escaping, others remember the GM just letting them go, others could remember being broken out, however they all escaped and are all hunted by the GM, its agents and other things. All the demons should have some basic questions. Why where they captured in the first place? What was the GM going to do? Can they get home? As for origins, each demon has its own theory, the created would claim the GM created everything, the sins might claim they come from a place of pain and blood where they existed long before anything called itself a god, the fallen angels might say they come from the true heaven where light shines and the true god exists, while the twisted might tell of a strange dimension where the laws of physics are vastly different and ancient alien creatures from the end of time are slowly remaking the universe and things the GM is just a smaller lesser of these creatures like an amoeba compared to them, the lost ones speak of multitudes of gods and goddesses they have seen, and others whisper that none of them are right and they were all created by the GM for some weird experiment and all their memories are false.

                                As for the GM angels, I would have some call them angles, while others vehemently opposes calling them that and instead call them servants of the imposter god (or machine). As I said, the GM would still be the lead antagonist, but now we have a wide selection of backgrounds to pull antagonists from. We can have other religions hunting their for their enemy/ally, they might have beings from other dimensions infiltrating the world studying the world and the GM, they might have more traditional angels come to clense the world, or we might have other native traditional demons fighting those contaminated by the GM, and finally we have the original DTD angels/GM playing havoc with everyone.

                                As for motivation, this gives way more freedom. Some might go after the GM like in the DTD book, while others wash their hands of it and seek to find a way home through learning about the world, others see a fresh untouched paradise of humanity they can start their own little empire of and the flip side is some might want to save humanity from they hell they created.

                                Of course, this is just a quick general idea of how I would have done it. You could still play the game exactly the same as it is now, but now nothing is really set in stone and you have more variety of beings to choose from and not just the DTD cyber demons.

                                It is late and running on fumes, I am probably not writing completely clearly and will most likely wince tomorrow when I see this after I wake up. :/

                                                    God "creates" angels and then proceeds to on occasion frankenstein them into completely different configurations, wipe their memories, reprogram them, and generally treat them as tools in need of repurposing.

                                                    This is the same entity that is capable of summoning various different sorts of beings from other dimensions and prefers to piggyback on existing work when it can.                                                     Arcadia may or may not be dozens upon hundreds of places mashed together into metaphors for themselves, and yet I have not seen any complaints to the effect that changelings are limited by "com[ing] from [Arcadia], the end."

                                                    So far the only major gameline in the nWoD whose splat has an absolute fixed origin point (as we understand it) is Mummy, whose entire setup rather necessitates a historical starting point to have cults to undying god-servants stretching back to the diaspora of an ancient vanished empire. Everything else is crossbred or naturalized or exists in a context in which its genesis has been mythologized, propagandized, obfuscated, or otherwise rendered cosmologically distant and impossible to conclusively gauge.

                                                    Demons have no idea where they come from. They just know that the Machine made them the way they were before they Fell.
