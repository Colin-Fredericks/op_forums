        One thing I will say, which may not be reflected in how people talk about Mage, is that a single Mage can be killed by an unsuspecting bullet to the head.

        So actually being trained for mundane combat and knowing what to do (in terms of basic first aid, situational awareness, and tactical things) is always useful. Mages tend to have a blind spot for doing thing with magic which they could do mundanely - which is often also the Wiser option (and I think that should be used as a weakness for various NPC antagonists). And in particular, when you attack a mage, the very worst time to pick is when they are out in public - in these cases mundane responces can be idealer...

        There are entities which have very high withstands where attacking them with an Unmaking spell (for example) may be next to impossible, and slowly wearing down their health levels may be necessary. Higher rank ephemeral entities for example (including Abyssal entities) and I think they would fall under the Arrow purview to deal with... In longer term conflicts, you have to consider resources. In a single fight you might spend 1 mana per Spell (to deal aggravated damage) or cast one spell to give yourself the ability to deal aggravated damage by hand (maybe making your weapons the entities bane with Prime). Then you can save a considerable amount of mana by being able to hit things real good.

        Not sure this is a complete answer, as it really doesn't imagine the Arrow complexly, it doesn't delve into their history of philosophy (nor at the darker mirror that is the Seer Ministry of the Praetorians).

        But it does touch on three things which combat conversations often ignore, is Wisdom, Withstand, and Mana costs.

                                Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post
                                Im not complaining about combat, only confused on how the martial Arrow could exist when combat seems so trivial/who throws a spell first.
                                The Arrow's magical praxis is "Challenge is Magical" and the full breadth of possibility is not the actual scope of most conflict — see also Werewolf, where Acrozatarim shared a playtest example of a pack of werewolves ganging up and killing their Pure opposition's Gauru specialist and getting their Irraka killed in turn in what was ultimately intended as a much lower-stakes conflict. The Duel Arcane exists as a practice because chucking lethal spells at your opposition to make a point is a good way to escalate a conflict beyond repair. You fight in a way that isn't guaranteed to annihilate the other guy so that they match that energy or are at least open to negotiations (or underestimating you enough to mess around).

                                Also, more to the point, there is a clear and obvious reason why wizards whose magic can go sideways or damage their ability to manage consequences would want to have well-honed options at their disposal that do not go away the minute countermeasures against specific or general forms of magic come into play.

                                Assuming most mages in the world are running on the typical Supernatural Tolerance range of 3-5 and approaching spellcasting with an eye toward the pressures of safe Reach, Mana costs, Arcana limits, and Wisdom (plus the need to actively develop or learn Rotes, Praxes, Merits, etc), and spreading their dots around at least a little bit to have more options available to them, unless they are actively specializing their magical persona toward being able to murder people they are going to be rather limited in the harm they can do without personal cost or consequence, especially in conflicts that they did not prepare for. Most mages cannot become more than an Adept in their non-Ruling Arcana without being tutored by a Master in those Arcana and need to spend regular Experiences in greater quantities and without the crutch of Arcane Experiences; without Rotes they are therefore limited in the free Reach available to them, which makes casting safely more difficult.

                                Turning people into lizards is bad for your Wisdom unless you're already very unWise, and the same goes for killing them on short notice. Inuring spells for murder is an opportunity cost that also requires you to lose Wisdom casting that spell in the first place. Rotes don't exist in a vacuum and developing Praxes from non-Ruling Arcana still requires you to put resources toward raising those ratings that could be spent on other things. Without a good argument to make about why you did it, other mages are likely to have opinions on your character actually putting into practice theoretically heinous applications of the Arcana.

                                The advice in Nameless and Accursed exists because the full breadth of reality-warping cosmic power nominally enables conflicts to be trivialized and it is therefore important to ground those possibilities in the world they would be dropped into. The Arrow engages in conflicts whose scope can extend below that of torture, murder, and dehumanization.

                                            Originally posted by Madmarcus View Post
                                            Can someone summarize the differences between 1st and 2nd edition mage sight rules? I'm slightly used to 2nd edition but have reason to want to know about 1e. Is the difference mostly that since it is a spell in 1e a mage has no magical sense of any kind unless they are running the spell?
                                            In both editions, Awakening provides enough knowledge of the Arcana to activate Mage Sight for at least your Ruling Arcana; 1e just makes the equivalent of Active Mage Sight and Focused Mage Sight a spell instead of a pseudo-Attainment with Arcana prerequisites.

                                            1e uses Intelligence + Occult instead of Gnosis + Arcanum for rolls to scrutinize resonance while a Mage Sight spell is active, gives a minor dice modifier based on the physical/metaphysical density of the subject, and treats it as a conventional extended action roll with gleaning information on various qualities adding to the required total instead of having some or all information gated behind different levels of Opacity.

                                            Peripheral Mage Sight is modeled in 1e as the equivalent of having the Unseen Sense Merit for every type of supernatural phenomenon, which functions as a cue to cast an Unveiling spell just as the Periphery it does for turning on Active Mage Sight in 2e.
