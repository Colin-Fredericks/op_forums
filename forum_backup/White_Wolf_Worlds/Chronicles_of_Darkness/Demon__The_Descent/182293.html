    "Exorcise a Demon"- or more exactly, freeing a Cover

    06-27-2014, 06:51 PM
    so, something random I thought about while I took a shower..

    In order to get a control over a Cover, a demon must make a pact, preferably, a Soul Pact. Doing so, would allow the Demon to take the person wholly, with the Soul just being an "anchor" for the deal- the demon doesn't need it after the Pact is complete and even throws it away afterward, but without it the Pact is meaningless (I remember that if the one bound to the demon lose his Soul, the demon can't take over the Cover until he finds his lost one/ gets a new one).

    Now, assuming that the way demons see a "soul" is the same way the GMC and Mage interpreted it (and not the way games like Geist do), could it mean that if, one way or another, someone takes a soul and stuff it back into a Cover, would the demon's hold over the Cover would be broken, and he'll be forced out of it? And now, with a new soul, the demon would have to try and bind the Pact again if he would like to take the Cover back? (sure, there aren't many things outside of mages and demons which can manipulate souls that way, but still).

    your thoughts?

                                      The way I read it is...it's not necessarily just the soul that you're taking out. The soul only does so much in a character; it's not the seat of your intelligence, your decision-making, etc. There's something beyond the soul, which I'm going to call the self, that exists separate from the soul. Even a thrall isn't a fully catatonic meat sack - there's still -something- in there behind the wheel driving him or her to do what they do.

                                      When you call in a soul pact, you're essentially shearing not only that soul out, but all that self, that driving force as well. Once all that's removed, who they are doesn't exist, only who they are in relation to other people and things. The demon takes this fully formed suit of contexts and identifying markers and wears it as it's new skin, hopefully fitting in well enough to cause minimal fuss to the relationships the cover has, thus minimizing damage to it.

                                      I'll reiterate that again, because it's an important thing to note: the entirety of what's left of the person is it's physical identifying characteristics - it's appearance, it's voice, etc - and the context it holds with other people and things. You didn't just replace the engine on the car, you somehow removed the paintjob, the tags and the VIN and slapped it all on another chassis. And if that paintjob doesn't fit with the chassis or someone notices that the VIN turns up something different in the registry...well, that's what compromise is.

                                      Edit: I mean, obviously do what you want with your games, YMMV and all that, I'm just trying to lay out the text as I understand it as a baseline.
