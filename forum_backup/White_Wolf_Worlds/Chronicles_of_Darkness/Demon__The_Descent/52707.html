    Raise Dead Questions

    01-18-2014, 04:16 PM
    So, I've got some stuff with Raise Dead that's come up... wondering if I could ask for thoughts or insights!

    Do you think the -1 per day penalty for Raise Dead is implied to come from body decay, or other reasons?

    On that topic, do you imagine that body preservation... either natural (cryogenic) or supernatural (some sorta weird power)... could negate the penalty?

    Could restoring the physical integrity of the body through other means lend 'successes' to such an effort, you think?

    Or alternately, do you imagine the -1 per day is due to some more intangible quality, like 'soul distance'?

    In such a case, could the service of a powerful being who could somehow call such a soul closer mitigate this penalty?

    Does doing the Raise Dead ritual in a bolthole prevent the difficulty from growing by one per day, as it does normally?

    Does storing bodies in a bolthole keep them in 'stasis', not affecting the difficulty?

    Thanks!

      I think the penalty is mostly because of the decay of the body, with probably a small metaphysical component (the strength of the bond between body and soul).

      Preserving the body could make it easier to resurrect someone, but most preservation methods damage the body, making it even harder to raise the dead.

      As far as I can see Raise Dead is the prime body restoration power, so I wouldn't really allow other powers lending successes (unless you get into the territory of really esoteric powers). Even the Resurrection Numen has the -1 penalty per day, so it seems that equivalent powers also don't help.

      As an alternative, if your players really want to bring Marco Singe back (as you said in your other thread) you could have your players discover a new exploit that enables his ghost to posess a new body completely (living or dead) or that lets him become a Revenant (undead). This way you don't have the problems with the resurrection with a five year old dead body. The only problem is that the players would have to go to the underworld to find his ghost (or if he still lingers, find where he is or if he still lingers), with possible opposition of the God Machine.

              I generally go with that the -1 is from the cumulative "momentum" the death has in the timeline. If you find a way to step around this (perhaps accessing a splinter in which the person never died, bringing the corpse with you and doing it there, perhaps swapping the alive/dead status between the two copies) then the penalties go out the window, or at least get decreased.
              As for ressing someone who has been dead for ages, I'd probably say that's more or less impossible for an angel using anything less than the result of a cipher. (do note though, there are other ways to ress someone than using these things, summoning the ghost from the underworld and shoving them into a living, or dead, human is a good example, and while demons can't do that they could easily enlist others, mages, vampires, werewolves, to do it for them in exchange for secrets or resources)

                                                      The primary problem with raising the dead over long periods isn't really the insane amount of successes needed, but whether or not the soul is even still available (unless you're putting in a new one).

                                                      That being said, the extra necessary successes is at least in part related to body decomposition, because you get extra successes for embalmed bodies. If your players are going to the extreme of setting up someone with Swift Resolution so that they can use Raise Dead on someone who died a year ago, and they actually get the 370 or so successes -and- are willing to deal with the -15 to a Compromise roll that will probably spawn an Angel up their backside after they focus for two weeks? I don't see a reason to think too long over it. Raising the Dead isn't as consequential as people tend to think it is, in my experience.
