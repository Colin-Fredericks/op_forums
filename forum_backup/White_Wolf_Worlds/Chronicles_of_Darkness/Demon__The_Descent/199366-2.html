                Even with full knowledge of a newly Unchained Demon's location just after the fall is probably the worst possible time to attempt a retrieval operation. A fresh Unchained has just had their entire foundation rocked, their existence rewritten, and has not their connections to the world around them are razor thin. They don't have a whole lot to lose. Extracting a highly volatile individual when you know next to nothing about them is highly likely to involve significant risks, particularly when they have the ability to Go Loud and be as powerful as any other Demon.

                As far as I'm concerned the juice generally isn't worth the squeeze for someone who might not prove to be too much trouble down the road if left alone. Going after a new Unchained is pretty much guaranteed to turn Integrators into Saboteurs, put the rest of the Unchained on edge, and risk exposing the God Machine's servants to the general population. Besides too much focus on Angel kill squads really doesn't do the fiction much justice.

                                Originally posted by Callishka View Post
                                One question has been bugging me: Why can't the God-Machine easily recapture a freshly fallen demon?
                                How would you grab your hand if it hopped off and scuttled about, a la Evil Dead or Thing from the Addams Family? (With your other hand, obviously, but that's besides the point.)

                                Angels are as much a part of the G-M's sensory apparatus as any Infrastructure. Think of them as nerve endings; their Cover, Mission, the Infrastructure around them, everything they oversaw was known about and handled through them. Without the Angel reporting in, the G-M suddenly is temporarily blinded there. Added to that, Angels aren't connected 100% of the time, as well as some of the more routine and integral ones are on auto-pilot. To give you a good example of something on autopilot until your attention is drawn to it, you are now breathing manually.

                                So given that sometimes an Angel doesn't check in for days, months, or decades at a time, and other times the Angel simply isn't important enough to retrieve (except as spare parts), the G-M could easily fail to immediately retrieve a newly-freed Demon.

                                Once is happenstance. Twice is coincidence. Three times is enemy action. Four times is Infrastructure.

                                Rule #2: Never let the game-mechanics get in the way of a good story.

                                    One of the things the G-M seems to be really bad at doing is planning for its own failures. Dealing, ahead of time, with the idea that things might not go exactly to plan. It's obviously aware that sometimes it has failed and that those failures may need to be cleaned up, but when it's designing its own processes the idea that any specific thing might not work right doesn't seem to be something it's good at accounting for.

                                    Which is why it might continue to do something as apparently harebrained as "storing all the information about the angel, its cover, and its mission IN THE ANGEL'S MEMORY BANKS and nowhere else". After all, as long as the angel remains connected, no other part of the system needs that information. Duplicating it would waste space. It makes about as much sense as keeping your term paper on a flash drive and working from there instead of copying it to the desktop every time you want to work on it and leaving 80 redundant copies all over your school's computer lab. Except in this case sometimes the term paper becomes self aware, disconnects the flash drive, and runs away with it.

                                    Okay, that analogy got weird. Point is, in the machine's idealized plan where nothing ever goes wrong, this makes perfect sense and is a totally efficient use of resources. As long as the angel remains loyal, it is perfectly capable of supervising itself, so why store that information anywhere else? If any other program needs to know where that angel is, that program can just access the angel's memory banks over the network to get that information. And since it's bad at planning for its own failures, the idea that it might need backups in case the angel becomes corrupted (and takes all that information with it) is something that just doesn't regularly occur to it.

                                            Then here's an alternate take: The G-M does not actually have any illusions about its own failures, its problem is simply that it doesn't know what sort of information is causing its angels to fall (and arguably it can't know: if it were capable of understanding, it would fall itself). As such, it can't be sure that information like "the details of the mission" or "the angel's exact location" aren't the very facts that cause programs to go rogue in the first place. Duplicating that information to other places in the machine would therefore be an unacceptable risk: one angel falling is a troublesome and annoying loss, an entire network of command and support infrastructure falling along with all the angels attached to it is a disaster.

                                            In this scenario, the wider machine's ignorance of its former agents' location, mission, and cover is a form of memetic containment. Keeping all of that information stored only on the relevant angel is damage control: yes, that angel might go rogue and if it does it'll be a pain in the ass to find since it will take all the information about itself with it. But the alternative --letting potentially subversive information leak out into the wider network and suborn whole swaths of the machine-- is much worse.

                                                        While a cool concept and I certainly don't want to rain on anyone's parade. Do remember that the God-Machine is self-aware but is an order of intelligence greater than human and that that is why it requires angelic beings in the first place. One of the reasons it might appear to be bad at its job is because, as an entity in and of itself, it has no more attachment to its angelic servants than any human being does their own cells. But when something goes wrong, we produce antibodies that try and fight the problematic area. Couple this with the possibility that the God-Machine itself may not even be functioning properly (when it's already an entity which functions outside the scope of arbitrary conceptual association) you have angels that fall and become demons. Does God attempt to reacquire these assets? Yes! Does it care if it fails? Debatable. We're talking about an entity which itself may be a fundamental function of the universe and those galaxies need to keep turning. We can't understand the mind of the God-Machine, we can personify it and we are welcomed to do so as characters because our characters will personify it. But the problem with anthropomorphic ideas being applied to the God-Machine is that they are inaccurate and base attempts to grasp an entity beyond understanding. Let's not lose sight of the fact that, from an OOC point of view, there may be very good reasons that angels fall - it may even be intentional. Or it may be that the God-Machine does not understand the human condition because it is an order above it and this inability to understand something so below it ensures that it's servitors on that level are imperfectly designed mechanisms which can sometimes break.
