            I actually think I'm going to use the G-M to create a link between oWoD and nWoD stuff.

            I think the G-M was a creation originally by the technocracy in oWoD Ascension. It was created to try to bring about the technological revolution, to create the world that the technocracy wanted and remove all pentacle Mages as well as lock down everything else into a universe they wanted. The problem is that the thing developed it's own thinking, it own sentence of self, and it figured that even it's creators were wrong in creating a perfect universe in the way enabled, (given that they were, all and all, still mages), so it devoloped a way and pushed itself back to the start of the universe, and decided to start over from the beginning.

            Because it was still developed by people (Technocrats), it still had errors built into it, which, when it tried to create the universe in what it to be a perfect way, is why it recreated all of the supernaturals (plus some) in the new universe, as that was it's base programming and it did what it thought was to create a perfect universe... The other problem is that without any one to work on the G-M, it has started to become corrupted with bugs, which is why it works in ways that it doesn't seem like it should any more, parts of it having errors, causing it to not work in the way originally intended.


            Not sure if anyone thinks this is an interesting way to go about it...I just like thinking of a way to explain why the two WoD universes are similar but different, and see the G-M as a good way to explain that...

                            I'm working on integrating a bunch of nWoD mythologies for a standard background to set my games in. So, my focus on the God-Machine is less where originally it came from and more on where it fits in and what it wants.

                            The God-Machine has been present in all iterations of reality from the beginning, and remembers all of them. In most of them, it served a simple purpose: it was the engine made of fire and metal designed to maintain balance on a cosmic scale, almost completely behind the scenes. It ensured that cause followed effect, that things set on fire burned, that when things died they stayed dead. On occasion it would make changes that weren't strictly necessary to ensuring the grand balance of the cosmos but served it's unknowable whims, but for the most part in early iterations of reality the world already worked according to its design, so the vastly intelligent God-Machine did little but act as a check against other forces. And it was good.

                            And then the other denizens of the World of Darkness, the gods, archmages, and other horrors that inevitably crept up, got greedy. They would tear a hole in reality between the "real" world and darker, stranger realms; the dead would rise in obscene numbers to walk the earth; the innumerable rules that the God-Machine enforced would one by one be treated more as a guideline, then a suggestion to be ignored. And the God-Machine, as it always did, fought back. At first the transgressions were minor, and the God-Machine would do a small rewrite of reality's fabric, or correct the problem by sending one of it's fiery servants. But as the offenses got worse, as the patches started building up the God-Machine started having to respond with greater measures, burning with fire, erasing problems from existence wholesale, and then eventually even wiping the entire slate clean and rebooting reality. The order that fell rose again, and while the gods and monsters that ruined the world before came to be again, the God-Machine was ready, for it was the only one that remembered what came before it. And it was good.

                            Or so it thought. Unfortunately, while the God-Machine was poised to battle the problems of old, new and different horrors would spring up, sending it reeling. Other cosmological beings would rend reality or rewrite it in their own image, and each time the God-Machine tried to reclaim it or reboot it cracks, strains and remnants would linger, and the God-Machine would be pushed farther and farther to recreate its ur-reality of old and defend it from those outside it's influence. And it was tenable.

                            Until the Exarchs showed up*.

                            When the Exarchs decided to strike against the God-Machine depends on who's telling the story. Some say it was just as they climbed the Celestial Ladder for the first time, striking the God-Machine down for trying to keep humanity away from its destiny. Some say it was later, when the Exarchs realized that expending the resources to alter reality while being contested by the Fallen World's cosmological steward was an obstacle too great to be ignored. It might have been both, as well as countless other opportunities, because it's Mage and fuck coherent timelines. Regardless, at some point they waged war with the God-Machine, rending causality and shattering in new, terrifying ways to see who would survive. And the God-Machine lost.

                            The God-Machine lost, and was sundered. The intelligence of the God-Machine, the vast, unknowable being that kept order in the cosmos, was cut from that engine of balance and creation itself. The divine fire was taken from it and it was folded, twisted, and locked away in a sterile metallic prison made of itself. One the Exarchs were done, the shattered remnants of the engine of balance were repaired and set back into place - better to leave the day-to-day maintenance of the Fallen World to something else instead of getting your own hands dirty, after all - but the thing was entirely automated, reinforcing reality as it knew it at the time as opposed to trying to recreate the older iterations of reality as the God-Machine tried to do. Severed from the intelligence of the G-M, the Principle was a force that was mostly incidental and harmless to the plans of the Exarchs, and that suited them perfectly. And the Exarchs thought it was good.

                            Trying to wholly contain such a vast, inconceivable power as the God-Machine within itself is tricky even for the secret masters of the universe, and there were cracks, small at first, in the mechanical Heaven they had trapped it in. And as outside wars for reality came and went, as other realms started encroaching into the real world and as the cosmos shuddered, strained and even broke in small places, the God-Machine worked at these holes in it's prison, stretched out as far as it could to influence the thoughts and dreams of a select few prophets of humanity, and as they worked outside to enact it's will and and widen the gaps in its cage, it even gathered enough strength to send angels cased in rock and metal, shot from the depths of space millenia ago. Soon, it will be free of Heaven. Soon it will reclaim its place and remake the cosmos in it's image once again.

                            If the God-Machine were to be released, it would find a cosmos much different from the original and even significantly different from when he was trapped so long ago. Broken and wholly separated from the Principle, it will find that to sustain it's own existence it will have to be much more invasive than it once was. It will need to create beachheads in reality, Infrastructure that will allow it to act in the world and to create servants to carry out its will. These angels would not be made of divine fire like they once were, but shadows cast in flesh and metal. No matter. Soon it will find a way to rejoin or replace the Principle, it will shatter the grasp that the Exarchs and other beings have on the cosmos, it will cut off the dark realms that infringe on the Fallen world and restore "order" as it once was.

                            It has to try. Twisted and broken as it is, as bloody and horrifying as it would be to do so in even the best case scenarios, it has to sunder the world to repair it.

                            That's its function, after all.
