    [Question] On installing gadgets.

    06-21-2014, 08:56 AM
    My players noticed something a little bit off about creating gadgets. In the Demon: The Descent book it states that, "... the creator must expend a dot of Willpower to cement the gadget into reality. Should he fail to do so, the Installed power will wither away, vanishing by the end of the scene." This would be simple enough if not for the fact that on the later pages under pacts, that a Soul Pact requires a willpower dot expenditure also. However the book goes into far greater depth with the concept of spending a willpower dot here.

    Another problem is that the creation of a gadget costing a willpower dot doesn't seem to differentiate between Embedded or Exploited gadgets. RAW it seems to suggest that all gadgets cost the expenditure of a willpower dot (which the player will then have to restore with 1 experience). This seems awkward when you're getting a lesser form of an ability that you already possess. Granted it is now portable and reliable (and in the case of an Exploited Gadget it is no longer a compromise roll) but that only seems to be a "good deal" with Exploited gadgets, and very hit-and-miss with Embedded gadgets (as they offer very little risk in their use already).

    Any thoughts? At current I've house-ruled that it was the expenditure of a willpower point. But... I'm not even sure I am entirely comfortable with that (as the only gadget that has been made was Exploited and I believe that might be worth the final cost of 1 willpower = 1 experience.)

                      Well, embedded gadgets do have some advantages over exploit gadgets:

                      Permanent without the required maintenance that exploit gadgets require.

                      Retain their original looks, so they're stealthier. A supergun with merciless gunman looks normal, a demonic flamethrower with hellfire not so much.

                                                          The god-machine is more than capable of giving out unseen senses, so of course it can give out demon hunting cultists unseen senses-demons.

                                                          As for rule interpretation, "You have a sixth sense for a type of supernatural creature, chosen when you buy the merit," seems pretty damn clear to me.

                                                          Which means that if you have unseen senses vampire, you can sense vampire, it doesn't help you sense ghouls. If you have unseen senses werewolves, then it doesn't help you sense wolfblooded or spirits.

                                                          So by the rules of the game, unseen senses-godmachine lets you sense the godmachine. If there are more than one godmachines running around, the merit would let them sense those as well, but demons, cryptids, or angels aren't god-machines.

                                                          Now there is nothing in the rules against taking the merit for more broad categories. Unseen senses-faeries could probably pick up true fae, changelings, and even hobgoblins, so a character with unseen senses-Things of the God-Machine would pick up angels as well as infrastructure, maybe even cryptids and demons as well, but unseen senses-god-machine and unseen senses-Things of the god-machine are two different things.
