            I would argue that Urfarah wasn't a pack predator. He hunted alone for Milennia before the First Pack formed, and from Sundered Workd's description of the First Pack, it sounds more like they followed in his wake rather than actually hunting with him.

            I don't think he was ever really the symbol of wolves, but rather that of predation. As long as there have been predators, there has been Urfarah (or perhaps, as long as there has been Urfarah, there have been predators). What form those predators have taken has changed over the centuries, and he eventually had children that took after the apex predators of their time, wolves. But that doesn't mean he has to have actually been a symbol of wolves himself.

            Not many people, including werewolves, got to see him and lived, and those that did usually only saw brief flashes as he ran through the Hunter's Paradise in pursuit of his prey. But if the description of Fox is any indication, he can't have looked that much like an actual wolf, and it's entirely possible that his association with wolves was a result of some combination of not having a better point of comparison and an assumption that if his children were wolves, he must have been a wolf as well. By modern times the story has gotten so distorted that the Uratha remember him as a spirit, so that's definitely not a reliable indicator. We really don't have much hard evidence that he was actually a wolf at all.

                                                You guys are going to want to break yourselves of this habit, quick. The Chronicles of Darkness books are written with the assumption that the players are playing in good faith, can interpret natural language, and are invested in cooperative storytelling. Technical readings with intent towards gaining a competitive edge are antithetical to the buy-in of the system.

                                                Originally posted by Grimbli View Post
                                                We're playing a mixed game and if we can't effectively neutralize the Uratha then we could all die when he rages.
                                                As I hinted at earlier, you're going to have balance issues in a mixed-template party. The game lines may be compatible, but they are designed as individual games - supbsplats within a template are more or less balanced against each other, but templates are not created equal. An analogy I've seen used before is that even though elves and dragons use the same rules in D&D doesn't mean they work well in the same party.

                                                That's not to say you can't do a crossover group, but you should know that it is not going to be balanced in the way that, say, a D&D party is balanced. You need to create your own "balance" by finding narrative niches each character fits into. Your werewolf can be the combat guy, or the scout/tracker, or the spirit medium, or all of the above, really. If you've got a vampire, they can be the face guy, the social networker, the mind controller, or all of the above. You might want to consider banning Mages, because while their primary niche is the investigator, they are so versatile they can easily step on everyone else's niche. Playing a Mage in a mixed template group requires a lot of discipline to avoid spotlight-hogging and let other players shine, even when you have a spell that could totally work in this situation.

                                                            Yes, weapon rating plus successes. Your werewolf rolls 9 dice, any successes grant an extra 2 Lethal.

                                                            In gauru form an opponent only goes by their Dexterity or Wits to determine their Defense, the usual skill like Athletics doesn't count. But keep in mind a werewolf in gauru has a great advantage to grappling. And if you can hit a target, you can initiate a grapple. To initiate a grapple you need to get over the target's Defense, but once in a grapple it comes down to contesting Strength + Brawl vs Strength + Brawl.

                                                            That's not counting the various Gifts and fetishes that may be available, and often become free and automatic during death rage, or the fact that other wolves can be triggered by the presence of a werewolf in death rage, and each character attacking a target in a round lowers that target's Defense by 1. Or Merits that grant Armor Piercing or increased damage.

                                                            But if you're dealing with a single werewolf who isn't inclined towards combat, and a mage who is very inclined towards avoiding it, that mage is going to still be good at avoiding combat. Though when you're taking Lethal damage, with armor you always take at least 1 Bashing damage.

                                                            If you offered more details, like the specifics of the mage's Defense, the werewolf's abilities, or the source of this armor, we might be able to point out some other things you've missed.

                                                            Incidentally, providing your werewolf is at a level of Harmony that allows for reflexive shapeshifting, a good maneuver to start with against humanoid opponents is to open up in urshul with an attack that causes Leg Wrack to lower Defense, or Arm Wrack if they have a dangerous weapon, Knocked Down only if they seem like they can run or your pack is with you. Then shift into gauru. Against dangerous opponents, I strongly suggest one eats the flesh, causing Aggravated damage whenever possible. But that doesn't fit the personality of all player characters.
