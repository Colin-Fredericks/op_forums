      I'm not done with it yet but my biggest question is still not answered yet. Has anyone else seen any mention of it in their reading of the book?

      Unease Sybaris. Being within [Sekhem] miles of a Mummy or their Tomb for 24 hours+ causes Unease Sybaris. The level of Unease is Memory minus Sekhem.

      Several of these Mummies (especially the Khent-henu) have low Memory traits, even a rating 1!

      Because of the Heka flows in D.C. Descents are slower.

      When you wake up you can last at Sekhem 10 for 2+ days. that's more than 24 hours for those who are counting...

      With so many Mummies in close proximity, shouldn't the whole city be in near constant Unease Sybaris as someone low memory wakes up, floods the place with Madness of Duat for weeks on end (weeks equal to Sekhem at the time the Unease started) for miles and miles? is there something that explains how this Sybaris affects the city? If a place if afflicted with Sybaris like that for potentially 2 hundred of year you'd expect there to be...something about dealing with it?

      it;s one fo the things I personally find hard to reconcile with the concept of Nomes and a society that stays in the same place for extended periods in large numbers. Madness for a few weeks every decade if there is one local Mummy can be explained away. The affects of that with many times the frequency? The affects of the Guildmaster ranked Afinities buffing the ability to make money and the option to trade Physical or Morality traits for wealth within miles of the Guildmasters is dealt with by the text so I know the authors are paying attention to these kinds of things so I'm wondering if there's somewhere in the book that deals with the Sybaris issue?

                                  Talismans...I may have to go back and reread their section in Guildhalls but the Talismanic Record never felt like a huge thing in that book, I vaguely remember it only being mentioned in one instance and no real discussion of how important they were or what they actually let people do. Though they then do start to erode the importance of actual Memory...if you can run a project as complicated and rapidly evolving as building a Geomantic City through a Talisman then you'd need some serious reason to need to know more about your past.
                                  I think its fair to say that Mummy has some elements that in the Core did cause some issues.

                                  The Memory aspect of the game seemed to screw over the larger idea of Guild Structure and Status for example. Not to mention the idea of the Guilds being long-lasting World-spanning organizations when Mummy's themselves are very rare supernatural creatures. Likewise, Unease Sybaris seemed needed to explain how Mummy's build cults so easily but at the same time had...problematic implications for the idea of Nomes. Cults were treated very much as a resource or a merit block, rather then basically the backbone of Mummy culture.

                                  These issues have been addressed to some degree in the splat books.

                                  Guildhalls did a decent (not fantastic, a lot of the following is inferred, hinted at and suggested rather then outright stated) job of getting across the idea that the modern form of the Guilds is very much a macro-grouping of allied cults doing the dirty work (often without knowing the Guild even exists as a larger entity). Likewise it stressed the idea that while Guilds are global they are at least regionally divergent in verity and approach e.g. contrast the Maa-Kep of South America with Europe.

                                  Meanwhile the Arisen masters of these Cults correspond to set Guild policy, aims and methods and sort out things like status via long-range methods (supernatural or mundane) and usually only within regional groupings of the Guild in question. With actually face to face meetings for Guild business being rare (once a decade for the Maa-Kep of Europe for example) or due to important business e.g. Trials.

                                  Multi-Guild stuff seems to only occur in the larger Nomes or for specific regional purposes.

                                  Likewise, Guildhalls provided a few more avenues for Memory retention and progression and actually made the idea of progressive Memory gain over multiple descents much more viable via Talismans.

                                  DC seems from what people are saying (I have yet to read past the introduction) to make how Nomes are organized and function make more sense and just generally be a more valid idea then was presented in the Core book. If Neall can get the Nome feature that deals with Unease Sybaris back in I'd say it did a pretty good job of giving yet another reason why Arisen might form Nomes and congregate around certain areas. Lesson the negative aspects of Unease Sybaris while providing steady cult recruitment targets seems pretty awesome.

                                  In general all the Mummy splat books so far have done a good job of playing up the importance of cults and demonstrating the myriad roles they fill in Arisen society.

                                  My is in my top 3 NWoD games (along with Vampire and Demon, it edged out Promethean) and its got a lot of strong points. However, the lines organization of info and content is its biggest weakness (perhaps closely followed by it being in the WoD 1.0 system). Half the stuff (if not volume then focus/emphasis) I mentioned above really should have been in the Core. As CAS has said it suffers from the very limited page count it got.

                                  Still, with each new release I feel Mummy becomes a better game and I feel that ST's have an easier time running the game and a wider variety of players can be hooked into it. I can see some people criticizing the game and taking issue with having to buy at least 3/4 extra books on top of the Core to get the "complete" game, but I'm not one of them.

                                  ​All in all, I'm glad we have more Mummy content with this book and that the game and setting continue to get support, get fleshed out and receive refinement for the few snags it had at its initial release.

                                  "There is no God in the Badlands." - Sunny
