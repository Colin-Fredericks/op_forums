                        Going public would be even worse, in literally every way.

                        The balance between isolationism and cooperation is definitely one of the themes Changeling explores, but even that is subservient to the big need all changelings share: Safety. All changelings want to be safe. The ways in which they achieve that will vary from one individual changeling to another, but if changelings are creatures of duplicity, secrecy, chaos (unpredictability), bindings, barriers and carefully chosen words, it's for a reason.

                        And that reason is Safety.

                                                        Well, first, lets put down the primary goal of each and every changeling that ever makes it out of the Faerie and the Hedge. "I want to go home." That's the central conceit of the game. You are called the Lost because you're trying to find your way home. You want to get back into your family, you want to get rid of your fetch, you want your life back. And, to be perfectly blunt, showing off your inhuman features to everyone is not going to get you that life.

                                                        So, why do Changelings have a Masquerade? Having one lets them at least try to fit back into life with humans than be singled out. Having a Masquerade lets them go to brunch with the parents, maybe fall in love, get married, and have a normal family.

                                                        Next, lets talk about the paranoia. They literally are out to get you. Betrayal can come from any direction. Standing out makes you a target - not only for the Gentry, but for other changelings to sell you out to the Huntsmen if necessary. Now, in an ideal world, we would be able to go jogging in a park at night and not be assulted, but in reality, you shouldn't do that. Its not the victim's fault here, but if you take actions that are known to be risky, bad things will likely happen. Same philosophy here. So, to avoid being a target, changelings hide. The fae are nearly unstoppable, but they can't catch you if they can't find you.

                                                        Now, lets talk Mask. Its easier to leave it up than to constantly have it down. Plus, you get to avoid people who might attack you just because of what you suffered. Happens all the time in real life. Someone gets abused, and society blames the victim. You asked for it. You wanted it. The cycle of abuse grows. So instead, you get used to lying about what you are. Its just... easier. Its a pattern. And that makes the next lie easier. And so on.

                                                        And, really, what are the benefits to revealing themselves to the world? It won't make it easier to resist the fae - its simple for the fae to blend into humanity. The only reliable way to test if someone is fae is cold iron, and mass produced iron doesn't count. Meanwhile, fae powers can get you past locked doors, around any security checks, and into anywhere. Fae powers are subtle, and hard to notice. So... what would be the benefit of outting the fae? It won't affect the Gentry/Huntsmen/Loyalist side of things, so what benefit would there be for the Lost to reveal themselves? It might make it easier to out fetches and reconnect with loved ones... but it also just as might backfire. Your loved ones deny you for the fetch as being real. After all, the fetch is the one that's been there for years now. Lots of prejudice around in the world, and being exposed can make you the target of hate crimes. Or your loved ones as well.

                                                        In 2e, we also have that little bit of knowledge that feeding on emotions can drain a person. If the fae were exposed, and that became public knowledge? There would be hell of a backlash on the issue. Its a violation. It would be outlawed, and likely people would make blanket accusations at the fae, and innocents charged. It would be a public outrage, and likely cost the lives of innocent Lost, even if one fed entirely from hedgefruit.

                                                        Simply from a cost-benefit standpoint, there's lots of costs involved, and very dubious benefits.
