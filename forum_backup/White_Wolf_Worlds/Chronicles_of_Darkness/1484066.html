    Integrity stats and Experiences: Alternatives and opinions

    06-14-2022, 08:03 PM
    Hello!

    After reading Deviant and see how they eliminate the Integrity stat in favor of a big Touchstone pool and the instability, I start to think about how most of the Integrity stats need experience to be increased.

    I understand why in various gamelines: Mummies and Demons really have progress bars disguised as experience system because their "integrity" is a asset only purchasable via one type of experience. But with other splats I really have doubts.

    My most important right now is the base mortal Integrity, and maybe Humanity/Wisdow being very close to the mortal Integrity concept. Why you need to spend experience to increase it? I really feel weird about the idea of experiences being capable to translate on upgrade your mental stability when most beats are result of negative things happen to the character (with the exceptions of Aspirations and Positive Conditions).

    I want to know if anyone has thinkered with the idea of a experienceless Integrity for mortals and more those with Integrity stat most close to them. The most I can think is borrow from Deviant or Werewolf:
    1. Deviant/Changeling: Replace Integrity as a Trait with dots that suffer damage and can be heal with the correct treatment.
    2. Werewolves: Create breaking points and anti-breaking points that can downgrade or upgrade the stat by distinct actions on play.
    I want to know opinions about it, what you think and if I'm the only one with this feeling.

    Thanks for reading!

      You need to spend experience to increase Humanity and Wisdom (and Hunter Integrity) because the point of those stats is to lose them. In all three cases the splat gets some ability to mitigate this by removing certain instances of loss from consideration; the first two also model some form of magical self-control on splats whose magic has its own priorities.

      Humanity works the closest to 1e Morality ladders, in that it is almost explicitly built for characters to drop to their intended level of desensitization and stay there, occasionally buying their way back up; Humanity 5, 4, and 3 are explicitly highlighted as the average ratings for neonates, ancillae, and elders, and the cost in Humanity of diablerie and the Embrace are such that characters won't undertake them lightly unless they are very far-gone.You're a creature that brings people closer to death to feed the crocodilian urges nesting in your unbeating heart, and keeping up the lie that you're a person takes practice you can't always spare the time on, and the compromises it offers you to pretend otherwise make you more of a weird monster in the process.

      Wisdom measures how much your magic does what you consciously want it to and how much it messes with people, places, and things you care about. You can make specific spells not shake your self-control in this fashion, but the cost of that is needing to put more attention on those spells' outcomes or embrace the fact that it's out of your hands, and going too far means you stop being able to have a proper mundane social life because you've gotten stuck on whatever bit of occult minutia has Enraptured you. It takes turning your Obsession in on itself and contemplating your place in the order of things for at least a contained narrative arc to bring yourself back up because you are a channel for the power of the gods and that is not an easy power-trip to shake off.

      As you observe, Pilgrimage and Memory are progress bars as much as they are Integrity analogues, and they cost Experiences to raise for a similar reason that Wisdom and Humanity do, that people to emphasize the difficulty of doing so (and, in Pilgrimage's case, to incentivize pursuit of the handful of freebies and situations where the stat can be raised, or tempt characters who know the risks of holding onto spare change to spend it on Azoth, calcifying Alembics, or other uses). Cover is not so much a progress bar as a maintenance bar that higher Primum allows you to get wholesale replacements for through soul pacts.

      Clarity and Stability are built to be incredibly swingy — a changeling can mark multiple boxes of Clarity in one event, and the constant chapter-by-chapter drip of Instability is one of the main driving game mechanics in Deviant alongside the reprieve from it that becoming a Guardian provides. Integrity is maintained through a dicepool characters can be good or bad at, and rolling normally imposes the same sorts of Conditions whether you succeed or fail.

      Harmony is likewise built to be swingy, but in a different way — moving up or down the ladder requires failing a roll, and you're more likely to fail the roll that moves you further from the ideal placement, such that a character whose Harmony is highly mobile is a character who also can't resist Death Rage very well at all. Integrity doesn't have a midpoint that is more desirable than its zenith, and "you have to succeed on this roll to regain a dot in this trait" was dropped as a mechanic in Deviant for a reason.

      The resource management game is tricky, but given all the things mortals can trade Integrity for and the comparative lack of things for them to spend Experiences on, I don't think it really benefits the game to provide them a means of getting it back for free.

          I made some effort into porting the way Clarity is handled over to Wisdom, you can see the results here: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...-wisdom-damage
          Please note that it is not playtested and does differ from Clarity in a few key ways.

          For Humanity on the other hand I am personally not as much of a fan of the idea. However, Danse Macabre (for 1e) had the Atrocity Die system, which is effectively a Humanity system without Experience tax.
          My personal take is, if you want to remove the Experience tax from the Humanity system, simply introduce "Mending Points" (or whatever you want to to call them): Rolls to regain Humanity after a major and meaningful action has been taken to reconnect with Humanity. You roll a certain number of dice, depending on how impactful the action or event is, and if you fail the roll, you regain a dot of Humanity. Why fail? That is just how the dice statistics of CofD work out. If you want it to be a good mirror of the Breaking Point systems, you should scale things based on how likely a dice roll is to fail (which is actually fairly hard as the system is relatively biased towards success).
          Obviously, this sort of system could also be applied to Wisdom, perhaps with the small adjustment that you can only commit Acts of Wisdom while "Become Wiser" is one of your Obsessions, though in that case you may actually want to remove the Arcane Beat reward. In fact, you may want to remove the Beat rewards for Breaking Points/AoH in general, as a "consolation" for risking a trait that costs Experiences is not necessary anymore when in fact that trait doesn't cost Experiences anymore.

          Notably, the system described in the second paragraph is not new. Demon: the Fallen (oWoD) allowed characters to reduce their (temporary) Torment via Acts of Kindness combined with a successful Virtue roll. However, the system made a distinction between temporary and permanent Torment, in which temporary Torment are the compounding hits that would eventually cause your permanent Torment to increase. Imagine if losing a single dot of Humanity or Wisdom was a longer process of multiple failed rolls, but in between you could heal the damage before the Xth failed roll would actually cause you to lose that dot (at which point you have to buy it back with Experience).

              From a verisimilitude argument, my perspective is that raising Integrity (and many analogues) with Experience is an appropriate model for processing your trauma in a healthy way, using hard lessons and self realizations to address your toxic habits and put effort into building better ones. I would say that going to therapy (or committing to spirituality, or following self help guidance, etc) is much more like developing a skill than it is like receiving wound care; it takes a lot of conscious effort to be aware of your own, say, Detachment and to deliberately use other skills - and it usually requires some really bad experiences (letting your emotions get the best of you, dramatically failing a social roll and pissing off people you care about) to realize you need to make those changes.

              That said, if I were to back one of those system, it would be treating Integrity like clarity, and shifting the emphasis to trauma and recovery. Fate systems do this with “Mental stress and consequences”, and, having played in and run a few of those games, it works perfectly fine. It can make it *more* of incentive for players to care about those stats and actively increase them, especially if there’s lingering problems and penalties, and situations that actively “attack” those stats.

              But do give Satchel’s argument ample consideration. Mechanics do affect play experience, and that emphasizes particular themes in a game. Having a “sanity health bar” in Fate makes it a resource management tool, and players absolutely treat it like HP, Willpower, Fate Chips, gold pieces, and any other system where it’s loss and recovery affect how effectively they can play. Handled poorly, it can turn into a mini-game, or a hand wavy downtime action. The thing is that Fate (and Changeling) have other systems that work in conjunction with that to emphasize shifts in relationships, motivations, and interpersonal drama. Systems that aren’t in Vampire and COD Mortals because those games are emphasizing something different.

                  I think it is worth considering the actual idea that Humanity is an XP tax when, if you play to the system (not try to game the system, just do what it's supposed to do) you actually net Beats on average if you're sticking to mostly high dice detachment rolls (so, you know, not being evil for the lulls but still actually making checks) with 2+ attached touchstones. That is, on average, you get more Beats on successful checks than you need to replace the lost dot in a failed check.

                  Lets say your playing a fairly "good" vampire that's generally sticking to Humanity 7ish and generally avoids breaking points at the lower tiers, and has 2+ attached Touchstones. That's 7 dice. That's ~10% fail rate. So, on average you lose a Humanity dot once every 10 rolls, generating 2 XP in the process. So that's sustainable on its own. You're also getting a Condition each time. Most of the normal success Conditions are going to lead to more breaking points, but that does mean you're going to cycle through them and get 1 Beat each time, plus the occasional exceptional success for a Condition with a Beat that doesn't immediately need to push you to a breaking point to resolve.

                  So, a Humanity 7 vampire, that regularly struggles with 4 dice breaking points, as their mistakes lead to emotional distress and more mistakes, essentially nets 2 XP every 10 rolls accounting for losing and replacing a dot of Humanity in the process.

                  Even if you assume there's going to be some lower dice pool checks (though there are other sources of bonus dice) here and there, you can still give yourself a pretty functional buffer to maintain your "Humanity tax" rebate.

                  Obviously you have roll a lot to see these sorts of averages in play, but it comes off as less of a tax when playing a struggling vampire actually earns you XP in the long run even account for paying to keep it high. Once a character is into this cycle, it's actually not mechanically hard to maintain high Humanity in general (though might be a pain as far as other vampires are concerned to have to deal with your emotional highs and lows constantly reminding them of their choices to just avoid that and fall lower and not have to make checks for those things any more.

                      I’m running a campaign with a whole lot of homebrew, mostly centered around giving mortals more access to supernatural abilities, with risking breaking points being one of the main balancing factors. Since this increases the frequency of Integrity loss, I’ve considered introducing “Integrity beats” to make it a bit easier for characters to stay sane if they put in effort, as well as to provide thematic contrast.

                      Things that might grant an Integrity beat include:
                      Avoiding the use of any active supernatural powers for a Chapter (only applicable to characters with such powers).
                      Once per chapter, spending a scene in therapy, prayer, or other mentally balancing activity.
                      Having regained willpower from your Virtue but not your Vice during the Chapter.

                          The first draft of Changeling Second Edition had a different system for Clarity than the one that made the final product, and had one mechanic in particular that I loved. Succeeding at a breaking point roll granted a condition that could be resolved by reaffirming the character's place in the world to gain a clarity dot.

                          It needed some polish but the core idea was that it incentivized characters to actively confront their trauma in controlled situations. Mechanically represented by intentionally provoking the roll in circumstances that provide enough bonuses to make success very likely. It was basically a simple model for exposure therapy and I wish it had been kept.

                              My largest problem with traits that needs to be rebought with XP (Integrity/Humanity/Wisdom) is that none of them deals with the psychological effects on the player.

                              XP is the primary way players measure progress in almost all RPGs. It's not necessarily a good metric for progress (especially in more story-driven games) but it's quantifiable and it directly translates to the character's growth on the character sheet.
                              So losing a trait that costs XP must surely mean you've lost progress? It absolutely doesn't, but it certainly still feels like it for many players who just end up frustrated by a perceived setback. The random nature of detachment rolls just exacerbates the issue, since it will cause players to lose dots at paces that doesn't fully correspond to how careful they've been. (For example: My human character went down to Integrity 4. I decided that this would be the turning point for the character and start to increase Integrity again. Despite rebuying Integrity, generally avoiding any violence, and playing to his Virtue, he's now down at Integrity 3, just because a couple of bad rolls. Another PC has during the same time been stable at Integrity 8 despite being much more violent but otherwise exposed to the same traumatic stuff.)
                              The only way to really counter this is to make it abundantly clear that losing them is not the game punishing the player, but a different type of character advancement. Rebuying them is generally completely optional because you're either likely to stabilise on a specific level (especially for Vampire; Mage's three tiered system does make low Wisdom mages more precarious as should've been a Wisdom 3 roll can p) or (in the case of non-Hunter Integrity) don't become unplayable at the bottom. That is, if you even play long enough for it to be a risk.

                              But that's really only if you accept the descent and doesn't care about keeping the trait high or rebuying it when you've reached an intended low point. In those cases, losing dots is literally the same as losing character progress. And that fucking sucks.
                              We've just houseruled that losing any traits that cost XP refunds that XP. Otherwise I'd never even bother attempting to raise my human character's Integrity, or plan to buy up to Humanity 8 with starting XP for a future vampire character.

                                  Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                                  My largest problem with traits that needs to be rebought with XP (Integrity/Humanity/Wisdom) is that none of them deals with the psychological effects on the player.
                                  Despite my previous point about the statistical math of the system, this is important to factor into any attempt to house rule things. Even Rose Bailey said as much regarding what she would do with another crack at VtR 2e's core book. The math might be sound, but that doesn't mean the play experience is.

                                  ------------------

                                  If you want something that's not XP based, and fairly easy to slot into how Humanity/Wisdom work (Integrity is a bit harder, but it also does way less anyway)?

                                  Grab Satiety from Beast. This is a bit easier on Humanity (because Satiety deals heavily with feeding and balancing human vs monster to integrate things like blood and frenzy states and so on), but the basic setup is a lot easier that trying to do something like Harmony.

                                  Basically, you have 5 Conditions that do most of the mechanical impact of different levels: Two untenable states at the extremes, and three playable states in the middle. Each Condition is linked to a specific rating range on the 0 to 10 scale (0 and 10 are the untenable two, 1-3, 4-6, 7-9 for the other three).

                                  The two untenable states should not be fail states, but exacerbate the risks of fail states. This is mostly a thematic concern to create risks that drive players towards the interesting areas of play denoted by the three middle states. A "Saintly" vampire at Humanity 10 in this setup needs to be pressed back towards "Humane" 7-9 to facilitate activity in the moral compromises at the core fo the game play experience.

                                  The key of the three middle states is that each represents a playstyle people can easily identify and pick to be a goal. None should really be mechanically superior in general (so avoiding Harmony 5 here) and instead be a goal that aligns with the character's desires. The pros and cons of each state appealing to some, and unappealing to others.

                                  Since these are Conditions, you can set the resolution of each to other central mechanics, and this means the Beat carrot remains as an incentive to take actions that risk shifting you out of your selected preferred state rather than feeling punished for doing so; and no XP costs means it's RP to setup situations to get back.
