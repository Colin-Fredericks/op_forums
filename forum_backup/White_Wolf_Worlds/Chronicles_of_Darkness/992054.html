    CofD cosmology idea: linked universes

    10-23-2016, 06:19 PM
    I recently came up with an idea for reconciling the "crowded" nature of the ChroD setting, that I really kind of like. Each line (plus Blue Book) represents a variation of a central universe, each close enough that the differences are not readily apparent and travel between them is possible. Some of the universes are closer than others- for example, the vampire and werewolf universes are close enough to be nearly linked. They aren't even so much different universes as different layers of reality in which the same places and people can exist simultaneously. As an example, a vampire out on a walk might experience a shift in Phase without noticing it, and might spend part of that walk in the Geist universe.

    However, they would eventually exist and slip back into their own unless they encountered a phenomenon native to the Geist universe and had a significant enough encounter to anchor them in that universe, thus causing the same person to simultaneously exist in both universes.

    Each universe has a Supernatural Primacy, a dominant supernatural paradigm. Werewolf has its spirit cosmology, Mage has the Supernal, Demon has the Machine, and so on. Just because these phenomena are native to one universe doesn't mean that they can't effect others. If a being from one universe contacts another and begins to exist in it as well, they still retain their connection to their native supernatural paradigm. So Contracts work in the Mage world, and so on.

    The most powerful beings of each world (extremely powerful spirits, archmasters, Gentry, the Machine) and so on are aware of the other universes, and can interact with them to a limited extent. However, they're usually interested in keeping the boundaries at least somewhat coherent. The Exarchs, for example, have tried to take control of the Machine, but found it impossible, and now mostly try to leave it alone and settle for letting it do what it does, since they at least have roughly similar short term goals. The various extremely powerful entities glimpsed in the "blue book" universe are in fact beings from these other universes reaching into it. For example, the Gentry can take someone from any of the universes, but find it easiest to take from the Changeling one. Mages and Demons tend to be the most likely to notice these patterns, being the most likely to investigate reality sharts, but most of them know better than to mess with them and try to pin things down, as this tends to have unintended consequences. For example, no one's been able to find the Zeka after that business with the two Arcadias.

    Crossover theories have a place here too. They form like mangrove roots, holding the universes together but separate. Without them, the worlds would collide and melt into nothing or drift too far apart.

    Hunter, Deviant, and Beast do not have their own universes. Instead, Hunters and Beasts are the result of someone from the "base" blue book universe being sucked into one or more of the other universes. Deviant takes place by default in the Blue Book universes, but it's entirely possible for Deviants to end up in any or multiple of the other universes.

    In the spaces between the universes are countless alternate timelines and half-formed universes. Various powerful entities find this space to be a useful place to stuff their various projects, including the alternate timelines the Machine experiments with. Assorted True Fae, archmasters, Incarnae, and so on have also noticed that there seems to be something very large some ways behind their cluster, something which looks sort of similar and is sometimes going through an apocalypse and sometimes isn't. They have collectively resolved to leave this thing alone.

    They've caught glimpses of other clusters father off in the distance, too. Supposedly an archmaster tried to communicate with one of the closer ones, and received a handwritten note, which simply read: "Fuck off. - The I.C."

                      I've always kind of assumed something similar. I posited a similar theory about the Marvel and DC comic universes (in terms of the books in each universe, not the two universes coexisting together).

                      That said, I generally look at them less as distinct universes and more as vibrational frequencies that frequently overlap. Hunter and Beast maintain pitches centered right in the middle and see the most overlapping fluctuations with the other lines. And the localized nature of the ChroD books and the general lack of global conspiracies means that most of the times these groups can do their own thing without ever encountering another type of entity. But when a vampire encounters a werewolf or a hunter runs into something that's not quite a mage but close enough to seem almost like one, it's because both frequencies have intersected at a place that's not quite the middle for either of them. The world of the two have warped slightly out of shape to better accommodate one or the other.

                      To put it in perspective, say our universe defaults at frequency 1.0, and the ChroD universe defaults at frequency 2.0. 2.0 is the world of The Vigil, where a hodge podge of movie monsters all manipulate the world, and Primordial is 2.1, where all the various creatures co-exist in their default forms and the frequency wildly fluctuates. Requiem, which resembles our world closely but also has a secret society of blood suckers, sits about 1.5; and Descent, largely detached from the going-ons of the other supernaturals and prescribing to a cosmology far removed from the real world, might sit at 2.6. If a group of hunters in Baltimore is dealing with a secret society of kindred that resemble the ideological and political split of the Requiem universe, that region of Vigil-Prime might curve towards 1.54, then snap back to its default setting once they become a tangential issue. It also means there are spaces between these universes where every individual tabletop game and homebrew setting can comfortably sit.

Ascension could probably do it pretty well, but it might be best to look at one of the superhero or pulp influenced tabletop RPGs, like Spirit of the Century.

                                                  ChroD and Unknown Armies are both good in that they have the systems necessary to model how psychologically broken everyone in VB is, though.

                                                  But can they simulate the comedy of it?

                                                  I'd definitely make Anxieties or Insecurities a core aspect of the game model, similar to Ambitions in ChroD, but more prone to an ST using it to sabotage your successes. Create a system where succeeding in the field where you really want to make it is an almost insurmountable obstacle.

                                                  Or maybe give it a party game vibe where everyone's Insecurities are out on the table, and any one player can snatch the card up and use it to twist the results of another player's rolls.

                                                  Edit: I think what you'd have to do is build a system established from the ground up around those concepts of failure, probably with a very stripped down mechanical system (using only core attributes and field specializations, perhaps?). You give your character a Crippling Insecurity that comes into play and always activates whenever the right criteria are met (like a tilt or condition in ChroD terms): faint in the presence of pretty women, never live up to your father's legacy, never fail to indulge a vice. The kind of things that can always show up at random and throw a monkey wrench in even your most orchestrated plans. Then set up a Failed Ambition (become the world's greatest super scientist or kill Doctor Venture, for instance), and say, four failures you've suffered in your past attempts to reach that. You can never pass up an opportunity to achieve your Ambition, but your Crippling Insecurity more or less ensures that you never succeed at it. Coming to terms in some way with your failures becomes one of your core objectives and a driving plot hook, and as you resolve them, you have the opportunity to replace them with healthier and more achievable objectives.

                                                  Also everyone gets to build their Arch. Maybe you take a troupe approach to storytelling, similar to the Shadows system of Wraith: The Oblivion, where everyone plays their own character and someone else's Arch.

                                                          I think the God-Machine could make an interesting cross-universe element here.Say the fundamental structure of every universe is girded by the cogs and gears of the machine, but in most universes, the God itself lies dormant or only peripherally effects the lives of those in it.
