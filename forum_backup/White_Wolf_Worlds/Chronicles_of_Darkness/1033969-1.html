    Ok, I have to say this: This thread need to have some serious clarification about what it means when it says, Just how "Gothic" is Requiem?

    Because: Gothic as a literary art form (because that is what I assumed it was asking since these are storyteller games after all) is a sub genre of Romantasism, and doesn't have any ties directly to the Cosmic Horror genre beyond what contemporary authors have ascribed to them. Nor does it have any direct connection to the Punk genre as well, because as far as I'm aware Punk is a sub genre of Expressionism (I very well might be wrong, but I'm quite sure it doesn't have it's roots in Romantasism or Gothic literature.)

    So, is "Gothic" really Gothic? Or is there a different question being asked here?

                                    Of course, the opening post does clearly indicate Stylanski's standard of Gothic. It quite clearly attributes the fear of old things (old cities, old rivalries, ancient cosmic horrors, the "old world" in contrast to North America, etc.) as the elements that define Masquerade's brand of Gothic. Granted, these seem more to me like elements of Lovecraftian literature than Gothic literature, but Stylanski did note that these are not elements of gothic literature but that they did contribute to Masquerade's particular brand of Gothic Punk. Likewise, Stylanski clearly defines the standard by which he is measuring Requiem's brand of Gothic by comparing it to American Gothic literature and Southern Gothic literature in particular, and further clarifies this by establishing that this type of horror arises from the characters rather than ancient external forces, and the old ways being baggage carried by the aristocracy as opposed to an ever-present threat.

                                    I mean, the thread title is unfortunately vague and somewhat bait-y, and the conversation has kind of drifted to the point where we're now talking about "gothic" in far more subjective terms. But the opening post was quite clear in what it meant by "Gothic."

                                                            Originally posted by PMárk View Post

                                                            True, Revised did not feel artificial. I was perhaps thinking of Revised as setting the stage for the Time of Judgement, fixing the extremes of 2nd edition, and preparing the ground for the transition to nWoD (now CofD). Right now, my train of thought is heavily influenced from reading VtM 1e core, and creating a 1e character. The book reads clean, elegant, creative, open-ended, and atmospheric. It hit all the right spots of teasing what lies deeper into the shadows of WoD, while maintaining a sense of dread and awe (for example, the Lasombra quote or how the Sabbat are presented). 2e and Revised set fire to the shadows, while Requiem 1e swapped tease and dread for vagueness.

                                                            Heck, VtM 1e almost reads like a FATE-based game, going as far as encouraging custom clans and Thaumaturgical rituals and paths. It was a really, really pleasant experience, and something which I feel has eclipsed in later publications. It puts forth an offering which I feel is missing from what is currently on offer by RPG products. Elegant simplicity (and yes, I get that a lot of the rules were not fully fleshed-out, which caused arguments around some troupe tables, but that's why STs should hide rolls and details from players) that excites and terrifies. It just hits the right spot between detailed and open.

                                                            We were done with character creation in half an hour (for a group of 3 players, one a complete newbie), and everyone was eager to start playing. It's been a while since I've seen this.
