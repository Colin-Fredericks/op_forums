                It's been awhile but wanted to say hello again. Still haven't gotten to finishing a Siren scenario nor introduce them in my weekly games (which has plenty too much to work with as is). Still, I'm aiming to make a Siren primer or cheat sheet for future play testing.

                I was noticing some recent changes to the drive and I wanted to address the biggest in the thread. Mainly, the change from Lucidity to Stability. (link)

                Once I was able to wrap my head around it, this was my question: why is it still set on the vertical 10-point Integrity styled scale?

                As it's broken down, a Siren is said to have her basic dot in the middle (Foundation) and then effectively 1-5 dots apiece that are gained or lost amongst Civilization and Nature. Which are diametrically opposed as per the core theme of the game. But for the Integrity derived purpose of Stability that isn't how it is said to function later in the document. A Siren always has her Foundation until she is ultimately unplayable as a Banshee (the true zero, or no total of Stability) turning this into a 12 point meter. 1 Foundation + 5 Civilization + 5 Nature. A total of 11 Stability if a Siren is able to "complete her raft" with the Banshee state as the aforementioned bottomed out state, or 12th step.

                Stability is reading as if it's trying to function like Werewolf Harmony, when this is incorrect. In Harmony, the 5-point is the ideal balance of Flesh and Spirit, with Werewolves starting closer to the Flesh side to reflect their human upbringing before their First Change. For Stability as described, the starting point has been moved to the middle (Foundation) and then Civilization and Nature grow on either side pseudo independently. The sum total being her Stability score.

                Which would make a lot more sense with the other functions ascribed to Stability. A Siren starts with her Foundation dot, 1 dot in both Civilization and Nature, and then two dots to distribute between the latter set however she wishes (starting with 5 Stability).

                Because if Stability was still treated as the vertical 10-point meter, how does being on one side tell me what my Siren's opposing Stability half is rated? It's later said Civilization and Nature provide once per scene bonuses to their purview (fair enough) and if one is valued greater than the other the difference incurs a penalty to the purview of the lesser (also fair, but not well explained with the classic meter height). Or an all purpose once per scene half-Stability bonus if the two are equal (but then there's the Foundation dot in the add up). And then Civilization can drop to 0, locking the Siren out of her mortal form, and Nature at 0 similarly locking her out of her diluvian form. Which, if you were trying to define this with 0-10 vertically, the moment the Siren moves off of 5 in the middle (or the narrow band of middle values) she can't use one of her two forms. And if both happen, that means she's down to her Foundation (the 1 Stability value before losing it to become a Banshee)


                My point to make is that a little polish is needed for Stability. Don't try to shoehorn it into a vertical 10-Integrity scale. Although Nature and Civilization are diametrically opposed, the mechanics suggests this acts less like Werewolf (Harmony) and more like what is hinted at Deviant. Although IIRC it was said to be Deviants have no typical 10-point Supernatural Tolerance, their two 5-point based conflicting loyalty/dedication traits sound much closer to the rewritten Stability.

                        The basic issue is with the chart as it is presented. It's fine to grandfather in the vertical arrangement, that's not the issue, it's trying to equate the values to the typical 10 steps. Going from Integrity or any other similar system, the labeling of the steps don't translate to the new values all that tidily. For example, 8 is now Civilization 3 and 1 is Nature 4, but once again the assumed Stability values (8 or 1) aren't correct in telling you how much Stability the Siren actually has, nor what the opposite Civilization or Nature has.

                        The simple solution is just to remove the 0-10 labels seen in the other Integrity derivatives. The important information is how much Stability the Siren has between her two worlds. You can still have Foundation in the middle of the scale, with Civilization and Nature apiece counting 1-5 away from the Foundation as they increase. Dissonance than strips Civilization or Nature back towards the Siren's Foundation.

                        The imagery of trying to "build and stay afloat on a raft" is very fitting for the mood of Siren, being the Drowned and surfaced heralds of the Song. Also seen for how the Siren has a good start of Stability (5, or half) but depending on her layout she may be skewed more towards either Civilization or Nature.

                        Which, given this creates a playable circumstance where the Siren could theoretically reach Stability 11, would that be particularly significant? A completed, solid raft between the two worlds. Was there still development for that 'ascended Siren' idea much like the circumstantial end games seen with the New Dawn in Promethean or the Inheritance in Beast?

                            I think I would still keep the 0-10 label, at least when it comes to gauging Stability as a whole rather than two parts.

                            As for the Ascension (and by comparison, Descension), I haven't gotten to work on either of those yet. I do have ideas for the two however. Ascension will deal with the Siren's soul, and Descension will deal with the Siren's body. Both states grant fully functional immortality however.

                            For Ascension, I think it would be attainable with Stability 11 and at least Akashic Record 6. The idea is something like a mash-up between Ariel's final fate in the Little Mermaid, with abit of a mashup in Siren's minor theme of history and chronology. The ideas I have is that an Ascended Siren's actions and her presence are temporally immutable. Meaning that the Siren and the effect of her actions are incapable of being stopped and will happen no matter what, and no amount of messing with time, fate or outside forces can stop the effect of the Ascended Siren's actions. In game, it would be like this trope in a nutshell.

                            http://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.ph.../NoSavingThrow

                            As for Descension, I have one idea and it is something Echidna has attained. Descension makes a Siren into the ultimate life form, the ultimate self-sustaining survivor. What the "Ultimate Life Form" entails is that the person gains the powers of each and every living being who has or ever will have lived. They can copy and reshape their bodies to have any genetic trait any animal/plant has had (such as the backwards aging of the one jelly fish, being able to secrete toxic slime from their bodies like the poison dart frog, reshape their bodies so they'd be able to fly, etc). This CAN also possibly include genetic traits of Supernatural beings, or copying effects/powers they use that involve genetics or their bodies. And of course, the user can "gift" these changes to others by mashing their own cells with the other being.

                            It's pretty much in the same vein (and inspired by) of Jojo's Bizarre Adventure, when Kars becomes the ultimate life form and his abilities in said form. I don't know if it'd be TOO broken to use (especially with the bit of copying supernatural life forms), but I feel like it'd be fine since Descension isn't something most Sirens have, and Echidna is still working out how far she can twist people.

                                When a Siren gains dots or loses dots within Stability, she gains or loses her connections to either Nature or Civilization. When Civilization is affected, dots gained go upwards past Stability 5 when increased and are lost from the topmost when it decreases. When a Siren loses Stability 6, they lose their connection to Civilization but they aren’t unplayable. For the ease of access, Stability could be separated as Stability 6-10 being Civilization 1-5, Stability 5 as one point in the middle from which they both grow out from, and Stability 4-0 being Nature 1-5, and “Stability” referring to the total number of dots a Siren owns in them put together.

                                When Nature is affected, dots gained go downwards below Stability 5 when increased and are lost from the bottommost when dots are lost. When a Siren loses Stability 4, they lose their connection to Nature but they aren’t unplayable. If a Siren faces Dissonance for violating Stability 5’s rule, then if she fails the roll she loses a dot from the higher part of her Stability. Not so for Siren, what's important is how much is filled, and you start in the middle and branch in both directions.
