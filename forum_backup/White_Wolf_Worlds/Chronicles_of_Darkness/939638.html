    101 Cults

    07-29-2016, 01:48 PM
    1. GetMoving! grew in the loamy fear of the "obesity epidemic". It advertised, like so many before and after, that its program of relentlessly positive parent-child exercise classes would get kids moving, foster healthy habits, and so on. GetMoving!, however, seemed to offer actual results, and so the yuppie superparents streamed in, adding yet more ecstatic online reviews of the program once they left. They have programs in twenty states now. If you could someone sneak past the carefully hidden security personnel (they look like fit, unfailingly upbeat twentysomethings, but they've been trained in practices more dangerous than jazzercise) into the rented gymnasiums, you'd see them: legions of parents and children endlessly repeating the same exercises with blank expressions, while a record message blares over the intercom: "You must become strong. Only with strength can you be pure. You must move. Only constant movement is pure. You cannot stop. Only pigs stop. Pigs are meat. You must become strong."

      2. The Everflame is a meditation and philosophy group doing their reunions around a central fireplace. They say their flame is the same, pure one that was lit on the creation of the group, about 20 years ago - Olympics rip-off, amiright? - and they often ask questions to the flames when no one has an answer. They also meditate by analyzing the flickers of its tongue. They're pretty open about what they do. It's a little weird, sure, but nothing illegal.

      The strange thing is that no one ever fuels the fire, and most members of the Everflame swear that sometimes, when they ask questions to the fire, it answers them.

          These were designed with Mummy in mind, and posted in greater detail on that subforum.

          3: The Celestial Brotherhood of the Lemurian Truth is one of the countless shoddy little New Age cults to come out the 1960s, preaching a nonsensical hodgepodge of free love and ancient astronaut theory. It would be little more than a collection of aging nudists had several B-list celebrities joined the Brotherhood within a stretch of a few months, drawing obsessive fans and stalking paparazzi to the smoke-filled compound. What's drawing people to this cult just now after fifty years of harmless sex, dope, and occult babbling? Who's been funding them this whole time?

          4: The Society of Tyr began as a support group for the veterans coming back from two decades of conflict in the Middle East with missing limbs. Most of the Tyr Project is still precisely that, organizing group therapy and a space for disabled soldiers to speak freely and seek help, but a rare few among their ranks are chosen and told about the twilight of the gods to come; they've paid their debt to America, but now have a divine duty to fight those who would end the world. Valhalla awaits those who fall.

          5: The Black List is a ghost story for law enforcement across the globe and the most prestigious club for the best career criminals in existence. Membership is provided by invitation only to the hitmen and heisters good enough at what they do to never make the news, and they're granted incredible paychecks and magical masks to help them avoid detection as they kill occultists and steal magical artifacts for an unknown patron.

              6. The Writers Pizza Workshop is a circle of fantasy, science fiction and horror writers ranging from the "working on their first short story" to the "is a billionaire from merchandising rights and movie deals" who meet at a local pizza place near the city library every Tuesday (because if you get the buffet on Tuesdays, you get an extra punch on your "buy 10 buffets get one free card).

              For the most part, it's just the writers brainstorming ideas and discussing the publishing business, with various discussions on politics, technology and pop culture (most of which spring from the discussion of their writing). Most book lovers in town know about the workshop, but what most people don't know is that the members all have some form of physic ability relating to knowing things they shouldn't, which they use to "inspire" materials for their books. At the lower levels, the ability is purely latent, with the revelations subconsciously making their way into their works, with many of the exercises of the workshop being designed to train these abilities. The four leaders of the workshop are masters of these psychic arts who know quite a bit about the chronicles of darkness.

                      8. The Miners mine gold. Or, rather, they farm it. And they're good at farming it. Where do they farm it? Anywhere there are people who will buy it. World of Warcraft still remains their focus, despite Blizzard's vigorous attempts to shut them down. To the company's chagrin, this seems to be impossible- the "Miners" sell their wares through an ever-shifting series of websites (all with the same pickax logo), but all of the contributors seem to come from different countries and backgrounds, and there's no central server farm. Regardless of what connection they're using, anyone given a contract by the miners has one thing in common: they're all better than anyone else at what they do. Somehow, they always accumulate gold faster than anyone else can, and no one, not even the makers of the various games, has any idea how they do it.

                          9. The Sigismund Foundation for Interfaith Outreach doesn't get much attention, and only a few crackpot Bible Belt fundie ministers have accused them of anything sinister. In fact, this Unitarian-founded nonprofit isn't involved in much weirdness at all (even if "Unitarian Universalism" does sound like some sort of mi-go UFO cult), with the notable exception of the CRPSBF. The Committee for the Recognition and Preservation of Small and Burgeoning Faiths isn't exactly a cult, but it's become the closest thing the cults and shadow religions of the world of darkness have to a United Nations. When the committee was chartered and sent to survey small and emerging faiths and religious movements, no one knew what a Pandora's box they were opening (or that they would end up negotiating with a cult that claimed to actually possess said box). You just can't go looking for fringe religions in the world of darkness without founding plenty of out-there shit. Doggedly adhering to its mission, the Committee slowly found itself becoming a neutral body that primarily handles negotiations between assorted cults and mystery religions and related issues, a role that has put its members in the path of some of the weirdest things the world has to offer. They've done things they're not very proud of, but they can usually rationalize them as mitigation. After all, if you find a lawyer who can get Unspeakable Deacon Ggarlkagh Ggar out of his DUI charge, that means he doesn't end up trying to summon a shoggoth to eat the bailiff during his trial. They do, however, take a dim view of charlatans, those who start cults and religious movements purely for their own benefit. It's fine with them if you worship a world-devouring snake monster, just as long as you really believe in it.

                                  11. The Lemurian Heresy (or as they refer to themselves, The Faith of the Clockwork Goddess) arose from one of the largest schisms in occult-religious history. The Mekhanists, one of the largest global God-Machine cults, underwent a full-blown civil war in the middle of the twentieth century, and no amount of crusading has been able to reunite them or quash either of the two primary sides. The Lemurian Heresy is a Techgnostic doctrine- in the parlance of those who study machine cults, that means they believe that the machine is flawed or broken in some way and needs to be escaped or repaired. The "Clockwork Goddess" they worship seems to be a sort of platonic ideal of what the Machine could be. Ideologically, the Lemurians resent not getting their shiny retro-futuristic techno-utopia, and are determined to "rectify" whatever flaw in the machine stole their glorious future.

                                  The Atlantic Schism, the event that created them, was in fact the culmination of many years of manipulation and scheming by a powerful Ring of Integrators, who had been experimenting on infiltrating cults and using them to influence the Machine. The Ring currently serves as the high priesthood of the Lemurian heresy, and has their fingers in quite a few other related groups. Their influence can be considered Global-to-Cosmic, but they act with utmost care to avoid revealing themselves.

                                      12. The Stewards of Goblin Creek: The obscure tabletop roleplaying game Goblin Creek would have been another unremarkable D&D clone had it not managed to attract an incredibly devoted fanbase. Anyone who played the game with its creator became absolutely convinced of its merits, as either the game or its creator seemed to have the ability to put people in a trance state, in which the events they played out seemed incredibly, gloriously real. Some players compared it to being a child again, feeling that rush of unfettered fantasy. As a result, the game maintains a core of incredibly loyal fans who continue to contribute to the game, even though it is long out of print. Realmland, the game's setting, has become one of the most detailed fictional worlds in fantasy history, as fans have obsessively detailed its geography, cultures, customs, languages, ecology, and so on. The Unofficial Goblin Creek Companion is now by far the longest book in the English language, and it steadily continues to expand.

                                              14. The Underhill Society is probably one of the least sinister-seeming collections of people ever assembled, being mostly composed of older academics in liberal arts fields, aging hippies, and the occasional younger nerd who ends up drawn into their orbit. They're open about being a society of Tolkien enthusiasts, but what they don't share freely is that they honestly, genuinely believe that Middle-Earth is the true history of the world, and the history we're taught is nothing but a sinister lie spread by a conspiracy they call the White Hand. Underhills spend their free time pursuing secret manuscripts and hoary old relics, searching for proof of their theories, occasionally tracking down an open-minded young academic and seeking to bring them into the fold. While mostly harmless, a few of them do have large collections of battle-ready medieval weaponry.
