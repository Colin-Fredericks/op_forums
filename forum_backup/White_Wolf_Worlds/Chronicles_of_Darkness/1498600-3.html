    Originally posted by maryshelly View Post
    Sorry to resurrect this topic, but it is too much fun to talk about.

    Has anyone mentioned Cannibalism or Ufarah's Bane? The Uratha bite can cause aggravated damage, and with the huge amount of damage that Uratha can cause, I imagine that a pack vs. pack fight between Uratha can be finished very quickly. Sure their harmony will go to hell, and there will be numerous violations of the Oath of the Moon, but the fight will be over quickly.

    That said, does Cannibalism only apply to the Forsaken? Do The Pure, Bale Hounds, and Ghost Wolves care if they descend to madness. I figure the Predator Kings would be perfectly fine biting their enemies to cause aggravated damange.

    The Rite Ufarah's Bane is similar, without all the violations of the Oath of the Moon.

    Once you factor this in, the Garou form is really just extra health levels.
    I'm not sure what these have to do with the overall subject of the Gauru form being overpowered or not.

    Cannibalism is more likely a concern in Death Rage, though it's easier to commit to it (and justify it) in Gauru if a werewolf wants to, and Urfarah's Bane is a Rite that is generally designed for murder within their own spheres(on the subject of vampires, I would argue that the clarification it's "werewolves only" caveat can be muddled with and argued if one really wants to at their table).

    The Pure suffer the Harmony hit for cannibalism same as the Forsaken-it pays to remember that the Oaths of the Moon and Urfarah respectively are more codifications to help each set of tribes understand the consequences of their own Harmony rather actual oaths, as opposed to the Tribal Oaths which do alter a werewolf's spiritual makeup. This is why Ghost Wolves also suffer the cannibalism hit. The Bale Hounds, by contrast....well, one fifth of them are built on cannibalism as a principle, and the rest aren't far behind thanks to the Rite of Absolution.

    Gauru's not more than just extra health levels-the combat thread I linked to back a page or so makes a point of how potent the whole kit is-but it's also not the absolute solution the rest of this thread makes it out to be. It's the answer when the rest of the work is done, the quickest way to tie up all the loose ends.

            Welcome to Chronicles of Darkness! You're the monster.

            Joking about facts asides, this came up as a particular point a few years when (I think) the latest iteration of the "Good Pure" argument rolled out, and the writers (again, Bunyip primarily) made the point that the morality of Forsaken Second Editions was generally darker and more neutral than it had been in First Edition. The Uratha are monsters that are, by and large, divorced for human psychology and social mores by their own being, and are generally motivated by a sort of blue-and-orange morality. To be blunt, being a serial killer is what they do. The Forsaken have no compulsion to be the best versions of werewolves out there, and the Pure, though easily motivated to be worse, have leeway in terms of their oaths, priorities, and sacred prey that allows them to be quite heroic, or at least more situationally ready to be the more "moral" between themselves and the Forsaken

            The Bales Hounds do remain exceptional, because they have incentive to take things to their most excessive when they can and to motivate and manipulate others into the same excess, but the general point is that Forsaken, in contrast to it's earlier fan positioning, is not secretly a game about darkly shaded heroes and good guys. THe Uratha are hunters, harrassers, and killers who hunt, harass, and kill in the name of shaping their territory into what they will it to be. Just because the Forsaken sounds nicer doesn't necessarily mean they are.

            EDIT: THough of note, that same writer pointed out the ways in which the Pure were in deep denial, so if that's something you hold against a group, then it's a thing to factor in.

                                    10-01-2023, 02:19 AM
                                    Originally posted by maryshelly View Post
                                    Silver is the most obvious balance against the Garou form. It is basically like Kryptonite to the Uratha, only is much more common. The most obvious ways to weaponize silver seem to be shot and hollowpoint filler, though there must be other clever applications.

                                    It has also the double whammy of dealing aggravated damage and being a Kuruth trigger (which is worse?). Squads of men in body armor carrying shotguns loaded with silver seem a good way to remind the Uratha who the apex predators are.
                                    Silver is also fairly expensive, and because of that it's hard to make a weapon with it designed for reusability-and all of that becomes excessive with a squad of men (presumeably trained by your framing) with body armor and shotguns.**

                                    Let's run with some averages here. For any game, the common player is usually not gonna have a Attribute outside of the realm of 1 through 3, particularly if they're trying to balance optimization with variety-3 dice, as a quick rule of thumb, is mostly reliable enough to get 1 success, which is all you need in the vein of keeping it simple, and while investment in the fourth and fifth dot of any given attribute can make it easy to keep skill costs down low and thus have more variety, it's more practical to have a solid dice pool or the better part of a dice pool built in at the base with Attributes where each dot has more lateral usage. Since Forsaken is a very Physical game, let's set our average at 3 for our average here for the relevant point of Stamina. If you then account for what Garou gives our lad for just taking damage, our Uratha has 12 Health Boxes (not including merits, because that gets so variable I'm not willing to call any particular a usable average, so that's 5 for initial, 3 for Stamina, and 4 for the combined matter of Gauru's Stamina and Size boost). For attack purposes, we'll also assume he has Strength 3 and Brawl 3, so in Gauru he's got a dice pool of 9, and since I'm not going beyond a turn for the purpose of illustration, I'll also have them spend Willpower to get 12, getting regularly 4 successes off of the die pool and 6 once we add in the bonus from claws and teeth, and we're gonna be generous and assume that our werewolf can reach any given shooter in 1 move action.

                                    For our shooters, let's assume you're talking about people who are not only trained in shooting but are also used to working with each other as a unit-that latter part isn't going to really come up, but I want to preface it to make it clear how much worse this assumption could gets. So, for our purposes, let's assume 3 for Dex, 3 for Firearms, with a statistical likelihood of two successes regularly, one as a near guarentee. And they're using shotguns, which in both the Chronicles core book and Hurt lock adds 3 Successes to any successful Firearms rolled for the purposes of shooting someone with said, and is also a 9-again*. I'm not assuming these guys are hunters, but I am assuming they're at least used to having to keep cool when shit's going down, and in fact for our basic assumption here, I'm openly going to assume idiocy because I want to make a point about raw numbers without tactical consideration, and I bring this all up because I think facing a giant fucking wolf-man from your nightmares warrants a Willpower expenditure on their parts, so we're looking at a basic dice pool of 9 dice. We'll also not be nice and assume they each have 2 Stamina, for 7 Health. And just to keep it simple, let's assume we have three of these guys.

                                    If our shooters, standing around like numbnuts, and at a bare minimum manage to just get 1 success each, these guys are still in just a turn delivering a full round of Lethal Hitboxes for our werewolf. Sure, he'll shrug that off on the next turn, but that's already Not Good. If we make a likelihood of just two successes each during one turn of shooting, our werewolf has 3 Aggravated Damage that's not going away, a full fourth of his extended health bar. Make it the actual statistical average of 3, and that's half of our Werewolf's bar gone in a turn that will not heal like that. And if any of those 9-Agains manages to land an additional success, that explodes even more. Even if you were to downgrade to Brief Nightmares and ran these guys as Minions so their best dice pool was 5 flat, it still demonstrates just how much three guys with shotguns can do.

                                    And then our werewolf gets to go in the turn, presumably healing all remaining Lethal Damage but very probably smarting bad. They run up to one guy, rolls, hits and gets out that 6 successes we established. If we just assume Kevlar Vests, that means 1 Person takes 5 Lethal, giving them a -1 to actions but still alive. It's not even that if we assume Flak Jackets or better, because that drops it down to a minimum of 4 or worse.

                                    And then very likely, in this incredibly stupid setup, it goes again and maybe one of them dies depending on the initative order, which is a nice moral victory before the werewolf goes bye bye.
                                    Now, as I kept iterating, this is a very stupid situation***, and I made it that way just to create a sense of the numbers at play with the proposed scenario and what relying on Gauru alone to survive looks like, but I also want you to consider what happens when you start including tactics into the matter, or even just changes in numbers. For our werewolves, there's obviously group tactics, Merits, Gifts, Rites benefits, and more, but for our humans it also includes their own tactics (and Tactics, if Hunters) and Merits. There's a lot of variables to really account for-are your shooters actually that decently trained, or are they more likely to run away in a scattered fashion or shoot in a haphazard way? Do those create accidental advantages? Werewolves like to operate together, but even on a focused hunt, there's oft-times where each is off doing their own individual thing, so are they being caught on their own? Is the fight happening in a place where the shooters have a lot of control, where there may be traps or security devices working to their aid? Did our shooters retreat and then do tactical surveillance to get an idea of their opposition's advantages in advance, or even start by doing that without a run in? How much of any of these apply to our werewolves in kind?

                                    This is why so much of the Uratha's tactics- as dictated by their Merits, Gifts, and Rites- includes controlling for so many of those variables as possible.​ That really dumb numbers game is why both sides need to think smartly about what they're doing and how much they have stacked in their favor-and just simply using Gauru as a win button clearly isn't that against any credible threat.

                                    TL;DR, any take on Gauru being overpowered without accounting for the actual numbers and variables is not a credible take.

                                    EDIT: also didn't account for Lunacy, again, going for numbers here as a basis to make it clear you can make a lot of arguments against the idiot argument which then basically makes it clear you have to account for the whos, whats, wheres, whens, whys, and hows of any given conflict.
                                    EDIT: also, going the other way, I didn't factor in the shooter's defense, honestly because I forgot, but also because that really doesn't help our werewolf out that much,

                                    *For just running estimatations, I treat the -Again rules as being in effect on 3/2/1 successes aspect-with 10-again, I need three successes before I assume a die is added, for 9-again, I need two, and for 8, every success adds a die, and from there the old standard of 3 Dice=1 Success. The Agains, in the way, aren't the most productive things in the universe, but they can be swingy.
                                    **If you want a more cost effective but very helpful solution, consider rock salt shells that inflict penalties with each one fired.
                                    ***without anymore additional context but trying to guess a reasonably smart situation where these people realize the other side as a threat suddenly but not in any way that grants surprise, our lone werewolf would more likely bolt taking advantage of either Urhan (for the size) or Urshul (for the speed)[assuming, of course, that our werewolf is not specced to bluff and lie their way into seeming like they belong there even if they are unexpected, or even just surrendering if the scenario lets it be advantageous], and our shooters will ward with their shots but not particularly directly chase the person what became a wolf, instead preferring to contact additional support to whatever end is most productive for what they're doing. A werewolf might take Gauru with the intent to scare off the shooters, but I would wager a scared-but-trained shooter set would retreat and otherwise try to sweep the legs, which they are very capable of doing and thus have a lot of benefit for escaping. Getting anymore specific is getting into the weeds but will invariably prove my point about Gauru by itself.
