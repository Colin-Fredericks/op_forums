                Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
                On the whole Werewolves are better combatants than Vampires in Gauru forum. The key phrase here is "in Gauru form", in all other forms Werewolves are squishy, and vulnerable to normal amounts of damage (as opposed to Gauru, which is vulnerable only to absurd amounts of damage). So it behooves a Vampire to avoid direct combat in favor of ambush tactics and coercion.

                However, if a vampire does have to fight a Werewolf head to head, the following approaches work best:
                • Bring Friends: Gauru form is absurdly durable, a single vampire is not likely to be able to put the damage forward to pierce its regeneration. Not unless they are some twinked out combat beast. So numbers are your friend. Three people with fully automatic rifles turns the tables quite nicely, though the more the merrier in this endeavor.
                • Bring Banes: Silver hurts Werewolves, sometimes it can be enough to deter a fight outright. Remember, every aggravated damage dealt to a wolf decreases the difficulty of rolling over with lethal, so just two or three swipes with a silver knife could be enough to turn the tide (though close quarters combat is highly inadvisable).
                • Be a Cheat: Vampires are well equipped to shut down conflicts through mental tricks, Majesty can remove the enemy's incentive to fight, while Obfuscate can remove their ability to attack you (being an invisible sneak). Two dots of Nightmare shuts down just about any combatant, sending the big bad wolf running with its tail between its legs.
                • Be a Bad Enough Dude: A suitably combat spec'd Vampire can do toe to toe with a Werewolf, even a very potent one. But this sort of character will probably be rare among PCs, requiring many dots worth of disciplines and devotions to pull off.
                Control the terms of the fight. Bring it into a public place, where the risk of Kuruth is just too high to use the Gauru form.

                                            Cutting of a limb (or permanently damaging it beyond use) is achieved by inflicting more damage than the target's Stamina on a targeted attack that inflicts aggravated damage.

                                                For humans it takes aggravated damage greater than the target's stamina at a -2 called shot penalty to cut off an arm or a leg, 1 aggravated at a -3 called shot penalty to cut off a hand.                                                     Well... I think there should be a different case between a sharp bladed weapon doing aggravated damage to a limb in this scenario versus guns or blunt weapons that do damage in a much different way.

                                                    Sure... a vampire might not take as much damage from cuts in muscle tissue and things like that, but a cut off arm is a cut off arm. It would also be so much easier to cut off a limb using a sharp bladed cutting weapon than a blunt weapon like a baseball bat and should be much more dangerous weapon to a vampire since they have such potential to do devastating damage. A fit vampire with some Vigor and blood pumped into strength should basically cut bodies in half with a sword with almost no effort (unless supernatural defenses of course).

                                                    I do think it shouldn't be unreasonable to deem an attack with a bladed cutting weapon that is a called shot to be deemed aggravated damage if a certain number of successes was rolled and enough damage done.. but that would be a house rule I guess for specific cases. But the aggravated damage would only amount to the necessary to cut off the limb... any other damage would still be lethal in that case.

                                                    Large cutting weapons should also be much more dangerous to vampires than weapons such as knives, clubs or other no cutting weapons with the means to do real devastating physical harm. I would not be a stranger to allow cutting weapons to do chunks of Lethal damage to vampires if sustained in enough quantities from any attack. At least part of it should become lethal, say anything above stamina is deemed lethal on a vampire. But that would also be a house rule of mine. Cutting swords are really devastating weapons to unprotected flesh and bone targets... blunt weapons that mainly maim and do internal bleeding wounds are not as dangerous to things such as a vampire as that of a sword.

                                                        1- The system seems to care more about usefulness of a limb than it actually being there. An arm crippled by a blunt weapon that just dealt agg damage is pretty much as useless as if it had fallen off. Depending on caliber, a gunshot CAN blow your arm off;

                                                        2- Such effect would be better delivered by a tilt built in the weapon description, making it mechanically an individual property of the weapon. Given 1, it is more probable that it is a tilt with related Persistent Condition (since arms don't grow up, usually);

                                                        3- Given balance, mechanical issues, and the overall state of the system, it is probably not worth an actual rule, unless you want to see it play out in your games. You don't need actual house-rules for this kind of effect if you want them to play out just rarely as GM Fiat effects for dramatic scenes. That can be easily done with player agreement and without rules. You need to house-rule it only if you want a common combat to have such risk in your game. And to see your players actually calling shots to cut-off arms.

                                                        4- During normal combat you can give through description the reasons for the called shot not really cutting an arm off. Just remember that the system doesn't care so much for precision, so you can describe a lot of outcomes.
