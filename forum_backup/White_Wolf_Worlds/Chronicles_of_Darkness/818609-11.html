The splats that do not suffer normal Breaking Points might be exempt from providing Larvae at ST discretion, but they can compensate in other ways (e.g. Demons can compare notes about Infrastructure with Geniuses and lend/sell Gadgets).

            The time travel rules have always been kind of arcane to me. I'll reread and add something after I refresh myself.

            EDIT: Okay, now I remember why I don't remember Genius's time travel rules very well: I kind of hate them.

            For starters, the timeline's way too ordered and simplistic, and enacting changes is far too difficult. It makes it too hard to really toy around with the "unintended consequences of time travel." The Time Masters Guardians of Forever kind of feels like a cheap way to penalize your time-travelling shenanigans when they should just come organically from the results of your stupid, arrogant chicanery.

            Second, it also doesn't make a huge amount of sense. If they exist in all points in time, then by definition, they have already experienced the future, which means that the future is already set for them, which means that they would have no need to police time, because the timeline for them has already been set. Any policing just prevents other, alternate timelines from existing that have basically no effect on them.

            I would completely discard the time travel rules as they are. Time travel is unregulated (and basically impossible to regulate without constant, extratemporal murder), and changing the timeline requires no more than a time machine, a raygun and a bottle of tequila. Your existence in a given point in time doesn't affect the stability of that timeline when you visit it. You can return to your time, but you still need to deal with your alternate self where applicable. If you prevent your existence, you don't blink out, you're just orphaned from time (and incur a serious Transgression roll). You don't get to just let things snap back, and you deal with the consequences of your actions.

                        I just realized something. The sorts of Experiments-as-Investigations that a Genius will be likely perform are going to be directed by their Catalysts, Aspirations and their Aesthetic. I believe the third should be expanded into a Worldview, which would be unique to each Genius and would provide them with a starter set of Experiments to perform and Plans to put into motion.

                        For example, let's take a Worldview off the top of my head: Eco-friendliness. Experiments might include "how do I fix the town's factories". The Clues (or Data) you get by following this could be applied to a relevant roll such as Intelligence + Science, with additional modifiers based on the current list for Investigations. What I'd like to see is that the more penalties you inflict upon the Experiment (accidentally or not), the more Wondrous Beats you get should you Succeed. A Dramatic Failure should yield a similar quantity of Wondrous Beats, perhaps slightly larger to make up for narrative conflicts like alerting the police or becoming indebted to a shady Genius or Mane. These multiple Wondrous Beats could be granted via thematically appropriate Persistent Conditions (we will probably need new ones to cover situations outside of Notoriety, Obsessed and such). This is meant to represent how Geniuses tend to pursue knowledge even when it would likely result in catastrophe.

                        Another important aspect of Experiments would be how they differ from normal science. The latter is more preoccupied with the whys of nature, refutability, reproducibility and practicality. The former are more concerned with "why nots", novelty, passion and creativity. For example: why not find a way to hybridize positive servile spirits and plants to revolutionize the food industry? The Data you receive as you follow your Plan and perform the Experiments (even if they are not strictly true according to other splats' metaphysics or mundane physics) can be incorporated into Wonders that match your Worldview.

                        Does this sound like viable gameplay? How would we represent changes in Worldview?
