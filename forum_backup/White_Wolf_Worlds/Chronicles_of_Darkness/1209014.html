    Anshega April - Cult of Bones 2e

    04-19-2018, 05:40 PM
    So, because I am incredibly busy at the moment (don't worry, the Tools of Hatred write-up will be coming!), and because I'm on the team for Shunned By The Moon and therefore keeping my best new Anshega material for that forthcoming product, I'm not going to be able to do more than one Anshega April poll this month. However, that doesn't mean I don't have new Anshega April material to share! Here's a 2nd ed update for the Cult of Bones, a Pure Lodge for those of you who want to feature Ivory Claw Belloc or Fire-Touched gravediggers in your chronicles, as requested by one of my patrons

    The Cult of Bones
    Gravediggers, Archaeomachs, Ghuls

    If the past is another country, the Cult of Bones want it to conquer the present. The zealous Pure who make up the Cult’s ranks have a grand goal in mind for their Lodge – the direct resurrection of the Great Predator itself. This isn’t a hypothetical or metaphorical plan, but an overarching purpose that drives everything the Cult of Bones does. The Gravediggers plan to gather up the earthly remains of the deity and, from its bones, build it anew. Then, as the Cult sees it, the Wolf will again walk the world, all wrongs will be set right, and the Pure shall rule over the paradise of Pangaea once more, subjugating all that exists and turning all of creation to the purpose of honouring their progenitor each passing day and night.

    What makes the Cult of Bones’ plan different to the wild schemes of the mad-eyed deluded is that it approaches this ultimate goal systematically, and has made considerable strides already. Whatever the metaphysical state that befell the Great Predator when it died, there do seem to have been tangible fragments left over – gobbets of divine substance that retain traces of the Wolf’s power. Cultists travel the world, seeking out major hotspots of Uratha history in search for these shattered shards of the Great Predator, reaching out to local Pure for aid in digging up the past in return for favours, fetishes, and influence.

    The Cult’s efforts go beyond digging up the dead pieces of Wolf; they’ve come into a number of other ancient carcasses and fragments over the years, including what may be truly dead Shards of the Spinner Hag, a massive mouldering colossus buried under Sudan, and what seems to have been a ritually mummified leviathan of the Void Beyond – a metaphysically-preserved nightmare, though who mummified it, how, and why, is still unknown. Some assume that it is these dead ancients that are the Cult’s prey, and that is true in a manner of speaking, but the actual prey to whom the Cult have refined their hunt is all the more worrying. Gravediggers hunt wolf spirits and Uratha ancestor spirits, not to draw upon their wisdom but to literally drain them of power or bind them. If Mother Wolf was weakened before its death, then the Cult must restore that strength, and so they hunt for those spirits with the closest resonance to the Great Predator. Cult ritualists bind the hunted spirits for Essence extraction, feeding their power into the relics that the Lodge has managed to gather, or imprison them in fetishes to further serve the Cult. A few are forcibly bound to work directly for the Gravediggers as attack spirits, trackers, and bone-hunters.

    The Gravediggers are not a hidden cult; they are widely known of amongst the Pure, and they have leveraged their considerable membership, the respect for their goal, and their network of resources into significant power as a result. The Cult doesn’t take just anyone, leaning towards the more cerebral and zealous; it wouldn’t be accurate to just categorise them as occult archaeologists, but the Cult of Bones does hold a significant number of scholars. Other Pure fear the Cult of Bones because the Gravediggers believe their goal justifies the means, any means, and they are more than happy to sacrifice lesser Pure to take another step up that ladder towards Wolf’s revival. Worse, the Cult’s treatment of spirits is at odds with the Pure’s reverence for the mighty totems that oversee the global war against the Forsaken, and that scares many Anshega. The Cult focuses its predations on wolf and ancestor spirits that serve the Forsaken, but they are not above picking off the spiritual allies of Pure who do not co-operate with the Lodge’s agenda.

    Totem: Father’s Howl
    The Cult of Bones holds that Father’s Howl is literally the echoing, final howl of Father Wolf – a death-scream given sentience and the driving will to mastermind its creator’s will. This is both true and false; Father’s Howl is an ancient spirit of death that was born from the tumult of the Great Predator’s demise, but it does not have any more of a divine link to the Great Predator than any spirit does to the circumstances of its creation. Nonetheless, Father’s Howl is a very powerful spirit, close to vying the might of the Firstborn themselves, and it is this spiritual strength that gives the Cult of Bones the leeway they need to pursue their schemes without the meddling of the other Pure totems.

    Father’s Howl appears as a presence, rather than a physical mass; the air ripples with a constant, wailing echo, as if a thousand wolves were distantly howling. The Howl can extend its presence to wash over a whole city block, or fold its scream down into the palm of a werewolf’s cupped hand; it also has the ability to Fetter itself to an Uratha’s breath, hiding in the stream and pulse of their lungs and voice. When it lets itself loose, stone shatters and buildings topple. It is sometimes mistaken for a storm or wind spirit, but it is very much a spirit of death; where it passes, weak lives are snuffed out, and ghosts shiver and dance in frenzied agitation.

    The Father’s Howl has a simple agenda, one that entirely matches with its followers; the return of Father Wolf to the world. It believes that when this happens, the Great Predator will embrace it as a favoured child and inheritor. Howl bears a burning hatred for Luna; in the aftermath of the Sundering, it called to the Moon to recognise it as the successor to Wolf, born of Wolf’s death and last howl as it was. Luna refused to acknowledge it, and denied it the Warden Moon’s favour and the mark of legitimacy the god could have bestowed upon it – not a metaphorical notion but a very real crowning with power that Howl believes would have rallied the Firstborn under its reign. By denying this, Luna instigated the strife and war that would follow, and so Father’s Howl holds the mad god responsible not just for the rejection of its own ascension, but for all the carnage among the Uratha that has followed.

    Howl’s relationship with the Firstborn has long been troubled; it claims to have aided the Pure Firstborn during the early formation of the Tribes, but while there is certainly obligation from the three great wolves to Father’s Howl, they also seem to resent its claims to greatness and its hunger for power. Howl has gathered many spirits to its court and established itself as a major pillar within Pure society, strong enough to be independent from the totems that flock to the Firstborns’ skirts, but it yet remains denied the respect and rule it feels it is due. Often, it takes out its frustration on Lunes that it manages to catch or that the Cult brings to it, brutally torturing them for its own amusement. Sometimes, it intentionally sends its Cult to target the ancestor spirits or wolf spirit allies of the Pure Tribes in order to steal away some of the Firstborns’ strength and assert its own power against them.

    Bonds
    Blessing: When resisting or contesting a rite that a Lodge member is conducting, spirits suffer a penalty equal to the dots in the highest Influence they possess that is either Influence (Wolves) or an Influence directly to Uratha, such as that which an ancestor spirit may possess.
    Aspiration: To retrieve remains that have been buried or hidden.
    Ban: A Lodge member cannot display humbleness about their own accomplishments.

    Sacred Hunt

    The Lodge Sacred Hun grants your character the ability to tear the Essence from certain spirit-prey. Against spirits that have Influence (Wolves) or an Influence directly relating to Uratha, your character is able to strike these ephemeral entities with their natural weaponry, and any such hit also drains a single point of Essence from the victim. This Essence is not destroyed; it is drained away into the Cult’s eldritch vaults where ancient god-fragments lie slumbering.

    Secrets of the Gravediggers
    • The Cult of Bones have tried ritual sacrifice of Uratha and Wolf-Blooded to empower the dead bones of Wolf, but with little success. Recently, however, several Gravedigger scholars have begun experimenting with burying victims alive or using other funerary-rites as execution tools; the experiments are still ongoing, but these methods seem to provide some positive results.
    • If a Lodge member falls unconscious due to suffocation or drowning, Father’s Howl can extend its perception through the cultist and can take direct control of the comatose werewolf, its howl filling their lungs with cold air as it puppets them.
    • The motherlode the Lodge seeks is, of course, the actual site of Mother Wolf’s death. The problem is that this is almost certainly buried somewhere in the Gauntlet, as far as the Cult can tell, although they’re still unsure why this is the case; there must be a physical or spiritual location in the world that Wolf fell, but the echoes of the Sundering are carried most strongly through the Gauntlet rather than in the twin worlds it splits apart. Some Cultists attempt to enslave Beshilu with the intention of having them gnaw into the Gauntlet and uncover the gravesite itself.
    • Cult ritemasters are prolific fetish creators. A number of Pure totems who have opposed the Cult in the past have disappeared, abducted and imprisoned into Gravedigger fetishes that have sometimes then even been used to buy influence with the oblivious Pure who the totems once lorded over.
    • Experiments continue with amalgamating Uratha and ancient god-fragments. These are largely conducted in deep temple-earthworks the Lodge maintains, far from the sight of other Pure who might deem such spiritual unions as blasphemous. A carnival of horrors slithers and capers through the vaults of the Cult.
    • Gravediggers can give brief resuscitation to freshly dead bodies by pressing their mouths to those of the dead and howling their breath into the cold lungs of the slain. This inflicts the Exhausted Condition on the Gravedigger, but reanimates the carcass for one day; it will obey the Gravedigger’s commands, but possesses some rudimentary, burned-out level of sentience from its life, and if left alone will act according to those instincts. The breath in their lungs allows these dead to speak; the Gravedigger who reanimated the corpse can speak through it with their own voice remotely, if they wish.
    Tools

    The Cult of Bones has access to the Lodge Connections, Lodge Lorehouse and Lodge Stronghold merits.

    Bonesplinter Talisman (Fetish ooo)

    Digging up the dead risks the wrath of restless shades, but the clattering bone fragments of this talisman ward the bearer and turn such malignancy into another revelation.

    Effect: The bearer gains a +2 bonus to resist or contest the Numina and Influences of ghosts; when targeted by such powers, the bearer also gains the clear impression of the ghost’s name – if it had one – and the date and era of its death.

    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
