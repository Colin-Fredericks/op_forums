            Hee hee hee gundressed. Ahem.

            It’s the day before the big dance! When a rogue statue is bringing Spirits into Paris, it seems a good time to bring our fledgling Werewolf along to hunt the Spirits and their source. But then Dearbháil finds herself reminded of struggles from a home left far away.

            Marek and Alla’s players unexpected had to miss at the last moment due to circumstances outside their control. However, the group remained together most of the session, and Lizette covered the events more thoroughly than usual, maintaining her focus despite distraction. Mostly. Some might find things clearer if the read Tatiana and Lizette in reverse order.

            Session XLIIII

            Tatiana’s Conversations

            What? No, volchonok, Marie is not a very tall human pup.

            Most human women are not taught to fight. She did very well, considering. She even hurt that spirit drowning Wolfram.

            Liselle was not there to learn to be a proper Uratha, so it does not matter how well she did.

            Marek was involved, speaking with the local spirits. This was supposed to be a long shot but good practice hunting, we did not expect fighting. So it seemed safe enough.

            Yes volchonok, we thought it was a long shot because we thought of asking Ocranny far later than we ought have. I’ve already apologized to him, leave it be.

            Yes, he is why we were able to succeed before Marek, once we brought him to the right place.

            Well, the statue was very short, so it could not move far. Wolfram thought of this.

            Murder spirits would likely be disinclined to carry. I do not know how the large head walking on its hair would carry it anyway. The drowned woman of the murderous flappers would be more capable, but still... carrying is not murder.

            Lizette has appeased it and I am certain they will talk the vampire into selling it, so all is well there.

            The red haired woman? She will probably not try to buy it. She had nothing to do with the statue, she just resonated with murder.

            I do not know precisely what she intended, but her backpack smelled like Lizette before she blows things up.

            Something about Ireland. That’s what she and Dearbháil spoke of earlier. The red haired woman was trying to get Dearbháil to help incite rebellion. Whoever she intended to kill, it made our songbird very angry.

            She was yelling in Irish. There is no human tongue like there is First Tongue. And at the cafe it just seemed complicated. I do remember she said the woman was acting like Therese, therefore foolish.

            Yes, volchonok, also I was distracted by Liselle’s patting my head. When I am in Urhan, sometimes I am as foolish as a volchonok. The treat Wolfram got for me was also distracting. I should remember, it is much easier to get raw meat when you are a wolf.

            Yes, it is also much easier to get raw meat when you catch it yourself. But I cannot run now - It is Bastille Day and Elena is expecting me. We will run later.


            Lizette’s Plans

            14th July 1924

            I am getting ready for the dance, and resting while Marlene gets ready to assist me. It’s been a long day, but today has gone better than yesterday. That is, I confess, a low bar. The dance will be the final determining factor, of course.

            So last night while I was laid out on the couch waiting for Dearbháil, Nicolette came in with some aspirin and prepared me a strong alcoholic drink to help me with the pain until Dearbháil arrived. I was I admit mildly annoyed when Dearbháil dismissed her in a brusque manner, but this was eclipsed by how angry Dacian made me. Nicolette left, as requested, and left the drink she was preparing me, so Dacian grabbed it and drank it himself. He is quite an impudent ass. I realize I have a ways to go before I can see myself as a proper lady in any environment, but at least I can console myself I am nowhere near as boorish as Dacian. This may even trump the leering thing.

            Dearbháil, unlike Dacian, set things right. She healed me and made sure I was all right. Wolfram fussed over me the rest of the evening. He is such a dear. I felt better as I fell asleep. Dearbháil finished healing me at breakfast. I can’t be wounded for the dance, now can I?

            Breakfast went well, as Dacian did not try to steal my drink while I was not badly wounded. Probably wise. Tatiana had the idea of getting Marie to go with us to track the statue to help her learn, and bring Ocranny to help us find it. Much better than my idea of looking off the top of the Eiffel Tower through binoculars, but at the same time less fun. I suggested as I was going to the Bellerose residence they could come with me. Marie is a friend of Liselle so I supposed Liselle might know where she was. Also, I had settled on asking Clara to the dance, and it is Bastille Day and the Olympics are going on. So Marie could be anywhere.

            Maybe we could have used a plane? We could have had bombs in case we saw it! Ah but it would not have worked anyway.

            We got there, and Clara greeted us. I spoke to her about the dance. She agreed, though I don’t think she quite gets the implications of my interest or invitation. It will be fun regardless. Presuming she does not pick me up. Or steal my drink.

            Liselle was happy to go with us to meet Marie, who was meeting her near the Champs-Élysées. We set out on foot, as Paris in the midst of gay celebration was rather difficult to navigate by motorcar (but probably not autosaw. Mayhem!) We walked while she tried to suss out our plans and we gave her a basic explanation. We stopped for cotton candy (which Liselle, Tatiana and I enjoyed, the others avoided), and Dearbháil had a fateful conversation with a woman named Caoimhe.

            Christina still likes to call cotton candy fairy floss, like they did when I was a kid. Probably a reason for that. But I am getting off track again.

            Caoimhe was an attractive Irish redhead. A Spring Court Fairest. She seemed to try to motivate Dearbháil with talk of revolution, then moved on. Dearbháil seemed to make a point to ask The Queen of Flowers and Lights about her, but she did not seem important at the time.

            We met Marie, and discussed the situation. Once we set off though the celebratory Bastille Day crowd, with Tatiana and Marie in wolf form, following Ocranny (with Wolfram, who had merged with his Geist so he could see Ocranny too.) Ocranny was using a power to seek murderous energy, and it led us across the Champs-Élysées down toward the stadium. On the way we saw Caoimhe again; Tatiana, Marie, and apparently Ocranny made a direct line for her, and Dearbháil whispered something to Dacian about her backpack. He declined to try to take it, but then it seemed a very difficult lift, as Caoimhe and Tatiana had a lot of attention. Tatiana was keeping Caoimhe away from the stadium (not that we were too close) and about to make a move for the pack herself when Dearbháil stepped in.

            The two argued, but they argued in Irish, so I could not really follow the conversation. I caught an “Erin go Braugh” in the chatter from Caoimhe. Tatiana grabbed the bag with her mouth as they talked and brought it to me, and I found a quite frankly impressive rigging of explosives. I neutralized them. Caoimhe stormed off, and Dearbháil wanted to go to a nearby café. She wanted Irish whiskey, and this being the first time Ireland as a nation was in the Olympics, got it. Caoimhe was with a group called the Irish Republican Army, and was going to kill the British Olympic Team while there. I saw the bomb, a well-done piece, and she would have taken them out while not destroying the stadium. Not the usual anarchist garbage as likely to blow up the user as anyone else. Dearbháil intends to make sure she speaks to The Queen of Flowers and Lights. That seems fair.

            Caoimhe had lost her family to the British, and sought to get blood revenge. I understood. I lost my family too. Like her, even my Fetch died. But no amount of dead Germans brought them back. Maybe I can talk to her. I can’t exactly say I didn’t kill a ton of Germans before that happened though.

            Mayhem!

            Anyway, Tatiana kept getting head scratches from Liselle. They got some meat treats from a vendor. It was kind of adorable. Marie for her part was disinterested in head scratches. This kept up through the hunting. I mean, it is fair, I like it when Christina scratches me behind the ear. But that is not quite the same thing. I don’t think, I mean I would not want Liselle to scratch me behind the ear. It was not weird when Liselle scratched Tatiana’s head despite me making it weird here.

            I wonder if a mechanical head scritcher would work. Four arms, scratch four heads at once. Keep four cats, or dogs, or werewolves happy. Now that could be a home defense device, scratching faces off or grabbing people. You know, if it could grab people and ignite, maybe it could set them on fire. Or throw them, on fire, at other people. Put it on wheels and send it in.

            Mayhem!

            We decided to try closer to the Seine, since we might have better luck there. It turned out that was right. Ocranny lead us to a flophouse, which looked pretty run down. Wolfram used his powers to haunt it while we waited and Tatiana got more scratches, and found a room had been turned into an abattoir, with several bloody bodies and two foreign men, I think Italian, with knives. They were clearly influenced by the statue.

            We decided on frontal assault. Dearbháil blessed us and stayed to watch Liselle. The crowds were much thinner on the docks, and much less friendly. Which is good, for all Liselle’s bravado she is no adventuress.

            I used her magic to help bless my Thompson, and charged in it in hand and chainsaw on my back. Tatiana was in the lead. We made it to the room, where I gunned down one of the men and she mauled the other. Then two Spirits, a Murder Spirit and some head Spirit, materialized, and a drowned woman Spirit joined in quickly. The latter two went after Wolfram. Wolfram and Tatiana put down the Murder Spirit as the drowned spirit tried to suffocate Wolfram. Because of the melee, I switched to chainsaw (despite it not being awakened) and made short work of it, and we whittled down the head. A second Murder Sprit appeared and was cut down.

            Dacian found the statue, and he and Wolfram figured out it was summoning the Sprits as blood had spilled on it and started a ritual. It took a human sacrifice to end. Tatiana and I had just killed, and could use that as the specifications were vague how it was done, as Dacian understood it. It was still summoning Spirits, so we had to close the ritual. Tatiana is best at fighting, but hopeless at Occult ritual. I only knew the basics, but with some Autumn Court training and Wolfram’s assistance I set to it. One Murder Spirit arrived, which was dispatched by the others, and then we finished the ritual.

            We left along with the fleeing tenants, and met up with Dearbháil and Liselle. Liselle reminded me that I was taking her sister out, and chided me to get ready, just as she had to go get ready herself.

            So here I am. Getting some help getting ready from Christina and Marlene. Then off to pick up Clara Bellerose in my motorcar.

            Hopefully, it will be a fun night. I plan to dance until I can’t walk, and just maybe expand Miss Clara Bellerose’s horizons a little.

            Sexy Mayhem!

            End Session XLIII

            Ocranny the Totem spirit could look for a certain resonance, one of its powers. We actually realized this going over options at the start of the session. That plus the fact we decided with two missing to put off the dance for next session and cover other events, lead me to have the PCs meet Caoimhe. Originally, she was supposed to be met at the dance, and the follow up scene (and Dearbháil having to decide whether to support or turn on her) was to take place the next day. I think the change of plans went well, and Ocranny’s power revelation made the progression with Caoimhe even more natural than it otherwise would have been.

            Dearbháil had long had a dislike for Englishmen and a strong feeling for her homeland, so I thought it would be interesting to see whether she was willing to go toward the dark side with that or not by setting her up with a very dangerous potential friend.

            Wolfram used Boneyard at the flophouse.

            The statue required a Presence + Occult roll to placate. As Tatiana and Lizette had gone first and killed the two humans, it had to be one or both. Lizette can’t reroll 10s, but has a healthy pool, and I let her apply her benifet to magic occult rolls from her Autumn Mantle to the ritual. So it worked out fairly quick.

            Just about to the end of the first part of this story. Next session is the ball, and after one session after that we will begin the next leg start of the New Year with a slightly changed cast. Comments and Questions welcome.

                The night of the big dance. Everyone dressed to the nines, either enjoying some traditional dancing in one room or Charleston in the other. Here are a couple accounts of the dance.

                Note Tatiana went to a village dance, not the one everyone else was at. So her journal does not touch on what everyone else was doing.

                Session XLIV

                Marek’s Thoughts

                15 July 1924

                Tonight was the big dance. I asked Dearbháil if she would like to go with me and she accepted. Marlene was escorted by Dacian, Christina was escorted by Wolfram, Lizette escorted Clara and Alla was escorted by Fenric. The ladies all looked lovely. Most of them had dresses made by Lizette, so they were quite good. It seems that my friend is just as good at making mundane things like clothes, as she is at making implements of destruction, usually on fire.

                As soon as we arrived at the dance Dearbháil headed straight for the Queen of Flowers and Light to talk to her about someone that she’d come across earlier. Her name is Kiva (not spelled like that). Kiva is an Irish compatriot that apparently had made a bomb and was planning on making it into the Olympic games and setting it off near the British team. Her intention had been to kill the British athletes. The others caught her and divested her of the bomb before she could actually do anything, so that was good. Dearbháil had made it her aim to talk to the Spring Queen when we arrived at the dance because the would be bomber is a threat to the Fae folk in Paris.

                While Dearbháil was taking care of her business I took care of some of my own. I noticed that Marie was at the dance, as was Marcel, so I decided to introduce the two to one another. I figured that Marcel could lend Marie aid and training should the need arise, and Marie would have someone local to talk to about Uratha related matters. So, I introduced them and we talked for a little bit. When I noticed that Dearbháil was done with her talk with the Queen of Flowers and Light I excused myself. Dearbháil made her way over to me and we danced. She dances as nicely as she sings. I by comparison was not all that good, but we made a good couple on the dance floor. I am not normally fond of such events, but this one was interesting and engaging. I certainly enjoyed dancing with my Fae lady.

                After a while someone announced that there would be a Tango. I am somewhat familiar with the dance so I asked Dearbháil if she would like to dance it with me. She agreed and we did our best. I think we did pretty well all things considered. Fenric danced with Lizette, Wolfram danced with Christina and Dacian danced with the Queen of Flowers and Light. They were particularly good and made a very good pair on the dance floor. Most of the attention was on them so I didn’t feel quite so self-conscious.

                Once the Tango was over Dearbháil decided that she wanted to socialize with some different people, so I took the initiative and asked Alla if she would like to dance. I think she was a little surprised, but agreed. It wasn’t the same as dancing with Dearbháil, but it was still nice.

                Meanwhile Wolfram had met someone while at the dance. She is a singer and her stage name is Amethyst. They talked for a while before walking over to talk to Dearbháil. I did not meet this new singer, but Dearbháil seemed to get along with her well. They talked about their profession for a while as far as I could tell. Alla also talked to her sister for a while. They seem to get along, but some of the smiles seemed a bit forced, so I’m not so certain that they are that close to one another.

                Later I decided to talk to Jamal, who I was surprised to see at the dance. We caught up for a while, talking business for the most part. He mentioned that he had talked to Alla. He said that she had invited him to the Chateau so we could all discuss the Golden Eagle with him. It seemed like a good idea to me. We have not had much contact with Jamal lately and we had agreed to give him our thoughts and findings on the device. We have also discovered other artifacts that seem to be of similar make, so it seemed prudent to mention those statues to him as well. Granted those statues caused a good deal of trouble for us, but they were of a similar design to the Eagle.

                All told I had a good time at the dance. I am not usually a social person except with those that I know. I am usually wary about meeting strangers, just because I’m a cautious individual, I always assume that there is more to them that they say or show. Some might call me paranoid, but I prefer to call myself a realist. There are a lot of dangerous people in the world, and I make it my business to protect my pack and my family from them if needs be. This dance however seemed to have a much lighter air to it. Everyone seemed to get along well. Marlene met several potential suitors. Wolfram made a new friend. Given that he just went up and introduced himself to her I will guess that she is Claimed like he is. The only real serious business that I saw was Dearbháil’s conversation with the Queen of Flowers and Light. Overall it was an enjoyable evening.


                Titania’s Conversations

                “Ivan arrived as the wedding feast was already in progress. Princess Elena saw Ivan and jumped up from her seat at the table. ‘There is my dear fiance, not this coward sitting next to me!’”

                Elena cleared her throat. Tatiana looked over at her and saw that a couple of the other parents were standing with her beloved, hiding their amusement with varying levels of success. She looked at the many children surrounding her, a couple asleep in each other’s arms, but most hanging on her every word and getting restless at her pause. Tatiana winked at Elena and continued, “The tsar, confused at this turn of events, asked for an explanation and the princess gladly told him everything. The tsar, obviously, was furious with the brothers Dmitriy and Vasiliy and threw them in prison. Ivan and Elena got married themselves and were so happy with each other that they never parted.”

                “Tell the story of the frog princess!” “No, tell the story of Marya!” “I want to hear about the princess who never smiled!” The children clamored as soon as she gave them the small flourish she traditionally ended her stories with.

                “I’m afraid not, children. It is late, and time for the young ones to go home.” Elena raised an eyebrow at Tatiana.

                Tatiana stood and clapped her hands. “Elena is right. Come now, there will be another time for stories.” She ushered them along.

                As they returned to the square, Tatiana stole many glances, imagining her in the red bell-shaped dress Lizette had made for herself, or with the Irish green shawl Dearbháil had worn and herself in Marek’s warm brown suit. As she imagined it, her mental image twisted a little so that she was wearing Dacien’s bright green pocket square and bowtie and Elena was in Alla’s fabulous sparkling dress. Elena glanced over as Tatiana snorted aloud. “What are you thinking of? Imagining all of the more beautiful women you could be dancing with in Paris?”

                “Ah, my love, I am but a poor soldier, from a long line of humble Russian country folk. When even the best artists of Paris could imagine nothing more beautiful than you, how could I hope to?” Tatiana pulled her close, and kissed her forehead. Elena rolled her eyes but leaned into her embrace.

                The children put to bed, the adults continued with their celebration for a time, with more dancing, singing and drinking. Tatiana had had perhaps a bit more than she ought have when Elena found her sharing war stories with some of the Greek refugees. “Tatiana? Will you walk me home?”

                Tatiana jumped to her feet, having to steady herself on the shoulder of one of the men nearby. “Of course! Is it that late?”

                “It is, quite.” Elena gave her a small smile.

                Tatiana offered her an arm and she took it. They walked together in quiet companionship, content in each other’s presence. When they reached Elena’s small house, Tatiana opened the door for her love with an excessive flourish and followed her in. When the door shut, she wrapped her arms around Elena from behind, pulling her close and burying her face in the shorter woman’s neck. “I can stay here tonight, yes?”

                Elena’s kiss, after she turned around, was answer enough.


                Lizette’s Plans

                15th July 1924

                Well, last night went incredibly well.

                I finally let everyone see my red dress I had been working on for some time. I considered something more butch, but I would get a lot less leeway to dance with other ladies if it looked more than playful. Marlene and Christina’s dresses were also gorgeous and Wolfram’s suit was top notch. When all was ready, I jumped in my car and drove out to get Clara Bellerose.

                Clara was my date. Wolfram was taking Christina, Dacian taking Marlene, Marek taking Dearbháil, Fenric taking Alla, and Tatiana was going herself to something smaller in Chantilly. There must be someone pretty interesting there to distract her from Madeline.

                Clara was wearing blue and looked enchanting. She could not stop giggling. I complemented her and she giggled more. We made our way to the hotel, and there were a lot of people there. My dazzling red and gold jewelry was self-defense in part; I needed to be visible with all those tall people on the floor.

                Stilts are of course right out; I mean I have to hold them. Also, I am likely to start stepping on feet. But if I could make a special exoskeleton, that might work. With optional flamethrowers in case you and your dance partner are attacked. Or maybe a buzzsaw launcher, to prevent accidentally setting people on fire on the dance floor. I mean unless you want to set everyone on fire on the dance floor; I am not judging.

                Mayhem!

                Wait, actually I should be judging. Maybe at least a tray that can pop out to offer tea?

                Focus.

                I went to the modern dance area with Clara, and we starting cutting the rug. She loves to dance and was adorable. She kept giggling, though. I will be honest, I was a bit worried that she was laughing at me, but no, it was because she found the situation hilarious. That…was not quite what I wanted to hear either.

                I had a dance with Christina (perfect grace) and Wolfram (impeccable style) on the modern and classic dance floors respectively. Wolfram even danced with Miss Skitters once. I did also dance with a shy and blushing Nicolette. I would have danced with Marlene, but she was attending to the important job of meeting potential suitors. I don’t blame her.

                I just finished dancing with Lucille Nadeau, from the Salon, when the Tango call went out. She was sporting a more butch chic. Always a hanger on, not really an artist, but her whispered reputation with the ladies was excellent. Anyway, they called for Tango, in the main room, and when the music started, we moved in. I love the Tango but am too short. Fenric however offered, and in a moment of weakness I agreed. Honestly, to be fair to him, he did a good job. He is built a bit different, with shorter limbs while I am a miniature woman, but we made it work well. Dacian and the Queen of Flowers and Lights, however, did a monstrously good job. Even I have to admit it. Impressive as Fenric and I were, they did excellently.

                I went to do the Charleston after that with Clara, and made a point of dancing with Wolfram, Christina and Lucille again. Even snuck in a dance with Dr. Hinshaw, who was fun to see again. He has done well. Not following Dana around the world well, but well.

                Eventually the evening wound down. I danced one last time with Clara, the only one I danced with three times. I took her home, it was sweet, she was giggly again. I thanked her for the good time, and gave her a kiss. That stopped the giggling, but not the smiling. She invited me inside, but we just shared some coca. She decided she had some thinking to do, and I was not about to put any sort of pressure on her. I think she felt flattered, when she realized my interest. I don’t know how things might shake out, but I will at least let her consider it. She is a friend, and we can see where things go.

                I planned to go to bed, being rather exhausted. That plan was foiled when Christina, in her nightgown, found me soon after I had walked in. I spent the rest of the night in the Klingemann room. Wolfram Christina and I had rather a good time.

                Now after all that walking and dancing and other stuff, if I can manage to actually make everything move properly I will get back to work again.

                End Session XLIV

                So, the dance was a large scale affair. Women could dance with each other for some of the dances, but not anything slow or intimate. There was a room for traditional dancing, one for modern, and a big sort of meeting and talking room.

                There were a number of socialize rolls to dance. A big one was when we had the Tango. If you are not familiar, Tango was scandalously daring, even at the time. The Queen of Flowers and Lights made a huge number of successes, as she is really rather good at the sorts of rolls called for. She was assisting Dacian, so he got a ton of extra dice, and they outperformed every other couple by a notable measure.

                That mostly covers things. It was a fairly long social scene. Next session will wind up this leg of the chapter, then we shift gears to the train trip. It should be shorter, then back to normal.

                    It was the closing session of this leg of The Golden Eagle. Mostly a social session, we had a few IC conversations after a group dinner. Marek’s player was out of town (this was day before Christmas Eve) and the last Dacian and Dearbháil session (in the short term, at least). We have a Tatiana and Lizette journal…Lizette’s is a story but it pretty well covers what happened in the session. You may want to read in reverse order.

                    Session XLV

                    Tatiana’s Conversations

                    Of course we are going to help Constancia, volchonok.

                    Because she is a friend. And the Olympics are getting boring.

                    I have told you not to talk of Elena that way, volchonok. No, I am not getting bored of Elena. And no, I am fleeing nothing!

                    Are you going to be civil now?

                    Not impertinent, I mean.

                    Do not sulk. I have told you... oh I see.

                    Yes, volchonok, we will be going by train. It will not be so bad, only 4, 5 days. Of course, then we will be on a boat.

                    It is far away, this Cyprus. Would you prefer we flew on one of Lizette’s planes?

                    No, the songbird cannot fly on her own. And she will not be coming.

                    I know, volchonok, this trip is the worst of all things for foolish volchonoks. But she and Dacian are having problems with the Eagle, Ruhadze says so. He wishes to make certain they are not in danger.

                    He will take good care of them or he will answer to us.

                    He thinks it is because Changelings are slippery. Lizette does much with machines, so maybe it holds faster to her? It made sense to the others.

                    I do not know when they will rejoin us.

                    I do not suppose they will want to go for a goodbye run. There are preparations to make, for all of us. There will be plenty of time to run along the train tracks.


                    Lizette’s Plans

                    The Help

                    Lizette Étoile entered spare room where her Motley’s Girl Friday, Nicholette, was finishing a hot drink before retiring for bed. She looked up when the Gremlin entered.

                            “And it will do you no good, if your Horror is not well-fed,” Azrael spoke, his words sounding as though they had been engraved upon the stone lid of a sarcophagus. “The Clay Boy is a part of you, but it is not governed by reason or inhibitions. It hungers, and when that hunger becomes too great, it will feed, whether you wish it or no.”

                            “He goes off on his own sometimes, when I sleep,” Laszlo admitted. “I have tried to stop him, but I can’t. He enters the dreams of those around me and devours their dream-selves. They always wake up terrified. If I look at customers in the bakery the next day, sometimes I can tell who he has visited.”

                            Azrael’s demeanor became stern. “You must not allow this to happen again,” he said sharply, and the dark feathers of his wings gave a dangerous rustle. “Once or twice is harmless enough, but if the Clay Boy visits the same dreams every night, it will take a toll upon the sleepers’ minds. And once he finds an easy hunting ground, he will revisit it again and again…”

                            “You think I don’t know that?” Laszlo shouted indignantly. “You think I want to be responsible for their nightmares? I didn’t ask for this…”

                            “No more than you asked to be a Polnischen Juden,” Azrael responded angrily. “No more than you asked to be small, or thin. No more than you asked to have dark hair. Would you complain about these things? Complaining about the Clay Boy is just as useful.”

                            “They don’t deserve to be hunted like that,” Laszlo roared, shaking the foundation of his Lair.

                            “Then find those who do!” Azrael responded, and all around the two of them the torches flared to brilliance, lighting the darkened passageways and bathing them both in an intense heat (though frost still bloomed on the windows). “Take control of your life once more, my nephew. If you worry about how good people will suffer at the hands of your Horror, then make it feast upon bad people instead! Hunt them deliberately, before the Clay Boy takes matters into its own hands!”

                            Laszlo reared back as though slapped. “I… I could do that?” he asked meekly. “But… the Clay Boy does not care who he devours…”

                            “But you do,” Azrael responded more gently. “Laszlo, the Clay Boy is powerful… it has the potential to be more powerful than any of the other Children I have met. But power without determination is useless. Left to its own devices it will feast wherever it can, and that sort of behavior has the tendency to attract… unwanted attention. But if you can give it a purpose, a reason to hunt, then you can help to curb its less savory appetites. My own Horror was not always as you know it. When it first awakened, it was simply death incarnate, mowing down the good and the evil alike, indiscriminately.”

                            “But… Azrael is the angel of death,” Laszlo supplied.

                            “Indeed, in recent years, Azrael has become identified with malakh ha-mavet, the Angel of Death,” Mateusz agreed, “but in the past this was not always so. The oldest Talmudic scholars argued that no being had command over life and death except Yahweh Himself. To their minds, Azrael was simply His instrument. In fact, the name Azrael means ‘help from God,’ or ‘godsend.’ This is how I choose to manifest my Horror… as one who helps his community.”

                            Laszlo thought about this for a moment. “They say that war is coming,” he said uncertainly. “I could try to enlist, and turn my Horror against the enemy…”

                            Azrael shook his head. “A small pale Jew who has yet to see his fifteenth birthday? The army will never be so desperate for recruits,” he said with disdain.

                            “Then what?” Laszlo asked, frustrated once more. “How else can I direct the actions of a ravenous monster?”

                            Azrael thought for a moment. “The Clay Boy is a Russian fairy story,” he mused, “but it is far from unique in lore. What other examples of clay men come to mind?”

                            Laszlo pondered the question. “Ever the rabbi,” he muttered to himself. “They say that Yahweh molded Adam from clay in the Garden,” he said eventually.

                            “Indeed,” Azrael agreed. “But I was thinking of something a bit more recent. Say… three centuries ago, in Prague…”

                            Laszlo’s eyes widened as he remembered the accounts he had read in his uncle’s books. “The Golem?” he asked in awed tones. “The great Golem of Rabbi Loew?”

                            “Indeed,” Azrael nodded. “By all accounts, a great friend to the Jews of Prague, and a terror to those who would victimize them.”

                            “But the Golem turned upon its creators eventually,” Laszlo replied.

                            Azrael chuckled. “Only when the rituals controlling it were abandoned,” he countered. “Only when the holy rites animating it were ignored. Only when those who made use of it grew prideful, and neglected to show the proper respect to Yahweh.” He paused for a moment to let that sink in. “In a way, the Clay Boy’s story is similar… one might say that Yahweh sent him to punish the baker and his wife, who had asked a witch for help rather than accepting Yahweh’s judgment that they should be childless.”

                            “So I could… hunt those who sin against Yahweh?” Laszlo asked timidly.

                            Azrael laughed. “That would be casting a wide net indeed, my nephew,” he said with good humor. “But there are many in this city who would take advantage of the Chosen People. We are not well-liked. Surely you would not object to teaching those who would prey upon us a lesson or two, eh?”

                            Laszlo nodded. “You really think I could turn the Clay Boy into the Golem?” he asked.

                            Azrael thought seriously about the question for a moment. “All that is lacking is fire,” he muttered to himself. “If he was fired, the Clay Boy would be the very image of the Golem. He would harden into the shape you desire… and it would make him stronger and tougher as well.”

                            “But how are we to fire him? There is no kiln in my Lair,” Laszlo said.

                            “Leave the fire to me,” Azrael breathed tonelessly. A moment later, he opened Laszlo’s lair to his own, and there was fire in abundance.

                            * * * * *

                            “Come on, Laszlo, I’m just asking you to look after the counter for a few hours tonight. Is that too much of a favor to grant your precious sister?” Łucja asked sweetly.

                            Laszlo rolled his eyes at the seventeen-year-old’s wheedling. “Tonight is my night off, Łucja. I only get one of those each week, aside from the Sabbath. I already have plans.”

                            “Is this because Andrezj and I have started seeing each other, Laszlo?” she said, growing more serious. “I know he’s your best friend, and…” She broke off and glanced around the empty bakery, making certain that Mrs. Szimański had indeed vacated the premises before continuing. “I know that you wanted him to be more than a friend,” she finished in a whisper. “I don’t want to do anything to hurt you, brother…”

                            Laszlo shook his head. “I realized years ago that Andrezj is… not like me. He is my best friend, Łucja, and I would not throw that away for a hopeless fantasy. You make him happy, and any fool can see that he does the same for you.”

                            She pouted adorably. “Then why don’t you want me to go out and have a good time tonight?” she asked with a hint of petulance.

                            He chuckled in response. “I have no objection to you enjoying your evening, sister. But I have plans tonight.”

                            She wrinkled her nose. “The Hedge Maze again?” she asked with distaste. “Laszlo, I know that club caters to people with your… tastes, and I’m glad you’re getting out and meeting people. But there’s something off about that place. It makes me uncomfortable.”

                            Laszlo sighed, thinking about the evenings he had spent in Prince Sascha’s club among the changelings of Berlin. They had opened his mind to so many things. He sometimes wished he could let Łucja see the club’s denizens as he saw them. While it was true that many of them had alarming or frightening appearances beneath the concealing magic they called the Mask, there were others of such beauty…

                            “Not that it’s any of your business, little sister,” he said sharply to dissuade further questions, “but I’m not going to the Hedge Maze tonight. I. Have. Other. Plans.”

                            Color rose in her cheeks, whether from embarrassment or anger he couldn’t immediately say. Łucja’s skin was pale, like his own, but while he merely appeared pallid and unhealthy, her face had a porcelain glow that set off the blue of her eyes and the dark gold of her hair perfectly. Attractive at the best of times, the flush in her cheeks made her beautiful.

                            “I see,” she said softly, almost sadly. “This is about whatever you and Uncle Mateusz talk about behind closed doors, isn’t it?”

                            Laszlo started, taken aback. He had no idea that Łucja knew anything about his condition. “Yes,” he said after a moment. He hated lying to her.

                            “We used to be so close, Laszlo,” she went on as she sliced a loaf into thin segments. “You are welcome to your secrets, of course…
                            everyone has them. But it makes me sad that you refuse to share something so important with me.”

                            He walked toward her and put his arms around her shoulders. Leaning in, he kissed her softly on the forehead. “I am sorry, Łucja,” he said as they embraced. “I truly think that it is best you know as little about Uncle Mateusz and I as possible. I think we would lose something if you knew. Do you trust me?”

                            She nodded. “I trust you with my life, Laszlo. And I will always love you, no matter what you think you must hide from me. You are my brother.” When he released her from his embrace, she returned to slicing the bread morosely as she continued speaking.

                            “I think I might know a little of what you won’t tell me already,” she said, her voice regaining a hint of its typical mischief. “I’m neither blind nor stupid, no matter what people might say about me in the neighborhood. I know that you did something to Lukas Fischer two years ago, after he tried to… hurt me,” she ended with a grimace.

                            Laszlo said nothing, inwardly seething as he did every time he thought of that schweinhund Fischer. That anyone calling himself a man would try to force himself upon a young girl – his sister, no less – brought the rage as nothing else did. He had called upon the Golem’s strength and frightened the life out of Fischer, chasing him through the alleys, punching through brick and concrete walls, and tossing heavy garbage bins around like skittles. In the end, cornered, Fischer had cowered against a wall, certain that he was going to die, but Laszlo had spared his miserable life. It had been a near thing, and Fischer’s dreams had been troubled by nightmares for weeks to come without any help from Laszlo.

                            “Lukas was afraid of you,” Łucja said with wonder, “and he’s not the only one.” She looked at him critically, barely an inch taller than she was herself, slight of build, pale, jug-eared, and altogether forgettable. “I mean no disrespect, brother, but why would anyone be afraid of you?”

                            He leaned in once more and kissed her, this time standing on his toes to reach the top of her head. “All you need to know, Łucja, is that you need never fear me,” he said softly. “And you need never fear anyone else while I am around to protect you.”

                            And in the back of his head, the Golem stirred restlessly. Down the block, the owner of an apartment building had been forcing his Jewish tenants out to increase the value of the property so that he could sell it. Many of those tenants had nowhere else to go. Tonight, he would see the error of his ways. Tonight, the Golem would hunt.

                            * * * * *

                            The blood pounded in Laszlo’s ears as he followed the burly man through the streets, always half a block behind, never too close, but never out of sight, either. Ernst Gerhardt was drunk; this much was clear to anyone who came within two yards of him… but even taking that into consideration, it had taken him much longer than anticipated to spot Laszlo.

                            Laszlo had rarely been so angry. Dale Müller could be a bit full of himself from time to time; a bit proud of his own success, but he was a good man for all of that. His wife Elsbeth had been a kind and soft-spoken woman; she had always taken the time to pass a few words with Laszlo’s mother when she had visited the bakery, and had often left coins in the tip jar on her way out. Their daughter, Gerda, had been a joy to know. She could brighten a room with her smile and had never spoken a harsh word within Laszlo’s hearing.

                            And this khnyok Gerhardt had killed them. And why? Because Herr Müller had succeeded in a business where Gerhardt himself had failed? Because he had made a happy life for himself, with a beautiful wife and a loving daughter? No. It was because he was a Jew, and Jews stole the birthrights of proper German Christians. So Gerhardt had burned the Müllers’ house to cinders.

                            In a way, Herr Müller had been lucky… he had been down at the local pub, celebrating the closing of an important business deal with a few friends. But Elsbeth and little Gerda had been at home.

                            It had been nearly a week since the fire, and Herr Müller had collapsed into a shadow of the man he had once been. The people of the neighborhood pulled together to help take care of him, but it was whispered that if he didn’t rise from his deep depression soon, his business partners would start to desert him. Laszlo knew that the poor man could not care less about such things at the moment; the loss of his family was all he could contemplate. But Elsbeth and Gerda would not have wanted him to throw his life away for their sakes.

                            Tonight, Laszlo hoped to give Herr Müller peace of mind. Tonight, Laszlo intended to see that justice was done. Tonight, Laszlo would make sure that Gerhardt never victimized his people again.

                            He knew that Gerhardt was guilty. Everyone knew that Gerhardt was guilty, but Laszlo had gone the extra mile to make sure. After five days of stalking the man, he had baked a tray of the fruit-filled pastries locally known as berliners and had carried them to the gentlemen’s club Gerhardt patronized. Ordinarily, he would not have been allowed past the doors, but when he explained to the doorman that the pastries were a gift from a prospective member, he had been ushered quickly through the common room to the kitchens. Along the way, he had heard Ernst Gerhardt bragging to his cronies about how he had chased the uppity Juden out of their district… and about how at least two of them would never come back.

                            When Gerhardt staggered out of the club that evening, he was both alone and drunk. It was well past midnight, and even cosmopolitan Berlin had mostly shut down for the evening. The streets were sparsely populated, and those few pedestrians who did roam late chose to avoid the stumbling man with his boisterous singing. Laszlo remained at least a hundred feet behind Gerhardt at all times, waiting for his opportunity to strike.

                            The Golem was hungry. Laszlo had not allowed it to feed for quite some time, and its senses were sharp as knives. He could feel its clay muscles knotting beneath his skin; its strength pulsing in his veins. He would not be able to restrain the Golem for another night. There was a clear transgressor before it, and it wanted to mete out punishment.

                            When they approached the system of alleys which so resembled Laszlo’s Lair, Laszlo struck. Checking to see that there were no witnesses present, he tossed a large garbage can at Gerhardt’s back. It struck the drunk between the shoulders, sending him sprawling into the street. When Gerhardt turned angrily to confront his attacker, Laszlo casually pushed a streetlamp over, never pausing in his advance.

                            Gerhardt screamed shrilly and turned to flee into the alleys, no doubt hoping that he could lose himself in their labyrinthine twists and turns. Laszlo smiled to himself at the man’s mistake; he had spent half of his life navigating such mazes… first in his dreams and then in his Lair, and they had lost the power to confuse him. He ducked into the alleys in time to see Gerhardt streaking around a corner in front of him. He stomped his foot, creating deep cracks in the cobbled street, and lashed out with his arm at the railing of a dirty stoop, pulling several cast-iron bars free and tossing them aside.

                            Gerhardt’s fear was thick and acrid in the air now, and Laszlo inhaled it with relish. He was so intent upon his goal that it was perhaps unsurprising he did not notice the other’s approach until he was nearly upon them. It was obvious that he newcomer was not attempting to be stealthy; he cleared his throat while still more than ten yards away.

                            Laszlo turned quickly to regard his new companion, upset that he might have to postpone the hunt. The man was tall and thin, with finely chiseled features, bright eyes, and dark hair. The tell-tale points of his ears and teeth marked him as a werewolf in near-human form, but Laszlo would have recognized him as Kin even in his fully-human form. All Children of the Dark Mother called to him.

                            Laszlo paused in his pursuit, confident that he could catch Gerhardt at his leisure later. The werewolf complicated matters. The fact that he was a supernatural being meant that Laszlo did not need to hide his own gifts, but… according to Uncle Mateusz, the local packs were not friendly to their kind. This man, however, wore a slight smile and held his hands out in a pacifying way.

                            “I am sorry to interrupt,” the newcomer said in conversational tones. “Might I ask what your interest is in that man?” He indicated the alley down which Gerhardt had disappeared with a shake of his head, leaving his hands visible the whole time.

                            Laszlo considered deception, but he had never been much good at such things. He shrugged instead. “That shmuts killed a woman and a little girl last week. They were good people. I wish to teach him the error of his ways, so that he will not do so again.”

                            The werewolf nodded as though he had been expecting this answer. “Elsbeth Müller’s cousin Hans is a friend of mine. He asked me to take care of the man who has attacked his family.”

                            Laszlo considered this. “You wish to kill Herr Gerhardt?” he asked.

                            The werewolf shrugged. “I want him dead. I suppose it is a matter of professional pride.” His German was faintly accented, marking him as a foreigner. Not Polish… Czech, perhaps?

                            Laszlo’s instincts surged forward, and the Golem bellowed in his mind. It had to be fed. “I must finish my hunt,” he told the newcomer helplessly.

                            The werewolf nodded, understanding perfectly. “I would not interrupt another’s hunt,” he said mildly.

                            “I do not need to kill Herr Gerhardt,” Laszlo explained, grasping at straws. “I must teach him. But I would not weep if he was to die shortly after learning his lesson.”

                            The werewolf grinned, and his teeth gleamed in the moonlight. He shook his head toward the alley once more, his pointed ear twitching a bit. “He’s getting away,” he said softly.

                            Laszlo returned his grin in kind. “No,” he said simply, “he’s not.”

                            A few moments later the chase was over. Ernst Gerhardt cowered in a corner, having attempted to cover himself in the garbage strewn across the alley. Laszlo loomed over him, the Golem giving him stature greater than his five and a half feet. The werewolf was nowhere to be found, though Laszlo assumed he hadn’t wandered far.

                            “Why are you doing this?” Gerhardt shrieked, his voice several octaves above its normal range.

                            “You have to ask?” Laszlo said with no small amount of disgust.

                            “They were only a couple of Jews,” the man said, as though that explained everything.

                            “No,” Laszlo said quietly. “They were a woman and a six-year-old girl.” His fist shot out and created a crater in the dark bricks by the man’s head. “And you killed them for what? Because you were angry?” Punch. “Jealous?” Punch. “Lustful?” Punch. “Spiteful?” Punch. Fragments of brick rained down on Gerhardt’s head, and he whimpered like a child. The warm, wet smell of urine filled the air.

                            Laszlo forced down the rage and made himself look the spineless coward in the eyes. “Do you know what it is like to burn, Herr Gerhardt?” he asked in silky tones.

                            Gerhardt shook his head.

                            “Of course not,” Laszlo conceded. “It is a terrible way to die, Herr Gerhardt. There is the pain, of course. But there are so many other facets to death by fire that people never consider. There is the fact that after a while you cannot breathe for the heat. Not the smoke, mind you, but the heat. It presses in on you from all sides and squeezes the air out of your lungs. If you are lucky, your nerves will die before you feel the skin flaking and sloughing off your bones. Before your eyes burst. Before your blood begins to boil.”

                            Gerhardt whimpered and covered his face with his hands.

                            “No one should have to experience that, Herr Gerhardt,” Laszlo said softly, “but I think I will give you a taste of it anyway.” And with that he opened a pathway to his Lair. Ever since Uncle Mateusz had helped him to fire the Clay Boy into the Golem, a bit of Mateusz’ divine fire had taken root in Laszlo’s sanctum. Where before there had been only twisting streets and sucking mud, now colossal ovens loomed at every corner, resembling both baking ovens and potters’ kilns.

                            The wall to the right of Gerhardt seemed to burst outward and gouts of flame leapt out to cover him head to toe. He screamed and clawed at his face, rolling about in the muck and garbage, trying to regain some semblance of control. After a few seconds had passed, Laszlo allowed the oven door to close, and it became a brick wall once more.

                            “That was a small taste of what you did to a woman and a little girl,” Laszlo said, backing away slightly. “Keep in mind that it probably took Elsbeth and Gerda minutes to die. Think about that the next time you get angry, Herr Gerhardt.”

                            “I will,” the cowering man said, his charred hands quivering. “I’m sorry. I will never do such a thing again, I promise you… not even to a Jew!”

                            “Ah,” the soft voice of the werewolf came from the entrance to the alleyway, “so Ernst has learned his lesson, then?”

                            “Yes!” Herr Gerhardt almost shouted. “I am sorry… so sorry! I will never do anything of the sort ever again. I will never hurt anyone as long as I live. Please, you must believe me!”

                            “I do believe you,” the werewolf said as he stalked closer, “but I don’t care.” With that, he extended his lethal claws and swiped once at the murderer’s throat. Herr Gerhardt goggled at him, blood running down his chest and bursting from his mouth in thick bubbles. He gave one last despairing moan at the unfairness of it all and then pitched forward face-first into the garbage.

                            The werewolf turned to face Laszlo, a look of mild curiosity in his eyes. “You do not approve, my new friend?” he asked simply.

                            Laszlo shook his head. “It is difficult to argue that he did not deserve it,” he allowed. “I might have killed him myself had I come upon him alone… but I did not want to deprive a noble werewolf of his kill.”

                            The werewolf nodded, taking the appellation in stride, and then sniffed the air delicately. “Mostly garbage,” he said after a moment. “Burned garbage, and piss, and sweat, and… baking bread. What are you?”

                            Laszlo laughed at that. “I am a baker,” he said after a moment, “and a Jew, and one who does not like bullies and racists. But to answer your real question… we call ourselves the Children. We are the monsters out of nightmares, the things under the bed, the all-consuming krakens in the darkest trenches of the ocean. But we are also people, like everyone else.”

                            “Uratha are strong,” the werewolf said, “but I have never seen anyone do what you can do.”

                            “I am the physical manifestation of the Golem of Prague,” Laszlo explained, “and I can call upon great strength when I hunt. It is very difficult to hurt me, too… though I am not the warrior your own people are said to be. Not by half.”

                            The werewolf nodded and extended his hand. “It is good that your people have such a guardian to look after them,” he said as Laszlo shook it. “If you ever have need of me, contact a man named Hans Ausfeld. He is a local. Tell him that you have need of Reznik.”

                            “Władysław Piekarski,” Laszlo said, and then, “Laszlo. My family runs a bakery on Marianstraße. We live in the apartment above. Please stop by when you can. Even if you do not have need of the Golem, the kremówka are divine. And I will give you a discount,” he added with a wink.

                            * * * * *

                            The scent of freshly-baked pastries filled the air as steam rose from the tray. Laszlo pulled it from the oven bare-handed, since Mama was in the front of the shop seeing to the evening’s few customers. Łucja winced at this, though she had seen him do it numerous times before.
                            “I wish you wouldn’t do that in front of me, Laszlo,” she said with a shudder. “I don’t like being reminded of…”

                            “Of what I am, dear sister?” he asked teasingly. “I tried to protect you from the truth for many years, but you seemed incapable of letting it go. You were the one who wanted to know everything about me, remember?” He began placing the pastries on a serving plate one by one, and set the pan on a metal rack to cool.

                            She leaned in and kissed his cheek when he had finished. “Of course,” she said softly, “and you know that I still love you, dear brother. I just… I’m not used to thinking of you as someone who might be powerful and dangerous yet.”

                            Laszlo snorted in mock effrontery. “Well, thank you for the vote of confidence,” he said wryly.

                            Her laugh was musical, and everything was all right between them once more. “I’m going to go up front and let Mama know that the Pączek are nearly ready to serve. If it’s not too busy, she might let us go out tonight. Andrezj has a new phonograph record he wanted me to hear; I’m sure he wouldn’t mind if you came along as well.”

                            Laszlo nodded his assent, though privately he was not sure of this at all. Though he and Andrezj still maintained their friendship, he often thought that the other man would prefer to spend more time alone with his pretty young sister. Perhaps I’ll drop by the Hedge Maze, he thought to himself. It has been too long since I looked in on the local changelings.

                            He was wiping the sweat from his brow with a towel when he heard excited murmurs from the front of the store. A moment later, Łucja’s head appeared in the kitchen door. “Laszlo, you must come up front for a moment,” she said breathlessly.

                            He gestured at himself: sweaty, grimy, dusted in flour. “Papa doesn’t want me at the counter when I haven’t washed,” he reminded her.
                            She winked. “Just this once, it will be okay. Now hurry up!”

                            Mama’s voice called from the front of the store. “Yes, Laszlo, come on out. It will be all right,” she said.

                            Despite their entreaties, Laszlo took a moment to clean his hands, and then reluctantly walked toward the front of the shop, conscious of his appearance. The sun had set while he worked, and for the most part the neighborhood had finished its shopping. The shop was empty save for Mama, Łucja, and two men… one of whom he recognized.

                            “Heinrich, my friend!” he said warmly as he strode into the shop. Heinrich von Klingemann was a frequent visitor to the bakery when his business brought him to Berlin, and Laszlo was always glad to see him. The two had met at the Hedge Maze almost a year earlier. They had begun a fumbling, awkward courtship at first, but each of them had quickly realized that there was no true romantic spark between them, and instead they had become fast friends.

                            Heinrich was nobly born, and while his elder brother – a war hero – had inherited the family’s land and title, Heinrich had contented himself with managing the family’s business interests, and had settled into a quiet life in the country running his factory. Realizing that rural Germany had little to offer a man of his proclivities, he often took advantage of Berlin’s nightlife when business brought him to the city. He made a point of visiting the Piekarski bakery at least once during each such trip, and on such occasions he and Laszlo often stayed up for hours talking together.

                            “Now that both of my children are here to tend to the shop,” Mama said graciously, “I should go upstairs and see to supper.” Her eyes twinkled merrily as she glanced at Laszlo, and he sighed inwardly. Ksenia Piekarski had long suspected that her son preferred the company of other men, but beyond that her insight failed her. “Łucja, perhaps you could help me?”

                            Łucja rolled her eyes. “I’ll be up soon, Mama,” she promised. “I’ll watch the store so that Laszlo can speak with his friends.”

                            Mama clasped her daughter’s hand and smiled beatifically. “You are a good girl, Łucja, both kind and pretty,” she said, with a pointed glance at Heinrich’s companion.

                            Once Mama had vacated the area, Laszlo turned his own attention to their other visitor. His resemblance to Heinrich was plain, though he was taller and broader in the shoulders. A few years older than Heinrich, the figure he cut was – there was no other word for it – dashing. His hair was fashioned with military precision, and his beard and mustache were immaculate.

                            But behind the mundane, this man carried an almost visible aura of death.

                            Laszlo broke into a guileless grin. “You must be Wolfram,” he said, extending
                            his hand.

                            The other man sketched a courteous bow before extending his own. His handshake was firm and strong. “Wolfram Johann Ritter von Klingemann, at your service, sir,” he said in a deep voice.

                            “Welcome to our bakery, Herr von Klingemann,” Łucja said with a bright smile. “Your brother is both a good customer and a delightful conversationalist, and it will be our pleasure to serve you as well.”

                            “Thank you, miss,” Wolfram said, leaning forward to kiss her extended hand. Behind him, Heinrich smirked ever so slightly.

                            “Wolfram, I’d like to introduce you to Władysław Piekarski, the best baker in all of Berlin, and my dear friend,” Heinrich said, indicating Laszlo.

                            Laszlo grinned. “You almost pronounced it correctly this time, Heinrich,” he laughed. “Please, Herr von Klingemann, call me Laszlo.”

But I fear that a great many of them slumber among the population, waiting to be born. Self-aggrandizement, pride, arrogance… anything which proclaims ‘I am good and everything else is evil,’ these are the hallmarks of the Hero. I see these things more and more on the streets of Berlin these days. National pride might seem like simple patriotism, but it can be taken too far. We must be careful. We must not draw attention to ourselves.”

                            Laszlo thought about that. “But… our Horrors must still feed,” he said helplessly. “We must still protect our people.

                            Laszlo contemplated what his uncle was saying. “Leave Berlin?” he asked in a small voice. “I… have always lived here. I would not know what to do outside of the city. It is my home, and I love it.”

                            They leaned against the railing for some time after that, saying nothing, and staring out together over once familiar streets which now seemed threatening.

                                                    Session XLIX

                                                    Marek’s Thoughts

                                                    20 July 1924

                                                    Today picked up where yesterday left off. Izzy continued her “search” for cannibals on the train by trying to order human flesh for breakfast. I think she just ended up disturbing the wait staff more than anything else. After breakfast was over Wolfram invited Amethyst back to our car to talk in private. She arrived a few minutes later and we all discussed the documents that Wolfram was holding for her. She eventually agreed not to deliver the documents or the message that they contained to the Yugoslavian King. In return she wanted to keep the papers that we found in Zvezdana’s belongings that were written in Serbian. She also said that she was not going to translate the documents for us. I was a little annoyed at that, but she had leverage and knew it. I know that Tatiana was not happy with that turn of events as well. We didn’t really have any leverage to use on her either. She knows the content of the original papers and could still pass along the message to the King. She could also mention our names and our interest in making sure the documents never made it to their destination. I do not know if she was planning this the whole time or if it just worked out for her in the end. I think it’s somewhere in the middle. So, we had to agree to let things lay with the Serbian papers and we would destroy the other documents at our leisure.

                                                    After we talked to Amethyst we confronted Calpurnia as a group about her involvement in Amethyst’s death. Wolfram told her straight out that we knew what she had done and why. We assured her that the documents that she was sent to retrieve will not see the light of day. I believe that she was content with that outcome. She would not be destroying the documents herself, but they would be dealt with regardless. Also during this conversation, we found out that Calpurnia is a British Intelligence agent who had been sent on this mission a while ago. I believe that she said the plan was conceived at the Olympics and she had been following Amethyst since then.

                                                    Before we left Calpurnia, Laszlo had a little “conversation” with her about Amethyst’s death. He used his aura of menace to scare the woman into admitting that she had murdered the Claimed woman. She seemed honestly contrite, and more than a little scared, after that. Laszlo for his part seemed satisfied that she had learned her lesson and was content to leave things there. After that he was also a little less physically imposing. I think he appeased the spirit of the golem that lives within him by making a guilty woman confess her crimes.

                                                    Later in the day we were all in the dining car when the excitement started. Abigail, Suzie and Iris were at one table making what appeared to be a business deal, when they were interrupted by Luciano. The Italian man confronted them and told them that they were under arrest. He named the charges as acquiring and selling of stolen artifacts. I think he was expecting the ladies to come quietly. That is not what happened. Paula moved up behind Luciano and said something to Suzie in Chinese. Then Abigail said “Fuck it” and flipped over the table she was sitting at and pulled a gun. At that point Christina and Regina fled the dining car and made their way back to our own car. Caoimhe and Calpurnia both pulled pistols as well. Then the shooting started. I was expecting a blood bath considering the number of people shooting at each other in such a confined space, but it appeared that no one could hit their targets. Not even Suzie and her fire ball could hit who she intended. Statistically speaking someone should have at least been hit, and at least one person should have died, but that wasn’t the case. I suspect that some sort of magic was in effect, though I do not know who used it.

                                                    Of all the people involved in the fighting Suzie was the closest to a friend that we had, so Fenric went to her side to assist her while Tatiana grappled Paula so the other woman could not shoot Suzie. While all of that was going on Miles and Iris had their own showdown. Miles turned out to be a creature like Iris in that his arm turned into a red molten looking sword, which he immediately swung at Iris. It cut through the wall of the train like it was tissue paper. After that swing he turned into a big metal shelled creature. Iris matched him with her own metal form and they fought. She attacked him with her scorpion tail and appeared to hit him. I did not know at the time if this had any effect on him or not, but in retrospect I think it was a poisonous sting.

                                                    Amethyst decided that discretion was the better part of valor and fled the room. I didn’t blame her. There was more shooting and missing from several people, which was getting dangerous. Caoimhe was shooting at Calpurnia because she was British and Calpurnia didn’t get a chance to shoot back at Caoimhe before Lizette make her gun jump out of her hands and stay near the mechanic. Piedad shifted forms and attacked Abigail which just made Luciano upset and he told her to back off and interposed himself between the two. I have to give him credit for telling a were-creature in Gauru form to back down from anything they were doing, or for standing up to one for that matter. It takes guts to do that. After that I decided that Caoimhe was just going to make things worse so I grappled her and pulled her weapon from her hands before she could shoot at anyone else. Unfortunately for me I forgot what type of fae she was and she burst into flames, setting me on fire. I have had enough experience with bad burns in my life and I really had not wanted to reacquaint myself with the feeling. Unfortunately for me that was not among my choices at that moment. Luckily, after Caoimhe started to run off, I was able to put myself out. So I still sustained some bad burns, but not as bad as it could have been.

She managed to dodge and he cut through the wall again leaving a large gaping hole in the side of the train car. Caoimhe and Suzie made for the exit, Suzie grabbing the bag full of artifacts on the way. Then Laszlo attacked Miles, hurting the metal man pretty badly from what it looked like. Then Tatiana joined in the attack on Miles. He seemed to stagger for a moment after her strike but then he collapsed into a pile of scrap metal. After that Wolfram and I blocked the exit so Suzie and Abigail would not leave the dining car. Alla, who had just recently come into the car, joined us. Laszlo jumped after her to retrieve the would be criminal. Iris climbed out the hole and made her way onto the roof of the train and headed back toward the sleeping cars. Suzie for her part held out her hand like she was going to shoot another fire ball. The three of us, having had enough of fire for the day, stepped out of her way and let her escape. Luciano followed. In the end, he never found Suzie or Iris for that matter. He did find a young stowaway girl though.

She did not look happy to have been brought back, but she was hurt pretty badly and needed medical assistance. I believe that Luciano took custody of her after that. I’m not positive on that since I headed back to our sleeping car. I needed to shift forms long enough to heal the burns that I’d sustained from my interaction with Caoimhe. I am thankful for my ability to heal since I became what I am. Back in the war I was badly burned when an explosive went off right near me and my brother. I managed to shield Andel from the worst of it, but was burned on my back because of it. It was worth it though. My brother was hurt enough in the war he didn’t need to deal with any more pain than he already had. I remember the burns taking a long time to completely heal. At that point in my life though I was not fully Uratha yet and had to heal normally. After I became Uratha however I discovered that I could heal rather quickly in my Guaru form. This has come in handy on more than one occasion.

So, the train eventually made it to its destination and we switched trains so we could get to Zara. Izzy decided that Alla was interesting and decided to come along for the ride. I’ll give the mage credit. She stays calm in the face of trouble. Almost the whole time the fight was going on in the dining car she was sitting there knitting. She paused in her craft a couple of times, but was knitting away when the fight finally ended. Makes me wonder what kinds of things she’s seen.

Tatiana noticed Piedad and asked if she wanted to go look for criminals together. The other Shifter seemed keen on doing that and the pair of them headed off.

He seemed interested in that idea and the pair of us headed out into the city. We decided to look for the criminal element in the city and see if we could find someone that needed to learn a lesson. We ended up running into Tatiana and Piedad. They were also looking for criminals and found them in the same place we did. We had ended up at a warehouse on the docks, and it was inhabited at the time by pirates. We decided to show them the error of their ways. Piedad shifted into a big panther and attacked a pirate while Tatiana started her own fight. She really does love to fight. And she’s good at it too. Tatiana harried people back towards Piedad and I worked on taking out those that were trying to escape. Laszlo intimidated the hell out of a couple of guys that he had cornered. That man can put the fear of God into people. It’s impressive.

Before this volchonok kept bouncing around pestering Tatiana until she finally asked Laszlo. Laszlo for his part seemed a bit surprised to be asked, but accepted the offer. I later took Tatiana aside and asked that she remember it’s my responsibility to offer someone a place in the pack. I admitted that I was going to ask Laszlo myself so it all worked out, but to remember that for the future. She nodded her understanding and volchonok seemed to calm down somewhat. Tatiana then offered to teach Laszlo how to fight properly and he accepted. The man knows the basics of fighting, but should really learn to refine his talent if he is going to continue to hunt successfully. Tatiana is a good teacher to have. I will assist as needed, but will leave much of the training to my packmate.

                                                    I do not know if it is fate or not, but strange and/or interesting things tend to happen around us. Not that I’m complaining mind you. I prefer an active lifestyle to a sedentary one. On this trip alone we successfully investigated a murder on the Orient Express, discovered a plot to rearm dangerous groups in Germany in violation of treaties already in place, and fought a large metal monster in the dining car. I’m not sure what it is about trains and those metal creatures we keep running into. Brandon was one and he was on the last train we traveled on. This time it was another unassuming man on yet another train. On the plus side, we also met new possible allies and added to our pack. Izzy is a mage who seems interested in following us where we’re going, and Laszlo is a good addition to the pack. I look forward to future hunts with him. So far I’d say that we’ve had a pretty positive journey, and it’s not even over yet. I suppose time will tell what else is to come on this trip.
