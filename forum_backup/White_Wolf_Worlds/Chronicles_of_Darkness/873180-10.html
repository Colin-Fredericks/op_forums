    Originally posted by MatthiasThalmann View Post
    Can a beast effectively immunise itself against anathemas by hiding a weapon which has been used for the weaponbound anathema in the lair?
    For the record, I'd like to point out that this is actually a classic trope. Hero finds the dragonslaying sword inside the dragon's own hoard (inside the own Lair, even!) and uses it kill the monster. So, I would argue that we're dealing with a feature here, not a bug.

    After all the rules state that a beast can only suffer from one anathema at a time, any further attempts to place one fail and instead reveal the current one. For most anathemas that is just as dangerous as placing a new one, but the weaponbound anathema is completely harmless for the beast unless the hero has the weapon in question. If it is hidden beyond his reach - for instance in a lair - he cannot exploit the anathema. Even if he has the open gate gift allowing him to enter the lair he has first to deal with the (potentially lethal) environmental tilts there.
    I've made a Beast PC that started off with Anathema already on them, and run with it. Dragon collectors that kidnaps "princesses." I did this more because I wanted to emulate the dragon legend a bit closer, rather than an attempt to immunize myself or anything. I liked the extra compulsion, thought it made the game more interesting. Also, I had fun going out of my way to make all the kidnapped princesses into Kin through some way or another. The one that went Ventrue was especially interseting with the constant attempts of taking over the little pack. Ah, so much fun.

    Anyways, my point is this - Trust me when I say that a good ST will use that against you. The Heroes aren't stupid - at least successful ones aren't. These guys are like hunters, and do their research. They find your "bane" and use it against you. You hide the anathama in the depths of your Lair, the heroes will figure out how to get into the Lair and track your weapon down to use it against your Horror while you're off busy doing other things. Imagine the shock when you suddenly realize that your personal Castlevania has its own Belmont trying to bring down the Heart, using the worst weapon against it. Kidnap people, and you'll find that your victims go Hero Party on you. I don't care how much armor, health and regeneration you think you have, I can attest that several knives in the back when you don't expect it are coming. Coup d'etre rules are vicious. RIP, dragon.

    You will never be immune to your anathema.

    Would a Beast be able to form Family Ties with their mirror universe counterpart (as in the ones in the core book, just after the Angels section)? Would they be able to find/form a Chamber in the alternate universe to travel between them via their lair? If so, someone with a narcissistic tendency could get very...kinky.
    You can form Kinship even with your worst (non-Hero) enemies - you can threaten and use as much Hard Leverage / Force as many Doors as you want to get it.

    Kinship doesn't mean best friends or healthy, stable relationships. Its possible, but it doesn't stop the mirror-double from following its nature. You'll need other magics to offset that somehow,

                                                            1#: Since beast is happy with crossovers, how does it stack up in terms of narrative and mechanical balancing. From what I've experienced in our young game is that beast starts fairly powerful, atavisms and nightmares being somewhat roughly 3 pip powers from something like vampire. However there is small concern in our group if beasts will get quickly (relatively) overshadowed by the non-beasts since there is really no way to upgrade atavisms and nightmares, so when the changelings and vampires will be buying their 5 dot powers, which by themselves probably unlocked 4 other powers for them to use giving them lot of variety, beasts are stuck with same 3 dot equivalents. So if the games run long, do beasts just get left behind in terms of efficiency, unable to contribute against the more powerful enemies the other lines might end up facing.

                                                            2#: The game masters section of the book describes the two methods of getting new chambers (crit on nightmare vs breaking point) being quite different. It describes crit on nightmare resulting in a basically exact facsimile of the area the event unfolded in, with hints of the nightmare adding flavour in there. However it describes chambers made from breaking point locales as "almost like a second heart, build from fragments of the soul", and the example given is more fantastic. The question here then is, are these chambers less effective for pathways since they resemble real places less. Honestly this might need more talking through than simple answer but start is a start.
