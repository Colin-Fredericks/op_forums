                        Originally posted by quelsec View Post

                        Yes it is. Why spend character points when every time you need something you can just cast a spell?
                        Okay, that's not really answering my question, because it's not actually engaging with any of the things that have already been described, in detail.

                        If you find those details unconvincing then maybe argue to that effect. Don't act as though they aren't there and haven't been addressed.

                        Still, to offer some kind of additional point, the thing to take away from this is not that Resources is a pointless Merit. It's that magic offers recourse to engage with the Availability system that a character might not otherwise have based on what's on their sheet. Magic actually does that for a lot of things; a mage only needs three dots in an Arcanum to be capable of inflicting significant harm on other people while lacking any skills for fighting, for instance.

                        But magic can come with complications based on the opportunity cost of using it or the possible scrutiny of peers or enemies, or other matters of practicality. The benefit of having something like a Merit is that it puts all of that stuff off into the background, and allows the player to focus on using it for the end result.

                        Like, a Storyteller should absolutely shut down any attempt to say that a person has used magic to acquire money off-stage; in the absence of mechanical backing or sanctity of Merits, it needs to be something that takes place in a scene. Apart from any other possibility of inviting complications from how one goes about it, I would have to imagine that gets unbelievably tedious after a while.

                        But again, that's just one point among many. The answer to your question is found in several preceding posts. One of my preferred ones is "Resources gives one a capacity to acquire things higher on the Availability rating than one's Merit dots, because finance is more than the number in your savings account, and bespoke acquisition of certain quantities of money won't substitute for that (and no you aren't casually pulling together hundreds of thousands at least on a whim)".

                                            Originally posted by quelsec View Post
                                            Also does that mean you can find a mage mentor using magic? Since the effect of the merit fades do you have to use a different mentor each time?
                                            The spell guides you to arbitrary objects or people who fit the criteria, requires time to work that can vary based on plausibility, and cannot guarantee you will encounter specific people.

                                            There's a comparable power in Deviant (higher-level Superhuman Manipulation) that specifies the people it brings you to are ordinary humans, and while the Mentor Merit includes people who are in a teaching position for you the mechanical representation being leveraged is "someone who wants something from your character can be asked for a favor commensurate to their rating once per session, which automatically succeeds but likely demands a favor of equivalent scope in return," which stands in contrast with Allies, Contacts, and Retainer, which all require rolls to use but are less strictly metered in their usability.

                                            That is to say, you can't use Shifting the Odds to guarantee a long-term magical teacher, though the people the spell leads you do could happen to be mages with the skills or resources to trade favors for greater guarantee of success in accomplishing what you need from them.
