    How much is known in your CoD?

    09-23-2023, 06:43 PM
    Obviously the Masquerade is in place but there's a lot of wiggle room between a shattered Masquerade and no one knowing anything. So does the general public know anything about anything in your world?

    This question brought to you by the movie Samaritan and the news articles mentioning random people having amazing regenerative abilities and the like.

      I kind of assume that everyone knows something, or saw something, or heard about something from a friend, but no one knows very much.
      In comparison to something like the Dresden Files, where most of the big movers-and-shakers know each other and even have a treaty, I like supernatural knowledge in the Chronicles setting to be incredibly fractured and siloed. Vampires trade rumors of changelings and spirits, hunters try to make any sense of demons, and so on. With that in mind there isn't room for an average citizen who isn't part of a cult and doesn't go looking for trouble to know much, outside of a passel of urban legends and a whatever stories their friends have mentioned.
      The nice thing about this is you can set up occult research by littering the setting with fragments of useful knowledge in amongst the misleading stuff and let the players be some of the few people in the setting to actually start putting them together.

          They know something's up, but not quite what it is. And very few are willing to talk to outsiders.

          E.g. The abandoned house on the hills is haunted, and everyone in town knows it, save for the most naive or clueless folks. But no one knows exactly what haunts it. A ghost? A spirit? But what's the difference between ghosts and spirits? The reclusive weirdo over there might know, but there's bad rumors about him... Some people in town also know that any blood spilled near or in the house disappears over night. But what does that mean? Vampires? Ghouls? Meat-moss with an icky diet? You could try spending the night there, surely it's nothing too serious...

                          Beyond the baseline of "Everyone knows the supernatural is there and doesn't want to know more"?

                          From that baseline, I generally run that that there are conspiracy theories about the older and more coherently organized organizations-the Diamond Orders, Arisen Guilds, Aegis Kai Doru, the Ascending Ones come in the same breath as the Illuminati, stuff like that. Not everyone knows that when the news mentions that the Vanguard Serial Crimes Unit from the FBI is taking over a case mans, but a lot of people have their ears perk up, knowing it's at least gonna be A Story (what comes out of it, anyways). There's gangs, and then there's gangs, and the gangs that keep pace with them.

                          On the other side of immersion, well.....it's not a whole lot clearer, but it is more aware of each other. A lot of what looked like where the supernatural was seeping through turns out not to be. You won't be able to tell whose running what, but you bug enough people in the know, they probably know a guy who knows a guy who can get you someone willing to talk to you about your particular-maybe lie and manipulate you, maybe just send you on a goose chase, maybe actually help you. Most people know their corner, both their locality and their metaphysically related purview (if they're in one), but a lot of it (again, outside of specific bullshit magic communities) is threads rather than pages or windows-you pull on it to see if you can get anything out of it rather than knowing.

                          Some organizations are more open than others on principle-the Ascending Ones entire schtick relies on them having a reputation and having people in their community know it, for example-but even in those cases, it takes a bit to really get someone speaking authoritatively- going back to our example, not everyone's dealt with the Ascending Ones, they don't brag about how they work, new people appear all the time, they often don't announce that they ARE the Ascending Ones in the first place, and of course there are liars and pretenders. Names that are known get the respect and caution they deserve, and invoking them isn't something to do idly-but if you're on this side of immersion, you've probably stopped being the sort of person idleness of thought kills rather quickly. That's not to say you can't make a case an Obloquy if you want to appeal through what civility there is for monsters-but you better know what you're doing, who you're dealing, and come with a loaded argument.

                          The more time you spend trying to go at it, the more connected it all is-in it's way-but at no point does it really end up as clear. You really do learn that just because something looks like an angel and says it's angel doesn't mean it's an angel*-but you gather that there are reasons it presents as an angel, and if that works for you both, you don't really need to know what it is. There's always someone who knows something, and handling the world becomes a bit like really working encyclopedias-you don't need to know everything in it (a relief, that), you just need to know where to look.

                          It has to be enough, because it's best most anyone can get.

                          *The Demon, Demon, Demon Problem is in full effect, after all.

                              In my chronicle I’d say that generally speaking the average person, and thus the majority of the population, falls into one of three groups. This isn’t including supernatural beings themselves obvi as they’re not gen pop imo.

                              Group One — These are the people who deny any and every supernatural phenomena/entity/attack but haven’t experienced anything supernatural in their life. Generally. When brought evidence or info proving the existence of supernatural they flat out refuse to believe it. But do they offer a possible explanation? No. If and/or when they do have a first hand experience that proves supernatural exists they still hold steadfast to the notion that it’s not real. It just can’t be. People who see/hear proof and go "not my problem" and thus ignore the entire incident fall in this group.

                              Group Two — These are the people who when brought evidence or who experience supernatural phenomena they explain the occurrence with science. The laws of nature, science, and physics simply don’t work that way so it’s impossible. These people tend to give complex explanations when confronted with the supernatural. Or they perceive the victim’s claims as symptoms of a mental illness which is manifesting these wild beliefs and/or hallucinations. The human mind is a powerful thing and oftentimes the mind will, in a sense, play tricks on the person when they experience traumatic events as a coping mechanism.

                              Group Three — These are the people who know that the supernatural exist and have proof. Either they’ve experienced it firsthand, lost a loved one to an attack, or were an unfortunate witness to a horrific tragedy. Most of the time they’re labeled as mentally ill, drug addicts, or stuck in some archaic belief from generations past. Many are also believed to be straight up lying about the incident to garner attention, sympathy, or to enact revenge on someone. Like I said they have the proof — sometimes lots of it — but that never matters to anyone they tell their story to. Your hunters, changelings, fae-touched, ex-ghouls, and stigmatics tend to fall under this umbrella.
