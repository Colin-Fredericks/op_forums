                    Originally posted by MrImperator View Post
                    How do you describe the spirit/shadow world and how dangerous do you believe it should be for werewolves?
                    Acrozatarim, a writer for Werewolf, wrote up this great description of the Hisil of a hospital. There's obviously a few examples in the books, as well.

                    As for how dangerous, mild-to-very. Spirits are naturally not going to like new werewolves, as a werewolf means basically an outsider is there and they're going to start kicking ass. So any loci spirits might want, any plans they might have, are likely to be taken or disrupted by their presence. However, if the werewolf's made himself known and gained the respect of some spirits, word can spread and it can be less dangerous, especially if the werewolf has shown that he's not going to be a tyrant or bully. The more powerful a werewolf is, specifically with their Honorary Spirit Rank, the less likely it's going to be dangerous. Even more powerful spirits will likely pause before trying to wipe you out, as sending a bunch of minions at you is pointless if you can go through them faster than you would a human.

                    It should always be dangerous, though. Even a territory where every spirit's been tied down in alliances or sworn to this or that has the chance of exploding into chaos and bloodshed.

                        Originally posted by MrImperator View Post
                        How do you describe the spirit/shadow world and how dangerous do you believe it should be for werewolves?
                        Mr. Shopping's AP Detroit Rock City was hugely inspirational for me when it comes to describing the shadow. He seriously knocks it out of the park almost every time. It is long, but really good.

                        https://forum.rpg.net/showthread.php...Rock-City-quot

                        I'll quote some of my favorite descriptions from the prologue.

                        Here is his description of the Ithalunim's lair, the Detroit public library:
                        The circular chamber you enter is massive, maybe a hundred feet in diameter, marble tile broken here and there, trails of faint silvery light wisping from the cracks, drawn upwards in waterfall trails. Around the perimeter are bookshelves that rise into the heavens, pitching forward and bent under their own weight, like some kind of Salvatore Dali imagining, sloping to form a roof, for there is nothing else overhead but a starry void. Flapping books use their covers as fluttering wings to fly. They are like insects, swarming, hundreds of them, moving here and there, hovering in front of bookshelves before worming into place against the rustling protest of their fellows.

                        The center of the chamber is sunken in, broken like a massive boulder was dropped from the sky directly below. Silvery white light caresses a card-catalog cabinet the height of a skyscraper. It too is swarming with spirits - tiny bifocals with white gloves that constantly 'Shhh!' at each other to be quiet. Drawers open and close, cards moved to and fro in an eternal test of spiritual bureaucracy.

                        A massive presence is here too, were all that you've seen not enough to rob you of your sanity. It rises nearly 30 feet high, a being that radiates argent light, silvery wings spanning from its back and rustling down to form something of a trailing robe, body crooked and hobbled like a vulture's, dressed in a robe of white. It is hazy and indistinct, with arms like a man that end in hooked claws, bent legs that end in sharp talons, a craned neck and head of a carrion bird. It shimmers and chimes, and when you look at it, you can hear Mother Luna's song, ever so faintly. It walks with the aid of a silvery staff, waving its claw and coaxing books into proper order, a great ring of tomes circling around it.




                        You keep pace with Jack at first, underneath the gaze of broken, blasted buildings scorched by the fire of some unknown cataclysm, crumbling and gutted, buried and choked by rubble. Flitting eyes peer out from yawning corpse-mouth windows, hateful in their watchfulness. You can hear chittersqueals in the savage First Tongue, warnings and curses against your kind, before it fades into silence, black ooze dripping from cracks and holes in the wall. Rattling chains dangle in the place of clotheslines, bits of flesh hanging and flapping grotesquely in the breeze, dripping pinkish liquid against the ground. The ground undulates under your feet, rusty fanblades and fingernails, some catching your pants, tearing at your skin. Jack negotiates these easily, as the two of you run through the twisted streets of Detroit's Shadow.
                        Party Town descriptions:
                        The ziggurats loom large in the distance, their outline bigger than any man-made building ever could be, stretching towards the strange Hisil sky. They sit like giant black shadows, covered in throbbing multicolored neon lights that pulse as veins would, making you wonder if they might be somehow partly organic. The neon covers their smooth geometric faces like overgrown vines. As you negotiate the broken Shadow streets with Jack, you can see dancing lights on the horizon, like a wash of phosphorescent wave.

                        And then there's the music. It's faint on the air, but you can hear it, harmonics below the level of a man's, but not a wolf's. The closer you get, the louder it becomes. Even at this distance, you can feel your ribs pound at the spectral base.

                        The music is somehow familiar, like it were halfway human. Jack seems to be enjoying it, and as you reach an archway made of two buildings that have fallen together, windows blown out and blackened, he stops. The Elodoth looks to you with a grin.

                        ...

                        You notice the ground begin to change under your feet. All this time it was desolate and bare, cracked asphalt like desert mud, scorched pavement, rubbled concrete. But now you notice growth everywhere. Wires, dead television screens that stare up like tiles, posters of a splash of writhing limbs and color, subtitled in a strange, block-letter language that makes no sense to you. The both of you emerge into an open courtyard that is lit by a dim red haze, filled with narrow, crooked alleyways and shop faces that have no windows. The openings yawn like corpse mouths, wrinkled and misshapen. Signs printed in the block-letter script hang askew everywhere, while billboards form a crude ceiling, stacked like a steepled church roof. Openings here and there let you see the massive buildings in the distance, and the hint of Luna's shining light.

                        You are not alone. There are spirits everywhere, motes of colored lights bobbing and weaving, squirming and shaping themselves like soundwaves to each beat of the ever-changing music. The sound seems to come from everywhere - the ground, the air, the creatures themselves. Stranger still, you see that the alleyways and shop fronts are occupied by mannequin legs, walking here and there with a hooker's gait, swinging plastic hips, feet clad in high heels. Some are more complete than others, but all are missing their heads.

                        As you step over the television tiles, they begin to light up, one by one, displaying black and white images that look like they're from the 1950s.

                        "Bastard-wolves..." A television announcer sneers.

                        "...Don't belong..." A chef stirring a pot warns.

                        "...In this place..." A homemaker frowns.

                        The images quickly turn to scenes from a flickering bondage film, where you're strapped on a table, cherry ball tied in your mouth, silver knives flashing all around.

                        Jack spits on one of the screens. "FUCK OFF." he growls, stamping his foot on one of them so hard that the glass cracks.

                        A pair of spinning, levitating vinyl records begin to float curiously around you.

                        "[Why is she here
                        incantrix, vilatrex
                        lookin' or finding
                        mezmiderix
                        Spin spin baby...]"

                        They crackle, a weird lyrical jumble of First Tongue that sounds similar to hip-hop, but garbled and distorted.
                        The Wastewander, a barrens:
                        Partytown slowly transitions to somewhere else entirely. From smooth obsidian floors and massive ziggurats, vibrant lights, colors, sound and motion to rust and crude industrial shapes. Pipes, coiled rivets and corroded rebar stick out of the ground like the orange spines of bodies picked clean by scavengers and time. Your feet clank against metal planks that have been lain haphazardly, the walls high and tight, like compacted trash, all manner of rust-covered junk sticking out in a broken tangle. It is strangely, eerily quiet here.

                        You feel like you're walking through a maze, Partytown swiftly disappearing behind high walls of rust and junk many times larger than you. There is the uncomfortable feeling of being watched.

                        Sounds, scrabbling from above, from the walls that obstruct everything, even as Jack leads you through unerringly, the monotony broken only by the glimpse of a great black sea beyond.

                        Something just ran up along the top of the wall, skittering.

                        Pipes that should be hissing and gurgling steam lay dormant, your breathing sharp and too loud in the still air. You can hear Jack's heartbeat, the terse breath that moves from his lungs.

                        ...

                        You see it crouching there as you round a corner. A long, slinking black catlike thing with the face of a little girl and long, matted black hair. The teats that hang down from its body look thin and withered. The grotesque thing lets out a plaintive mewling that sounds like a little girl's sob before it slinks off, leaping away. Jack pays the creature no heed.

                        Eventually you come to a dead end. The rusty walls seem to have welded together recently and form an ugly, tattered seal, like a newly formed suture. The joint rises with the wall up about thirty feet.

                        ...

                        The two of you get to your feet and survey that which is the edge of Wastewander - like out of a bad dream, the darkened vault of the sky is framed by pollution, Luna's edge barely shining through, weakly illuminating this place. Like a coastline, a vast labyrinth of rusty walls stretch from horizon to horizon, a hedge maze grown wild and dead for a hundred thousand years. Ahead is a great black sea of inky black oil. Pipes of all sizes protrude from this carpet of sludge, sticking out in crazy angles that form no complete structure or system. There are some as tiny as spiderwebs and others as large as smokestacks.

                                                    Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                                    Here's the thing though: Why not?
                                                    Let's for a second look at Urshul and not Dalu for simplicity sake. It's also a form that you can theoretically spend as long as you want.

                                                    So you get +2 to all Physical attributes from Urshul in addition to your 1 that you have to have as minimum. It means that every werewolf character that knows how to lay punches (Brawl 2) can take this fighting style.

                                                    For me it's totally opposite of what Merits should be, quoting CofD:

                                                    Originally posted by Chronicles of Darkness
                                                    Merits represent a character’s unique abilities and assets. They can cover anything from her wealth to her uncanny ability to knock out her opponents.
                                                    Easy accessibility of that merit would diminish its value for players and storyteller, they will feel obliged to take it and GM to give it to every other opponent. It's better to build up PCs with merits to make them feel unique - "Oooh, Mark is a very smart and sly wolf, you can't catch him in a fight, he always slips away from your claws" (Tactical Shifting), "Brenda has this presence in herself, you have to hear her once, her howls come deep from her heart and pierce your soul" (Warcry).

                                                    Seriously, I would save "Relentless Assault" for those situations where PC pack stands encircled in Fire Touched wolfblooded village and the crowd splits and calls to itself in fear and awe "Mad Eye!
