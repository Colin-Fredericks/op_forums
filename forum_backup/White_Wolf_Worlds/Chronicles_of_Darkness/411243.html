    What do you want from a Second Edition?

    02-19-2015, 05:58 AM
    We'll be seeing a Vigil 2e at somepoint, and if as much love goes into the project that we're seeing for Mage and Promethean, then I'll be happy. But what do you want from a revised version of the game?

    I've got two pressing needs. First, I'd love for the game to open up the Vigil to something beyond "kill the monster." HtV has something as a reputation as the D&D of nWoD, and I feel like a lot could be done to combat that expectation; goce Hunters things to do beyond "investigate until combat," maybe open up supernatural relations a little, and throw some emphasis on life beyond the hunt. Let Werewolf be the game where your life revolves around the pursuit.

    The other is an overhaul to the organizations. I feel like the core Compacts/Conspiracies list can be shaken up a little (the Knights of St. George deserve a core spot, and I'd love to see more of VASCU), and the existing groups definitely need some love; the Ascending Ones need it the most, but I feel like the Aegis Kai Doru needs a rewrite an awful lot. Define the groups beyond "here's how you kill things, and here's the tools you kill with," especially in a mundane sense; what does downtime look like for a member of the Lucifuge? Make them feel more like groups to belong to. Give them some history that feels nice and heavy.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

          Here's a repost of the stuff I'd like to see with some additions.

          1) The lack of any real support for actually playing a Tier 2 or Tier 3 story. There's no mechanics for operating at that scale, and far more importantly, there's virtually no fluff explaining how the organisations are... well organised. Adding a new section (or replacing the Hunters section if space is too limited) that gives an outline of each groups organisational structure and their general strategy would be a massive improvement.

          2) Explicitly setting a theme and mood for each organisation. Sometimes "alone and ignorant against the darkness" suits TF:V, and sometimes they're flying a black helicopter loaded with inferno missiles towards a vampire prince. I get the feeling that there are occasions where TF:V has been forced square-peg-round-hole into "alone and ignorant against the darkness", it would be better if there was a nice round hole for every organisation.

          3) A clear separation between Hunters who have and who have not been subverted by monsters. My preferred version will be to present each organisation once with a full write up that portrays them as un-subverted. Then in the antagonists chapter have a paragraph detailing an alternative version of the organisation that is under a monster's sway.

          Note that I don't consider minor templates like Ghouls to be "monsters". Morally dubious, sure, but still human. I'd also make a special exception for God being in control of the Long Night and the MM; provided that 1) A reasonable interpretation of the books could say that he's not actually in control. 2) A reasonable interpretation of the books could say that it's a good thing for humanity that he is in control. I am ok with additional interpretations on top of this.

          4) More hunters being awesome. Apart from the Mage orders, Conspiracies are canonically the largest and often the oldest organisations in the WoD. They also seem to have far less infighting than the Pentacle/Inviticus. I want to see that power. The Masqurade exists because of Hunters, let me play the MM when it goes on the warpath outright exterminating continents worth of vampires. Let me command entire TF:V battleships against giant monsters. At the least I'd like one "big gun" Endowment for each Conspiracy that may only be used in special circumstances, a TF:V attack helicopter or a Benediction to smite people with lightning bolts.

          What's the point of having three different Tiers, if you don't have one of those tiers where the average monster quakes in fear of your name and you go toe to toe with entire Freeholds or Scorpion Cults.


          Another thing that Hunter could do that isn't in God Machine Core. Religion. I don't know if it's intentional, but here's a list of religious organisations in Hunter: Ahl al-Jabal, Long Night, Ascending Ones, The Faithful of Shulpae, Knights of Saint Adrian, Knights of Saint George, Les Mysteres, Lucifuge, Malleus Maleficarum

          And here's a list of the explicitly Abrahamic religious groups: Ahl al-Jabal, Long Night, Ascending Ones, Lucifuge, Malleus Maleficarum

          Out of twelve official Conspiracies, seven! are explicitly religious. That's the majority. Out of the corebook, three of six are explicitly Abrahamic! Obviously I'm counting the Ascending Ones as 1 rather than 1/3

          I don't want to see region becoming a huge deal, at least not bigger than it is but I think that hunter should support the following kinds of monsters: Abrahamic angels. Abrahamic demons. Egyptian Gods. In short, the monsters with explicit ties to a core 12 org. The best way to do it would probably be creating an equivilent to WoD Inferno (the concept of a template applied to different kids Ephemerial being will work as well for gods/angels as for demons), then putting in a little bit of support for tying WoD Inferno and WoD Empyrean into Hunter. I wouldn't say no to some ST advice.

                                  ...personally I want a LOT more support for crossover play (it seems like the most obvious gap in the current literature), but that's getting a bit off topic.

                                  Anyway, I don't actually disagree with you that how it stands now, it's exceedingly weird. But the issue is that Conspiracies are to Hunter what both clan and covenant is to vampire -- Hunters already get far less in the way of 'toys' than other splats, so restricting things to a single Conspiracy/Compact reduces the possible variety further still. And in my experience, players grumble and gripe about that kind of restriction a lot (especially if they can flip through the rest of the book and see all the cool stuff they aren't allowed to have).

                                  Thinking on it... I think that if you want to make all the conspiracies dramatically separate, that could work... but in that case you need to give each compact and conspiracy far more in the way of development. Make it possible to have a party of four or five people from the same conspiracy, all of whom can have different and memorable suites of Endowments. You'd also want to stop making scores of new compacts and conspiracies (which I suspect also makes me your enemy now), since you'll end up with a Hunter game that is a mile wide and an inch deep -- you have what, twelve Conspiracies and twenty Compacts at the moment, but even the best developed of them have perhaps twenty pages of detailing scattered across half a dozen books, and the non-core ones have much less. It's an inefficient use of space if 95% of the material is unusable in any given game.

                                  So, I'll phrase my thoughts a bit differently. Hunter 2E could benefit from a greater conceptualization of different 'classes' in a typical Hunter party. This might come from treating the current Conspiracies and Compacts as 'classes' (in which case the setting needs to be more open to that), or it might come from expanding the diversity of roles available in a single conspiracy or compact (in which case each one needs both more setting development and a larger pool of mechanical thingies). Or one could try something else altogether, perhaps divorcing Endowments from Conspiracy/Compact, so that you could have a set of different 'classes' of Hunter all in one Conspiracy. I think making Hunter more 'Conspiracy Crossover' friendly is the simplest way to go about it, but it's hardly the only one.

                                          I could see a 2e Hunter being a corebook that talks about Hunters in general, with a nice fat chapter of building antagonists, describing how to Hunters hunt things socially, etc. In other words, the first few chapters should be about Tier 1 play, with barebone frameworks for Tier 2 and Tier 3 play. It should be story-hook heavy and light on mechanics for the player side of the ST screen. Make it mostly an ST book, with territory creation stuff and whatever else they feel fit to stick in there.

                                          Then, have a free appendix (kinda like how GMC has a free appendix) that outlines the Hunter "template", with a beefed-up version of the Professional Training and Mystery Cult Initiation merits, a mess of Conditions, and an updated version of The Code which works better with Integrity.

                                          Then, you get a "pack" of Compacts and Conspiracies; Compacts are just a few pages of fluff or whatever along with maybe a unique merit or Condition (besides their "Status" benefits), while Conspiracies have what Compacts get plus an Endowment section. You get the pack of "core" Compacts/Conspiracies as part of buying Hunter 2e...

                                          And then scatter them. You want the Cainite Heresy? Buy a copy of Vampire 2e, and you get the Cainite Heresy add-on. Promethean Brotherhood? Grab Mage 2e. They want to publish a Mysterious Places 2e? Hey, who squirrelled that Compact in there?

                                          Basically, Hunter as a full major template of its own always felt odd to me (though I love Hunter to death); why not take advantage of what Hunters are (i.e., humans that know about the Supernatural, and aren't happy about it at all), and spread it around liberally?
