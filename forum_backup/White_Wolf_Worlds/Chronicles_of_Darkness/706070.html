    New Wizened: Inspiration characters?

    10-17-2015, 10:28 PM
    I don't think I've made it a secret that I'm not particularly enthusiastic about the new aesthetic they've given the Wizened, with the perceived emphasis on pseudo-technological self-reconstruction. I've recently realized that part of the issue is that I have trouble seeing that sort of aesthetic as part of the fairy-tale thematic which Lost is based on.

    That is, however, something which can be helped. Can anyone point out to me fairy-story characters which would suit the new Wizened aesthetic? I say fairy-story rather than fairy-tale because I want to include options like the Tin Woodsman (who I admit probably falls closest to this aesthetic, to my knowledge) but I *do* want to focus on stories with thematics that're strongly Lost - otherworlds, stolen children, Fair Folk, and the like.

    So - who can people point to and say "That's a Wizened?"

                                          0) My point is that between the two of us, one of us has the perspective of clarity the other one does not, and that trying to tell the one with the clearer perspective that they are wrong is a baffling bad decision.

                                          0.1) That would be a Watsonian reading. I was speaking from a Doylist perspective.

                                          1) What I am saying is that classical dwarves, kobolds, and goblins and other things traditionally associated with the Wizened still happen with frequency today. There's not a a glut of 'cyborgs' in today's world of changelings. The perception that the classic Wizened icons are somehow less in action now than they were before is wrong.

                                          2) It's not about transhumanism. It's about iconography and archetypal psychoanalytical lenses colliding with a narrative about weaving one's sense of self in opposition to the loom of reality. Some Wizened will be 'cyborgs'. Some will be classical fairy tales. It depends on the icons the character rips out of the threads for their own self within the latitude of their particular Arcadian Realm and degree of awareness.

                                          3) Going even further from the text and simply talking about fairy tales and high fantasy and their icons in today's society, the simple fact of the matter is that there is a greater-than-ever intersection of ideas at work with the icon bases. A few examples being Artemis Fowl being a popular series with fairies that puts traditional fae alongside highly advanced sci-fi tech, Holly Black's Tithe series grunging the fuck out of the icon base, Cinder using robotics to tell a fairy tale, Hanna appropriating the theme of Little Red Riding Hood to inject some new blood into the child soldier narrative, and thousands more examples I can't even speak for. Changeling the Lost Second Edition isn't trading those out for the root icons of the classic Wizened-they are still here in abundance. But while it's core imagery is still built on the classics, it has broadened it's base to include the now muddier waters of images that people associate with fairy tales. It is expanding what it looks like to be a fairy tale while still leaning hard into the soul of the fairy tale.

                                          4) I am not saying you can populate your freehold with mine-dwelling, gold loving, Nordic-based dwarves as Wizened. I am saying that Changeling the Lost Second Edition has always said that you can populate your freehold with mine-dwelling, gold loving, Nordic-based dwarves as Wizened.

                                          5) You will not now or ever need Grimms to have the dwarves you grew up with. Being blunt, that's a stupid thing to think.

                                          TL;DR: This concept of the Wizened only being cyborgs in Second Editon is wrong. Stop throwing it around.
