    Homunculi

    05-24-2023, 11:53 AM
    I made a take on homunculi a while ago. It might need to be adjusted for balance, and you might want to just use it for villains or make it more or less difficult, but I hope you like it:
    Homunculi aren’t normal monsters. They are a type of construct that can be created by anyone with the right tools. That is, assuming they know the ritual. It’s fairly obscure; however, there are a number of mystic tomes that contain it.
    Step 1: Choose the material and craft the shape.
    A homunculus can be any shape, depending on the material used to craft it. The roll to make the shape requires an extended roll of ten successes with each roll representing an hour of work. The additional benefits depend on the material.
    Flesh: No effect.
    Wood: +1 armor.
    Obsidian: 1L unarmed attacks. -1 to health.
    Stone: +2 armor.
    Marble: +2 armor. +1 to social rolls that rely on appearance.
    Metal: +3 armor.
    Flesh homunculi require Dexterity+Medicine. All others need Dexterity+Craft.

    Step 2: Attune the homunculi.
    Homunculi must be magically attuned. This will determine their purpose and the stats of the homunculi, which use the character creation rules. Magically attuning the homunculi requires an extended Intelligence+Occult roll of 15 successes with each roll representing an hour of work.
    Nonsentient: Nonsentient homunculi have a maximum intelligence of 1 and can only learn physical skills and intimidation; however, they blindly obey their creator’s commands.
    Sentient: Sentient homunculi can have any intelligence and can learn any skill; however, they are their own being and, while having some loyalty to their creators, have free will.
    In both cases, you make them as a starting character after their maximum intelligence and learnable skills has been determined.

    Step 3: Animating the homunculi.
    This step doesn’t require any rolls; however, it requires the heart of a monster. The heart will merge with the body and give the homunculi a soul. It will also give the homunculi a potency rating equal to the monster’s, which gives the same willpower bonus, maximum stat and skill levels, and supernatural tolerance. Additionally, homunculi gain experience points equal to Potencyx4. They have a number of dread powers (to be decided by or in conjunction with the GM) equal to the creator’s Occult dots with one additional one if they have a specialty in homunculi.

          They're more meant to be servitors with the more intelligent ones having sentience, maybe being slightly more like prometheans but not really. They also aren't intended to be player characters. Instead, they're meant to be NPCs who serve people like sorcerers and maybe player characters. They're also meant to be somewhat difficult to create as well, given that you need to kill monsters to make them. And some materials are meant to be better than others. The idea is that better materials make better homunculi.

          As for the name, these things honestly resemble golems more than homunculi and are meant to. But I decided to not call them golems because golems are a thing in MTA while homunuli, as far as I could find in the book and other COFD books, aren't. But I could have missed them. So, think of them with their name changed to golems to get the most accurate picture.

          And you have a good point that marble skin would have difficulty interacting with normal people. Maybe that bonus would only apply to those aware of and accepting of the supernatural and would become a penalty around people not aware of and accepting of the supernatural? The marble could be something that spellcasters use to show off to other spellcasters and similar things more than anything else.

                      Yes, which is why I said that the mechanics for the unusual spells presented in Tome of the Mysteries to accomplish this — which no longer work as-written thanks to Mage: the Awakening 2e having all spells resolved as an instant action — could make for a useful basis for the Revised Storytelling System's approach to Extended Actions, particularly as it relates to crafting.

                      The three principal spells for making a homunculus that isn't animated by an ephemeral being are an extended action to create the form — this roll requires an alchemical lab to perform, and its successes go toward a number of factors, including its Size, whether it counts a person for the purposes of spells that target people (and if so, how long its lifespan is), Physical Attributes and Skills, Armor, and natural weaponry, with each failed roll introducing a noticeable imperfection into the homunculus's form and the degree to which the homunculus resembles the human mages who participated in its creation affects the degeneration roll each of those mages has to make — an extended action to create the personality of the homunculus — including its Mental and Social Attributes and Skills, the degree of loyalty it has towards its owner, the number of additional people it takes orders from, and which derangements and Flaws (if any) it can be known to manifest if the animation process doesn't go perfectly — and an instant action to animate the proto-homunculus with its designed form and personality.

                      Given all of the above, it may be prudent to not have the process boil down to a pair of fixed-target extended action rolls with an interval of an hour regardless of what the homunculus's stats are, especially since you're referencing using Horror systems like Potency and Dread Powers.

                              Originally posted by xela_nut View Post
                              For the soul part, maybe we could change it so that it just says that the heart brings the homunculus to life? It'd be less confusing and leave more up to the GM regarding whether or not homunculi have souls.
                              That bit is reasonable. The next questions then are "What counts as a monster for this purpose?" and "What counts as a heart for this purpose?" i.e. Can a piece of a spirit-Claimed or a mortal psychic empower a homunculus? What about a monster with no humanoid form or origin? Does the "heart" have to be the organ in the chest of a living being or can the motherboard of an evil computer serve the same purpose? Can a manifested ghost (or one bound into one of its Anchors) do the trick?

                              As for the fixed-target rolls, that's another good point. So, how about these for starting changes:
                              Rather than a fixed number of successes, the target number of successes for the Crafts roll could depent on the materials used?
                              Like:
                              Flesh: 1 success.
                              Wood or obsidian: 2 successes.
                              Stone or marble: 3 successes.
                              Metal: 4 successes.
                              That way, the better materials have a cost to them.
                              I note that this hierarchy maps pretty straightforwardly to Durability (which itself translates to weapon bonus and/or Armor rating for characters), so it may be sensible to set the base cost as Size x [Durability + 1] with potential extra modifiers like Availability, depending on how you want the biases of creation to skew, i.e. is a homunculus made of gold more or less desirable than a homunculus made with equally durable metal, or does that not matter?

                              By a similar token, it's probably worth addressing whether Dread Powers modeling physical capabilities ought to be part of the body-construction roll instead of the attunement roll or the animation process — and whether the creation's "natural" weapons and armor already count toward the limit of Dread Powers, particularly if homunculus creation is something theoretically anyone with the right knowledge can do.

                              As for the Occult roll, perhaps the target successes could be equal to the Potency of the homunculus? That way, more powerful homunculi are more difficult to make all around?

                              I like the idea of special materials being required as well. At the very least, they should need a workspace with fitting equipment for crafting the homunculus' body and making it so that the heart will animate it.
                              Obviously in the model where the Potency of the contributing monster directly translates to the Potency of the homunculus this is less of a concern, but it's probably also worth treating the donor's Potency as an upper limit that the homunculus's creator can fall short of or aim below to have better odds of completing the project before they run out of extended rolls. Maybe make donor Potency in excess of target Potency count as bonus successes towards the attunement roll or other step of the process? (If more expensive parts are more desirable, maybe have Availability factor in in a similar fashion?)

                              There's not a direct mundanely-accessible equivalent of Alchemical Lab in 2e, but Safe Place, Resources, and appropriate Library dots can probably contribute to the roll and/or target number adjustment in some way as well.
