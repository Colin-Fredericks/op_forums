                                                Originally posted by Malus View Post

                                                The book wasn't originally presented as a new edition. The first God Machine product, and rules updates in Strix Chronicle was to be optional, because some third party (CCP) that had squandered the IP couldn't decide on a good marketing approach to a second edition. Correct me if I'm wrong, but from Strix Chronicles to Second edition, the most significant change was adding blurbs to attributes and the cover change.
                                                The Revised Storytelling System - the rules changes that first appeared in the God Machine Chronicle - was always meant to be a second edition. Onyx Path decided that the Storytelling System had a good run, and wanted to apply what they had learned to a new edition. Crowd Control Publishing, who owned the IP at the time, were working on an MMO in the Classic World of Darkness setting, simply titled “World of Darkness” and were not keen on the idea of Onyx Path releasing a product called “World of Darkness Second Edition” at the same time and causing confusion over whether or not their MMO was up to date. So, Onyx Path pitched a few ideas to CCP for how to get the Revised Storytelling System out there without calling it a Second Edition. The one CCP approved was the “Chronicle Books;” books sold as chronicles featuring optional rules revisions. Then the MMO got canceled and CCP gave OPP permission to finally start calling their Second Edition what it actually was. The only differences between Blood and Smoke: the Strix Chronicle and Vampire: the Requiem Second Edition is the title, references to the title, and the cover art. I seem to remember hearing mixed information about if there were also any typo fixes, but either way the actual content of the two books is identical.

                                                Pretty much everything confusing about the naming conventions of WoD and CofD comes back to the terrible decision to call the new IP World of Darkness. The folks who produce content under that IP have been dealing with the fallout of that decision ever since.
