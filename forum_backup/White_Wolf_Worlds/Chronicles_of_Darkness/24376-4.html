                        Good question. What do people pact for? Health, influence, money, things like that. Even to excise problematic parts of one's life (and then going on to learn why it's never that easy). I'd say that curing persistent Conditions could be part of that kind of thing. I'd allow it. What I'd probably do in terms of grades:

                        Lesser: Addicted, Amnesia, Mute, Obsession
                        Medial: Fugue
                        Greater: Blind, Broken, Disabled, Madness

                        I'd say that they can cure supernaturally-inflicted Conditions, yes, but the severity determines the grade. False Memories would be Medial, while Enslaved is Greater. I would say that pacts probably can't cure soul loss Conditions: Soulless, Enervated, Thrall. Though it does lead to some interesting implications if they can....

                        Also, yes, I would say that a pact could grant a persistent Condition to the mortal, though it'd be strange. It could make a normally temporary Condition persistent, maybe: you could be Intoxicated 100% of the time. There would be no way to come down from it short of destroying the contract or maybe some other magic, however, so it'd quickly drive you nuts, I think. Something like a persistent Intoxicated Condition would probably be a Lesser aspect, because it's pretty easy to grant (and probably makes the assignee a big target for other demons wanting to forge some pacts).

                                        Good questions again! This sort of thing gets tricky. I'd say granting fertility is certainly doable. Seems like about a Greater aspect to me: it's important and dramatic, not unlike allowing the blind to see again. The ability to have specifically male or female children seems easier than that, assuming someone is able to conceive. Desperate people making pacts with demons for children definitely seems like something that could happen.

                                        The demonic genetic engineering I'm not so sure about, largely because there are limits to what demons can do. Rumors do exist of generational durations on pacts, but I didn't put in any hard rules on that because it seems like something best left to myth, rumor, and maybe a chronicle's story hook.

                                        Re: children

                                        By the rules, a mortal need not know exactly what she's signing to sign a pact. I think a case could be made for a child unable to even understand the concept being unsuitable for signing a pact. This offers some interesting implications. However, if you want to rule that children can sign pacts, go right ahead.

                                        By the way, I used to talk about one thing in particular with Demon being the most horrific thing in a game I've worked on. Soul pacts are that thing, because the thought of being subsumed so wholly that you are annihilated from the universe is absolutely horrific to me.

                                                        Personally, I'd rule it mechanically based on XP cost. Since a Pact can grant Merits, which have an XP cost of 1 per dot (GMC values) I'd say that restoring/granting 1 Dot of Integrity (3XP) would be equal to granting a NEW 3 dot Merit, so I'd say it would be a Medial bonus.

                                                        Also note, however, having your MORAL OUTLOOK ALTERED by a Demon using a mystic pact is probably gonna worry and/or scare any even remotely sane person, so consider (and tell the player this beforehand) that this may either cause an immediate Breaking Point, or add an additional Breaking Point Trigger, "Do I really feel this way, or did the Demon MAKE me feel this way?"


                                                        However, for non-human morality traits, like Humanity, Wisdom, and especially Memory, I think the system would have to be higher, since supernatural morality traits tend to have a more extensive impact on the game.

                                                        If a Morality trait (like Memory for example) Requires specialized experience (Sebayt Experience) to raise, then granting a Point is definitely a Greater (+3) benefit. Likewise, if there are system downsides that could apply, they should probably apply.
                                                        Again to use Mummy as an example, since the Judges aren't too keen on the Arisen building their Memory, Doing a Pact with a Demon to gain Memory PROBABLY counts as going against your first purpose and the will of your judge, and would mandate a Descent (power stat loss) roll.


                                                        Also, I think the additional breaking point trigger would end up applying to most morality traits - "Is this really how I feel, or is it something the Demon MADE me feel?"
                                                        Oh, and to be a REAL jerk, a Demon could always, say, Grant a Mage two points of Wisdom with a One Month duration. :-)

                                                            I have a question about transferrence of obligations from the mortal's perspective (i.e. their soul). Having just watched The Devil and Daniel Webster (an old one, but a good one), a question was triggered that didn't appear to be in the Demon ruleset.

                                                            Could a demon approach a mortal whom he/she does not have a soul pact with and get this mortal to fork over a soul in place of a mortal who does have a soul pact?

                                                            Example: Demon manages to get Daughter of attractive Cover potential to sign a soul pact. Demon now goes to attractive Cover potential and shows contract, laughs and says that if attractive Cover potential wants to change place, by all means the Demon will be happy to allow an amendment to the contract (strikeout the soul of "Daughter" with blood and write in new name of attractive Cover potential with siganture amendment).

                                                            Why I ask is that, despite this being a bit of a trophe in fiction (The Little Mermaid pops as likely the most widely known example), the person whose soul ends up being given to the Demon gets no benefit themselves (the consideration of the contract, be it resources, other merits, etc) which seems to fly in the face of the "universe editing" scheme of pacts.

                                                            This also begs the question as to whether the contracts themselves could be amended and whether this isn't too great a strain on what Demons should be allowed to do.
