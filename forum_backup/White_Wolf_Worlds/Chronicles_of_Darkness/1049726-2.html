            Originally posted by SoulGambit View Post
            The question is, if a Mage stops their aging process and someone dispells it, do they "catch up" to their actual age or do they start aging again from that point?
            The wording of Veil of Time makes it reasonably clear that the mage would simply start aging from that point. The real drawback to that spell is that there's no way to spend Experiences while under its effects.

            It also takes a total of 3 Reach to be able to recover from Conditions and Tilts, heal normally, and avoid aging while under the spell's effects--all before spending the 2 reach for indefinite duration. Yes, that's surmountable, but it's non-trivial.

            From the book's text concerning immortal mages, and from the one spell (that I know of) that mentions immunity to aging, it seems that immortality is meant to have some serious baggage attached.

                    I'm fairly sure that there are the odd immortal or at least superhumanly old Mages around in regular Mage politics, but they fake deaths/disguise their nature etc because that's easier than overturning the general idea that Mages have about immortal Mages being heavily involved in things. Same with Archmages.

                    I think that there is often a tendency, both in and out of setting, for people to justify things like that as a sort of natural law thing. All mages distrust all immortal Mages inherently! Whereas really it's a case that, in most places in general, the prevailing cultural view of Mage society is that immortal Mages and Archmages involving themselves is seen in a dim light. Whether that's something that is a natural response, or a regressive cultural viewpoint, or a good cultural viewpoint etc is probably debated in setting in the same way we talk about morality in the real world.

                    In the latest edition, we're seeing that Mage society is less about the translation of grand cosmic laws, and more like real society, something that comes from somewhere and has an actual history and so on. The Diamond might say their existence is written into the law of reality itself, and maybe it is, but maybe it isn't and they are just cults founded in the ancient world that have survived, albeit changed, like modern religions.

                    Immortality is distrusted for cultural reasons, and that's a setting element designed to make the game better to play. Works fine to me. Why do we have the little cultural quirks we do? Because we do, really. You could explore where they came from, but they're not cosmic law. We just have them.
