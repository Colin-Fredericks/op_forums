                    Maybe remove Beasts out of it directly. It is just Horrors, proto-Horrors, and Unfettered that enter the dreams of a child for the first time in the child's life. The kid has a natural fight or flight response. Those that choose to run or cower live on to potentially become Heroes. Those that stand their ground or try to fight earn the respect of the monster and can grow up to become Beasts. The kids forget the dream as people usually do, but after Innocents end, around puberty, they have either connected to the PD or not.

                    They also instinctively remember the experience so nascent Beasts are never as alarmed by the strange nightmares they might have. They remember in their heart it will work out fine whether they are predator or prey in the dream. Would be Heroes remember they were afraid. Their moment of weakness haunts them and turns into self-loathing. Should they lose their grip and are exposed to something similar all they hate they have built up over years gets projected upon the Beast that "awakens" the Hero. But the Hero's heart knows that it never actually saw what caused the initial incident. The Hero only knows that it had a feeling to it that is the same feeling they get from any Beast. So the cycle of hate never ends as long as the Hero lives.

                    Beasts dare not unleash their Horrors. The Horror must be sated because most children would run. Trying to create more nascent Beasts would create more nascent Heroes, and Heroes in greater numbers. Beasts learn to teach through fear because it is what they know and they have to try to keep people stable and productive. It seems to keep the number of Heroes down. If Beasts could only keep all the nascent Heroes on the straight and narrow then they wouldn't have to face them, but there are never enough Beasts. Beasts "recruit" all the nascent Beasts that they can. They try to teach the newbies the program. It isn't pretty, but surviving never is.

                    Becoming a Beast isn't hereditary, but it does seem like children that grow up around Beasts end up Heroes or Beasts themselves. Beasts can't see this connection that people have unless they are ripe to become Beasts so it might be best to stay away from family. It is the only way to keep them from ever becoming Heroes. It is better to create a new family. It will have members that are "safe." Members that aren't really human, but that's okay because Beasts aren't really human either anymore. They might be monsters, but nobody is perfect. ​

                    Seems to be all nicely tied with a bow and no religious overtones. The whole thing is purely self-serving.
