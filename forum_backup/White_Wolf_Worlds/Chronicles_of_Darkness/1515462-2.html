            Survivors Club

            The Survivors Club could have easily been my favorite conspiracy in the book- they are a very cool organization which takes the abused and gives them power used against their tormentors, their Endowments are incredibly cool, and they really let you play Buffy in the CofD (which the Maiden's Blood Sisterhood have also did, yet to a lesser degree I think). Overall, they are very cool- but like some other organizations in the book, they feel a bit too "compact-y" in presentation, with the main reason for it being, probably, their name. A world wide organization can't use the name "Club" without it undermining its place as a global entity, unless it is added with some occult feature (for example, the "Hellfire Club" or "Hunts Club" could work as a conspiracy name, imo, because it creates a dark "feeling" which the name "Survivors Club" lacks). As such, I would have personally renamed the Survivors Club into something like the Survivors Society, but it is probably not a game changer overall. There are a few other "weak points" which I find in their writeup, but for those I'm going to refer through the text itself.

            While still being relatively "young", the Survivors are quite old, having around 50-40 years in existence. They have splintered from the Maiden's Blood Sisterhood, which honestly makes sense considering that the two cover similar "last girl" themes (even though they are more flexible towards who they recruit), mostly because they felt the "darkness within" which made them more violent and dangerous compared to other hunters. Then they started reaching towards other people who have had a similar experience, time skip (argh), and they are now a conspiracy! So.. yeah, the main issue with this conspiracy is the said "time skip"- it is not clear how did they became a conspiracy, especially as much of their vocabulary still uses academic jargon, such as "campus" and "advisory council", but at least we do know how they have access to their Endowments- a parting gift from those who tried to murder them, yet died instead.

            There is no need to beat around the bush- the Survivors all have a little slasher inside. The Mirror Darkly ritual is not a means to get Endowments, but to cultivate them under a strict control. Each member of the conspiracy already had the darkness lurking within them, they only need a way to face them under the comfort and support that their organization offers. That's explicitly mentioned that while the hunters of the Survivors maintain access to their Endowments even without membership, if they are to leave the conspiracy they quickly degenerate into slashers simply because they no longer have the same support network. So while the fact that the survivors have a little slasher inside of them, waiting to hatch (while they work very, very hard to make sure it won't), they interesting thing is how they gain that slasher- by surviving a slasher attack, and specifically, by killing it. And why is it important, you say? Because according to 2e, the CofD is currently suffering from a Slasher Epidemic- and the Survivors Society might have just been the ones to identify the vector.

            While the writeup seems to focus on those who have dealt the killing blow as the "infected", by the fact that the founders are made of 2 people which have both survived the attack of the slasher and shared responsibility in killing it shows that the criteria is more flexible than "the one to put a bullet in their head". As such, I think that it should be related to a more nuanced case- the idea of not merely surviving a slasher attack, but also taking up the Vigil as part of the incident. Naturally, in most instances, that would mean hunting the slasher, and generally it would even mean killing it, but it is a more flexible criteria that makes sense especially when we consider the origin of the Code. In many ways, the Code serves as some sort of a "natural defense mechanism" of the human mind- a person stumbles upon something that they can not and will not understand, and gets traumatized by it, yet instead of trying to rationalize the process or deny it, hunters are those where their mind takes a stand against the darkness. Where there is a "fight or flight" mechanism, the Code takes "fight" and turns it into the default process, shaping their whole mentality around "the Enemy", and it is not a "person. Now, we also know that this shift in mentality may accelerate degeneration into becoming monsters, as slashers are more common among fallen hunters than the general population (that's was especially well defined in 1e's Code, alas we have lost this one). However, let's take everything that we have learned and put it into a different context- what if the Code is not merely a "fight or flight" mechanism on steroids? After all, the human mind is very adaptable, and it is known to shape its identity around trauma. Abusers create abusers, those who experience violence may end up adopting it. Violence forever leaves it mark on the soul, as cognitive patterns try to explain the trauma, give it meaning- and the experience to the supernatural is violent and traumatic by nature. In that case, what if the Code is not, actually, a defense mechanism- but pattern harnessing mechanism? The hunter embrace the Code not because they hate the supernatural, but because their monkey brain takes the trauma and shapes their identity around them. The abused will become the abusers. The hunters see the darkness, and its patterns are embodied within them. Taking the Vigil is not to fight monsters- is to become like a monster.

            Now we are getting somewhere- lets start pushing it forwards.

            So the Code is the process where humans look upon monsters as say "that's horrifying, I should shape around this trauma my all personality". However, in most cases, this does not lead into becoming a slasher- at least, not immediately, and not as long as their mind is stable. This is different for those that the Survivors recruit- their mind is contaminated by slasherhood from the get-go. The darkness is always found within them, putting them apart from the common hunters. In order to explain this, we need to consider that unlike most other forms of monstrosities, such as vampires and werewolves, slashers do not generally form by supernatural effects- those the mind can't mimic, and as such can not produce (thankfully). Slashers are a different story- the reason that they form is that their mind becomes alternated to the point that they no longer think like human beings, and no longer recognize other human beings as related to them- only then the darkness seeps in and gifts them with their Dread Powers. As such, the very essence of being a slasher is in their behavior- their tells, their targets, their Undertaking, their modus operandi, everything is contained within the psyche. And if the Code is about the hunter getting through trauma which mimics the behavioral patterns of being a monster, and the source of being a slasher is found within the same patterns, then by adopting the Code while surviving a slasher attack, the mind, instinctively, adopts a sliver of the slasher's own entity into it. Their Code becomes contaminated, like by a virus, which means that their own personality now revolves around a trauma which tells them one, simple instinct- kill.

            Again, all hunters share that instinct- it is found within the Code. However, the Code also upholds the separation between a "human" and a "monster", which is very important considering that it makes sure that the hunter only "kills" who they need to kill. Sure, different hunters may argue about what makes a monster a "monster", which shapes how they apply the Code- but it is still an essential part of their identity. This is different for slashers, who no longer have that distinction. Heck, their mind becomes so twisted, that the reason that slashers become Soulless is probably because it is just no longer compatible with the human soul. And the Code of the Survivors becomes contaminated by the same, less defined distinction between "people" and "monsters", giving a slight more advantage to the "kill" instinct. And the "kill" instinct has the tendency to drive the hunters into Breaking Points, which weakens further their ability to distinguish the two, which further increase the "kill" instinct, and so on and so on. I mean, even if you decide that "slashers existing in their patterns" is a reach, the Code being a trauma is very much a canon- and what triggers the Code for the Survivors? The act of defining an entity which, by all external accounts, is a person, as a monster. Slashers are supernatural entities, sure, but the mind can't really pick it up- what it sees is that you killed a human being, and that all human beings could become monsters. Which means that the distinction between "people" and "monsters" must be looser, as they have seen by their own eyes the proof that the two are not that different from one another.

            As such, what the Mirror Darkly ritual does seems to be that it simply allows the hunter to slowly and selectively control their Code- redefine what does it mean, for them, to be a person, and what does it mean for their enemies, the slashers, being monsters. Through careful description of who they are and how they are still human, they can control the corruption within their Code, and stop it from bringing down their whole personality. Without the comfort and help of the conspiracy, however, hunters with contaminated Code are doomed to degenerate into slashers by themselves, which means that they cause more people to experience the Code, which enforces upon them the exact same corruption- hence the epidemic. The Survivors, in that regard, are not much of a vaccine as much as they are a treatment for a chronic illness- you can live with the tendency to become a slasher, you can't get rid of it completely but it is possible to keep it under control and stop it from erupting. As such, in theory, everyone with the tendency for slasherhood, not only hunters, may join the conspiracy- however, they need to adopt the Code in order to keep the eruption under control, and they are harder to be found, or understand that they need to be treated. As such, the vast majority of the Survivors are those who had the contaminated Code in the first place, as they have the highest tendency to fall into such dangerous behavior, and also the ones who recognize the fastest that they may need help.

            While that explains the Endowment of the Survivors, I want to go back a bit to how the Survivors became a conspiracy- again, the name and focus around academic culture is a bit complicated, but after being part of the Debate world I can see, a bit, how it could work- different debate societies are being organized into national leagues, which in turn create international leagues, with their own cultural codes and constitutions and nice things like that (hence a common joke that Debate is basically joining a cult). The problem with that structure is that while it allows a relative quick expansion using university infrastructure, Debate (and, I assume, similar societies) don't really have much of a budget- the university either gives them money or that the members pay by themselves, and a lot of people (such as myself) work at managing the societies and clubs volunteers. As such, while the infrastructure would provide a reach, it lacks the money and resources needed for this expansion (for example, how do you go to your university and explain that you need a budget in order to go around and kill monsters?). As such, it is likely that the conspiracy is likely to both have members from within the university staffs, which makes it very integrated into the academic background, as well as people who complete their degrees and either get integrated in consulting services, support groups or even startup companies (which is probably from where most of the money is going). LinkedIn based conspiracy, more or less. Argh, I really don't like using the internet as an excuse, but in the case of how the academy and money are intertwined, the only way to rationalize it is by turning to a infrastructure which combines the League system, support from academic staff, and LinkedIn. We can only work with what we have, after all. (Note, the main reason for substituting "Club" with a "Society" is how interchangeable the words are in reference for clubs in the debate world).

            Anyway- back to the interesting subject: if the Survivors are indeed people with a contaminated Code which lets them have a nascent slasher in their heart, what is their endgame? What is their "true goal"?

            It is very easy, actually. We have already talked about it. The Survivors are the treatment, but that's not enough to stop the epidemic.

            You need to develop a vaccine.

            As slasherhood is a mental condition, the vaccine itself should also be a mental process- therapy of some sort, using psychoanalysis or CBD or even psychiatry. The mind needs to be coded, or even Coded, in advance, in order to stop the spread. The Survivors work at helping potential slashers to keep them under control- but also study their cognitive patterns, how their psyche works and how it reacts. The Code is a product of trauma, so understanding trauma is essential in order to understand how to stop the infection. It could be a process leading to the creation of a new Code- perhaps one which is unified enough, or one which is more flexible. It may require a mass exposure of the supernatural to the world, allowing people to go through the trauma all at once. Or perhaps a normalization of it- if monsters are viewed as a natural part of the world, they would no longer cause the same trauma, which would lead to the creation of new slashers. Or it could be the identification of the sites in the brain which are related to the soul, and find a method to stop the detachment of the later from the former. And it might just be that there is only one solution to stop slashers from emerging once and for all- the elimination of the Code itself. There are other reactions to trauma, after all, but slasherhood could always trace its origin to this reaction. That in the end, all hunters are monsters, and as the slasher virus lives in the patterns, the Vigil itself must be sacrificed in order to put an end for the darkness once and for all.

            The light may be gone- but it would take the shadows away as well, and isn't sacrifice what the Vigil is all about?

                        So after a very long week, we finally reach to our new entry in the thread-

                        The Watchers

                        So let's start with the shallow and less interesting part of this post- the name. Again, I do not really like names which are too mundane or simple. I assume that in a similar manner to the Enigmatics, the choice of such a name was to make them more global and inclusive (as it is very likely they could have just chosen a certain culture which emphasis reincarnation in its metaphysics), yet it still feels a bit too shallow for me. As such, I would probably use the term "Watchers" as a shortcut for a longer title, based on the entity that the Watchers do watch- Sirius, the Dog Stars (or Sothis, as the Deathless like to call it). Se we could call them something along the lines of "Watchers of the [X] Star", with [X] being... Dog? Demon? Foul? Mad?.. I think that as a tribute to Tarja Turunen, I would call them Watchers of the Dark Star. Yeah, let's go with that one, it sounds cool.

                        Now, let's start from the actual lore of the conspiracy- and let's not beat around the bush. We know who the Watchers are and what they hunt- they are Reborn, and their targets are the Deathless and/or Lifeless.

                        But are they? And do they?

                        Well, let's not get ahead of ourselves. Let's start from the basics.

                        The Watchers seem to date all the way back to 1600 years ago, where a certain village of unknown region was attacked by the Arisen, one occultist revealed themselves to be a Reborn, taught other survivors how to become Reborn themselves, and then they started reincarnating throughout the eras, fighting mummies while waiting to the next apocalypse to sweep throughout the world. This is great for two reasons- first, it gives us an actual timeline for their formation, and second, mummy also have a great tendency to keep track of calendars and such because, you know, Sothis and all of that. So even though that we don't really get info about the exact location of the Watchers in that period of time, we can still look around through the Arisen timeline and seek out for proper candidates. Sure, 2e messed things up a bit with the whole "non-linear time" thing and Irem becoming a global, parasitic event which forces its existence regardless of location as long as it did happened when Sothis started it march (hence Iremite Antarctica), but let's still consider Sothis Ascends as mostly canon for the "main timeline", or else we are going to some metaphysical chaos which we are not prepared to deal with. Let's just say "the timeline follows Sothis Ascends, yet temporal anomalies can and will happen because of the Judges and their annoying lacunae creating actions". Now that we have a timeline, let's see where it take us to.

                        Not to anywhere special, actually.

                        1600 years ago the year was 423 CE. That's 1333 years too late for the Second Turn and 128 years too early for the Third. Considering that 123 is an order of magnitude smaller compared to 1333, it is likely that we are talking about the Third Turn, which, honestly, feels a bit early? I was sure that some Reborn would have noticed the pattern earlier, or create some organization to deal with it (when I made a similar homebrew it traced its origin all the way to Irem, actually)- but it might be the latest reincarnation of the conspiracy or something along those lines. After all, we have around 5000 years at least worth of Arisen involvement, it is more than enough for groups to rise and fall with the flow of time. Anyway, while there are 123 year difference in the timeline, between the fact that time starts losing meaning when you are immortal and the misinformation that hunter conspiracies generally spread intentionally or not, I can accept that they have lost a hundred years or so while counting (or, like, maybe the Watchers got confused between the 5th Century and year 500. It happens to me all the time). Sure, you would have expected from a conspiracy known as the Watchers to keep track of the calendar somewhat more carefully, but well, they are still only human, that's life what can you do.

                        Anyway, going to the Third Turn, the consensus among the Arisen is that it was not a fun time period, known by the delightful names of the "Turn of Dusk" or "Year of Plague". Irem is forgotten, Rome is broken, monotheism is on the rise and cults are in chaos. The Arisen culture has also spread across the the globe, with their presence being felt across Europe, Africa and all the way to India. More than that, during that era a "dark shadow" covered the Heavens- silencing the Judges and weakening Azar, and that shadow seems to have come from the East. Yet during this time, when the Arisen were more free than they have ever been and ever will be, when the eyes of Fate are blinded and the Devourer slumbers, that's when the Watchers seems to rise for the first time. That's... interesting.

                        Let's put a pin on that.

                        So the Watchers were formed when a village was decimated by the Arisen and a mystic have shared the ability to see through their past lives. That makes the immediate logical leap to "well, than those guys are Reborn, right?", which, well, has some issues with it- the reason is that being a Reborn is an independent state. It can not be taken from you. However, the rituals that the Watchers use do not do it- instead, they are able to remember their past lives only as long as they are a part of the conspiracy. As such, you may say "well, so maybe then while the original founder of the conspiracy was a Reborn, the rest of the conspiracy does not- and as such the ritual merely allows them to 'awaken' to their previous reincarnations". Which is a fair theory- but alas, it also fails in the test of reality. Why? Because Time Capsule outright violates that principle- the hunter's future selves can affect their past lives retroactively. That's... weird. That's even weirder as Lives Remembered explicitly mentions "future lives" as entities from which they can derive knowledge. That's beyond what Reborn can normally do- that's some non-linear BS that Immortals generally can't achieve. Somehow, the Watchers are both more and less than the Reborn. Yet, there is also a stronger implication in here- for it is known that from Mummy's perspective, at least, souls generally don't reincarnate. Instead, they almost definitely ends up consumed by Ammut (unless, maybe, they are Awakened, because Watchtowers calling "dibs" on souls is within the Pax Arcana as it seems, and the Diamond Wheel is part of the Guardians of the Veil's symbolism). So not only that they are not Reborn, they also reincarnate instead of getting muched by the Devourer, and have non linear time perspective- not as powerful as the Arisen's, sure, but it's still there. That's.... f-ing wild, by every standard. So what in Duat's burning wastes is going on?

                        And what about the fact that we don't have one founder- but two?

                        There is a very interesting symmetry in the writeup, you see. A mystic with knowledge of the past, and a scholar with a knowledge of the future. The mystic was a reborn, and she viewed how the Time of Demons have happened again and again and again. The scholar, on the other hand, knew the paths of the stars- and knew exactly when the Arisen are to be awaken once more. Like, I am not very well versed with Mummy's lore, but the fact that Sothis decrees when the Deathless would rise is definitely not a common knowledge. At the very least, this scholar should have been a high ranking cultist, or a sorcerer, or even an immortal! That guy knew what they were talking about, and together with the mystic they gathered the survivors and made a ritual that would allow them to reincarnate and get reminded of their past and future lives as long as they uphold a Vigil against the Demon Star? That also means that those reincarnations need to more or less stumble upon the conspiracy in order to be reminded of their reincarnations- or, that in a way, that they are destined to become part of the conspiracy. Or should I say, Fated? Again, most people do not end up reincarnated- the vast majority, actually. Only a few have this gift to deny the Devourer's maw, so it must be that the same people rejoin the conspiracy again and again, and if there's entity which has the goal of putting the right people in the right place and in the right time, it is Fate. And that means that someone have twisted Fate to make sure that the conspiracy members will reincarnate and be granted access ton their memories as long as they uphold the Vigil.

                        And guess what? Sekhem Sorcery can't just do that BS.

                        Like, maybe it was once able to do so- yet already in the time of Cleopatra, Sekhem Sorcery have dwindled in power. Common sorcerers can't do it, especially without a sacrifice. Yet you know who generally fuck with Fate the whole time? The Arisen. And you know who have an extensive knowledge of when the Arisen are going to wake up? The Arisen. And you know who have some extensive knowledge about their previous lives and draw upon their memories? The Arisen. And you know who have the ability to experience time non linearly? The Arisen. So yeah, the mystic and the scholar which established the conspiracy seems like that while they may be a Reborn and a sorcerer, it could very well be that they were, actually, Deathless by themselves. That's however, weird- for why would the Arisen turn against their own gods? Why would they turn against their own kind? And even if they want to do so- how could they even do it, as the act would definitely make the Judges angry?

                        Because there is a shadow covering the eyes of Heaven.

                        Because they can.

                        The Turn of Dusk is the time when the One God rose to prominence, when Azar was weak, when the Judges were silence, Fate was blind and the Devourer was asleep. It was a time of heresy- a time when the Arisen were free. And if there was a time for mummies to turn upon their tyrannical gods, it would be then. I mean, think about it- if the conspiracy reincarnates, then you would have expected that both the mystic and the scholar would have remained figures of high importance- not nameless actors. Like, maybe they are- maybe in a C&C style book we would have had some extension on their place and role in the conspiracy, but they might just be Arisen which have turned against the Judges and used the opportunity to activate this powerful ritual, a mini-Rite of Return, that ties souls with the cause of fighting the Judges for all eternity, investing a semblance of their own non-linear perspective in the powerful Utterances which shake the world. If you want it to be even darker, you may even claim that the destruction that came upon that nameless city was in fact a sacrifice made by the Arisen themselves as payment for the ritual- as Sorcery does require sacrifice, after all. And maybe that's a reach- maybe it was indeed the act of a Reborn and a rogue sorcerer, working together to use the deaths caused by a Arisen as a sacrifice to give that semblance of Timelessness to the Watchers. And maybe there wasn't even a sacrifice- Heaven is blind, after all. If there was a time to cheat the rules and steal a bit of eternity it would have been then.

                        Ok, so the Watchers are a product of Sorcery used by abusing the Turn of Dusk in order to create a mini-Rite of Return spell which made a group of survivors of a mummy attack (potentially by the mummies who attacked them)into immortals yet only as long as they are devoted to the Vigil against the Judges. Fun. Yet it still doesn't answer how the spell works exactly- I mean, there seems to be some shared collectivity in Time Capsule, and Fate does seem to guide them towards a common goal through the years. That makes me think that the souls share some sort of a collective entity- a Akashic Library of some sort, floating in the Astral or something like that, through which the souls reincarnate and in which the memories of all past and future lives are being contained. Instead of the lone soul of a Reborn being sealed in Sekhem and going through the cycles of reincarnations, there is a collective of souls, perhaps a soulmeld construct which sends slivers of itself to reincarnate across ages. That's how the collective goal is being preserved- the Watchers are part of the same collective soul, and by aligning themselves with its agenda they are granted their memories back. If you want to make it more insidious, you could say that the Library doesn't actually reincarnates, merely granting memories of previous members which are contained within it to those who join the conspiracy and as such the Watchers do not actually access their own past and future selves, but that's kinda annoying and not fun to me- but you do you.

                        Anyway, now that we have established how the ritual have acted, who have casted it, and what was the result, we are left with one more thing- what the Watchers truly aim for? Is it really just "fighting during the next Sothic Turn"? Or is it something else? Well, you probably know the answer for that question- you are in a conspiracy theory thread, obviously it is something else.

                        They want to end the Rite of Return.

                        That's the obvious goal of the conspiracy- Irem parasitically forces itself upon the world, making sure it exists. The Rite endures for all eternity, twisting time itself to satisfy the ever lasting hunger of the Devourer. Breaking the spell will not be an easy act- but it may be possible, if one were to achieve a complete Timelessness, freed from the Judges, and go back to the past of Irem and put an end to the Shan'iatu before they begin their dark project. Before they bargain with Ammut. Before they murder Azar. A world where Will stands triumphs upon the Law. The Akashic Library, after all, already stands near non-linearity in timeline, which is what allows the Arisen and the Judges to parasitically corrupt the world to accept their existence. If it were to reach complete Timelessness, for example by undergoing another Dusk, than it might just be possible. It could be that the Watchers work towards invoking the same power which brought the Dusk and engulf the world in darkness for all eternity, making everyone Immortal and denying the Judges and the Devourer from their cruel acts of Suffering. That would definitely require the Watchers to go back in time and view the acts of the terrible Rite of Return with their own eyes. And perhaps they already did- you see, I am no fool. A forgotten village with no name or home region? That's not a nameless city. That's the Nameless City. What if the Watchers were always Iremite in origin, and that the story we get in the book mixes two facts- the slaughter of the Iremite population as a sacrifice for the Rite of Return, and the awakening of those memories during the Time of Dusk, which allowed those individuals whose souls somehow escaped the ritual through reincarnation to remember the faults that happened to them and stitch up a pseudo version of the ritual in order to reach non-linearity, at least partially, and then use it to slowly create the unified Akashic Library? To create a perfect memory by harvesting the pieces of the original Rite, all while inverting them in order to bring an Eternal Dusk and make reincarnation open for all humanity? Shoot the Demon Star from the heavens and bring an end to the terror of the Judges and their slave-gods?

                        And maybe they have already succeeded in doing it, and we just don't know yet?

                        Only time will tell.

                                    White Hare Society

                                    One of the cooler conspiracies out there imo, the White Hare Society are claimed to be a "current" incarnation of an older organization formed during Portugal's Age of Discovery- a good time to be a European and a very bad time to be, well, anyone else. The conspiracy seems to be very fractured, being composed of different chapters (like, really- couldn't 2e actually have developed this concept? The idea of "conspiracy regional variants" is really cool but other than a few examples where none actually used the name the concept is just hanging there, and it's so annoying *sad face*). Anyway, it is claimed that the conspiracy have had numerous name changes, with the current one, White Hare Society, getting stuck at around the 1886- which is, like 137 years of consistency, which is not bad. If we consider that the conspiracy have started in around 1420-1500 (like, depending on when exactly they were formed in relation to the Age of Discovery, so let's say around 1450), that's 436 years of them changing names, so I think that no more than 5 name changes have actually taken place for the conspiracy on average. The fact that their history is even more fractured and that different chapters have their own version for it complex things a bit more, but we are going to deal with that. Anyway, we also know that the conspiracy have originally being focused around discovering other unknown lands, but in modern times they are focused on exploring the otherworlds- which is something we should point out in that regards. Why, you ask?

                                    Because their Endowments are very otherworlds specific.

                                    Infusions seems like a strange pick for the conspiracy- basically, they use alchemy is order to distill the otherworldly essence of invisible realms and absorb it into their bodies. The First Infusion makes the hunter attuned to the fabric of the otherworlds and give them a special sense for gateways and entities of a chosen world, yet also enforce upon them a consistent madness. While most of the Infusions seems to affect the body of the hunter directly, at least one also targets other entities, which reinforces that most of the alchemical process is by distilling the ambient energies of different realms into liquid form. Considering that those Endowments are both derived and target otherworlds and their entities, it makes it very hard to imagine a version of the conspiracy which does not deal with those worlds as its primary target- and yet, this seems to be the state. As such, either the WHS was a very different organization in the past- or that things are more complicated than they initially seems.

                                    For example- what exactly happened in 1886, and how it is related to a White Hare?

                                    I assume that you have probably connected the dots by yourself- but if you didn't, you'll have to wait patiently for just a little bit more.

                                    Anyway, due to the strange shift in the conspiracy's capabilities, even if not themes, and the fractured nature of the conspiracy, I tend to believe that the WHS have formed not through a singular entity but as a collection of disparate organizations, each pursuing the Age of Discovery in their own way- and, as it seems, across many different cultures. Like, other than the focus on exploration, I don't really see something which screams "Portuguese" through their writeup- not their names, not their symbolism, and not even the year 1886. Like, maybe I am missing something, but hares and Portugal do not seem to have some strong tie to each other. As such, I tend to believe that there were numerous compacts or near-conspiracy compacts (or even an actual conspiracy and numerous other compacts) which have coexisted alongside each other through the Age of Discovery, yet as its decline have started so the power of those hunter organizations. Portugal is commonly being viewed as the one who initiated the period, which makes it the beginning of the WHS- yet it could even be that the original Portuguese conspiracy/compact wasn't even the one to survive to the modern time, and instead the modern WHS is being composed from the organizations growing from its fall, or those who were inspired by them, eventually being collected together and shifting focus after exploration have died out, forming the modern conspiracy in 1886. Yet what exactly have happened in 1886? Nothing in Portugal, especially not something that is related to a White Hare. However, there was an event which was related to a White Hare in 1886- it just wasn't in Portugal. It was in England.

                                    Alice in Wonderland- the Musical.

                                    While the book was originally written in 1865 (and the sequel in 1871), the musical was first performed in 1886- a story about a young girl which follows a white rabbit to a psychedelic world, where she never fits and keeps consuming strange potions which adapt her to the insane world she now roams. Basically, the similarity between the WHS and the premise of Alice in Wonderland that it becomes very clear that the conspiracy was heavily inspired by the story, using it as a symbol to gather the disparate organizations under a single flag, creating the multitudes of histories which was converged when the story of Alice became wildly known, and the founders of the conspiracy became inspired by it. At least, that's what I tend to say- but the similarities are too striking. It is one thing to be inspired by Alice in Wonderland to start looking around for new otherworlds, it is another to have their Endowments a direct parallel to the story. Not only that, the one elixir which is not consumed by the hunters creates a living scarecrow- and that immediately brings to mind a different story which is very similar to Alice in terms of a young girl getting lost in a strange otherworld: the Wizard of Oz. The more we look at it, we can see that the conspiracy is less tied to the real life history of the Age of Discovery and more to the strange stories written after that time period. It seems that things are more complicated than they initially looked like- as again, it doesn't make sense to base a whole monster hunting organization over a children story.

                                    Unless it wasn't a children story.

                                    I mean, we already talk about a universe where Dracula and Frankenstein are more of documentaries than fictional works- what stops Alice and the Wizard of Oz from being the same? What if Lewis Carroll wasn't just inspired to write the story as a gift for Alice Liddell, but actually documented a experience which she went through, with her either telling him the story or him being a witness for the strange world? What if L. Frank Baum didn't just made up the Wizard of Oz, but have himself went beyond the Rainbow, following the footsteps left by other hunters who fell through the rabbit hole? In that case, the modern WHS, while being composed from the remains of compacts which have risen during the Age of Discovery and declined alongside it, is in fact a English organization formed by following the footsteps of storytellers and poets, and the discoveries they made when they accidently reached beyond the mortal realm and into worlds invisible- or at least, unified by them. Again, each chapter holds its own history, yet they all converged when Carrol's experiences became widespread, drawing those explorers to Carroll who have shared the secrets of his experience with them, including, potentially, the elixirs which formed the First Infusion. Lewis Carroll was a mathematician, after all- what if through his exploration of mathematical theories, he have somehow stumbled upon the mathematical proof for the existence of otherworlds, as well as, potentially, the theory for how to distil their effects to form the First Infusion of the conspiracy? A reach? Perhaps. Am I biased considering that I'm a sucker for Alice in Wonderlands adaptations and reference? Probably. But is it a possibility? Again, this is the CofD- Lewis Carroll being a hunter who discovered otherworlds and disguised his knowledge within a children story and Baum also being part of the same conspiracy after his discover of the Land of Oz is all within the realm of possibilities.

                                    Ok, so let's look at the timeline- in around 1450~ we have a rise in the number of hunter organizations dedicated for exploration, with the dominant one (the one who was potentially a conspiracy) coming from Portugal. However, eventually, Exploration started declining near 1700~. Many of the organizations collapse, others manage to survive. Britain, being the main colonial power at that time period, hosts the majority of surviving organizations. Then at 1886, the Alice in Wonderland musical starts, hunters which have already shown interest ion Lewis's books start to have many questions about the play- perhaps it was fitting their own pervious experiences, as some have probably stumbled upon a otherworld during their exploration. They reach to Lewis, and manage to convince him to share his theory about otherworlds and how he applied it to open a gate to an absurd reality, as described in the books. They rebrand themselves as the White Hare Society after the White Rabbit character, and they unite together to become a new conspiracy, growing beyond the exploration of distant lands to the exploration of unseen realms, maintaining a close contact with communities of writers who document their experiences as children srtories.

                                    That, in turn, leads to a very interesting concept- it is already established that the WHS holds a close contact with writers- but what if it goes beyond that? What if the conspiracy as a whole is built around those children stories? I mean, what if publishing and sharing those stories is the way which the conspiracy communicate between the different chapters, sharing information about their experiences and otherworlds in order to preserve knowledge in plain sight? Like, what if it doesn't end with Alice and Oz- what if this includes, let's say, Narnia? Neverland? Maybe even Middle Earth? The Japanese chapter may be very busy, producing every Isekai manga as part of this information network. I mean, what if Welcome to Demon School Iruma Kun is not merely a metaphor about growing up, self discovery, lgbtq people and neurodivergence- but an actual guide for humans who walk to Hell and now need to deal with demons? Sure, this focus around writers may sound close to the Enigmatics, but I would take the WHS over the Enigmatics every day, not to mention that the territory is wide enough in vacuum. The WHS, in this case, is very much interwind into the world of authors and poets, making most of the Seekers Guild, with the Slayers taking the more active role of being the heroes spoken in those stories. Basically, they immortalize the Vigil in plain sight, keeping the knowledge and the warning of the otherworlds alive within the cultural sphere.

                                    Ok, that's nice and all- yet in that case, what is their endgame?

                                    It is very simple, actually.

                                    Discovery.

                                    Colonialism.

                                    Monopoly.

                                    The WHS wants to explore the otherworlds, sure- but it is definitely not their endgame. The conspiracy currently controls the access to the paths for different worlds, demanding payment for such access from other hunters who are interested in such passage. but that's probably not enough for the conspiracy- there are many more worlds to explore, many more entities who seek to control the pathways. The old gods lurk near the gates, seeking entrance, seeking to control the paths- yet the WHS can not let them to do that. The world is theirs to explore, theirs to claim. They should be the ones who control the paths, allowing humanity to take over the otherworlds and shape them in its own image. They will allow humanity to adapt to those new realms, to make sure they are not too big or too small, and establish their own wonderful realms within them- their own emerald cities, with them being as the great wizards who rule without magic, yet equally feared. And anyone, mortal or otherwise, who would want a safe passage through those realms, would be required to pay to the WHS- and all worlds will be tied together, all paths will be connected, and all will be governed by the gatekeepers, the rulers of the realms invisible.

                                    Alice will go through the rabbit hole once more- but this time, she will be the one to behead the Queen of Hearts and take the throne for herself.
