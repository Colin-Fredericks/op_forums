    Conspiracy: MI18, The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare

    11-01-2013, 06:20 PM
    MI18
    The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare

    Art by Reaper Verse


    The Industrial Revolution brought the best and worst of the period to Victorian Britain but it also brought a very peculiar sort of gentleman. As technology began to affect daily life in hitherto unimagined ways it began to, shall we say inspire people. Beneath the sheltered senses of the nobility in hidden factories and workshops disturbed individuals built blasphemies of science. Men powered by steam and textile looms powered by blood and sinew. They preyed on the poor for test subjects and... parts. Just one more monster attracted to the growing shadows in a newly urbanising society.

    It was Queen Victoria herself who had the misfortune of seeing what lived in the underbelly of her kingdom. While travelling from London to the Palace of Holyroodhouse she requested to stay the night as the guest of the reclusive Lord Reginald Blackthorn. Dinner went well, Lord Blackthorn was a cheerful host who talked at length about the latest technologies delighting the nation. After dinner however he declared he had been doing a little research of his own and requested the privilege of making a demonstration.

    His demonstration was Eve. A young woman stolen from the graveyard and given a false life through a tiny steam engine in place of a heart and an ingenious array of clockwork connected to her bones. The royal party were horrified and disgusted, seeing their reaction Lord Blackthorn instantly changed from a polite and enthusiastic host to a psychotic wreck. He attacked her Majesty and was quickly killed by the royal guard. It was then that Queen Victoria created her famous Decree Regarding the Uncanny

    “We are not to have such things in our Kingdom”.

    This decree gave birth too and became the name of The DRU. A network of special branches in police forces that spread across the British Empire. It's mission, to identify mad scientists and detain them at her majesty's pleasure. Though the DRU was mostly ignorant of the mad scientists they hunted. The mad scientists were also mostly ignorant about mad science and certainly lacked the psychological stability needed to remain hidden, they were rounded up and sent to Australia.

    It took World War Two to change the fortunes of the DRU and the mad scientists it captured. Hearing reports of German Wunderwaffe or “Wonder Weapons” that could turn the tide of the war Prime Minister Winston Churchill ordered a study into counters. Word came down to the DRU, mad scientists were taken out of asylums and set to brainstorming. Legend has it that Churchill took one look at the proposals and wrote the entire project off. The DRU and it's mad scientists were cut adrift in the military machine as one department after another concluded they had nothing to offer (they also gained the short lived mocking name of "The Department of the Rum and Uncanny" after a particularly bad day attached to the Royal Navy), until finally they found their niche creating small specialist equipment for the intelligence services. Rebranded as Military Intelligence, Section 18.

    After loyal and successful service to the war effort Churchill personally wrote to MI18 to thank them for their service and to tell them that now the war was over some things should not be aloud to exist. They were to burn all the blue prints, smash all the blasphemous devices into pieces no bigger than a man's fingernail, and put the mad scientists back in the asylum. MI18 agreed, then promptly filed everything away in wherehouses. A near century of policing Mad Science had taught them that you can't put the genie back into the bottle.

    This foresight was proved correct when MI18 was once again reinstated to join the cold war with an expanded remit. Instead of simply creating gadgets for spies MI18 was now to become a comprehensive intelligence service running their own technology focused spy rings against the Soviets and their allies.

    The foresight proved itself again with the end of the cold war, when ministers began to get queasier stomachs around a mad scientist's lab and budgetary belts were tightened. When you can't afford proper surveillance gear it helps to have someone who can build his own out of scraps.

    It proved itself a third time when MI18 was renamed the Extra-ordinary Intelligence Service (the name is never used outside official documents) and expanded to fold in all the disparate semi-unified police and military units that previously made up the official government response to the supernatural. With only the barest token increase in support or budget.

    [Picture: A agent looking like Colin Firth from Tinker Tailor Solider Spy stands in a tuxedo sipping a Martini. He is wearing a strange gothic looking wristwatch. In his other hand hangs a pistol with smoke coming from the barrel, screaming faces are visible in the smoke.]

    Don't tell me how it works.
    Tell me how to use it.
    And tell me it won't burrow into my brain to lay it's eggs.


    The Enemy

    The DRU's most common enemy is Mad Scientists. In the past this job was easy enough, Mad Scientists were often poor and isolated; lacking the social acumen and sanity to be anything else. Today even the most maladjusted mad scientist has access to the internet and, if not a community, communication with her peers. Today's mad scientist is more paranoid and better prepared, it takes a more sophisticated approach and frequently a little mad science of your own.

    However for all that they were founded to deal with the uncanny MI18 still has an unusual definition of a Mad Scientist, at least compared to occultists who study the Fire. As far as MI18 are concerned, if you're cutting people up to perform gruesome experiments, you are mad scientist. It doesn’t make any difference if you're channelling the fire to create stitched reanimated minions or just unhinged enough to enjoy cutting up screaming victims while wearing a lab coat. Oh they're aware that one is worth asking to build a gadget and the other isn't, but they'll call both Mad Scientists all the same. It even works in reverse too, if you're channelling the fire to build harmless telescopes and haven't killed anyone (yet) the DRU doesn’t consider you to be a Mad Scientist.

    MI18 also has a remit to deal with the inventions created by Mad Scientists. Often this means claiming them for their own use, but if an invention escaped “into the wild” they're not usually that concerned unless it's likely to be dangerous over and above a mundane equivalent. A gun powered by Mad Science is dangerous, but so is a regular gun and the budget is limited. To MI18 the Reanimated fall under their remit as creations of a Mad Scientist.

    Lately two enormous changes have come upon the DRU. The first was during the cold war when they began to run epsionage against foreign governments (they only made equipment in WWII). Unlike most Conspiracies MI18 considers regular mortals to be part of the enemy. The Cold War may be over, but there will always be someone to spy on. The second change was more recent, under the orders of Tony Blair's government MI18 folded in a large number of semi-unified Compacts spread across the armed forces and law enforcment that had served as the government's general response to domestic supernatural threats, expanding their remit to cover all monsters that haunt the night.

    MI18's tactics are mostly influenced by their history in policing Mad Scientists and espionage. They have developed a manipulative and psychological approach to the Vigil. They favour heavy surveillance and psychological profiling of the monsters they hunt, learning the ins and outs of their thoughts until they can lead them into a trap, set two enemies on a collision course or just know exactly when they'll let their guard down.

    Hunters

    Your grandfather was one of the greatest agents that British Intelligence had ever known. Your father while not as great was well regarded for his service. The Boy's Club mentality of the agency expects great things from you as well. Unfortunately you don't seem to have inherited their knack for espionage. You do have the raw physical talent and have trained with the best but you can't stand the sight of blood and intensely dislike violence. You managed to play it cool through training but you'll be receiving your first assignment soon. You're not sure you'll be able to keep up this facade.

    This technology is ancient, and it was built by madmen so nobody could figure out how to modernise it even if there was the budget to do so. There used to be a whole team of technicians, and soldiers on standby. But budget cut after budget cut hit, and now it's just you and sometimes the boy from the Chinese takeout next door watching tape drives spin and lights blink in endless monotony. Until today, today the machine is spitting out warnings. You've made the call, London can have people here in three hours, according to these readings you have one. There's still old equipment gathering dust down here, Queen and Country call.

    Give a man enough power and he starts thinking with something other than his brain. Gold diggers and star struck groupies are common. You are refined, elegant and educated. Someone a foreign government official or shady titan of industry might invest time into acquiring. Naturally it ends with you stabbing them in the back, or just drugging them and rifling through their files. All's fair in love and politics.

    As an English Gentleman you're expected to keep a stiff upper lip and always maintain a poised and sophisticated outlook on any situation. You do your best but the things you've seen; a man in Frankfurt meticulously stitching together murdered prostitutes to make his ideal lover, a cult in Dubai wiring people's brains into a massive analog computer, swarms of all consuming, self-replicating nanomachines nearly descending upon Cartif. You'd like to forget it all and just live out a normal life in the countryside but nobility obliges an agent of the crown.

    You were recruited straight off the streets after blundering into an active investigation and handling yourself better than anyone expected. MI18 washed away the alcohol, put you into a Savile Row suit and sent you off to basic training and finishing schools. You think the manners and formality is a bit of a joke, the people here are twice as vicious as any of the football hooligans you used to socialise with. But you have to admit, you like the way you look in a suit.

    You've seen so many horrors, you've committed so many yourself, that it's hollowed you out. The charm, the manners, the snappy one liners, they're all a shield to protect you that's remained long after everything it was supposed to protect wasted away. But perhaps they still serve a purpose, every time some bio-engineered plague or Ancient Egyptian curse is diverted from the public your smooth received pronunciation and wit keep the ministers calm and the public reassured.

    Stereotypes

    The Dirge: The Dirge almost have the right idea. They don't trust us, which is bloody sensible of them. When taking care of Reanimates they know to remove the brains of the operation.

    Task Force: VALKYRIE: So you saw the Yanks' tech? As uncreative and risk averse as ever? Did your report remember to mention the Americans still can’t mix a decent drink?

    Cheiron Group: The occasional bit of corporate espionage makes for some nice RnR, let’s just say that the secretaries at that company will show you the "filing cabinets" if you provide them the proper loans.

    KSI 21: God, I miss the cold war.

      Ministries

      MI18 is divided into multiple sections, nicknamed ministries. Each has a unique and vital role in the larger conspiracy.

      The Ministry Regarding the Uncanny is the oldest of the three ministries, tracing it's history back to Queen Victoria herself. It's role is narrow and highly focused. They look for mad scientists and escaped inventions, when they find some they take appropriate action. The asylums and laboratories run by MI18 also fall under The Ministry Regarding the Uncanny.
      Free Speciality: Science (Mad Science)

      The Ministry for Extraterritorial Affairs are spies. They were founded during the cold war as an expansion of MI18's remit. Instead of providing Gadgets to the other services they were to directly run intelligence operations. Members are mostly concerned with spying on purely mortal governments but they do turn up the occasional monster hiding in the corridors of power. Naturally some friction exists with MI6, but the Ministry's tendency to get the jobs that are too dangerous or too dirty for the Secret Intelligence Service eases the tensions somewhat.
      Free Speciality: Subterfuge (Secret Identity)

      The Ministry for Impossible Contingencies are the newest addition to MI18's line up. They were created by nuLabour's lust for centralising, folding a large number of semi-official and semi-unified police and military units held together by the old boys network into a single cohesive and bureaucratic department of monster hunters. Members go against monsters more dangerous than any human and use every dirty trick in the book to even the odds. Snipers, bombs, backstabbing, assassinations, seduction, poisons. If it gets the job done someone from the Ministry for Impossible Contingencies has probably done it.
      Free Speciality: Crafts (Traps) or Subterfuge (Charm)


      Status

      Status in MI18 is bureaucratic and somewhat nepotistic. Getting the job done is always helpful for status, but so is personal networking. Being on good terms with the boss and your co-workers is perhaps even more important than actual competence. The culture is somewhat darwinistic, with various managers and departments acting as little fiefdoms competing for budget and promotions. Sometimes this leads to a genuine survival of the fittest that weeds out the week and incompetent. Other times it simply rewards the best at petty power games. At the upper echelons the sheer difficulty of the job tends to quickly weed out the power players. Those who remain often fit a standard mould: elderly, dedicated, bitter and supremely competent British spy masters who, it is sometimes joked, never got the memo about the end of the Empire.



      O: Welcome to the front lines, you get orders not privileges. Living in the seedy world of spies, lies and secrets you depend on your wits to survive. Take a dot in the Trained Observer merit, because that one twitch of the eye you didn't see could quickly be a knife in your back.

      OOO: You're middle management. You're given wide ranging objectives and it's you're job to divide them up into missions and delegate. What you don't have is a sufficient budget, what you do have is more dirt on more people than anyone has the right to have. Take the Fixer Merit, when corners must be cut you know who's best to wield the trimmers.

      OOOOO: You're upper management in the DRU, but you're still middle management in the British Government. Squeezed between the minister and the intelligence services who don't viscerally disgust politicians every time they ask for a signature on ethical approval forms. Still you have some advantages. Divide thee dots between Contacts and Allies, the profits from a lifetime of backroom deals and favours traded.

      Endowment: Gadgets (O - OOOOO)

      A gun loaded with human flesh that shoots screaming wraiths at it's foes. The encryption software with a demon's contract written into the source code for "extra protection". A single round firearm hidden within a bow tie. The car with no engine, just an elephant's heart forced to beat long after death.

      Once the DRU tried to keep Mad Scientists from creating anything. Now MI18 funds them to create Gadgets. Each one the dirtiest of dirty tricks, the most ungentlemanly of warfare, a blasphemy of science, and lets be honest, one hell of a tool in the DRU's arsenal.

      Their use of mad geniuses gives MI18 what may be the most impressive research and development department among any Conspiracy. New Gadgets can go from a simple need like “something to detect Reanimated” into a fully working device in a matter of days. This comes as at price, as the Products of Mad Science are rarely reproducible or reliable. And even though the official policy is that hearts of orphan boys can only be used in national emergencies, they're a lot more flexible about "ethically sourced" orphan blood.

      At the beginning of a Chapter the player may use the Endowment research and development rules to create various Endowments for their Hunter, up to twice their dots in Gadgets. When the Chapter ends the Hunter has the option of turning in any of their Gadgets to free up space for new devices. Players don't have to use all their dots at the beginning of the chapter, they may leave some space for problems that arise in the course of their duties. There is a slight catch; every Gadget must have at least one frailty. The frailty provides the usual cost discount but it must be present, mad science is powerful but it is nothing close to safe.

      If the hunter wants to keep a tool permanently it is acceptable to buy it with exp as per the usual research and development rules. MI18's exceptional research capacity means that the Hunters may not have to do any work themselves, it can be as simple as writing a requisition form, spending the exp and waiting for the results.

                  As an addendum to my previous post. Genius is the best themic parallel to MI18, but as Genius is an examination of Mad Science themes (a really good one) you could still replace the Inspired with any other Mad Scientists.

                  Mad Scientists, especially once you move past the gothic horror era, are in a nutshell: People who try to change the world and fail*. MI18 are the opposite: People who try to stop the world from changing and fail. They were born in the hight of the British Empire to supress new and dangerous ideas, such as making killer robots. They move form suppression to funding those ideas during WWII, shortly before the final fall of the empire; and as it's mentioned that the upper ranks are full of old bitter dinosaurs who live in the past.


                  * You can draw parallels, as genius does, to the idea that people who really do change the world never get what they were trying for. Look at Tim Burners Lee's views on the modern internet for example.
