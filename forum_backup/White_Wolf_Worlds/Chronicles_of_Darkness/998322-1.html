        I've made me case adamantly enough elsewhere. Folks who come at these games and drop half of the premise will never make any sense to me (the Dresden Files RPG is right there, and so are Monsterhearts and Urban Shadows!) but at least you're having fun.
        Every single part of an RPG is an advisory note. Some system handle things better than others and it can be useful to skin the beast and drape it in fresh leather instead of trying to make a new animal altogether. Chronicles of Darkness provides a diverse range of ways to play it, and it's entirely possible to turn the game into a very wide array of play styles. The base game can have all the supernatural elements excised and be played as a strict investigation story, for example, with nothing lost.

        Personally, I love the setting as-is, but that doesn't mean I've not meticulously picked it apart like a dice-loving discount Jeremy Bentham. I've used the bones of CofD and its splats to make at least two entire fantasy settings, for example. I'm on my way to using Exalted 3e to make a third.

            Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
            I've made me case adamantly enough elsewhere. Folks who come at these games and drop half of the premise will never make any sense to me (the Dresden Files RPG is right there, and so are Monsterhearts and Urban Shadows!) but at least you're having fun.
            As has been noted, rule set is a big thing. Yes, there might be other systems made for running the type of game someone is wanting to run, but just because they were made for that purpose doesn't make them good (be it objectively or just by the sensibilities of the person wanting to run said game). The solution a lot of people come to is to find the system they like the most that fits the closest/easiest to what they want and to then jury rig that. There's a reason there's a d20 version of pretty much everything.

                I think that CoD games are best described as dark urban fantasy, with 'mortals' take as most horror-ish. Each gameline have elements both really in horror area, but also those as more urban fantasy and playing those can get you different 'flavors' of CoD games or even different chronicles in the same game.

                Vampire has both Beast and Banes ( clearly horror tropes ) but also Disciplines ( one for more fantasy ) and some mixed bags ( like Blood Bonds ). Mages are much more on fantasy scale ( magic, yay! ) but also have some very horror elements still ( Abyss, Paradox, Obsessions, Ephemera & Intruders ). Changeling is probably the highest in fantasy, but still delivers horror elements ( like every scary antagonists send by True Fae or Lords and Ladies themselves ).

                What is ratio of fantasy to horror on your table depends on chosen game, particular chronicle foundations and general this parties tastest. And probably many other things also...

                    Yeah. I don't use CofD for horror, specifically. What I've been saying recently is that CofD is that it's really, really good for introspective play, horror or not, because so much of the props and tools available are intended as triggers for making opportunities to introspect. And horror is a really good way to narrow a designer's focus down in a way that supports introspective play really well, even if the players aren't using it that way.

                    I know that that's not how most people use it, but that's what I like about it.

                                Why are some people fighting about "My System Is Bettah" here? Well, lets at least do it in a more constructive manner...

                                I played and liked Monsterhearts. The catch of the system is to focus on relations than anything else. Like, every aspect of the system is about relations and how to play them, use them, and abuse them, both as means and end. It is good when your mainly concern in the chronicle is the soup opera aspect. Very good, indeed, even for long term chronicles.

                                My only experience with FATE is Accelerated. I liked it pretty much, and it is good when you need to focus on fast and creative action of any type. It is really good at being easy to apply any kind of solution you can think of, to any given problem. It is also good for crossovers where you want to guarantee balance without loosing the characteristics of any given character.

                                Shadowrun 5th Ed I didn't read yet, I kind of forgot it... its a shame, since I'm a big fan of 2nd Ed, even it being such a messy system. What I can say about 2nd Ed is that it delivers lethality. The major problem of it is to make money into a category you must choose how much to focus on character creation.

                                D20 already have Mont Cook's World of Darkness. It is very interesting, if very distinct. I also adapted some cWoD splats myself to TRPG, a brazilian d20, for a chronicle I was playing. It also made some interesting ways of playing WoD. The focus on balanced levels makes for an interesting way of play.

                                CoD, in the same way, have a lot of particular characteristics and tools that make it for a distinct behavior as system. That's not only a reason to play it with other genres. I actually specifically choose CoD system for both my adaptations on Pokémon and Green Lantern Corps for the characteristics of the system.
