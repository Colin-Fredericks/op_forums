    Spectrum of the Dark Rainbow - Horror and the Chronicles of Darkness

    08-22-2016, 06:42 PM
    I'm terribly sorry for what I figure will probably become an abusive rant, but I just needed to get this off of my chest. As we all know, the Chronicles of Darkness are inherently a horror game. Everything about each and every game line is crafted, from the ground up, to paint a picture of a world steeped in shadow, the supernatural, suffering and sin.

    But... there's many different kinds of horror. More than I have names for, ignorant fool that I am. But the one genre of horror that the Chronicles pride themselves on selling, that its openly stated here to be the fundamental reason behind what makes every single splatline scary, is Personal Horror.

    This is, to my understanding of it, a genre of horror built around introspective and personal decline. It is the horror of spiraling into corruption, madness and malignancy, without even realizing it until it is too late to stop the descent. It is the horror of realizing your beliefs about yourself are false, that you will cast aside your morals if the price is right, and the pain you inflict even on those you care about without stopping.

    Now, let's get this out of the way; this is a very deep and intimate sort of horror. It's a very personalized horror, something that depends, more than any other form of horror, on being tailored to a given individual. It can be truly terrifying, for the kind of person who cares about soul-searching deeply and has the slightest fear about who they are.

    But... is it the right horror to uniformly paint every single gameline in the Chronicles with? Every single Chronicles of Darkness line is supposed to be about Personal Horror, but I just don't think that label fits them all. Vampire, sure, I can buy it there. Even sort of in Changeling, although I think that falls more under the "paranoid horror" label. But in a mortal or hunter game, the horror, to me, is more that of a cosmic horror; it's the horror of realizing that the world is a darker place than you ever realized, and that the civilization you cling to and support is nothing but a veil of lies that struggles to conceal your true place in the world - as prey for creatures far more powerful than you.

    I'll be honest, I first came into the Darkness settings by sheer chance. I stumbled across a battered, secondhand copy of the original edition Clanbook: Nosferatu for Vampire: The Masquerade. I bought it on a whim and I found myself enthralled. I knew absolutely nothing about the greater world that this was part of, but it painted an intricate picture of a world dark, shadowy, and filled with terrible monsters.

    As time went by, my sparse collection of Masquerade books led me to discover the New World of Darkness, after having immersed myself to a very mild degree in the greater metaplots and shenanigans of the Old World. I tried the jump to Requiem, and what I found was in many ways a revelation. Requiem seemed to shake off many of the things that bugged me, like an iron-clad backstory for vampiric origins, the Generations mechanic (how I loathed Generations), and the fact some Clans just seemed fairly monodimensional. There were some problems, mostly a niggling sensation of "tooth pulling" in comparison to Masquerade, but ultimately Requiem was my gateway to the Chronicles and I haven't really looked back since.

    And yet... I can't get past my origins. The Gothick-Punk Dark Urban Fantasy "essence" of the World of Darkness has stained my bones, and I find that, by comparison, even if I love the lore of the Chronicles - the Clans & Bloodlines, Father Wolf and Mother Luna, the Towers of Magic, the Hedge of Thorns, the God-Machine, the Arisen of Du'at - a lot of the actual portrayal of the gamelines just feels... empty.

    I try not to complain about it. Am I not a StoryTeller? Is it not my prerogative to tweak, houserule and homebrew in my own games? If I shuffle the sins of Humanity around under the premise that only the youngest vampires truly wangst over their lost humanity and those who survive more than their first half-century find solace in the Requiem for its own sake, with the Covenants serving vital touchstones to further strengthen a vampire's sanity and hold back the Beast, or if I portray werewolves as having pride in who they are and accepting death in battle as the fitting end for a life of glorious personal freedom, if I say that changelings can learn to embrace their beautiful madness without forsaking their sanity, who am I harming? If I add traces of Gothicism to the architecture and Punk to the street cultures, what offense have I committed against those who prefer a more "rooted in the real world" approach to their game-worlds? if I diminish human importance in the setting and say that monstrosity can be its own valid right, what sin am I committing against those who proudly fly the banners of Humanity Is Special in their own games?

    But, all the same, I seem to have a harder and harder time "getting" the Chronicles of Darkness. Beast, which I thought seemed so promising, ultimately turned out to be just a disappointment - I haven't even been able to muster the conviction to fully read through the corebook yet, I keep getting so far and then I just have to put it down. Promethean was, well, always a bit of a case of Audience Alienating Premise to me, and that feeling still hasn't changed, even though I think that the 2e version, from what little I've had time to read, has made vast improvements towards the kind of Crossover Chronicles I so love. Dark Eras was wonderful, and Demon a delight, but these two... I'm even having a hard time getting too excited about Deviant, just because I get this feeling it's going to borrow and mingle some of the aspects of Beast & Promethean I found most alienating.

    Is there something wrong with me? Everyone on these forums loves the Personal Horror, they shower it with praise and glorify it as the pinnacle of ChroD's design... so why can't I get it? Why do I keep feeling that the gamelines are so much stronger when they embrace a different form of horror and make it their own - that the strength of Mummy is not Personal Horror, but the horror of slavery, despair and futility? That Demon shines so brightly because it discards the tired rote horrors of other settings and instead embraces a heady cocktail of horror, mingling cyber-horror with paranoia with cosmic horror?

    I just... don't understand.

                          To be frank, I think that each CoD game has it’s own flavor of horror. Yeah, in most fundamental level, most ( but not even all ) have some string in Personal Horror because of Integrity mechanics, but as all – each game gives you it’s own horror experience. And it’s WHY they are different games in the first place. Here is base sum up:
                          • Vampire: The Requiem – Classical Personal Horror of ‘what monster I become’
                          • Werewolf: The Forsaken – Savage Horror of true brutality and ‘I will hunt you’
                          • Mage: The Awakening – Alternative Personal Horror of consequence and ‘what can you do with power’
                          • Promethean: The Created – Horror of being ‘Other’ with a large dosage of nihilism ‘looking for humanity is pointless’
                          • Changeling: The Lost – Horror of ‘never look back or they will find you’
                          • Hunter: The Vigil – Twist on Personal Horror of ‘Fighting with monster watch out to become one’
                          • Geist: The Sin-Eaters – Horror of ‘dead don’t forget’
                          • Mummy: The Curse – Horror of how much you forgot ( I suspect here, did not read more than few spoilers of game )
                          • Demon: The Descent – Paranoid Horror of ‘God Machine can be everywhere’
                          • Beast: The Primordial – Horror of ‘I’m monster and I NEED to feed’ – It can be said as variant on Personal Horror of Vampire.
                          • Deviant: The Renegades – Probably it will be a mix of Demon and Beast horrors in a way ‘What have they made me into?!’
                          Nominally Werewolf, Promethean and Beast have the least connection with Personal Horror.
