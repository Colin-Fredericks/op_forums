              I'd wondered about adding a "factional" splat - while Hunger can change, it's not something that happens often or easily, and isn't really chosen - it's more like two X-splats than an X and Y. Which is interesting, but I'd thought about a Y.

              Preachers: These are the ones that take the teaching-through-fear aspect of the second draft to heart. How they teach may coincide with how they feed, or the two may be orthogonal. Either way, while they take what they need from humans, they see themselves - sincerely, wishfully, or hypocritically as the case may be - serving humanity as a whole. At their worst that's an excuse for their abuses, while at their best it's a reason for them to hunt down Beasts who prey wantonly.

              Isolates: One thing I like about Beast, especially the first draft, is its potential for Beasts to engage with the world much as mortals do - they frequently feed via normal-seeming activites, they don't have a society that imposes a supernatural life on them to replace their human one. So Isolates are the Beasts who go that route - their lives mostly parallel their former human ones - even if they have extra concerns with their Hungers and the hidden world around them. In this, they can be as good and bad as humans are.

              Revelers: "The Predator must hunt. The Collector must take. The Tyrant must rule. These are not cravings; they are categorical imperatives." That's the heart of the Revellers. Feeding their Horrors is simply right. Revelers feed without guilt or shame, though not necessarily without conscience: while feeding may be right, that doesn't mean a Beast won't ever try to balance that with other things she thinks are right.

              Mediators: The Mediators are the ones who take matters of family and extended family most to heart. They seek out other supernaturals, and use their kinship abilities to build themselves a place as ambassadors, messengers, and diplomats - getting on with your cousins is good, but helping them get on with each other is even better. More selfishly, it's a route to power, because opportunities to form kinship ties are plentiful.

              [I'd want a fifth of course]


              For a proper Z, I'd go with what MCN suggested, except I'd make it explicitly based on both: your Z* comes from a specific intersection of your Family and Hunger. One effect would be to lock in your Hunger permanently, so it can't change even under the rare circumstances it otherwise might.

              * Maybe call it Anima, Ador, or Genius.

                      I think you've fundamentally misunderstood what a Z-splat is.

                      The lettered pattern starts at X-splat for the inborn and unchangeable aspect of your template, goes on to Y-splat for the social/philosophical axis of the splat as a whole that your character identifies themself by, and stops at Z-splat, which is typically an overtly optional degree of further specialization like bloodlines or lodges or Legacies or entitlements.

                      You're describing a demisplat or a B-splat, which is a wholly separate category of entity that you don't specialize into if it has any causal relationship with the main/A-splat whose name is in the game title.
