    The Monster from The Ritual (will be referring to said monster as a she, as the director confirmed it was a she) screams Beast the Primordial to me.

    She's a Namtaru Predator who's likely earned her Inheritance, seeing as she never leaves her Horror form, I'm assuming the one she took was the Merger. The forest is her Lair and the pagans in the film are her Cultists, the mummies in the attic her Heralds that earned eternal life in service to their master.

    The Monster relies more on Nightmares than anything, Her kit includes Soldier's Heart, The Walls Have Eyes and You Deserve This and she uses them to break her prey before the kill. Her most prominant Atavisms are Unbreakable and Enemy Within.

    Spoilers for the end of the movie below

    There are multiple ways to view the ending and why Luke was able to leave the Monster's Lair and she remained inside. Perhaps she willingly let Luke go, he had tried multiple times to kill her and she may have been impressed with his perseverance. The fact she'd feasted on both his fears and that of his friends would certainly leave her in a better mood.

    Or perhaps the Monster can't actually leave her Lair anymore and Luke simply got lucky.

    Or...Luke may very well be a budding Beast himself, and the Monster recognized an infant family member. Would explain why she marked him in the first place, she knew he resonated with the Primordial Dream.

                            Whenever Mob regains 1 Willpower from his Vice or uses Patter Scouring to remove an emotion-based Condition from his Pattern, the ST places a token on his character sheet. On a Failure, Mob becomes completely overwhelmed by his current emotional state, which shapes his every Imago and removes his usual inhibitions regarding the harmful use of magic. He is considered to suffer from the Rampant and Fugue Conditions for the rest of the scene. He retains enough awareness to distinguish friend from foe and can contain Paradox, but otherwise acts in accordance with what whatever emotion is taking control of him. Any rolls for Wisdom Degeneration are postponed to the end of the scene.

                                    And now, from the obscure property folder:

                                    Purge (HERO System)

                                    Rather than a single individual, the vicious vigilante known as Purge is actually a series of people that have bonded with the symbiotic armor of the original Purge, Nigel Coburn. A powerful Remade with relatively potent psychic powers, Coburn realized his time would soon be up, and during his last battle, he controlled his final Blowout to burn a mental pattern of his ideology into his suit, along with the ability to learn, repair itself, find bearers, and a canny tactical mind. From there, the suit regularly finds its way into the hands of people across the chronicle's urban area, seeking out individuals with a profound, overwhelming grudge to chose as its temporary master; it was Coburn's belief that only genuine hurt from cruel actions produces the level of hate that the suit is attracted to, so it doesn't judge the morality of the feeling, only that the person is willing to kill to see justice done. It is for this reason that Purge is a nightmare of police departments; because "he" can just as easily be an Iraq vet punishing the murder of his family by a serial killer protected by the Mob, a wife finally boiling over for her physical and emotional abuse, or a murderer who had his ego punctured by the police actually pinning his crimes on him, it's impossible to build a psychological profile apart from "deeply hates singular targets, willing to kill others who attack him to an unpredictable degree." The suit does not care; all it knows is that its wielder has a mission, and it seeks to assist them.

                                    The current Purge is treated as a normal human with an Aspiration based around avenging a personal wrong, with some Supernatural Merits at most - the suit doesn't bond with supernatural beings for fear of damaging its internal psionic circuitry. Upon binding with the suit, gains the ability to dematerialize and materialize it at will for as long as they own it. In fact, if Purge is enraged, they must succeed at a Resolve + Composure roll to not instinctively summon the distinct red and black cloth of the face-covering armor. While wearing it, the current Purge has the general physical profile of Coburn, a 6'7'' muscular man with relatively feminine hips; the armor deliberately configures itself to hide Purge's current real identity. While bonded, Purge must succeed at a Resolve + Composure roll to pursue any action apart from furthering their Aspiration, protecting their identity, or survival. They do not suffer breaking points based around actions pursuing that goal while, although they will have to roll one with a -3 penalty when the suit unbinds itself for the sudden jolt of their original self snapping back and no longer having the haze block the cost of what they're doing. They also instinctively grow angry at the idea of innocents (in their view) being hurt, and if they fail or chose not to resist the urge to suit up, Purge will instinctively intervene to protect them and punish the oppressors if possible in the same scene - failure to do so successfully results in a breaking point. Once the vengeful Aspiration is resolved, the suit pressures the wearer to transport it nearby a new host, which it can sense from miles away; once a new master is found or the current Purge is dead, the suit reverts to an unclaimed state and calls to the vengeful to accept its service; to assist, it can teleport once per week with a range of about two city blocks, including as soon as it thinks nobody is looking should its master be killed.

                                    The current Purge is treated as a Horror; because the psionic circuitry interacts differently with each host to give the armor new capabilities, there is also a "free pool" of two Dread Powers to add, and its Attributes are distributed by the GM among their respective categories (the veteran may be a brute with highly boosted Strength and Dexterity with Dread Powers based around endurance and direct combat, while an actor may be a stealthy planner with higher Wits and Composure, for whom the armor serves as a Chameleon Horror and can use a Hypnotic Gaze even in civilian form).

                                    Attributes: Power 10, Finesse 7, Resistance 12

                                    Skills: Same as the wearer, but raise Athletics, Brawl, Firearms, Survival, and Streetwise to 5, and chose three from that list to add Specialties to; the armor learns from its bearers and offers specialized skills needed to complete its master's missions.

                                    Merits: Add Ambidextrous, Gunslinger, Quick Draw, Disarm, Danger Sense, Fast Reflexes 3, Fleet of Foot 3, Iron Stamina 3, Heavy Weapons 5, Tolerance for Biology to the character sheet of the master for as long as they remain the master, even in civilian form (and in fact this is one of the clues to discover Purge's current identity; people notice).

                                    Potency: 6

                                    Dread Powers: Hunter's Senses (target of Aspiration), Surprise Entrance, two others depending on current identity.

                                    Armor: 2/3, while suit is materialized.

                                    Vice: Changes to Ruthless for duration of being Purge

                                    Ban: Besides the obsessive nature of Purge as outlined above, the armor's enhanced Attributes and Skills do not apply while dematerialized, as they are traits of the suit itself, and the armor's master can only use one of their custom Dread Powers while in civilian form. In addition, being mentally fused with a wearable psychic killbot made by a violent fanatic isn't good for rational thought; each master has a unique Ban based on their personal psychological problems representing an aggravated mental problem for as long as they are bond to the suit (the veteran may need to roll Resolve + Composure to avoid being fascinated by wounded soldiers being cared for by their family, while the angered wife may need to follow any reports of domestic abuse she personally is told about, no matter how credible they are).

                                                Speaking of, here's the character from the Asylum I think salvageable:

                                                Dr. Franklin Reeves, the Freak (HERO System)

                                                Family: Namtaru (Unfettered)

                                                Legend: Childish

                                                Rank: 5

                                                Attributes: Power 14, Finesse 12, Resistance 7

                                                Corpus: 11

                                                Willpower: 10

                                                Essence: 45

                                                Size: 4

                                                Speed: 34

                                                Initiative: 19

                                                Defense: 7

                                                Influences: Fear 1, Phobias 4

                                                Numina: Awe, Bane Sense, Dement, Drain, Hallucination, Implant Mission, Invade Dream, Innocuous

                                                Manifestations: Fetter, Mortal Mask, Nightmare Apparition, Realm Gateway: The Shadow, Realm Gateway: Temenos, Realm Gateway: Underworld, Unfetter

                                                Ban: At his core, the Freak is a dark parody of his living self when he was about seven years old, As his frequent demands for others to "play with him" indicate, he really doesn't have much of an agenda beyond waiting for his mentor, Dr. Gans, to finish his Incarnation and his "game" of picking at the Integrity of his current "playmates". He cannot use Implant Mission except in the wake of his target losing an Integrity dot, and then only to act out something directly related to their own personal issues; he can command someone who doesn't think she's truly human to disguise herself and kill anyone who knows she's really not who she says she is, but not command her to lead him to a new feeding ground.

                                                Bane: A copy of the first draft of Dr. Reeves' post-doctoral thesis; it symbolically forces the Freak to confront he isn't actually an echo of a childhood of a man who doesn't exist anymore, but a lost Horror.

                                                    Effect: Whenever the Batter's Health is filled with Aggravated damage, he does not really die. A Success lets him manifest for the scene, while an Exceptional Success lets him manifest for the entire chapter. Furthermore, any Beast may willingly bring him back at any time. Doing so is as simple as opening a Primordial Pathway for him to step through. The Batter manifests inside the Lair of another Beast who the summoner has met personally and can name. If the Batter returns this way, he remains until killed again by a Beast. Summoning the Batter causes the Notoriety Condition.

                                                    Indomitable (Epic, ••••)
                                                    Prerequisites: Advanced Indomitable, Resolve •••••
                                                    Effect: Any time you successfully resist or contest a supernatural power using Indomitable, the opponent suffers the Shaken Condition.

                                                            I know this is going to sound reaaaaally freaking stupid? But I've recently been on an old cartoon-network cartoons binge trying to remember what kind of fever dream my childhood was (long story short it was a fever dream surprise!)

                                                            Which made me watch Cow and Chicken. At first I was like "whyyy did I watch this as a kid again" but then I suddenly became *fascinated* when my brain was like "OMG THE RED GUY IS TRUE FAE AND COW AND CHICKEN ARE IN ARCADIA" and I was actually laughing more at how much Red Guy *fits* as Fae than I was at the actual show. The way he switches roles using his Title of the Red Guy, how seamlessly he plays around in his world, if it is even his cuz, given how weak he is, the world might be some other True Fae who also only has one Title, leaving neither with much, uh, control.

                                                            The existence of Contracts sort of made the show more watchable too, as I suddenly realized that the kid's strange fixations might have to do with taboos and Contracts, like, the whole Supercow thing comes from Cow making a Contract to protect her brother in exchange for power even Red Guy can't deal with.

                                                            It also explains why these kids are baaasically adults in animal bodies, mind-broken into their rolls as kids to hobgoblin parents that they've forgotten the time they were really grown-ass adults. The world's capacity to do that would also explain why the Red Guy can get away with it too. The show also often contradicts itself about whether the main characters are adopted or born naturally, and being in Arcadia and changelings somehow makes it more sensible.

                                                            Okay so that's the really weird one.

                                                            Less weird was Dexter's Laboratory, which I decided Dexter is a Stigmatic in service to the God-Machine and builds Infrastructure endlessly in a facility set aside for Dexter to do this very purpose. All his advances go to the Machine, rather than humanity. Anytime Dexter gets out of hand, the G-M restarts the timeline and reboots Dexter's life, and on and on it goes, Dexter toiling away on gigantic towers of electronics and endless experiments that all seem to add up to *nothing*. Why is there so much stuff lying around that does seemingly nothing? The most valuable circuitry isn't what the human eye can see! Not even the engineer can see the occult mainframe. Perhaps his computer is an Angel.

                                                            My favorite part of this particular bit of madness is that, Dexter was shown in an episode to be terrible at efficiency and even an average electrician was able to massively improve any aspect of the Lab, far above Dexter, kind of hinting that a practiced and trained professional is still out of Dexter's league, so, how did he build all that stuff, and what's it actually doing if its so crappy?

                                                            Oh ye of little faith young Dexter, he needn't know the answers, as long as he does his experiments, God obtains Its Output.
