    Tracking Damage for Antagonists

    08-25-2025, 02:19 PM
    My homebrew system uses a health box system just like chronicles does. It works exactly the same. I love about everything about the Bashing/Lethal/Aggravated system, except for one thing : It is such a chore to track on antagonists.

    Sure when the party is fighting a single enemy, it goes fairly well.

    Where I begin to struggle is when multiple adversaries are involved. There are some things I already do to speed things up :
    • When an enemy takes more lethal damage than their stamina in one hit, I remove them from the fight.
    • I don’t let every adversary fight till the end, when they realise they are losing, they start to flee.
    • I ''wing it'' with some weaker ennemies, they are either dead 1 one massive hit, or dead in two regular hit, but i kind of dislike doing this, there's too much risk of confusion, and some weaker ennemies still wear armor that would realistically make them survive a few hit.
    It works well when only one type of damage is dealt, but when mulitple type of damage starts stacking up, it becomes a nightmare.

    For example, if a stamina 4 human(9 health box) sits at 5 lethal, 3 bashing, and they take 4 bashing, it means they suddenly find themselves at 8 Lethal, 1 Bashing.

    To me, this is not intuitive at all, I had to take a sheet of paper to graph it out. Its not that hard, but when running a fight with multiple players and multiple opponents, the time it takes to calculate damage can rack up easily.

    Currently, I use an excel sheet that stacks type of damage in a way that shows me when an NPC’s health track is full of a certain type of damage and if that track has any ‘’over’’ that I need to convert, but it’s fair from perfect.

    As anyone had that problem before?

    How do you folks handle it?

      First, the "Beaten Down" rules for CoD 2nd Edition has it that humans flee/give up upon taking more than Stamina in Bashing, regardless of how many hits to get there, or any level of Lethal or Aggravated. I personally think it works fine most of the time.

      But really, I usually don't bother with damage type if it won't make a big difference.

      Most opponents will leave combat when full of damage regardless of type. Mortals can't function full of Bashing, others will still prefer to give up rather than start accruing Lethal. If they're not willing to die in this fight, then they'll probably give up as soon as they take half their Health in damage. If push comes to shove, almost everyone will still stop and fall when full of Lethal.

      In this take, in most battles damage type serves only to decide if the fallen opponents are just unconscious, dying or dead, and you only have to know the type of their last Health Box, so I make a judgement call on it without really calculating and call it a day. The other function is to track healing, but most of the time I won't track healing for NPCs.

      So I only track damage types in their Health Track fully when the dramatic needs of the scene calls for it. The PCs must pull their punches to not kill, they're on a hit-and-run tactic where tracking healing for their target is relevant, the opponent is resilient to their attacks and tracking rollover is the whole point, things like that.

              Probably, if your main intent is to have traditional fantasy battles that are regular and end with death most of the time.

              Originally posted by Algarik View Post
              but I really love how the basic health box system works for player characters.
              PCs aren't NPCs.

              While I understand the appeal, I really do, but you shouldn't be so attached to the idea of using the exact same rules for PCs and NPCs.

              I have a couple suggestions that may interest you. Choose one if you like it.

              1- Simplify the rules for NPCs, they just track damage. If they should be able to fight full of Bashing, double their Health Boxes. If they should be able to fight full of Lethal, triple their Health Boxes. When PCs deal damage to enemies, double Lethal if they have double Health Boxes, triple Aggravated if they have triple Health Boxes. You'll have something sufficiently similar to how the system works, but will only have to track damage straight;

              2- Simplify one rule for everyone: remove roll over damage. If a character is full of Bashing, the next Bashing damage becomes Lethal. If a character is full of Lethal, the next damage becomes Aggravated. But the attack that fills their Health Bar discards any excess damage, so if a character has 2 unfilled Health Boxes and takes 4 Bashing, they only take 2 Bashing and discard the other two.

              Option 1 makes for a result that may take some time to get used to, but is mathematically very similar to the current system. The enemy will require basically the same amount of hits to defeat they would require, however you combine this damage.

              You really change how the game plays out a little with option 2, but not necessarily in a bad way. It changes the strategy around the decision over which type of damage to apply and slightly increases the advantage of causing a "better" type of damage. It also guarantees that using an inferior type to the one required by the enemy won't ever 1-hit kill them, which in a combat-heavy strategic kind of game is a win in my book.
