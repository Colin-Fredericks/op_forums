                                                Originally posted by Mrmdubois View Post
                                                It does screw with the idea that the soul is made up of and influenced by the subtle arcana though.
                                                The "made of the subtle Arcana" was an in setting theory, to explain why the Subtle Arcana each had influence over the soul. Not saying it is a bad theory, but there were other suggested explanations of the nature and composition of the soul, as well as inconsistencies with said theory in the mechanics in 1e. For example, Archmastery of Death (I forget what Dot requirement) allowed wholesale creation of a Soul without other Arcana, which points to Death having sole purview of the soul as a complete entity; this doesn't completely contradict said theory, but raises questions about it.Also, the logic behind the Subtle Arcana making up the soul wasn't always able to supoort how they directly interacted: fate for example was more indirect soul effects, such as being Unfettered and its benefits(i.e. preventing Soul Theft) which stemmed more from it just countering magic that would be to the detriment of a mage destiny rather than being specific to the soul.

                                                If 2e cleans things up and places the soul primarily under the purview of just Death, it might help mechanically make things more consistent and change up a bit of the cosmology of the soul. Still, it doesn't completely invalidate that idea though, so who knows what 2e will bring.

                                                            I've felt it was fairly obvious that what Mages describe as "souls" as influenced by the Death Arcanum cannot, in fact, be the entire soul. If a Mage with a Legacy (specifically said to be something that shapes your soul) loses their "soul" due to magic and then reacquires a "soul", even one from a Mage with a different Legacy or a Sleeper, they keep their Legacy. A Mage that loses their "soul", becomes a Sleeper, and then regains any "soul" (again, even a Sleeper's), once again Awakens even in 1e; Awakening is also a process that modifies the soul's connection to the Supernal.

                                                            Any character that loses their "soul" has the effects of their Virtue and Vice swapped, but they do not replace their Virtue or Vice. This, again, indicates that simply losing their "soul" does not actually affect who they are in any fundamental way any more than losing a hand would. And if a character were to get a new "soul", from any source, they do not change anything about themselves (i.e. Virtue and Vice and overall personality stay the same or are changed only insofar as events would normally change them).
