    [2E] Grappling Zombies

    10-13-2016, 12:16 PM
    I prepare Halloween 'mortals vs. zombies' one-shot story for our RPG club. I wanted to use new Tilt Cards, especially Immobilized Tilt to eat victims brains. For this, I need zombies that can really grab characters. What my surprise when I looked on Horde Horror's stats and see that 1 zombie only have 1 in Grappling Dicepool. With roll limited by Defense of target ( at least 2 in mortals, 4-5 in PCs ) my zombies could not grabbed anyone on -1 to -4 dices.

    What can be changed in their CoD Rulebook write-ups to make this works? Probably Best At Dicepools but I still try to grasp mechanics of Brief Nightmares. How does contest Strenght + Brawl roll is does when Zombies do not have those Attributes?

      Roll it's relevant dice pool against the other character's Strength + Brawl.

      So if we have a person with Strength + Brawl 7 in total up against a zombie which has Grappling as it's best dice pool, you'll just roll those against each other.

          The Horde is meant to model Ramero-style slow zombies, the kind you see in The Walking Dead. They're meant to be practically harmless alone, a nuisance at best, but deadly in numbers, hence the 1-die pool at doing anything alone and the 7-dice pool at dragging targets down by weight of numbers. You can think of the stat block as representing a large group of them all acting together. Instead of each individual zombie trying to grapple a different target with their pathetic 1-die pool, they all pile on top of a single target and roll 7 dice to grapple them all together. Since they also have 7 health, you could say each health box is one zombie, and each one you kill reduces their "best at" dice pool by 1.

          If you want more of a Return of the Living Dead style story where each individual zombie is a serious threat on its own, you could try changing their "best at" and "worst at" pools to make the stat block represent a single, powerful zombie instead of a swarm of weak ones. Maybe your zombies are best at grappling and fighting through massive physical damage instead of dragging you down by weight of numbers and being a wall of flesh. Maybe they're worst at discerning live victims from corpses or something instead of acting alone.

          Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
          What can be changed in their CoD Rulebook write-ups to make this works? Probably Best At Dicepools but I still try to grasp mechanics of Brief Nightmares. How does contest Strenght + Brawl roll is does when Zombies do not have those Attributes?
          Brief Nightmares don't have Attributes or Skills. They use their "best at", "worst at", or "all other actions" dice pools any time a roll is called for. So, if your zombies are best at grappling, they roll 7 dice vs. the target's Strength + Brawl.

                          Well, part of the reason TWD and other Ramero zombies can be deadly individually is that all it takes is one bite and you're dead. Maybe not immediately, but within a matter of hours. In CofD this is represented by their Toxic • Dread Power. One zombie shouldn't be too hard to deal with, but you still have to be careful because even that 1-die pool jumps up to 4 if it spends a point of Willpower (which it can do 3 times per scene) and 6 if or also attacks all-out (which as a mindless creature with no sense of self-preservation, it should be doing every turn). When all it takes is one successful bite attack to deliver an incurable illness, you don't want individual zombies having a dice pool any higher than that unless you want your game to be a PC kill fest. I would recommend focusing less on tweaking zombies' stats and more on nailing the mechanics for the disease.

                          Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                          And by all means - I look for scenario like 'more of zombies, more dead you are'.
                          Well when you've got a small group of them, that grapple dice pool jumps up to 7 to start with and a whopping 12 if they spend Willpower and go all-out. Players are definitely going to want to stay out of melee range until they've whittled the numbers down. I would just put a damage threshold on it - at full health they grapple with their "best at" pool. One they've taken, let's say 3 aggravated, there are few enough left that they use their "all other actions" dice pool to grapple. 3 more agg and they're down to one zombie, which uses its "worst at" pool.

                          Then there's the Zombies! Tilt for when you just need that writhing mass of putrid bodies. Too many to fight, just run at full speed or get attacked with a 7-die pool that automatically knocks you down in addition to dealing damage, thereby preventing you from running full speed on your next turn and guaranteeing them another chance to do it again.

                                      Oh, no, it doesn't say that anywhere. The Horde stat block just represents a group of zombies, the actual number of them is highly abstract. My initial suggestion to treat each health box as one zombie was so you'd be more easily able to tell when they switched from being a big enough swarm to use their "best at" dice pool to being one lone zombie using its "worst at pool". On second thought, using each health box as one zombie is too precise. I would maintain the abstract nature and just say once The Horde has taken a certain amount of damage, it can't use its "best at" pool any more because there aren't enough of them to drag you down by sheer weight of numbers. And then once it's taken a certain amount more damage it's forced to use its "worst at" dice pool because there's only one left and it's acting alone. In my example, I suggested 3 aggravated and 6 aggravated as the thresholds, keeping in mind that targeted attacks to the head will always deal aggravated damage to The Horde due to its Bane but everything else will only deal one bashing due to its Unbreakable Dread Power.
