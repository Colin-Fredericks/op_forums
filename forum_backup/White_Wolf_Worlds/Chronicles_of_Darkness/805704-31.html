    Also, if you want a really easy way to conceptualise Darklings:

    When faced with the Trolley Dilemma, Darklings don't even see it as a dilemma; they pull the lever with no hesitation. Most people view that as kinda creepy.

Having both in the same society means that society will be lopsided towards one side or other depending on who is most effective and who is most numerous. Here is an example of one extreme towards the monster side of that scale. I draw my inspiration from a notorious Portland LARP community with a reputation of bullying other LARP communities. Please note I did not name which LARP community so if you are offended then you are also acknowledging being part of the problem, and I do not owe you an apology. Also this is a fine testament of why you maintain supernatural conspiracies in the chronicles of darkness because drawing the attention of emotionally damaged changelings who have issues with people being victimized is very dangerous. (The same can be said of righteous or self-righteous Mages, Vampires, or Werewolves but Lost being mentally unbalanced raises volatility in their case.)

                I think most of changeling society is going to be shaped by the Courts of a given Freehold, barring major upheavals that change the current courts for new ones. So yeah, I agree, but I also think that there's plenty of room for all sorts of Freehold attitudes and changeling psychologies.

                One thing I forgot to mention that I thought of last night, re: Darklings et al., is that Seemings are not something you have to embrace. When you escape Arcadia, the Wyrd grants you the Seeming based on the action you took to escape. The Wyrd is a force that empowers narrative in the world, and making a dramatic narrative statement, such as escaping, draws the attention of the Wyrd. The Wyrd then looks at you and says "This is who you are," and grants you your Seeming. The mechanics for gaining and risking Clarity are about your own psychological responses to that, however you want to justify them. Maybe the Darkling regains Clarity when he makes a hard decision for another person because they're performing a penance for the people they betrayed during their Durance. They think that since they're already "tainted", they can do the dirty/risky work for others, and that in so doing, they will eventually find absolution.

                The way I see Seemings is as a sort of nonverbal "contract" between the changeling and the Wyrd. The changeling escapes, proves that they're narratively important, and the Wyrd gives them a gift, one that comes with a Blessing and Curse, and goes "Okay, you got my attention, now go and be someone. Don't disappoint me."

                My homebrew hub.

                        I weather an action risks clarity or gains depends on the perpective of the Changeling more then objective fact. If the Changeling thinks he's in control of a situation, but in reality is relying on blind faith, but doesn't realize it, it doesn't activate the curse, but if the Darkling believes he's acting in blind faith, even if she's just being paraniod and isn't acting on blind faith, then the curse activates. Just personal opinion, this makes the in character perpective the abiter of the curse/blessing, not some attempt at external objectivity for something that will usually be entirely subjective.

                        So the question becomes does the CHARACTER believe based on their beliefs/values, that an action is blind faith, or honourable betrayal for the greater good.

                        This stops things from deevolving into a constant debate between the Storyteller and the players on if an action counts or not.
