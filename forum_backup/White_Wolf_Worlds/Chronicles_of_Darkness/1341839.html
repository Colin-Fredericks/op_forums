    Demon Built Infrastructure

    10-17-2019, 08:13 AM
    Hey! I was just reading Night Horrors Enemy Action and the Demon Storyteller's guide. I noticed that...Demons can actually learn to use Arcane Physics themselves and build their own infrastructure. of course without the skill and power of the god machine itself, but what i wondered...is there is an actual system for this? Like i know that you have the existing systems for creating infrastructure when the st is doing it on behalf of the god machine, but if let's say the players in a game are inquistiros all pursuing the hell/path of the Arcane Physicist. I kinda would like there to be a system for that, so it doesn't fall into complete handwavium on my part.

    For Sources on this see page 54 in Night Horros Enemy Action and page 27 in The Demon Storytellers Guide

              The problem with having a system in place to make Infrastructure is that it kind of undercuts how dang hard the task is. Out of all the characters we know about only one is confirmed to actually be able to create Infrastructure, who has lived since the age of the Romans, has studied Infrastructure all that time, and as the text notes even then he still has problems getting a reliable output to form from years of work alongside other projects.

              Infrastructure is not just about knowing various arcane facts. It’s about proper timing on a universal scale, knowing innate facts about the location it is being done in, and having the right materials no matter how arbitrary it may be to find/make them. That isn’t to say players can’t eventually get to a semi-similar state, but it would probably take a lot more game time and character development than most games really last/allow for, and handwaving it seems unwise.

              Hense the import of Handwavium. Ultimately it is up to the ST if they will allow players to make their own Infrastructure or not.

                  Wednesday is both old enough, broken enough, and practiced enough that, to him, there's a strong intuitive sense of how Infrastructure works-he knows it like a musician knows the math of their own compositions.                           1) Intentionally, it's vague. Too much insight into how Wednesday's angels work is insight into how normal angels work, and since that should be one of the big mysteries of the G-M since that can be used against It in big ways, I didn't answer it too much beyond pointing out that we know how angels operate.

                          Now one thing worth noting is that Wednesday doesn't have the personal infrastructure to do everything the G-M does, so a lot of the instant update of information that angels are privvy to for minor information isn't quite there-but that said, it seems reasonable that angels are able to pick up on his knowledge base concerning their mission. In as much as the G-M relies on Messengers as well for angels, at times, Wednesday also relies on Messenger Imperatives-I'll say now that Messenger and Analyst Imperatives are a relatively easy Output (which does not mean it's actually easy, but Wednesday can reliably pull them off.) Now, whether he is always aware of what they are doing or needs to do things the old fashioned way while the angels need updates, that's up to the storyteller.

                          The best way to think of it is that Wednesday is a small scale G-M, with the main disadvantage of being that while the God-Machine is the sum total of Infrastructure in existence, Wednesday the Demon is very much a central brain unit for the "Wednesday-Machine"-while he is very much the sum total of his own infrastructure, you put a bullet in his head, it all does go down.

                          That doesn't have to always be the case, though.

                          2) Yep! Wednesday's Angels can Fall! We sort of assumed that would be assumed.

                          Admittedly, we didn't think about the implications of that all the way through, because space and time are concerns. Regarding his angels, he has a benefit and a curse-because Wednesday isn't as rigid in his missions, there's a lot of normal circumstances where an angel would Fall but doesn't-but because he's not as rigid in his missions, there's actually a lot of new ways they can Fall, and a hell of lot more where they can wander off into Abandonment.

                          His primary answer to this leads into the above situation-he tries to avoid creating the sort of situations where Falling will happen, and he uses his latitude that the G-M doesn't demonstrate to cover unexpected scenarios. It's a good way to explore how the G-M became the way it did.

                          One way he is instantly like the G-M though is his response to Falls and Abandonment-while he plans long and therefore this needs to be understood in that context-as soon as that hole in his knowledge is formed, he starts to prioritze a plan to kill them. Sometimes that involves getting them to rely on as much of sense of safety in demonic society as can be managed before assassinating them, sometimes that involves taking advantage of the difference of experience to alienate and isolate the demon, and sometimes that involves dropping plans for the evening and hunting down the thing himself, but Wednesday tolerates misbehaving equipment less than the God-machine does. If enough of his angels fell to have Agendas regarding him, Turncoats would notably be rarer.

                          Worth noting is that Wednesday is also like the G-M in that he is very sparing in terms of making angels.The world is lousy with angels because the G-M has had the whole of human history and Not Failing As Bad As Wednesday Has (Hercules once squared off with him and Cacus-the story that humanity got is heavily truncated for [reasons])-meanwhile, Wednesday may be millenia old, but he doen't have the benefit of possibly having been around since time began, and he has often stumbled into an all too human problem of putting all his eggs in one Spear of Destiny and having it blow up in his face(weird metaphor is weird) due to emotion, acknowledgement of mortality, all the usual reasons, and of course it took him a long time to know how to do this(how long is up to the G-M, don't let me tell you you can't have Wednesday in the Sundered World making angels), so he doesn't have all the infrastructure in the world to churn things out. But Once you get around the volume comparison, he tends to make angels when he is very sure it's the right answer for the job and very sure the chances of Falling or Abandonment are minimal, particularly under a good set of parameters. Again, like the G-M.

                          Now regarding the fun question here, what would it mean to be a Wednesday Demon?

                          My central argument is that early on, it's freeing and at the same time limiting. His demons are still plugged into the network, so to speak, but at a remove, so compromise rolls inherently have a +3 bonus to them against the normal God-Machine (if you don't get a success, roll the three to see if Wednesday notices). On the other hand, Embeds are more limited to get a hold of. Any Mundane Embed can be gotten, but Storyteller's should probably figure out with the player which half of Cacophonic and Instrumental Embeds are missing, and be creatively minimal with Vocal. This lasts until the Demon is Primum 3 or 4, at which point the demon has asserted enough self-identity that they also plug properly into the wider G-M's network-though Wednesday may still be aware of them until they do something to seperate themselves completely from his infrastructural awareness, if such is possible.

                          Ciphers and Keys should be weird, and I would argue any Key obtained before the Primum rise asides from the Initial key should muddle with the entire affair, resulting in glitched Ciphers and off-beat Interlocks(even for Interlocks). If a Demon is properly plugged into the wider network, performing the Pentagram may "fix" things in as much as it "fixes" anything. The ideal, though, would be to not start on the Cipher until the Demon is freed up, as if any demon is going to do that.

                          The large point is that Wednesday is a Cosmic-tier antagonist because it gives your players a real angle to learn things about the God-Machine, as well as to explore what a more human like God-Machine would be like (and why that would be terrifying), and what happens when Gods collide. Players should get an opportunity to love him, fear him, and ultimately dread, defy, and end him, because his role is to microcosmically compare and contrast with the God-machine.

                          And beyond all of that, the large point of Wednesday is that he is a broken person who's stuck in his own cycle and is doing the best he can to do what he thinks is right-and maybe, underneath all of that, fix the holes in himself. He should be allowed to show himself as silly and liking to be around people, he should be allowed to show fear and remorse over losing his memories and loved ones-and he should absolutely be allowed to show how he turns all of that into fuel for cold calculation that plays over centuries, steel-hearted sacrifices that cut down anyone unessential, and arcane humanism that will ascend the masses into terrible nightmares.

                          He's a broken person who is far too good at playing God-Machine.
