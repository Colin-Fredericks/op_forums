        Originally posted by Michael Brazier View Post
        I don't quite see how Deviant covers the ground claimed by Genius or Princess. The themes of Genius are covered by Mage - in both games you play someone with incredible power, but not the wisdom to use it well. That's not a natural fit for Deviant, where the guiding metaphor is "powers are a handicap" and the main enemies are organizations of ordinary people.
        The literal core mechanic of Deviant as a gameline is that you need to obsessively pursue the ruination of someone or something — deserving or otherwise — or protect and serve someone with a probably-unhealthy level of devotion — ditto — in order to keep your soul from degenerating your existence in a variety of fun and awful ways, including paranoia, loss of empathy, escalatory murderous anger issues, splinter personalities, addiction to superpowers, and more. It is also a gameline where your specific supernatural powers are the majority of your template abilities and reproducing them is within the scope of an organization you may now or once have worked for or controlled, with a not-insubstantial amount of experimental-subject horror baked into the Origins and the examples of what conspiracies can be.

        "Point-built powers-at-a-price on both sides of the Victor/Igor divide" doesn't stop being a strong contender for the same headspace just because the base game supplies a unifying antagonist framework that gives you people who're easy to hate to draw Conviction Touchstones from; a game of Deviant can just as easily see Renegades tormenting people with no ties to the Web of Pain, just like you can run a Ghost Wolves game with no tribal interaction or an Uncalled game where your Messenger tries to lay low in an Infrastructure town.

        And with Princess - well, the biggest knock on that fansplat is that it doesn't fit the Chronicles setting at all, and it's partly justified, too. Whereas Deviant, with its half-broken protagonists and occult conspirators with nefarious plans, is probably the official game closest to the tone of the original blue book. The only overlap I see is that both games can do some kinds of superhero stories, and even there the similarities aren't that close.
        To repeat: Half of the gameline's core mechanic is having someone or several someones who you need to protect. The aesthetic breadth of Variations is such that "you can manifest a magic sword at any time" is a sample skinning of the basic Harmful Attack Power, and the breadth of Scars is such that you can have bad luck be the primary consequence of failing to treasure your relationships as a reincarnated psychic half-angel.

        Far be it from me to overplay the prominence of Pretty Cure in the genre, but when mahou shoujo now has sixteen generations of tokusatsu-adjacent material to work with it doesn't take a lot of effort to see "evil corporation that makes kaijin" and "demonic otherworldly invaders and their legions of scrubs" massaged into street-level conflicts between Guardians and the servants of the greater-scope elements of the Web of Pain. (Or, if that's not your bag, "use your magic powers/try to resist using your magic powers to make your friends' lives easier" is also an established subset of genre content.)

                    "Deviant-adjacent" is exactly right. In Deviant you play a victim of mad science - even when your PCs did it to themselves. In Genius you play a perpetrator of mad science. Different perspective, leading to different conflicts, mood and themes. It's like putting the story of a military campaign as told by an officer in the victorious army and one told by a refugee from the battles in the same category because both of them are about "war".

                    With magical girls ... I do think the fact that magical girl antagonists are normally an otherworldly invading force, not other humans exploiting secret knowledge, distinguishes them from the stories that inspired Deviant. And while I can see, now, more similarities than I did at first, introducing that kind of antagonist would IMO shift the tone of the game. For instance, you'd lose the aspect of the PCs being fugitives, with only tenuous connections to mundane society. Fighting actual demons leaves a much better impression in the public eye than fighting Men in Black with secret nefarious intentions.

                    Maybe the Devoted Companion will have tools for campaigns like that? Games with a "good" conspiracy fighting other conspiracies, possibly otherworldly, sounds like what we've been promised there.

                                                Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                                                The reason each gameline has its own systems that works in particular ways is because they are designed to facilitate certain themes, moods and stories that the games are about. Those things are the main identity of each game, with the game's monster type being chosen to fit that identity. If you remove the unique systems and replace it with generic systems that are supposed to fit multiple games, then you'll lose the identity of the.
                                                I agree. 2nd edition has put more effort into making the mechanics follow the themes and moods of the games. Haven't played it yet, but from what I see Promethean 2e is a great example of this.
                                                The thing is, this design is *very* oriented towards player characters and specific settings. It works great if you're a PC and play within that gameline's specific theme and setting.
                                                The need for a more streamlined system only arises with NPCs and crossovers.

                                                If you're running a Changeling chronicle and you want to introduce a Promethean NPC, you don't need to flesh out all its Pilgrimage and roles and whatever, just the basic information for personality and background. You could handle it the same way as Hunter does, with basically little more than Horrors rules and Dread Powers. So I guess a more streamlined system would be something somewhere between Horrors and fully-fleshed PC sheets as a toolkit for STs.

                                                Perhaps the problem doesn't come up if you only consider a single game line, but when you compare several it can be really annoying. For instance, the Integrity-like traits are supposed to be somewhat similar, or filling the same role, but they're completely different from each other. Yes, they're one of the main systems that provide unique identity and theme to each game line, but... come on, do they *really* need to have each a different dice roll for breaking points? Vampire rolls a fixed, decreasing dice pool based on a list of breaking points, Werewolf rolls Resolve + Composure, Changeling rolls an *increasing* dice pool + a roll for damage... ugh.
                                                (Speaking of which, there's a Changeling Contract that, when used against non-changelings, temporarily decreases the Integrity-equivalent trait. Which means that werwolves get shifted towards Spirit, Sin-Eaters lose their Supernatural Resistence trait, and Beasts... uhm, lose their Supernatural Resource I guess, or maybe they're immune? There's a lot of implications when considering Traits as equivalent but are actually very different. )
                                                Again, this is only a problem if you're considering crossovers.

                                                More in general, as it is I feel like CofD lacks uniformity and a sense of a global vision. You can find the same "you go unnoticed and people ignore you" power in Vampire Discipline, Werwolf Gift and Promethean Transmutation. There are also a lot of telepathic powers of some sort, that let you learn people's emotions, or Vices etc. that are quite similar to some degree. You might as well have a list of common supernatural powers in a Core rulebook and have each gameline reference them, or perhaps a common framework in the same vein as Twilight rules.
                                                If you want to make a new power that makes you immaterial, you only need five words: "you enter Twilight [frequency] state". That's it. No need to describe every time how it works because it's a universal framework shared between all games. In the same way you could have a universal Telepathic powers framework etc.

                                                For example, the Variations Tags from Deviant, like Overt, Toggled etc. are a great instance of rules framework that could be applied uniformly to all games: activating the Celerity Discipline is Toggled and using Protean is Overt, stuff like that. Or the Krewe rules could be shared as a more general "organizations" rules for Vampire cults, Werewolf packs, Mage labyrinths etc. Do I really need to get a copy of Geist to have a cult system for my Vampire game?

                                                Anyway, apologies for the rant-ish post, and sorry if I'm deviating a bit from the actual thread. This topic would require a thread of its own.

                                                So, new book ideas. As far as classic horror tropes go, I think that Aliens may be one of the few left that could be explored in its own game line. Not sure how though.

                                                            If, hypothetically speaking, there was reason enough to reuse themes from existing games, I would be quite interested in new games based on Beast and Mummy.
                                                            Beast has been badly hurt by its reputation, but it's also, in my personal opinion, not great mechanically speaking, and I also think a lot of people were disappointed by the "type of monster" was chosen for Beast.
                                                            Certain design decisions, such as lack of gameplay loop outside of "feeding leads to complications leads to Satiety expenditure leads to feeding", no organisational systems other than "broods exists, have fun" for a supposedly social splat, and a "build a monster" approach that is lackluster (especially after Deviant) all just don't mesh that well with me.
                                                            The PCs being hosts to the psychic representations of monsters and can draw powers from them is kinda cool in its own way, but it definitely creates a disconnect between monster and PC. You can spend time and effort building a cool Pyramid Head knock-off PC to scare the shit out of people by invoking sirens and "rustic" architecture, but in actual play, it'll be your very human-looking PC posing as not!Pyramid Head. What I'd like to see is Boogeyman: The Spoopy where the character actually transforms into humankind's various fears and embody them more directly. Less Urged and more Claimed, basically.

                                                            For Mummy, well, what I want is probably less a game that retreads the same themes and more a new core book that isn't stupidly opaque. It's like trying to read Werewolf except almost every single splat term is written in First Tongue with no English translations.
                                                            Mechanically, it's also a bit wonky. Most of it doesn't concern me that much, but it's enough that my group refuses to play it even after shifting target numbers were removed so they won't get oWoD PTSD anymore.
                                                            Edit: Basically, what I'm saying is that I'd like a game that's basically Mummy but actually disgestable and also playable for my group so I can actually play it.
