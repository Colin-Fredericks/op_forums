                                                            You're welcome.

                                                            Apart from the fact that this whole outline looks awesome, the bits about Lost Clans especially peeked my interest. I'm currently finishing the Preludes in a modern Athens chronicle, where the main plot point is a slumbering ancient and one of the Vrihi bloodline/clan.

                                                            The Vrihi were clearly intended as a nod to the Brujah and True Brujah of VtM. It all started with my gripe with clan names in VtM and how ahistorical they were. So I came up with the Vrihi (Those Who Roar in ancient Greek), a clan of Philosopher Kings that fled demonic entities from either the Middle East or lower Egypt, and rose alongside the classical Greek civilization (if not directly influencing its emergence). Their weakness? Tragic and awesome. Though they struggled to be detached, scholarly and just rulers, their use of the Beast gave them the ability to influence the general aura of politics in their city states, similar to how feeding grounds get influenced by the Kindred who hunt there. The weakness meant that this tie could become uncontrollable, and in the event of strong and negative experienced emotions (such as Frenzy etc.), that would ooze to the mortals who surrounded them and threaten to ruin the political structures they worked so hard to establish.

                                                            If the Vrihi ancient were to rise, she would discover that her clan now mystically resembles more of a bloodline.

                                                            The chronicle is set to take place during June 2015, the time of the Greek referendum. I do not intend to use the Vrihi is the driving point behind every major event in Greek politics. But the powers and weakness described above, definitely heightened certain pre-existing trends in politics and the people. She's not the reason people disagree, but she might be the reason why people and the country's rulers disagree so passionately.
