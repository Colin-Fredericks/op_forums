                    Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                    I can't fully parse what you're saying but I don't think it's relevant to the fact that the XP cost for Integrity in the Supporting Cast appendix in MtA is just taken from the GMC book instead of the CofD book.
                    Integrity costs 2. Hunter Integrity also costs 2. Humanity costs 2. Wisdom costs 2 and is the only one of these that is paid for exclusively with non-standard Experiences (Arcane Experiences).

                    Cover costs 3 Cover Experiences. Pilgrimage costs 3 Vitriol Experiences. Memory costs 3 Reminisce Experiences. Wisdom costs 2 Arcane Experiences and is the only one of this batch that concerns a character that becomes unplayable when the Trait bottoms out.

                    Harmony goes up based on failed Resolve + Composure rolls to hold onto your spiritual half. Clarity goes up with Icons or Wits or Composure or when you heal it by spending time with Touchstones. Satiety goes up when you feed. None of these interact with the Experience economy except Clarity indirectly.

                    Synergy doubles as a Supernatural Potency Trait and thus costs 5 Experiences (Synergy Experiences or regular) when you're not getting the freebies from Burden and Remembrance. Stability gets added to when you buy Acclimation, which is part of a deviant's Supernatural Potency Trait and priced accordingly. Neither of them are being paid for purely as an Integrity stat, and thus they're as much outliers to the pricing scheme of the other Traits as the free ones.

                    Unless you're trying to pick a fight, my observation has nothing to do with how relevant you think it is to the misprint in the sidebar, which is founded on outdated information old enough to have come up back when the Demon was in the errata-gathering phase. I am presenting precedent for why some Integrity stats cost differently than others. You are not obligated to respond to that.

                                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                                        This is bullshit, despite common in RPGs for whatever reason.

                                                        Garrotes aren't beheading devices and cutting lines only make it less effective. It is a strangulation device and should follow the same rules as a bare hands choke hold, but with equipment bonus to keep the hold.

                                                        A few rare variations have nots that can crush the larynx with the proper maneuver, but that's not the one we see in media and it doesn't work like a normal garrote.

                                                        Cutting with a garrote-like instrument would require far more pressure force than cutting with a blade, with the same edge. Too much to be necessary to track the cutting damage.

                                                        "Cut" was an unfortunate and inaccurate substitute for "kill". As second edition has well noted blunt force trauma kills just as surely as edged weapons, and weapons default to lethal damage with a few specified exceptions.

                                                        What I meant to distinguish was whether it was an implement designed to quickly kill a victim, or an implement designed to render the victim unconscious but able to recover. I am aware that the distinction between the latter and the former can come down to nothing more than the amount of pressure applied or the length of time held. However, what matters to this abstracted and simplified game system is whether the intent of a successful action is to leave the victim rolling (and ultimately failing) to stay conscious, or dead. To that end it does matter to distinguish a weapon that can deal bashing more efficiently than bare hands from one that deals lethal.

                                                            Originally posted by HelmsDerp View Post
                                                            To that end it does matter to distinguish a weapon that can deal bashing more efficiently than bare hands from one that deals lethal.
                                                            Since the garrote kills through strangulation, there's not much to differentiate damage type. Little differences in efficacy may exist, but they would apply more as distinct damage rating or equipment bonus to a maneuver than as differing damage types.

                                                            Different styles of garrote try to achieve more ease on applying or holding the weapon's grapple, because they're still all killing through the same method. And not the same method as in cutting or stabbing, but the same method as in crushing the frontal lobe.

                                                            It is an extremely specific weapon, and that's why it doesn't make sense for changing damage type based on either material or construction. Probably a very effective Bashing weapon, since it usually renders the target unconscious before dying, and its actual advantages are to silence the target, immobilize and keep them under relative control. It's not to kill fast, but to kill silently and minimize struggle.
