    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    If you would make crossover, what would be your mechanical answer for Witch Hunters that are not Sleepwalkers, to effectivly target the Awakened as culprit? When you need to bypass the Lie?

    I ask, as mine Mage game may return to the Witch Hunters plotline, and I would rather not dwell in making every hunter meet by PCs the Sleepwalker.
    I'd handle it two ways, one mechanical the other narrative:
    • Mechanic: Sleepers exposed to supernal magic who fail their Integrity roll can opt to take a Dramatic Failure (as normal) to remember, albeit somewhat hazily, the supernatural scene. So long as they suffer the associated Condition their memory remains intact, but fades when they purchase/earn the dot back. Note, when I say the scene I don't mean that one spell, but the whole even which, given the nature of scenes, could mean one brief stint of combat or a day long journey through worlds beyond.
    • Narrative: In a hunter game especially I'd strive to highlight how mages can seem...unhinged. Not all ravening lunatics, but certainly a tad bizarre. Extreme personality quirks, behaviors and interests would be common*. This can easily be justified from the Mages' point of view as symbolic acts, Yantra, Personas, etc. But the sleepers see only a discordant fellow. Additionally, just because sleepers struggle to recall Supernal magic doesn't mean they struggle to recall the Mage, nor that they cannot suspect the Mage of being 'witchy' if his behaviors are suspect. Finally, hunters with knowledge that weird shit exists (vampires, changelings, etc.) would be allowed to reason out that something supernatural is up with the Mage, even if they only have circumstantial evidence to go on.
    As for fighting against Supernal magic, well, they are pretty fucked in that department. But not appreciably less fucked than if they were, say, staring down the maw of a raging wolf, or standing in the unveiled Sybaris of a newly risen Mummy. Being outclassed is, after all, one of Hunter's running themes.

    *For those who've read or watched any of Nisio Isin's monogatari series, the way side characters (Gaen Izuko, Kaiki Deishuu, Oshino Meme, etc.) tend to behave is an excellent example of how I'd present Mages to sleepers.

                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                            Or are we going back to the bad old days of the cWoD, where we needed Imbued Hunters to be able to have “mortals” who could stand up to the supernaturals without getting slapped down by various ambient mind-warping effects?
                            To be fair, it seems like something that is, so far, not a problem with all tempaltes. Vampires still have their old weaknesses, and while they have ways to get around it, they aren't universal to vampires. And werewolves still have their old weakness. Both seem to have a 'power upgrade' to their abilities and white rooms do point out that in 1 vs 1 or 5 vs 5, the supernaturals have the advantage, but hunters who play smart are still likely to be able to come out victorious, particularly if they don't try for even numbers. Demons and mages seem to pose more of a problem from a hunter standpoint, demons probably less so. The idea behind battling a demon seems to fit the demon story, where you fight through 'host' and 'possessed' and everyone else the demon's made contracts with until it unleashes its final form. Mages, though, have this amnesia aura to their abilities, which seem high indeed. And a problem with the last hunter was, as Dave Brookshaw mentioned, using the actual mage stuff some of the tactics simply wouldn't work. I haven't kept up with the other second editions so I couldn't guess how they'd deal with hunters.

                            So if we do get a Witch Finders second edition equivalent, it would be nice if the things in it were actually applicable towards mages.

                            I like the idea behind Dataweaver's tactic, perhaps giving an effective sleepwalker merit towards mages they've encountered, but the problem with that is now you have a mage who is more powerful than before, as they're not hindered by the hunters being sleepers. Something that pops up in all sorts of hunter fiction is simple witchcraft, little tricks they've learned that can give them an edge against magic. Human occultism is something that happens a ton throughout the World of Darkness books, but there's very little in the way of rules for it, usually in a single chapter of a book where it heavily implies only evildoers touch it. It wouldn't match an Awakened mage but it may give them defenses normal mortals wouldn't have access to, and allow a hunter to create a cell with a plan towards killing a mage without being retroactively cast out of existence.

                                Hunting Awakened mages is exceedingly difficult because supernal magic is so damned versatile (much more than other template powers) and mages are so very different from one another. For instance, say you Hunter group carefully investigates people they believe are mages and prepare to fight an Acanthus or Moros. If their Obrimos or Mastigos cabal-mate shows up, the Hunters are in for a world of hurt.

                                As I indicated earlier, mages, particularly those of a less martial bent or are more merciful, also have a myriad of abilities to easily and quickly escape most situations.

                                Lastly, mages are still essentially human, and do not suffer from any inherent supernatural weaknesses like sunlight or silver. Even the risk of paradox is usually just an annoyance for moderately powerful mages, and not really a concern at all in a life or death struggle with mortal humans.
