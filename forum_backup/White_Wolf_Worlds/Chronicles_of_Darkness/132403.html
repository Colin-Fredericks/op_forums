    A question about Bound 'pataphysics.

    05-11-2014, 01:35 PM
    In games that you have run, what does learning Manifestations/Keys look like? I imagine at least part of it involves tutelage from one's own Geist, but the Stygian Key and Pit Manifestation in Book of the Dead imply delving into the Underworld to learn the proper lore, not to mention Tier-Three Codices, which can impart knowledge of dead magics. So, what have you made it look like? What are your aesthetics for such? Considering the materialist nature of the setting, in my mind I picture a Sin-Eater channeling the power of the Underworld into an actual, physical key to represent her connection to the metaphysical Key.

      For the Keys, I'd do a little bit of studying the general principles of reality ala Mage, then focus on witnessing actual Threshold events and their aftermath: Killing and being killed by their methods and studying ghosts, mementos and anything else with an actualized Threshold. I'd actually award a Key-only XP or two any time somebody dies from something that could be related to a Key they don't have, though there is an obvious limitation to that as a habit. For Manifestations, learning from people who know them could help, but I'd focus on the personal relationship with the Geist itself; All the same things that could be useful for Psyche.

      I don't know about calling any of that 'pataphysical, though. Krewe mythology might be better for that term,

              The main thing about 'pataphysics is how its approach to metaphysics involves total immersion in metaphor in order to touch something at another level of abstraction beyond "meta." Since occultism deals with metaphysics by building elaborate metaphorical myths and acting within them as if they were true in order to get a grip on what's either unknowable or what is literally created by the ideation of metaphors, most kinds of magic could be defined as applied 'pataphysics. With fictional magic, though, a lot of the weird stuff isn't metaphorical at all, so you have to look for the parts that the characters understand to be mythologized. Krewes are excellent for this, as it's pretty clear from their disparity that much of what they're doing is just immersing themselves in a whole system of metaphor, rather than objective reality, and the Krewes that don't get that point are listed under the antagonists: The Sacrosanct.

              While I'm seeing Manifestations as being trained in a manner kind of like physical skills, getting a sense of your Geist and the flow of plasm within yourself, a character seeking to understand the Keys, depending on how they like to think about things, could have any mix of understanding it in objective terms or in mythic terms. The more they integrate their Krewe mythos into their practical skills, the more of a 'pataphysical process it would be, and the metaphysics of all Krewes themselves could be considered 'pataphysical, whether those Krewes agree with you or not. Having a Krewe that's into Alfred Jarry and his subculture and successors is an excellent idea.

              Mage is also really good for 'pataphysical discourse. Interacting with the Supernal their relies on that kind of thing.

                  Baron Samedi made a very detailed list of what it would take for Keys, Manifestations, and Ceremonies for The Balance of Shadows game. I have the document but need to ask him if it's okay to repost. Will get back to you when I know for sure.


                  This is kinda big so I'm putting it under the spoiler cover.


                  ~~Keys

                  Learning Keys involves exposing yourself to whatever element or aspect the keys represent. You must do so for 11-Psyche hours, though this need not be in a row. Once done, you can buy that key anytime. However, you must buy it before you can begin counting hours toward another key. Attempting to pick up the second Key of your Threshold never takes more than 1 hour. The Sin-Eater can meditate, but otherwise cannot be engaged in any other significantly attention demanding activity. They can eat or write or surf the web, but not do research (except about things relating to the key thematically) have in depth conversations (same) while getting in tune with the key. Each also has a “visceral” requirement that must be done at some point over the course of the time the character is getting in tune with the Key.

                  Cold Wind Key: Going around in snow, somewhere windy, somewhere high. The top of the Trinity Church will do, as will the roof of the Dark Haven Bluffs Manor or just sitting and looking out over the bluffs from the top. Standing in a nor’easter or hurricane certainly qualifies.

                  In order to obtain this Key, the character must put themselves in a situation, alone or with help, where they cannot breathe for longer then they can hold their breath.

                  Grave-Dirt Key: Hanging around in a cemetery, a cave, or underground.

                  In order to obtain this Key the character must remain buried alive for more minutes then their Composure (which can prompt a Resolve + Composure roll not to panic).

                  Industrial Key: Spending time around stuff. Almost any time spent in buildings, cars, or gazing at structures. Driving the car is not too much distraction, but engaging in a race or chase is. Housework in a building is fine too.

                  In order to obtain this Key the character must disassemble or reassemble something wholly man made.

                  Passion Key: Spending time with people (Sin-eaters count) will do the requirements for this key. You don’t have to interact with them, but if you are not they have to be interacting with each other; sitting in a silent room won’t do it however many people. Sex or playing sports also counts.

                  In order to obtain this Key the character must engage in passionate intercourse with another, be it an argument, confession of love, sex, or competitive sport (passionate, not a quiet game of chess).

                  Phantasm Key: To get this Key, you must spend time in strange places. Haunts count, thus Dark Haven Bluffs qualifies, but so does the Yale Sanitarium. Anyplace associated with ghosts, madness or illusion, such as a carnival, magic show, or circus counts too.

                  In order to obtain this Key, the character must experience illusion via a hallucinogenic drug trip, vision quest, or even sleep deprivation. Alternately, they can have a ghost successfully use a mind affecting Numina on them.

                  Primeval Key: Spending time outside in an area with relatively few man-made buildings is required for this Key. Most any park will count, and the area around Long Wharf is good for an evening stroll or two.

                  In order to obtain this Key, the character must stand at least briefly fully naked, where no man-made buildings or objects are visible.

                  Pyre-Flame Key: Spending time near a large fire, such as in the grand fireplace, or being outside in a thunderstorm.

                  In order to obtain this Key, the character must burn themselves for at least 1 lethal level of damage.

                  Stigmata Key: For this Key, spending time in places associated with blood or ghosts qualifies. Most Haunts qualify for this, including Dark Haven Bluffs, but so do murder scenes or anywhere with a very recently killed body still present, even an animal.

                  In order to obtain this Key, the character must taste a quantity of blood from another person. Generally one health level’s worth. The person need not be alive.

                  The Stillness Key: Spending time where the dead are is good for this. Cemeteries work, as does the morgue. Hanging around zombies works if they are not trying to eat your brain.

                  In order to obtain this Key, the character must have full body contact with a corpse. They can do so clothed of course, and the corpse need not be human.

                  Stygian Key: This is a special case. As described in Book of the Dead, this requires a journey to the Great Below, then drinking from three Underworld rivers, and being taught by Kerberoi or Chthonian or similar, then making a sacrifice robbing them of an Attribute dot till the end of the next story. This will take more then 11 hours.

                  Tear-Stained Key: Spending time in water, or at the beach.

                  In order to obtain this Key, the character must submerge themselves longer then they can hold their breath.


                  Manifestations

                  Learning Manifestations requires a teacher. This can be a Sin-Eater with the Manifestation, or a Ghost that has a Numina that is similar to the effect. Assuming you are getting along with your Geist you can potentially learn it from them, if the Manifestation seems like one the Geist would possess or resonate with…the seductive Laughing Lady is unlikely to be able to tell Natalie much about The Rage, but The Marionette sure works. This takes a negligible amount of time, maybe about 10 minutes. Once you possess a Manifestation, you need only use it to be able to raise it, though you can continue to learn from someone with it in lieu of using it if you wish, so long as they have the appropriate level (or are a Ghost or Geist with the right knowledge) Such training also takes about 10 minutes.

                  If the character experience a pronounced psychological strain or change, it is possible to acquire a new Manifestation with no teacher, or increase a Manifestation with neither practice nor a teacher. This can happen within a number days equal to Psyche of a character gaining or losing a dot of Synergy, or acquiring, intensifying or shedding a derangement


                  Ceremonies

                  There are two ways to pick up new Ceremonies. One is to be taught the Ceremony by someone who knows it. This takes very little time, maybe about 10 minutes. The other is through research.

                  To research a Ceremony takes a good library with religious and occult works. The Dark Haven Bluffs library serves perfectly well for this requirement, as do the Yale and Albertus Magnus libraries. The number of successes required is 5 per Ceremony level, so 5-25. Each roll is 30 minutes, and need not be done at once, so long as the character takes notes. The Dark Haven Bluffs library gives a +2 equipment bonus to this roll. The Yale library gives a net +1 (+3 for size, -2 for obscurity) and Albertus Magnus libraries give a net of +0 (+2 for size, -2 for obscurity).

                  If you purchase Ceremonies as a specialty for the Library background, than each roll only takes 10 minutes, not 30. This can be done for ones private Library (in which case only they get the benefit) or the whole Krewe, in which case the Krewe does.

                  There is no rule saying you can’t get a Krewemate to research for you. That’s between you and them. If they research it (but don’t learn it themselves) you have to study their notes for 10 minutes per level of the Ceremony.

                  Ritual Magic

                  Learning Magic Rituals takes a similar system to Ceremonies.

                  Researching a Ritual takes the same roll as researching a Ceremony. The Dark Haven Bluffs library does not give a –2 to learn Rituals for Apostles of the Dark One, Ceremonial Magicians, or Hedge Witches, thus it gives a net of +2. For Hougans, Taoist Alchemists, and Shamans, it gives a net +0. Creepily enough, both Yale and Albertus Magnus libraries are friendly to the study of Ceremonial Magicians and Apostles of the Dark One, giving a net total of +3 and +2 respectively, while Hedge Witches, Hougans, Taoist Alchemists, and Shamans get the same +1 and +0 as Ceremonies.

                  It is possible to buy a Magical Tradition’s Rituals as a dot of the Library background, much like Ceremonies. Doing so reduces the research time to 10 minutes just like Ceremonies, and in the case of Hedge Witches, Taoist Alchemists, or Shamans, removes the –2 for being obscure penalty.

                  Only Apostles of the Dark One and Ceremonial Magicians can have someone else research for them, and it has to be one of their Tradition.

                  Anyone can teach someone else of their Tradition a Ritual. This takes one hour per dot of the Ritual. Certain Traditions have other options; an Apostle of the Dark One can use Communion to request tutelage from their Dark One, while Hedge Witches and Shamans can use Invocation or Evocation with Spirits (depending on options and inclination) or Hougans use Invocation or Evocation (ditto) with Ghosts to learn a Ritual. This takes one hour per level of the Ritual (once the Ritual to summon or commune is complete, if used instead of seeking the spirit out normally). Taoist Alchemists have no such shortcut, but they can meditate instead of doing research. This works just like research, except that they must learn the Ritual when done. They get their natural +1 to meditate for this, and good Feng Shui may give more bonuses. However, they cannot benefit from the equivalent of Library for meditating (but can use a library as well as anyone else, or have a Taoist Alchemist specialty Library). While Ceremonial Magicians get no special shortcut, they do get their +1 to library use when researching.


                  This is at least how we do it in Balance of Shadows. There is a little game specific information towards the end, but these are examples either way.

                          You're right, it is supposed to be meta-metaphysics, but getting into a position to be able to (or to be able to pretend to) talk about "meta-meta" is supposed to involve getting as comfortable with dealing with the world entirely in metaphor first, by talking and thinking exclusively in non-literal terms (though many descriptions of language and thought have it that it's already 100% metaphor). It's not really a sound concept, by the standards of rigorous philosophy, but it's not necessary to be sensible or logically valid when the real point is to get into a weirdly artistic mindset.

                                      I crank the trash and gutter magic of the setting to 11. The Bound, being the kings of thrift shops and the queens of second-hand have to Macgyver their new keys, building it from pieces of everything else. 1 part spiritual, 1 part scavenged, 1 part ritual and 1 part materialistic; I've had players assemble makeshift totems to unlock keys, using their Geist to "make the magic happen". The totem in question was for the Tear-Stained, using newspaper clippings (of an accidental drowning at a pool), a child's bathing suit (the person who drowned was 7), a bright blue hair tie, and a bottle of pool water. Clippings were slid into the bathing suit, the hair tie under that, a little bit of plasm was invested and then the pool water poured over the top. End result, the Geist shared the experience of drowning with the Bound, and the key was unlocked forever more; oddly enough the Bound kept the hair tie on at all times afterwards.

                                      Sure, the Bound could have just drowned themselves, but where's the trash culture in that?
