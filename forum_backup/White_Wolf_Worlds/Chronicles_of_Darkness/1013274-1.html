I came around and can profess my faith in the one, true Vampire game.

                                                    The stereotypes in Masquerade can get to be a bit much: Malkavians were not a very good representation of mental illness, and the independent clans were practically caricatures in their first edition appearance. One thing that I find odd is that people view the original seven clans as having very narrow stereotypes, but in first edition they were actually fairly broad, similar to the Requiem clans, keeping in mind my comment about the Malkavians.

                                                    One of the things I really like about Requiem is that it went for that broad portrayal and stuck with it. The five clans are quite diverse and while each clan represents certain vampiric stereotypes, they aren't narrow (Requiem Nosferatu being a broader archetype than Masquerade's Nosferatu, for example).

                                                    I mean I love both games, but I found myself wanting to house rule Masquerade into Requiem so I am probably much better off running Requiem than coming up with a host of house rules for Masquerade.

                                                    I don't really care how people play Vampire, although it is a bit frustrating when people assume their preferred play should be universal.

                                                        I dislike the notions of "improved masquerade is requiem" or "masquerade sans embarassing bits isn't masquerade." I think there's plenty to make the game fun and distinct with more grandiose stakes and oddly specific character types. You can have illusionists with strange compulsions and curse-prone assassins be staple character types without being racist (which don't really map to the broader-in-concept requiem clans and covenants.) Most the powers and mechanics are perfectly capable of having more elegant and balanced permutations without ceasing to recognizable. (Except Generation, which is unfair in various specific, narrative-vital manners).

                                                        Any sort of change is bound to make some people upset, especially given that there's plenty of dissent about various editions and character types already. It'd still be nice for there be an edition for me for when I'm feeling a bit more "Vampires who throw fireballs" without having to deal with rock-paper-scissors or 4 attack rolls.

                                                        (For the record, I prefer requiem but I don't like one game being used to shut down discussion of the other.)
