    Ok, I just went back and re-read all the Seer stuff in the core, so maybe I can help you a bit more.

    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    1- Exarchs, the once-mage gods;
    That's not confirmed at all. It's just a theory held by the Atlantean orthodoxy. Same as the theory that the Watchtowers are the work of the Oracles and that the Oracles are Atlantean princes. All unconfirmed.

    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    2- Recruitment through The Dream Quest of Unknown Kadath;
    That's not how Seers recruit. It's how they send marching orders.

    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    3- Traitorous backstabbers to the point of inneficience;
    Honestly, they seem to work together as well as the Orders do, but I guess that's just my interpretation. If anything, they seem like a very good pyramid scheme. Sure, they might interfere with each other in the lofty ideals, stuff like interpreting the grand goals of the Exarchs, but on the ground they do at least agree on opposing the Orders.

    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    4- Sheer numbers.
    This one's easy, they have all the money and the creature comforts. Not hard to see why they'd have an easy time recruiting. From what I've gathered, they are bigger than any individual Order, but not bigger than any two, but that could also be my interpretation.

    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    It does make sense, tough I have no idea what an "ochemata" is. If it was in the core, I don't remember. Just two remain.
    Ochemata are servants of the Exarchs (or any ascended being, really) sent directly to the Fallen World. They weren't in the core, sorry.

    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    Yet a huge amount of them. I wouldn't mind so much as I do now if they where only the biggest order. But to say they're bigger than the four most traditional orders united!? Even when those orders seemed a bunch of nutjobs to me, it was still a huge number. And the Free Council being even bigger doesn't help things.
    That's not exactly the impression I got. It could just be me, but I thought that the Seers were bigger than any one of the Pentacle, but not bigger than two combined. I did get the impression that they were better than the rest at entrenching themselves in government bodies, so perhaps they have greater reach than any of the Orders.

    All the population talk is kind of moot, though, because every Storyteller controls the exact population of mages as they see fit. No one at White Wolf is making any charts as to the exact population of mages because that's left of to the ST at each individual table. The numbers of mages in your setting should serve YOUR story, not the other way around. There isn't some grand unified canon that you MUST adhere to in your story. If you wanted, you don't have to have a Seer presence at all, or perhaps in your city they might outnumber the Pentacle ridiculously to where it's like Neo and Trinity against a bunch of Agents all the time. It's up to you.

    Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
    To me the major power should be the regional minor orders with roots on local culture.
    What do you mean by minor orders? Because there are such things as smaller orders that aren't the Pentacle. The Pentacle generally calls them Nameless Orders and they are a thing, but they don't have the same kind of support or magical might that the actual Orders have (rotes, artifacts, Merits like Masques or Adamantine Hand) because well ... that's just a conceit of the setting. Rather than being about local Magical Traditions (which are still a thing) it is about globe spanning conspiracies, a term which applies to the Pentacle and the Throne.

                                        Originally posted by Kirby Jerusalem

                                        It's more that there are Ministries within the Seers, while there are still plenty of Seers who belong to no Ministry, their basic geographic establishment tends to be pan-Ministry, and part of the nature of their hierarchy ultimately ties them all together in a convoluted but ultimately comprehensive network. The central commandments of the Seers are not necessarily tied to any given Exarch, so you can have multiple Seers of different creeds all working together on any given one of them, similar with their reward system and perspectives on those rewards. I'd also say that the Ministries are insufficiently different from one another to really be considered as distinct as Diamond Orders or Pure Tribes; on that note alone, I would view the collective as being a form of Order.
