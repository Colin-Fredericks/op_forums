                    It might not require much in Space (technically possible even before you get access to Sympathetic Range) but it does require actively searching for certain sympathetic connections before suspecting that they exist.

                    Also, remember the fact that Correspondence (and thus probably also other Knowing/Unveiling spells cast on the self) only reveals the nature of a sympathetic connections from the perspective of the subject. By RAW this means you can't find stalkers without knowing about them in the first place, and even if you use the common house rule to provide somewhat objective information (otherwise you could literally never identify sympathetic connections from non-thinking subjects) you should still have to search for something like "stalker" or "unseen/unknown observer".

                        Totally agree. Maybe I play up the horror aspect a bit more in my game than you do in yours; in mine the head of a Seer Pylon is paranoid enough to jump through these hoops by default. I mean they commonly advance by sabotaging or killing each other.

                        Rather than a mage regularly sieving all her sympathetic connections though, I think it's more efficient to keep up a long-Duration effect that alerts the mage to changes in sympathetic connections (assuming the caster can spare the spell control). It's like instead of regularly querying a database to poll for changes, you instead set up a trigger to execute whenever a row is updated or created. Adjust potency if desired to ignore Weak connections. I also believe it's possible design spells to help track down the entity at the other end of a sympathetic connection using the connection itself. I put a couple of spells along these lines in the Creative Thaumaturgy thread. The write-up on Sympathy makes it clear that the connections themselves are viable subjects for spell casting.

                        I'll also note that the way I run sympathy, connections have more nuance than a single meaning because you can't always boil down complex relationships into a single tag-line. Consider two Seers, one the head of a Pylon and the other her lieutenant. The connection from the lieutenant to the Pylon head might encompass "my leader," "my lover," and "the one I"m plotting to overthrow" all at once, and for me any of those are viable keys for searching/identifying that connection.

                        Of course I also don't run what you described previously, where A can form a connection to B purely because B has surveilled or plotted against A, even when A doesn't know B exists. It's like that scene in Endgames where Scarlet Witch is like "You took everything from me!" and alternate-timeline Thanos is like "I don't even know who you are." Sure, Scarlet Witch has a connection to Thanos as soon as she returns to existence, but I'd say Thanos has no connection to Scarlet Witch prior to fighting her. But I am considering whether your perspective is somehow better (the spells I wrote are useful primarily if you're correct on this point).

                                Originally posted by Menace View Post
                                So I'll be running a game, we decided that we'll use the Los Angeles setting from book and in order to flesh out the game I'm asking:

                                What would be good source to learn general info about the city? Or maybe do you have any interesting tidbits to share? Tried browsing wiki but there's a lot of text to go through.
                                Well, for one, there's a lot more city than just Los Angeles city proper, you're in pretty continuous suburbs or city from the southern end of Orange County until you hit the farmland in Ventura County, and then there's a brief interruption before you hit Santa Barbara County.

                                In LA city only downtown LA has decent public transportation, and I use "decent" quite loosely, you really do need a car to get around. Distance is denoted in time rather than distance ("yada yada is about 20 minutes away"). Anything less than a two hour drive is considered close.

                                Freeways are called "the <number>" (e.g. "the one-oh-one", "the four-oh-five", "the one-eighteen") except for Highway 1 which is called PCH (Pacific Coast Highway). Toll roads are super rare and are generally privately owned bypasses.

                                Food trucks are super popular, places will have things like a "food truck Friday" where food trucks will all set up to sell food. And the food trucks sell diverse and gourmet food, everything from the classic taco truck to gourmet grilled cheese sandwiches, to sushi and dim sum.

                                On the topic of food: the best food can be found in the weirdest places, my girlfriend and I got some super incredible authentic Indian food from a random gas station downtown.

                                There's a super cool distillery in the Arts District call Lost Spirits, it's Willy Wonka's chocolate factory for adults (they even have a boat ride).

                                LA has lots of cool museums. The California Science Center (with the Space Shuttle Endeavor), Natural History Museum, and African-American History Museum are all right next to each other, and are next to the coliseum for the 1984 Olympics. There's also the La Brea Tar Pits, which are real tarpits with bones and such they actually pulled out of the pits on display; and the Griffith Park Observatory, which has appeared in numerous films and even some anime.

                                Hollywood is in LA, and that's its own entire thing to unpack. There's active movie sets and you can see very famous people walking around living their everyday lives. Sometimes streets will be blocked off for filming, or you'll see a crew set up in a park or something.

                                Disney has the El Capitan theater which does special events after major movie releases (they built Pizza Planet and an Army Men obstacle course for Toy Story). In Anaheim (which is not in LA but is close) there's also Disneyland and California Adventure.

                                LA also has quite a bit of nightlife, but I moved away before I was really old enough to enjoy it and came back after I was nightlifed out from being in the military, so I can't speak too much to that.

                                        Reflexive casting is +2 reach and a mana, you don't need to spend a reach on Instant Casting.

                                        Turn Momentum is a bit different, you cast the spell beforehand and get the effects of the spell for the duration. The spell effect is turning aside a number of things equal to potency, by default this requires you to hold your action, for a reach this can be done reflexively. There isn't an option to cast the spell reflexively, only to use its effect.

                                        I would allow Velocity Control to be cast reflexively, otherwise you'd have to hold precast it on the projectile you want to affect or hold your action.
