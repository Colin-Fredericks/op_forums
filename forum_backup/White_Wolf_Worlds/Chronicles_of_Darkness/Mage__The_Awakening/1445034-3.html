            Originally posted by Excess View Post
            You seem to have come to the conclusion that the Path/Order/Praxis can replace the methods of initiation but I can't find that anywhere.
            That is not what I'm saying.
            There are two parts to joining a Legacy (ignoring general trait limits on Gnosis and Arcana): Fulfilling a prerequisite, and going through initiation.
            1.) The prerequisites (sharing Path with the originator, being part of an associated Order, or having an equivalent Praxis) is horribly underexplained. Does these limits exist within the setting, or are they artificial limits imposed on the game?
            1.a.) If it's the former, how does the associated Order prerequisite form? And why do these limits exist in-setting?
            1.b.) But if it's instead an artificial prerequisite placed on the game that doesn't reflect the setting, then it makes a lot more sense that fulfilling one of the prerequisites merely means you don't have to go out of your way to steal a Daimonomikon or Soul Stone or Soul; you're just accepted and either get to read a Daimonomikon or get a tutor assigned to you or just do whatever the Legacy does to initiate new members.
            By RAW it's definitely the former because any exceptions aren't detailed, but it's also very dissatisfying because of how underexplained it is. And anyone who has enough experience with 2e CofD books knows that much of what's written doesn't accurately reflect what the writers intended because OPP doesn't seem to have a developmental editing phase to catch such stuff in their process. What I mean is, if anything in CofD feels underexplained, there's a good chance it actually is and that RAW (rules as written) does not match RAI (rules as intended).

            2.) Initiation, however, is very adequately explained. You always have to go through initiation because that's how it's done in-setting. What I'm saying is that you can gloss over that part (meaning, it happens in the game, but you don't have to play it out since it's not interesting to play) if the character is just accepted by the other members of the Legacy due to having shown enough aptitude or shared values.

                I always saw the originating Path and Order as diplomatic facilitators. If you are of the same Order or Path, a mentor will be much more compelled to teach them. This does not restrict others from being thought, they just need to put more time and work into convincing a teacher. This means than a member of the Guardians that wants to learn a Silver Ladder Legacy can gain that tutelage easily should they have bought one dot of Order Status:Silver Ladder or earned enough respect through their actions.

                Some Legacies are actively suppressed outside and/or inside their Orders. The Guardians of the Veil tend to target ones that make it more difficult for them to obtain intel on their targets (the Bearers of the Eternal Voice are one example) or that make parsing out the truth on their terms more difficult (such as the Eleventh Question). The Arrow would actively work against the propagation of any Legacies that cleave to Vedantus principles, due to the fundamental incompatibility with their philosophy. In order for outsiders to obtain mentors in these cases, it would probably require a major boon from the teacher or Order.

                Finally, a mage can always try to appropriate the knowledge. Stealing a Daimonomika, mentally controlling a mentor, procuring souls and soul stones from traffickers allow the mage to initiate themselves into it. Such an usurpation will certainly earn the ire of other members once it comes to light, if not outright escalation due to them becoming a sympathetic liability.

                In summary, the originating Path and Order represent the path of least resistence for the mage to be taught, other approaches carry their own set of complications.

                                I feel this tone would be uncalled for in any case, but it stands out particularly when addressed at a post that it appears to have not properly read or understood.

                                Namely that Excess is not disputing the prerequisites for learning Legacies, but KaiserAfini's suggestion that they constitute a soft limit based on cultural values.

                                As far as the in-character basis for learning Legacies goes, I feel the logic of it is distributed in a few places.

                                The description of Gnosis itself refers to what it feels like per Path; the substance of one's Supernal connection and how it affects insights and outlook. I find that to make sense as a necessary basis to both understand and internalise the nature of the shaping of Gnosis entailed by initiating into and progressing through a Legacy; that there are mystical insights that just can't be communicated to people of another Path, deeper than the simple need to translate the semiotics of Mage Sight.

                                The description of Praxis refers to how it's an Imago of such familiarity and significance to the mage that they gain greater insight into it and it exerts a shaping effect upon their Gnosis. I can follow the logic that says that such insight can become a hook for the Legacy mentor to impart its teachings to a mage who otherwise would have no means of internalising them, although notably a Praxis cannot be the basis for actually originating the Legacy.

                                Order is one I admit needs to be taken more from what the game as a whole has said rather than specifically what the core had available wordcount for*, but it bears remembering that the Orders are more than just political associations; their ideologies emerge from actual recognition of mystical qualities of reality and engaging in practice and ritual that aligns one to them. That's notably how things like Masques and Egregore are able to work; the capacity to have a Medium sympathetic connection to every single member of the Mysterium while present in one of their communal spaces is not the result of spells, it's because their ideas about magic being alive are actually on to something and living in accordance with that principle allows one to connect to it. The Mysterium book goes so far to say that mages who leave the Order ultimately lose their egregore benefits because it can be reasonably presumed that they're no longer living in accordance with those practices and it results in disengaging from the communal element. In any case, that would also be a basis for Supernal insight into using Legacy practices to shape Gnosis, so long as the Legacy's nature is one that incorporates certain Order perspectives.

                                * As far as complaints about how elaborate the description of Legacies is, I would point out that Dave Brookshaw has reported that other things needed to be cut from this book in order for the description of Legacies to be included at all, something that Requiem and Forsaken didn't even get with their Z-splats.

                                                            First Attainment: I am thinking that in addition to investing the bonus from opening Doors to making new ones maybe I should include a Mana cost as well. Some part of me recoils from the idea because when I contemplated making the success requirement for making new Doors ten minus Prime dots I imagined a cosmic event. A great Archmaster of Prime finally achieving Assumption and total total access to Prime would then have zero needed successes in order to create Doors. Adding a Mana requirement might prevent such a cosmic master from enjoying infinite Doors during the Duel. Of course I realize that forsaking game balance for a far-off hypothetical might be a bad idea.

                                                            Second Attainment: still going to make it about starting with additional Doors equal to Prime dots.

                                                            Third Attainment: roll Prime and Intimidation to force the enemy to give up their own Doors to bind their own offers. Clash of Wills ensues with a bonus depending on how many Doors the enemy still has. Failure means the Duelist must give up her own Door to bind the offer. The wager aspect removes the need to balance the Attainment with a Mana cost.

                                                            Fourth Attainment: instead of a bonus for the next spell or rebuilding Doors the mage instead trades successes for removing additional Doors. Five successes and a point of Mana for each additional Door. The reason the Mana reduction of Legacy Attainments does not work here is because each is a separate use of the same Attainment. Perhaps this rationale should be used for the first Attainment. Should have a visual component to show extra losses.

                                                            Fifth Attainment: opening a Door allows the mage to gain extra Arcana immunities. Not sure if this should be the fourth Attainment or have a cost. A visual element as well to show everyone which Arcanum the Duelist chose to shut down.
