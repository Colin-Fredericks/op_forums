      Yes, with caveats.

      Long story short, any place a Mage can stay, a human can, too. Which in the barest sense means any realm, none of them is inherently hostile to human life. That said, survival may pose a challenge just as it would any dangerous environment on Earth, on top of the challenge of getting them there (which isn't terribly hard for most, but is for some).

      The main problems are dangerous local inhabitants and, depending on the place, getting water and food (air isn't usually a problem).

      Abyss - Anything can happen, anyone is unlikely to survive long. Leaving a human here is asking for shit to happen, just as leaving anything here, including Mages.

      Astral - Usually death in it only means being released in the waking world again, although there may be exceptions. It's unclear if dream-stuff can sustain humans physically transported here, but otherwise they're fine. This is probably the safest place for a human, and Beasts actually bring them physically with some frequency.

      Duat - Nothing dies for real in this realm, although bringing anyone here is quite a challenge to begin with. Even then, it happens. That said, this place will cause a lot of suffering and probably trigger a lot of Breaking Points, so it isn't really safer than the Astral. It's unclear what happens when a non-Mummy completes the journey through Duat, if they'll be judged by the Judges and what they'll do to a living mortal depending on their verdict.

      Hedge - Humans can live here as normal and eat and drink local edibles. Those edibles may or may not have weird effects, that may or may not render them unsafe. The environment is dangerous in many ways, the locals are dangerous in many ways, and simply staying here for long may cause weird shit to anything that isn't an inhabitant yet. IIRC now there are even rules for humans living here to become a particular kind of Goblin, but I didn't read 2nd Edition yet.

      Neter-Khertet - This particular manifestation of Death Twilight has added denizens that are very dangerous to humans, especially the Amkhata, and a storm that mostly hides things. Also there are visible passages to both the Underworld and Duat, that a human may then try to use if they're here. It is otherwise like any other Twilight, which means you have to bring food and water from elsewhere.

      Shadow - It may be that a human left for years in the Shadow start to become something else, but in principle nothing exceptional happens. I'm almost sure humans can survive on local "food", but getting it is tricky as everything is a spirit or at least spirit-stuff that can become a spirit eventually. Surviving the local "fauna" in general is hard.

      Underworld - Definitely devoid of food, although it has water. Drinking from the Rivers is not advisable as they may have many weird effects, but rarely they'll be life-threatening if you're left with no choice. They may also be made of things like blood, pus, metal chains, whatever, but they're all actually drinkable (they still taste as they look). The water trickling down through the upper caves is usually safer, but the best option for a living being is still to bring water and food. Food may get stale a little faster here, but not terribly so, and it won't actually rot AFAIK. If you manage to cross the rivers, which is doable if not cheap, the Dead Dominions may be infinitely varied, so it isn't impossible to find one where you have food fit for humans. I wouldn't count on it, though.

          I think it was Deviant's Clade Companion that describes how Inferno is survivable for living humans trapped there. I don't remember what it said about hell's dangerous environments, but food is interesting there. There are delicious feasts to be found there, but they provide no nourishment.                                   Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                  I didn't know there was a book that actually described Inferno, since the book that introduced it basically made it a non-place. Which other books describe it?
                                  The Clades Companion for Deviant has a functional stealth-update for the Inferno, detailing Malapraxes and giving the working unified name of "devils" for the demons of the Inferno. It's still pretty difficult to escape an open Hellmouth if you get too close and looking into one shows you personalized horrors that inflict conditions from the "dramatically fail a breaking point roll" list.

                                  Theoretically everything is possible, so nothing bad happening is also possible. But that's extremely unlikely, while the most probable results of leaving a human there for months is that they'll perish and the body will be effectively irretrievable, making the details all but irrelevant. Suffice to know it was horrible.
                                  I mention this mainly because, while getting tossed into the Abyss is typically a one-way trip, world-beings of the sort that make beachheads into ordered reality have denizens close enough to human to justify "a doppleganger of the spellcaster emerges" as an example Manifestation Paradox in Mage 1e (and one of the sample characters from 2e discusses in the fiction anthology his memories of another life in a particular Abyssal world), so in cases where the goal is survival rather than escape the distinction is significant — the rules can change from moment to moment in the wider Abyss, but you can learn to live in the Annunaki.

                                  At least that means not becoming a Hedge Denizen is relatively easy if you have the means to survive on your own, which can be mundane (or almost mundane). A Mage that leaves the human with enough resources can reasonably expect the human to still be human in the future.

                                  Can you find edible things in the Hedge without Hedgespinning?
                                  No, but with luck or the right approach one could steer the Hedge's natural process of spinning itself toward providing normal food if relying on the aid of hobgoblins doesn't serve, and becoming a hobgoblin is itself a reversible process largely boiling down to working off some of the Debt to your creditor and then gaining or losing Integrity-equivalent. (Given that goblin fruits do not sustain non-fae, it is debatably prudent to take a stint as a hobgoblin for survivability so you can work towards finding a way out, but that is admittedly How They Get You.)

                                  There's also a Fate 2 + Mind 2 spell called Malleable Thorns that lets a mage state a goal for the Hedge to spin itself to somehow fulfill using a number of Hedgespinning successes equal to the spell's Potency; food that isn't high-quality or specific takes four successes, whereas more particular fare takes six successes and would require a Reach for Malleable Thorns to be able to produce, and if the way that food is provided is in the form of a pre-existing larder there is a distinct possibility some predator or other opportunist is hanging around to take advantage of the creatures it attracts.

                                                  1- A well prepared trip to the Abyss may deliver you somewhere survivable and stable enough. Would it be safe long term enough for a human colony? Maybe;

                                                  2- You can make a colony in the Hedge. Providing food and water is a challenge, but with enough experts with the right training, or a single Mage with the right Arcanoi/Rote, it can be done locally;

                                                      Personally, I find some of the premise of Annunaki as bubbles of coherence within the Abyss to kind of shift it away from what I regard as some of the more intriguing prospects of that plane.

                                                      I choose to reconcile this in terms of the Annunaki having coherence only along certain subjective frames of reference. Like, imagine you saw the Nemesis Continuum from afar. "Good", you think; "a place to stand where I don't have to worry that any given section of my body might randomly change shape or become subjected to different laws of physics or gain independent sapience or have its colours become different parts of a spectrum that cause them to mix and annihilate one another or-".

                                                      You approach the Nemesis Continuum and step confidently into its boundaries. Only to discover that, what looked coherent from afar, was actually a trick of perspective; all of its different parts are at separate distances from one another, such that it all looks singular and connected when viewed from the right angle, but is spread out from right up close, with all the undifferentiated Abyss in between. It is the nature of the slumbering gods of the Abyss that they can be fully themselves even when interspersed like this, but that is not a reality shared by most hypothetical visitors.

                                                      You smack your forehead in frustration, and instantly shatter from a sudden new fragility, the shards of you fluttering away as butterflies on the breeze before passing through a shaft of sunlight that refracts them into bubbles.

                                                      I have approximate knowledge of many things.
