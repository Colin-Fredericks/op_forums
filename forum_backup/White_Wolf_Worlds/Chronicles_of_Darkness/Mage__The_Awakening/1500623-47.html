        Lorewise, when you cast a Rote from a Grimoire you construct the imago from the instructions within. When you use the Mudra, you're using mnemonics to aid constructing the imago from your memory.
        Using both would be the same as constructing something using two sets of instructions. If it's two instructions for two different Rotes (even if the base spell is the same) then it's obvious to me you can't combine them. But if the Grimoire contains the same Rote you've memorized, then it wouldn't be illogical to supplement your following of the instructions with the information you already have, but on the other hand for IRL skills that generally just means it doesn't take as long as you don't have to spend as much time interpreting the instructions in front of you.

        Textwise, it's clear as day. The book separates casting from a Grimoire and casting from memory, and gives two different sets of rules associated with each. You can't use the Mudra when casting from a Grimoire.

        Dev clarification-wise, I don't think we have any explicit confirmation either way, but we do have some strong evidence leaning towards a literal interpretation of the book. Here I answered a different question about Rotes, and mentioned that you probably can't combine Grimoires and Mudras as an aside. The lead dev at the time confirmed my answer, and even though he didn't directly refer to this subject I strongly doubt he'd ignore an error in a post he otherwise agreed with as an answer to a rules question.​

            He refers to "just borrowing a Grimoire," which is the specific thing I am saying I am not contesting doesn't give you access to a mudra you haven't internalized. Dave was answering a question about whether you had to use the Mudra Yantra when casting a spell as a rote (which you don't).

            Grimoires give you the rote action quality because having the steps laid out in front of you is one of the textbook sources for rote actions. You can learn the mudras for a rote's encoded Skill by studying the grimoire and spending Experiences to learn it, but you don't lose the ability to perform mnemonic gestures just because you have instructions in front of you.

            Rote action is a dice trick. It gives better returns with bigger dicepools and worse returns with smaller ones. On a failed initial roll, rote action alone translates to a single reroll of the base pool, and unlike in 1e where extended casting was a thing, 2e handles all casting in the fashion that provides extra spell factors by making the dicepool smaller.

            The bonus from a Mudra Yantra being based on your dots in an encoded Skill is 2e's carryover of 1e making rote spells an Attribute + Skill + Arcanum roll instead of a Gnosis + Arcanum roll. 1e's grimoire-creating spell had no other benefit than allowing a character who didn't know the rote to cast it and giving characters who do know the rote a +1 bonus to the casting roll, with the net result being that (provided a character with high Gnosis + Arcanum ratings isn't using a rote whose roll involves Traits they have bad ratings in) using a grimoire increases your odds of success.

            I do not believe the intention of an edition where grimoires were specifically tweaked to be more desirable (and where scribing a grimoire does not remove your knowledge of a rote) is to make using your own notation on a codified spell that you know stop you from using the tools you have for casting efficiently to reliably add more spell factors to its casting, particularly given that spellcasting is not a purely intellectual process and doubling your casting time can translate to as little as twenty minutes (without factoring in extremely powerful Time magic) and Signs of Sorcery notes that rotes created without the Sects' compression techniques can't even be cast instantly — i.e. if a Nameless Order mage learns a rote from one of their Masters and then scribes a grimoire of it, they can benefit from its encoded Skill if they take half an hour to cast it, but under the reading being proposed they can't do so if they take a full hour to cast it.

            It is clear as day that if you didn't create the rote you only get rote action on the casting if you're casting with the benefit of a grimoire. The core developer clarified that you don't have to use a Mudra Yantra when you're casting a spell as a rote and that simply casting out of a borrowed grimoire doesn't allow you do so. These things do not directly imply that you cannot mechanically combine reading from a spellbook and doing ritual gestures over an extended period of time despite being able to spend that same amount of time without the book and get a bigger bonus.

                    Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                    I'm aware that Dave didn't directly address this point. I specifically and explicitly stated as much.
                    And I specifically and explicitly stated that I wasn't talking about the thing that Dave was unambiguously talking about in his parenthetical, after it had already been pointed out that a statement that casting a rote from a grimoire instead of from memory being a condition for getting the dice trick on a rote does not logically necessitate that you cannot benefit from the tools the study of that grimoire will grant a character to cast the rote without it at the same time. You can presumably understand why I moved to bring in additional points instead of simply repeating my initial statement in frustration.

                    I might address the rest of your post when I have the energy.
                    I would frankly not bother unless you can come up with a direct citation or dev clarification, because so far the "clear rules" that have been cited are the contrapositive of the statement under discussion and the fact that the book mechanically distinguishes casting from a Grimoire and casting from memory alone.

                    Stylistically there is no reason a character should lose the benefits of their rote's ritual gestures while they are opting to benefit from a reference Imago. Mechanically there is no reason a character referencing a Grimoire they have already studied should be required to substitute different Yantras or spend even more time on the casting to get the same dicepool with the same factors they would get ritual casting without the Grimoire when no other method of applying rote action to spellcasting rolls applies this restriction on top of their existing risks or costs. Historically the game has made Grimoires allow for an additive bonus to spellcasting rolls in addition to their function as learning implements, with the only possibility of the tool making you do a worse job is if you are specifically worse at the skills that tool uses.

                    Given all this, "You cannot use what is probably the single biggest Yantra bonus you could get from knowing this spell if you also use its blueprints as a reference" is an extraordinary claim which requires much more definite evidence than the separation of Grimoires' mechanical effects on casting a rote from the mechanical effects of knowing its Mudra. Changing my mind on this would take at the very least a persuasive argument concerning what genre emulation conceits or game balance concerns are supported by removing a substantial dice bonus from consideration in return for a dice trick instead of allowing the two to be combined when both are available, and if neither that nor a direct statement by the books or the developers or writers are readily available then it may be prudent to save your energy.
