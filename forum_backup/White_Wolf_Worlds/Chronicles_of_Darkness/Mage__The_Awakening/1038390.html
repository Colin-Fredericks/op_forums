    What's You Obsession?

    01-26-2017, 09:47 AM
    So I'm still new to GMing Mage, 2e or otherwise, and I constantly question whether I'm interpreting the rules correctly. Last night we were playing and we got to talking about Obsessions and whether or not the characters in the game currently have valid Obsessions. So, logically, I decided to come here for some help and inspiration.

    We have four characters: Arbiter, Erstwhile, Foxtrot, and Zoltar. Arbiter is a living Artifact who has the Obsession of "Acquire Magical Items." Erstwhile is Private Detective who is determined to turn back enough time to save his wife and has an Obsession of "Learn enough about time magic to change my past." Foxtrot is a member of the GoV and is convinced that Arbiter is a Lich so he has a Obsession of "What is Arbiter." And Zoltar is a conman who runs a business specializing in Divination/Medium services and has the Obsession of "Learn how to turn off Ghostly Voices."

    Now, I haven't really been handing out a lot of Arcane Beats, which means I'm laying down on the job. So do you guys have any tips on how to use this mechanic? Or have we picked Obsessions that fall a little flat?

    And what are your character's Obsessions? I'd love to hear them

      Those all seem pretty reasonable. They're also fairly divided, though, and it might be best to try and create some unity between them. You can do this by either subtly suggesting a change to them towards something that might come up more regularly, or you might drop Mysteries that relate to them all; maybe an ancient Artifact appears that has connections to the Time/Death arcana; letting it create a ghost from the remains of a dead person by reaching back to the moment of their death, for example, and which reacts oddly around Arbiter.

      If you want to give out more A-Beats, give them for partial progress towards the goal of a particularly long-term Obsession, like Foxtrot witnessing some startling effect relating to Arbiter.

      Also, never be afraid to tempt your players. If there's something related to their Obsession that they could do but either are hesitant about or may not even realize, offer an A-Beat as a subtle nudge and let them hang themselves with their own rope. For example, Foxtrot might get an offer of a Beat for breaking into Arbiter's home and going through his things to find evidence of his strangeness; Arbiter might get an offer of a Beat for trying to steal a magical item from another Mage or for being willing to make an otherwise disadvantageous deal with some spirit or another to get access to some magical trinket.

      Edit: And, if you're curious, my Thyrsus's current Obsessions are "Uncover the full extent of the Awakened conspiracies in Seattle" and "Figure out where all the Thyrsus are". Until recently, that second slot was kept open as a reserve for anything related to healing, shapeshifting, or spirits.

              If it's necessary to do so, yes, I would. Ideally, though, you can avoid this a bit with how you build your cabal.

              To that end, I recommend checking out Sanctum & Sigil. It's a great book. The entirety of what I consider most valuable is probably contained on one page but the rest is still pretty great.

              Without just essentially copying what the book says, a recommendation is to decide on their cabal's purpose. Cabals don't form "just because"; these Mages have some reason that they'd swear oaths to work together, despite whatever potential clashes of interests and disagreements of ideals may occur. Are they looking to gather as much knowledge as possible? To become rich beyond the dreams of avarice? To overthrow the Hierarch? To wipe out the Seers? End the Lie? This can be as abstract or particular as possible but, whatever it is, it defines the cabal and, through it, ensures that your players will mostly have Obsessions along the same lines.

              The cabal wants as much knowledge as possible? Find a common thread of knowledge they're interested in gathering and people will focus their Obsessions around that. For example, the cabal might be heavily interested in the effects of resonances upon the world, so the Obrimos learns to work with ley lines, the Thyrsus with the Shadow, a Moros with hauntings, etc., but all with a focus on how these things affect the resonances of places and those places in turn affect them. Then, all it takes is plopping down a Mystery that brings some or all of these things together. Maybe the Obrimos follows the ley lines to a Hallow, but the Hallow's Mana has some freaky-deeky resonances. The Thyrsus and Moros arrive and find that the region it forms a node in was the site of a murder; the ley lines are pure resonances "upstream" but the node itself is contaminated and is now also a locus that's been feeding some nasty spirits.

              It won't always be this clean; sometimes, some players will be in the spotlight more than others, since not every Mystery will play to everyone's strength. It shouldn't be hard to have everyone have a role somewhere, though, given the utility of the arcana.

                  Advise I myself uncovered on forum and give it to players - Try to phrase Obsessions simpler. My Guardian player had for a long time 'Are they Hieromagus?' which give him A-Beats when he was checking over PCs for signs for Messiah of Magic. BUT it did not let him take A-Beats for looking on general lore about Hieromagus in his or other Orders libraries or even looking in to messianic lore of Sleepers ( like Jesus, Budda and rest of them ). Simpler stated Obsession of 'Hieromagus' works better here, as letting for more variety of scenes or even tempting players - like examples mentioned before.

                  On general - stick to Mystery name as Obsession, maybe marking your theory as simple variant in bracelet. Many PCs and NPCs in my games have Obsessions of Time Before ( i.e. 'Atlantis' thing ) - I different them by giving in their main thought on it in bracelet - so I have mages with 'Time Before ( Camelot )', 'Time Before ( Metropolis )', 'Time Before ( First Dream )', etc. But each one of them will generally get A-Beat when seeing or looking for ANYTHING from Time Before - confronting this with their theory could grant them even more A-Beats.

                  As for examples or broadening your characters Obsessions options - check those I uncovered from Orders books and their dogmas. They are great 'first Obsessions' for characters to start on.

                  Side Advise: As ST, you can make a simple house rule and let players choose 1 extra Obsession for general cabal / main plot Mystery - because starting with only 1 Obsessions is hard.

                      My characters Long Term Obsession is research of souls and soulstealing in order to find missing soul of her uncle. This makes her interested in all kinds of soul related incidents, including hunting Reapers. Before her Awakening, she was working as a private detective in her uncle's agency, and they got both of their souls stolen. Later, someone restored her soul, but her uncle wasn't so lucky; some time after that she Awakened and started investigating that tragic mystery.
                      The big twist she doesn't know about (but I as a player requested) is the fact that her uncle's soul *is* inside her right now, as it was the one used to plug the hole in her.

                              I'd personally say that three of those obsessions are actually personal goals. Obsessions are supposed to be about learning the truth behind supernatural mysteries. In addition, obsessions are generally supposed to be short term, with the exception of very opaque mysteries that draw an entire concilium to the scene (maybe also excepting a mystery that draws an entire cabal together).

                              There's much to be learned from magical items, but the abilities, origin, and purpose of magical items range in difference nearly as much as the various different kinds of people making them. Any one of those three would make more sense as an obsession, and it would be better if it had at least a broad category of magical items that Arbiter is interested in. A few examples might be "What are magical weapons like?" or "How are all the different kinds of dedicated tools created?" or perhaps "How do I work?" I suppose that last one is probably the closest to a supernatural mystery, since Arbiter is actually a living artifact. Granted, as an artifact, the opaqueness of that mystery could end up being fairly high, but it's actually a mystery.

                              It's a great idea to have a goal like making changes for yourself (and if messing with Time to do it is your shtick, then I say go for it), but this one definitely reads like a goal and not an obsession (would make for a great long term goal, aka he can still get beats, just regular ones, from it). If, on the other hand, it were written like: "What does a timetravel spell look like?" or "How does Temporal sympathy work?", then you would be dealing with something closer to a mystery. Secondary obsessions can then come into play where a particular event happens in the plot that relates to Time magic, and by becoming obsessed with the nature of the second event Erstwhile can move forward with his first obsession.

                              "What is Arbiter" is a GREAT example of an obsession. Arbiter is a living artifact, thus a supernatural mystery. You can stat it/him, the question is short and simple, and it's specific enough not to include other mysteries within itself. It could lead to other mysteries, but they wouldn't necessarily be completely within Arbiter's existence, but rather related to him.

                              "Learn how to turn off ghostly voices" is definitely worded as a goal (this one seems more like a short term goal, but once again, still an opportunity to earn regular beats). However, it is specific enough that it can be fairly easily altered to something like "What are ghosts?" or "How do ghosts use numina?" or "Why do ghosts interact with people?" Another possibility would be "Why are all THESE people hearing voices?"

                              I guess what I'm saying is that any and every obsession ought to have a supernatural mystery that you can give stats to and ought to sound more like a question than a goal. What, how, and why are the supreme words to be used. Where and when can fit decently, especially if the mystery is directly related to the Space and Time arcana. Who can work if you're trying to figure out the nimbus/supernatural-presence-thingiemajig of whatever caused something supernatural to happen.

                              I hope this makes sense and helps you! Granted, I wouldn't say that you absolutely can't use what you already have (White Wolf does a pretty good job of saying "Use what you need and do your own thing at your own table"), but that's my two cents on it.

                              Edit: Almost forgot to add it my character's obsession. Currently it's "Werewolf transformations." Yeah, dangerous stuff there!
