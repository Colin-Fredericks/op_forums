        Sure, my bad. But I still maintain that there's a lot more done with horror archetypes than what some people think. Why are Prometheans vulnerable to fire? Because "fire bad!" is something that's famously associated with the Creature. The way Sekhem works in Mummy is made to allow players to play unstoppable tomb robber-shredding ancient monstrosities and do stuff straight out of The Mummy. The reason it has Death Curse mechanics is right out of mummy folklore. Changeling draws extensively from both mythology and modern UFO media. Deviants are hunted by conspiracies because conspiracies are most often the antagonists in the inspirational media.

        Demon is most often called out as a deviation from the archetype it's based on, but it really isn't- it represents more modern monster archetypes, such as the reality-warping mysterious stranger (Agent Smith, the G-Man, Bill Cipher) and the killer robot/malevolent AI (the Terminator, the WAU, Agent Smith again).

        So yes, ChroD primarily uses recognizable horror archetypes as a basis. Even weirder things Hosts are based on horror archetypes: body snatchers, parasites, and the old standby of "a guy but he's actually full of spiders/worms/rats".

                                        Originally posted by Geckopirateship View Post
                                        Back to Mages, I want to keep the fact that being a Mage is something you choose to become, but add more consequences: make magic bleeding into your mind and the world around you more of a threat. Playing up the "you did this yourself" aspect by making the condition of being Awakened equivalent to: you deliberately turned yourself into a walking hole in reality because you wanted power/knowledge/to take over the world". I reeeeeeeeally want to make Mages feel less like "Humans 2.0".
                                        You don't really choose to become a Mage. You can choose whether or not you use magic, but a Mage who doesn't cast is still a Mage.

                                        Magic bleeding into the world around you is done through your nimbus running rampant through your sympathetic links. Theoretically it could get really bad, but that's one of the reasons why Shadow Names and the separation between your mundane and magical identity is a thing. Mages and their society attempts to put a buffer between themselves and the world around them, so as to avoid their magic needlessly spilling out though the people and places they care about. Usually that means that any time their magic does seep through and mess with the lives of their friends and family, it is due to a personal failure. Had they been cautious enough, safe enough, they could have prevented it from happening.

                                        I feel that that packs more impact than some mechanic that just fucks you over regardless in some vain attempt at 'Oh, look how evil and monstrous you are not being able to stop that thing you had absolutely no control over! How bad it is to be you~". Also, frequency plays a decent role in impact too. If it's something that happens all the time it sort of loses its flair. At that point Mages would logically just cut all ties to people around them and avoid the issue completely. With it being less frequent and even preventable, it allows them to stay close to their loved one long enough for that eventual slip up that ruins everything. So it becomes is really big high moment rather than some constant low key annoyance.

                                                            I'd generally say that the player characters being the monsters, and thus making them into protagonists changes the theme and mood of each of the lines quite a bit from their thematic roots. As Charlaquin says, Vampire still shares a lot of headspace with its inspirations, a blood sucking parasitical entity cursed to roam the night for eternity. But, as mentioned, the other games change up the protagonists of their setting quite a bit. Werewolf, rather than being about a curse that activates during a period of time each month wherein you become a bestial killer is about animistic wolfmen who police the border between humanity and a spiritual world. Mage, rather than being about people having congress with demons for arcane power is about Gnostic humans who've seen past the veil of illusion laid over the world and reformed their souls to access an arcane realm above ours.
