                Treat it as both. Wine is simultaneously its own thing, water with other stuff in it, and a poison; it can be interacted with on any of those levels.

                Well the description in Transubstantiation might be the closest thing to a singular citation, and Alter Integrity and Shrink/Grow are next along, but it's really mostly a thing acquired from a holistic reading of the magic, how it's understood and applied, as well as gathered from how writers talk about the thing across years.
                Transubstantiation lets you treat wine as a singular thing, it doesn't say you have to. For that particular spell there's probably not a reason not to treat wine as a singular substance, but the reach option exists.

                This seems to be indicative of a common misunderstanding:

                Saying that you can exploit e.g. coal and diamonds being allotropes of carbon is not the same thing as saying that magic cannot just be magic, too.


                Is there any instance in any book other than the Chronicler's Guide that treats magic as though it is not?
                Transmission is one example. In the pre-errata draft of the book WiFi, Bluetooth, and "radio waves" were treated as separate things that could not be turned into each other with a Ruling spell (the spell specifically said that frequency couldn't be altered). It changed in the final release because that just doesn't make sense (wifi and bluetooth are radio, and if you can't change frequency you can't manipulate FM).


                For the idea that it's not just that the books have as yet not depicted any spells or mages who engage with subjects in terms of directly targeting and manipulating in terms of molecular composition (despite ample opportunity to introduce such things with at least the Free Council) but that it's specifically not how magic can work, my own reasons include that it relieves a lot of headaches for trying to reconcile the effects of spells with a deeper understanding of chemistry and physics (we don't need to act as though tactile telekinesis is the reason Superman can lift buildings without them collapsing under their own weight), and because I find the standard for how imagos and the Supernal work to make magic more understandable and approachable.
                The writers aren't scientists, and they write what they know: which is fluffy. When they do try to write science-y it tends to be... not great and mostly pop-fluff (this isn't a dig, they're not scientists).

                And the science flavor is referenced heavily in the Free Council descriptions for paths and such.

I think the core idea of the spell is purification, making something into a purer and better version of itself with two specific mechanical effects to represent it. In the end, neither the game systems nor the in-game metaphysics care for scientific concepts like allotropes, just with what you are *actually* trying to accomplish.
