    The idea of the future not being written is an interesting one, and one that I totally have agreement with. I wouldn't really want to force players to be unable to do anything major just to keep history intact. Of course there is the issue of using modern knoweldge to effect the past ("So through my eternal wisdom I have devised what I call the number 0") in a way that bypasses the difficulty and the long time to design nature of some of our systems.

    Personally, I always like the idea of the magical society that manages to survive by hiding out in either a pocket area or underground or in a sky continent or some other magically potent place. That way the world actually keeps its general timeline shape but there is an active group which still exists behind the scenes.

    An idea for a major saga is by collecting certain divine artifacts held by the gods of the People a realm of power can be created where the People can fashion their own future.

    Another idea is that the skies lit up with the fire of a meteorite. Where it flashes supernatural energy manifests creating werid bizarre monstrous effects, and where it crashes matter is transformed into a different more pure or mystical substance which could change the face of the group that uses it.

    I had an idea that the peace is broken within the society of the People by the news from traders of entire towns going dark, which seems to be mysteriously moving in the direction of where the players community lies. As the shadows of the destruction move refugees from destroyed towns come to the player one which has to decide if they stay and fight or run and hide. What is the monstrous dark forces, what caused them to appear, and what can be done to stop them.

            Also, with all the shenanigans that come into play with Archmages and other such beings, you could create an entire alternate timeline that then gets erased... For the most part. There are plenty of different style of Ruins of the Time Before. It is possible that your alternate Order and Culture created them before being erased.

            For example, Camelot is given as an example of a kingdom that existed which was absorbed into the Supernal Apotheosis of Merlin, which led to it now instead merely being a Myth versus actual history. The same could be a possible cause of the disappearance of traces of the alternate history, along with other changes.

            Overall, this would allow you to have multiple games during different periods, with the history seemingly unconnected... Until the players explore the Ruins of the Time Before and see remnants of their adventures from past periods.

                Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
                I don't think creating cool institutions in the Neolithic need necessarily change Workd history in a significant fashion beyond possibly leaving cool artifacts and ruins that humans speculate on.
                Pretty much this. I'm hesitant to dismiss the idea of lasting change on that type of timescale and obviously you -can- leave a history behind or we wouldn't know about the real-life Vinca...but I want to reitarte that 7,000 years is an unimaginably long time when it comes to the human timescale. I don't mean this in a dismissive way, but unless things get incredibly large scale or out of hand, they can probably do whatever they want and it'll wash out over the course over human history barring supernatural shenanigans. Is that "lasting change"? Eh. It very much depends on your definition and what your goals are. I know some people who -hate- doing historical games or games in lost Hyborean ages because that automatically means that their struggles will be rendered moot. Personally, that doesn't bother me at all - I think it would still be worth doing a samurai historical game for instance, even though eventually the samurai class becomes irrelevant to "modern day" proceedings.

                All a matter of perspective. Anyways, I digress.

                Edit: Put another way, since it's on my brain: I make a character who's a meglomaniac, and I have a statue of myself built. How long does it have to last before tearing it down renders it forgotten or "meaningless"? A year after it's built? Five years? A decade? A century? A millenium? How long does it have to lay in ruin before the same thing? These are all questions that you have to answer yourself, but for me personally, it matters more that the statue got built in the first place than how long it stands.
