    Levels of directness in Acts of Hubris

    12-19-2017, 05:21 AM
    I recently played as a Moros of the Thread-Cutter Legacy (mystical assassins using Death and Fate), a legacy I think is a lot of fun to explore. In the session I had a mission to kill off a mid-level drug dealer and it ended up as a small massacre with him and 3-4 of his gangster bodyguards. My character however did not kill anyone, he did not even hurt anyone. He simply used Fate to "summon" a 3 dot Retainer, an ex-military who had vowed to avenge the death by overdose of his sister. I then persuaded him that we had the same goals and guided him into the right building and used Fate again to boost his skills as he went through all the bodyguards and then the drug dealer himself.

    This got us thinking if I would have to roll Wisdom or not. Premeditaded murder and violence is an example of an Act of Hubris at Wisdom 7, but since I did kill someone with magic but in a rather indirect fashion we were not sure if I would have to roll for degeneration or not. Also, I did not expose the sleeper retainer to obvious magic, only subtle Fate spells. What would you say?

                          Originally posted by Aristarkos View Post
                          THank you for your answers! This is not me trying to worm away from a Wisdom roll, this is me wanting to understand better the principles behind Acts of Hubris.

                          I was not sure about whether magic needs to be involved or not for it to be an Act of Hubris, but if you say it is then I will follow that logic.
                          Wisdom is explicitly not tied directly to morality like it was in previous editions. However, some of it is still bound up in morality, and for good reason. You will notice than many lesser crimes are not considered Wisdom violations, and if you inure yourself to a spell you will never have a Wisdom violation when using that spell, no matter what you do with it.

                          The idea behind Wisdom and Acts of Hubris in second edition is about how far will your character go when it comes to ends justifying the means. This is why you get Rampant or Megalomaniacal when you fail a Wisdom degeneration roll. Mage is not a game that asks "can you do it?" Instead, it's a game that asks "should you do it?" Mages can do basically anything (especially at 4 or 5 dots, to say nothing of archmastery). But is the shortcut always the right path?

                                                  Wisdom in Mage is pretty flexible. First, the book says that the list they give is just a set of examples, it's one of the places that can experience heavy editing by the ST. Second, there are multiple ways of undermining those examples through the mechanics of Mage via the player's choices. Mages suffer Wisdom loss primarily through impulsive action that creates unintended consequences, but over the course of their development, Mages get to decide what's Wise and what isn't. This may lead to giving the Abyss an advantage in attempting to influence their magic, but in the end, it's a Mage who by him or herself who determines what's Wisdom. The flexibility of Wisdom is why no one Order or Legacy has ever been acknowledged as Wise above all the others, including the Scelesti, Banishers and the Seers. No one has a monopoly on Wisdom, except the Mage who has Obsessed over Wisdom and how to obtain it, and even their beliefs are undercut by the Mage who is diametrically opposed, but no less dedicated to finding the truth of Wisdom.
