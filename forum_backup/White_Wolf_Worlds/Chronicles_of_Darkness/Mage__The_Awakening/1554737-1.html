        ⯌ 0.3 | Down the Rabbit Hole


        “Who in the world am I? Ah, that's the great puzzle.” — Lewis Carroll, Alice in Wonderland


        ⯌ ⯎ ⯌




        This session was shorter than usual because of scheduling issues. Despite that, and the fact that the entire session takes place within the same building, there's a lot of information gained and foreshadowing of future events. This’ll be a fun one to look back on in future stories. Keep your hard hats on, everybody, because the twists and turns are only just beginning!


        Sam Bishop stands behind a car in a parking garage, sucking on a chocolate Tootsie Pop with a phone pressed to his ear. He doesn't bother to introduce himself as the call connects.

        "Cover's been blown—I was forced to interfere."

        He quickly corrects himself.

        "Okay, perhaps forced to is not quite accurate. But I made a snap judgment, and it was my call to make. I don't micromanage you, so lay off a bit, huh?"

        He seems to take in the voice on the other end, thinking.

        "So, speaking of jobs,” he says, voice muffled by the candy in his mouth, “here's how this is going to go down. After I hang up, you're going to dial the last number in your call history. I imagine you can handle the rest on your own." He pulls off the Tootsie Pop with a wet pop and loudly crunches down on it.

        "There, that better?"

        The voice on the other end is silent.

        "Cheer up, I've got eyes on her right now.”

        In the trunk lies a woman, unmoving, limbs and hair splayed in every direction.

        Sam slams it shut and gets down to business.

        Switching perspectives at the beginning of the session here was something I did more or less on a whim, but players responded really well to it. And so, from this point onward, it’s become an Echo Chamber tradition to start with the camera somewhere else (and occasionally end that way too). My players are great about not metagaming using the information their characters don’t know, and it gives them a chance to see what the NPCs get up to when the PCs aren't around. In that way, it’s both a tool for generating intrigue and something that makes the world feel fuller and more alive.



        Damian gets a call from Oliver asking if he knows where Sarah is. He doesn't; the last time heard from her was this morning on the way to the funeral. Ollie says he talked to her just before her lunch break, which she has a habit of taking early, around 11:30. But he figures that she might be off with Damian's “friend.” This immediately raises some warning flags for Damian, who does not have any friends who would have a reason to interact with his staff in any capacity. He grows increasingly more concerned when Ollie shares his texts with Sarah, who was visited in the office by this “friend” who claimed he and Damian went way back. Damian makes a note to check in on Sarah when he's done with this weirdness at UCLA.

        Collin, listening in on the conversation, gets incredibly nervous and decides to give Dave a call for good measure. After four long rings, the call connects.


        "Hey, Dave, um… question. Has your internet or phone service been really spotty recently... since being out here?"


        All he hears on the other end is the sound of cars whooshing by.


        "Are you safe, Dave?"


        He checks the Find My App and discovers that Dave has turned off location sharing.


        "Dave, I don't know if you can hear me, but I cannot hear you. All I can hear is cars. If you can hear me, message me when you can, okay bud? Stay safe, please."


        Collin freaks out after hanging up, and Damian is curious about the app he was using to track Dave. Being an elderly man (read: millennial in his late 30s), he has no clue about newfangled technologies like that. Unfortunately, he’s not the kind of guy who has the funds for an iPhone, and even if he did Sarah probably wouldn’t share her location with him 24/7. As they talk, Damian hears the sound of footsteps approaching and recognizes a few voices. He turns the corner and sees The Gothip Girls (and their ordinary-looking third wheel) from the funeral arguing amongst themselves.





        "Ugh. I can't believe how bad my luck is. What are the chances Elias would be on break right now?"






        "Well, if Emily hadn't taken so long..."

        Emily, the odd one out in terms of both personality and appearance, decides she doesn’t want to be a part of the conversation and excuses herself, staying behind as the other two round the corner. None of them notice Damian surreptitiously eavesdropping on their conversation as he hangs out by the bulletin board. Meanwhile, Collin is looking into potential service outages in the area and why someone might not show up on the Find My app. He finds some generic advice about removing devices from settings and reinstalling, doing factory resets after backing up data, and other things he classifies as “bullshit.” But he does see a recent post in the UCLA subreddit from an hour ago entitled "What is up with the signal around the quad today?"

        Did you consider that maybe Dave just doesn’t want to be around you right now, Collin?

        Seeing the girls around the corner, he immediately recognizes them and accosts the leader.

        "Oh. Oh! You were at the funeral, right?"

        She seems taken aback by the out of the blue question from a stranger. When she asks if he’s a student here (since she doesn’t recognize him and the graduate program is rather small), he says yes… but online. Then he asks her about her cell service and whether it’s been spotty at all today.

        "No, it's fine, creepy dude.... It says I've got like, 5 bars."

        When he tells her that he was friends with Jacob, it’s like a flip has switched within her. She goes over to him and tries to hold his hand (though he instinctively flinches away from the contact), saying that Jacob was a joy to be in class with. He was a star student, and apparently was well-liked, if her reaction is anything to go by. Collin figures that since she was a friend and classmate, she’d be the perfect person to give him some answers and pulls out the medallion he found in Jacob’s room earlier.

        “Do you recognize this?”

        Before he even realizes what’s going on, she lunges for the medallion, trying to tear it from his grasp. Fortunately, Collin is able to keep it out of her reach, and he decides to slip it back inside his shirt where she won’t be able to get it so easily. He shouts to Damian for help as she calls him a thief and demands he return it. When Damian rounds the corner and asks for an explanation, the girl says Collin has stolen something that doesn't belong to him. Collin responds by accusing her of being the one trying to steal something.

        Damian points out that the medallion technically belongs to Mrs. Carver, and since they’re her representatives here, it only makes sense for them to have it. Seeing that arguing is futile, Gothip Girl #1 dials a number, puts the phone to her ear, and runs off. Her satellite raises her hands in surrender and follows behind.

        Given all the drama the medallion has caused thus far, Damian and Collin figure it’s best to put it someplace safe. The back window of Damian’s Chevy Cruise is cracked open, so he asks Collin to head back and put the unlucky trinket inside. In the meantime, he heads around the corner and finds the third wheel of the Gothip Girls standing around with earbuds in, listening to music and apparently unaware of the squabble that just occurred. He approaches and tries to get her attention. Surprisingly, she seems to recognize him.

        "Oh, you were in the back with that guy who totally ate shit."

        If you remember, Derrik nearly split his skull in two trying to climb backward over a pew at the church.

        She tells him about the odd little club that Deeanne, the queen bee of their little clique, is a member of. According to her it’s a weird Greek Life-esque situation funded by alumni of the school. Members wear togas during celebrations and speak in Latin, and they call themselves the Legion, though she’s sure they refer to themselves as something else that she can’t recall. Deeanne is a “Centurion,” which appears to be a middle-ranked leadership position.

        "Weren't you talking to Abigail at the funeral? I think she'd be the person to talk to. It is her club, after all."

        Damian can tell she’s holding something back, and though she’s a little tight-lipped at first, not wanting to gossip about the others lest she inadvertently become a pariah, Damian doesn’t have to try very hard to get her to spill the beans. She seems to be fairly miffed with the others, and grumbles about Deeanne deciding to go to the funeral at all when she hated Jacob. Apparently they were enemies, but it was completely one-sided. Emily figures that Deeanne’s reason for attending was Elias, her former TA who she’s “down bad for.” Unfortunately (or maybe fortunately), he didn’t show up to the funeral.


        Back in Elias’s office, the man of the hour tells Ray and Derrik to make themselves comfortable, “though it seems like you already have.” He says that people have been barging into his office all day, but hopefully with the door locked they’ll have to at least knock first next time.





        "So, are you the temps... or?"

        Derrik gives a noncommittal answer, not denying it but not actually saying they are either. Elias doesn’t seem to notice and asks if they want tea. Derrik seems about to refuse when Ray mentions that he’d love some. Elias turns to his cabinet, pulls out some mugs, and begins to get to work. Meanwhile, Derrik is looking at Ray in panic, as if to say, What are we supposed to do now? Ray just shrugs back, looking equally out of sorts.

        Elias asks how much they know about what the requirements are for the temp job. Derrik continues to be as vague as possible with his response, asking Elias to assume they haven’t been appropriately briefed on the responsibilities. The other man sighs and grumbles about the lack of preparedness despite all the funding that the department gets.

        Regardless, he gives Derrik and Ray the broad strokes of the situation. The original professor for their course on mythology and magic had a medical emergency at the beginning of the year and is still on leave. As such, it fell to the TAs to do everything, and they are woefully understaffed. He needs temps to collect assignments, field emails, and proctor the final exam if possible. When he asks if either of them have any experience with anthropology, Ray says he took a course when he was in undergrad, and Derrik says it wasn’t part of the department, but yes.

        As Elias sets down the tea, Derrik notices the steam wafting from the surface reshape itself into odd geometric patterns and can’t tear his eyes away from it. Ray hands over syllabi for the course and asks the two of them to familiarize themselves with it, saying he’ll get them the student list later. He gives them a sticky pad to jot down their numbers, and asks them their names, writing them on the sticky pad above their numbers. Surprisingly, he spells Derrik Thorpe right on the first try, without any assistance.

        "I think that's the first person I've ever seen do that right aside from family members…"

        Elias shrugs and says that people spell his name wrong all the time, too, adding a W to Ren.

        "I suppose I'm good at guessing."

        Outside, Collin looks around as he slips the medallion through a crack in the window. But as his fingers make contact with the glass, it shatters. A burning sensation wells up in his fingers as they start to bleed. All around him, silver butterflies hover in the air, seemingly drawn toward his bleeding fingers. Upon closer inspection, their wings appear to be completely metallic, their antenna and legs made out of segments of tiny silver arrows placed end to end like segments without any visible joints. Each of the butterflies seems to be sporting an elaborate silver eye mask, like they’re set to attend a masquerade. They land on his bloody fingers before taking off in another direction.

        Collin looks away for a brief moment to find something to wipe his hand, but when he looks back he’s no longer in the parking lot and nothing is as he remembers it. In front of him rests an ornate silver chariot with crimson accents. It is dark out, nighttime, with a large crescent moon glittering in the sky. All around there are chariots in various designs, all made of the same glimmering silver. Wildgrass grows freely from the ground, along with bushes laden with black roses. The butterflies from before have landed on the front bumper of one particular chariot across from what was once Damian’s car. This one was rimmed wheels with a spiky and rather modern look to them. The butterflies stop their flapping as he approaches, turning to him.

        "Hello."

        A series of high-pitched giggles echo around him as the butterflies shatter one by one, splattering the surface of the chariot crimson. There is the sound of straining metal as what was once the bumper of a car dents and twists as if subjected to unimaginable force.

        Screwy: Oh no, this is going to be Dave!

        Panic rises in his chest, but despite it all Collin manages to tamp it down.

        7 Successes!

        The eerie giggles fade as mist rolls out in all directions. As Collin turns to look back, what was once the UCLA Anthropology building is now a set of massive doors to the large grandfather clock he remembers from his dream. It towers above him, reaching up into the sky. Unfortunately, he’s unable to see the clock face. reaching up into the sky. More silver mist flows from the open doors to the clock, and a cloud of butterflies float inside, the sound of giggling accompanying them.

        When he enters, he finds the interior of the building exactly how he left it aside from the mist billowing from around the distant corner and the butterflies that seem insistent on taking him somewhere. Damian cannot see any of that but notices a silvery mist or aura of some kind wrapped around Collin’s head, seeming to move along with him. He reaches out and tries to swat at it, causing Collin to hear the sound of glass shattering and tell him to stop. Without much explanation, Collin heads further down the hallway, eager to follow the butterflies to his destination.

        Damian is suddenly overcome by a sensation of vertigo despite being very firmly planted on steady, stable ground. When he blinks, every surface around him is mirrored. For some reason, Collin doesn’t seem to reflect in any of them, but he does—in every single surface. Hundreds, maybe thousands of Damians stand in a seemingly endless sea of recursive reflections of reflections, each of them looking at him with a different expression on their faces. The air smells slightly of sulphur.

        Like any sane man in this situation, Damian immediately goes for his cigarettes, but he can’t find them. What’s more, as he looks down he notices a metal plate on his chest, with a round dial on one side. The metal is bent in places, damaged, with scratches and gouges marring the surface. What’s more, this metal plate seems to be protruding directly from his chest like the door to a safe. He hears the sound of rattling metal and notes heavy metal cuffs on each of his wrists, the chains connected to the outer edges of the safe door where a group of other chains are attached. Each is a different shape, thickness, and size. Most look painfully sharp, covered in barbs, hooks, and in one case, razor wire. What those chains connect to is impossible to tell, as they hang down low on the floor, stretching out all the way down the corridor in both directions and around the corners and out of sight.

        Continuing to follow behind Collin, Damian digs his fingers into the underside of the safe door, trying to see if he can pry open the door. Oddly, he doesn’t feel flesh or his ribs underneath, just empty space. Still, he continues to tug, wanting to get the thing out of his chest. As he does, the hall fills with the sound of creaking of metal and the myriad reflections around him have crowded in close, their hands pressed up against the reflective surfaces, palms stained red., feels like he could rip this open if he put his back into it. All the reflections around him have crowded in close, their hands pressed up against the reflective surfaces, hands stained red. Finally, the metal door gives and he tears it open, but his sense of satisfaction is short-lived as he sees the swirling black void in his chest and another wave of vertigo overtakes him. An overpowering pressure begins to build in his neck as he feels his body being pulled toward the epicenter, his neck bending at an unnatural angle as he crumples inward on himself, sucked into the void he unleashed.

        He’s back in the hallway, braced against a wall and freed of that awful vertigo. His cigarettes and lighters are splayed out on the floor, and the only sign that anything weird had even happened is the faint but inescapable smell of smoke and brimstone.

        I’m very happy with how this scene turned out. As I’ve mentioned a little before, my philosophy leans toward “Don’t prep plots,” or as I prefer to call it, “situations over scripts.” Though this entire session took place in the Anthropology building and there was a set of characters I knew were inside, none of what you’ve seen so far (aside from the opening and closing scenes) was scripted. Even the trippy weirdness Collin and Damian just experienced was off the cuff and a result of the actions they’d taken. I honestly hadn’t expected them to fight with the Gothip girls or drop their fancy Key Item in an unattended car, but boy am I enjoying the aftermath of it! There’s almost nothing I love more than putting player characters in a situation and seeing what they decide to do about it.

        Also, can I just say that Collian and Damian’s reactions to the trippy weirdness they’re experiencing are hilarious to me? Neither of them have stopped to process what’s going on, much less suggested to each other that weirdness is afoot. The onward march toward the goal cannot be stopped. But even so, the energy they’re bringing with them is different. Collin is giving small stepchild from a cruel European fairytale following enchanting creatures into the jaws of danger, whereas Damian is giving, “The mirror dimension torture chamber hallucination again? Huh, I guess it is Tuesday… Man, I need a drink.”


        "Is there anything else you need from me?" Elias asks after he’s collected their contact information.

        Derrik makes a comment about Elias being invited to the funeral. He flinches, saying there were no invitations as far as he's aware, and asks why the two of them were at the funeral if they’re not part of the Anthropology department. Ray pipes up that he was there for work.

        By way of response, Derrik hands over his polaroid and the invitation, running his fingers over the embossing. When he sees the name on the kids’ shirts, Elias mentions that there’s an unincorporated community in California named Whispering Pines. Apparently they had a set of cabins or a resort of some kind that burned down in the California fires some 10 odd years ago.

Funny enough, Keni was the one who came up with the name of this South Dakota faith camp. I was extremely amused when I first saw it in Derrik’s backstory. I love a happy coincidence.

        After a pause, Derrik begins to ask a question he’s been wondering ever since he saw that Elias was the screensaver for Jacob’s laptop.

        "Were you two...?"

        Elias slowly sips his tea.

        He says that Jacob was a bright, motivated student who was always fascinated and curious about the content. He showed up for every set of office hours and was a model student in the class.

        “He was a wonderful person, I'm sure. It's a shame."

        "Sorry,” Derrik replies somewhat coyly, “I thought you guys were closer."

        "That's hard to imagine. He's not really the type to take pictures."

        Derrik bursts out laughing.

        Au contraire, Mr. Teaching Assistant. He had polaroid pictures, ID photo pictures, webcam pictures, grainy camera pictures… Seems like he was an equal opportunity picture taker.

        Ray suddenly turns around as if he's noticed something, but then turns back and shakes his head.

        "It would be quite a misstep if I forgot to send my temps the schedule and the roster of students... but you're not really here to be my temps are you, Derrik?"

        "What makes you say that?"

        "Well, for starters, you're not a student at this school. You graduated, was it… 2 years ago, I believe? From the Law School?"

        "You're a lawyer—"

        "—Not Yet."

        "A soon to be lawyer, and you live in the Hollywood area, correct?"

        "Why do you know where I live, Elias?"

        Elias slaps the polaroid of Whispering Pines on the table facing Derrik.

        "Well, I imagine the reason I know where you live is the same reason you have the strange idea that Jacob has a picture of me as one of his screensavers. Probably because this picture was in his room."

        "...This was in his room?"

        "Oh, you didn't know that?"

        "Nope," Derrik says, smirking.

        Elias says that once he learned about Jacob’s situation, he’d told him how absolutely mental it all was. But Jacob didn’t seem interested in listening to what he had to say.

        "But he did have a lot to say about you and... who were the others? Damian... Whelan. And Collin... Whitman, I believe?"

        "And that’s how you know how to spell my name."

        "Bingo. Quite sharp."

        Elias asks why Derrik lied about being a temp, and Derrik defends himself by saying that Elias was the one who locked him and Ray in the room. Elias retorts that he found them canvassing the room when he returned.

        "I'm looking around for a key, I guess. And his mom sent me here to go through his stuff because we were such good friends," Derrik says, the last few words positively dripping with sarcasm.

        "Well you should tell her no. And drop this investigation that you're doing. Honestly, immediately."

        "Why?"

        "Because clearly Jacob was suffering from severe mental health issues. That wall was mental, as I said. And whatever it is he got wrapped up in, that's the reason he died."

        "Yeah, but doesn't the back of your board have something to do with his mental illness?"

        Woof. Just go for the jugular, why don’t you.

        Elias slowly shifts, his shoulders straightening as he leans back against his chair. Derrik can hear the sound of fabric rubbing against itself as he crosses one leg over another. Derrik, meanwhile, has his chin resting on his knuckles, a shit-eating grin on his face.

        "That sounded an awful lot like an accusation."

        "Just curious why so much of you and him are related, mostly."

        "I imagine that's because he was one of my former students. And because his academic interest was very closely related to the subject matter of the class that I taught at one point."

        "Gotcha," Derrik says, with attitude.

        "What I'm curious about,” Elias throws out, matching the snark, “is why he wanted to write his dissertation about you and that Bible Camp you were a part of. That's what's curious to me."

        And once again the tables turn. Derrik averts his eyes, the easy, carefree attitude crumbling as he swallows and curls into himself, looking deeply uncomfortable.

        "Why was he seeking out the assistance of a PI? And why is it that you received an invitation to a funeral that, as far as I'm aware, wasn't giving out invitations? And now, conveniently, you have access to his room and are ‘investigating.’ How strange."

        Derrik says he doesn’t want to be a part of this. He really, really doesn’t.

        "Oh? Really? Then how about you walk across the room, unlock the door, and…” he trails off for a bit. “Bugger off? Then you don't have to have to be part of this, no? Seems simple."

        And this is the point where the conversation starts spiraling out of control and civility starts going out the window. Which is unfortunate, as you'll see, because Derrik no longer has the advantage in the verbal sparring session and now he actually wants to get answers out of Elias.

        "What did he, um... what did he write about the camp?"

        "And why should I tell you that?"

        "Because maybe... he got some things wrong and.... it should just be trashed."

        The flame of the lavender scented candle on Elias’s desk shifts from a bluish purple to a red to green to yellow, the wisps of smoke matching the color of the flames. At the corner of his vision, Derrik can feel the intense gaze of the biblically accurate angels drawn on the blackboard. They blink, and Derrik tenses himself up immediately, making himself small and trying not to hyperventilate.

        "Dontlookdontlookdontlook."


        Collin follows the butterflies through a maze of corridors before they finally reach their destination: a massive door with intricate silver filigree. Elias’s name is carved into the surface of the door in an intricate cursive script and inlaid with gemstones. According to the clock on the door, he should be returning anytime soon. As Collin watches it, he realizes that the clock uses a decorative silver arrow, engraved with patterns and symbols, instead of an hour hand.

        An array of strange symbols that call to mind those from the medallion carve themselves into the frame of the door, starting from the bottom and slowly climbing on either side and meeting at the center. As they reach the top and the entire frame is filled, a silvery crescent moon appears facedown above the door, lasting only a few moments before it fades out and the carved runes fill themselves in from the top of the door to the bottom, as if they were never even there to begin with.

        The cloud of butterflies around Collin moves toward the door and melts into it, disappearing from view.

        Meanwhile, Damian grabs his cigarettes from the floor, the slightest physical contact enough to make him feel a little better.

        Addiction is a hell of a thing.

        As he follows behind Collin, Damian takes note of the fact that he’s expertly navigating the corridors without any guide or reference, never hesitating or looking back. He’s never been here, but it’s as if he’s navigating his way through his house rather than an unfamiliar, labyrinthine building. For some reason he can’t imagine, Collin stops in front of a plain wooden door, staring at it in wonderment and awe.

        When Collin knocks on the locked door, he feels a familiar burning sensation as the arrow is dislodged from the clock, falling to the floor. Its tip is stained crimson, and the entire thing is oddly warm to the touch. To Damian’s eyes, Collin swats at the plastic hand on one of the cheap time in/time out clocks on the door, knocks it to the ground, and then examines it as if he’s staring at the Hope diamond.

        "Colln, what are you doing?"

        "This arrow is warm."

        He’s never beating the allegations.

        Collin's blood seems to sink into the grooves that are carved along the surface of the arrow, running from point to end, causing the fletching to shift from a rainbow starburst pattern to a bright crimson. Damian, naturally, does not witness any of this. He notes the name on the door and Collin's odd behavior over the past several minutes.

        "I thought you didn't know where he was?"

        "Where who was?"

        Exasperated, Damian decides it best to ignore him and knocks loudly on the door, at which point he hears the rattling of metal and swears he can see a metal cuff around his wrist. Some of the chains hanging from it trail all the way to the ground and through the gap under the door.

        Back in Elias's office, Ray sniffs a couple times and turns to look at the door, murmuring that he thinks Damian is on the way. Derrik, incredulous, asks if he just sniffed the air to identify a person. Ray simply says that it smells like cancer and cologne, but all Derrik can smell is lavender.

        He makes the mistake of glancing at the candle, at which point the hum of a choir picks up in the background, hundreds of perfect voices harmonizing.

        "Nononononono."

        When they hear the knock at the door, Ray excitedly announces that it's Collin and Damian. Derrik rises to his feet to let them in, though halfway through he realizes he’ll have to look in the direction of the blackboard and so decides to walk backwards to the door instead. The scent of lavender in the air fades, replaced with the unmistakable smell of ozone. Before he reaches the door, Elias calls out to the others to enter, saying the door is unlocked. To Derrik’s surprise, it is.

        Damian makes his way in and goes to shake hands with Elias, who seems to know everyone's names already. Elias initially reaches out with his bandaged right hand, then realizes and swaps to his left.

        To Collin, the office looks relatively normal, except that the five butterflies are floating in a slow circle around Elias's head, their antennae made of arrows creating the silhouette of a crown of thorns over his head. The area behind his desk seems to be coated in a silvery fog that makes the back wall impossible to discern and casts Elias in an intense backlight. In fact, the more he stares at the other man, the more his features seem to shift, looking more androgynous in moments and far more masculine in others.

        "The butterflies led us here," Collin says, pointing to the empty space above Elias’s head.

        "Ignore him," Damian grows.

        Hanging out with Collin is a lot like spending time with a small child who is just beginning to learn how to use language and strings together sentences you could not have possibly imagined.

        Though he's pretty distracted by the butterflies, an unusual drawing on Elias’s chalkboard catches his eye. It’s the silhouette of five tower-like structures, all captured using a single, unbroken line. Despite using only one line, each of the structures has a distinct shape language and style to it.
