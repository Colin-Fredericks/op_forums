                                        Originally posted by monteparnas View Post
                                        Again, though, that may be both a severe take on the meaning of "precision" and demanding too much "realism", so I'm open to arguments on lowering this bar.
                                        I mean, the "argument" is that this is about the weapons' utility as symbolic aids for spellcasting, so "realism" doesn't enter into it unless a character is using that realism to recognize additional symbolic latitude (or contrariwise, experiencing a mental block on recognizing applicable semiotics because of it).

                                        "Precise" weapons in that context are mostly going to be lighter-looking piercing weapons, just like how a whip is a "cruel" weapon and a double-edged sword is a "noble" weapon. The symbolism of the weapon is "this hits a small area and can thus be used to strike precisely," which isn't communicated with crushing weapons like maces or hammers or with weapons designed to make big cuts — the Ars Nova uses blackjacks as an example Acanthus weapon because they're used mainly for blows to the head (often as part of a surprise attack), which is harder to hit than the body in general. The weapons of the Acanthus are the tools of meticulous planners and quick-witted opportunists.
