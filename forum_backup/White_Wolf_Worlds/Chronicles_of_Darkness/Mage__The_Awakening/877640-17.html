                                                    Originally posted by Mrmdubois View Post
                                                    Suspension basically seems like an attempt at an advanced Ban, is that right?
                                                    Sort of, but not really. Ban and Suspension could both be classified as spells that "stop stuff," but they do it differently. Both ban and Suspension can trap something, but Suspension actually stops the motion of the subject. Ban is better for hiding (obviously), but Suspension can create a wall. While ban loses some of it's protective value the larger it gets, Suspension doesn't. Ban prevents an enemy mage from physically reaching you (presuming you are not both on the same side of the ban), Suspension goes further and prevents a mage from implementing yantras that include physical motion (grasping a magical tool, forming mudras, using brawl/athletics/weaponry, looking in a mirror (presuming they aren't already), etc). Suspension can trap an individual without risking them from dieing of asphyxiation (unless you ruled that air wouldn't move, in which case this spell is instantly evil). Ban inverts space while Suspension transforms it from being dynamic to being static.

                                                    The more you think about, the more different applications you'll find. And one of the beautiful things about that is that Suspension doesn't make Ban obsolete (not even the wierd, +1reach advantage obsolete).
