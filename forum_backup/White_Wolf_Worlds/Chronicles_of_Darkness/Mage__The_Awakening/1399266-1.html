    Also, for entities from there, keep in mind creatures from the Lower Depth want to steal or experience what they lack, and have pretty understandable minds; fiends of Duat want to torture people because it makes them hurt less, Strix steal bodies because otherwise they have no Life to experience physical existence or sensation with.

    Abyssal beings either hate the world or cannot adapt it to fit them, so they destroy things out of hand, either intentionally or due to their natures crashing with it. They're often extremely alien and often arguably not sentient in any meaningful way.

    A good rule of thumb is that a Lower Depths demon is incomplete and seeing completion, an Abyssal gulmoth is an oxymoron in some way, and that paradox damages everything nearby.

            Emanations are essentially the foundations for alternative settings for Mage. They're planes of existence situated between the Material and the Supernal. Each is themed according to a particular Aracanum and symbol set. You get to one through a special kind of Iris. Signs of Sorcery explains them in detail, but the bottom line is that if you want to play Mage in some tropey alternative reality like Dinosaur World, Post Apocalyptic Urban Decay, Water World, or Burning Hellscape you can situate it in an Emanation.

                    In 1e, specifically Inferno, they make the distinction that the Abyss can be viewed anti-reality and ultimately wants to annihilate the world, subsuming it piece by piece. Off-hand I don't recall if the Abyss is doing the same to the Lower Depths, but at least Inferno actually love (in-so-far as they are able) the world as it is. The Abyss is destroying what Inferno seeks to claim for its own, and there is a brief sidebar describing how the two realms occasionally clash in open battle. In short, the Abyss wants to devour what Inferno seeks to corrupt, and the two goals are mutually exclusive

                        Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post

                        Oh. I thought all other realms besides the supernal/abyss/Shadow/astral were considered Lower Depths?
                        It is a bit more complicated than that, but still not a bad way to go, IMO.

                        The first thing we need to remember that the Lower Depths is something that mages ue to describe some realms of existence. The fact that mages would call a certain realm a "Depth", does not immediatly means anything that those realms share in common, beside the fact that those realms lack some aspect of themselves, and that they devour that aspect from the material world. Depths have almost nothing in common, and mages simply group them together because that's what mages do- but again, if you were to have some realm that is not based around lacking a certain aspect of reality and eating that aspect in some way or form, it is not a Depth, but something else which is found outside of Mage's cosmology all together.

                        Now, the thing is that when you examine Mage's most basic definition for a Depth, Duat does checks out- it is a world outside of the overall model, which lacks some aspect (Sekhem/ Souls) and which devours that aspect from reality. Ammut, as a being of hunger, fits those themes, and a mage who would discover the Arisen and their cosmology is likely to claim it to be a Depth without a second thought. On the other hand, Duat does seems to be more "special"- as many of the Depths are "just" dark corners of existence while Duat seems to be related to the primordial concepts of souls and creation. If it is what the Arisen claim it to be, it is a realm where all souls are being judged, where the worthy should gain access to A'aru, whatever that means and wherever it may be. But then again, who said that mages would not lump realms of great importance as a part of the Lower Depths just because they are important? No one ever said that Depths can't be important- Inferno is such example, and Dis is also a world we shouldn't disrespect.

                        So in short, the Lower Depths ia a term which mages use to describe many realm which do not fit to their model, and which all lack a certain "thing". It is an in character term, which of course puts the Supernal in the middle and the Depths at the periphery because that is Mage's ciosmology model. Are those realms indeed perepherial? Are all the realms that mages call "Depths" are indeed just the same? How much of Inferno, Dis and Duat share with one another beside all lacking something? That's the thing- Mage's model only has one dimension: moving from the top to the bottom. What about going sidewise? How many dimensions do we really need in order to truly decribe the multiverse?

                        That leads me back to the main topic of this thread- the difference between the Lower Depths and the Abyss. On the surface, the two of those do have something in common- they are antagonistic towards existence as we know it, and they both could contain countless of different realms and subrealms. However, there is a key difference between the two- the Abyss hates us, and the Depths love us. The reason is that the Abyss is about replacing aspects of our existence, while the Depths want to preserve the world as is- only make it worse, and worse, and worse. The reason is that the Depths eat parts of the world, and if the Abyss were to replace them, it would interfear with their nourishment. For example, what do you think would happen if the Abyss would replace all of the blood in the world with acid? Vampires will have to adapt to it, or go extinct. Vampires going extinct means that whatever gods which rule over vampirism would also die out. And as such, those blood gods would do everything they can in order to stop the Abyss from replacing blood with acid, because that's the existence they need in order to survive. A better - and canon - example would be the concept of the Abyssal Virtue, where the Abyss replaces what people have as Virtues with some other twisted morals. Inferno feeds upon Virtue. It is defined around corrupting Virtue. If you were to replace Virtue with something else, Inferno is no longer defined, its testaments mean nothing and it can no longer feed. As such, Inferno would fight any attempt of the Abyss to replace Virtue.

                        So here is the main difference between the two- the Depths are tied to the world. They are what the world lacks, and as such they need it in order to feed. The Abyss, however, is what this world is not- it is defined by not being this reality. Everything which never was is found in the Abyss- and as the number of things which do not exist in inifnite and the number of things which do exist is finite, we are talking a vast realm of none existence, endless possibilities for how this world could be not what it is. The goal of the Abyss is to replace existence with all other existences- which means to destroy the world, for if all possibilities are ture, none are ture, and we are found in the Abyss. The Depths, however, wants to have to world to survive as is as long as possible, and remain in constant suffering, pain and decay. If the Depths are zeros, the Abyss is infinity. Try to multiply them together and all hell breaks loose- literaly. That's actually why I highly doubt that Duat is Abyssal in nature- it does not try to replace anything, does not represents any alternative reality, and wants to preserve the world as much as possible so it would suffer as much as poissible to feed it as much as possible. The Ocean of Osiris, however? Yeah, that may very well be the Abyss.

                        To sum it up- the Lower Depths is a Mage term to describe things which Mages don't really understand, but they do have common traits (that is, lacking something about the world). The Depths differ from the Abyss because they love our world, seek to preserve it as much as possible and feed upon its misery, while the Abyss hates the world, seeks to end it as soon as possible and just wants silence. It is very likely that mages would call Duat a Lower Depth, but the fact that they do means practically nothing about whether it is true or how significant Duat is to the world, because their model only has one axis and is known to fail when you look sidewise.

                        Just my view on the subject.

                                                    Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                                                    The Inferno lacks Virtue which they "consume" by spreading Vice in the material world.
                                                    I should point out that this is an inference based on the "world without X" descriptive model that doesn't quite bear out from how the Inferno operates. Virtue and Vice alone are not morally weighted, and Inferno makes distinctions between, for example, a goetic demon and a goetia that is an Infernal demon.

                                                    A more concrete way of putting it is that, back on the old forums, Malcolm Sheppard once answered the question of why the Inferno was a Lower Depth with (slightly paraphrased) "It's because evil is a lack."

                                                    Fast forward a couple years to the Mage 2e open development blogs, where this tidbit comes up in the discussion of the Paths' relation to morality:
                                                    Originally posted by Malcolm View Post
                                                    To my mind, evil is really about cultivating a lack of compassion within yourself--it's not a force coequal with good, a part of a cycle, or anything that balances anything else. It's not black on the chessboard. It's spitting in the other guy's face when you play, and smashing his king with a hammer instead of losing.
                                                    Appropriately enough, there's bits of this in Mummy, which Malcolm also contributed substantially to; Dreams of Avarice's distinction between the Self and the Will, as well as its illustration of how the Law of Suffering locks people into deliberate cruelty out of fear for their own comfort and well-being, bears mentioning when one looks at how the agendas of Infernal Dominions tend to break down — not for nothing do the two least-complicated categories of true demonic behaviors boil down to "make the world materially worse to live in" and "kill people to hurt other people."

                                                    The akathartoi warm themselves in the Inferno by getting people to forsake that crucial bit of sympathy for others. One of the key ways the Supernal differs from the Phenomenal World is that classical basis for compassion: all things are one, and separation is an illusion that leads us to hurt ourselves when we hurt others — moreso still when we double down on the idea that this is not the case and revel in having power over other people. Whatever the Judges truly are, they have a certain investment in propagating that form of evil at the very foundations of human civilization as they consume the stuff of manifest existence through their Arisen servants.
