                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                        But I find the fact that you can't create an otherwise identical spell with swapped Primary Factors awkward and forced.
                        Because primary spell factor is supposed to stem from the nature of the spell. Primary spell factor is literally the last thing you pick before casting a spell in creative thaumaturgy, and it is supposed to be an extension of the previous steps in the process. If you did the same thing in the previous four steps, you should end up with the same result in step 5. Doing something different in the previous steps (esp. the first one) means you're no longer making an identical spell.

                        To me, wanting to do everything identical but the last step of the process is like saying you want to fire a gun without Firearms, but don't want to justify it by doing something different than just firing a gun normally. You don't get to swap Intelligence + Science (Ballistics) for Dexterity + Firearms to shoot someone in combat just because you want to "science" shoot them instead of normal shoot them. I can imagine situations where you can justify it (like having set up a remote firing gun-trap), but those situations are when you're doing something "similar" instead of "identical."

                        "Artificial" is a useless term when we're talking about how to model something in an abstract game system, since it's all artificial. The magic system isn't modelling something real where we can point to limitations in the system being artificial constraints compared to how it actually works.

                        You can't pick your primary spell factor directly, because you don't pick it directly. You pick it indirectly when you design the rest of the spell. Your character's ability to "pick" comes in when modifying spell factors as they actually cast the spell.

                                I feel that "Primary Factor is not a choice, it's a logical path of least resistance for the spell in question to take and making it go against it requires extra deliberate effort from the Mage represented by Reach" would be much easier to swallow for a lot of people if the game actually went out to teach you about the concept and presented some sort of guideline that would help you absorb the logic and grok how to sort the "this is obviously a Potency Primary kind of EFFECT, and this is obviously a Duration Primary kind of EFFECT".
