                                Shifting Sands (Time •••)
                                Practice: Fraying
                                Primary Factor: Special
                                Cost: 1 Mana
                                Suggested Rote Skills: Academics, Occult, Survival

                                The mage may step back through time a short distance, undoing a few precious seconds. This spell sends the subject back through time a number of turns in Reach used on it. The subject retains any injuries and Conditions gained in the undone turns, and spent Mana and Willpower do not return. Spells cast on her person in the undone time remain as long as she cast them. All other spells she may have cast or had cast on her in the intervening time are canceled. Until the subject catches up to the present, the distortion caused by this spell is visible under Active Time Mage Sight. Once she does so, any changes she made to history become Lasting.

                                +1 Reach: The subject travels back a one turn. This Reach effect may be applied multiple times.
                                Maybe it's a typo, but you should get at least one turn of time travel from the spell, and the spell should have an actual primary spell factor, even if it's a neutered Potency for dispellation purposes. Also, the spell doesn't have any utility as an extended casting, so the free Reach is essentially wasted. Half of the options of an Exceptional Success are gone with a "special" primary factor, making it less valuable as a Praxis, and diminishing the power of Yantras in its casting.

                                Suggestions: first, back to Potency. Next, get one 'free turn' of time travel. OR, the subject can revise the turn's action with a bonus equal to Potency, or change the target of the action, or choose a different action, or apply a modifier to another character's dice pool for that turn or the present turn. +1 Reach extends the depth of the time travel by another turn's worth (multiple applications). +1 Reach applies a dice trick to rolls affected by the spell. Find some way to make it useful in extended castings, or at least some way where it's useful outside combat situations. I'd feel pretty cheated were I an Acanthus player getting nerfed on the spell that exemplifies Time magic's power.

                                                            Actually, I don't think the game is perfect, but if talking about differences at the table is something you want to do...well okay. I have a group that's working its way through Reign of the Exarchs that's running on 1.5 rules, they aren't halfway through and yet all of them are at least 1st degree Masters by now. Time shennanigans have been a feature for a while now. Are the range of options available to the players broader? Yeah, of course it is, but when you are able to take those things into account you can create a game that isn't going to hit as many hiccups just because someone can jump through time.

                                                            If removing or heavily restricting time travel is what Wyrd needs to do to make the game work for him, that's fine. It still isn't an actual indication that the mechanic itself is broken or bad. My useful solution isn't aimed at mechanics, it's true, but if you think that any given ST doesn't have room to improve his ability to run a game then you're gravely mistaken. I'm not saying that Wyrd (Or any ST who has problems with time travel) is a bad ST, I'm raising the question of is he trying to learn from this experience that made him uncomfortable or is he reflexively moving to what he's been comfortable with in the past rather than trying to improve?

                                                            In other words, my useful solution is to think critically, is it the game mechanic or is it the ST that needs to change? Since there have been a number of threads on time travel and how to handle it already I'd suggest going to look at those. Charlaquin and Arthexis have both had some pretty insightful things to say about it.

                                                            By the way your assumption that the game's mechanics don't match the fluff is flawed. We don't know the complete story of the characters involved. The Present in Mage is set as a continuously moving point and the future is in flux. The Past is set until events in the Present change it. The events in the story were set in the (or a recent) Present. If we continued to follow the characters we might find that the main character did in fact learn Time eventually and manage to succeed in preventing the death of her partner. It doesn't necessarily service the piece of fiction to take into account all points in the timeline of what comes next, and since the story is set so closely to the Present and the future is in flux it cannot in fact be known yet if the Past will change. This can in fact be directly mimicked by the way the actual game is played. You don't play the game as if the future has already happened, you play as if you're experiencing the Present when you don't have the skill with Time to change the Past. Maybe later in the chronicle your character will feel driven by guilt to learn Time in order to go back and rectify her mistakes, but -right now- you can't.
