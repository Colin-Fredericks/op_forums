                                            I'm firmly in the "tempering" camp - that it takes an active/deliberate effort to perfect a material. It just fits the metaphysics of the setting better.

                                            When Mage talks about "Supernal truths", its putting a magical twist on a philosophical concept known either as "universals" or "Platonic forms". Its a cool concept - if you're into philosophy - but the basic idea is that all mundane (Fallen) objects are just bad imitations of "perfect" versions that exist on some higher plane of existence.

                                            Take a look at gold, for example. According to Mage's metaphysics, the stuff you buy at the jewelry store isn't actually "gold". Instead, its a crude imitation of the Platonic form of "gold", clumsily built out of totally unrelated bits of matter like "protons" and "neutrons". As a result, all sorts of extraneous (or "junk") properties are present, interfering with the true/magical properties of "gold".

                                            So orichalcum isn't merely some enchanted form of everyone's favorite yellow metal. Its a radical new state of matter, created by systematically stripping the mundane metal of every principle and component that isn't found in both the physical and spirit realm versions of the stuff. The mage is actively pushing it into something as close to the idealized universal/Platonic form/Supernal manifestation as possible - if a mundane scientist could examine orichcalcum (or any other perfected material), they'd be talking Nobel prizes and revolutionary breakthroughs in materials science.

                                            Exactly what that means will depend on your campaign, but you can get some possible ideas by looking at neutrons. Neutrons are subatomic particles that serve primarily to stick the protons in an atom together - under the current model, they contribute nothing to the nature of the material itself. So, from a Platonic form/Supernal perspective, they're just garbage getting in the way - something the mage will have to rip out to get orichalcum.

                                            Now - assuming your game ever gets that detailed - this could mean that orichalcum has a radical new form of nucleus, laid out so that its stable with only a fraction of the neutrons that mundane gold has. Or maybe it has no neutrons whatsoever. Heck, maybe it doesn't even have a composite nucleus at all - perhaps the perfecting process has replaced the traditional nucleus with an exotic new form of subatomic particle.

                                            Which is probably why it takes magic to create alloys of the perfected metals. In perfecting the metals, the mage has removed the chemical properties that normally allow them to be alloyed, or to otherwise (chemically) react with other substances.

                                                    As for why mages can't perfect machines?

                                                    Well, metaphysically/philosophically, there's no inherent reason why they shouldn't be able to. Computers, cars, cellphone chargers - there should be a Platonic form for all of them. So, in principle, perfecting one should be possible.

                                                    And in-game, there's certainly support for the idea. The second attainment of the Forge Masters, "Primal Tempering", allows them to create a sort of poor-man's perfected machine. Detailed in Legacies: the Ancient, it uses the example of a typewriter to explain how it works. The attainment strengthens the core (Platonic form) properties of the typewriter, allowing . . . oh, say, a ghost to type a story on it. But it does nothing to help if you decide to bash someone upside the head with said typewriter - the fact that it can be used as an impromptu club is an incidental ("junk") property, and thus not affected by Primal Tempering.

                                                    However, the fact that Primal Tempering requires the mage to reshape his soul hints at the problem. Outright perfecting a machine may simply be too complicated for a normal mage. After all, if you just perfect the components, you'd probably wreck the machine, since perfecting metals alters their conductivity (etc.). A mage with enough Science/Engineering skill could probably come up with a design the can benefit from the altered physical properties of the perfected metals. But trying to do it without those skills would probably fry your cellphone, start a fire, or worse.

                                                    And that's not the same thing as perfecting the actual machine. Now, its not hard to imagine the properties of a perfected cellphone charger. It would (almost) instantly charge any cell phone (regardless of manufacturer), using only a fraction of the power of a normal one, the cords would never tangle or break . . . But getting the cell phone charger into such a perfected state would be a nightmare. You'd have to preserve the charger's ability to convert AC into DC, simultaneously ramp the resistance both up and down, lock in perfect shape memory . . . Looking through the lists (both old and new) of perfected materials, none of them have those properties, and some of them seem to be mutually exclusive. If a perfected metal would get mundane scientists talking about a Nobel prize, a perfected machine would get mages talking about the same.

                                                    Now, some techgnostic cabal or other will probably figure out a way to perfect a machine . . . eventually. It'll probably result in a new Legacy, and possibly require multiple mages working in concert . . . But that will take time - and complex machines are relatively new. Remember, electric machines (for example) are only about a hundred years old. By comparison, humans have been working with the (known) perfectable materials since prehistory. Thousands of years, after all, is a lot of time to get good with a particular skill
