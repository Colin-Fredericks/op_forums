        So, in the interest of throwing out ideas for how to handle time travel problems narratively when you don't have a time traveling antagonist:

        Make it difficult to use time travel in the place where the bad guys are located. Maybe they're holed up in a place like an Ansho.

        Have plot critical scenes happen at time sensitive junctures. In other words the PCs show up right as the bad guy is about to complete their evil plan. This way if a PC goes back, any time he spends trying to be better prepared is time not spent toward getting to that critical point when the bad guy is just about to succeed.

        Have multiple things happen at once or in close proximity. If the PC jumps back in order to prepare better or get to the critical event sooner then he runs the risk of not achieving some goal that happened very recently such as having an important interview with the Heirarch. You can also effectively use splitting the party to achieve a similar effect, the PC can't go back to tell his buddy to be better buffed because the buddy is having the important Heirarch interview.

        A scene is roughly a half-hour to an hour, but it's not chronologically precise. Within the framework of how many scenes a PC goes back via his Reach you can plop him down just about anywhere making for some possibly tense, awkward or plot fueling consequences.

        The bad guys can move while the PCs delay to be better prepared. Maybe they're done with their hidey-hole or step out for coffee.

        If you do end up needing to replay a scene you can speed it up by reusing the results of dice rolls unless someone performs a different action from what they did last time. Only the time traveller really knows what is or isn't different (This one could require some good cooperative roleplaying and a bit of metagaming) and so can communicate what needs to change. Restrict his ability to do this communication (I.e. No blabbing out a whole plan of a different course of action as a free action).

        I think using strategies like this and devising more of them would do a lot to improve the ability of an ST to run a game without attempting to rewrite the game.

                                                    ...ok, im really annoyed with the forum software, as it first replicated my post 4 times, and now it ate the only I left unedited with my original post.

                                                    Anyway, because I don't have all day:

                                                    1. Add 1 Mana cost to the Shifting Sands.
                                                    2. Add following line to the spell itself: "Character casting or targeted by this spell gains Temporally Unstable Condition. Resolving it does not grant Beats."
                                                    3.
                                                    TEMPORALLY UNSTABLE
                                                    You are currently wrestling with Sands of Time, trying to manipulate the past. This makes any further attempts at Time Travel harder and more complicated; any attempts at Time Travelling demand 1 Mana in addition to any other costs, in order to lubricate the Timestream. This cost grows by 1 for each Time Travel attempted during this Condition's duration. This cost also isn't a part of the spell itself, so effects that allow to bypass it - like Attainment's Mana Refund, or Exceptional Success at Spellcasting - don't interact with it. You may not spend multiple turns to pay this special cost, you need to pay it in one go.
                                                    Possible Sources: Shifting Sands spell, using a Time Machine
                                                    Resolution: Start of a new scene that wasn't initiated by Time Travel.
                                                    Hardcore Version Resolution: End of the original scene that was Time Travelled from, or of the scene that will take it's place in new timeline.

                                                    What it does:
                                                    It doesn't limit how far you can time travel, but it limits the spamming problem. It basically puts a cap of how many times you can use Shifting Sands in a scene equal to your Gnosis, *and* it drains your Mana like crazy. This introduces a common trope in Time Travel Stories, where characters have a limited number of "repeats", and can't just replay at their leisure until they win, as they have to make the most from their spare time. The Hardcore Version could be used when characters are traveling SCENES, not TURNS, so you have only, let's say, 2 chances to save your friend from being terribly murdered.
