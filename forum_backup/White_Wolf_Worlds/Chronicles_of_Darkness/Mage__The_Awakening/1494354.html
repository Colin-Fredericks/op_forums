    The Expeditious Explorers, an Obrimos Legacy of speedsters

    10-04-2022, 06:32 PM
    In short: A Legacy of speedsters that focuses on using extreme speed to meet the challenges of being an adventurer archeologist. The oblations and yantras reward organizing expeditions to seek out new and unexplored places in the Tapestry. The Attainments allow them to outrun vehicles, dodge attacks in bullet time, even phase through obstacles and more.

    Greetings everyone, this is a Legacy inspired by Indiana Jones, the Matrix and the Flash, specializing in maximizing speed. I also added design notes for those interested. Feedback is very welcome.

    Expeditious Explorers

    Origins



    “Adventure is a path. Real adventure – self-determined, self-motivated, often risky – forces you to have firsthand encounters with the world. The world the way it is, not the way you imagine it. Your body will collide with the earth and you will bear witness. In this way you will be compelled to grapple with the limitless kindness and bottomless cruelty of humankind – and perhaps realize that you yourself are capable of both. This will change you. Nothing will ever again be black-and-white” - Mark Jenkins, author, climber, explorer and speaker

    Nicknames: Indies, rovers, Lindenbrocks, voyagers, quicklings, celeritous, looters (derogatory)
    Parentage: Obrimos, Mysterium, Adamantine Arrow

    Background:

    The Indies trace their history to the archeological boom of the late 18th century, with discoveries such as the ruins of Troy reinvigorating the archeomancers of the Mysterium with a plethora of new members. With cabals uncovering new Atlantean ruins and launching multiple expeditions, the Athenaeum swelled with unearthed secrets.

    These journeys were not without risks, with many succumbing to the perils of these lost temples. While being able to prepare and ward themselves well was vital, veterans recognized that eventually survival would come down to how quickly one could react to an unexpected situation.

    Working together with the Banner-Wardens of their cabals, the archeomancers codified a doctrine of superlative speed. Initially finding great success in preserving the lives of researchers, it was later taken up by Reclaimants as well, using it to stage quicksilver heists for magical resources.

    Adherents were plentiful within the ranks of the Arrow, its ability to respond quickly to threads and added mobility finding a plethora of tactical applications. Some would challenge themselves to push its speed to new heights, aiming to become the fastest beings alive.

    Appearances:

    The voyagers often recruit those with a profound sense of wanderlust, who seek to expand their horizons, travel to lands undreamt of and come back with a riveting tale to share. Because of this, members often tend to have hobbies like parkour, spelunking or mountaineering, which allow them to pursue new experiences while maintaining the physical fitness their magic requires.

    When departing on expeditions, they tend to favor a mix of style and practicality. High-crowned, wide brimmed fedora hats are a known favorite. Work boots and A-2 bomber leather jackets combined with kakhi trousers and a sturdy leather satchel are all considered classic looks. Aviator goggles are favored by experienced members, making it more comfortable to see when moving at high speeds. Those that prefer an aviator look complement them with scarfs.


    Doctrine



    "You cannot discover new oceans unless you have the courage to lose sight of the shore.” -André Guide

    Prerequisite: Forces 2, Athletics 2, Fleet of Foot ●, Danger Sense
    Initiation: The mentor creates a challenge course for the aspirant, usually a fake ruin, escape room or simulated heist. Prospective members are to locate a given item hidden somewhere within and escape before being trapped. Some designs emphasize tests of problem solving skills and the ability to improvise against unexpected issues, others prefer to create tests that require a keen attention to detail to perceive problems and the judicious application of speed to stop a domino effect from making things worse. It's common for members to work together to create this, exchanging ideas and stories of especially amusing ones.

    The focus on tailored trials makes physical Daimonomika less useful. Because of this, self taught members usually seek out Astral Daimonomika, normally hiding as ancient temples, decrepit mansions or ruins of castles that shift to provide suitable challenges.

    Organization: Lindenbrocks can be found in archeomancer expeditions, their abilities helping to expedite the preparation stages and staying ahead of the competition. When traversing a ruin, they scout ahead and use their Attainments to ensure their companions can duck, weave and sidestep any unexpected dangers at a moment’s notice. Veteran members are able to drive efficiency up, quickly analyzing the finds and compiling the research in moments.

    Those that specialize in stealing magical materials are able to plant surveillance devices and trackers faster than the eye can see. When the time comes for the heist, they surge past security like a bolt from the blue, moving through heavily fortified vaults and security lasers with the fluidity of a zephyr.

    Combat specialists are able to dodge bullets and engage multiple opponents with ease. Some utilize visual obstructions when blinking across the battlefield, taking out snipers in moments and bypassing fortifications completely, creating a sense of paranoia on their opponents. When upholding the Veil is not possible, some have been known to achieve feats like outrunning jet engines, dismantling the weapons wielded by opponents in moments or stealing strategic resources amidst the chaos of battle.

    Masters tend to invite their pupils on missions, mentoring their students as they gain practical experience. Indies are known to create local adventurer’s clubs, when they regale their peers with tales of stunning vistas and recruit those interested in sharing in an exciting journey.

    Theory: An endless horizon stretches before us, waiting to be explored by the daring, amidst remote corners of the Tapestry. Countless secrets lie buried by the sands of time, ready to be unearthed. Discovering them requires an inquisitive mind, well prepared, acting efficiently to stay one step ahead of their rivals.

    Eventually even the most seasoned explorer will be caught off guard by a boulder trap, pitfall or some other clever trick they are not warded against. In these moments the difference between success and failure is how quickly you react, acting decisively while making things up as you go. We gain the edge needed to survive with unparalleled speed and come back to tell the tale.

    Magic

    “Speed, agility and responsiveness are the keys to future success.”
    - Anita Roddick

    Ruling Arcanum: Time
    Yantras: adventurer's gear (+1), maps, inscriptions, journals or ancient texts related to the spell (+1, +2 for the original item), running equipment (+1), high speed vehicles (+1, +2 for rare ones such as customized motorcycles, jets planes or jetpacks), temples, ruins or other sites of ancient civilizations (+1, +2 if discovered by the mage), personal journal with notes (+1, +2 if related to the spell), library or similar repository of knowledge (+1, +2 if it specializes in the topic of the spell), survival equipment (+1).

    Oblations: Participating in a marathon or other speed focused athletic competitions, researching ancient cultures, compiling a dossier of an expedition, updating one's personal expedition journal, solving or creating an escape room, retelling the tale of an exciting adventure, giving a lecture on one’s research, making a daring escape, practicing a skill or hobby that requires eye-hand coordination, spelunking, mountaineering, deep diving or similar hobbies centered on exploration.

    Attainments

    First: Time to Spare
    Prerequisites: Initiation



    The first step to moving quickly is to make sure that one faces less obstacles. After a scene reviewing a list of their tasks or plans for the day, the explorer gains the effect of Green Light / Red Light. Things align to expedite their goals, they find places to procure resources faster, their planes arrive just when they do and depart faster, they stay a few steps ahead of the competition or find a doctor more quickly during an emergency.

    Reach is assigned to advanced duration.

    Optional: Forces 1
    Ancient ruins contain all sorts of clever traps and mechanisms that make the faintest of sounds, enemy holdout weapons make noise in the pockets of carriers, an ambusher’s gun clicks before firing. Being able to pick up on these minute details can make the difference between reacting in time and being caught unawares. After a scene meticulously surveying their surroundings, the rover becomes attuned to even the smallest sounds of danger.

    This replicates the spell Receiver. Reach is assigned to advanced duration.

    Second: Seize the Moment
    Prerequisites: Time 2, Athletics 3
    Sometimes the key to survival lies in one precise burst of speed. Switching the idol from the weight switch in one fluid motion, making a great jump or landing one good punch. After spending a moment bringing all their attention to the present, the celeritous becomes able to make their next move in an accurate blur of motion.

    This replicates Perfect Timing with Reach assigned to instant use and changing the primary factor to Potency.

    Optional: Forces 2



    Whether dodging falling rocks, scaling a mountain or moving through a labyrinth, being able to run faster, climb higher and swim longer allows one to adapt to obstacles easier. After a scene of doing kinesthetic exercises, the voyager becomes able to replicate Kinetic Efficiency.

    Reach is assigned to advanced duration and changing the primary factor to Potency.

    Third: Quicker than the Eye
    Prerequisites: Time 3, Athletics 4, Fleet of Foot ●●
    Achieving superlative speed requires more than just moving fast, it also needs finely honed reflexes to take the greatest advantage of it. After spending a scene running, going ever faster as they increase their speed in the temporal axis, the Indie spends a point of mana to break through previous limits.

    This replicates Acceleration, with Reach assigned to advanced duration and a point of mana being used to activate the spell.

    Optional: Forces 3
    Fine tuning one’s temporal motion is only the first step in realizing truly unassailable speed. When activating the main Attainment, the Indie now optimizes every physical and temporal aspect of their movement. Rather than increase for every level of [Time], their speed is doubled for each level of [Forces]. In addition, this combination of finely honed reaction speed and prodigious mobility grants them an increase of Defense equal to the lowest of [Time] and [Forces].

    This is a Perfecting of Time and Forces building on the elements of Velocity Control and Acceleration.

    Fourth: Dodging the Bullet
    Prerequisites: Time 4, Athletics 5
    When exploring the unknown, eventually one will run into a situation where facing multiple opponents is necessary, in these cases it is pivotal to decrease the time needed to process the situation and adapt to it. Taking a moment to sharpen their awareness and spending a mana, the voyager becomes able to take stock of the situation so quickly that their defense no longer decreases against multiple opponents and they calculate their defense using the highest between Dexterity and Wits. Lastly, dodge actions and Athletic rolls gain the rote quality.

    This is a Perfecting of Time with Reach assigned to instant use, advanced duration and rote action movement. Mana is spent on rote action and activating Time in a Bottle, truncated to one point per Legacy rules.

    Optional: Forces 4
    Despite moving at top speed, certain obstacles can still pose significant dangers. Cave ins, avalanches, giant boulders filtering the unwary towards an array of traps and more. The rovers have learned to move past them by blinking from one point to another in moments, which they use to great effect when scouting for perils.

    All they require is a moment of focus and spend a point of mana, afterwards they may disappear from the flow of time when moving from one point to another. They remain aware of their surroundings during this time and must phase in to interact with anything, but are otherwise able to move through solid objects. Should anything occupy the space they reappear in, the subject with the smallest size is Knocked Down.

    This is a custom spell mixing elements of Temporal Stutter with a Patterning of Forces. Reach is assigned to advanced duration and instant use, with a point of mana used to activate Time in a Bottle.

    Fifth: Leaps and Bounds
    Prerequisites: Time 5, Fleet of Foot ●●●
    Having ample time is a luxury that is not always available. Sometimes inscriptions must be translated before a curse consumes someone, a room fortified to stop an incoming assault from a temple guardian or a complex device dismantled while racing against the clock. When the situation calls for it, the greatest Indies are able to concentrate their speed to such an extent that they can accomplish in moments what would have taken weeks.

    This replicates Blink of an Eye. Reach is assigned to instant use.

    Optional: Forces 5
    Sure footing is not always a guarantee in expeditions. Sometimes progress may be halted by a giant chasm, the ground may give way to a pitfall or gravity could warp in unexpected directions. Leveraging their deep understanding of the secrets of motion, the Lindenbrocks have learned to move freely across all axes of the terrain.

    After spending a point of mana, they are able to replicate the effects of Levitation. Reach is assigned to instant use, moving freely and advanced duration. A point of mana is used to activate Time in a Bottle.

    What were you thinking design wise ?

    The first Attainment is pure optimization, you get things done quicker if you get convenient breaks, which is good in everyday life and very helpful when studying Mysteries, especially in emergencies. The optional Attainment is great for detecting ambushes and traps, always a concern on field work.

    The second Attainment lets them take a sec to swap the idol without triggering a trap mechanism, pause a moment to come up with a good excuse, focus to deliver one good punch, take a running position to make that one difficult jump or quietly steal the McGuffin in one quick move. It sacrifices the Indies’ defense during the turn of preparation, which is a risk, but the potential gains might outweigh it. The optional Attainment lets them jump farther, run faster, climb higher, lift more and be more enduring. It is the first optimization on their quest for speed.

    The third Attainment is their main claim to fastest beings in the world and was the starting design point for the speedster toolkit. Acceleration increases reflexes and reactiveness, whereas Velocity Control is purely dedicated to travel distance. A custom spell that optimizes both should be able to leverage the best elements of both while allowing the user to use both aspects to the fullest to increase defense. A master could use transient stacking to easily boost their speed far beyond that of even an air superiority fighter jet, even without dashing. The activation process is a homage to the World Record short from the Animatrix, with these speedsters running so fast as to break one of the constraints of the soul cage.

    The original draft of this Legacy had the third Attainment replicate the Sleight of Hand merit, but with some additional benefits, allowing the speedsters to be able to disarm traps and swipe important items quickly, with the speed boost being the optional. After some feedback, it was pointed out that since the optional Attainment can be pretty powerful, it might be a better idea to build the speed boost using both Attainment slots. The main Attainment didn’t seem too absurd, but I preferred to play it safe and made a new version. I will include the original version here, should anyone prefer using it in their games.

    Third: Quicker than the Eye
    Prerequisites: Time 3, Athletics 4, Fleet of Foot ●●
    Ancient structures conceal many clever devices. Trapped chests, trick locks, secret passages and many more. Having the tools and know-how to deal with them reliably and quickly, especially under pressure, can go a long way to ensuring the safety of the crew.

    After a scene maintaining or tinkering with their lockpicking kit, the quickling gains the ability to perform a Larceny instant action reflexively once per turn and their moves are so fast that they cannot be detected by mundane means, with any supernatural attempts provoking a Clash of Wills. In addition, their heightened hand-eye coordination grants them 8-again on all Larceny rolls. Should they also have the Sleight of Hand merit, the rolls instead gain rote quality.

    This is a duration primary Perfecting of Time, with Reach assigned to advanced duration.

    Optional: Forces 3
    Achieving superlative speed requires more than just moving fast, it also needs finely honed reflexes to take the greatest advantage of it. After spending a scene running, going ever faster as they optimize every aspect of their physical and temporal aspects, the Indie spends a point of mana to break through previous limits. Their speed is doubled for each level of [Forces], they are able to apply Defense against firearms and they gain Defense equal to the lowest of [Time] and [Forces]. They always go first in a turn unless they choose to delay their action, in which case they may interrupt any other character’s turn with their own as a reflexive action, then return to the front of the Initiative queue the next turn. Other characters using pre-empting powers provoke a Clash of Wills.

    This is a Perfecting of Time and Forces building on the elements of Velocity Control and Acceleration. Reach is assigned to advanced duration and a point of mana is used to activate the spell.

    Design notes:The third Attainment is the classic speedster ability to steal items without anyone noticing. It has many other applications, such as placing trackers, covertly delivering a note, stealing an opponent’s weapon or magic item mid combat, etc. While it's very useful for disarming traps, it also starts to blur the line between the toolkit of an adventurer archeologist and a thief.

    The optional Attainment is their main claim to fastest beings in the world and was the starting design point for the speedster toolkit. Acceleration increases reflexes and reactiveness, whereas Velocity Control is purely dedicated to travel distance. A custom spell that optimizes both should be able to leverage the best elements of both while allowing the user to use both aspects to the fullest to increase defense. A master could use transient stacking to easily boost their speed far beyond that of even an air superiority fighter jet, even without dashing. The activation process is a homage to the World Record short from the Animatrix, with these speedsters running so fast as to break one of the constraints of the soul cage.


    The fourth main Attainment is a Matrix style bullet dodge. It recognizes that combat is an eventuality of the job and being ready to deal with it is another important aspect. A speedster should be hard to hit, so this synergizes well with the third optional Attainment.

    The optional Attainment allows them to run through walls, jump down ceilings, pass through minefields, dash through laser defenses, scout stealthily and more. They still need to be tangible to do anything, but it adds a nice bit of utility and builds on the theme of the speedster as impossible to confine.

    The capstone Attainment allows them to transcribe ancient paintings on a cave wall while escaping a collapsing temple, disarm a deadly machine while racing against the clock, setup or dismantle a research station in moments, complete a report on an expedition in seconds, research through an entire library in almost no time at all or even disassemble the guns of the attackers in their hands before they even acknowledge what happened.

    The optional capstone gives them absolute freedom of movement, letting them explore even more than before. They can avoid pits full of snakes, move through Escher mazes untroubled, reach the peak of the highest mountain, jump out of airplanes with no fear or explore cities in the sky. It synergizes with the other Attainments to give them even more entry points into an area and further expands their skill at scouting.

    The original concept for the Legacy was conceived by imagining what would be helpful for an archeomancer. Since the archetypical example of the adventurer archeologist is Indiana Jones, I started to consider what he would create. Given how many deadly situations he faces, he would probably use multiple Shielding spells and other safeguards. Failing that, he would default to his skill at improvising. But that would not work if he wasn’t able to survive long enough to get to the improvisation. The solution would be to become so fast that he could seize those opportunities.

    Their approach is that of pure practicality, everything needs to lead to more speed. Their Attainment toolkit was built by looking at the things experienced archeomancers thought were useful for securing success for expeditions, ways to deal with emergencies and proactively create opportunities. They were made without any concern for the Veil because it's normally not an issue when delving into supernatural ruins.

    Since the Attainments have a lot of general utility, some used them for unintended applications, which would draw more initiates. An Arrow could have learned it as a way to keep the expedition team alive, but found it lent itself well to a lightning bruiser fighting style, allowing them to deal with multiple enemies, smartly using the terrain to break line of sight. With transient stacking, they can dash in and out of range of both enemy aimed spells and sensory range, making them very good at hit and run tactics. They can use the speed to infiltrate complexes, use the fourth optional Attainment for Batman entrances/exits or for psychological effect by blinking in wherever their targets run to. Even during a car chase, they can simply move over to the other vehicle, steal the key and return to their seat.

    Some students could have moved from the archeomancers to the reclaimers. They could easily repurpose their skills for thieving purposes, planting bugs on unsuspecting targets or scouting out the site of a future theft. When time comes for a heist, they easily navigate past the security systems, disabling them for their cabalmates and using their keen senses to listen for trouble. If the alarm is raised, they ensure the team has the speed to complete the objective quickly. If they need to evacuate, they guarantee everyone has the speed and agility to evade security.

    Their appearance is not only based on the classic Indy look, but also tries to evoke the theme of the adventurer explorer of Jules Verne's books, which is why the nickname Lindenbrock references the main character from Journey to the Center of the Earth. The yantras and oblations are designed to support their adventures into unknown areas of the world, with the adventurer's clubs being a good source of plot hooks. They also include activities needed to maintain their conditioning for high speed.

    The initiation helps prepare them for field work. The objective is not to do everything as fast as possible. Rather it's about balancing proactiveness, being inquisitive and remaining careful. The trial itself could be fun for the players to design or face. It could also make the Legacy into one of the rewards for navigating through a temple in the Temenos.

    Speedsters often have a dedicated costume, which normally doesn't follow the aesthetic of the adventurer archeologist. The style itself is surprisingly uncommon, much less in combination with speed powers. So instead I chose to use a dynamic shot by Ruskull for the opening image, a temple labyrinth by Serg Souleiman and some watercolor art by Robert Watts, which recreate the feel of an old movie poster.

    That concludes the design notes, hope you had as much fun reading it as I had writing it. Thanks in advance for the feedback.
