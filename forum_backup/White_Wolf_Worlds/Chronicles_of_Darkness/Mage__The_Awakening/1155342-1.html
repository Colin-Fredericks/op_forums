                        I similarly say the dice bonus can't be applied to spellcasting with regards to boons, and just let them use the guide in the hex/boon writeup.

                        But yeah, I personally agree that Fate can be pretty overpowered even among other mages; a large majority of its effects break many of the CT rules for what level is generally able to do what effects (creating tilts from nothing, for instance), and the Charmed condition is in and of itself far too potent, especially when you can do a large amount of other things in the same casting.

                        One way to help might be to split the Boons/Hexes into separate effects (meaning a different rote for different types of boons for a given spell effect), or removing the condition/tilt options altogether and reintroducing it later as a more advanced effect.

                                            Right, so regarding the point of exceptional luck not infinite, it still is super potent In buffing spellcasting, and stopping it otherwise requires some house-ruling to limit it from being otherwise exceptionally potent. And it would do so in a fashion that does seem to be a particularly strident case per RAW and ambiguous RAI.

                                            That is still good to know, that there is an upper limit as written, and that one could reasonably house rule a solution with minimal effort and alteration.


                                            I do find moderate annoyance in Fate's ability to effectively request to skip most of the interesting bits of any mystery or investigation though. Need to find something or someone, get somewhere like the shadow, or convince someone to help you? Well, the Fate mage can do all that with unprecedented ease, combined with the benefit of not needing to affect anyone but himself with magic, sparring them the issue of a werewolf taking issue to their mind influence, or a geist not liking being harassed and spied on by their pet ghost. This is mostly just an aspect of nothing mundane really being able to slow a Fate mage down at all. Lack of evidence? Luck past it. Stubborn rival? Magically boost your dice pool through the roof and talk past them. Fight? You just happened to spill coffee on your shirt earlier and avoided it, and even if they press the issue, you've got an attack pool through the roof, great defence, and can just use shared fates to make punishing you an awful idea. The one arcana seems an acceptable solution to all problems.

                                                        Originally posted by Octavo View Post
                                                        Player Character casts "Exceptional Luck" to gain "informed", and resolves it to get the answer.
                                                        Said it before and I'll say it again:

                                                        That's not how Informed works.

                                                        "Who killed Mister Stone?" is not going to be a roll-based question. You get a lucky lead on your Investigation roll and find an especially valuable piece of evidence (or find a single remaining scrap of evidence amid a veritable desert of clues) that points you in the right direction, just as you would a mundanely lucky roll.

                                                        Originally posted by PixelPuzzler View Post
                                                        I do find moderate annoyance in Fate's ability to effectively request to skip most of the interesting bits of any mystery or investigation though. […]. The one arcana seems an acceptable solution to all problems.
                                                        Relying exclusively on Fate all but assures that you're going into most situations blind and drawing potential complications into your metaphysical orbit to get out of the trouble that doing so got you into. Keep track of what happens to let the character circumvent given obstacles and extrapolate bigger issues where they can fit. Fate isn't going to immediately help with extradimensional threats, counterspells, the Long-Term Nimbus, spell control, Paradox, or plain lack of foresight. The ST's job is to make the PCs' lives complicated and Fate is a blank checkbook to bring in weird shit on the bridges.
