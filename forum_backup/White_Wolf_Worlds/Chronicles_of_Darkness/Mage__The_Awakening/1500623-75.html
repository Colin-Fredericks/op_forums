                    To try to put it succinctly: Combined spells are not a mechanic for multicasting spells simultaneously. They're a mechanic for when you want to cast one spell whose effects incorporate multiple individual spells. You could cast a spell that, for example, crafts a shadow into a physical form (Shadow Crafting, Death 3) and crafts that physical form into pure silver (Transubstantiation, Matter 4). It's one spell, one thing you're doing to a shadow, but the one thing you're doing is multiple spell effects. You wouldn't use a single combined spell to craft one shadow into silver and another into gold. That would be two castings.

                            I am not going to waste my time trying to rehash in detail the premise that if you come to a thread for simple questions you have about a point of ambiguity in a set of books that does not explicitly resolve that ambiguity you do not get to complain about the lack of explicit textual support for any interpretations used in the answers you get. If you automatically disagree with the answers you get in that circumstance there was little point in asking the question. "Given the general tenor of the rest of the game and the basic definition of what the thing you're asking about is" is the closest to written assumptions we have.

                            Spells that use Area Scale to apply non-Lasting effects apply those effects to any valid targets in the area as long as they remain in the area. Spells that use Subjects Scale can have their separate targets move wherever they like from the Range they were cast at and are Withstood by their separate targets as usual, with the number of affected targets being permitted to be less than the maximum allowed by its Scale factor; you can dispel a spell on one subject without disrupting it on the others, but you can't increase the number of subjects a non-Area spell is affecting without re-casting the spell because those subjects weren't part of the original casting. Increasing a spell's factors is done at the time of casting by accepting penalties and/or through the free steps available to the primary factor, and spell factors that are used more than once use the largest applicable one to determine what is necessary for the effect to work as intended. Dissonance lowers spell factors by steps on the chart and Paradox both only affects them by toggling Reach effects and only applies at the point of casting, while the Hung Spell effect can only delay when a spell starts to take effect, not stretch out its effective Duration. Spells cast with the Practice of Dynamics can be viral or self-repairing and the nature of their casters means they automatically win any Clash of Wills with sub-transcendent beings, but a general question about how Travelers use combined spells cannot be answered with mechanics specific to Seekers. Combined spells cannot incorporate the benefits of Rotes and require all component spells be Praxes to get the benefit of casting them as a Praxis, meaning the largest possible common Yantra type for the most codified type of spell can't be used in a combined spell and having practiced one spell so much that casting it is second nature to you doesn't extend those benefits to combined casting with other spells you haven't internalized to that degree.

                            Set aside the idea that a combined spell is multiple spells cast in a single action: if you saw a magician cast a spell in a piece of fiction where magic spells are concretely defined in what they do, chances are good that a Spell That Does More Than One Thing acts on the same targets, creates a shared area of effect, or involves a degree of oversight/micromanagement we would recognize as casting or re-casting separate instances of spells.Similarly, if the narrative device of a spell has symbolic grounding, it's grounding relevant to the whole effect, because while burying someone's personal effects might be symbolically resonant with hiding them from sight it's not going to do anything to improve the odds of making them fly, which has different semiotics altogether even if both are part of carrying out a larger scheme.

                                                        Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                                        Yes, two AoE effects will affect all valid subjects, and thus since you are combining two different effects they can have different subjects.

                                                        If you cast a Death/Life spell, adding dexterity to the living and making the valid deathly objects (castoffs, ghosts, etc) manifest. Then I can't see anything saying this isn't a perfectly valid area of effect.

                                                        But does it need to be an AoE spell to be cast? Can you just target one ghost and one human ?
                                                        Again: a shared area of effect on a single combined spell has the areas of effect on its effect overlap, and the number of affected targets within that area is arbitrarily large because the effect only affects targets while they remain in the area. That doesn't work for Subjects, because a combined spell shares its spell factors across all its effects — if you target one human and one ghost you are making the spell target two subjects, and those subjects will remain under the spell's effects until it ends regardless of where they go afterward.

                                                        A combined spell shares its targeting just like it shares Potency and Duration. Unless you spend a Reach to split the effects, every effect applies to valid targets in the whole Area or every nominated Subject. Just because a spell effect doesn't do anything to a given target doesn't mean they don't have a spell on them; an Area effect that reanimates corpses and binds ghosts is "touching" everything in the assigned Scale and applying the relevant effects to any applicable targets, but the equivalent of being in the spell's Area for Subjects Scale is "at the time of casting, is the spell being targeted at these subjects, the upper limit of which is determined by the Scale factor?"

                                                        You can target a smaller area or number of subjects than the limit set by a spell's Scale factor with that effect, so you could, say, link the fates of everyone in an apartment building and make everyone on a specific floor look like the same person, give a classroom full of people heightened Intelligence and only give the teacher extra dots in Expression, or make a house more valuable and give its owner increased Presence, but all of those would necessitate a Reach to split the effects in that way and none of them are completely unconnected in concept.

                                                        One more time: the purpose of combined spells is to let a single spell have multiple discrete effects. To draw a comparison to Changeling, spell effects are Goblin Contracts — conceptually simple and highly focused on doing one thing — whereas combined spells fold multiple effects into one like some Arcadian and Court Contracts. The spell is still one spell, conceptually speaking, and that means anything it does or uses to work has to work with the whole framework of a unified spell. If you can come up with a solid symbolic reason for an area to both manifest ghosts and make living people more coordinated, you can use any symbolism that supports that combined effect as an appropriate type of Yantra, but the Scale still sets the outer bounds for how big an area or how many free-roaming subjects it can affect overall.
