      For new mages, especially Silver Ladder, I typically have PCs have to run errands like deliver or retrieving things, filling out paperwork, etc, on behalf of their mentor or whoever's responsible for them. Something could go wrong in the midst of that, if you want conflict, or you could have most things go well and just keep it a training type thing. I like to show the tediousness and bureaucracy.

                      I would have novice mages investigate things. The paperwork, message running and other council business is just a front to get the players into the real story. Put something interesting or dangerous in their path and see how they deal with it. You can get a lot of mileage out of various one dot investigation spells when you have two dots in an arcana, and mage sight is all about investigating mysteries. Alternatively a PC could run into a mundane crime, getting caught up in that but find that something supernatural caused it. A spirit possessing someone to steal something for example. Combat should be against groups of street thugs or one or two higher point types. Investigations should be against the minions of enemies that will show up later.

                              Instead of "dies", I would go with "isn't answering phone calls, that lazy bastard", which could lead into something bigger (especially if you can isolate them from means of getting help, like by transporting them into a twisted dimension or something).
                              It would be good first to create expectations of nothing-weird happening by making it looking like another quest in a chain of filler quests before "something happens" - a good technique for this is to create expectations, for example
                              "Finish 4 tasks for me, and I will take you on a real mission" - players are going to either expect that 1st, 2nd, or 4th mission will have complications, because they will think that real deal will start after end of the tutorial. So you subvert it by doing it at 3rd task.

                                  Originally posted by Silver Ladder, p.102
                                  Promising young willworkers have been watched carefully and tested thoroughly, and the mentor or foster cabal almost always provides an extensive portfolio of the potential member’s achievements. If the novice meets the approval of the caucus, the mentor or foster cabal returns to her with an offer of membership, coupled with a task of initiation. The task is rarely terribly difficult, is usually of a social or political nature (though often a mixture of the two), and provides the initiate an opportunity to put her understanding of the Lesser Elemental Precepts into action. As the recruit fulfills the task of initiation, her mentor guides her to a basic understanding of the Greater Elemental Precepts.

                                  Initiation tasks are usually goal-oriented. A typical task may be securing a specific position in a specific company. The task may be given in a straightforward manner (“We need you to become the head of IT at Enrond Electronics”), though it is more often laid out in a more circuitous manner (“We need you to gain long-term influence over the way internal systems are analyzed and repaired at Enrond Elec-tronics”). A recruit who finds a creative method of completing the task is often granted a great deal of respect, relative to her experience, from the moment she undergoes the induction rite (see below). Use of magic during the task, however, is strongly frowned upon, and an obvious reliance upon magic to complete the task is grounds for reconsideration of membership. Théarchs are expected to be able to stand on their own two feet when the use of magic isn’t an option.

                                      The Illuminated Pentad's interests are a good starting point. Khumeia has her bankers, socialites and the IRS, Thanatos has his ghosts and Faustina has her construction companies. Since they have run their operations for quite some time the PCs aren't needed for the daily upkeep. However, if anything unusual crops up, they can be sent to investigate:

                                      - Khumeia's IRS ally has been suffering from missing time and their work performance has gone down as a result.
                                      - Thanatos's Ghosts are reporting poltergeist-like activity but can't pinpoint anyone responsible.
                                      - One of Faustina's geomantic projects is experiencing delays and Fate magic has been detected.

                                      Then, make one of the other Cabals responsible. This needn't be malicious. When confronted they have a good explanation, or give an apology and fix the problem. The man from the IRS saw something they shouldn't and had their memory edited. A Spirit controlled by someone else got trapped by their Ban/Bane and is acting out. As an unintended consequence, a Blessing cast on a Sleeper had them benefit from the construction delays.

                                                      How populous is your Mage setting? Are there many Mages running around, both novice and experienced, or are they relatively rare, especially compared to Sleepwalker and brainwashed cultist retainers? It's important to take a stance on this, because novices and newbies will be given different tasks if there are, like, 1000 of them running around, and if newcomer Mages are relatively rare and you get maybe 1-2 every few years, they will be given much more care and probably won't be wasted on paper pushing stuff.

                                                      Another thing is that for novices, I would steer away from plots and tasks involving other Mages. Mage is pretty complicated, and interacting with other Mages is even more complicated; a good initial playground is one where Mages interact with non-Mages and problems that aren't necessarily generated by other Mages.
                                                      Mostly because, as you noted, other Mages can and probably will just solve the issue with a spell.

                                                      Checking up on ongoing projects that are being overwatched by Sleepwalker retainers is a good starting point; it doesn't involve other Mages directly (so they can't solve problems for PCs), and still explores and involves the Mage society.
                                                      And of course, there are probably some Mysteries that mentors of your PCs don't have time to take care of, because TIME IS MONEY and they are too busy with something closer to their personal Obsession....but they still called dibs on in order to use them as tokens of political power later. Or as food for newbies.

                                                      Actually, you might send them to investigate some minor Mystery that was "left for later" and figure out that someone is actually trying to solve it under the radar, thinking that no one will notice. Then they can see what kind of traces a sloppy mage lefts behind and track it back to the offender. It's a relatively fun way to explore Magic Sight, Unveiling and Knowing Spells.

                                                          maybe she wants a translated copy so that she doesn't have to cast a spell every time she wants to check something.
                                                          Or
                                                          She sends them get a grimoire she has recently negotiated a price for (they would be carrying whatever she has promised in exchange with them to make the trade).
                                                          Or
                                                          She sends them with a letter of request to another Mage/Cabal. Someone who would think it impolite to be approached by phone or e-mail, or someone who needs a little incentive to agree to the request (so the PCs will be carrying a bri... Incentive)

                                                          In the case of them carrying something valuable either way, complications can arise from that, especially if it's alive (which could turn into the cryptic hunt suggested above) or if it has very particular rules as to how it should be handled.


                                                          Also remember that Mages aren't above giving tasks that seem to be about one thing but that is really about something else. A task stated to be about collecting signatures or securing votes in the consilium could really be about having the PCs become accquainted with the different cabals in the area (perhaps with the idea of questioning them afterwards to see what their impressions are/what those cabals seem to be up to).
