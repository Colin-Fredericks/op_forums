    Age Alteration

    05-26-2024, 02:41 AM
    Does anyone know how the mechanics for magic that alters age are supposed to work? I know that in 2e it requires Life 3 and Time 3, but I was not able to find details on how such magic actually functioned. Based on the text, it seems like the idea is that the mage decides on a specific age beforehand, and a success indicates that the alteration is successfully conducted. Also, the text does not specify if the physical attributes of the target are affected by the change in age. I am guessing that additional magic is required to adjust the physical attributes of the target, so presumably an individual who was rendered an old man by the spell would not actually suffer a reduction in physical capabilities. Does anyone know how magic of this sort is supposed to work?

    I am also interested in what people think about the Arcana that are used for this kind of magic. I remember that in 1e, age alteration required Life 4. In a way, age alteration magic is both more easy and more difficult to access in 2e.

      Depends on what you mean by age alteration. In 2e Time can do it on its own with Temporal Summoning (weaving) but this isn't lasting.

      Life is also able to do it, with Accelerate Growth as an example, Transform Life might also be used creatively to give some deaging process which several animals appear to have, but I think taking an elderly person to the health of a 25 year old would be perfecting...

      You could also compare with Regeneration... I am pretty sure none of these are Lasting, as per the section on Liches.

      Death may also be an option, fraying the Decay that causes aging or the reverse, but it seems more blunt a tool.

          I would note that the Many Faces spell already contains parameters for rearranging the target's Physical Attribute dots in a manner consistent with the new body that the subject is given. I'm going to suppose that it can't actually increase them, regardless of the apparent physical qualities of the new body, because it's outside of the spell's Practice.

          So you probably can end up with a situation of turning an elderly person into a younger one, and while they may well have a number of ancillary benefits (if they had arthritis, that clears up), it doesn't quite restore their original strength without it being at the expense of something else.

          If one wants to make it thorough, it's probably a good idea to make it a combined spell with Honing the Form.

          The Arcana thing is simply a function of how the Edition is making the Practices a lot more consistent as the basis for how spells work, without regard for the supposed relevance of the end result. Outright mind control up to the level of making a person do something suicidal is 2 dots because it's a Ruling effect. Turning fungi into insects is 4 dots because regardless of how insignificant one thinks either of those life forms are, it's a transformation to the most thorough extent, and that means Patterning.

          I would also note that Many Faces probably isn't a great choice to revert one's life span continuously, because even if the spell is Indefinite, you're eventually going to age back up again, and then your only way to wind it back again is to cast the same spell with a higher Potency to overwrite the currently active one. You're eventually going to hit a point where the Potency gets higher than you might effectively be able to exceed with the next spell. And you can't just drop the spell to start off with a fresh one at low Potency, because then you'll turn back into your real age.

                      I would say reverting age back is Life Perfecting or Death Fraying. Time Weaving could probably rewind personal clock back (as per Temporal Summoning). In case you want to live (almost) forever, your best option would be getting a Life or Death Shielding spell stopping aging, like Veil of Moments, but without it's downsides, and then casting it with Indefinite Duration while safely relinquishing it. In case it is dispelled, just cast it again. This would prolong your lifespan long enough that instead of dying to an old age, dying to Mysteries would be your main concern. As for reverting age back permanently - I would allow it on Regeneration with +2 Reach option. Yeah, I know that corebook Regeneration does not have that, but I see it as cheap drama forcing you to look for ways around it, such as Weaving regeneration abilities from lizards or hydras and then waiting for them to grow your parts of the body back. Ultimately, as always, Storyteller decides what is possible and what isn't on his table.

                              Originally posted by Verlan View Post
                              I usually think of physical damage when thinking about Regeneration, so I had not considered how it might affect the aging process.
                              I don't really think it works, when the spell is specifically described in terms of restoring tissues and bodily functions that are lost or originally absent. I can see Regeneration fixing a trait or feature that one has lost to aging, but the end result will be a healthier example of a person at the same age rather than reversing aging altogether.

                              This is all in recognition of the fact that aging as a biological mechanism is actually kind of weird and still not well understood. Despite a lot of study, biologists still don't really have a great grasp of exactly why animal bodies change over time as they do, and ultimately break down.

                              This is not to say that Awakened magic shouldn't be able to affect the matter due to lack of scientific understanding, just that conceiving of aging as a form of damage that can be reversed in the same way as a severed limb is a bit crude.

                              Originally posted by Verlan
                              I like the idea of applying the regenerative capabilities of certain animals to oneself through the use of magic, as you and another in the thread have suggested.
                              Whereas this is something that I unreservedly dislike. As far as I'm concerned, it's one thing to follow the rule that creative applications of the Arcana can arrive at conclusions other than the ones presented in the book, and quite something else that trivially renders a suggested spell, and all of its dramatic conceits, completely obsolete.

                              A person might regard that drama as "cheap" in the first place, but I place a bit more respect in the motivations of the writers who put it there to begin with.

                              That's not to say that I'd rule granting oneself reptilian or amphibian regeneration to be off the table entirely, just that the results may be a bit different to (and maybe somewhat inferior) what could be granted with a high level of Awakened magic.
