    Originally posted by Theravis View Post
    This is the first draft of a legacy I've been toying with. First, it gives the Arrow another legacy, because really? 4 legacies, one of those Left-Handed? Second, it's a combat-focused legacy that isn't combat-exclusive like the Brotherhood of the Demon Wind or the Perfected Adepts.

    The Sworn Companions

    In Ancient Greece, the shield, not the sword or spear, was the symbol of war. The Sworn Companions understand this truth: The heart of warfare is to protect and defend, not for the purpose of destruction. These warriors are dedicated to the defense of a person, place, thing or group.

    Arcana: Space, (optional) Life

    Oblations: Standing watch over a place or person, meditation on vows, training regimins

    Names: Companion, Hetairoi, Knight, Shield, Mook (Perjorative)

    Special: Must possess the Oathbound merit. If they are not currently under an oath, all attainments cease to function.

    1st Attainment: Semper Intenti- Space 2, (Optional Life 1), Gnosis 3
    The Companion is permanently under the effects of Space 2 Scry, and Space 1 Finder towards the target of their oath. If they have life 1, they also gain the effects of Life 1 Healer's Trance toward their oaths target. However this only tells them whether the target is totally healthy or not unless the Companion takes the time to concentrate.

    2nd Attainment: Semper Paratus- Space 3, (Optional Life 2), Gnosis 5
    The Companion may open a portal to their charge as a reflexive action for the cost of 1 Mana. With Life 2, they receive an always-on Life 2 Organic Resilience. This armor stacks with any other mage armor, but not Organic Resilience.

    3rd Attainment: Semper Armati- Space 4, (Optional Life 3), Gnosis 7
    The Companion gains a Pocket Dimension, per the spell, that may only be used to store weapons and armor, including their ritual tools. They may spend an instant action and 1 mana to retrieve or stock any arms or armor to and from this pocket dimension, and if they choose to retrieve armor, they may choose whether it is summoned near them, or already worn.With Life 3, the Companion gains their Life Arcana in extra dots of physical attributes. They may redistribute these spare dots by spending one mana and an instant action. This stacks with Honing the Form.
    Cool legacy. It reminds me of the Votary of the Ordained, a guardian legacy (http://whitewolf.wikia.com/wiki/Votary_of_the_Ordained). Not the same, as their goal is to protect a person of great destiny from having it changed or demolished, but some of the abilities are similar and would be a good guide.
    Attainments

    Being a Votary of the Ordained requires that a mage be intimately familiar with the Mysteries of Fate. Space and Life or Matter are also advantageous. Most Votaries are well-muscled, large and healthy, with a strong knowledge of how to apply natural forces effectively, though some maintain the sanctity of their charges by utilizing more subtle means.


    1st Attainment: Soldier of Fortune

    Pre-Requisites: Gnosis 3, Fate 3

    The first Attainment allows the Votary of the Ordained to dedicate himself to a single charge of his choice, whether a person, place, or object, in a variation of the Fate Arcanum's oath spells. If the charge is a living being, it does not have to be aware of its new status, or even be willing to become a charge.

    Once the mage has declared his charge and spent one Mana to seal his intent, from that point on it is the Votary's sole responsibility to see that his charge meets its true fate. Generally, younger Votaries of Fate take on charges with simpler needs (guarding the asp that would bite Cleopatra from its birth until the event) while their elders see to the difficult ones (protecting a scroll for 100 years to ensure that only the proper person learns its secret). Once the Votary of the Ordained has dedicated himself to a charge, he gains the benefit of an Intimate sympathetic bond with his charge at all times.

    Once the charge's fate has been met, the bond between the Votary and his charge dissolves. If the charge has another important destiny in the future, the Votary may re-dedicate himself to the charge. Otherwise, he is expected to find a new destiny that needs his assistance.

    For each scene in the story, the Votary gains the benefits of the Fate 2 Exceptional Luck spell on dice roll per dot he has in the Fate Arcanum, although this benefit must be used to protect the charge or ensure that it meets his ordained destiny.

    Optional Arcanum: Space 1

    The Votary gains the benefit of the Space 1 Finder spell with respect to his charge, as long as he is within a number of miles equal to his Gnosis from his charge. If his charge is a location, he instead gains the effects of the Space 1 Omnivision spell as long as he is within that location.


    2nd Attainment: Doctoring Fate

    Pre-Requisites: Gnosis 5, Fate 3

    The Votary can also fool others about the nature of his charge's destiny. As long as he is in the same room as his charge or, if his charge is a location, within it, he becomes instantly aware if someone is using supernatural perception to judge the nature and strength of his charge's fate. He may reflexively dim or misdirect this person's awareness as if he had already cast the Fate 3 Occlude Destiny and Fabricate Fortune spells at a Potency equal to his rating in the Fate Arcanum.

    If the Votary has Life 2 and his charge is a person, he gains the ability to purge poisons from his charge's body, similar to the Life 2 Purify Bodies spell. Make an instant Stamina + Medicine + Life roll.

    Optional Arcanum: Space 2

    The Votary may, as an instant action, view his charge, wherever it might be. This is similar to the use of the Space 2 Scrying spell, but it requires no roll or Mana expenditure.


    3rd Attainment: Fate Defends Itself

    Pre-Requisites: Gnosis 7, Fate 4

    When someone threatens the Votary's charge, this level of Attainment in the Legacy allows the guardian to hinder that threat's every action with fate itself. The cords of destiny act to prevent the intruder's victory, causing her to stumble over nothing and catch her knife in her coat. The Votary can inflict an aggressor with a massive streak of bad luck, as if she were subject to the Fate 5 Great Curse spell. The Votary's player rolls Resolve + Intimidation + Fate as an instant action, and she must spend one Mana per use, although the effect is covert rather than vulgar. She can increase the number of targets affected by suffering dice penalties to her roll, per the rules for Target factors.

    If the roll is successful, the target(s) suffers a -1 dice penalty per dot of the Votary's Fate Arcanum; this penalty only applies to those attempts by the target(s) to affect the Votary's charge. The effect lasts for the remainder of the scene.

    Optional Arcanum: Space 3

    The Votary may choose to summon his charge to him through use of the Space 2 Apportation spell. If the charge is a location or person, he may not use Apportation at all but may instead instantly take himself to his charge through the Space 3 Portal spell. Neither of these requires any roll.

        Thanks for teaching me something about history I never knew. Also, I love that their third attainment basically gives them access to hammerspace.

        Neither of these are fleshed out the way my last Legacy was, but here are two more ideas I'd be happy to flesh out if people like them (I may do so regardless)

        The Socialites: This sarcastically-named clique of Free Council Mastigos may be the first Mages to seek Supernal correspondences in the "digital world;" specifically, by using Forces to charge computers and code with Mana, they translate their Mind magic to social media. They are in particular intrigued by the way widespread social networking has changed cultural ideas about privacy, and about identity-is a man who lies about his name, face, and age, but finds a new community who accepts him under these false pretenses just lying, or is he changing supposedly immutable truths about himself through an act of will, reinforced by the good faith of those "around" him in cyberspace? Most of the experiments of this legacy (those recorded and published openly for the rest of the Free Council to read, that is) tend toward elaborate attempts at "Catfishing" or other juvenile social experiments such as leaking a celebrity's nude pictures and then changing the way Sleeper pop culture feels about the leak through mass manipulation of the blogosphere. However, there are rumors that the Socialites have created functioning "trinary" computers that approach true artificial intelligence without the use of magic, or discovered a way to manipulate "Digital Sympathy," gaining a real connection to Sleepers or other Mages through online interactions with no need for anything as crude as a drop of blood or lock of hair. These rumors are part of the reason you won't find too many willworkers with active Twitter accounts.

        The Colony: This Left-Handed Thyrsus Legacy was born when an unknown Shaman of ages past did something unthinkable to Mages of the Diamond Orders: he called to the gods of the Primal Wild and, rather than attempting to hunt them as predator, outwit them as prey, or contract with them as equals, he bowed to them, offering his soul's sworn oath of fealty to any great Beast or Totem that had need of him, with no request for power in return. This strangely selfless ritual called to Queen Bee, whose servants work, fight, and die for her with no thought of their own personal gain. She touched his soul, and took him through the twisting combs of her hive, showing him how her colony moved its many bodies with one mind to keep harmony. The Shaman understood that highly individualized human minds were getting in the way of mankind's harmony with nature, and of the Fallen's harmony with the Supernal. He understood how he could fix this, and sought other Mages quietly until he found another Shaman seeking truths of the Primal Wild. He showed her the way, and the nameless Mage and his apprentice became a Colony of two. Mages of The Colony gain an affinity with Mind magic, despite being uniformly Thyrsus, and two or more who come together truly do act as one. Those at the highest levels of Attainment can further spread their hive-mind to animals, other Mages, or even Sleepers in their vicinity, eventually creating an entire small ecosystem that moves together governed by one mind-imagine a zoo where one of the visitors walks into the tiger cage fearlessly and is devoured without raising an arm to defend himself, for he knows that the tigers are hungry and have not eaten well. Later, the same tigers will allow other people to pick over the remains of the devoured man, and find his unscratched wallet or diamond wedding ring, because the tigers know that the humans can use these objects to obtain food to eat and good wood to make fire with.
        Mages of The Colony have the potential to become some of the most powerful Thyrsus in the world, but the price they pay for this power is freedom; Queen Bee feeds them her honey, and buzzes in their ears every night as they dream. She tells them her wishes for the Fallen World, and her wish is The Colony's command. Even if they remembered they could defy her, they would not-the "individual" identities of their souls have been stripped away.

                                    Sure, I'll post what I got on this one. 'Tis extremely rough, so any polish is welcome. I am unlikely to haunt this site much for a while, though.

                                    The Pit Baiters
                                    Fate (Death, Prime)

                                    This would be a legacy that practices Fate magic in an attempt to learn how to use Fate and Luck to bind and control the Abyss.

                                    They see some of their applications of their art as "cursing" the Abyss and making it less likely to affect the world. As far as these see it, the Supernal Realm defines what is possible, while the Loom of Fate defines what is. The Abyss represents what isn't and cannot be. If appropriately handled, then, the forces of Are Not can be carefully handled be weaving carefully and ending the trouble so caused. Paradoxes are snarls in the weave of reality, which a dedicated weaver can go back and repair, but is better served by preventing initially...

                                    1st: Curse the Darkness
                                    Fate Prime
                                    The mage tries the primary force of the legacy, and curses the Abyss with the power of Fate.
                                    The mage uses an action, and rolls Fate + Resolve + Composure. Each success affects one Paradox roll. Affected Paradox rolls are reduced by this caster's Fate rating. The range is sensory to the mage at the time of the casting. The mage cannot select the affected spells, just the Paradox rolls in the scene.

                                    Furthermore, the mage can either reduce or increase the penalty to their own casting rolls caused by Paradox successes by up to their Fate rating. This does nothing to mitigate the effects of the Paradox in any other way. Spells still flip out of control or breaches in reality still form, etc., but the penalty imposed on the mage's magic is reduced after the Paradox is calculated. Generally, this means that they reduce the spell effect, preferably removing it. This must be declared before the results are finalized. Once the targets of havoc are declared, it's too late... (unless that's a temptation we'd like)

                                    2nd: Forging the Bane of Darkness
                                    Fate Prime
                                    The mage may craft a Spindle of Fate, which is alternately known as a Paradox Ward. This item may be any device wrought from iron or silver. Items could also be crafted from both. The functional structure must be pure iron or silver, and have been worked with hand tools. The creation of this item requires a mana.

                                    The mage must hold the device in their hand to use it. Only with such a device in hand can the mage use this attainment. This further limits useful shapes, but otherwise the device can be essentially any item. A ring could be worn and still count as held.

                                    The mage must use an action to empower the item in hand, and only one such device may be empowered by the mage at any given time. While the device is held in a hand and lifted up, the Abyss shrinks back. This subjects any Abyssal creature in direct line of sight and within sensory range to a penalty to all rolls equal to the caster's Fate dice. This also reduces all Paradox pools by one die within sight of the object. Both caster and the area affected by any spells cast must be within line of sight of the Paradox Ward for this effect.

                                    Those strongly tainted with the power of the Abyss, such as Tremere cursed liches and vampires, are affected by the curse as would be any other.

                                    (It's reasonable that Havoc would usually not target the character, too, but I haven't thought of any good system for that. Maybe just declare it so. )


                                    3rd: Striking the Heart of Darkness
                                    The mage's Paradox Wards now strike out at the Abyss.

                                    The device now deals lethal to any Abyssal creatures on a touch equal to the creator's Fate. If the device is a weapon, the mage may add Fate to the extant equipment bonuses.

                                    It is rumored the Fourth Attainment is similar to the spell Swarm of Locusts, which renders Paradox much simpler to deal with.


                                    I like the theme much better than these mechanics, though. I didn't come up with anything particularly evocative of the "binding up the abyss in rules" aspect I was looking for. Got me a migraine today, so I'm not even going to try to tackle this, now.

                                    Grump, grouse, and/or gripe.

                                            I've had a huge interest in psi since...well, I don't even remember when it started any more, since I was a kid. Probably dates back to my early X-Men fandom some time in the early-mid 80s, but might go back further. Ascension makes this both easy and hard (paradigms model anything, but a model of pure mental manifestation is tricky for balance reasons), but in Awakening, it's just plain easy, thanks to Legacies. Without further ado, a bare-bones version of the Psychonauts (based on the practice of Psychonautics as a path to psychic ability, not the video game -- though it's a great game):

                                            Psychonauts (Free Council, Mind, Optional: Forces):
                                            Psychics are real (note: see Second Sight). They are an objective Truth of the world, people who transcend the Lie through direct gifts rather than rituals. Often it is also a curse, but psychic ability is, to followers of this Legacy, an example of the Lie's vulnerability -- people strive for Truth, become living conduits of Truth, even wield the Truth as a weapon. Though seen as the odd stepchildren of science by some Libertines, the Psychonauts point to the undeniable (to Awakened) existence of Second Sight, and use it to transform themselves to mirror this model. Psychonauts, or mindwalkers (sometimes derisively called Scanners or brain-divers), take this transformation seriously, and their culture is strongly inclined to ethical use of their abilities. This isn't to say that they're immune to corruption -- far from it, some terrible Fallen have come from their Legacy -- but rather that they police themselves fiercely, given the abuses a telepathic Legacy is capable of.

                                            The primary Sphere is Mind, for its astral insights and telepathic communion; while Psychonauts are supportive of mediums, they're still Libertines, and their psychic model connects them to the Mysteries of humanity, not other Realms. When sh*t gets real, their Optional Attainments draw on the Symbol of mind over matter for telekinesis, shielding, and even manipulating heat for "thermokinesis," roasting or chilling targets with equal facility. (The new five-rank model will allow for more abilities; I'm thinking one will be flight-via-levitation, but am divided between more straight-up pyrokinesis and the less-classical but no less valid electrokinesis.)

                                            Psychonauts view themselves as explorers, using their mental Attainments to seek humanity's magical potential within the minds of those they wish to liberate. However, they've gained a reputation within the Free Council as guardians and crusaders against the Lie, due to the useful mix of being able to read an opponent's actions, then counter them at range. Since Pyschonauts are typically even more offended by servants of the Lie than the average Libertine, they end up drawn to this role in spite of most mindwalkers' vocal objections to the contrary. A few embrace a role as adventurer-heroes, but they tend to be...odd, even for Psychonauts.

                                            Heroes die. Hope is a phoenix.
