    Little Pieces of Heaven - An attempt at rewriting Artifacts

    01-09-2017, 07:49 PM
    So, there's been some discussion on Artifacts lately and it's gotten me wanting to try and formalize my own house rules about them. I've written down my own copy on my Drive but, truth be told, getting it out there is about as necessary as putting pen to paper for me; not only does it require you to explain what you're going for and how you intend to invoke it (and why), it also lets you get feedback on that from people who have no reason not to honest.

    As such, here's my attempts to rejigger Artifacts. Comments and critiques are, as ever, welcomed.

    ---

    What This Is: An attempt to rewrite Artifacts, both to make them more distinct from Imbued Items and to give them a role befitting their natures in the stories of the Awakened. Rotes and grimoires are no so potent that it is not hard to see why the Awakened would kill for them. Artifacts, though... Well... I've never honestly felt like they're something a character would kill for. They're interesting, but not murder-worthy.

    This rewrite reshapes the powers of Artifacts significantly, making them more reliable and, hence, more potent. The rewards for possession and study of one are also enhanced in this, beyond the few A-Beats that may fall into a player's lap for their possession. They are intended to be things that would be useful even in the hands of a Mage who is already a master of the arcanum the Artifact draws upon. For these reasons, they are adamantly not intended to be things that player characters collect as trophies. Even a single Artifact being in the permanent possession of a cabal could become a feature in the game's landscape. This will be addressed more later on.

    In addition, a part of this write-up is also to hint at another project coming up; one to differentiate Imbued Items (and other Awakened creations) from true Artifacts, and to try and give each distinct roles in the secret world of the Awakened.

    ---

    Artifacts Revisited

    What is an Artifact? A lucky Sleepwalker who glimpses such things of wonder may describe it as an object which contains magical power, like a saintly relic that provides miraculous cures. This is an accurate, if mundane, description; each Artifact is indeed an object which may, properly induced, provoke a grand magical change in the world. However, the Awakened know them to be so much more.

    Artifacts are not mere objects imbued with magical power; they are magical power, fallen from the Supernal and crystallized in tangible form. This divinity in mortal shell is described in some Awakened theories as a "fixed idea"; a captured perspective on a Supernal Truth. It is, in essence, a spell in physical form, with its actual shape like an Imago pressed into raw Mana.

    This nature as a fixed idea means that Artifacts are relatively fixed things; producing the same effect every time, without much variation. They do precisely what is in keeping with their natures, and nothing more or less.

    Designing an Artifact

    Artifacts are a merit, rated at 3+ dots, depending on how many features or traits they have. Despite their mechanization as merits, it is not recommended that they be allowed to be purchased by players and, instead, are bestowed (and taken away) by the ST during play. The merit dots, instead, act as a useful guideline for the ST on their relative power and value, and for determining their Mana pools.

    Artifacts are designed similarly to Legacy attainments; beginning by determining the most powerful effect and the number of arcanum dots required to achieve it (achieving it in this case including also covering all the Reach required; a Spirit Shielding spell with Instant Casting and Advanced Duration requires 3 dots to achieve). This rating is used to determine the Primary Factor of the spell, as normal, and then additional factors equal to it may be distributed as desired. Once chosen, however, those factors are set, and are the same in every casting (usually; extra triggers may be in place to cast it in a different way, but that's the exception, not the norm).

    If desired, the Artifact's dots may be raised to provide the extra factors for a spell, but it cannot be pushed above five. If an Artifact performs an extended casting, the minimum casting time is one hour, but there is no "usual" time; many are idiosyncratic, such as beginning casting when exposed to the light of sunset and casting at sunrise.

    When activated, an Artifact casts the spell as designed above and achieves success on the casting equal to the number of dots in the arcanum in question that it "possesses", without any rolling. Artifacts may not be counterspelled but their magical effects may be dispelled. In a Clash of Wills, they do not roll, instead attaining automatic successes equal to the arcanum dots used.

    In addition to this, Artifacts are not activated by an act of will but, rather, by an action, which may be a spoken word or an act. A magical sword bursts into flame when swung; an Artifact key opens portals to any destination when put into a lock and turned. What these triggers are is not immediately evident; many Artifacts have languished in Athenaea for centuries with their secrets still yet to be fully unlocked.

    Artifacts have Mana pools equal to their dot rating. Their dot rating is equal to 3 + the highest arcanum used + 1 per Utility Attainment included + 1 per additional spell included. These pools regenerate at a rate of one Mana per day.

    Example Artifact: The Whispering Lion - Artifact ooooo ooo

    The Whispering Lion is a small lion statue in Egyptian style, not much larger than a house cat, which appears to be carved and polished by hand from natural granite rock. When the statue is set before a dwelling and a ritual is performed involving freshly spilled blood being poured across its teeth, its eyes shine with the light of the full moon. Until the next full moon, evil spirits cannot enter the domicile, nor harm those within it, as the lion`s baleful gaze keeps them at bay. Usually, ritualists do not invoke the lion save at the full moon anyway, as its protection ends at the turn of the full moon, regardless of whether it has been a full lunar month between then and its activation.

    The effects of the Artifact are Space 3`s Ban with the Advanced Duration and Area Reach effects, using Spirit, Death, and Mind to target spirits, ghosts, and goetia. The Ban is keyed to allow in these entities invited by the ones who performed the ritual. Since this requires four dots in the arcanum, the Artifact is considered to have Space 4. Any Clashes of Wills inflicted must contest its four automatic successes.

    In addition, the Lion contains a secondary effect, wherein any ephemeral that tries to breach the Ban is inflicted with an overriding fear, inflicting the Frightened Condition upon them. This is a Ruling effect, combining the Spirit, Death, and Mind arcana. It uses only the Instant Casting Reach effect, meaning it only has two arcanum dots in the effect. The Primary Factor is Potency. Its secondary two factor levels are applied one to Potency and one to Duration. The Lion is considered to "touch" any spirit who tries to enter the area of the Ban.

    ---

    But What About Paradox?

    It is said that, when the Awakened reach to grasp the Supernal Realms, their hubris draws in the Abyss. Yet once a spell is in the world, does it cause Paradox? Does it invite in the Abyss? The answers is no. Artifacts, as pieces of the Supernal, are not subject to Paradox in their activation, even in the presence of Sleepers. Despite this, however, the Awakened have ample reason not to use Artifacts in front of Sleepers. For one, the effects of the Artifacts are just as subject to Dissonance and unraveling as any spell.

    More importantly, however, Artifacts themselves are subject to Dissonance. Though a Sleeper does not destroy an Artifact merely by looking at it, recognizing it for what it is triggers the same horrifying response as recognizing an existing spell. Any time a Sleeper witness sees an Artifact unleash its grand powers, it accrues Dissonance; suffering the successes as damage that cannot be repaired by any means short of the blessings of Supernal entities, and such things are rare indeed. In this way, Sleepers essentially permanently diminish the presence of the Supernal through their ignorance; destroying precious fragments of higher reality.

    For this reason, Artifacts are protected violently by the Awakened. While Seers of the Throne have magical sugar daddies throwing diamonds from heaven, even they know they will face the wrath of the Exarchs if they casually allow their priceless gifts to come undone. More than one mystagogue has killed Sleepers just for getting close to where Artifacts are hidden.

    ---

    Making It Real

    Artifacts are not mundane objects. Each one is a fragment of higher reality given form. That form is representative of whatever Truth they are a part of. In this way, Artifacts take the form of legendary items and precious impossibilities; things that feature prominently in myths and stories that seem impossible in mundane reality. Even the simplest Artifact that gives no signs of being overtly magical still signals that, on some level, it is not mundane. The crystal shines just a bit too brightly; the surface is just a bit too perfect at being what it is.

    All Artifacts appear to be "perfected" versions of whatever form they take, be it as simple as a feather to as grand as a magical chariot. While this does not mean they are all flawlessly beautiful, even an object that represents decay and disease is somehow morbidly fascinating.

    This perfection bestows the following qualities on all Artifacts:

    Imperishable: Artifacts gain Durability equal to the highest arcanum rating they possess.

    Inexhaustible: Even Artifacts that would seem "one use", like elixirs and potions, are fated never to be forgotten. Such Artifacts either regenerate their apparently lost features or reappear somewhere else.

    Glorious: All Artifacts give off a kind of resonance that can only be described as virtue, in the classic Greek sense. Whatever it is that the Artifact's natural form does, it adds +2 to the equipment bonus. Artifact clothes radiate their natural mood; Artifact knives cut more deeply; Artifact harps cannot help but play elegant music.

    Something To Kill For

    In addition to these effects, every Artifact also possesses the following far more important trait:

    Vision of Truth: The physical forms of Artifacts are, in effect, an Imago. By studying this Imago, dissecting it, and coming to understand it, a Mage may gain access to a glimpse of Supernal Truth. By accessing the Deep Information of an Artifact with Mage Sight, a Mage may learn the rote for the spell(s) encoded in the Artifact as if it were a grimoire.

    If a Mage who has learned the rote chooses to cast it, they may use the Artifact as a grimoire. However, the pure, unspoiled Supernal knowledge is more "pure" than that in a grimoire; unburdened by the biases of the author of such Fallen tomes. Reading the book of the Supernal, a Mage may cast such spells with greater haste; allowing them to apply the rote quality when casting with reference even on instant castings (though referencing the grimoire does fill a yantra slot). Understandably, this makes such Artifacts tremendously potent, even for experienced Mages.

    ---

    Alright, so... That's a lot to get out. I'll leave it at that for now. See what people think.

    So... What do you think?

          My goal for Imbued Items - when I get to them - is to hopefully put them in a different narrative space than Artifacts. Imbued Items would, ideally, fall into a role of "all-purpose plot duct tape"; acting as a one-time use tool that lets a character accomplish something they couldn't normally but without ultimately affecting their long-term narrative. If the ST needs a character to have a certain spell that they lack in order to slay a monster, the Imbued Item fulfills the role. They hurl the lightning spear to slay the dragon, after which it is spent; they drink the magic potion. You get the idea.

          If characters want iconic items for their characters or odd magical tools that provide interesting bonuses but aren't much more than a dice bonus, I usually apply those things under "natural magic" (i.e. magical objects that come into existence without the use of Awakened spells) or something that's been enhanced with a relinquished spell. Even small but rather distinctive effects - like a gun that shoots lightning but is, for the most part, narratively no different from a slightly more powerful gun - aren't really worth modeling as one of these. If the player wants a magic gun, just give them a magic gun, not what amounts to a modular talent for magic.

          Glad you like it. The Mana thing isn't really necessary either, so exclude it if you want; it's more a relic of 1e than anything.

              This is great! I especially like how they're subject to Dissonance now. It gives a really good reason for Mages being so protective of them, even more so than usual.

              I would personally say that Artifacts should be completely immune to any damage that doesn't come from Dissonance (this includes immunity to magic both Supernal and otherwise, unless cast by an Archmage). If they are merely durable, you can break them with Matter, or even mundane equipment. That doesn't sound fitting for such legendary items.

              If an Artifact is destroyed by Dissonance, can a Supernal entity still bring it back? They are Platonic ideals, so I believe it should be possible, even if the Supernal entity must be of high Rank and ask a big favor in return.

              The equipment bonus given by the Glorious aspect feels underwhelming. I'd say it should give bonus Successes equal to the highest Arcanum used + 1 and 8 Again. That puts it on par with the Props of the True Fae.

              Can Artifacts possess a mind of their own? Even if they can, I believe they shouldn't be able to act of their own accord. Instead, they should manipulate people into using them, like many cursed items do. Instead of rolling to manipulate people, they would score automatic Successes equal to the highest Arcanum used.
