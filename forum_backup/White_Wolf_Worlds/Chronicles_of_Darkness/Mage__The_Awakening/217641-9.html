        I'm honestly of the opinion that, in a Mage game, mortal hunters are hideously outclassed and quickly are got rid of or manipulated into being pawns of one Mage or another.

        Yes, this doesn't seem very fair or nice, and it goes against the tradition of supporting the underdog, but Mage is about Mages and the world us neither nice nor fair in the face of hubristic shapers of reality.

        That said, Mages aren't the only witches out there, and I think Mage does benefit from the presence of mortals with the odd minor supernatural talent, in fact, they probably outnumber Mages. A few mortal hunters might successfully go after someone who can talk to ghosts and communicate telepathically. That said, they might regret it when that minor occultist gets rescued by their full actual Mage patron, who is comparatively a scary, scary bastard.

        I run all the game lines as being in separate worlds myself. Just prefer it that way. So I have no issue with Hunter and Mage clashing.

                A distinct part of Mage is that Mages are actually exceptionally powerful and privileged people who think they're the underdog, when they can be bothered to turn their thoughts towards a higher cause. A Mage getting hunted by people is just chickens coming home to roost as someone reminds them they're only the hero of their own story, not necessarily everyone else's.

                There's an entire Order whose central thesis is based around being able to dismiss a Sleeper who says "What the hell is wrong with you!?" by answering "You don't know what you really want, and that's why I am in charge!".

                And yeah, there's way more Sleepwalkers, psychics, thaumaturges, and other "low" magic occultists making magic amulets, building spirit traps, or monkeying with destiny than there are Mages in a given region. Them ending up fearing their Mage patrons is a thing that tends to happen a lot.

                    I tend to feel that Mages often have a network of occultists, minor psychics, fringe academics, sleepwalkers, occasionally proximi and such who they cultivate over time.

                    Whether they are relatively benevolent teachers, mentors and patrons or ruthless bastards who use them as pawns and sacrifices, or wherever in between, varies wildly. But I think it's often the case. In that situation, mortal hunters do have a role to play. I'd potentially use them as threats to that exact network.

                    Like, you get a phone call from one of your sleepwalker occultist allies. She's worried, and thinks she's being followed. As it happens, she is, by a cell of hunters convinced she's a witch who is responsible for things that you actually did, but whatever. Do you abandon your own shit to go and check to see if she really is being followed? Every time? You can find out the truth quite easily, probably, but you have a number of people, and you can't always be running after them when they jump at shadows. In that sense, mortal hunters are a source of pressure on others that you value, people you can't always protect, especially as some of those hunters are actually pretty dangerous, determined and careful.
