                After thinking about this a great deal I’ve come to the conclusion that the only fair way to deal with this mana and willpower refund or the condition that grants beats is to say that it only applies in situations with dramatic tension.

                An example: You have a spell that lets you jump really far. You are out in the middle of nowhere practicing your jump spell. Jump over this, jump over that, but nothing is chasing you. There is no consequence if you fail. If your spell goes well that’s cool but your life didn’t depend on it so you aren’t that PLEASED or REWARDED. There’s no RELEEF of dramatic tension of having overcome a fearful situation. Replenishing will and mana in this case is like a trip to the gas station only you have a gas station everywhere (more on this later).

                Now you are running from a pack of werewolves. They are closing in. You approach a river and your jump spell fails. You splash across but the wolves are faster. They’re gaining on you . . . You reach a cliff with a ledge far above. There’s no time to climb the cliff face but if you make it you think you can lose the werewolf pack. You call up your jump spell, make the leap and find yourself soaring through the air past the ledge you were aiming for all the way to the top of the cliff! The werewolves are ants far below! They are as far below you as you are below the sun! The supernal shines through your elation at surviving! You are so grateful for your powers! You float down to the top of the cliff and catch your breath hearing the frustrated howls of the werewolves far below. You’ve got a huge lead on them now and with any luck you’ll make it out of the woods before they find you again.
                Do you see the difference here? In one situation there’s no dramatic tension and thus no corresponding relief if the spell goes well. In the other situation your life depends on the spell working and the exceptional success made the difference between you having to continue running from the werewolves (thus prolonging the scene) and effectively ending the encounter. If I were a storyteller in a mage game that would be my yardstick. There has to be something at risk to reap the benefits from an exceptional success on a spell roll whether that spell is a praxis or not.

                Now let’s take another example: You’re driving your car in the desert. It’s been a while since your last gas station but you’ve still got half a tank. You’re pretty sure you can make it to the next one on the map with some gas to spare. Suddenly some ominous black car starts chasing you. In the ensuing chase you have to race down several side roads or even off road. Through lots of fancy driving you finally get the ominous black car stuck in a ditch. You get back to the road heading toward the next gas station. By the time you get there you’re running on fumes. You pull in only to see that the station is abandoned . . .

                Now here is my two cents on having a repeatable, spam-able, willpower or mana engine. If you are carrying your own gas station with you there is no dramatic tension in seeing the station empty. Your car is always full when it comes to the chase scene and always full afterwards. If you have Life magic hit points are not a problem between encounters. There are specific healing spells in the game, spelled out in the core rules. Therefore the game is designed around replenishing hit points between encounters. This is even truer at higher levels where even aggravated wounds can be healed with extra reach.

                A master of Prime or a master of Spirit with a familiar (essence fountain) won’t have as many problems with mana between encounters. Keep in mind that these are master level spells. Higher level mages should be able to call on more magic. However I don’t see to many spells that flat out heal willpower. Yes you can rip it out of people with Death magic or heal one point a day with the Knit spell (equivalent to a good night’s sleep) but I don’t see any spell that explicitly heals willpower to full from nothing. Taking advantage of critical successes in a literal wording of the rules so that EVERY SPELL heals willpower reliably between encounters with no storyteller input seems to cheapen the other mechanics of replenishing willpower (virtue and vice, rest ect). This is another reason that if I were storytelling a game I would only allow critical successes on spells to replenish willpower and mana if that critical success helped resolve a stressful encounter.

                The essence fountain “mana engine” or the ability to create permanent hallows as a master of Prime are the equivalent of having a solar powered car (essence fountain) or a solar powered house (lasting create truth). Both have their uses, both have their limits, neither spell replenishes willpower.

                        I don't like making Praxes work only during INTENSE DRAMATIC LIFE DEPENDING SCENES, mostly because for cost of 4 praxes, you could just get a new dot of Arcana. You buy them for a reason, this reason being "exceptional success", and taking that away seems like creating a trap buy.
                        Most of Mana Engines can be accessed by starting characters, btw. There is a Mind Spell at 3rd dots that restores you one willpower per use, and Life 3 has a spell that gives you benefits of a nights rest - night rest restores 1 willpower too.

                        EDIT
                        Again, note that Full Refill on Willpower isn't as good for Mages as it is for other splats. Mages BURN willpower. Math is so stacked that you are going to spend Willpower on pretty much every single Mage-specific ability in order to have decent chance of success. For starting Mages, Willpower is A MUST to reliably cast anything past parlor trick. 2 or 3 rounds of combat can burn off a "Willpower 6" Mage from 100 to 0, especially if it involves Clash of Wills.
