              Originally posted by CharonFerryman View Post
              1) Shadow Names are Resplendent Destinies to a much lesser mechanical degree.
              Only in an extremely roundabout way — your Shadow Name exists to keep magical phenomena (which naturally exerts a traumatic force on the mortal mind that they cope with by denying it) from aggregating outside of the part of your life that you dedicate to the study and pursuit of magic, which isn't quite the same as allowing your character's impact on people to stick.
              2) Nimbus are just anima flares and anima banners without anima powers?
              Your Nimbus is the symbolism that surrounds your magical nature; it attracts magical strangeness to things you have a strong relation to, flavors the after-effects of your magic, and can apply a minor benefit or penalty to certain other characters' rolls for a short time.
              3) What is a Mystery?
              A Mystery is an occult quality of something in the world that merits investigation, like the cause of an annual rain of toads or the creations of a sleeping god's dreams.
              4) Is a Legacy like an Essence 6 upgrade of Charms?
              No. I'm still quite far behind on my reading for 3e, but you might think of it as a set of Evocations for a magical theory.
              5) Gnosis = Essence? How does it interact with Arcanum? Can I have 5 in Arcanum and still be at Gnosis 1?
              Your Gnosis sets how high your highest Arcanum rating can be and Arcanum 5 isn't available for anything until Gnosis 5.
              6) It says Praxes are not rated in dots, so does that mean I can start with a 5 dot Praxes right off the bat?
              No, because you still need the relevant Arcanum rating to be able to have a spell as a Praxis. Praxes aren't rated in dots in that learning an Arcanum 5 spell as a Praxes is no more difficult for a character with Arcanum 5 than learning an Arcanum 1 spell would be.

                  Ok this is the list of stuff I'm not getting:

                  2) Nimbus

                  What I can gather so far are these:

                  1) Shadow Names are Resplendent Destinies to a much lesser mechanical degree.
                  A Shadow Name is a magical identity, but it's not a magical alternate identity, which I believe Resplendent Destinies are. Which is where I think your "lesser mechanical degree" impression likely comes from. Magical alternate identities would be the Masques used by the Guardians of the Veil, a society of mages who channel the Supernal ideal of concealment.

                  In fact, more on that "channeling a Supernal ideal" stuff... That's what Shadow Names basically are. You name yourself after or as a concept, and you slowly become that concept. For example, if someone's Shadow Name is Odin, he likely wields a lot of Death and Fate magic, acts somewhere between a poet and a warrior-king and a shadowy manipulator. You can also expect he'll eventually blind himself in pursuit of knowledge if he already hasn't, and eventually get hurt real bad by some lupine monstrosity. Also he's probably quite a hubristic one, since he named himself after a god.

                  2) Nimbus are just anima flares and anima banners without anima powers?
                  Again, not quite sure what Animas are... but basically, there are three degrees your Nimbus manifests as. The Long-Term Nimbus is a series of small coincidences and events that happen around you. Purely narrative stuff. The Immediate Nimbus is a powerful flare of your magical aura that can leave a Nimbus Tilt. This can counter the aura-flares of other supernatural beings, and can affect even Sleepers who won't see what they're being hit with. Lastly, the Signature Nimbus is exactly that; a signature your spells leave. You can intentionally leave them on stuff, though I myself have yet to figure out what'd be the use of doing so.

                  3) What is a Mystery?
                  Anything supernatural a mage can Obsess over, studying it and picking it apart (sometimes literally) to glean insight from it, which serves to further their understanding of the Supernal (even if it doesn't fit into how the Supernal works).

                  4) Is a Legacy like an Essence 6 upgrade of Charms?
                  A Legacy is basically a prestige class. It gives you more than access to Attainments, which I think would be the equivalent of Charms. Essence 6 Charms are high-end stuff, right? Not all Legacy Attainments require significant power, though.

                  5) Gnosis = Essence? How does it interact with Arcanum? Can I have 5 in Arcanum and still be at Gnosis 1?
                  Gnosis is the "potency stat" like Essence for Exalted is, but I don't know how such a stat works for Exalted in the first place. Gnosis gives you the following: Supernatural Tolerance (resist supernatural effects), superhuman ability (your Attributes and Skills are capped by the higher of 5 or your Gnosis), and a bunch of other stuff, but most importantly the maximum dots in Arcana you can have. 5 dots in a single Arcanum requires Gnosis 5 and higher. Unless you're talking about 5 or more dots spread out through the Arcana, which is perfectly doable at lower Gnosis ratings, though I can't remember the exact numbers.

                  6) It says Praxes are not rated in dots, so does that mean I can start with a 5 dot Praxes right off the bat?
                  You need to be able to cast it improvised (i.e. normally) to get it as a Praxis. It's just that a Praxis' Experience cost is flat, not rated according to their Arcana dots. So no 5-dot spells right off the bat.

                      Analogies will not help that much with some of these; not everything is a direct reskin of something from an older White Wolf game.

                      1) Mages use Shadow Names for three primary reasons.
                      First, because another mage can more easily target you with curses and harmful spells from a distance if they know your sympathetic name (which is usually your birth name/legal name). Using a fakey wizard name when you're around wizards doesn't prevent other mages from finding out your sympathetic name, but why make it easy for them?
                      Second, because little quirky spookiness tends to happen around mages and those who are close to them (this is the so-called "long-term nimbus"). Mages have found that those fakey wizard names, when used consistently in the context of wizarding, pick up the symbolic relevance of their wizardness in a way that results in that spookiness concentrating around their wizardly associates and laboratories, instead of everybody they know. It shuffles weird stuff over into a corner so it doesn't bother your everyday life or point back to you as easily.
                      Third, since magic is symbolism, some mages get into acting out the symbolism of whatever fakey wizard name they choose. These wizards find that they can just use "acting like Merlin/Gilgamesh/John McClane" as a yantra or magical focus, meaning they can cast spells more effectively without having to wave baubles and trinkets around as much. These particular wizards are the ones who have the Shadow Name Merit. Other wizards without that Merit still have shadow names, they just don't really care much about acting like Merlin.

                      2) The nimbus is the closest analogy out of your provided questions. The nimbus section is confusingly divided into three effects, including the "long-term nimbus" of generic spookiness I mentioned above, and the "signature nimbus" which just means "every mage's spells have kind of a different metaphorical scent, so if a mage sniffs around they can try to distinguish who cast what."
                      The "immediate nimbus," however, is what folks are usually talking about when they just say "the nimbus," and it is basically a lower-level peripheral anima banner with a lower-level anima power, that power being the Nimbus Tilt. Each mage has a unique immediate nimbus with their own Nimbus Tilt, which might buff or debuff certain attributes or skills based on the symbolism. In terms of visuals, maybe a little less Dragonball Z auras, a little more picture that scene in the Lord of the Rings movie where Frodo offers Galadriel the ring and she terrifies him with a glimpse of what she would be like as a beautiful and terrible queen.

                      3) This is one of those "jargon-not-jargon" words that looks like jargon from the context it's put in. It's actually not. A mystery is a mystery. A mystery is when something mysterious, especially supernatural, is happening. The Chronicles of Darkness has weird shit all over the place, people staring at you from within the mirror, houses larger on the inside than the outside, lakes that hate. Mages investigate mysteries because a) they're just generally intellectually driven people who want to understand what they don't understand, b) there are limits on how far your understanding of the Supernal World can progress without uncovering secrets (a.k.a. getting arcane beats), and c) finding out how these things happen often translates to knowing dangerous secrets you can use to your own advantage, like tricking people into getting eaten by the lake.

                      4) A Legacy is like a prestige class in Dungeons & Dragons terms. You typically come to one later in your career rather than sooner, you really only get room for one to invest in, and it involves investing in a narrowed-down concept of just what kind of character you're interested in being. Some Legacies learn to do unique things that aren't quite spellcasting, while others simply get very good at using their invested effects easily. In Exalted 3e terms, you might also think of obtaining a Legacy as getting to designate a Supernal Ability and benefit from Supernal-only effects, though it won't let you leapfrog to spells you don't qualify for.

                      5) There is a table in the character creation chapter that lists a bunch of values you derive from your current Gnosis. Gnosis is your broad level of cosmic understanding and power, while Arcana are your applied learning, so to speak; both traits are used to assemble your spellcasting pools. Gnosis does cap how many dots you may have in each Arcanum, so no, you can't have Forces 5 until you first hit Gnosis 5. As your Gnosis increases, you can store more Mana (Essence motes), perform ritual castings quicker, improve your spellcasting with more yantra baubles, and risk Paradoxes more. (Yes, that last one is a bad thing.)

                      6) Since you can't exit chargen with an Arcanum at five dots because you won't have enough Gnosis, you wouldn't have a five-dot praxis because you don't know how to cast five-dot spells. But you're correct, a one-dot praxis or a three-dot praxis would cost you exactly the same.

                                          Gnosis caps on Arcana aren't a 1:1 thing like Essence with Charms. The chart on page 86 of Awakening 2e has the numbers. Part of this is that there's two caps, your highest Arcana (which generally has a higher cap) and the rest (slightly lower). At Gnosis 1 it's 3 for highest and 2 for other. Each dot in Gnosis increases one of the caps by one, and it alternates. So Gnosis 2 raises other by one (to 3), Gnosis 3 raises highest by one (to 4), and so on until they're both at 5.

                                          A few things:

                                          1) A way to think about shadow names is the Destiny-Manifesting Method Charm from Exalted 3e rather than one of the default Exalted powers; even if it is more of a default thing for mages. It's not a direct analog of course, but conceptually it's closer in purpose.

                                          7&8) Aspirations and Obsessions are more like Intimacies in terms of the game space they occupy. They can be almost anything that the player character feels strongly about. They can be "good" or bad" because they're just things your character pursues. The separation between them is that Aspirations create "mundane" learning experiences (they provide normal Beats), while Obsessions create "supernal" learning experiences (they provide Arcane Beats). Aspirations don't have to be strictly mundane goals, but resolving them results in more traditional learning experiences, while pursuing Obsessions grants magical epiphanies.

                                              Originally posted by CharonFerryman View Post
                                              7) Are Aspirations social goals?
                                              8) Are Obsessions Limit Breaks?
                                              Aspirations and Obsessions are things you write down to indicate the plot arcs you're interested in hitting and the actions your character is pursuing. Aspirations are general-purpose things you care about (which can involve investigating things or supernatural conflicts), while Obsessions are specifically arcane mysteries and supernatural pursuits. Your Obsession is typically where you put something like "study different ways to raise the unquiet dead" or "document every otherworldly portal known within the county," whereas Aspirations might include things like "secure a hideout against intruders," "bind a ghost to serve me," or "find a jerk to humiliate."

                                              They matter because pursuing them gives you more beats.

                                              Originally posted by CharonFerryman View Post
                                              Also, would the Path be more like the type of Exalted you become and the Order as the Caste of that particular Exalt?
                                              You can think of Path as analogous to either the type of Exalted you become or the caste of a particular type of Exalted. Either way, your Path is how you earned and marked an Awakening of a particular broad stripe, closely identified with certain symbols, methods, and powers. Dawn Castes emerge from those with glorious potential for battle, wield the powers of Archery and Brawling, and are associated with tigers and the sunrise. Theurges on the Path of Obrimos emerge from those with insight to seek truth from within the inner order of things, wield the Arcana of Forces and Prime, and are associated with angels and thunder.

                                              Order is a political and philosophical alignment based on which of several established schools of mages took you in and taught you their ways. Each has guiding teachings on how they view magic and how it should and should not be wielded, and to what ends. You might think of a mage's Order as analogous to a Sidereal Exalted's chosen Faction in narrative role, or perhaps a Cainite vampire's Sect. A mage's Order is chosen, not inborn, and they are free if they so choose to join no Order at all, though this denies them a great breadth of established learning.

                                              The Orders like to share their teachings and recruit new mages because they want to convince more mages that their way is right, and because they acknowledge that even when the student is not particularly interested in their mystic secrets, it's still more prudent to fill a rookie in and bring them up to speed than to leave them out in the wild as loose cannons.
