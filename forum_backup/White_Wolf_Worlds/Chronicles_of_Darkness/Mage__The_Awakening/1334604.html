    Lex Magica Questions

    09-09-2019, 07:10 AM
    I am basing this off p. 69 in the book so if there is more detailed information elsewhere that is up to date with the 2nd edition, let me know...

    So as I understand it the Gold Laws are kind of the "Constitution of the Awakened World" and you can go pretty much anywhere in the world and expect these laws to be respected. "They're rare" is kind of a weird way of putting it, I imagine this means that they are rarely invoked and there just aren't very many of them. They cover
    • Mages have the right to study magic, so long as they do not interfere with other mages' studies (my right to swing my arm ends at your nose)
    • The right for a mage to maintain a Sanctum and control Hallows without fear of trespass
    • The right for a mage to be recognized for their mastery of the Arcana
    Given how universal the Gold Laws are, I would think it would be easier to just innumerate them in the book?

    Also, the Silver Laws are the level where local Consilium Charters are enumerated, suggesting that a person living outside of a Consilium in theory should be protected by the Gold Laws. Yet on the whole they seem pretty weak. Suppose there is a hermit who holds a sanctum out in the wilderness far from any Consilium's jurisdiction, and a mage barges into his sanctum, steals his Mana, destroys his research and writes "Yo mastery of Death magic ain't shit!" on the walls. Who exactly is this hermit going to complain to? Who is going to enforce the Gold Laws?

    Incidentally, beyond insults, what exactly does being "recognized for their mastery of the Arcana" really entail?

    I suppose he could bring his complaint to the next Convocation (in the style of ancient Vikings bringing their legal complaints to the Thing) but beyond that it's kind of the Wild West, I suppose... or is that maybe more at the Silver Law level?

    So what level are the Precepts at? The text is pretty vague on this, almost suggesting that they are a mixture of Silver and Bronze, but given how universal they are, it seems like they should be Silver at the minimum. There are probably numerous Bronze laws regarding how they should be implemented, maybe that's what they are referring?

    Also, has anyone come up with some interesting Silver laws for your games?

Add an occasional fabrication and you can bet every Silver Ladder and Mysterium Caucus has its own, slightly different interpretation.

              If they are nobodies, they are in a difficult situation, especially if the perpetrator is a member. The Hierarch may not be very keen to send a Sentinel in the middle of nowhere, so the actual investigation will probably be delayed or delegated to some nobodies (here's an opportunity for your cabal). And even if the offending mage is guilty as sin, the actual punishment may involve nothing more than a very stern talk.                       The Gold Laws are general legal principles, even above constitutional laws. They are like, the right "to claim and make use of private property", "to seek mediation and arbitration from a judicial institution", "to freedom of movement and speech" and so on, except regarding magic and the mysteries.


                      According the the Silver Ladder book, a Golden Law status is only granted by Artifact (comes from the Supernal or from the Time Before), by Harmony (is geographically ubiquitous) or by Supremacy (it hasn't been challenged and it's universally accepted by Convocations). The Gold Laws include the Great Rights of mages, and the right of mages to challenge one another to legally binding duels on neutral ground (though not the specifics). Most Consilia acknowledge these Gold Laws in their own Charters, making them also Silver in the specifics of each law.

Who exactly is this hermit going to complain to? Who is going to enforce the Gold Laws?
                      A Consilium charter (especially one drafted in a Convocation) is more like a formal Constitution, and it's considered Silver Laws. Many old Bronze Laws become Silver when a Consilium legislates them as a bill of rights, or when a Consilium is formed and its constituents try to work on their predecessors' body of law. However, the Lex Magica operates on the general principles of recognition and precedent, and the ability to uphold the law depends more on who can enforce it and to whom, than its abstract legal value. In the example you mention, the particulars are highly dependent of context and the actors involved. Is the hermit an outlaw, or is it a member of the Consilium? If the latter, is he a member in good standing, or is he a known apostate? Does the perpetrator belongs to the Consilium (does a subject under the law), or is he a Seer? As others have said in this thread, the hermit could ask for a Lictor, go to the Consilium or to his own Caucus, or wait for the next Convocation to make his case there, but the answer and the type of justice he may get can be completely different depending on where he goes for satisfaction.


                      Originally posted by Froggy711 View Post
                      Incidentally, beyond insults, what exactly does being &quot;recognized for their mastery of the Arcana&quot; really entail?
                      Let's suppose you are mage, a master in the Arcana, in a new Consilium, fresh off the boat. You keep a low profile, give your Shadow name to the Herald when word reach him about you and for the most part you keep to yourself. No one's gonna put a gun on your head and make you confess about your magical capabilities, but no one's gonna trust you either: you are an unknown quantity, an inconsecuentual solitary at best, or a dangerous loner at worst. You get from the Consilium what you put into it, so to speak. Now, let's suppose that you get tired of being left out of polite society, you go ask for a Council hearing and demonstrate a Death making spell in front of the Ruling Council. Now you are a known quantity, someone worth recognition as a master. The Right of Emeritus will be upheld by many cabals that will treat you with deference and respect. You will be expected at any important Consilium gathering, and your expert opinion will be required on any mysteries concerning death and the dead. A few apprentices will petition to be tutored by you on the Death Arcanum, and and other masters will start offering small favors and see if you correspond them the same way. The more you put into society, the more you reap later.
