    Originally posted by empe999 View Post
    Q: I am not sure if I am playing paradox wrong. If a mage tries to contain paradox by rolling wisdom the worst that can happen is a Paradox Condition? It sounds pretty weak.
    It can be terrifyingly effective once you realize how quickly the mage can lose control of the situation.

    If you allow the Paradox Condition to resolve, you made your life more complicated by taking on the consequences of it.

    If you allow it to lapse, the every single spell you cast in the meantime gains a Paradox dice, starting the accumulation cascade.

    The higher your Wisdom is, the less often this will happen, but when it does, it lasts longer. Plus you have to actively strive to maintain high Wisdom and be able to deal with the resistant bashing damage when it actually contains the Paradox. If you aren't the most physically fit, it can get very nasty, very quickly. Especially if you are containing a spell tainted by the Paradox cascade mentioned above.

    You can scour the Condition, but cause a point of resistant lethal damage and uses up a daily scouring.

    Then finally add the individual details of the Condition itself and you have a domino effect of doom. If the mage is in a situation where they need to cast quickly and often (difficult social encounter, breaking into a ruin or Sanctum, combat, navigating through a Realm Invisible, etc), where they don't have the time and/or resources to reduce Paradox risk at every turn, then the threat of the Abyss will be an ever present companion.

    All it takes is one moment of desperation to make them release a Paradox they cannot contain, bringing new threats that cannot be into the Fallen World. For madness is like gravity, all it takes is a little push.

                    Originally posted by empe999 View Post
                    Q: If I cast dispel magic on a subject protected by wards and signs, do I have to beat the potency of wards and signs itself as a withstand rating? Or does it only shields the subject but not the spells on him/her?
                    Dispel Magic is cast on a spell, not a person, so unless Wards and Signs was cast on the spell you're trying to dispel the withstand is just the Arcanum rating of the spell's caster.

                    [Edit] A more clever mage might cast Wards and Signs with a small area of effect centered on themself so that it does affect all spells on them, but this is not the default and might not work depending on your ST.

                                Originally posted by LordHeru View Post
                                Is there a way to make magically created goods, alchemical potions, or Perfected Materials to be permanent, that is Lasting. I was thinking something like the magic comes and goes but the result, the Perfected Material, is now fundamentally real.

                                I mean, yeah, I can add the "+2 Reach: Add one point of Mana and the effect of the spell becomes Lasting" effect to say the spell to create Perfected Materials or whatnot but I was wondering if there was another way to go about it.

                                I don't mind it being difficult or whatnot but I do think, with effort, a mage should be able to create materials that once conjured or formed don't need active magic to stay around.
                                Depends on what you mean. You could arguably cast with enough duration to make it indefinite. The Permanence Attainment from Matter makes this a lot easier. The problem is that each item would consume Willpower to unsafely relinquish and the spell could warp after a while. At that point, you would need to look into alternative relinquishment methods, such as via breaking points, destroying a soul, having someone else pay the price (a spirit, ghost, summoned entity, other mage, etc) or simply take on the XP burden on board. Signs of Sorcery goes into more detail on making magical items and on alternative relinquishment costs.

                                Lasting means the effect is part of the natural world. Now it doesn't warp, provoke Clash of Wills and more importantly, does not trigger Quiescence or Disbelief.

                                The final and simplest solution is to have a crafting focused Legacy. An Attainment uses no spell slots, plus is immune to Quiescence and Dispellation. Even those with less than indefinite duration make up for it by sheer availability.

                                For those purposes, I recommend the Anima Meccanica for nanotech styled magical items, the Thaumatech Engineers for steampunk gadgets, the Clockwork Knights for power armor (all of them are posted in the forum) and this version of the Transhuman Engineers for cyberpunk creations:

                                https://m45t1g05.blogspot.com/2016/1...egacy.html?m=1

                                Hope that helps
